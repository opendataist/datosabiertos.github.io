The introduction of Meta IA in WhatsApp has sparked significant debate, primarily centered on the tension between technological innovation and the erosion of user privacy. Users are increasingly hesitant to adopt these AI tools due to valid concerns regarding data control, algorithmic surveillance, and the potential for personalized advertising to exploit personal information. This resistance highlights a growing demand for transparency and autonomy over how digital platforms handle sensitive user data. From an open data perspective, this controversy underscores the critical importance of data governance and individual agency in the digital ecosystem. The article illustrates how opaque data practices by major tech corporations can lead to public distrust, emphasizing the need for clearer boundaries regarding information sharing. It suggests that without robust mechanisms to protect user privacy, the integration of AI into everyday communication tools faces substantial ethical and social hurdles. Ultimately, the text advocates for proactive user management of privacy settings as a necessary response to these risks. By providing specific methods to silence or delete AI interactions, it empowers individuals to mitigate unwanted data collection. This approach reinforces the principle that technology should serve users securely, highlighting the relevance of open data principles in fostering trust and ensuring that technological advancements do not come at the cost of fundamental privacy rights.
