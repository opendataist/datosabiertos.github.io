Estados Unidos limitó el acceso de China y Rusia a los datos sensibles de los estadounidenses

The United States government is implementing a significant executive order to prevent foreign adversaries, particularly China and Russia, from accessing sensitive personal data of American citizens. This directive aims to block transactions involving health, financial, genetic, and behavioral information, recognizing that such data could be exploited for surveillance, blackmail, or military intelligence. By restricting these cross-border data flows, the administration seeks to safeguard national security against threats generated by foreign artificial intelligence systems training on American information. The policy specifically targets the extraction of genomic data and profiles of political figures, journalists, and marginalized communities, viewing these as high-risk categories for national security threats. It empowers the Attorney General to impose strict penalties on transnational data transactions that endanger citizens, while simultaneously encouraging cloud service providers to disclose when foreign AI entities utilize domestic infrastructure. This move reflects a broader strategic shift toward limiting technological dependency on rival nations while attempting to balance security concerns with the operational needs of major technology companies. This development is highly relevant to open data discussions as it illustrates the growing tension between data accessibility and data sovereignty. While open data advocates promote the free flow of information for innovation and transparency, this policy highlights how governments are increasingly prioritizing security and privacy over unrestricted data sharing. It underscores a critical global trend where the openness of datasets is being weighed against potential geopolitical risks, forcing a re-evaluation of how public data can be shared responsibly without compromising individual safety or national interests in an era of advanced AI and surveillance capabilities.
