Presenta Inai controversia por nombramiento de comisionados

The National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) has filed a constitutional controversy before the Supreme Court due to the Executive Branch’s refusal to formalize Senate-appointed commissioners. This omission threatens the integrity of the institution by preventing it from achieving the minimum quorum required to hold valid sessions. Without a fully constituted governing body, the INAI risks becoming legally paralyzed, directly undermining its ability to function as an autonomous check on state power and maintain the system of checks and balances established by the Constitution. This procedural blockage has significant implications for democratic governance and civil liberties. If the INAI cannot operate, citizens are deprived of their fundamental right to access public information and have their personal data protected. The paralysis of this key oversight body not only weakens institutional design but also creates a vacuum in accountability, affecting the broader democratic framework that relies on transparent governance and the enforcement of human rights guaranteed by the Mexican Constitution. This situation is crucial to open data advocacy because it highlights the vulnerability of institutional mechanisms that ensure data accessibility. It demonstrates that technical access to information is inseparable from the political and legal health of the institutions managing it. The case underscores the necessity of defending the autonomy of oversight bodies to guarantee that open data principles are not merely theoretical but are actively enforced by fully operational, legitimate authorities capable of protecting citizens' rights against opacity and data misuse.
