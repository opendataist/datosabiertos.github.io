Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Argentina’s Ministry of Justice has implemented significant reforms to modernize the National Vehicle Registry, aiming to drastically reduce bureaucratic burdens and financial costs for citizens. These changes allow citizens to freely choose their registration jurisdiction and introduce a Unified Digital File, which streamlines processes by eliminating the need for physical document transfers. This digital shift enhances operational efficiency and significantly lowers the risk of document loss or theft associated with traditional mail services. The measures also establish new fee structures that lower transfer costs and eliminate charges for essential documents like titles and plates, while capping profits for registry administrators. By removing territorial divisions and enabling remote, standardized procedures, the government seeks to dismantle an archaic system. These adjustments not only simplify the buying and selling of vehicles but also ensure that services are more accessible and equitable for all users across the country. This initiative is highly relevant to open data principles as it promotes the digitalization of public records and the transition from physical to electronic documentation. By creating a unified, accessible, and transparent digital infrastructure, the state facilitates greater data interoperability and public accessibility. Such reforms reduce opacity in government transactions, fostering a more open environment where information is standardized and readily available, thereby improving accountability and user experience in public administrative processes.
