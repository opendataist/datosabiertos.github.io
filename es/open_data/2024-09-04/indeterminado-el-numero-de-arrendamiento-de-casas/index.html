Indeterminado el número de arrendamiento de casas

The article highlights a significant imbalance in Mexico’s housing policy, which heavily prioritizes homeownership credit over rental markets, leaving lower-income families struggling to afford mortgages despite support programs. This structural bias ignores the reality that renting is often the only viable option for those who cannot sustain long-term debt, creating a barrier to adequate housing access for vulnerable populations. Data reveals a substantial informal rental sector and a lack of precise statistics on rented properties, as current censuses capture only a fraction of the actual market. This opacity prevents a clear understanding of housing dynamics, leaving the needs of renters largely unmeasured and unsupported by official policy frameworks that focus almost exclusively on ownership financing. This case is crucial for open data because it exposes critical gaps in statistical coverage regarding informal economic activities and rental housing. Improving the transparency and granularity of housing data would allow for better policy design, ensuring that statistical records reflect the full spectrum of housing solutions rather than just formal ownership, thereby supporting more inclusive urban planning and resource allocation.
