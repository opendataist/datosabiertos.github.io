Se generaron 206 mil empleos de abril a junio

The labor market expansion driven by a surge in workforce participation masks rising unemployment, highlighting a complex disconnect between job creation and actual employment stability. Although both formal and informal sectors added significant roles, the influx of new job seekers outpaced these gains, leading to a higher unemployment rate. This dynamic underscores the necessity for open data systems to capture not just static employment figures, but the fluid movements of labor supply and demand to accurately reflect economic health. Disaggregated data reveals that formal sector growth was broader than traditional social security records suggest, while informal employment remains a dominant force in the economy. The contrast between thriving sectors like commerce and construction, versus shrinking areas such as government and extractive industries, provides critical insights into structural economic shifts. Transparent access to this granular breakdown allows policymakers and researchers to identify specific areas of vulnerability and growth, moving beyond aggregate statistics to understand the true composition of the labor market. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how detailed, accessible statistics enable a nuanced understanding of socioeconomic trends. By making diverse datasets regarding formal and informal sectors available, society can better analyze the real impact of labor policies and economic events. Open data fosters accountability and deeper analysis, ensuring that the narrative behind the numbers is fully understood rather than relying on simplified summaries that may obscure underlying disparities.
