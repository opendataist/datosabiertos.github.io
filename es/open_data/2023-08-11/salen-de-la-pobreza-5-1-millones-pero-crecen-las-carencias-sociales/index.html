Salen de la pobreza 5.1 millones, pero crecen las carencias sociales

Between 2018 and 2022, Mexico reported a decline in overall multidimensional poverty, lifting millions from this specific status. However, this aggregate improvement masks a concerning rise in social deprivations, particularly regarding access to healthcare and education. The data reveals a paradox where headline poverty metrics drop while the underlying quality of life indicators worsen for a significant portion of the population. Simultaneously, the number of people living in extreme poverty actually increased during this period. This trend highlights a growing divide within the poorest sectors of society, with certain states experiencing disproportionately high rates of deprivation. The structural gaps in essential services like health and schooling have widened, indicating that while fewer people are classified as poor, those remaining face more severe and entrenched social exclusions. This article is crucial for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the necessity of granular, multidimensional datasets. Relying solely on aggregated income-based metrics can obscure critical societal shifts. Transparent access to detailed social indicator data allows for a more accurate understanding of reality, enabling policymakers and citizens to address the specific deficits in health and education that pure poverty reduction statistics might otherwise hide.
