Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Heart disease emerges as the leading cause of mortality across Mexico and Nuevo León for the first half of 2022, significantly surpassing diabetes and malignant tumors. This preliminary data, compiled from civil registry and forensic services, highlights a consistent pattern where men represent the majority of fatalities in both national and regional contexts. The findings underscore the critical public health burden of cardiovascular conditions and non-communicable diseases, signaling an urgent need for targeted prevention strategies rather than focusing solely on infectious disease impacts. The article emphasizes that these statistics are preliminary, derived from administrative records like death certificates and police logs, with definitive figures requiring later cross-referencing with health authorities. This distinction is vital for understanding data integrity, as final counts will adjust for specific categories like maternal deaths or suspected homicides. By acknowledging the limitations of early data, the report illustrates the complex lifecycle of statistical production, where initial estimates provide immediate insight while rigorous validation ensures long-term accuracy and comparability. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the transparency of government statistical processes and the public accessibility of mortality metrics. It showcases how raw administrative data is transformed into actionable societal insights, allowing citizens and researchers to monitor health trends over time. Furthermore, the distinction between preliminary and definitive datasets educates users on data maturity levels, encouraging informed interpretation of public statistics and supporting evidence-based policy-making in public health administration.
