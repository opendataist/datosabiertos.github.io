Extorsión en aumento mantiene presión sobre empresas mexicanas, denuncia COPARMEX

The article highlights that extortion has become a structural criminal threat in Mexico, severely hindering business activity and formal employment. Despite recent federal legal reforms and a national strategy aimed at combating the crime, open data reveals a significant rise in victims, particularly in key industrial and border states. This persistence suggests that current measures are insufficient to contain the expanding networks of extortion, which now function as a mechanism of economic and political control over various territories. The disparity in crime rates across different regions underscores the critical importance of localized data analysis and targeted policy implementation. With a majority of victims concentrated in specific states, the effectiveness of local security strategies proves decisive. The lack of uniform protection allows the crime to flourish in areas where institutional presence is weak, creating a fragmented security landscape that undermines national efforts to standardize justice and prosecution protocols. This situation is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible information on criminal incidence is essential for diagnosing systemic failures and guiding public policy. By making victimization data publicly available, organizations can verify official narratives, identify "black figures" (unreported crimes), and demand accountability. Open data serves as a vital tool for civil society to pressure governments for effective, evidence-based solutions rather than relying solely on political declarations or aggregated statistics that may obscure regional realities.
