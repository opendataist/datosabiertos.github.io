El juez Peinado niega a Begoña la anonimización de sus cuentas bancarias

The judge denies the wife of the Spanish Prime Minister’s request to anonymize personal data not directly linked to the investigation. He argues that early anonymity is impossible because unknown information might later become crucial for criminal proceedings or implicate third parties. This stance highlights the tension between privacy protection and the integrity of judicial inquiries. Authorities prioritize retaining raw data to ensure no evidence is inadvertently lost, deeming the request for immediate redaction as too vague and premature given the ongoing nature of the case. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the real-world conflict between transparency and legal security. It underscores how strict procedural requirements can delay or prevent the immediate release of information, challenging efforts to maintain open, accessible datasets in the context of high-profile political investigations.
