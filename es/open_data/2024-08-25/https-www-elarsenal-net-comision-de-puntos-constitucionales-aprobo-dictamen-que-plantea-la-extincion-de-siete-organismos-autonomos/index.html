Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Mexico’s Congress approved constitutional reforms to extinguish seven autonomous bodies, including the INAI, which serves as the primary guardian of citizens' rights to access public information and protect personal data. By transferring these functions to central government agencies or the national statistics institute, the reform effectively removes the independent oversight mechanism that currently ensures transparency. This structural change centralizes authority within the executive branch, eliminating the institutional checks and balances that previously required government entities to justify their disclosure of information. The legislative shift aims to consolidate control over telecommunications, energy regulation, and social development metrics under federal dependencies, citing economic savings and efficiency as primary justifications. However, this consolidation raises significant concerns regarding the potential for state monopolization of critical sectors and the erosion of specialized regulatory expertise. The removal of these entities disrupts the established framework where technical autonomy ensured unbiased evaluation of public policies and fair market competition, potentially aligning regulatory outcomes more closely with current political interests. This development is critically relevant to open data because it undermines the foundational guarantee of information autonomy. Open data ecosystems rely on independent institutions to enforce transparency standards and protect citizens' rights against arbitrary withholding of information. By replacing these autonomous safeguards with internal government controls, the reform risks reversing decades of progress in institutional accountability, potentially leading to reduced public access to reliable, unbiased data and weakening the transparency infrastructure essential for an informed society.
