En 2023, la pobreza aumentó al 41,7% afecta a 19,4 millones de personas y en el NEA es del 48,4%

The article reports that extreme poverty and indigency rates in Argentina rose significantly in late 2023, with children and vulnerable regions being the most affected. However, the author argues that this official data presents an outdated social picture because it does yet not capture the severe economic recession that began in December. Consequently, the statistics likely underestimate the current humanitarian crisis, as they fail to reflect the immediate impact of inflation and income collapse on household welfare. Independent analyses indicate that poverty surged well beyond the official figures in the subsequent months, reaching levels not seen in two decades. These estimates suggest that nearly twenty percent of the population now lives in extreme destitution, highlighting a rapid deterioration in living standards. The discrepancy between official reports and real-time observations underscores the critical lag in statistical methodologies when addressing fast-moving socio-economic shocks. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the limitations of delayed official statistics during times of crisis. It emphasizes the need for timely, transparent, and frequently updated data sources to accurately reflect real-time societal conditions. Furthermore, it demonstrates the value of independent verification and the importance of making raw data available so that civil society can assess the actual impact of economic policies on vulnerable populations.
