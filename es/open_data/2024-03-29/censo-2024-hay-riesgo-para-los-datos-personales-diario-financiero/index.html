Censo 2024: ¿Hay riesgo para los datos personales? | Diario Financiero

Chile’s personal data protection framework explicitly distinguishes between private and public sector regulations. While private entities generally rely on consent, public services like the National Statistics Institute are bound strictly by legal mandates for data collection. Census data processing is justified by this legal obligation, ensuring statistical integrity through rigorous confidentiality and anonymity protocols. Consequently, challenging the census’s constitutionality lacks legal basis, as the law prioritizes statistical utility and public interest over individual privacy expectations during this specific process. The treatment of census information involves irreversible anonymization, transforming personal identifiers into non-identifiable statistical data. This process ensures that while names facilitate verification, the final data protects citizen privacy through secret obligations inherent to the law. Even potential security breaches do not inherently delegitimize this treatment, provided the anonymization process remains robust and compliant with statutory requirements for statistical secrecy. However, the article highlights a critical inconsistency regarding open data. Despite legal protections for census privacy, related personal information has been declared publicly accessible through transparency rulings and subsequent legislation. This creates a paradox where sensitive data becomes commercially available for profit, undermining the intended protective framework. This relevance to open data lies in the tension between transparency mandates and fundamental privacy rights, illustrating how poorly aligned regulations can compromise the ethical handling of public sector information.
