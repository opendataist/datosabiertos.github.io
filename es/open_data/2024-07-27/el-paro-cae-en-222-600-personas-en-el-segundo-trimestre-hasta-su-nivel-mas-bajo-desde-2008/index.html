El paro cae en 222.600 personas en el segundo trimestre hasta su nivel más bajo desde 2008

The recent employment data reveals a historic improvement in labor market stability, marked by the lowest unemployment rate since 2008 and a record number of jobs created. This widespread growth across nearly all sectors and regions indicates strong economic confidence, driven primarily by the private sector. Notably, the surge in permanent contracts and full-time positions suggests a structural shift toward more secure employment, reducing precarious work conditions. Household dynamics have improved significantly, with the number of families experiencing total unemployment at a historical minimum and those with all members employed at a peak. This positive trend reflects broader economic health, as reduced joblessness among youth and increased hours worked contribute to better living standards. The simultaneous rise in both national and foreign employment highlights an inclusive recovery, reinforcing the notion that workers trust the labor market's current dynamism. This article is relevant to open_data because it exemplifies the value of transparent, standardized statistical releases from national institutes. By making detailed breakdowns by sector, gender, and nationality publicly accessible, it allows researchers and policymakers to analyze complex socio-economic patterns. Open access to such granular data enables independent verification of economic narratives and fosters evidence-based discussions on labor policies, ensuring that transparency drives accountability and informed decision-making.
