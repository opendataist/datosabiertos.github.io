Cuál metodología utiliza el INE para calcular el IPC y las canastas básicas

The article explains how macroeconomic indicators, specifically the Consumer Price Index and the Basic Food Basket, are calculated to inform public policy and measure the cost of living. By analyzing household spending patterns through national surveys, statistical agencies determine the specific goods and services that constitute these baskets, adjusting for cultural habits, nutritional requirements, and regional differences between urban and rural areas. This approach ensures that economic metrics reflect actual consumption behaviors rather than just normative nutritional standards. The methodology involves extensive data collection from thousands of sources to track price fluctuations across various categories, from food to transportation. These prices are weighted according to their importance in an average family’s budget, allowing for the calculation of inflation rates and the minimum income needed to sustain a household. The use of a "positive" approach, based on observed expenditure rather than ideal diets, incorporates even low-nutritional-value items if they are commonly consumed, thereby providing a realistic view of economic pressure on citizens. This information is crucial for open data because it highlights the transparency and complexity required in public statistical processes. Understanding the composition and calculation of these indices allows researchers and citizens to verify government claims, analyze poverty lines accurately, and hold institutions accountable. Open access to the underlying data structures and weighting methods empowers users to replicate findings or identify biases, fostering trust in official statistics and supporting evidence-based decision-making in social and economic sectors.
