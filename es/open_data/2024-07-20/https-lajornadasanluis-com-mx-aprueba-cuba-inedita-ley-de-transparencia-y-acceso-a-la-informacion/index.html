Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Cuba has enacted a groundbreaking transparency law, marking the first time the nation establishes legal obligations for state organs to ensure public access to information. This legislative milestone signifies a decisive shift toward institutionalizing transparency in public management, aiming to foster a culture where government actions are subject to popular control and oversight. By mandating openness, the state seeks to align its administrative practices with international commitments and sustainable development goals, demonstrating a political will to modernize governance structures. The core implication of this regulation is its dual focus on accountability and security. While it aims to reduce corruption and create efficient institutions responsive to citizen needs, it simultaneously defines strict exceptions involving national security, personal data, and commercial confidentiality. Citizens are held responsible for the appropriate use of disclosed information, facing potential liabilities for misuse. This framework balances the right to know with the protection of sensitive interests, reflecting a nuanced approach to data governance that respects both public interest and individual rights. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates as it establishes a formal legal framework for public information access in a previously opaque context. For open data communities, it represents a critical opportunity to engage with state agencies regarding the practical implementation of transparency. Although the law introduces safeguards, its existence creates a precedent for accountability in Latin America, where most neighboring countries already possess similar legislation. It invites scrutiny on how open data principles will be applied within these legal constraints to maximize public value.
