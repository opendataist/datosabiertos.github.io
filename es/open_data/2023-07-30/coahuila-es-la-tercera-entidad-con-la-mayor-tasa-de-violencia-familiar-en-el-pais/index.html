Coahuila es la tercera entidad con la mayor tasa de violencia familiar en el país

Coahuila ranks among the top entities in Mexico for family violence incidence relative to its population, highlighting a critical public health and human rights issue. While absolute numbers place the state eighth nationally, its high rate per capita indicates a significantly greater likelihood of residents experiencing and reporting these crimes compared to other regions. This statistic underscores a localized intensity of violence that demands targeted policy interventions rather than general national comparisons. The data reveals a gendered pattern, with women comprising the vast majority of victims. The persistence of high reporting rates, alongside thousands of investigation files opened, suggests that formal mechanisms for documenting abuse remain active despite a slight year-over-year decrease in incidents. However, the sheer volume of recorded cases and emergency calls points to an entrenched social problem requiring sustained preventive measures and legal accountability. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how standardized, government-published statistics enable civil society to identify geographic disparities and advocate for justice. By making incident rates transparent, open data allows for evidence-based policy making, ensuring resources are allocated to areas with the highest prevalence of violence and facilitating accountability for systemic failures in protecting vulnerable populations.
