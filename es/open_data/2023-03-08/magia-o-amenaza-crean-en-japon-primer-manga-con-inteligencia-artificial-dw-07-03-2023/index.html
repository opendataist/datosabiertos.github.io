¿Magia o amenaza? Crean en Japón primer manga con inteligencia artificial | DW | 07.03.2023

The release of the first manga entirely drawn by artificial intelligence in Japan, created by an author with no traditional artistic skills, marks a significant shift in the creative industry. This publication demonstrates that generative tools can democratize access to manga creation, allowing individuals with strong narrative ideas but limited technical drawing abilities to produce and distribute complex visual stories. The speed and efficiency of this process, completing a full book in weeks rather than months, highlight the potential for AI to lower barriers to entry for aspiring creators. However, this technological advancement sparks intense debate regarding copyright, ethics, and the future of professional employment. While some legislators and traditional artists express concern that AI tools, which often train on copyrighted data without permission, may threaten the livelihoods of young artists and devalue human creativity, legal frameworks struggle to adapt. The tension between the efficiency of automated generation and the need to protect intellectual property rights remains unresolved, creating uncertainty for both publishers and creators in the manga sector. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of transparent, lawful data sourcing in machine learning. The legal challenges facing AI developers emphasize the need for open, ethical datasets that respect copyright and authorship, rather than relying on opaque scraping of protected works. As AI becomes more integrated into creative industries, establishing clear standards for data provenance and open access becomes essential to balance innovation with the rights of original creators.
