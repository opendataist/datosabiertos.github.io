Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Argentina has officially launched the automatic exchange of financial information with the United States under the FATCA agreement, aiming to significantly enhance tax transparency. This initiative allows the Argentine government to access data on accounts held by citizens in the U.S., starting with targeted requests and expanding to mass data transfers. The implementation of this regime marks a pivotal shift in fiscal policy, moving from sporadic data collection to a structured, ongoing international cooperation framework designed to combat tax evasion and ensure compliance with global standards. To facilitate this process, the tax authority established a new reporting regime requiring financial institutions to conduct due diligence and submit detailed account information, including balances and holder identities. By mandating that banks provide data on both active and closed accounts, the state ensures comprehensive visibility into offshore assets. This structural change forces greater accountability on individuals and entities with foreign ties, reinforcing the administrative capacity to detect undeclared assets and align domestic regulations with international anti-money laundering efforts. This development is highly relevant to open data as it exemplifies how government-to-government data sharing initiatives can drive digital governance and fiscal transparency. By formalizing these exchanges, Argentina contributes to the broader ecosystem of interoperable public information, setting a precedent for how open standards can be leveraged to improve public service integrity and citizen trust. The accompanying amnesty program further illustrates the intersection of data availability and policy, showing how accessible information can be used to incentivize compliance and repatriate capital, ultimately fostering a more open and accountable financial environment.
