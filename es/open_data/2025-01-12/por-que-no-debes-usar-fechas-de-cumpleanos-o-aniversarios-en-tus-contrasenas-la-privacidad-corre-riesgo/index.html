Por qué no debes usar fechas de cumpleaños o aniversarios en tus contraseñas: la privacidad corre riesgo

Using personal dates like birthdays as passwords exposes users to significant security risks, as this information is easily accessible through social media and public records. This vulnerability is particularly dangerous during high-activity periods when cybercriminals intensify attacks, relying on brute force methods to exploit weak credentials. The article emphasizes that obvious personal data facilitates unauthorized access, leading to severe consequences such as financial theft and identity fraud. The relevance of this content to open data lies in the tension between data availability and privacy protection. Much of the information exploited in these attacks stems from publicly available or shared digital footprints, highlighting how open data practices can inadvertently compromise individual security if not managed carefully. Users must recognize that the ease of accessing personal history online directly fuels the success of phishing and credential-stuffing attacks, necessitating a shift toward more secure authentication practices. To mitigate these risks, experts recommend creating robust passwords that exclude personal details, incorporating mixed characters and phrases, and utilizing password managers alongside two-factor authentication. These measures add critical layers of security against increasingly sophisticated digital threats. Ultimately, protecting financial and private information requires vigilance and the adoption of strict hygiene habits, ensuring that the convenience of digital transactions does not come at the cost of personal safety.
