El aceite de oliva español cierra otra cosecha "raquítica", que deja el mercado con las existencias más bajas en 40 años y la posibilidad de quedarse sin stock

The recent data confirms a catastrophic olive harvest in Spain, driven by severe climate challenges such as drought and high temperatures. These conditions have drastically reduced both the volume of olives collected and their oil yield, resulting in stock levels at their lowest point in forty years. Consequently, the market faces an unprecedented risk of Spain running out of olive oil supplies for the first time in history by the end of the campaign. This scarcity has triggered a significant escalation in retail prices, as consumption remains robust despite the cost surge. While government measures like a VAT reduction aim to mitigate consumer impact, the underlying supply deficit ensures that costs will remain elevated. The sector highlights that olive oil has no viable nutritional or functional substitute, meaning demand persists even as production fails to meet basic needs, creating a tense economic environment for both producers and buyers. This situation is highly relevant to open_data initiatives, as it underscores the critical need for transparent, accessible, and timely agricultural statistics. Accurate data dissemination allows stakeholders to monitor supply chain vulnerabilities, anticipate market shocks, and advocate for evidence-based policy interventions. Enhancing data openness in this sector is essential for building resilience against climate change and ensuring food security, enabling more informed decision-making for policymakers, investors, and consumers alike.
