CEGAIP impuso 40 multas por incumplimiento a ley de Transparencia

The report from the State Commission for the Guarantee of Access to Public Information highlights a significant volume of public requests for information, underscoring the growing citizen demand for transparency. This surge in inquiries reflects the vital role of open data in fostering accountability and enabling citizens to actively engage with government authorities. Despite the high number of requests, many responses were incomplete or invalid, leading to a substantial increase in legal review procedures. Municipalities, political parties, and unions remain the most resistant subjects to complying with transparency obligations. These non-compliance issues persist as systemic weaknesses, often requiring regulatory intervention and sanctions to ensure adherence to access laws. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the practical challenges of data quality and availability. It emphasizes that transparency is not merely about publishing information but ensuring it is accessible, complete, and usable. The focus on enforcing compliance for key entities like political parties highlights the ongoing effort to maintain robust, open data ecosystems that truly serve public interest.
