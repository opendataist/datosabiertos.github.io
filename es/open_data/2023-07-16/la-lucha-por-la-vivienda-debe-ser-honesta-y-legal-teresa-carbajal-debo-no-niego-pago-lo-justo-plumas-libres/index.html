La lucha por la vivienda debe ser honesta y legal / Teresa Carbajal / Debo No Niego Pago Lo Justo - Plumas Libres

The article highlights a critical crisis in Mexico where a majority of household debt stems from mortgage loans, revealing deep structural issues in housing access. It argues that regulatory bodies must intensify oversight of credit institutions, as many families face financial ruin due to abusive lending practices rather than mere economic hardship. This situation transforms homeownership from a stable asset into a source of prolonged anxiety and legal vulnerability for millions of families. Through a specific case study, the text illustrates how vulnerable homeowners can be exploited not only by financial entities but also by unscrupulous advocacy groups. These groups often mismanage legal claims, charging excessive fees for unsuccessful lawsuits and further burdening clients who are already struggling with high monthly payments. This double exploitation demonstrates that the lack of transparency and ethical standards in both the financial and social defense sectors exacerbates the initial problem, leaving victims with increased debt and no resolution. This narrative is relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and standardized datasets regarding household debt, lending terms, and legal outcomes. Without open data on mortgage portfolios, default causes, and the efficiency of legal interventions, policymakers and citizens cannot accurately diagnose the scale of financial abuse or hold institutions accountable. Access to such information is essential for designing effective regulations and empowering consumers to make informed decisions, thereby preventing the systemic exploitation described in the article.
