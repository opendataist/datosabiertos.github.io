Piden no adelantar dictaminación

Civil society groups demand that the Mexican Congress ensure active public participation in debating the initiative to eliminate autonomous bodies like the INAI. They warn that rushing this discussion violates open parliament principles by excluding civil society and academic institutions from critical scrutiny of transparency reforms. This acceleration undermines the government’s promise to avoid fast-tracking executive initiatives, raising concerns about democratic deficits and reduced accountability. The move shifts oversight functions away from an independent institute, potentially weakening citizens' right to access information and data protection through bureaucratic absorption rather than transparent, participatory governance processes. This article is relevant to open_data because it highlights threats to the institutional independence crucial for safeguarding data rights. When transparency bodies lose autonomy, the integrity and accessibility of public data may be compromised, affecting citizens' ability to access and utilize open government data for accountability and civic engagement.
