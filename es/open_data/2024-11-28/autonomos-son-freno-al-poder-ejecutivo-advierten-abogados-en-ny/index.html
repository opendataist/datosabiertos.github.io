Autónomos son freno al Poder Ejecutivo, advierten abogados en NY

The proposed extinction of Mexico’s autonomous regulatory bodies, including the IFT, Cofece, and INAI, threatens to undermine democratic governance by centralizing technical authority under the Executive Branch. This shift risks replacing specialized, impartial regulation with political fluctuations, thereby weakening institutional independence and the separation of powers that are essential for a modern state. Such a structural change poses significant legal and economic risks, potentially violating international trade commitments like T-MEC that mandate independent antitrust authorities and transparency mechanisms. By dissolving these safeguards, the government jeopardizes fair competition and anticorruption standards, which could negatively impact Mexico’s economic development and its standing within international trade frameworks. This development is highly relevant to open data because it endangers the legal infrastructure that guarantees public access to information and protects personal data. The loss of bodies like the INAI weakens the enforcement of transparency rights, reducing citizen accountability and potentially restricting the availability and integrity of public information, which are foundational principles for a healthy open data ecosystem.
