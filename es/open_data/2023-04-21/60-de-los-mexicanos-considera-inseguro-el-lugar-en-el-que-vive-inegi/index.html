60% de los mexicanos considera inseguro el lugar en el que vive: INEGI

A significant majority of adults in major Mexican cities perceive their living environments as unsafe, revealing a persistent crisis in public security despite a slight recent decline. This widespread fear highlights a deep disconnect between statistical trends in violence and the daily reality experienced by citizens, where more than half the population lives in constant apprehension. The data underscores that insecurity is not merely an abstract statistic but a pervasive condition affecting social cohesion and individual well-being across the nation. The perception of danger is sharply gendered, with women reporting significantly higher levels of fear than men, pointing to distinct vulnerability dynamics within society. Specific cities exhibit extreme levels of perceived violence, often correlating with documented spikes in homicides, armed confrontations, and organized crime activities. These hotspots demonstrate how localized criminal violence directly fuels community trauma, creating environments where institutional authority is challenged and civic trust is eroded. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it illustrates the power of standardized, granular public surveys to reveal complex social realities. By making INEGI’s data accessible, citizens and policymakers can identify specific geographic and demographic patterns of insecurity. Open data transforms raw numbers into actionable insights, enabling targeted interventions and fostering transparency in addressing the root causes of public safety challenges.
