Creel pide a SCJN prioridad a controversia del Inai por falta de comisionados - Noticaribe

The article highlights a critical institutional standoff where Mexico’s transparency agency seeks judicial authorization to function despite missing council members. This situation underscores the fragility of governance structures, as the lack of a quorum prevents the agency from exercising its constitutional mandate effectively. This impasse raises serious concerns about democratic accountability and the right to information. When oversight bodies cannot operate, citizens lose a vital mechanism for holding power accountable, potentially leading to increased opacity and corruption within government institutions. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how political and legal bottlenecks can stall transparency initiatives. It shows that technical access to information is insufficient without functional institutions to enforce it, emphasizing the need for robust, legally protected frameworks to ensure data openness remains viable.
