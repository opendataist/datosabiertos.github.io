La AMI apoya la demanda de "The New York Times" contra Microsoft y Open AI al usar la IA sin derechos de autor

The central tension highlighted in this article is the legal and ethical conflict between major technology firms and traditional media regarding the unauthorized use of journalistic content for training artificial intelligence models. The New York Times’ lawsuit against Microsoft and OpenAI serves as a pivotal case study, illustrating how AI development currently threatens the economic sustainability of news organizations by leveraging their intellectual property without consent or compensation. This situation raises critical questions about data ownership and the foundational principles of open data ecosystems. It underscores that treating copyrighted journalistic works as mere raw material for AI training violates creators' rights and undermines the value of original content production. The article argues that responsible innovation requires mutual respect, where technological progress does not come at the expense of denying creators the benefits of their labor, thereby challenging the current norm of extracting data without reciprocal value exchange. The relevance to open data lies in the urgent need to redefine how public and proprietary information is sourced and utilized. It emphasizes that sustainable open data practices must incorporate equitable compensation models and strict adherence to copyright laws. Without such frameworks, the integrity of the information ecosystem is compromised, risking both the financial viability of media outlets and the quality of information available to society, which are essential components of a healthy open data landscape.
