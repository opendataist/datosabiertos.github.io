Fracasa votación en el Senado para elegir a Ricardo Salgado como nuevo comisionado del INAI

The Senate’s failure to appoint a new commissioner to the INAI highlights a critical governance crisis for Mexico’s transparency institution. Without the required five commissioners, the autonomous body is paralyzed and unable to fulfill its essential mandate. This impasse underscores the urgent need for political consensus to restore the agency’s operational capacity and protect citizens' rights to information. The rejection of the candidate, driven by partisan disagreements rather than merit, reveals deep political fragmentation threatening institutional stability. Opposition protests demanding transparency against the ruling party’s stance further illustrate how legislative gridlock directly impacts the enforcement of access to information laws. Such political maneuvers risk eroding public trust in democratic oversight mechanisms. This situation is highly relevant to open data because an inactive INAI cannot effectively regulate data protection or enforce transparency standards. When the oversight body is non-functional, accountability systems weaken, potentially leading to unchecked data misuse and reduced government openness. Restiring the commission is vital for maintaining the integrity of Mexico’s open data ecosystem and ensuring that access to information remains a robust, enforceable right rather than a theoretical promise.
