Un 54% de las familias de Balears habla en castellano y sólo un 27 % en catalán

Recent analysis of 2021 census data reveals a decisive linguistic shift in the Balearic Islands, where Spanish has become the predominant language in almost all social contexts. This dominance is not merely a cultural choice but is heavily influenced by demographic changes, particularly the influx of new residents who adopt Spanish as their primary medium of communication across family, friendship, and professional networks. The data highlights a significant decline in the consistent use of Catalan, with substantial portions of the population reporting that they never use it in daily interactions. Concurrently, the linguistic landscape for foreign languages has transformed; Arabic has displaced German as the most spoken foreign language, reflecting evolving immigration patterns from Latin America and North Africa rather than traditional European tourism demographics. This article is relevant to open data as it demonstrates how granular demographic statistics can explain complex sociolinguistic trends. It underscores the importance of accessible, systematic data publication for understanding real-time societal shifts, allowing researchers to move beyond static census figures to analyze the dynamic impact of migration on local culture and language policy.
