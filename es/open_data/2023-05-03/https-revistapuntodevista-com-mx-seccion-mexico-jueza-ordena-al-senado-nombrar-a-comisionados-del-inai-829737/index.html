The court orders the Senate to appoint INAI commissioners, highlighting the critical link between judicial enforcement and institutional integrity. This ensures the transparency body can function legally, safeguarding citizens' right to access information. The ruling prevents the Institute from losing its quorum, a prerequisite for valid decision-making. By mandating these appointments, the judiciary protects INAI’s operational capacity against political delays and proposed structural dismemberment. This case is vital to open data as it demonstrates that transparency frameworks require independent, fully staffed institutions. Without legal compliance to fill these seats, public accountability mechanisms risk paralysis, undermining data availability and government trust.
