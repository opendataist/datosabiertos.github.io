Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The Tamaulipas Institute of Transparency faces imminent dissolution next year, yet persists in safeguarding public access to information by seeking state funding. Its leadership advocates for dialogue to highlight the critical value of maintaining an autonomous body that ensures governmental accountability and transparency in the region. This struggle mirrors the recent elimination of Mexico’s national transparency institute, underscoring a broader systemic threat to open data frameworks. By continuing to process complaints and appeals, the local institute demonstrates the operational necessity of independent oversight in preventing administrative opacity and protecting citizens' rights to know. This situation is highly relevant to open data as it illustrates the fragility of institutional guarantees without robust legislative support. It serves as a warning that the physical existence of transparency organs is essential for enforcing data access norms, suggesting that open data ecosystems require stable, protected structures to function effectively against political pressure.
