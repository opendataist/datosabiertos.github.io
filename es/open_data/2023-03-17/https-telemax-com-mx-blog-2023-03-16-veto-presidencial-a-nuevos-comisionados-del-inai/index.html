The Mexican president vetoed INAI commissioners approved by the Senate, arguing the selections resulted from political horse-trading between governing and opposition parties rather than merit. This action challenges the independence of transparency institutions by prioritizing partisan alignment over ethical selection processes. The decision undermines the credibility of public oversight bodies, suggesting that access to information and data protection are susceptible to political manipulation. This erodes trust in democratic safeguards and sets a concerning precedent for how independent agencies should operate without external interference. This case is crucial for open data advocates as it highlights the vulnerability of data governance to political agendas. Protecting the autonomy of institutions that manage public information is essential to ensuring transparency, accountability, and the integrity of democratic processes remain uncompromised.
