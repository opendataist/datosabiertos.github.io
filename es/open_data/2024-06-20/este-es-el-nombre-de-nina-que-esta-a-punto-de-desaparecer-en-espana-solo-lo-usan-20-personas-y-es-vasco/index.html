Este es el nombre de niña que está a punto de desaparecer en España: solo lo usan 20 personas y es vasco

The article reveals that no unique names exist in Spain, highlighting a demographic shift where rare first names are effectively endangered. These uncommon names are almost exclusively held by an elderly population, with an average age exceeding eighty years. This trend underscores the rapid evolution of naming conventions, illustrating how cultural shifts are erasing linguistic diversity within personal identification, as fewer younger generations adopt these traditional or obscure monikers. Conversely, the data emphasizes the homogenization of modern naming practices. A small set of traditional names dominates the population, while contemporary parents heavily favor a limited selection of trendy options for newborns. This concentration of popularity suggests a move toward social conformity in identity markers, reducing the variety of names registered in official records and contrasting sharply with the scattered, aging distribution of less common names. This information is critically relevant to open data initiatives because it relies on the Registro Civil, a public database containing sensitive personal information. The analysis demonstrates how government-held data can be anonymized and aggregated to reveal significant sociological trends without compromising individual privacy. It showcases the value of transparent, accessible public records in understanding demographic changes and legal frameworks governing civil status, proving that open data can drive meaningful cultural insights when properly governed.
