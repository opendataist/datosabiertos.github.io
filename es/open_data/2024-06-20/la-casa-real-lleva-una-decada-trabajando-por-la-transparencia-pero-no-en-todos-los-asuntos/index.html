La Casa Real lleva una década trabajando por la "transparencia"... pero no en todos los asuntos

The Spanish Royal Household has significantly improved its transparency by publishing financial details and undergoing audits, fulfilling early promises of integrity. Yet, gaps remain regarding total expenditure, security costs, and inherited assets, highlighting that full accountability is still a work in progress. This case illustrates the persistent tension between institutional opacity and public demand for openness. For open data advocates, it underscores the necessity of granular, accessible financial data to ensure true oversight. Ultimately, the monarchy’s efforts demonstrate that while structural reforms aid trust, comprehensive data release is essential. The incomplete disclosure of spending serves as a reminder that partial transparency is insufficient for genuine democratic accountability.
