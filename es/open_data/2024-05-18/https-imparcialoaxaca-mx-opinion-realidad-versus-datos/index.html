Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article highlights a stark disconnect between official government inflation statistics and the tangible economic reality experienced by Mexican citizens. While institutions like INEGI report a specific cumulative inflation rate, merchants and families observe price increases for basic goods that far exceed these figures. This discrepancy suggests that official data fails to capture the true magnitude of the cost-of-living crisis, particularly regarding essential items like food and medicine, leading to widespread dissatisfaction among the population who feel that wage and social program increases do not adequately offset rising prices. This narrative underscores a critical issue regarding the reliability and transparency of public data. When official metrics significantly underreport actual economic pressure, it erodes trust in governmental institutions and obscures the real impact of fiscal policies. The article argues that such discrepancies are not merely statistical errors but potentially political tools used to mask the negative effects of populist measures, such as decreed minimum wage hikes, which appear insufficient against the backdrop of uncontrolled retail price surges. The relevance to open data lies in the urgent need for verifiable, transparent, and accessible information that reflects ground-level realities. For citizens, educators, and policymakers to make informed decisions, data sources must be accurate and comprehensible. Without reliable open data that aligns with empirical evidence, it becomes impossible to hold governments accountable for economic outcomes or to implement effective social policies, highlighting the danger of information asymmetry in democratic societies.
