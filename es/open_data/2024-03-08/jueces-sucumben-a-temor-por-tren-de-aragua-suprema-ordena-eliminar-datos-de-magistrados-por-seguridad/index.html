Jueces sucumben a temor por Tren de Aragua: Suprema ordena eliminar datos de magistrados por seguridad

The Chilean Supreme Court removed judges’ personal data from its website during a trial against Los Gallegos, citing risks to judicial independence from organized crime. This preventive measure prioritizes magistrates’ safety over routine transparency when facing severe threats from criminal organizations. This action is relevant to open data as it illustrates the tension between public access to information and the necessity of protecting sensitive personal details. It establishes a precedent where privacy concerns can temporarily override data disclosure, reflecting how open data principles must adapt to real-world security risks. Such cases demonstrate that transparency policies require flexible frameworks. Balancing accountability with safety ensures that open data initiatives do not inadvertently endanger participants, highlighting the need for context-sensitive data governance in high-stakes judicial proceedings.
