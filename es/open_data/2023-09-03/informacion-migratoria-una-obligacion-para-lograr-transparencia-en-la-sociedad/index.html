Información migratoria: una obligación para lograr transparencia en la sociedad

The article highlights that despite institutional efforts, Colombia lacks timely, granular, and accessible statistical data on migration, undermining effective public policy planning. Although entities like the DANE and DNP have initiated studies, their outputs are often delayed, incomplete, or insufficiently specific, while other agencies have ceased publication or sharing of critical information. This gap hinders accurate diagnosis of migratory flows and complicates the design of integration strategies for both local governments and national actors. The core issue lies in the insufficiency and instability of current data sources. While surveys like the GEIH and Pulso de la Migración provide a baseline, they require greater territorial specificity and long-term consistency to be truly useful. The article emphasizes that transparency and rigorous, updated statistics are fundamental obligations for a democratic society and essential for coordinating responses to complex migratory phenomena, particularly regarding Venezuelan residents. This is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how the absence of accessible, standardized, and regularly updated datasets directly impacts social equity and administrative efficiency. By calling for specialized, territorially detailed, and consistently available migration data, the article advocates for robust open data frameworks that empower civil society, researchers, and policymakers to make informed decisions based on evidence rather than estimates.
