ES EN

The article highlights that Mexico’s mortality figures for the first nine months of 2022, while lower than the pandemic peaks of 2020 and 2021, remain among the highest levels recorded since 1990. This indicates that despite a decline in the death rate compared to the previous year, the country continues to face a significant public health burden, with cardiovascular diseases replacing COVID-19 as the leading cause of death. The data reveals a persistent excess mortality rate, particularly affecting men and older adults, signaling that underlying health vulnerabilities and systemic issues have not been fully resolved. Understanding these demographic and epidemiological trends is crucial for open data initiatives, as it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and timely statistical information. High-quality open data allows researchers, policymakers, and civil society to monitor health outcomes accurately, identify disparities across regions, and evaluate the effectiveness of healthcare interventions. When such data is publicly available, it fosters accountability and enables evidence-based decision-making that can address the root causes of elevated mortality rates. Ultimately, the relevance of this report to open data lies in its demonstration of how comprehensive registries, derived from administrative records and civil status documents, provide a clearer picture of population health. By making these granular details accessible, open data platforms empower stakeholders to analyze complex health patterns, such as gender and age-specific mortality risks. This transparency is essential for driving social impact and improving public health strategies in the long term.
