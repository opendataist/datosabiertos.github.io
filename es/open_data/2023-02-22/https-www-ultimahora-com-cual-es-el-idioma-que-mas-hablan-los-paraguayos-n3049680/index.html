The article reveals Paraguay’s high bilingualism, where a significant portion of the population primarily uses both Guarani and Spanish at home. This data highlights the nation’s unique linguistic landscape, challenging monolingual assumptions and showcasing the coexistence of indigenous and colonial languages in daily life. This information is crucial for open data initiatives as it provides granular, reliable insights into cultural demographics. By making such statistics accessible, developers and researchers can create more inclusive digital tools and services that reflect the true multilingual reality of the user base. Understanding language usage patterns helps improve data interpretation and service design for diverse communities. It emphasizes the importance of accurate metadata and representation in public datasets, ensuring that open data efforts support and preserve linguistic diversity rather than marginalizing non-dominant languages.
