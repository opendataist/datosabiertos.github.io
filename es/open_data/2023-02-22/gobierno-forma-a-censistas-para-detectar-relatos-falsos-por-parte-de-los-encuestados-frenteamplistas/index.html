Gobierno forma a censistas para detectar relatos falsos por parte de los encuestados frenteamplistas

The article alleges that Uruguay’s census training includes political bias, instructing agents to detect and reject "false" narratives likely to criticize the current government. It claims officials fear citizens will misrepresent socio-economic conditions due to partisan indoctrination, suggesting specific cultural markers like mate preparation as identification tools. This content is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how state-sponsored data collection can be compromised by political ideology. When enumeration processes are manipulated to suppress dissenting views, the resulting datasets lose objectivity, fundamentally undermining the integrity, transparency, and utility of public statistical resources. Such interference erodes trust in official statistics, making it difficult to rely on census data for policy-making or academic research. It highlights the urgent need for strict neutrality in data gathering protocols to ensure that open data remains a truthful reflection of societal realities rather than a tool for political narrative control.
