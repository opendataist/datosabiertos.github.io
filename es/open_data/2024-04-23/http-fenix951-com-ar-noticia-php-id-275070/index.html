Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The global military expenditure reached historic highs in 2023, driven primarily by geopolitical instability and a global retreat from peace. This surge, led by the United States, China, and Russia, signifies a significant shift toward militarization as nations prioritize defense capabilities over diplomatic conflict resolution. The trend highlights a concerning spiral of armament that threatens further geopolitical destabilization, with NATO countries significantly increasing their budgets in response to the war in Ukraine. Regional conflicts have profoundly influenced spending patterns, particularly in the Middle East and Asia-Oceania, where tensions have triggered notable budget hikes. Additionally, internal security crises, such as rising criminal violence in Central America, have forced governments to deploy military forces domestically. These diverse drivers illustrate that modern defense spending is no longer limited to traditional state-on-state conflicts but increasingly encompasses complex hybrid threats and internal security challenges. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent, accessible, and standardized global defense datasets. Organizations like SIPRI demonstrate how aggregating and releasing detailed financial intelligence allows researchers, policymakers, and citizens to monitor arms races and accountability. Without open access to such granular data, understanding the true scale of militarization and its impact on international stability remains opaque, hindering evidence-based decision-making and civic oversight.
