La deuda pública suma casi 20.000 millones en febrero y supera por primera vez los 1,6 billones

Spanish public debt has reached a historic peak, driven by the compounding economic pressures of the pandemic, geopolitical conflicts, and inflation. While the total amount exceeds previous records, the debt-to-GDP ratio has shown signs of moderation due to economic growth. The government projects a continued downward trend in this ratio over the next few years, suggesting that despite rising nominal debt, the economy's expansion is helping to stabilize the structural deficit. The burden is unevenly distributed across different levels of administration. The central government and autonomous communities have seen significant increases in their borrowing, whereas municipal debt has slightly decreased. Interestingly, Social Security debt remained stable recently, primarily because state loans were used to cover its budgetary deficits rather than new external borrowing. This internal financing mechanism highlights complex fiscal dependencies within the public sector's accounting structure. This information is crucial for open data enthusiasts and analysts because it illustrates the importance of accessing granular, administrative-level financial data rather than relying solely on aggregated national figures. The dataset reveals how different entities contribute to the total, including the technical adjustment for double-counted liabilities between regions and the state. Such detailed transparency allows for a more accurate assessment of fiscal health, accountability, and the true impact of external crises on specific public institutions.
