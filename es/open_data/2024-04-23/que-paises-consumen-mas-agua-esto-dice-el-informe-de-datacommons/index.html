¿Qué países consumen más agua? Esto dice el informe de Datacommons

The article highlights a critical global water crisis driven by exponential population growth and unsustainable consumption patterns, particularly in Latin America. It reveals that countries like Colombia and Peru have alarming per capita water usage rates, exceeding those of Mexico despite significant regional water availability. This disparity underscores a urgent need for better resource management, as high consumption combined with environmental pressures creates substantial vulnerability to droughts across the region. This narrative is vital for open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible datasets from sources like OECD and WRI are essential for identifying these complex socio-environmental trends. Open data allows researchers and policymakers to visualize the stark contrast between resource abundance and inefficient usage, facilitating evidence-based decision-making. Without such clear statistical transparency, the scale of the crisis and the specific outliers driving it might remain obscured, hindering effective global cooperation. Ultimately, the relevance lies in leveraging open data to bridge the gap between raw statistics and actionable policy. By making consumption metrics widely available, stakeholders can implement targeted conservation strategies and infrastructure improvements. This transparency fosters accountability and empowers communities to demand sustainable practices, proving that open data is not just a tool for information but a cornerstone for addressing the most pressing ecological challenges of our time.
