Los estudiantes y amanuenses también dibujaban (IV)

The article highlights two distinct cultural artifacts from 18th and 19th-century Navarre: a rare manuscript illustration of bulls fighting dogs and ornamental drawings in a Benedictine convent’s ledger. These examples reveal historical practices, such as using dogs to subdue bulls before their eventual ban, and the integration of symbolic floral and avian motifs into administrative documents to express contemporary devotions. This content is relevant to open_data initiatives because it demonstrates the value of digitizing and sharing specialized historical records. By making such diverse materials accessible, researchers and the public can uncover nuanced socio-cultural insights. Open access to these archives facilitates broader interdisciplinary studies, preserving heritage while encouraging the discovery of unexpected connections between art, administration, and animal husbandry history.
