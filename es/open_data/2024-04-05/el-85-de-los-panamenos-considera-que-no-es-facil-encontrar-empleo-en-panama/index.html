El 85% de los panameños considera que ‘no es fácil’ encontrar empleo en Panamá

The article highlights a critical disconnect between official statistical data and public sentiment in Panama, illustrating how aggregate economic indicators often fail to capture individual lived experiences. While formal unemployment rates have decreased, a significant majority of citizens perceive the job market negatively, driven by economic instability and high youth unemployment. This divergence underscores a major challenge in open data governance: the necessity of validating macro-level datasets with qualitative, perceptual information to avoid misleading policymakers and the public. The persistence of high labor informality further complicates the narrative of economic recovery, revealing that reductions in unemployment numbers do not necessarily translate into improved social conditions or job security. With informal employment surpassing formal sectors, the structural integrity of the labor market remains fragile. This dynamic suggests that relying solely on quantitative metrics is insufficient, as it masks the lack of formal contractual protections and the stagnation in the creation of stable, formal jobs since the pandemic era. This case is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it exemplifies the limitations of traditional indicators and the urgent need for multidimensional data approaches. It argues for the integration of diverse data sources, including citizen perceptions and sector-specific analyses, to provide a holistic view of societal well-being. By acknowledging territorial inequalities and the qualitative aspects of economic life, open data frameworks can better support equitable policy-making and ensure that statistical transparency leads to actionable, inclusive insights rather than just accurate numbers.
