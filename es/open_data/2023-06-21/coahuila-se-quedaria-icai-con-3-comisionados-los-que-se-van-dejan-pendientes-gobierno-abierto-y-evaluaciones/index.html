Coahuila: Se quedaría ICAI con 3 comisionados; los que se van dejan pendientes Gobierno Abierto y evaluaciones

The selection of a new commissioner for Coahuila’s Access to Information Institute carries significant weight for regional transparency. Civic leaders emphasize that the appointee must demonstrate strict impartiality and professional integrity to ensure the institution effectively safeguards the public’s right to know. This recruitment phase is critical, as it aims to restore legitimacy and autonomy to a body that serves as the primary guardian of state transparency. A pressing concern involves the Institute’s delayed and reactive approach to information dissemination. There are unresolved projects, such as the Open Government program, and outdated evaluations of public institutions’ compliance with transparency standards. Experts argue that the Institute must shift from merely responding to specific requests to proactively monitoring and verifying that government entities continuously update their mandatory public data. This article is highly relevant to open_data because it highlights the institutional mechanisms necessary for data transparency. It underscores that reliable open data requires not just legal frameworks but also proactive, autonomous oversight. Without rigorous, impartial monitoring, public institutions may fail to maintain accurate, timely, and accessible data, thereby undermining the fundamental principles of open government and civic accountability.
