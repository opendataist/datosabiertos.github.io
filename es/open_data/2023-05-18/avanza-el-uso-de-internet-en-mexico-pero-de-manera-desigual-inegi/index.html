Avanza el uso de Internet en México, pero de manera desigual: Inegi

The article highlights a significant digital divide in Mexico, where internet access is heavily skewed toward high-income households and urban centers. This inequality reveals that socioeconomic status and geography remain primary barriers to technological inclusion, leaving low-income and rural populations severely underserved. Understanding these disparities is crucial for open data initiatives, as it underscores the need to identify and address structural gaps in connectivity to ensure equitable digital participation. Furthermore, the data shows a strong preference for smartphones as the primary device for internet connection, indicating a shift in how populations interact with technology. While overall usage has grown since 2017, the concentration of access among specific demographics suggests that growth has not been uniform. For open data practitioners, this implies that mobile-first strategies are essential for reaching broader audiences, while static data collection methods may fail to capture the full extent of digital engagement in marginalized communities. Finally, the ICT labor market remains male-dominated, with significant gender gaps in both employment and income levels within the sector. This imbalance affects the diversity of perspectives in technology development and policy-making. From an open data perspective, these statistics are vital for monitoring social equity in the digital economy. They highlight the urgent need for inclusive policies that encourage female participation and fair wage distribution, ensuring that the benefits of the digital transformation are shared more broadly across society.
