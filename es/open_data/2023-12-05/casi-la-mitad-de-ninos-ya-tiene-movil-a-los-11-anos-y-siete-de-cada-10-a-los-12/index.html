Casi la mitad de niños ya tiene móvil a los 11 años y siete de cada 10, a los 12

The article highlights the rapid proliferation of smartphones among minors in Spain, using INE data to show that ownership skyrockets during the transition to secondary education. This statistical evidence underscores a significant societal shift, demonstrating that mobile devices have become ubiquitous at a surprisingly young age, with the vast majority of teenagers now possessing one. The surge in device adoption has triggered a strong parental and educational response, leading to organized movements demanding strict regulation or outright bans for children under sixteen. Families and schools are urging legislative action to address concerns over early exposure, reflecting a broader crisis of confidence regarding digital safety and the well-being of young users amidst this technological saturation. This content is relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparent, publicly available statistics can drive concrete policy debates and civic engagement. By making usage trends accessible, official data empowers citizens and educators to identify specific problem areas, such as the sharp rise in early adoption, and effectively advocate for protective measures based on empirical evidence rather than anecdote.
