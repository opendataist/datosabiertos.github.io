Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

This article highlights significant challenges in livestock management, particularly regarding animal mortality and production efficiency, which are crucial for developing robust agricultural open data systems. The high rates of loss due to disease in young cattle and theft in adults, alongside varying production practices, underscore the need for granular, standardized datasets to monitor sector health accurately. Understanding these specific pain points allows for better policy-making and resource allocation in the agricultural sector. Regarding pork production, the data reveals stark contrasts between subsistence and large-scale farming, especially in reproduction rates and mortality during lactation. The reliance on concentrated feed in larger operations versus natural grazing in cattle highlights a sector in transition. These disparities in production methods and economic destinations, from wholesale markets to industrial supply chains, demonstrate the complexity of the livestock value chain and the necessity for interoperable data standards across different farm sizes and purposes. The relevance to open data lies in the potential to transform these fragmented statistics into actionable public insights. By making detailed information on birth rates, losses, and farming systems accessible, stakeholders can identify inefficiencies and improve sustainability. This transparency fosters accountability, supports research into disease prevention and breeding optimization, and ultimately contributes to a more resilient and competitive agricultural economy through informed decision-making.
