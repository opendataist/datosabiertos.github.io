La última semana Santa Cruz incrementó en 6 mil la cantidad de agentes censales, pero falta un 60% más | El Deber

Santa Cruz faces a critical shortage of census agents, lagging significantly behind other departments in recruitment efforts. Local authorities and the National Institute of Statistics are urgently calling on municipal employees and the general public to volunteer. The goal is to ensure sufficient coverage for the upcoming national census, emphasizing that widespread participation is essential for the process to proceed effectively on the scheduled date. The article highlights a stark contrast in recruitment progress across the country, with some departments already fully staffed while others struggle. This disparity raises concerns about the operational capacity to conduct an accurate count. Stakeholders, including civic leaders and politicians, stress that the success of the census depends on mobilizing volunteers quickly to meet legal and logistical requirements, warning that delays or insufficient staff could compromise the integrity of the data collection. This situation is highly relevant to open data because a census constitutes the foundational dataset for public policy, resource allocation, and social planning. If the data collection process is rushed or incomplete due to labor shortages, the resulting statistics will be flawed. Accurate, transparent, and complete open data is crucial for fostering trust in public institutions and ensuring that government decisions are based on reality rather than gaps or errors in demographic information.
