#Datos Comunicaciones, Educación y Salud: los sectores con mayor inflación en 2023

The article highlights that while Venezuela experienced a significant overall slowdown in inflation during 2023, this aggregate statistic masks a critical disparity across essential sectors. Public utilities, particularly communications, and education saw price increases far exceeding the national average. This divergence reveals that basic services, which rely heavily on state pricing and suffer from infrastructure failures, are undergoing severe adjustments. The rapid rise in these specific costs disproportionately impacts consumers, eroding purchasing power even as headline inflation metrics suggest a period of relative stabilization. The persistence of high inflation in vital areas like health, housing, and household equipment points to deep structural economic issues, including deferred tariff adjustments and supply chain failures. These factors prevent businesses from achieving economies of scale or productivity gains, thereby sustaining elevated price levels regardless of broader macroeconomic trends. The contrast between these soaring essential costs and the more moderate inflation in sectors like food and transportation underscores a fragmented economic reality where the burden of inflation is not shared equally among all goods and services. This analysis is relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the necessity of disaggregated datasets for accurate socioeconomic assessment. Relying solely on aggregate indices like the INPC can obscure the lived realities of citizens facing steep hikes in survival necessities. Open data advocates for the publication and analysis of granular sector-specific metrics to ensure transparency, allowing policymakers and the public to identify specific pain points and advocate for targeted interventions rather than accepting generalised economic narratives that may hide severe localized distress.
