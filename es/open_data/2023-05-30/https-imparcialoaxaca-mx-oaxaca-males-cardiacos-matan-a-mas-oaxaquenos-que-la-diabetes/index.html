Health data from Oaxaca highlights a concerning divergence in mortality trends for cardiovascular diseases between genders during the first half of 2022. While male deaths decreased compared to the previous year, female fatalities saw a slight increase. Heart diseases remain the leading cause of death for both sexes, followed by diabetes, indicating that lifestyle factors such as poor diet and sedentary habits continue to drive high mortality rates despite overall improvements in male health outcomes. The article emphasizes that cardiovascular conditions are a critical public health crisis due to the significant economic and social burdens they create, including increased healthcare costs, workplace absenteeism, and reduced productivity. Authorities identify key risk factors like hypertension, obesity, and stress, suggesting that preventive measures, such as adopting healthier diets and reducing processed foods, are essential to mitigating these fatal outcomes and improving quality of life. This article is relevant to open data because it relies on structured statistics from INEGI and health secretariats to track demographic health trends. It demonstrates how open datasets can reveal gender-specific health disparities and inform public policy. By analyzing such open records, researchers can identify emerging patterns, such as the rising incidence of diabetes and hypertension in women, enabling more targeted and effective interventions for at-risk populations.
