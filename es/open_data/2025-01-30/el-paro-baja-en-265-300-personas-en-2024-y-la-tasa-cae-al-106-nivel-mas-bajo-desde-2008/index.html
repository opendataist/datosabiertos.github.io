El paro baja en 265.300 personas en 2024 y la tasa cae al 10,6%, nivel más bajo desde 2008

The Spanish labor market achieved historic milestones in 2024, marking the lowest unemployment rate since 2008 and a record number of employed individuals. This positive trend reflects a sustained four-year recovery, significantly reducing long-term unemployment and the number of households with no active members. The labor force expanded, driven primarily by substantial job growth in the services sector, which accounted for the vast majority of new positions. A key feature of this recovery is the shifting composition of employment. There was a notable increase in permanent contracts, which reached an all-time high, while temporary positions declined. This shift suggests a move toward labor market stability and quality improvement. Additionally, employment gains were broad-based, affecting both men and women, with female unemployment reaching its lowest level since 2007. Youth unemployment also dropped, contributing to a healthier overall economic profile. This data is relevant to open_data because it demonstrates how public statistical agencies, like the INE, provide transparent, high-frequency indicators that allow researchers and policymakers to track socioeconomic trends in real-time. By making such detailed labor metrics openly available, the data enables rigorous analysis of policy effectiveness, supports evidence-based decision-making for economic interventions, and fosters accountability. Open access to these indicators ensures that the public and private sectors can independently verify trends and collaborate on solutions to improve employment conditions.
