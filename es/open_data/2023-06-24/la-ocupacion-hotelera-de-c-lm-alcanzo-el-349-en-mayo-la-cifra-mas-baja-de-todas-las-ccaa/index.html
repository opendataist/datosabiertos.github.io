La ocupación hotelera de C-LM alcanzó el 34,9% en mayo, la cifra más baja de todas las CCAA

The Spanish hotel sector demonstrated a robust recovery, surpassing 2019 occupancy levels through a significant increase in overnight stays and a substantial rise in prices. This dual growth indicates that the industry is not only attracting more guests but also leveraging stronger pricing power, resulting in higher average daily revenues and improved financial performance for establishments across the country. Domestic tourism played a crucial role in this resurgence, with Spanish residents accounting for a significant portion of the surge, while international markets remained vital, particularly British and German visitors. Regional disparities highlight specific hotspots like the Balearic Islands and Barcelona, which led in both occupancy and revenue generation. This geographical concentration suggests that tourism dynamics are unevenly distributed, with certain areas experiencing much denser demand and higher economic returns than others. This article is relevant to open data because it relies on precise, granular statistical indicators from the National Statistics Institute, such as occupancy rates, revenue per available room, and visitor origin breakdowns. Understanding how these standardized metrics track economic trends allows analysts to validate data quality and consistency. Furthermore, it illustrates how public statistical bodies provide the foundational datasets necessary for researchers and policymakers to monitor sector health and make informed decisions based on empirical evidence.
