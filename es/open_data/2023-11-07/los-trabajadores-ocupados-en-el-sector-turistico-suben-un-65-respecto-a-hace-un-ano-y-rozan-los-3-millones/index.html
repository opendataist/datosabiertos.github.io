Los trabajadores ocupados en el sector turístico suben un 6,5% respecto a hace un año y rozan los 3 millones

The Spanish tourism sector demonstrates a significant structural shift toward labor stability, contributing heavily to national employment growth while improving job quality metrics. By fostering an environment that favors permanent contracts and full-time hours, the industry has successfully reduced seasonality and dependency on temporary positions. This transformation underscores a broader economic transition where tourism is no longer just a driver of volume, but a model for sustainable, high-quality employment that reinforces the sector's social value and long-term viability. Data reveals a substantial rise in permanent staff across all major sub-sectors, including hospitality and transport, alongside a notable decline in temporary roles and self-employment. This trend indicates that policy interventions aimed at de-seasonalizing tourism are effectively translating into more secure and better-remunerated jobs for workers. The consistent increase in indefinite contracts marks a historic milestone, proving that strategic focus on service quality directly correlates with improved labor conditions and greater worker security throughout the economy. This narrative is highly relevant to open_data because it illustrates how transparent, granular employment statistics can validate policy effectiveness and track socioeconomic trends. By making detailed data on contract types, regional variations, and sectoral growth publicly available, stakeholders can move beyond aggregate figures to analyze the true impact of tourism policies. Open data thus enables rigorous evaluation of whether economic strategies are genuinely delivering stability and equity, providing a transparent baseline for future decision-making and accountability in the public sector.
