La inflación se aceleró en octubre y superó el 4%

The recent rise in inflation to 4.3 percent signals a shift after five consecutive months of decline, although it remains within the central bank’s target range. This rebound is primarily driven by significant price increases in transport and housing sectors. Specifically, soaring costs for diesel, gasoline, and airfare, alongside rising rents and gas refill prices, are the key factors influencing this upward trend. The relevance to open data lies in the critical need for transparent, accessible, and granular statistical datasets. High-quality public data allows citizens and analysts to verify official figures, understand the specific drivers behind economic fluctuations, and assess policy effectiveness. Without open access to detailed indices, such as those showing variations in specific food items or transport costs, public trust and informed economic participation are compromised. Ultimately, this data underscores how open information fosters accountability in economic governance. By making these metrics freely available, governments enable better scrutiny of inflation drivers, helping the public understand real purchasing power changes. This transparency is essential for maintaining confidence in official statistics and supporting evidence-based decision-making across society.
