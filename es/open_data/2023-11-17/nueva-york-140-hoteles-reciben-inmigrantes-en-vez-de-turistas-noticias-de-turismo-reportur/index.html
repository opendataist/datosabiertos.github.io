Nueva York: 140 hoteles reciben inmigrantes en vez de turistas | Noticias de turismo REPORTUR

New York City has shifted its hotel inventory from tourism to housing immigrants, significantly reducing available rooms for travelers. This government-led consolidation creates scarcity, which operators anticipate will drive future rates upward as supply contracts and long-term leases limit near-term availability. The situation highlights the tension between regulatory mandates and market dynamics. Restrictions on alternative lodging platforms like Airbnb have already inflated hotel prices, while enforcement gaps push unregistered listings into unregulated markets, complicating transparency and oversight. This case is vital for open data because it demonstrates how official housing and occupancy metrics can diverge from visible tourism data. Understanding these hidden flows requires access to granular, transparent datasets to accurately assess the true impact of policy on market behavior and accommodation availability.
