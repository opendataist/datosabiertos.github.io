El paro baja en 7.452 personas en febrero y la Seguridad Social registra su mejor mes en 17 años con 103.000 empleos más

Spain’s labor market shows continued resilience in February, marked by a slight reduction in registered unemployment and a record surge in Social Security affiliations. The data indicates a robust recovery, with job creation leading figures not seen since 2007, driven primarily by strong growth in the hospitality and education sectors. Although youth unemployment rose slightly, the overall trend reflects a healthier economic environment, with the total unemployed dropping to its lowest level for February in over a decade. This improvement underscores a structural transformation of the labor market, characterized by a significant increase in permanent contracts and a notable rise in female employment, which reached historic highs. Political leaders attribute these gains to government employment policies, emphasizing that the quality of jobs is improving alongside quantity. The sectoral breakdown reveals that while construction, industry, and services are gaining traction, certain areas like healthcare and commerce experienced minor declines, highlighting an uneven but positive adaptation. This article is relevant to open data because it relies on the public release of granular socioeconomic indicators by state agencies like the SEPE and the Ministry of Social Security. These official datasets are essential for independent verification, academic analysis, and the development of data-driven solutions to unemployment. Transparent access to such detailed employment statistics allows researchers and citizens to monitor policy effectiveness, identify regional disparities, and understand the evolving dynamics of the modern workforce without relying solely on interpreted narratives.
