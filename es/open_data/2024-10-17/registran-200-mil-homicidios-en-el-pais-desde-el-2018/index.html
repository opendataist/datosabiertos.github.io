Registran 200 mil homicidios en el país desde el 2018

The article highlights a disturbing surge in intentional homicides under Mexico’s "Fourth Transformation," reaching historic levels with over two hundred thousand deaths recorded since 2018. This data reveals persistent, high-level violence averaging ninety-three daily fatalities, showing no significant decline despite administrative changes. Understanding this trend is crucial for open data advocates. It demonstrates how public statistics can expose critical governance failures and inform policy debates. Transparent access to such datasets enables civil society to hold authorities accountable for public safety and human rights outcomes. Ultimately, the continuity of high murder rates across administrations underscores the urgency for robust, open information systems. Reliable data empowers citizens to demand effective strategies against violence, making transparency essential for democratic oversight and meaningful social improvement in Mexico.
