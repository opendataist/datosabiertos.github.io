Inflación cayó a su nivel más bajo del año en octubre: ¿Qué precios subieron y disminuyeron?

The National Institute of Statistics and Informatics (INEI) reported a decrease in Lima’s Consumer Price Index in October 2023, driven primarily by significant reductions in food and beverage prices. This decline highlights how volatile specific agricultural markets, particularly fruits and vegetables, can disproportionately influence overall inflation metrics. The data illustrates the critical need for granular, item-level transparency, as broad index figures often mask the extreme price swings of individual commodities that directly impact household budgets. Conversely, other sectors such as transportation and services experienced price increases, partially offsetting the gains in food costs. This divergence underscores the complexity of interpreting economic indicators without disaggregated data. By revealing exactly which goods drove inflation up or down, this report enables a more nuanced understanding of economic health than aggregate percentages alone. It emphasizes that policy and consumer strategies must account for these structural differences rather than relying solely on general trends. This information is vital for the open data community because it demonstrates the value of detailed, accessible statistical reporting. When institutions release comprehensive breakdowns of price variations across hundreds of products, it empowers analysts, journalists, and citizens to verify claims, identify specific cost-of-living pressures, and hold institutions accountable. Open access to such precise data transforms raw numbers into actionable insights, fostering greater economic literacy and trust in public information systems.
