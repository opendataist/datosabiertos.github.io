Estos son los tres municipios más ricos de España: rentas de 27.000 euros por habitante

The INE’s new Atlas of Household Income Distribution highlights significant geographic disparities in Spain’s wealth, relying on tax data to reveal a stark North-South divide. This open dataset provides crucial visibility into economic inequality, demonstrating how public statistics can expose the concentration of income in specific municipalities, particularly in the North and major cities, while southern regions and smaller towns face lower purchasing power. The findings confirm that the wealthiest municipalities remain largely unchanged, with Pozuelo de Alarcón retaining the highest average income, followed by Matadepera and Boadilla del Monte. Conversely, the lowest incomes are concentrated in Andalusia and Extremadura, where municipalities like El Palmar de Troya record the lowest rents. This persistent pattern underscores that regional economic hierarchies are stable over time, offering researchers a clear longitudinal view of Spanish socioeconomic stratification through standardized public metrics. This report is vital to open data because it transforms raw tax information into accessible, granular insights that foster transparency and academic research. By making detailed income distributions publicly available, the INE enables citizens and policymakers to understand the root causes of inequality beyond simple averages. Such data-driven transparency is essential for designing effective social policies and holding institutions accountable, proving that open statistical standards are key tools for democratic oversight and informed public debate regarding economic justice.
