Escasea la lluvia

The article highlights severe agricultural losses in Mexico, particularly in Tamaulipas, where drought caused damage exceeding ninety percent. This crisis underscores the fragility of food security and the urgent need for transparent data regarding water scarcity and crop yields. Understanding these metrics is vital for open_data initiatives, as precise statistical visibility allows policymakers and citizens to address systemic vulnerabilities in resource distribution. Beyond climate factors, the report identifies high input costs, soil degradation, and insecurity as compounding challenges that hinder productivity. The data reveals a stark disparity in land ownership, where limited agricultural output stems from unequal access to resources rather than insufficient land area. This structural inequity necessitates robust, open datasets that map land tenure and support equitable policy interventions to restore balance. Ultimately, the severity of the 2022–2023 drought serves as a critical case study for the importance of accessible environmental monitoring. Reliable open data enables better forecasting and adaptation strategies, ensuring that agricultural development remains sustainable. By democratizing this information, stakeholders can drive informed decisions that protect ecosystems and support the livelihoods dependent on healthy, water-resilient farming systems.
