INE anuncia mesa nacional "técnica" para analizar el censo y califica de "políticos" los cuestionamientos

The INE director dismisses criticisms of the 2024 census results as politically motivated rather than technical, defending the data's scientific validity. By proposing a national technical table with academic authorities, the institute seeks to isolate the discussion from regional political disputes. This approach aims to validate the census through rigorous, internationally recognized protocols, reinforcing the importance of evidence-based governance over partisan narratives in public administration. Regional leaders, particularly in Santa Cruz, argue that significant population omissions indicate serious methodological failures. However, INE officials contend that demographic calculations, including births, deaths, and migration, adequately support the published figures. This tension highlights the critical challenge of maintaining public trust in official statistics when local perceptions of undercounting conflict with national aggregate data. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of transparent methodologies and accessible raw data for independent verification. When official institutions face accusations of manipulation, the ability to audit results using open, reproducible datasets becomes essential. Ultimately, resolving such disputes requires moving beyond political rhetoric to establish clear, technically sound frameworks that allow diverse stakeholders to validate public information independently.
