Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article highlights a significant transparency victory where a regional councilor obtained detailed records of construction failures in public housing projects managed by the Servicio de Vivienda y Urbanismo. This request reveals systemic quality control issues, including structural defects and poor installations, across multiple residential complexes. The sheer volume of documented problems suggests a pattern of negligence that affects vulnerable populations relying on state-subsidized housing, raising serious concerns about the efficacy of current oversight mechanisms. Relevance to open data lies in the critical role that transparency laws play in exposing administrative inefficiencies and holding public entities accountable. By accessing granular data regarding inspections, complaints, and repair statuses, citizens and officials can scrutinize the actual delivery of public services rather than relying on aggregated or sanitized reports. This case demonstrates how open data initiatives empower local representatives to demand higher standards and verify whether government contracts are being fulfilled effectively. Ultimately, the narrative underscores that transparency is not merely about releasing information but about using it to drive policy decisions and improve service quality. The revelation of hundreds of annual defects in a single building challenges the assumption that public construction standards are being met. It serves as a powerful reminder that open data is essential for democratic oversight, enabling the identification of systemic flaws in public works and ensuring that taxpayer funds result in safe, habitable living conditions for all citizens.
