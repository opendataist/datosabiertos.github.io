Contesta Corte a Segob sobre salarios: no pueden disminuirse

The Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation clarified that its members' salaries cannot be unilaterally reduced by other government branches, nor can they be paid differently among the justices themselves. This ruling reinforces judicial independence by citing constitutional principles that prohibit wage cuts during a judge's term and mandate equal pay for equal work, thereby shielding the judiciary from political pressure exerted by the Executive or Legislative powers. Furthermore, the Court defined "remuneration" broadly to include not just base salary but also extensive benefits like housing, security, and health services, which the President receives. By highlighting that the Executive’s total compensation package is comparable or superior when all benefits are accounted for, the Court countered the argument that its members earn disproportionately higher wages. This distinction is crucial for understanding the full economic reality of high-level government positions and prevents simplistic comparisons based solely on cash salaries. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, comprehensive, and comparable datasets for public accountability. It demonstrates how selective data presentation can obscure the true cost of government positions and lead to misinformation. For open data initiatives, this highlights the importance of standardizing definitions of public sector compensation to ensure that transparency efforts facilitate genuine understanding of fiscal policies and resource allocation across all state branches, rather than being weaponized for political narratives.
