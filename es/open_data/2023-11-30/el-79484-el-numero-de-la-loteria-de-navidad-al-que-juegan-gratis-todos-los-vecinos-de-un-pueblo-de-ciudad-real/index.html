El 79484, el número de la Lotería de Navidad al que juegan gratis todos los vecinos de un pueblo de Ciudad Real

In Santa Cruz de Mudela, every resident plays the same lottery number, a tradition that highlights community cohesion through collective financial participation. The local lotteries agent selects the number, and political parties fund individual tickets for each villager. This system ensures broad social inclusion, replacing past methods with symbolic tickets distributed annually on Constitution Day. The initiative reflects a unique social contract where municipal resources support shared cultural traditions, fostering unity and collective hope among the town’s inhabitants. The relevance to open data lies in the transparency and accessibility of this civic information. The process relies on precise municipal registry data to determine eligibility, as only those registered by a specific date can claim any potential winnings. While this article focuses on cultural tradition rather than technical datasets, it underscores the importance of accurate public records. Open data principles emphasize that such demographic and administrative information should be reliable and accessible, ensuring fair participation in public benefits and maintaining trust in local governance mechanisms. This case illustrates how local data management supports social equity and cultural preservation. The reliance on the municipal padron (registry) for prize distribution demonstrates the critical role of accurate citizen data in executing public policies fairly. Understanding these local practices helps researchers and policymakers see how data infrastructure underpins community well-being. It serves as a reminder that behind every statistical record is a real-world impact on social inclusion, making the quality and openness of civic data essential for sustainable community development and transparent public administration.
