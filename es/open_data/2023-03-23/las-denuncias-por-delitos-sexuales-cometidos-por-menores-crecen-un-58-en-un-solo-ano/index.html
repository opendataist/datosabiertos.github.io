Las denuncias por delitos sexuales cometidos por menores crecen un 58% en un solo año

Spain reports a sharp rise in sexual offenses, particularly those committed by minors, with allegations tripling over the last decade. This trend has reignited public debate regarding the legal age of criminal responsibility, highlighting urgent societal concerns about juvenile delinquency and the perceived gaps in current legal frameworks. Analyses suggest that early exposure to violent pornography may be a significant contributing factor, as many adolescents use explicit content as their primary source of sexual education. This early consumption, often starting in pre-teen years, appears to influence real-world behavior, potentially normalizing harmful actions and driving the observed increase in aggressive incidents among younger demographics. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent, accessible, and standardized public statistics on crime and youth behavior. By making official data from the Public Prosecutor’s Office and the National Statistics Institute freely available, researchers and policymakers can identify emerging trends, evaluate the effectiveness of interventions, and foster evidence-based discussions on digital safety and legal reforms.
