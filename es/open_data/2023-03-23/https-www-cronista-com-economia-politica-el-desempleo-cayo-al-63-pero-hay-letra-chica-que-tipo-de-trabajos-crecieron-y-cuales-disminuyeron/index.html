Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The official unemployment rate declined in late 2022, reaching its lowest level in recent years alongside overall economic growth. However, this quantitative improvement masks a significant deterioration in employment quality. There has been a shift toward informal work and public sector jobs, while private employment has contracted. Consequently, real wages have continued to lose value against high inflation, and youth unemployment remains disproportionately high, indicating that the labor market is becoming less stable despite the lower headline unemployment figures. This contrast between statistical availability and working conditions is crucial for open data advocates. Official indicators often present a fragmented view of labor realities, potentially obscuring the erosion of living standards. By analyzing diverse datasets, including those from private consultancies, one can observe that gains in employment quantity are offset by declines in job security and income power. This highlights the necessity of complementary data sources to provide a holistic understanding of economic well-being beyond simple percentages. Ultimately, these findings underscore the importance of scrutinizing the qualitative dimensions of employment when using public statistics. The tension between reduced unemployment rates and worsening working conditions suggests that data alone is insufficient without context regarding informality and wage erosion. For open data initiatives, this case illustrates the need to promote multi-dimensional analysis that captures the complexity of labor markets, ensuring that policy decisions and public discourse reflect the true challenges faced by workers.
