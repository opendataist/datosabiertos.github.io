El mapa de los agujeros negros del Estado de derecho en España

The report highlights a significant deterioration of the rule of law in Spain, primarily driven by the politicization of the judiciary and the misuse of legislative tools. The prolonged blocking of the Council for the General Power of the Judiciary has severely compromised judicial independence and efficiency, leading to substantial delays in resolving cases and a perceived lack of impartiality. This political gridlock exacerbates public distrust and undermines the fundamental principles of a fair trial, creating a crisis that extends beyond mere administrative inefficiency to affect constitutional guarantees. A critical issue identified is the abusive and ordinary use of emergency decree-laws, which bypasses necessary parliamentary debate and erodes democratic checks and balances. Concurrently, there is a pronounced problem of budgetary inefficiency within the justice system; despite high financial investment relative to other EU nations, Spain yields poor results in case resolution times and maintains a low judge-to-population ratio. This discrepancy suggests that increased funding alone is insufficient without strategic digitalization and better resource management to enhance the actual efficacy of legal proceedings. This document is highly relevant to the open data movement as it represents one of the first instances where a Spanish organization rigorously applies the European Commission’s methodology using public data to assess democratic health. By demonstrating how transparent, accessible data can reveal systemic governance failures—such as the arbitrary use of pardons or the lack of objective merit-based promotions—it underscores the necessity of open data for accountability. The report serves as a model for civil society to leverage publicly available information to monitor and demand improvements in institutional transparency and judicial integrity.
