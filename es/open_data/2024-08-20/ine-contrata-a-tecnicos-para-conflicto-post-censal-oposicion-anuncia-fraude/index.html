INE contrata a técnicos para “conflicto post censal”; oposición anuncia “fraude”

The article highlights growing political tensions surrounding Bolivia’s national census, as the National Institute of Statistics hired specialized personnel to manage anticipated post-census conflicts. Opposition lawmakers argue these contracts indicate preparations for manipulating population data, which directly impacts the redistribution of parliamentary seats and economic resources. They claim the government is secretly altering figures to favor specific political factions, thereby undermining democratic representation and regional equity. This situation is critically relevant to open data because it exposes how opaque administrative processes can compromise data integrity and public trust. The contracts emphasize confidentiality and restricted access to census materials, creating an environment where independent verification is nearly impossible. Such secrecy prevents civil society from validating results, which is essential for ensuring that statistical data serves the public interest rather than political agendas. Ultimately, the controversy underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and verifiable public data systems. When governments withhold or classify vital demographic information, it erodes accountability and facilitates potential electoral manipulation. This case serves as a stark reminder that true open data requires not just publication, but also methodological clarity and institutional openness to external scrutiny, ensuring that statistical outcomes are perceived as legitimate by all stakeholders.
