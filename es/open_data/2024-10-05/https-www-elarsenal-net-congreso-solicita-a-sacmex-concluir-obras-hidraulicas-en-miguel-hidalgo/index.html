Mexican lawmakers demanded urgent action from water and public works authorities to resolve unresolved infrastructure projects in Mexico City’s Irrigation neighborhood. Residents face ongoing hazards from open manholes and unfinished road repairs, exacerbated by years of delays and a lack of communication with the community. The situation highlights severe gaps in municipal transparency and accountability. When public works are stalled without clear explanation, it breeds public distrust and endangers citizens, undermining efforts to improve essential services like water management where significant losses already occur. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical need for transparent, accessible project status information. Open data initiatives can empower citizens to track infrastructure progress, hold officials accountable for delays, and ensure that public resources are managed efficiently to prevent such prolonged disruptions.
