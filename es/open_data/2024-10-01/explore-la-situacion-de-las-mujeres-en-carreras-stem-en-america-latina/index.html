Explore la situación de las mujeres en carreras STEM en América Latina

The article highlights the critical need to address the gender gap in STEM fields, emphasizing that educational institutions must actively promote equal opportunities for women. It concludes that fostering female participation in sciences and engineering is essential not only for economic empowerment but also for intellectual and social realization, challenging entrenched stereotypes that limit women’s professional potential in high-growth sectors. Several structural and cultural barriers sustain this disparity, including early educational gaps in math and science, a lack of female role models, and workplace environments that are often unwelcoming to women due to unpaid care burdens and gender bias. These factors contribute to a global trend where women remain significantly underrepresented in STEM employment, despite their equal capability to succeed in these areas, ultimately limiting their access to better-paying and faster-growing jobs. This content is highly relevant to open data because the underlying statistics and UNESCO research cited form a crucial dataset for analyzing gender equity. By making such data visible and accessible, stakeholders can track progress, identify specific regional disparities, and design evidence-based policies to dismantle systemic barriers, ensuring that data-driven insights directly inform strategies for inclusive economic development.
