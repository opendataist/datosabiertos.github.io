Ciberataque: filtran una base de datos con 11 millones de registros de ciudadanos argentinos

Cybersecurity experts recently uncovered the illegal publication of a database containing millions of Argentine citizens' personal records, likely extracted from an electoral roll. This breach exposes sensitive information such as national IDs, addresses, and electoral details, highlighting the severe risks associated with inadequate protection of civic data repositories. Concurrently, access to numerous security cameras was compromised using a known vulnerability in specific camera models. The attacker exploited flaws stemming from poor manufacturing practices and a lack of timely security patches, demonstrating how unresolved software defects can lead to significant physical and digital privacy breaches. This incident is highly relevant to the open data community as it illustrates the critical need for rigorous data governance and security standards. It underscores how improperly secured datasets, even when intended for public or administrative use, can become vectors for mass privacy violations. The event serves as a stark reminder that transparency must be balanced with robust cybersecurity measures to protect individual rights and maintain public trust in open data initiatives.
