Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article highlights the first official national survey in Paraguay revealing that nearly 80% of women have experienced gender-based violence. These findings underscore a critical lack of reporting and access to specialized services, as most incidents remain hidden from institutions. The data exposes the widespread nature of psychological, physical, sexual, and economic abuse across various life stages and public spheres. This transparency is vital for open data initiatives because it transforms isolated personal tragedies into measurable public health and human rights issues. By making this hidden prevalence visible, the survey provides the necessary evidence base for policymakers and civil society to demand accountability and resource allocation. Without such open, official statistics, the scale of the crisis remains obscured, preventing effective systemic intervention. Ultimately, the report emphasizes that visible, high-quality data is a prerequisite for social change. It challenges the status quo by quantifying the severity of discrimination and violence, thereby driving the need for educational reforms and protective infrastructure. For the open data community, this case illustrates how accessible government statistics can empower advocacy efforts and ensure that vulnerable populations receive the specialized assistance they desperately need.
