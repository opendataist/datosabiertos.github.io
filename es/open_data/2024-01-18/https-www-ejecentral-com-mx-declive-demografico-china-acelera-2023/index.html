Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

China experiences a severe and accelerating demographic contraction, marking a critical shift in its population dynamics after decades of growth. The decline is driven by high education costs, cultural shifts regarding marriage, and women’s increased participation in the workforce, leading to fertility rates far below replacement levels. Despite ending strict birth controls and implementing pro-natalist policies, the trend remains largely irreversible due to profound changes in young people’s attitudes toward family size. This crisis poses significant economic and social challenges, particularly regarding the shrinking labor force and the burden on the "sandwich generation" tasked with caring for aging parents. The government is responding with substantial investments in elderly care services, as immigration is deemed unviable due to public opposition and restrictive policies. The situation highlights a fundamental structural transformation that traditional incentives have failed to address. This data is crucial for open data initiatives, as it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and longitudinal demographic statistics. Understanding such complex societal shifts requires public access to reliable datasets to inform policy, economic forecasting, and social planning. Open data enables researchers and citizens to analyze the root causes and implications of demographic changes, fostering better-informed discussions on sustainable development and social welfare in an aging society.
