¿Estará Perú en camino a la recesión económica?

Peru is facing a severe economic slowdown and deepening food insecurity, marked by rising prices, shrinking household incomes, and significant declines in key industrial sectors. The interplay between stagnating economic growth, private investment contraction, and environmental shocks like El Niño has exacerbated poverty and malnutrition, particularly affecting vulnerable children. This narrative highlights the urgent need for transparent, real-time data to monitor economic indicators and food access, as the current crisis underscores the limitations of fragmented government responses and the lack of comprehensive, accessible information to guide effective policy interventions. This situation is critical for the open_data community because it illustrates the tangible human impact of insufficient data visibility and coordination. When economic and nutritional data are siloed across different ministries or delayed, it hampers the ability to identify systemic failures and implement timely, evidence-based solutions. Open data initiatives can bridge this gap by providing unified, accessible platforms for tracking inflation, food availability, and poverty metrics, enabling researchers, journalists, and citizens to hold institutions accountable and advocate for more coherent strategies. Ultimately, the article serves as a stark reminder that data transparency is not merely a technical issue but a fundamental tool for social justice and economic stability. By promoting open, standardized, and interoperable data practices, stakeholders can better understand complex socioeconomic trends and ensure that resources are allocated where they are needed most. Encouraging the publication of granular, up-to-date datasets can empower civil society to drive meaningful change, ensuring that economic policies are responsive to the realities faced by the most disadvantaged populations.
