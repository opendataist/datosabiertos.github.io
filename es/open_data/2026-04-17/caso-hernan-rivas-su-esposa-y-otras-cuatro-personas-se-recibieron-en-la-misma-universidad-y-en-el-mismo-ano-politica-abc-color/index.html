Caso Hernán Rivas: su esposa y otras cuatro personas se recibieron en la misma universidad y en el mismo año - Política - ABC Color

The article examines the controversy surrounding Senator Hernán Rivas, whose law degree from the Universidad Sudamericana is publicly verifiable through the Ministry of Education’s open data registry. This dataset allows citizens to confirm graduation details, revealing that Rivas and his wife both appear as 2020 graduates. However, discrepancies in registration dates and resolutions have fueled persistent doubts regarding the authenticity of his credentials, highlighting how open data serves as a critical tool for verifying public officials' qualifications and holding them accountable. Despite the data suggesting legitimacy, political and legal scrutiny has intensified due to perceived irregularities. Rivas faced denunciations for potentially holding a false title, leading to his temporary disqualification and subsequent legal battles. The case illustrates the tension between publicly available institutional records and the complex reality of document verification processes. The inability to provide corroborating peer testimony, despite promises, has deepened public distrust, showing that while data is accessible, contextual interpretation often drives political consequences. Ultimately, the case remained relevant to open data by demonstrating its role in uncovering potential inconsistencies in professional histories. Although the legal proceedings concluded with the dismissal of the case due to prescription, the incident underscores the importance of transparent public records in monitoring political integrity. The open nature of educational data enables journalists and citizens to question narratives, proving that accessible information is vital for democratic oversight, even when legal outcomes are uncertain or controversial.
