The event highlighted the crucial intersection of transparency, data protection, and artificial intelligence, emphasizing that digital citizenship requires a dual understanding of information rights and privacy. By leveraging the National Transparency Platform, citizens can effectively exercise both access to public information and personal data protection rights through accessible technological tools. This demonstrates how open data infrastructure serves not only as a mechanism for government accountability but also as a vital instrument for individual autonomy over personal information. Conversely, the discussion warned that the proliferation of AI and online platforms poses significant risks to privacy, as individuals often unknowingly trade valuable personal data for free digital services. The narrative underscores that virtual actions have tangible real-world consequences, making it imperative for users to remain vigilant about the data they generate and share. This perspective shifts the focus from passive consumption to active, informed participation in the digital economy, where privacy is recognized as a non-negotiable asset. This article is relevant to open data because it reframes transparency initiatives as essential components of broader digital rights. It illustrates that open government data ecosystems must evolve to include robust personal data protection mechanisms, ensuring that technological advancements in AI and information access do not come at the expense of individual privacy. Ultimately, sustainable open data practices require empowering citizens with the knowledge and tools to navigate these complexities, balancing public transparency with personal security in an increasingly interconnected world.
