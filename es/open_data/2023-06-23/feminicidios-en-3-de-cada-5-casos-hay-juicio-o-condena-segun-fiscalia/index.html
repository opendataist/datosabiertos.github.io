Feminicidios: en 3 de cada 5 casos hay juicio o condena, según Fiscalía

The classification of femicide as an autonomous crime has significantly improved legal visibility and reduced impunity rates compared to other offenses, demonstrating the power of open data to highlight systemic shifts. By distinguishing femicide from generic homicide, the legal framework has successfully exposed the gendered nature of these crimes, allowing for more accurate tracking of judicial progress. This transparency ensures that cases are no longer obscured by lesser charges, fostering public recognition of the specific violence against women. Open data analysis reveals a persistent and growing trend in the number of femicides, contradicting hopes that criminalization alone would stop the violence. While the legislation has increased penalties and ensured that a majority of cases with sufficient evidence are advancing through the judicial system, the absolute number of victims continues to rise annually. This disparity underscores that legal recognition is necessary but insufficient to address the deep-rooted social and structural causes driving this epidemic of violence. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how accessible, standardized government records can drive accountability and inform public policy. By making prosecution rates, geographical prevalence, and demographic details transparent, open data transforms raw statistics into a tool for advocacy. It allows society to monitor the state's response to violence, compare regional impacts, and measure the real effectiveness of legal reforms beyond mere legislative changes.
