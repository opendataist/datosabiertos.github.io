Más de dos millones de turistas que visitan las Islas se alojan en casas de amigos

Official tourism statistics reveal a significant discrepancy between recorded hotel stays and the actual number of visitors in the Balearic Islands, highlighting a vast segment of tourists accommodated in "non-touristic" lodging. This includes friends' homes and second residences, which collectively host nearly as many guests as legal vacation rentals. Consequently, approximately one-fifth of all tourists bypass regulated accommodation entirely, escaping taxes like the ecotasa and avoiding official oversight. This hidden migration of travelers helps explain the severe overcrowding observed during peak seasons, as these unregulated stays supplement the limited legal hotel and rental capacity. The data underscores that while the majority of visitors use hotels, a substantial minority operates outside formal channels, creating a challenge for managing mass tourism and ensuring equitable tax collection. This article is vital for open data because it illustrates the limitations of current statistical frameworks in capturing the full scope of tourism activity. It emphasizes the urgent need for transparent, granular, and accessible datasets that can expose informal economic sectors, enabling policymakers to make evidence-based decisions rather than relying solely on traditional, potentially misleading metrics.
