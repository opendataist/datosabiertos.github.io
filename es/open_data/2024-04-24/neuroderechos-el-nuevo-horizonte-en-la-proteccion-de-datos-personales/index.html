Neuroderechos, el nuevo horizonte en la protección de datos personales

The advent of the Fourth Industrial Revolution, driven by artificial intelligence and advanced neuroscience, is fundamentally transforming societal structures and economic models. This technological leap introduces unprecedented challenges for human rights, particularly regarding the protection of personal data. As neurotechnology evolves, it creates the potential to scan, interpret, and even manipulate neural activities, thereby blurring the lines between biological processes and digital interactions. This progression necessitates the establishment of "neuro-rights," including mental privacy, identity, and neurological self-determination. These rights aim to shield individuals from unauthorized access to their brain data and prevent cognitive manipulation or algorithmic discrimination. Without robust legal frameworks, there is a significant risk that individuals could face new forms of inequality or coercion, as neural data becomes a target for exploitation by both state and private entities wielding powerful neurotechnological tools. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical need to classify highly sensitive biometric data under strict regulatory regimes. It underscores that traditional transparency and access paradigms are insufficient for neural information, which requires specialized protection to prevent misuse. The text advocates for binding constitutional reforms, citing Chile as a model, to ensure that the open sharing of non-sensitive data does not compromise the fundamental integrity and autonomy of the human mind.
