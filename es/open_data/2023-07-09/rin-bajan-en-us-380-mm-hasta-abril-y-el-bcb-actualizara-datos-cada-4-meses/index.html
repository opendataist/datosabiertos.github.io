RIN bajan en $us 380 MM hasta abril y el BCB actualizará datos cada 4 meses

The Central Bank of Bolivia has significantly reduced the frequency of publishing its Net International Reserves data, shifting from weekly updates to quarterly reports. This decision, justified by the new "Gold Law," results in a substantial delay in transparency, with the most recent figures now reflecting a status two months prior. Economists criticize this move as a violation of citizens' right to information, arguing that outdated data prevents businesses and individuals from making informed economic decisions and fosters unnecessary uncertainty among market agents. Analysts highlight conflicting interpretations of the reserve fluctuations, ranging from sharp declines to minimal real decreases, depending on whether external factors like gold prices are accounted for. However, the core debate centers on opacity rather than specific figures. Critics argue that the government is withholding critical financial details under the guise of legislative compliance, thereby obscuring whether reserves were actually sold to purchase gold from miners. This lack of timely disclosure undermines trust in public institutions and hampers the economic planning of various sectors that rely on accurate and current macroeconomic indicators. This situation is highly relevant to open_data principles because it illustrates a regression from proactive transparency to restricted access. By withholding timely public data, the Central Bank impedes accountability and the independent analysis essential for a healthy democratic economy. The case underscores the importance of maintaining regular, accessible, and up-to-date publication of public financial records, as delayed information effectively deprives society of a public good and erodes the foundation of data-driven decision-making.
