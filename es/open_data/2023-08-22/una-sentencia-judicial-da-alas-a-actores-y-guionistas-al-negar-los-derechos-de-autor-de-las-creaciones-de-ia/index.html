Una sentencia judicial da alas a actores y guionistas al negar los derechos de autor de las creaciones de IA

The ongoing Hollywood strikes by writers and actors highlight a critical conflict over the introduction of artificial intelligence in creative workflows. The core issue revolves around the lack of regulation regarding AI contributions to scripts and the exploitative practice of scanning background actors to reuse their likenings without fair compensation. This technological shift threatens job security and professional rights, challenging the traditional value of human creativity in the entertainment industry. A recent judicial ruling denying copyright protection to purely AI-generated works significantly impacts these negotiations by limiting the studios' legal leverage. This decision underscores that human authorship remains essential for intellectual property rights, offering a potential legal backbone to the unions’ demands. It reinforces the argument that AI cannot independently replace human creators, thereby shifting the power dynamic back toward the workforce. This development is highly relevant to open data as it illustrates the urgent need for transparent, legally clear frameworks governing AI usage in creative sectors. It emphasizes that data used to train these systems must respect human authorship and rights, preventing unregulated exploitation. The situation serves as a warning for open data initiatives to proactively address ethical boundaries, ensuring that data practices do not undermine human creativity or labor rights in an increasingly automated world.
