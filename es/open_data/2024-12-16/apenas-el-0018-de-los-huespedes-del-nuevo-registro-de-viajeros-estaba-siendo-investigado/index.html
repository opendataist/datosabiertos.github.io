Apenas el 0,018% de los huéspedes del nuevo registro de viajeros estaba siendo investigado

The mandatory data registration under Royal Decree 933/2021 demonstrates that indiscriminate data collection is an ineffective security strategy, capturing negligible illegal activity while exposing millions to risk. This highlights the critical need for data minimization in public policy, proving that broad surveillance disproportionately impacts privacy without enhancing safety. The resulting vulnerability to cybercrime underscores how mass data accumulation creates targets for fraud and identity theft. By retaining sensitive information unnecessarily, entities increase the impact of potential breaches, revealing that current regulatory approaches fail to balance security with the protection of fundamental rights against growing digital threats. This case is relevant to open data as it illustrates the dangers of centralized, unmanaged personal data pools. It advocates for transparent, privacy-by-design frameworks that limit data retention, ensuring that any shared or stored information serves a specific, justified purpose rather than creating unnecessary exposure for citizens in an increasingly connected digital ecosystem.
