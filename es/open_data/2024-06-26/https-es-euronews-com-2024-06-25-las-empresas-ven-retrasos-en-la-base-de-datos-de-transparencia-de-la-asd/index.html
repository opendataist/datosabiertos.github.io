Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The implementation of the Digital Services Act reveals a significant disparity in transparency compliance between major tech giants and smaller platforms. While designated Very Large Online Platforms have already submitted detailed content moderation data to the public transparency database, the uptake among smaller entities remains critically low, with only a single non-VLOP actively contributing. This bottleneck highlights a systemic challenge in achieving comprehensive digital accountability, as the majority of smaller platforms are still in preliminary testing phases or have yet to integrate their reporting systems entirely. The delay is largely attributed to regulatory fragmentation and administrative lags across member states. National Digital Services Coordinators, who are essential for guiding smaller platforms into the transparency framework, have failed to be properly established or empowered in several countries. Consequently, the enforcement mechanism intended to ensure uniform oversight is struggling, leaving gaps in the monitoring of content decisions for a vast portion of the online ecosystem that operates outside the immediate purview of the largest corporations. This situation is pertinent to open data because it underscores that regulatory mandates alone do not guarantee data availability. For transparency to be meaningful, technical integration and institutional capacity must be synchronized across the entire market, not just the most visible actors. Without robust participation from all platform tiers, the public and researchers lack a complete picture of digital content governance, limiting the utility of open datasets for auditing the true scale of online moderation and ensuring genuine democratic oversight.
