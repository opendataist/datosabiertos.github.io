Subieron las ventas en supermercados, mayoristas y centros de compras en agosto

Retail sales across supermarkets, wholesale clubs, and shopping centers showed positive growth in August. When adjusted for inflation, sales increased compared to the same period last year, with a steady upward trend continuing in recent months. This indicates that consumer activity is recovering, despite high inflation rates driving nominal price increases. The real purchasing power is expanding, suggesting a healthy recovery in domestic consumption habits. Different sectors contributed differently to this growth, with food and household items seeing significant nominal rises. However, the constant-price data reveals that the underlying demand for essential goods remains strong. This distinction between nominal and real growth is crucial for understanding the true state of the economy. It highlights that while prices have surged, the volume of goods sold has also increased, reflecting genuine consumer confidence. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how statistical institutes like INDEC provide transparent, standardized metrics to interpret complex economic realities. By offering both nominal and constant-price indices, along with seasonally adjusted series, open data enables researchers to separate inflation effects from actual economic activity. This level of detail supports evidence-based policy making and allows citizens to accurately assess economic health beyond headline figures.
