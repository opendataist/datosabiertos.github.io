La inflación de EEUU se modera al 3,4% en abril y la subyacente cae al 3,6%

The recent US inflation data indicates a modest moderation in price increases, aligning with market expectations and signaling that inflationary pressures are beginning to stabilize. While core inflation has reached its lowest level in three years, the persistence of high service costs keeps the Federal Reserve cautious. The main conclusion is that while this cooling trend supports the eventual case for monetary easing, it is insufficient to trigger immediate interest rate cuts, with policymakers likely to maintain a restrictive stance until more definitive evidence of sustained disinflation emerges later in the year. Retail sales data reveals a surprising deceleration in consumer spending, suggesting that American households are becoming more cautious with their expenditures. This slowdown, driven partly by temporary factors like early Easter sales, highlights a potential resilience in the broader economy while indicating shifting consumer behavior. The combination of moderating inflation and softer retail figures provides the Federal Reserve with the confidence to keep current rates steady in the short term, setting the stage for potential rate reductions in the autumn as the economic narrative continues to normalize. This analysis is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical role of timely, transparent statistical releases in shaping global economic narratives and financial market reactions. Access to standardized, high-frequency indicators like the Consumer Price Index and retail sales figures allows researchers, journalists, and policymakers to independently verify trends, assess the health of the economy, and understand the complex interplay between inflation, consumer behavior, and monetary policy decisions without relying solely on filtered interpretations.
