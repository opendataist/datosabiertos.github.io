Una de cal y una de arena: el análisis de economistas sanjuaninos tras el 8,8% de inflación de abril

The article highlights April’s inflation rate as a tentative sign of cooling price pressures, though economists caution that the underlying economic recession remains severe. While the deceleration in inflation is noted as a positive step, the persistence of a shrinking economy, particularly in construction and industry, underscores that immediate recovery is not yet guaranteed. Experts emphasize that true stabilization depends on fundamental structural reforms and the implementation of delayed fiscal measures, such as fuel taxes, which could still impact future pricing. This data is particularly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the critical need for transparent, accessible, and frequently updated economic indicators to validate official statistics. When government-reported metrics show signs of improvement but remain at high levels, citizens and analysts require detailed breakdowns—such as core inflation versus seasonal fluctuations—to understand the real economic health. Open data facilitates this scrutiny by allowing diverse stakeholders to cross-reference figures with local market behaviors, like the frequency of price list updates. Furthermore, the divergence between official celebration and local economic reality illustrates how open data can bridge the gap between macro-level reporting and micro-level lived experiences. By making granular data on sector performance and business closures available, society can better anticipate recovery timelines and assess the effectiveness of proposed legal reforms. Ultimately, this case reinforces that open, granular economic data is essential for fostering informed public debate and holding institutions accountable during periods of economic uncertainty and transition.
