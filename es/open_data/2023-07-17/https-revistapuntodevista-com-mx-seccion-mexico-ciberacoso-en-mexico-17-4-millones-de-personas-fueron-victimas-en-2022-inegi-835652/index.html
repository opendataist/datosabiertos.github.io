Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The INEGI’s 2022 Ciberacoso report highlights a critical shift in digital safety dynamics in Mexico, revealing that while cyberbullying prevalence decreased slightly, it remains a widespread issue affecting one in five internet users. This data is vital for the open_data community as it provides a transparent, granular view of how digital violence disproportionately impacts women and young adults. By making these findings publicly accessible, the dataset empowers researchers and policymakers to analyze patterns of online abuse and design targeted interventions that protect vulnerable populations, moving beyond anecdotal evidence to data-driven advocacy. Geographic and demographic disparities are stark, with specific states and age groups bearing the brunt of this violence, indicating that digital risks are not evenly distributed across society. The report underscores that victims often spend more time online and frequently encounter harassment through fake identities and social media platforms, yet a majority of perpetrators remain anonymous. This transparency in data allows open-data advocates to identify systemic gaps in platform accountability and regulatory frameworks, emphasizing the need for clearer metrics on user safety and the effectiveness of current digital protection measures. Furthermore, the psychological toll and subsequent defensive behaviors, such as enhanced password security and antivirus use, reveal the direct impact of cyberbullying on user well-being and digital habits. The high percentage of unreported or unidentified aggression points to significant challenges in legal recourse and platform moderation. For the open_data ecosystem, this report serves as a crucial benchmark for monitoring digital health trends. It illustrates how standardized, open statistical information can drive public discourse on digital rights, encouraging the development of more robust, user-centric digital policies and fostering a culture of online safety in Mexico.
