En México el amor ya no dura tanto; repuntan divorcios tras pandemia

The article highlights that divorce rates in Mexico have risen significantly, signaling a shift toward viewing partnerships as non-permanent. While legal separations increased notably between 2011 and 2021, a pandemic-induced dip was quickly followed by a resumption of the upward trend. This data suggests that modern relationships are increasingly viewed as transient rather than lifelong commitments. However, the analysis points out a critical gap in official statistics, as many informal separations occur without legal divorce proceedings. Furthermore, couples in informal unions handle conflicts more openly than those in formal marriages, reflecting how marriage is now seen as a rigid institution with higher barriers to exit compared to cohabitation. This research is vital for open data initiatives because it exposes the limitations of current registries in capturing the full scope of relationship dynamics. Recognizing the discrepancy between formal legal records and actual social behavior underscores the need for more comprehensive datasets that reflect real-world human interactions beyond bureaucratic definitions.

Source: vanguardia.com.mx
