4T: logros y fracasos | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The article concludes that recent Mexican social policies successfully reduced poverty and increased household incomes, challenging critics who labeled the approach as populist. This outcome validates the "first the poor" strategy, demonstrating that targeted social transfers, alongside wage increases and remittances, effectively improved the economic situation for the lowest deciles. The data suggests that fiscal efficiency and budget reorientation by the legislative majority allowed these social goals to be met without imposing new taxes, refuting the notion that such measures inevitably lead to inflation or economic stagnation. However, the narrative highlights a significant contradiction: while monetary poverty declined, the lack of access to health services and educational backlogs worsened. The proposed health reform faced substantial operational and political obstacles, ultimately leading to its integration into the existing social security institute rather than functioning as a standalone, effective system. This underscores that financial metrics alone do not capture the full quality of public welfare, revealing structural gaps in social infrastructure despite improvements in household purchasing power. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical importance of integrating multidimensional indicators beyond simple economic figures. Open data initiatives must enable the tracking of both material well-being and access to essential public services to provide a holistic view of social policy efficacy. By making comprehensive datasets available, societies can hold governments accountable not just for reducing income poverty, but for addressing deeper structural inequalities in health and education, ensuring that progress is sustainable and inclusive.
