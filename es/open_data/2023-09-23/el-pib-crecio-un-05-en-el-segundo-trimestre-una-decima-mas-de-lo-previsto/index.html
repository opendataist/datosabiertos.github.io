El PIB creció un 0,5% en el segundo trimestre, una décima más de lo previsto

The Spanish economy demonstrated modest growth in the second quarter, driven primarily by robust domestic consumption and increased public spending. While the gross domestic product expanded, marking a slight upward revision to previous estimates, this growth rate represents a slowdown compared to the immediate preceding periods. The domestic demand emerged as the central pillar of this economic activity, effectively offsetting a negative contribution from external trade, which saw a notable decline in both goods and services exports. This shift highlights the internal resilience of the Spanish market despite weakening international demand. Employment figures reflect a broader recovery, with significant job creation reported over the last year across various sectors, particularly in services and construction. Wage growth for employees accelerated, suggesting improved income dynamics for households, which further fueled the rise in consumer spending. However, this positive labor trend was accompanied by a slight dip in per-worker productivity compared to the previous year, indicating that growth was achieved through labor intensity rather than efficiency gains in this specific period. This data is crucial for the open data community as it underscores the necessity of high-quality, transparent, and accessible statistical information for accurate economic analysis. Reliable national accounts enable policymakers, researchers, and citizens to monitor economic health and validate official narratives. By making such detailed economic indicators openly available, societies can enhance accountability and foster informed public debate on fiscal and social policies, ensuring that data serves as a true public good for democratic oversight.
