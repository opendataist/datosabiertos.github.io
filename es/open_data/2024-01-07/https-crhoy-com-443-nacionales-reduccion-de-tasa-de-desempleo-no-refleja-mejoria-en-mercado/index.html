The report indicates a significant decline in national employment, driven primarily by reduced labor participation rather than genuine market improvement. This trend highlights structural weaknesses, particularly within the informal sector, where job losses are most volatile and unstable, suggesting that lower unemployment figures mask a deteriorating labor condition for Costa Ricans. The disparity in outcomes by gender is stark, with women experiencing a sharper drop in occupational rates compared to men. These divergent trends reveal deep-seated inequalities in the workforce, where female participation suffers disproportionately during economic shifts, undermining efforts toward equitable labor market development. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it demonstrates how accessible statistics can expose the nuances behind aggregate economic indicators. By making granular demographic and sectoral data transparent, stakeholders can better analyze labor market health beyond surface-level metrics, fostering informed policy decisions and public accountability regarding employment welfare.
