INE: La pandemia aumentó la cantidad de divorcios y redujo la tasa de fecundidad

The pandemic triggered a profound demographic transition in Bolivia, marked by rising divorce rates, increased cohabitation, and a significant decline in fertility. This shift reflects broader societal changes, as women now have fewer children at a later average age, fundamentally altering family structures. These trends contribute to a complex open data landscape, requiring updated methodologies to capture evolving social dynamics. Accurate census data is essential for understanding these shifts, ensuring that statistical tools reflect modern realities rather than outdated assumptions. Relevance to open data lies in the necessity for transparent, accessible population statistics. High-quality datasets enable policymakers and researchers to address the implications of these changes, fostering informed decisions on social welfare and public health in a rapidly changing society.
