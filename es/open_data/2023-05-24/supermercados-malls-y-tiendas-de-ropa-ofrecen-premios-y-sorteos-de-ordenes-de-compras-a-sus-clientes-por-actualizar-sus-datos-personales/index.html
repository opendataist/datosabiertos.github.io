Supermercados, malls y tiendas de ropa ofrecen premios y sorteos de órdenes de compras a sus clientes por actualizar sus datos personales

The recent enforcement of the Personal Data Protection Law highlights a critical tension between regulatory compliance and commercial interests. While companies are legally required to obtain explicit consent for data processing, many are incentivizing customers to update their information through rewards and prize draws. This strategy underscores the urgent need for a cultural shift within businesses to respect user privacy rather than treating data merely as a marketing asset. The article reveals that major retailers and shopping centers are leveraging benefits to encourage data updates, often implying that failure to comply may result in the loss of services or benefits. This practice raises significant ethical and legal concerns regarding the voluntary nature of consent. It demonstrates how companies navigate the fine line between legitimate data management and coercive tactics that undermine individual autonomy under the new legal framework. This scenario is highly relevant to open data advocates because it illustrates the challenges in establishing trust between corporations and citizens. When businesses prioritize data accumulation for commercial gain over transparent, consensual practices, it hinders the development of a robust ecosystem for responsible data sharing. Understanding these dynamics is crucial for promoting policies that ensure data protection laws effectively safeguard individuals while fostering ethical data stewardship.
