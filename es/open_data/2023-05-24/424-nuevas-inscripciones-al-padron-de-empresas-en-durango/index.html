424 nuevas inscripciones al Padrón de Empresas en Durango

This municipal data reveals significant turnover, with ten percent of business records being new registrations. These figures highlight the challenges in maintaining an accurate open dataset, as many closures go unreported in official systems. Consequently, verification relies on physical inspections rather than purely digital administrative records. The introduction of stricter renewal deadlines aims to improve data currency by incentivizing timely updates. However, this approach struggles against the reality of unreported business closures, creating a gap between registered entities and actual operational status. Such discrepancies underscore the limitations of relying solely on self-reported administrative data. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the inherent friction between static databases and dynamic real-world economic activities. It emphasizes the critical need for open data initiatives to incorporate verification mechanisms and acknowledge data latency. Understanding these gaps is essential for creating reliable public datasets that truly reflect current business landscapes.
