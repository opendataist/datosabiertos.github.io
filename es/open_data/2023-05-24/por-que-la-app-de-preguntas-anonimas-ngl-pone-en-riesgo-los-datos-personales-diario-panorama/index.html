Por qué la app de preguntas anónimas NGL pone en riesgo los datos personales - Diario Panorama

The viral integration of third-party anonymous question services like NGL with Instagram presents significant privacy risks that extend beyond simple social interaction. By requiring external profiles and forced acceptance of privacy policies, these applications gain access to extensive personal data. This practice highlights a critical vulnerability in the current digital ecosystem, where users inadvertently compromise their security for the sake of social features, often without fully understanding the extent of the data they are surrendering to external entities. The application collects comprehensive information, including device identifiers, location, browsing habits, and contact details, which are shared with third parties for advertising purposes. This aggressive data harvesting demonstrates how user information is commodified and treated as a financial asset. The implications are severe, as this data may be retained indefinitely or transferred during corporate changes, such as mergers or bankruptcies. Consequently, users lose control over their digital footprint, facing potential exposure even after attempting to delete their accounts, as the company retains discretion over what constitutes "necessary" data. This case is highly relevant to open data discussions because it exemplifies the opaque and unregulated nature of personal data handling by private entities. It underscores the urgent need for transparency and user sovereignty in data management. When data collection practices lack clear consent mechanisms and robust security guarantees, it erodes public trust and complicates efforts to establish ethical standards for data usage. Understanding these dynamics is essential for developing policies that protect individual rights while navigating the complexities of modern social media integration.
