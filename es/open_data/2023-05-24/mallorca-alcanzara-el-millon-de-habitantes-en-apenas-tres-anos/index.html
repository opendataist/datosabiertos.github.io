Mallorca alcanzará el millón de habitantes en apenas tres años

The latest INE statistics reveal that the Balearic Islands are experiencing sustained demographic growth, with Mallorca projected to exceed one million residents in just over three years. This expansion is driven primarily by an influx of foreign nationals, particularly Colombians and Moroccans, rather than natural population increase. As the largest island absorbs the majority of new arrivals, the data highlights a significant shift in the region’s social fabric, where immigration becomes the main engine for residential expansion. In contrast to Mallorca’s rapid ascent, Eivissa shows signs of growth stabilization, while Menorca is attracting a notable number of foreign buyers, especially from France. This trend suggests that Menorca is poised to reach the 100,000 inhabitant mark in the coming year, challenging Eivissa’s previous dominance in population growth among the smaller islands. Meanwhile, Formentera is unique in that it has suffered its first population decline in recent years, indicating that not all destinations are benefiting from the current migratory boom. This article is highly relevant to open data as it demonstrates how publicly accessible government statistics can expose nuanced, island-level trends that aggregate national figures might obscure. By providing detailed, granular breakdowns of nationality and migration flows, such datasets enable precise analysis of socio-economic impacts. This transparency allows policymakers and researchers to identify specific challenges, such as housing pressure or demographic imbalances, ensuring that data-driven decisions accurately reflect the diverse realities within the archipelago.
