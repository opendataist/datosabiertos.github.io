Las pernoctaciones aumentan casi un 21% hasta abril y superan el dato de 2019

Spain’s hotel sector has demonstrated robust recovery, with overnight stays in the first four months surpassing 2019 levels by nearly 21%. This growth is driven largely by British and German tourists, while domestic travel also shows significant strength. Consequently, average daily hotel revenues have risen sharply, indicating a strong rebound in profitability and demand compared to both the previous year and pre-pandemic benchmarks. The distribution of tourism highlights distinct preferences between resident and international visitors, with southern and island regions like Canary Islands and Balearic Islands leading in occupancy rates. While prices have increased, the inflationary pressure on hotel rates has cooled compared to 2022, suggesting a stabilization in the market. The data reveals that premium segments and specific high-demand urban centers are capturing the highest revenue yields, reflecting a resilient luxury market alongside broader tourism growth. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how public statistical institutes, such as Spain’s INE, generate and release granular, time-series datasets on economic activities. These open datasets enable researchers, policymakers, and businesses to monitor sector health, identify regional trends, and assess the impact of tourism on local economies. Access to such structured, transparent information is crucial for evidence-based decision-making and for fostering transparency in public administration and economic reporting.
