Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The 2023 National Public Safety Census reveals that Chihuahua handled the fewest federal police crimes in Mexico last year. This data highlights significant gaps in public security statistics, demonstrating how official metrics can obscure local realities. For open data advocates, this underscores the critical need to question the completeness and source of government statistics, rather than accepting them at face value. Conversely, the report shows a massive surge in the seizure of weapons and the rescue of migrants, alongside a drastic drop in people handed over to authorities. These contrasting trends suggest that security strategies may have shifted toward specific interventions rather than comprehensive crime reduction. Such discrepancies emphasize that open data initiatives must look beyond simple aggregates to understand the underlying operational changes and policy impacts. However, the data also coincides with political concerns regarding human rights abuses by federal forces. While statistical reports quantify activity, they often fail to capture qualitative issues like citizen mistreatment or corruption. Therefore, open data ecosystems must integrate these statistical records with diverse sources, including civil society reports, to provide a holistic and accurate picture of public safety and institutional accountability.
