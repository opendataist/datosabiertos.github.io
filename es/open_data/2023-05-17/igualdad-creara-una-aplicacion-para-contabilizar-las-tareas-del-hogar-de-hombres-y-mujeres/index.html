Igualdad creará una aplicación para contabilizar las tareas del hogar de hombres y mujeres

The Spanish Ministry of Equality is launching a digital tool to accurately track household labor, aiming to quantify the disproportionate time women dedicate to domestic chores compared to men. This initiative directly addresses the significant gender gap in unpaid care work, which has been documented as far exceeding male contributions. By systematically measuring these invisible efforts, the government seeks to highlight structural inequalities and promote a more equitable distribution of family responsibilities, moving beyond anecdotal evidence to data-driven policy. Concurrently, the National Statistics Institute will resume its survey on time usage after a long hiatus, complementing the new application with robust statistical data. This renewed focus on temporal allocation underscores the critical role of open data in validating social realities. The information gathered will inform broader strategies, such as the Corresponsables Plan, which funds awareness campaigns to dismantle sexist stereotypes. These combined efforts aim to transform care work from a female-exclusive duty into a shared societal responsibility, using empirical evidence to drive cultural and legislative change. This development is highly relevant to open data as it demonstrates how government initiatives can leverage digital tools to make gender disparities visible and measurable. By committing to transparency in household labor statistics, Spain enhances accountability and enables researchers and citizens to analyze equality metrics. Furthermore, the article highlights the importance of using such data to defend feminist policies against political backlash, showing that open, accessible information is essential for sustaining social progress and ensuring that gender gaps are not only identified but actively reduced through informed public debate.
