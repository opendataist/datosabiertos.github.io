Las ventas minoristas en EEUU suben un 0,4% en abril, menos de lo esperado

US retail sales modestly increased in April, missing higher expectations but showing resilience as broader economic indicators suggest consumption remains stable despite tighter lending standards. This data implies that consumer spending is cooling gradually rather than collapsing, which may influence the Federal Reserve’s monetary policy decisions by providing more time to assess inflation trends before raising interest rates again. The slight rise in core sales supports the view that the economy is navigating a soft landing scenario, where inflation remains high but manageable without aggressive tightening. Analysts predict that nominal retail sales will likely decline in the second quarter, reflecting the cumulative impact of previous rate hikes and stricter credit conditions on household spending power. This information is relevant to open_data because it highlights the importance of transparent, real-time economic statistics for accurate forecasting. Accessible data on retail performance allows researchers and policymakers to monitor economic health, evaluate policy effectiveness, and make informed decisions about fiscal and monetary adjustments in response to shifting market dynamics.
