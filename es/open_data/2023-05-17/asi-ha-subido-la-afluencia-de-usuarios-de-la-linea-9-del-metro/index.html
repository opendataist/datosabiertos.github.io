Así ha subido la afluencia de usuarios de la Línea 9 del Metro

The Mexico City Metro’s Line 9 has seen a significant increase in daily ridership, surpassing pre-pandemic levels despite overall system attendance not fully recovering. This surge is largely driven by maintenance work on the parallel Line 1, forcing commuters to rely heavily on Line 9 as an alternative route. Consequently, the line now handles nearly double its original passenger volume, leading to severe congestion during peak hours and necessitating safety interventions to prevent overcrowding on platforms. This situation highlights a critical infrastructure challenge where temporary disruptions cause long-term systemic stress on a single transit corridor. The constant saturation affects service quality and passenger safety, demonstrating the fragility of urban transit networks when redundancy options are limited. The reliance on a single line to absorb displaced demand reveals vulnerabilities in urban mobility planning and the urgent need for robust contingency strategies during maintenance periods. This case is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and granular mobility statistics. By making detailed ridership data publicly available, authorities can facilitate better analysis of urban movement patterns, support evidence-based policy decisions, and empower citizens to make informed travel choices. Open data initiatives allow for greater accountability and help identify systemic bottlenecks that might otherwise remain hidden in aggregated reports.
