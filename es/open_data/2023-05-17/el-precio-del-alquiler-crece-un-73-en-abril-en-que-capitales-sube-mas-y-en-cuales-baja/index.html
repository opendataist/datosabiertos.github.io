El precio del alquiler crece un 7,3% en abril: ¿en qué capitales sube más y en cuáles baja?

The rental market in Spain is experiencing record-breaking prices driven by a severe imbalance between supply and demand. A significant reduction in available housing, as properties are sold, converted to tourist rentals, or left vacant, has created intense upward pressure on rents. Although the rate of increase has moderated slightly, the trend of consecutive monthly records indicates that housing affordability remains critically compromised for tenants across the majority of the country. This crisis is geographically uneven, with coastal tourist hubs and major urban centers like Madrid, Barcelona, and the Balearic Islands suffering the most dramatic surges. In these high-demand areas, rents have far exceeded historical peaks, reflecting the specific pressures of tourism and limited housing stock. Conversely, some inland regions have seen negligible changes or even slight decreases, highlighting a fragmented market where accessibility varies drastically depending on location and local economic dynamics. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, high-quality real estate metrics for policy-making. Accurate, publicly accessible information on rental trends allows researchers, policymakers, and citizens to identify regional disparities and understand the impact of housing laws. Without such open datasets, it becomes impossible to effectively address the structural supply issues or evaluate the efficacy of interventions aimed at stabilizing the housing market and protecting tenants.
