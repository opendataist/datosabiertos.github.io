Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article highlights Oaxaca’s significant digital divide, ranking second to last in internet usage nationwide. This disparity underscores the urgent need for equitable access to information and communication technologies, a core principle of open data initiatives. By revealing how socio-economic factors influence connectivity, the data illustrates the structural barriers that limit civic engagement and access to public information in marginalized regions. Demographic analysis shows that younger populations and those with secondary education dominate internet usage, while older adults and those with lower schooling levels remain largely offline. This uneven distribution affects how communities interact with digital public services. Understanding these usage patterns is essential for developing inclusive data strategies that ensure all citizens, regardless of age or education, can benefit from open information. Furthermore, the data reveals that residents primarily use the internet for communication and social media, with minimal engagement in online banking or cloud services. This behavior reflects a gap in digital literacy and infrastructure. For open data advocates, these insights are crucial for designing targeted interventions that bridge the digital divide, ensuring that the benefits of technological development reach the most vulnerable populations effectively.
