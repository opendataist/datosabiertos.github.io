El 82% de la población paraguaya tiene “vivienda propia”, según el INE - Nacionales - ABC Color

Recent statistics from Paraguay reveal a complex housing landscape where nearly 82 percent of the population identifies as having "own housing." However, this figure is nuanced because it includes those living in structures on municipal, invaded, or ceded lands, as well as those paying installments. This broad definition highlights a significant gap between perceived ownership and legal tenure security, affecting millions of residents despite the high nominal rate. Only a small fraction of the population actually rents homes, while others reside in housing built on land they do not legally own. This discrepancy suggests that many families inhabit structures without full property rights, creating a vulnerable segment of society that official statistics might misclassify as securely housed. Understanding these distinctions is vital for accurate social policy planning and recognizing the true extent of informal housing settlements. This data is particularly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, detailed metadata in official surveys. To support informed decision-making, databases must clearly distinguish between physical structure possession and legal land ownership. Open access to granular census information allows researchers and citizens to analyze these hidden disparities, fostering greater accountability and enabling more effective advocacy for housing rights and urban planning reforms.
