Más de la mitad de los ganadores del concurso de Diputados aparece afiliado a la ANR - Política - ABC Color

The article exposes a significant disconnect between the Paraguayan Chamber of Deputies’ attempt to rehabilitate its image after nepotism scandals and the reality of its new hiring practices. Although the institution launched a selection process for 50 new positions to distance itself from the "nepobabies" controversies, data verification reveals that a majority of the selected candidates are members of the ruling Colorado Party. This suggests that the recruitment drive may simply be a mechanism to institutionalize political patronage rather than eliminate it, as many appointees hold party affiliations that contradict the spirit of a merit-based public service reform. The core implication is that the state apparatus remains deeply entangled with partisan interests, undermining the principle of political neutrality in public administration. By filling key roles with party loyalists under the guise of a competitive selection process, the government perpetuates a system where public employment serves as a reward for political support. This reinforces the notion that access to public resources is contingent upon party membership, thereby perpetuating cycles of clientelism and limiting opportunities for qualified individuals outside the ruling coalition. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how publicly available information—such as party affiliation registries and official hiring lists—can be cross-referenced to hold power accountable. The investigation proves that open data is a crucial tool for detecting hidden biases and corruption in government operations. Without the transparency provided by these datasets, citizens would remain unaware of the extent to which political networks influence public employment, highlighting the vital role of data activism in ensuring democratic integrity and institutional transparency.
