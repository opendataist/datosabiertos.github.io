La Junta solo ha desestimado el 0,5 % de las 414 solicitudes de acceso a la información pública resueltas durante 2024

The article highlights the Junta de Castilla y León’s robust commitment to transparency through its self-developed open government portal. This platform serves as a central hub for active information dissemination, allowing citizens to access mandatory public data and request additional information with a remarkably high success rate. By prioritizing responsiveness and minimizing rejections, the administration demonstrates a tangible respect for citizens' right to know, moving beyond passive compliance to active engagement. A key component of this strategy is the Open Data Portal, which offers hundreds of datasets in structured formats designed for easy reuse. The government emphasizes that these data are not just for viewing but for creation, enabling individuals and organizations to build new services and derive independent conclusions without copyright restrictions. This approach shifts the focus from mere administrative record-keeping to fostering an ecosystem where public information becomes a resource for innovation and societal benefit. This initiative is highly relevant to the open data community because it exemplifies the transition from static transparency portals to dynamic, machine-readable data ecosystems. It validates the importance of structured, reusable datasets in empowering citizens and developers to create value from public administration data. Furthermore, the integration of citizen participation features within the same framework illustrates a holistic model of open government that combines data access, reuse potential, and direct civic engagement into a cohesive digital infrastructure.
