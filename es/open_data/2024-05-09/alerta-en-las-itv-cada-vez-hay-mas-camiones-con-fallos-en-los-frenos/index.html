Alerta en las ITV: cada vez hay más camiones con fallos en los frenos

The article reveals a critical situation in vehicle safety inspections, highlighting that a significant portion of heavy trucks fail due to severe defects, primarily in braking systems. This high failure rate underscores an immediate danger to public safety, as malfunctioning brakes can lead to catastrophic accidents. The analysis points out that inadequate maintenance and aging fleets are direct contributors to these failures, posing a substantial risk to both drivers and other road users. Furthermore, the data indicates that an aging vehicle population correlates with a sharp increase in fatal accidents involving uninspected older trucks. This trend is also visible in smaller vans, where a notable percentage fail inspections due to lighting, suspension, and emission issues. The gradual aging of both vehicle categories reflects broader systemic challenges in maintaining fleet safety standards over time, suggesting that current inspection regimes are struggling to cope with the growing proportion of obsolete vehicles on the road. This topic is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how public statistics can expose hidden societal risks and inform policy decisions. By making inspection failure rates and vehicle age data transparent, authorities can better target safety campaigns and regulatory enforcement. Open access to such metrics encourages civil society and researchers to analyze trends, fostering accountability and driving improvements in public safety infrastructure through evidence-based advocacy and informed public discourse.
