El sueldo mensual de los nuevos diputados del Congreso: ninguno baja de 3.000 euros y la presidenta llega a los 15.300

The article reveals that salaries and allowances for members of the Spanish Congress, published via the Portal of Transparency, highlight significant discrepancies in compensation based on political roles and residency. It concludes that the transparency of this financial data allows citizens to scrutinize how public resources are distributed among elected officials, regardless of their party affiliation. The core implication is that the President earns substantially more than regular deputies, while Madrid-based representatives receive significantly lower indemnities than those from other constituencies. This structural inequality in payments underscores how administrative rules directly impact the net income of lawmakers, creating distinct financial categories within the same legislative body. This topic is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical role of accessible government information in ensuring democratic accountability. By making detailed remuneration structures public, authorities enable civil society and media to monitor potential conflicts of interest or excesses, fostering a culture of integrity and trust in public institutions.
