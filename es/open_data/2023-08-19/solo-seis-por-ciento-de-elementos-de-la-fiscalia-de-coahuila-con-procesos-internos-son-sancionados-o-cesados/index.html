Solo seis por ciento de elementos de la Fiscalía de Coahuila con procesos internos son sancionados o cesados

Data from Coahuila’s Attorney General reveals that disciplinary proceedings rarely result in dismissal, with only a small fraction of sanctioned agents actually losing their positions. This stark disparity highlights a systemic issue where accountability measures like suspensions or fines replace termination, even in cases involving serious misconduct. The low rate of actual removals suggests a lack of effective internal oversight and deterrents for wrongful acts within the judicial police force. This transparency is crucial for open data initiatives, as it exposes the gap between reported sanctions and actual consequences. By making disciplinary records public, citizens can assess institutional integrity and demand higher standards of justice. The data underscores the importance of accessible, detailed information in monitoring public sector accountability and preventing impunity among law enforcement officers. Ultimately, the article demonstrates how open data serves as a vital tool for civic oversight. It allows society to verify whether institutions are effectively punishing misconduct or merely applying superficial penalties. This visibility is essential for building trust and ensuring that public servants are held to appropriate ethical and legal standards.
