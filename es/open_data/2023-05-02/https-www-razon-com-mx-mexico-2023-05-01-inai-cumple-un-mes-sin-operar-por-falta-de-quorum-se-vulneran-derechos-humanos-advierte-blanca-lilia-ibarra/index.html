The article highlights a critical operational crisis at Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), where the lack of a legal quorum has paralyzed its Plenary session. This stagnation directly threatens the full exercise of constitutional rights, as thousands of pending cases cannot be resolved. The situation underscores the fragility of institutional safeguards when political appointments fail, potentially eroding fundamental human rights for citizens seeking accountability and privacy. The inability to vote on resolved resources represents a severe regression in democratic guarantees. Without the minimum number of commissioners, the institution cannot effectively address grievances, leaving individuals without recourse when their information rights are violated. This impasse demonstrates how administrative vacancies can instantly compromise legal protections, emphasizing that institutional functionality is not merely bureaucratic but essential for upholding justice and public trust in governance mechanisms. This scenario is highly relevant to open data because the INAI serves as the primary guardian of transparency and data privacy in Mexico. Its paralysis risks creating a vacuum where government data remains inaccessible or personal information unprotected without effective oversight. Ensuring the institute’s proper composition is vital for maintaining the integrity of open data ecosystems, as a functioning regulator is necessary to enforce the rules that keep information systems transparent, accountable, and respectful of citizens' digital rights.
