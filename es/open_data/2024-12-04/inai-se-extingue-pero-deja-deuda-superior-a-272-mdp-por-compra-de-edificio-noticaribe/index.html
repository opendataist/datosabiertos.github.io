INAI se extingue, pero deja deuda superior a 272 mdp por compra de edificio - Noticaribe

The article highlights the complex financial legacy left by the impending extinction of Mexico’s INAI, specifically a substantial debt incurred for its headquarters. This case illustrates how long-term public sector commitments, such as financial leasing agreements, create enduring fiscal obligations that survive institutional changes. The transfer of these liabilities to absorbing entities like the Anti-Corruption Secretariat raises critical questions about accountability, continuity, and the true cost of restructuring state organs. Regarding open data, this scenario underscores the vital importance of preserving digital assets and records during institutional transitions. The reform mandates the transfer of platforms, electronic systems, and data registries to the new administrative body. If this transfer is not managed with transparency and technical rigor, there is a significant risk of losing access to historical information or compromising data integrity, which directly impacts the availability and reliability of public information for citizens and researchers. Ultimately, this situation serves as a cautionary tale for the governance of open data frameworks. It demonstrates that institutional stability is not just a legal matter but a technical and fiscal one, requiring clear protocols for asset and data handover. Without robust mechanisms to ensure the continuity of digital repositories and the transparency of underlying contracts, efforts to maintain open government can be undermined by administrative discontinuity and unresolved financial disputes.
