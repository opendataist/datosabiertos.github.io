Mintió Álvarez-Buylla contra el CIDE en la ‘mañanera’ y archivan el caso | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The article exposes a systemic failure in Mexico’s transparency and accountability mechanisms, highlighting how public officials can make unfounded corruption allegations without consequence. It details a case where the former director of Conahcyt publicly accused academics of corruption based on non-existent evidence, leading to harassment and data privacy violations. Despite a legal mandate for the Secretariat of the Civil Service (SFP) to sanction such abuses, the office closed the investigation, effectively granting impunity to the official. This precedent raises serious concerns about the erosion of administrative justice and the protection of individual rights against state-sponsored defamation. The narrative emphasizes the urgent implications for open data governance, as the upcoming dissolution of autonomous oversight bodies like the INAI threatens to centralize complaint processing within the executive branch. With the SFP now tasked with handling transparency requests under its new anti-corruption mandate, critics fear a conflict of interest that may further weaken checks and balances. The central conclusion is that without independent institutions to enforce transparency laws, citizens lack a reliable recourse against government abuses, undermining the foundational principles of open data and democratic oversight. Relevance to open data lies in the demonstration of how information integrity directly impacts civil liberties. When officials manipulate or fabricate data for political purposes without accountability, it corrupts the informational ecosystem essential for informed public debate. This case illustrates that mere access to information is insufficient; there must be robust, independent verification processes to prevent the weaponization of data against citizens, ensuring that transparency serves truth rather than political agendas.
