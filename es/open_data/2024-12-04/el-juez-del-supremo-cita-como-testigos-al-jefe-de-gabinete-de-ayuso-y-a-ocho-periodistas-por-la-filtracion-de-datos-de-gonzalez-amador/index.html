El juez del Supremo cita como testigos al jefe de Gabinete de Ayuso y a ocho periodistas por la filtración de datos de González Amador

The Supreme Court’s decision to investigate Álvaro García Ortiz for revealing secrets marks a critical juncture for open data and press freedom in Spain. By ordering testimonies from high-ranking officials and multiple journalists, the judiciary acknowledges the tension between state secrecy and the public’s right to information. This legal process highlights how the classification of data as "confidential" or "secret" can directly impact journalistic investigations and the transparency of public affairs. The core implication lies in the potential chilling effect on whistleblowing and investigative reporting. When legal mechanisms prioritize the protection of specific private or state interests over public interest disclosure, the ecosystem for open information contracts. The requirement for journalists and aides to testify underscores the risks faced by those who handle and disseminate sensitive information, suggesting that the boundaries of data sharing are being strictly policed by the highest judicial authorities. This case is vital for open data advocates because it challenges the balance between transparency and security in governance. It demonstrates how legal interpretations of secrecy can suppress the flow of information necessary for democratic accountability. Understanding this conflict is essential for defining future frameworks that protect open data practices while ensuring that legitimate security concerns do not unduly restrict the public’s access to meaningful information.
