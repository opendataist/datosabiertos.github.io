La criminalidad sube un 4,8% en 2022: Los robos de coches y los homicidios, los delitos que más crecen - MelillaHoy

Melilla experienced a notable increase in overall criminality in 2022, aligning with the upward trend observed across most Spanish regions. This rise marks a reversal from the significant drop seen in 2019, yet it highlights a divergent path compared to neighboring Ceuta, where crime rates decreased. The data underscores a complex local security landscape where traditional offenses are becoming more prevalent despite a broader long-term decline compared to pre-pandemic levels. A critical shift occurred in the nature of offenses, with a marked surge in serious conventional crimes such as vehicle thefts, homicides, and sexual aggressions. Conversely, drug trafficking and certain crimes against sexual freedom saw substantial declines. This juxtaposition suggests that while some illegal markets are contracting, violent and property-related crimes are intensifying, indicating a change in the specific behaviors threatening public safety rather than a uniform increase in all forms of illegality. This article is relevant to open_data because it demonstrates the value of transparent, standardized governmental statistics in identifying nuanced societal trends. By making granular crime data publicly available, authorities enable researchers and citizens to analyze specific security challenges, such as the rise in cybercrime or the disparity between neighboring territories. Open data thus facilitates evidence-based policy discussions and allows for a deeper understanding of how digital and physical security environments are evolving differently.
