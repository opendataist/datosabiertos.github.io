The article highlights how persistent inflation in Spain has fundamentally altered consumer behavior, driving a significant shift toward the secondary market as households seek essential savings. This economic pressure has made purchasing pre-owned goods, particularly technology, a strategic necessity rather than just a trend. The rising cost of living forces buyers to look beyond traditional retail channels, prioritizing affordability and value over newness. In the smartphone sector, this demand for second-hand devices has surged dramatically, contrasting sharply with declining sales of new units. Consumers are realizing substantial financial benefits by opting for used phones, which offer prices far below their retail counterparts. This trend illustrates a broader adaptation to economic constraints, where durability and price efficiency become the primary drivers of purchasing decisions, reshaping market dynamics across the telecommunications industry. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical role of digital platforms in making economic information accessible and actionable. Platforms like Milanuncios generate vast amounts of user-generated data that reveal real-time shifts in consumer sentiment and market trends. By analyzing such open datasets, researchers and policymakers can better understand how inflation impacts everyday choices, fostering transparency and enabling data-driven solutions to help consumers navigate financial challenges more effectively.
