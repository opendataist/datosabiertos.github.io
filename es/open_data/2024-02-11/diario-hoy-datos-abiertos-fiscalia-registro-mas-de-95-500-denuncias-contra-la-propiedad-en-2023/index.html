Diario HOY | Datos abiertos: Fiscalía registró más de 95.500 denuncias contra la propiedad en 2023

The article highlights how the Public Ministry’s Open Data program transforms raw crime statistics into accessible public information. By publishing detailed monthly records of property crimes, including theft and robbery, the initiative enables citizens and researchers to analyze trends beyond mere aggregate totals. This transparency is crucial for holding state institutions accountable and fostering trust in public security management. Analysis of the data reveals that Central, Alto Paraná, Asunción, and Itapúa are the primary zones for these offenses. The consistent publication of such granular details allows for a deeper understanding of spatial and temporal patterns in criminal behavior. This level of detail supports evidence-based policy making, helping authorities identify high-risk areas and allocate resources more effectively to combat property crimes. The relevance to open data lies in its role as a mechanism for governmental transparency and civic engagement. By making this information freely available, the program empowers civil society to monitor public safety outcomes independently. Ultimately, this approach promotes a culture of accountability, demonstrating how open data initiatives can bridge the gap between state administration and public scrutiny.
