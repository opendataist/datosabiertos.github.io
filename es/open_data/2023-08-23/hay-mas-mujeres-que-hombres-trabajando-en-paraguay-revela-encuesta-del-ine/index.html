Hay más mujeres que hombres trabajando en Paraguay, revela encuesta del INE

Recent labor data in Paraguay highlights a significant recovery in employment, driven primarily by a substantial increase in the active female workforce. This shift is particularly evident in rural areas, signaling a transformation in how women participate in the economy, with growth observed across both urban and rural sectors, including small businesses and domestic services. The gender balance among the unemployed has also shifted, reflecting broader changes in labor market dynamics. Beyond employment, upcoming national census results reveal profound demographic changes, notably a sharp decline in fertility rates over the last seven decades. This reduction is linked to women’s increased autonomy, including delayed marriage and childbearing, higher educational attainment, and greater access to family planning. Consequently, household structures are evolving, with a rise in single-person and non-traditional family units, challenging the dominance of the nuclear family model. These findings are crucial for open data initiatives as they demonstrate how high-quality, accessible statistical information supports the analysis of complex social transitions. By making such detailed demographic and labor insights publicly available, governments and researchers can better understand societal shifts. This transparency empowers evidence-based policy-making and fosters public engagement with critical issues regarding gender equality and changing family structures in modern societies.
