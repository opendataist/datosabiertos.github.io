Baleares recibió en 2022 a 22.676 personas más de la que se fueron

Spain recorded its highest migration surplus in a decade, driven significantly by strong inflows from Latin America and Ukraine. This net positive movement highlights a demographic shift where major urban centers like Madrid and Catalonia attract large international populations, overshadowing internal departures and underscoring the role of megacities as primary economic magnets for global mobility. Simultaneously, internal mobility reveals a trend toward stabilization, with nearly ninety-seven percent of residents remaining in their original municipalities throughout the year. While smaller regions often experience net population losses to these hubs, this data illustrates a complex geographic redistribution of labor and residence, challenging simplistic narratives of rural decline by showing nuanced flows between specific provinces and autonomous communities. This information is vital for open data initiatives because it transforms raw census figures into actionable insights for public policy. By providing granular, transparent metrics on both international and domestic migration, open datasets enable researchers and governments to optimize urban planning, allocate resources efficiently, and understand the evolving social fabric of the country based on accessible, standardized statistics.
