Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The World Bank reports a moderation in global remittance flows to low- and middle-income countries, highlighting divergent regional trends. Latin America and the Caribbean maintained growth driven by a strong US labor market, whereas regions like the Middle East and North Africa saw significant declines. This disparity underscores the critical link between migration destinations' economic health and funds reaching origin countries, affecting household resilience and development financing. These fluctuations reveal vulnerabilities in development funding, as economic slowdowns in host nations or policy changes can abruptly reduce financial support for vulnerable populations. The reliance on specific transit corridors and destination labor markets creates systemic risks, demonstrating how macroeconomic shifts in high-income countries directly impact the financial stability of families in the Global South, potentially exacerbating poverty if inflows contract unexpectedly. This report is relevant to open data because it emphasizes the necessity of transparent, real-time monitoring of financial transfers to inform policy. Open access to such granular migration and finance data allows researchers and policymakers to detect emerging trends, assess the impact of geopolitical events, and design more effective social protection strategies, ensuring that data-driven insights can mitigate the adverse effects of volatile remittance flows.
