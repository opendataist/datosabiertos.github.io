Consumo en caída libre: los gastos en recreación bajaron un 43% y la compra de indumentaria un 27%

The recent data reveals a profound deterioration in household consumption, driven by recession and the loss of purchasing power. Although inflation has decelerated, rising energy costs and stagnant nominal wages continue to erode families' ability to buy goods, particularly in leisure and clothing sectors. This negative trend represents the sharpest decline since early 2021, signaling deep structural weaknesses in the economy. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the critical need for transparent, timely, and granular economic indicators. By monitoring such detailed metrics, citizens and researchers can better understand the real impact of macroeconomic policies on vulnerable populations. Open access to this information fosters accountability, allowing for independent verification of official reports and enabling more informed public debate regarding social welfare and economic recovery strategies. Ultimately, the report highlights that current transfers and benefits are insufficient to restore purchasing power, suggesting that recovery will be slow without significant wage growth or structural adjustments. The persistence of declining activity across most sectors, except transport, confirms that the economic downturn is broad-based. These insights emphasize why open data is essential for tracking the long-term effects of inflation and subsidy cuts, providing a necessary foundation for evidence-based policy-making and social advocacy.
