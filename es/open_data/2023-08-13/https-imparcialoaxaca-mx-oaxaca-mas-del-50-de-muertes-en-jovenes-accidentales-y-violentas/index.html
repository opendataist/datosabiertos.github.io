The data reveals a critical public health crisis in Oaxaca where preventable accidents and violence are the leading causes of death among youth. This trend highlights a systemic failure in protective measures, suggesting that current strategies are insufficient to safeguard young people, particularly males aged twenty-five to twenty-nine, who face disproportionately higher risks due to aggressive behaviors and traffic incidents. A significant gender disparity exacerbates the situation, with young men facing mortality rates driven by intentional harm and transportation accidents, while women primarily succumb to traffic incidents and obstetric issues. Additionally, suicide rates among youth remain a pressing concern, indicating a deepening mental health emergency. These patterns underscore that the vulnerabilities of this demographic are not random but are structured by social roles and limited access to preventative care and health services. This article is vital to open_data because it demonstrates how transparent statistical records can expose hidden social inequalities and guide effective policy interventions. By making mortality causes and demographic breakdowns publicly available, stakeholders can identify specific risk factors and allocate resources more efficiently. Open data transforms raw statistics into actionable insights, enabling governments and organizations to address the root causes of preventable deaths and create safer environments for the next generation.
