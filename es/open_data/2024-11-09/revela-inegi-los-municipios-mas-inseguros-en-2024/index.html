Revela INEGI los municipios más inseguros en 2024

INEGI data reveals Tapachula leads Mexico in perceived insecurity, reflecting a broader national crisis where a majority of adults feel unsafe despite a slight yearly decline. This high-profile metric underscores the vital role of open data in transforming abstract public safety concerns into measurable, localized realities. By making such granular statistics accessible, governments and researchers can pinpoint specific geographic areas requiring urgent intervention rather than relying on vague generalizations. The availability of these official surveys enables transparent accountability and targeted policy-making. Open data platforms allow civil society to monitor trends, demand resources for the most affected regions, and evaluate the effectiveness of security strategies over time. Without public access to this detailed information, meaningful democratic oversight of public safety measures would be significantly hindered. Ultimately, this case illustrates how open data democratizes understanding of complex social issues. It empowers citizens and analysts to engage with factual evidence, fostering informed public discourse. The release of ENSU results ensures that the persistence of insecurity remains visible and actionable, proving that transparent data is essential for addressing urgent societal challenges effectively.
