La mortalidad en CLM subió un 5% en 2022 respecto a 2019 y la causa que más creció fueron las enfermedades hipertensivas

The analysis of Spain’s 2022 mortality statistics reveals a significant post-pandemic shift in public health patterns, dominated by a resurgence in respiratory diseases and a sharp rise in cardiovascular conditions. Despite a decrease in deaths directly attributed to COVID-19 compared to the previous year, respiratory illnesses emerged as the third leading cause of death, highlighting lingering vulnerabilities in the population. This trend underscores the complex interplay between residual viral impacts and the return of other endemic health threats. A critical driver of these changes was the extreme heat wave observed between May and August, which disproportionately affected older adults and exacerbated pre-existing chronic conditions. Mortality spikes in this period were closely linked to heat-related factors and the worsening of hypertension and diabetes, illustrating how environmental stressors can intensify long-term health vulnerabilities. This correlation emphasizes the growing urgency of addressing climate-sensitive health risks within national medical strategies. This data is highly relevant to the open data community because it exemplifies the value of granular, disaggregated statistics in identifying emerging health crises. By making detailed mortality records by cause, gender, region, and season publicly available, institutions enable researchers and policymakers to detect subtle epidemiological trends that aggregate figures might obscure. Such transparency facilitates evidence-based decision-making, allowing for more targeted interventions in public health and climate adaptation planning.
