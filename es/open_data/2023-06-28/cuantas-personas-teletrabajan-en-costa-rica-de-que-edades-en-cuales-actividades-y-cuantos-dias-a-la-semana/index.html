Cuántas personas teletrabajan en Costa Rica, de qué edades, en cuáles actividades y cuántos días a la semana

The article highlights the significant gap between official government statistics and on-the-ground realities regarding Costa Rican businesses and labor trends. While the Ministry of Economy records over twenty thousand registered micro and small enterprises, independent research from the University of Costa Rica indicates a declining trend in telework adoption among entrepreneurs. This discrepancy underscores the importance of cross-referencing administrative data with independent academic surveys to understand actual economic behaviors. Detailed census data reveals that telework remains a niche practice, limited to a small fraction of the salaried population and heavily concentrated among young, highly educated professionals in specific sectors. The findings demonstrate that while formal employment data exists, its interpretation requires context. Relying solely on aggregate numbers can obscure the specific demographics and conditions driving current labor dynamics, leading to potential misinformed policy decisions if these nuances are ignored. This content is critical for open data initiatives because it exposes the necessity of data triangulation and transparency in public reporting. It illustrates how diverse data sources—from government registries to university surveys and private sector anecdotes—must be integrated to provide a holistic view of the economy. For open data advocates, this serves as a reminder that accessibility to raw data must be paired with the ability to analyze and compare multiple datasets to ensure accuracy and effective public policy.
