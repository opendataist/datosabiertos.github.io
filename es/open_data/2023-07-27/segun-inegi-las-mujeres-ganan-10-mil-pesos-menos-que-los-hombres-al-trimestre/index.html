Según Inegi, las mujeres ganan 10 mil pesos menos que los hombres al trimestre

The article highlights significant income inequalities in Mexico, revealing that earnings are heavily skewed by gender, family size, and education levels. Women with large families and individuals with lower schooling attainment face substantial financial disadvantages, while those with postgraduate degrees and male earners with children secure significantly higher wages. This structural disparity demonstrates how socioeconomic factors compound to limit economic mobility for vulnerable demographics. Beyond individual characteristics, the data shows a stark wealth gap between income deciles, where the richest households earn vastly more than the poorest, despite a slight narrowing of this gap over recent years. The burden is disproportionately carried by the majority of the population, who occupy the lowest deciles and possess a fraction of the income held by the elite minority. This concentration of wealth indicates systemic issues in how national resources are distributed among different socioeconomic strata. This report is crucial for open data advocates as it exemplifies the necessity of accessible, granular statistical information to understand and address social inequities. By making such detailed socioeconomic metrics public, policymakers and researchers can identify specific at-risk groups, such as indigenous speakers or people with disabilities, and design targeted interventions. Transparent data serves as the foundation for accountability and evidence-based strategies to reduce inequality.
