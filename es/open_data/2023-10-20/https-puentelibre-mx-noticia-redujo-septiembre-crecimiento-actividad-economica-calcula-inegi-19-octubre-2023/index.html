The Inegi reports a modest 3% annual economic growth for September, driven by industrial and service sectors. This figure indicates a slowdown compared to August, suggesting recent economic cooling rather than sustained expansion. This data serves as an early proxy for GDP, offering timely insights before final reports are released. By tracking these real-time indicators, policymakers and analysts can adjust strategies based on current economic trajectories rather than waiting for lagging statistics. Open data is crucial here, as free access to such granular, frequent economic metrics allows for transparent, informed decision-making. It empowers citizens and businesses to monitor national health objectively, fostering accountability and reducing information asymmetry in financial markets.
