The National Statistics Institute summoned departmental governors to resolve mapping discrepancies in a border community. Chuquisaca officials accused the INE of misplacing homes into Tarija, allegedly to balance population counts, while Tarija recorded fewer houses in Chuquisaca. This highlights significant data quality issues in official census updates. Such inaccuracies undermine the reliability of public statistics, which are foundational for resource distribution and policy planning. Correcting these errors is essential for maintaining trust in government data and ensuring fair administrative boundaries. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how errors in geographic information systems can impact social equity. Transparent and accurate open data standards are crucial to prevent such political manipulations of demographic records, ensuring that public information remains objective and useful for citizens and researchers.
