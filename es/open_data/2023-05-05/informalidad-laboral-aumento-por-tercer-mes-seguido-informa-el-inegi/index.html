Informalidad laboral aumentó por tercer mes seguido, informa el Inegi

The article reveals a persistent rise in Mexico’s informal employment, indicating that over half of the workforce lacks formal labor protections. This trend highlights a structural vulnerability in the economy where a majority of workers are excluded from social security and legal stability. For open data initiatives, this statistic is crucial as it underscores the urgent need to monitor and expose the gaps between formal labor laws and actual worker conditions. Transparent, accessible data allows policymakers and civil society to track these informal sectors accurately and advocate for better regulations. Simultaneously, the report indicates a decline in unemployment, suggesting that the labor market is absorbing workers primarily through informal channels rather than creating secure, formal jobs. This paradox presents a significant challenge for data transparency, as traditional economic indicators might mask the precarious nature of employment growth. Open data frameworks must go beyond simple headcounts to analyze job quality, ensuring that reduced unemployment figures do not obscure the lack of decent work opportunities. Furthermore, the distribution of income reveals that a vast majority of employees earn minimal wages, with very few achieving higher earnings. This disparity emphasizes the importance of open datasets that capture income levels alongside employment status. By making this granular data publicly available, stakeholders can identify economic inequalities more effectively, driving evidence-based reforms aimed at improving living standards and reducing the prevalence of low-wage, informal labor in the country.
