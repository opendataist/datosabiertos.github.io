Sheinbaum descarta propuesta del INAI para evitar su desaparición | Revista Punto de Vista

The potential dissolution of the INAI by the current administration highlights a critical juncture for public transparency in Mexico. By delegating the agency’s fate to legislative bodies and citing internal corruption, the executive branch signals a shift away from autonomous oversight. This move threatens the institutional independence that has historically ensured accountability, creating uncertainty about the future of data protection and citizen access to information. This situation underscores the vital role of independent bodies in maintaining open data ecosystems. Without such safeguards, the risk of opaque governance increases, potentially undermining public trust and the integrity of shared governmental information. The proposed elimination of multiple autonomous organs suggests a systemic restructuring that could significantly weaken mechanisms designed to enforce transparency and protect personal data rights. The relevance to open data lies in the precedent this sets for institutional autonomy. If these organizations are dismantled, the ability to independently audit and release public information diminishes, affecting societal oversight. Therefore, the debate is not merely political but fundamental to preserving the infrastructure that supports transparent, accessible, and reliable data for citizens and developers alike.
