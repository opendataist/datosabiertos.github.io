La violencia contra los policías cuesta al Estado 400 millones de euros

The study reveals that violence against police forces imposes a massive financial burden on public administrations, primarily driven by the high costs of criminal investigations. This significant expenditure highlights the systemic inefficiency in how resources are allocated during the justice process, suggesting that current frameworks prioritize procedural costs over victim support or deterrence. Beyond direct fiscal impacts, the report underscores broader operational consequences, including staff shortages and increased workloads resulting from injuries and deaths. The inability to fully quantify losses from permanent disabilities or missing personnel exposes critical gaps in public data collection. These omissions prevent a comprehensive understanding of the true societal cost, emphasizing the need for more granular and transparent reporting on security incidents and their long-term effects on law enforcement capacity. This data is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates how aggregated, public-sector financial records can expose hidden societal costs and inform policy debates. By making such economic and operational metrics accessible, stakeholders can demand greater accountability and more effective legal strategies, such as direct administrative participation in prosecutions, to better protect public servants and optimize judicial resources.
