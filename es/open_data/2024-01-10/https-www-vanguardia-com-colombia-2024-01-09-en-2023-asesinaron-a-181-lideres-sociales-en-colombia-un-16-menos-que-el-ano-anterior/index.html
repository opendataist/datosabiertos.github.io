ES EN

Colombia recorded a decline in the murders of social leaders and human rights defenders in 2023, yet the continued high rate of violence remains an unacceptable threat to fundamental rights and democratic stability. Indigenous and community leaders face the greatest risks, highlighting systemic vulnerabilities that require urgent, targeted state intervention to prevent further silencing of courageous voices. This ongoing crisis demonstrates the critical necessity of open data in monitoring human rights abuses and holding institutions accountable. By making detailed victimization statistics publicly available, transparency enables civil society and policymakers to identify trends, assess the effectiveness of protection measures, and advocate for evidence-based public policies. Ultimately, the consistent publication of such data serves as a vital tool for detecting patterns of illegal armed activity and illicit economies. Without reliable, accessible information, it is impossible to design focused strategies for prevention or to measure progress toward a safer environment for defenders, making data openness essential for protecting democracy and justice in conflict-affected regions.
