Pide INAI a Sedena transparentar operación Fuerza de Tarea Marte

The National Institute for Transparency has ordered the Mexican Secretariat of National Defense to disclose detailed records regarding the commanders and units involved in Operation Task Force Mars. This military campaign, executed between 1987 and 1996 to eradicate illicit crops in four states, was initially denied on the grounds that no records existed. However, the oversight body determined that the military failed to conduct a comprehensive search across all relevant administrative archives, violating transparency laws and the principle of maximum publicity. The decision emphasizes that access to this historical data is crucial for societal oversight and historical memory. By revealing operational details, the public can analyze the effectiveness and legitimacy of security policies. The ruling links past military actions to contemporary issues, such as current lethality indices in law enforcement encounters. Without transparent historical data, citizens cannot accurately assess the evolution of security strategies or identify potential patterns of illegitimate force use by state agents. This case is significant for open data because it establishes that institutional silence or claims of missing records do not override the duty to disclose public information. It reinforces the necessity of systematic data preservation and availability in government institutions. The ruling serves as a precedent for treating historical military documentation as open data, ensuring that transparency obligations extend beyond current operations to include past activities. This enables rigorous public scrutiny of state actions and supports a culture of accountability where historical records remain accessible for long-term analysis and democratic oversight.
