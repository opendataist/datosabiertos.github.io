Sustituir informes... - La Capital

The article highlights a profound crisis of credibility facing Mexican political institutions, driven by persistent voter skepticism toward legislators and municipal authorities. Despite high approval ratings for the presidency, the general distrust in elected officials remains pervasive, stemming largely from unfulfilled campaign promises and a perceived disconnect between political discourse and tangible results. This erosion of trust necessitates a fundamental shift in how government entities communicate their achievements and plans to the public. The core implication is that traditional, opaque reporting formats exacerbate the alienation between the state and citizens. To reverse this trend, officials must adopt transparent, accessible, and participatory communication strategies that explain policy impacts clearly. By fostering genuine understanding and inviting public opinion on complex issues, such as judicial reforms, governments can begin to rebuild the social contract and demonstrate accountability, moving from mere declaration to demonstrable action. This narrative is critical for open data advocates because it underscores the necessity of transparency in restoring institutional legitimacy. Open data serves as a foundational tool for this accountability; when governments provide clear, understandable, and accessible information about their activities and decisions, they empower citizens to verify claims and engage meaningfully. Ultimately, the push for better data practices is not just a technical improvement but a political imperative to bridge the trust gap and ensure that governance serves the public interest effectively.
