Privacidad de los datos personales todo un reto para las empresas

The article highlights the escalating challenge of personal data privacy in Colombia, driven by rapid digitalization and increased internet penetration. As consumers submit more data for services and financial products, the expectations for security and proper handling have intensified. The Superintendence of Industry and Commerce has responded to thousands of complaints by launching numerous investigations, underscoring the critical need for organizations to strictly adhere to national regulations like Law 1581. This regulatory environment mandates transparency, requiring companies to obtain informed consent and ensure individuals can access, rectify, or revoke their data. A key implication is the principle of "Accountability," which obligates data controllers to demonstrate that they have implemented appropriate security measures. This is particularly relevant for financial institutions, where the "financial habeas data" regulates the use of personal information for credit risk assessment. Furthermore, the lack of an adequacy decision from the European Union means Colombian entities processing data from EU citizens must exercise extra caution to avoid legal sanctions and ensure cross-border compliance. This topic is highly relevant to open data because it establishes the legal boundaries and ethical responsibilities surrounding information management. While open data initiatives promote transparency and innovation, they must coexist with robust personal data protection frameworks. Understanding these regulatory constraints is essential for developers and policymakers to design systems that respect individual rights while fostering a secure digital economy, ensuring that data openness does not come at the cost of privacy violations.
