The 2022 Encuesta de Condiciones de Vida reveals a structural shift in Spanish healthcare usage, characterized by increased reliance on specialized and dental care alongside a decline in primary care visits. This trend is compounded by significant waiting times in the public primary care system, suggesting users are bypassing family doctors due to access barriers and resorting to other levels of the health network. A critical implication of these data is the persistent socioeconomic inequality in healthcare access. Individuals with lower incomes visit specialists and dentists far less frequently than wealthier counterparts and bear a disproportionately heavy financial burden for medications and dental treatments. This disparity highlights how economic status directly influences both the utilization of advanced medical services and the out-of-pocket costs required to maintain health. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how standardized, large-scale statistical indicators can uncover systemic inefficiencies and social inequities within public services. By making such granular data publicly available, institutions enable researchers and policymakers to validate citizen experiences with real-world evidence, fostering transparency and informing strategies to optimize resource allocation and reduce health disparities.
