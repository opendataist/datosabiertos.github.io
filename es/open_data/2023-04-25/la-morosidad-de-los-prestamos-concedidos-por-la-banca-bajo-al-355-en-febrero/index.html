La morosidad de los préstamos concedidos por la banca bajó al 3,55% en febrero

Spain’s banking sector shows a slight improvement in loan quality, with the bad debt rate dipping to 3.55% in February. This decline, alongside a reduction in total loan volumes and provisions, signals a stabilizing financial environment compared to the previous year’s higher distress levels. The data highlights a divergence between traditional deposit-taking banks and specialized credit establishments, which maintain higher non-performing ratios. Notably, methodological changes affecting how certain financial entities are classified impact the overall statistics, revealing that underlying risk metrics remain slightly higher when excluding these entities. This information is crucial for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, accessible financial metrics for public scrutiny. By providing granular, standardized data on credit health, authorities enable researchers and citizens to analyze economic resilience, fostering accountability and informed decision-making within the financial ecosystem.
