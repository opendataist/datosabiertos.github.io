CCOO alerta de que casi la mitad de la población de C-LM tienen alguna dificultad para llegar a fin de mes

The article highlights a significant deterioration in living conditions in Castilla-La Mancha, driven by soaring energy and food prices that outpaced wage growth. Despite a marginal decrease in the overall poverty risk rate, severe material deprivation has risen sharply, leaving hundreds of thousands unable to maintain adequate home temperatures or afford nutritious meals. This trend underscores how rising costs are stripping household income, pushing more residents into extreme precarity even as average household income shows slight nominal gains. These findings are critical to open data because they demonstrate the necessity of high-quality, granular statistical indicators to reveal underlying social disparities that aggregate figures might obscure. By making detailed datasets like the Household Conditions Survey accessible, policymakers and researchers can accurately track the impact of economic shocks on vulnerable populations. Transparency in such data allows for a deeper understanding of how inflation disproportionately affects basic needs, moving beyond simple income metrics to assess real-world quality of life. The report serves as a vital tool for advocating evidence-based social policies, emphasizing that official statistics must reflect the lived reality of citizens to drive effective change. It illustrates how open, transparent data can empower civil society and unions to hold institutions accountable and demand targeted interventions, such as controlling corporate profits and improving wages. Ultimately, the accessibility and interpretability of this data are essential for ensuring that economic policies address the root causes of inequality and effectively support those at risk of exclusion.
