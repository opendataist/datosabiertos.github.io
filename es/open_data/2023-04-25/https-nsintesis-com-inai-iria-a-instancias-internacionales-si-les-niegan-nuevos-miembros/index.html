The INAI faces a critical operational challenge due to the lack of consensus in appointing commissioners, forcing the remaining five to seek a suspension from the Supreme Court to maintain quorum. If denied, the institute threatens to escalate the matter to the Inter-American Court of Human Rights. This bold stance underscores the institution's determination to safeguard transparency rights regardless of political gridlock. The article highlights the resilience of democratic institutions when faced with legislative inaction. Despite the absence of three commissioners, the INAI continues to process citizen complaints and promote privacy standards nationwide. This demonstrates that the mandate for transparency is resilient, relying on the commitment of current officials and civil society rather than just formal appointments. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the fragility of the legal frameworks that guarantee data access. The potential international legal battle emphasizes how political disputes can directly impact the enforcement of transparency laws. It serves as a stark reminder that open data ecosystems depend on stable, fully functioning oversight bodies to protect citizens' rights to information and ensure accountability in government operations.
