Save the Children: una de cada cinco familias en Puerto Príncipe está al borde del hambre

The article highlights a severe humanitarian crisis in Haiti, where gang violence and shrinking aid have pushed a significant portion of the population toward acute hunger. This deterioration directly challenges the effective implementation of open data in disaster response, as the lack of transparent, real-time information complicates the coordination of essential services and hampers the ability to allocate resources efficiently to the most vulnerable areas. Open data systems are crucial for tracking the rapid expansion of food insecurity and monitoring the impact of security blockades on supply chains. Without accessible, standardized datasets, humanitarian organizations cannot accurately assess needs or verify the reach of interventions, leading to potential gaps in protection for children and families facing starvation. The relevance to open data lies in the urgent need for transparent, publicly available metrics to drive international funding and accountability. By making crisis data open and accessible, stakeholders can better understand the scale of the emergency, advocate for policy changes, and ensure that aid reaches those who need it most, thereby mitigating the human cost of the escalating violence and political instability.
