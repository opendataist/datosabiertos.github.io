La ley de transparencia que prevé eliminar la Oficina Anticorrupción, a debate este martes en el Parlament balear

The proposed transparency law in the Balearic Islands aims to abolish the anti-corruption office, a move defended by the PP as eliminating an inefficient and redundant body. This legislative initiative has sparked intense political debate, with the PP arguing that the office was politically motivated and ineffective, while critics warn that its dissolution removes crucial checks on power. The most significant implication for open data and governance is the restructuring of complaint mechanisms. The new law transfers the processing of grievances to a department under the Presidency, effectively turning the government into both the accused and the judge. Critics from the PSIB and MÉS warn this increases impunity by dismantling the existing sanctioning regime, raising concerns about accountability and the independence of oversight processes. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the tension between political convenience and institutional transparency. The closure of an independent oversight body and the consolidation of complaint handling within the executive branch threaten the objectivity required for effective data monitoring. It underscores how legislative changes can directly impact the integrity, accessibility, and trustworthiness of public information, a cornerstone of effective open government.
