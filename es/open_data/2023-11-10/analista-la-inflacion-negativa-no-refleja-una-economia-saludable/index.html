Analista: 'La inflación negativa no refleja una economía saludable'

The article reveals that Ecuador experienced negative monthly inflation in October, signaling a contracting economy rather than a healthy deflationary trend. Experts emphasize that this decline stems from reduced consumer purchasing power and lower incomes, exacerbated by a growing informal labor sector. This contraction aligns with World Bank projections, which have significantly downgraded GDP growth forecasts for the current and upcoming years, indicating widespread economic stagnation. While specific food items like onions and lemons saw price drops due to increased local supply or previous spikes, these isolated declines do not mitigate the broader structural issues. The narrative highlights that negative inflation often reflects a lack of demand and economic weakness, where individuals are buying less due to financial constraints. This situation underscores the fragility of household budgets, as basic food baskets consume a significant portion of limited monthly incomes, even when average calculations suggest sufficiency for dual-income households. This report is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how statistical indicators, such as those published by the INEC, provide critical insights into real-world economic health. By making inflation data, labor statistics, and family basket costs publicly available, authorities and citizens can track these trends to advocate for transparent policy responses. Open access to this information allows for a deeper understanding of how macroeconomic shifts directly impact daily life, fostering informed public discourse and accountability regarding the government's economic management strategies.
