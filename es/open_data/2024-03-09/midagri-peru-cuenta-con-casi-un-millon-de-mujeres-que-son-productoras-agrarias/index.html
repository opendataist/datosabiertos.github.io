MIDAGRI: Perú cuenta con casi un millón de mujeres que son productoras agrarias

This article highlights the critical role of women in Peru’s agricultural sector, revealing they comprise 45% of registered producers. Despite often managing smaller plots, women are central to family farming, which generates the vast majority of food for national markets. This underscores their indispensable contribution to the country’s food security and demonstrates how gender-inclusive data tracking is vital for understanding economic resilience. The data indicates a significant portion of women operate within subsistence agriculture, possessing fewer resources than their male counterparts. This disparity suggests that while women’s labor is foundational to national sustenance, they face structural inequalities in land access and organization. Recognizing these patterns through open data allows policymakers to identify gaps and design targeted support systems for marginalized agricultural communities. Open data initiatives, such as the official producer registry, are essential for making this hidden workforce visible. By publishing detailed demographic and production statistics, the government enables transparency and accountability. Such information empowers civil society and researchers to advocate for equitable policies, ensuring that contributions of female farmers are not only recognized but also supported with appropriate resources and recognition.
