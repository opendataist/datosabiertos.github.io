Ningún país brinda igualdad de oportunidades laborales ni legales a las mujeres: Banco Mundial

The World Bank report highlights a persistent global disparity where women enjoy, on average, only about two-thirds of the legal protections afforded to men. This inequality is exacerbated by a significant implementation gap; despite theoretical legal progress, fewer than 40% of the necessary enforcement mechanisms are actually in place. Consequently, legislative advances often fail to translate into tangible rights, leaving women vulnerable to discriminatory practices in pay, childcare, and protection against violence, which severely limits their economic participation. The economic implications of this gender gap are profound, as closing it could potentially boost global GDP by more than 20% within a decade. However, the study reveals that reforms have stalled, with women facing substantial barriers in entrepreneurship and public procurement. The disparity extends through the entire lifecycle, resulting in lower lifetime earnings and inadequate pension provisions due to earlier retirement ages and career interruptions, thereby creating long-term financial insecurity for women despite their longer life expectancy. This article is crucial for open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and comparable datasets to monitor legal reforms and their real-world impact. By exposing the disconnect between enacted laws and actual enforcement across 190 economies, the report illustrates how open data can drive accountability. It emphasizes that rigorous data collection is essential to identify specific gaps in implementation, measure the effectiveness of policies, and advocate for evidence-based reforms that truly empower women economically.
