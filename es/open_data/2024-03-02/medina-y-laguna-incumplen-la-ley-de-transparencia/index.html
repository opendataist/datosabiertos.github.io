Medina y Laguna «incumplen» la Ley de Transparencia

Two Spanish municipalities, Medina del Campo and Laguna de Duero, are failing to comply with national transparency laws by withholding crucial financial data from their public portals. Medina del Campo presents a more severe situation, as it lacks an approved budget for the current year and its website offers no updated information on expenditure execution, relying instead on data from years prior. This opacity has prompted the opposition to file legal complaints, arguing that the lack of accessible information prevents effective oversight and leaves citizens and councilors working in the dark regarding how public funds are utilized. The case of Laguna de Duero, while slightly less critical, still exhibits significant gaps in disclosure. Their transparency portal fails to report recent execution degrees, executive salaries, or current subcontracting details, with some records dating back decades. Despite including some current contract lists, the absence of key fiscal and personnel data undermines public accountability. Like Medina, opposition members in Laguna have also sought legal recourse, highlighting systemic issues where timely information is denied or provided out of legal deadlines, thereby obstructing their ability to perform their oversight duties effectively. This article is highly relevant to open data advocates as it illustrates the tangible consequences of non-compliance with transparency standards. It demonstrates that merely having a website is insufficient; the data must be current, comprehensive, and easily accessible to fulfill the spirit of open government. The legal actions taken by opposition groups underscore that transparency is not just a bureaucratic formality but a fundamental requirement for democratic accountability, highlighting the urgent need for robust, real-time data publication by local administrations.
