Ocho de cada 10 peruanos en situación de pobreza no cuentan con refrigeradora

The article highlights how rising poverty in Peru significantly restricts access to essential appliances like refrigerators, gas stoves, and computers, severely impacting food security, health, and educational continuity for vulnerable populations. This data underscores the critical gap in basic infrastructure that hinders quality of life and social mobility. For open data initiatives, this situation illustrates why granular socioeconomic indicators are vital. Transparent statistics on asset ownership and income levels reveal systemic inequalities that traditional metrics might overlook, allowing policymakers and NGOs to identify specific needs rather than relying on broad assumptions. Understanding these limitations enables targeted interventions, such as supporting circular economy models that provide affordable, guaranteed goods to low-income families. By leveraging data to map these gaps, stakeholders can better allocate resources and design programs that address the root causes of poverty, ensuring more effective and equitable social support systems.
