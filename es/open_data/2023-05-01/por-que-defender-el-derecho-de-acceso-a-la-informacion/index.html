¿Por qué defender el derecho de acceso a la información?

The article argues that defending the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection is crucial because it safeguards the fundamental democratic principle that public information belongs to society, not the state. The author contends that while transparency fosters a participatory and accountable citizenry, state actors often prefer opacity to avoid scrutiny and debate. Dissolving this institute represents a direct attack on citizens' rights and an attempt to shield government actions from necessary public oversight and fiscalization. By illustrating extensive personal experience with access to information requests, the text highlights how this mechanism exposes systemic failures and hidden injustices that would otherwise remain concealed. Without independent tools to demand data, critical issues such as the misclassification of femicides, inadequate mental health resources, and environmental negligence cannot be rigorously investigated. These examples demonstrate that transparency is not merely bureaucratic but essential for revealing administrative disorders, fiscal mismanagement, and human rights violations within various government sectors. This piece is highly relevant to open data as it validates the practical necessity of accessible public datasets for social accountability and journalistic investigation. It underscores that open data initiatives are not just technical exercises but vital democratic instruments that empower society to challenge authority and demand justice. The narrative emphasizes that without robust mechanisms to ensure data availability, the public remains ignorant of significant governance failures, making the protection of open data frameworks a prerequisite for a healthy, informed democracy.
