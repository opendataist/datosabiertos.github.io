El Indec postergó la difusión del dato de inflación por las elecciones en cinco provincias

The National Institute of Statistics and Census reversed its decision to delay the April Consumer Price Index publication, restoring the original schedule following public criticism. This action underscores the critical need for transparency in official statistics, especially when political events like elections threaten to obscure timely economic data release. The initial postponement stemmed from conflicts between statistical reporting and electoral blackout periods, particularly in regions where inflation triggers automatic salary adjustments. Rescheduling these reports ensures that citizens and markets access accurate price information without being muffled by political restrictions, maintaining the integrity of public records. This case highlights the importance of safeguarding open data protocols against political interference. By publishing data as scheduled, the institute preserves trust in official metrics, which are essential for economic analysis and policy-making, demonstrating that statistical independence must prevail over electoral convenience.
