Oruro lideró el crecimiento regional en 2023 con 7,84% y Tarija se contrajo

The article highlights a stark economic divergence among Bolivia’s departments in 2023, revealing a nation in transition. While Oruro emerged as a surprising leader in regional growth, other key areas experienced stagnation or contraction. Notably, Tarija faced a severe crisis, marking its only negative growth year in recent history and suffering a significant decline in its economic weight. This contrast underscores a broader cooling of the national economy, where major hubs like Santa Cruz, La Paz, and Cochabamba recorded lower growth rates than the previous year, signaling that the country’s overall economic momentum is weakening rather than expanding robustly. The data exposes deep structural imbalances, particularly regarding dependency on natural resources. Tarija’s economy, heavily reliant on hydrocarbons, has seen its national share plummet, illustrating the risks of such specialization. Meanwhile, regions like Beni and Santa Cruz have expanded their relative importance, with the latter remaining the dominant economic engine. These shifts demonstrate that while nominal GDP increased, the underlying reality is one of uneven development. The disparity between nominal figures and real growth suggests that economic improvements are not broadly distributed, leaving significant portions of the population and specific regions behind in the national wealth accumulation process. This content is relevant to open_data because it exemplifies the critical need for transparent, accessible, and high-quality statistical information to monitor regional disparities and policy effectiveness. By making departmental PIB and growth metrics publicly available, institutions like the INE enable economists, journalists, and citizens to critically analyze national trends beyond aggregate numbers. Open data facilitates the identification of emerging crises, such as Tarija’s contraction, allowing for timely academic and civic scrutiny. Furthermore, it empowers stakeholders to assess government projections against actual outcomes, fostering accountability and informed decision-making regarding fiscal health and social stability in a complex, multi-regional economy.
