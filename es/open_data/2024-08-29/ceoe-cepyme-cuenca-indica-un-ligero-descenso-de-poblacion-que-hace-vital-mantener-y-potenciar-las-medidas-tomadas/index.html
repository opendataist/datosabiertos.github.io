CEOE CEPYME Cuenca indica un ligero descenso de población que hace vital mantener y potenciar las medidas tomadas

Cuenca experiences a slight, albeit provisional, population decline, reinforcing the urgent need to intensify anti-depopulation efforts rather than relying on short-term fixes. The local business federation emphasizes that addressing this issue requires a long-term strategy focused on generating employment through new enterprises to retain residents, as the current demographic trend indicates that the crisis remains unresolved despite recent policy measures. The demographic profile reveals a significantly aged population with a disproportionately small youth cohort, highlighting structural vulnerabilities that hinder sustainable growth. This aging trend and the low number of children suggest a lack of future workforce and family stability, necessitating administrative cooperation to implement programs that attract and keep people in the province for the long haul. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how raw statistical indicators from official institutes like the INE are interpreted by civil society to drive political and economic action. It demonstrates the critical role of transparency in identifying social challenges and the importance of contextualizing provisional data to formulate effective, evidence-based public policies that address regional disparities.
