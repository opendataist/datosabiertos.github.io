Alberto Garzón no sabe utilizar Google y paga 140.000 euros por un estudio que ya existe

The article argues that the Spanish Ministry of Consumption is funding a redundant study on childhood obesity, despite the fact that major institutions like the INE, the OMS, and the Ministry of Health have already published comprehensive data on the issue. This initiative is portrayed as a political maneuver by Minister Alberto Garzón, occurring amidst previous legislative failures to regulate advertising, suggesting a desire for visibility rather than a genuine need for new data. The core implication is a misuse of public resources, as the new contract aims to collect and analyze data that is already known and available. By commissioning an independent report with similar objectives to existing official statistics, the ministry is essentially duplicating efforts. This raises significant questions about administrative efficiency and the prioritization of government spending, as it ignores the established work of specialized national and international health organizations. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical importance of data reuse and transparency. If government agencies produce redundant studies when high-quality, accessible data already exists, it undermines the principles of open data, which advocate for avoiding duplication and maximizing the value of public information. It demonstrates a failure to leverage existing open datasets for policy decisions, leading to unnecessary fiscal costs and inefficient governance.
