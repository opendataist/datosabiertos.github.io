The article concludes that vacation opportunities in Guanacaste during Holy Week are severely stratified by income, revealing a stark socioeconomic divide in who can afford leisure activities. By correlating household income data with travel costs, it demonstrates that the ability to disconnect and travel is not a universal right but a privilege reserved for specific economic tiers, highlighting significant inequalities in access to tourism resources. The analysis identifies three distinct categories of affordability based on net income. Lower-income families can only afford basic lodging without all-inclusive amenities, while middle-income households may access beach hotels but still face budget constraints for food and transport. Only the highest-earning segment of the population has the financial flexibility to afford luxury all-inclusive resorts, underscoring how rising costs restrict holiday access for the majority of the population. This analysis is relevant to open data as it exemplifies the critical intersection of disparate datasets, such as census information and commercial pricing, to reveal hidden social realities. It demonstrates how combining official statistical sources with real-time market data allows for deeper insights into economic exclusion. Such methodologies encourage transparency in public policy and help visualize how economic barriers impact quality of life and social mobility.
