Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Mexico’s constitutional framework guarantees universal access to public information and data protection, principles enforced by independent oversight bodies like the INAI. These institutions are essential for ensuring government transparency, allowing citizens to monitor public resource usage and hold officials accountable through legal mechanisms. The validity of these democratic rights depends on the proper functioning of the guarantee organs, which serve as the final recourse when authorities improperly withhold information. Currently, the INAI is partially inoperable due to a legislative failure to appoint commissioners, leaving it unable to adjudicate appeals against unjustified information denials. While citizens can still submit requests, they lack a functional tribunal to resolve conflicts with public entities. This paralysis stems from a political impasse where presidential vetoes and Senate delays have created vacancies that prevent the governing body from meeting legal quorum requirements, thereby paralyzing the enforcement of transparency laws. This situation highlights the critical vulnerability of open data ecosystems when institutional oversight is compromised. Without a fully operational regulator, the balance between the state’s duty to disclose information and individual privacy rights is destabilized. The article underscores that transparency is not merely an informational right but a functional necessity for democratic accountability, making the immediate restoration of the INAI’s composition vital for upholding international obligations and protecting the integrity of public data access.
