El ministro Carlos Cuerpo: "La economía española crecerá en 2025 por encima del 2,4%"

The Spanish government projects robust economic growth for 2025, surpassing official initial estimates due to strong performance in 2024. The central conclusion is that this expansion is sustainable and balanced, significantly contributing to the Eurozone’s recovery. However, a critical gap remains between macroeconomic indicators and public perception, prompting a strategic shift to ensure citizens directly experience and feel the benefits of this prosperity in their daily lives. To consolidate this trajectory, the administration prioritizes the approval of the 2025 budget, viewing it as essential for translating political goals into concrete financial resources. While negotiations face parliamentary challenges, the government aims to secure agreement to maintain stability. Furthermore, officials distinguish the current real estate boom from the 2007 crisis, citing stronger household finances and a smaller sector weight, thus mitigating fears of another bubble despite record mortgage signings. This analysis is relevant to open_data because it highlights the necessity of transparent, accessible, and high-quality economic statistics to bridge the trust gap between institutions and the public. Accurate, timely data allows for better monitoring of these macroeconomic trends, enabling citizens to verify claims about growth and job creation independently. This fosters accountability and ensures that policy debates are grounded in verifiable facts rather than anecdotal perceptions, which is fundamental for effective democratic engagement with economic policies.
