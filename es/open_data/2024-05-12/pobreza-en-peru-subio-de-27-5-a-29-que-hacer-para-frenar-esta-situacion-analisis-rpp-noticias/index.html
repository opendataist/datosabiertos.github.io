Pobreza en Perú subió de 27.5 % a 29 %: ¿qué hacer para frenar esta situación? [ANÁLISIS] | RPP Noticias

The article concludes that the rise in Peru’s poverty rates is primarily driven by persistent political instability and poor governmental management, rather than solely by external shocks. Experts emphasize that economic recovery is impossible without first achieving social and political pacification, as the continuous "political noise" has eroded progress made during the pandemic and hindered structural improvements. A critical shift in the nature of poverty is its urbanization, with city-based poverty nearly doubling since 2019. This geographic transition necessitates new policy strategies tailored to urban challenges, moving beyond traditional rural-focused approaches. Furthermore, the situation remains dire in specific regions like Loreto, highlighting deep disparities that simple economic growth alone cannot resolve, thereby underscoring the need for targeted interventions such as unlocking major infrastructure and mining projects. This content is relevant to open data because it highlights the necessity of transparent, accessible statistical reporting by institutions like INEI to accurately track these complex socioeconomic shifts. Reliable open data allows citizens and policymakers to identify emerging trends, such as the urbanization of poverty, and holds governments accountable for the specific drivers—like political instability—that contribute to worsening social indicators, enabling evidence-based decision-making.
