Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article highlights the critical role of official inflation data in shaping public perception during a tense electoral period. As the national statistics institute prepares to release October figures, the economic team emphasizes how currency devaluation significantly influenced recent price increases, underscoring the volatility of the current financial landscape. Independent estimates suggest a slight deceleration in inflation, contrasting with official metrics and projecting a sharp rise in food costs due to the end of price agreements. This divergence between official and private data sources creates significant uncertainty for markets and consumers, illustrating how differing methodologies can lead to conflicting narratives about economic stability. This situation is vital for open data initiatives because it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, accessible, and comparable datasets. When government statistics conflict with independent analyses, public trust erodes. Open data advocates must ensure that all datasets, including methodologies and assumptions, are publicly available to allow for independent verification and informed decision-making by citizens and policymakers alike.
