La natalidad en Italia se desploma a mínimos históricos y baja por primera vez de 400.000 neonatos

Italy’s annual births have fallen below 400,000 for the first time, marking a historic low since unification. This demographic shift reflects a stark imbalance where deaths significantly outnumber births, driven by population aging and a shrinking cohort of women of reproductive age. Consequently, the national population declined despite positive net migration, highlighting a severe structural challenge. The aging trend has tripled the centenarian population, while life expectancy shows modest recovery post-pandemic, failing to offset the overall contraction. This data is vital for open data initiatives as it underscores the necessity for transparent, accessible statistical repositories. Reliable public datasets enable researchers and policymakers to monitor such critical socioeconomic trends, fostering informed decisions regarding healthcare, pension systems, and long-term social sustainability.
