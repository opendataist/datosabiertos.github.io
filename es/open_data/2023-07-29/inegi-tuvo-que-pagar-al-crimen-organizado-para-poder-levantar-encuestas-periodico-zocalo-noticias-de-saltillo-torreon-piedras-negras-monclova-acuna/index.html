Inegi tuvo que pagar al crimen organizado para poder levantar encuestas | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The INEGI’s acknowledgment of paying organized crime groups to access restricted zones for economic and agricultural censuses reveals a critical compromise in state data integrity. This practice implies that official statistics may be biased or incomplete, as data collection depends on illicit negotiations rather than standard legal authority. Such systemic vulnerability undermines public trust in government figures and highlights the severe operational risks faced by statistical agencies in conflict zones. This situation underscores the urgent need for greater transparency and security protocols in open data initiatives. If official sources require compromising ethical standards to gather basic information, alternative data collection methods must be explored to ensure unbiased results. Policymakers must recognize that data quality is directly linked to the safety and independence of field investigators. Ultimately, this revelation challenges the reliability of national databases used for economic planning and resource allocation. It serves as a stark reminder that open data efforts cannot ignore the political and security contexts in which information is produced. Ensuring the freedom and safety of data collectors is essential for producing accurate, actionable, and trustworthy information for society.
