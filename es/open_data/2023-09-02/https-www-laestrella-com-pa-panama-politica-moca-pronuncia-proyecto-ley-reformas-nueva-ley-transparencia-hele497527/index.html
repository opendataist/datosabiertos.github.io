Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The proposed legislation regarding public transparency is criticized for introducing bureaucratic hurdles that centralize information requests, thereby delaying citizen access rather than enhancing it. By creating new administrative units, the law shifts responsibility away from immediate duty-holders, effectively creating obstacles that undermine the public's right to information. This structural change prioritizes institutional convenience over democratic accountability. Furthermore, the framework compromises independence by making the transparency authority fully dependent on the executive branch. The removal of personal liability for officials who fail to provide information strips citizens of legal recourse, while broad interpretations of data protection laws shield public funds from scrutiny. These changes risk consolidating a corrupt system where political discretion dictates the flow of essential public data. This issue is critical to open_data because it highlights how legislative designs can actively hinder data accessibility and integrity. When laws obscure public records or remove enforcement mechanisms, the potential for open data to drive transparency and accountability is neutralized. Understanding these political risks is essential for advocates aiming to protect the infrastructure necessary for truthful, accessible, and usable public information.
