Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article reveals that the apparent drop in unemployment rates is largely illusory, driven instead by a significant withdrawal of people from the labor force rather than by new job placements. This disengagement highlights a deep structural crisis where economic instability and lack of opportunities force many individuals, particularly young adults, to stop seeking work entirely. This trend challenges the effectiveness of current employment metrics, as they fail to capture the true extent of economic exclusion and the growing population of discouraged workers who are effectively invisible in traditional job statistics. A stark gender disparity persists, with women facing nearly double the unemployment rate of men and significantly lower labor force participation. This inequality stems from systemic issues such as the feminization of vulnerable sectors like care and tourism, which are highly sensitive to economic shocks, and the disproportionate burden of unpaid care responsibilities. Furthermore, lower educational attainment and existing labor market discrimination exacerbate women's exclusion, demonstrating that employment gaps are not merely statistical anomalies but reflections of deep-seated social and economic barriers that persist regardless of broader economic fluctuations. This report is critically relevant to open data because it exposes the limitations of relying solely on standard unemployment indicators, which can mask severe social crises by ignoring those who have exited the workforce. To foster transparency and informed policy-making, open data initiatives must incorporate broader metrics, such as disengagement rates and participation gaps by gender and age. By making this nuanced data accessible and understandable, stakeholders can advocate for targeted interventions that address the root causes of labor market exclusion, ensuring that statistical transparency leads to more equitable and effective social policies.
