Mexico’s inflation continues its fourth consecutive month of deceleration, falling below market expectations and marking the first time in over three years that it has recorded negative monthly growth for two periods. This trend suggests a stabilizing price environment, driven significantly by summer electricity discounts and varying sectoral behaviors, rather than just temporary fluctuations. The slowdown is largely attributed to declining underlying inflation, which reflects less volatile prices for goods and services. While merchandise prices continue to rise, the reduction in energy and agricultural tariffs has pulled down the non-underlying index. This divergence highlights how specific policy interventions and seasonal factors are currently shaping consumer price dynamics more than broad economic trends. This data is relevant to open data initiatives as it underscores the importance of accessible, granular statistical indicators from institutions like INEGI. Transparent access to such detailed metrics allows for better analysis of inflation drivers and policy effectiveness, enabling researchers and citizens to understand how specific sectors and government actions impact the cost of living.
