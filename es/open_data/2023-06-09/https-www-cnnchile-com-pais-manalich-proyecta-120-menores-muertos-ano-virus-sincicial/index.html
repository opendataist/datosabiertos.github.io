Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Former Minister Jaime Mañalich warned that infant mortality could spike due to the respiratory syncytial virus if the healthcare system fails to adapt, urging immediate improvements in critical care capacity. This stark estimate contrasts sharply with the current Health Minister’s assertion that official data remains robust, emphasizing that current death rates are historically low and effectively monitored by national epidemiological systems. The discrepancy highlights critical challenges in open data practices, where conflicting projections between independent experts and government bodies can obscure public health realities. Transparent, accessible, and comparable data models are essential to resolve such divergences, ensuring that decision-making relies on clear, unified evidence rather than isolated estimates. This situation underscores the necessity for open, interoperable data standards to build trust and enable accurate crisis management. Ultimately, the debate reveals how data accessibility and methodological transparency directly impact public safety perceptions. When open data frameworks allow for independent verification and cross-referencing of health metrics, they empower stakeholders to challenge official narratives responsibly. This case serves as a vital reminder that open data is not merely about availability, but about creating accountable, verifiable ecosystems that protect vulnerable populations during health emergencies.
