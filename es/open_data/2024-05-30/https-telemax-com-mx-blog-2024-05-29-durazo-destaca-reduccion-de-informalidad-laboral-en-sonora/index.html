Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

This article highlights how community-led photography projects empower local residents to document their own histories and identities. By providing analog cameras, initiatives like Grupo México’s Taller Ambulante enable participants to narrate their lived experiences, shifting the focus from external representation to self-representation. This approach validates local perspectives as essential cultural knowledge. The relevance to open data lies in the creation of accessible, user-generated visual archives. These communities generate valuable qualitative data about their territories and traditions, which can be digitized and shared openly. This democratization of information challenges centralized narratives, proving that grassroots data collection offers richer, more authentic insights into social realities than top-down methods. Ultimately, such projects demonstrate the power of open data in fostering inclusivity and preserving intangible heritage. They show that empowering individuals to capture and share their stories creates a more diverse and transparent information ecosystem. This model encourages other sectors to adopt participatory data strategies that prioritize community agency and ensure that marginalized voices are accurately represented in the global knowledge base.
