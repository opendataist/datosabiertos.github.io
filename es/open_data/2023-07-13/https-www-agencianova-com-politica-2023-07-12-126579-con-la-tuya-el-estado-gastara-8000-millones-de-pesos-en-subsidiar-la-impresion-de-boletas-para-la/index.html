The article highlights a significant increase in the number of political formulas for the upcoming primary elections, resulting in a sharp rise in state subsidies for printing ballots. This proliferation of candidates across various categories, from presidential to legislative, means that the government must disburse substantial funds to numerous parties, including smaller and newer entities that receive millions of pesos despite limited political representation. Crucially, the current funding model allocates equal subsidies to every officialized list of pre-candidates, regardless of their actual voter base or historical strength. Because the number of competing lists has reached a record high, this system drives up the total cost to astronomical levels, straining public resources during a period of economic scarcity. The financial burden is amplified because each distinct list within a coalition receives a separate subsidy, multiplying the expenses compared to systems where a single ballot represents a broader alliance. This situation is vital for open data advocates because it underscores the need for transparency in public spending and electoral funding. The article reveals inconsistencies in how parties justify expenses, prompting stricter judicial controls to prevent fraud. Open data initiatives are essential to monitor these disbursed funds, analyze the inefficiency of the current distribution model, and support reforms like the single-paper ballot, which could drastically reduce costs and ensure greater accountability in democratic processes.
