Spain’s tourism sector has demonstrated a robust recovery since the pandemic, reaching visitor levels that significantly surpass 2021 figures, though they remain below the pre-crisis peaks of 2019. This partial recovery highlights a sector in transition, where international demand is returning but has not yet fully normalized. The data reveals a clear disparity between volume and financial impact, suggesting that while travel is resuming, the economic footprint of tourism has not entirely returned to its historical maximums, indicating a lingering shadow from the global health crisis. The financial dynamics show that non-European tourists contribute the largest share of spending, with specific nations like the UK, Germany, and France driving significant revenue. Notably, there is a marked increase in expenditure from Nordic countries, signaling a shifting geographic pattern in tourist preferences. Regional analysis indicates that while destinations like Catalonia and the Balearic Islands are attracting record numbers, regions such as Madrid and Andalusia are still lagging behind previous years, pointing to uneven recovery patterns across different parts of the country. This article is relevant to open data initiatives because it illustrates the critical role of standardized, publicly available statistical data in tracking complex economic trends. By providing transparent metrics on visitor origins, spending habits, and regional performance, such data empowers policymakers and businesses to make informed decisions regarding tourism management and infrastructure. It underscores how open access to national statistical institutes allows for a nuanced understanding of sector resilience and the specific impacts of global events on local economies.
