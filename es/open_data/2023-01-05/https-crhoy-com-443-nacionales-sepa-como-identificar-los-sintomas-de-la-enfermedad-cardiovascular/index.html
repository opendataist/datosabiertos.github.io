Cardiologists report a troubling rise in heart attacks among young adults, driven by modern lifestyle changes like sedentary habits, poor diet, and high stress. This shift has led to earlier onset of cholesterol and blood sugar issues, creating a public health crisis that overwhelms emergency services and reduces productivity. The increasing burden on healthcare systems is evident in the steady growth of cardiac emergency admissions. This trend highlights the urgent need to identify and mitigate preventable risk factors early in life to avoid premature cardiovascular events and preserve quality of life. This article is relevant to open data because transparent, publicly available health statistics empower citizens to recognize these trends. When healthcare agencies release detailed, accessible data on disease incidence, it enables individuals and policymakers to make informed decisions about prevention, resource allocation, and health education, ultimately fostering a more proactive approach to public wellness.
