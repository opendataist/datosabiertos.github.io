Cultura sostiene que ya "hay herramientas para proteger a los creadores" ante la IA

The article highlights the urgent challenge of reconciling artificial intelligence development with intellectual property rights, a critical issue for open data ecosystems. It emphasizes that while AI models require vast amounts of data for training, the current lack of transparency in their internal processes threatens the moral and economic rights of content creators. Without clear mechanisms to ensure that AI systems disclose their training data, the open access principle is undermined by opaque algorithms that may exploit copyrighted works without consent or attribution. A central proposal is the implementation of mandatory "opt-out" rights, allowing authors to restrict the use of their materials in AI training datasets. This approach seeks to shift the burden from proving infringement after the fact to preventing unauthorized usage through upfront transparency requirements. By ensuring that entities developing foundational models communicate their data sources, the framework aims to protect creators' ability to control their digital assets, thereby preserving the ethical integrity of data sourcing in an increasingly automated environment. This debate is highly relevant to open data because it interrogates the sustainability of the data supply chain for AI. If open data practices fail to respect intellectual property safeguards, they risk losing public trust and legal viability. The article suggests that future open data policies must integrate strict transparency and consent protocols, ensuring that the openness of information does not come at the expense of creators' rights, but rather evolves into a balanced framework where data usage is both accessible and legally compliant.
