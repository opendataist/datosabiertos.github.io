Según estimaciones privadas, la suba de alimentos se acerca al 13% en septiembre | Análisis

Estimates indicate that Argentina’s September inflation decelerated slightly compared to August but remained within double digits, driven primarily by significant price hikes in food and basic consumer goods. Despite government price anchors and exchange rate controls, the widespread impact of currency devaluation and seasonal factors kept inflation elevated, with food prices rising sharply across almost all categories. This highlights the difficulty of maintaining price stability in a context of strong external shocks and structural economic pressures. The narrative reveals a consensus among private consultancies and official reports that while the monthly rate of price increases is slowing, it has stabilized at a higher level. Weekly data suggests a moderation in specific weeks, yet the accumulated annual inflation remains near one hundred percent. This pattern indicates that short-term policy measures have not been sufficient to curb underlying inflationary pressures, leading to a persistent erosion of purchasing power and a disconnect between official figures and public perception, where citizens expect even higher inflation rates. This article is relevant to open data because it exemplifies the critical distinction between official statistics and real-time market estimates. In environments where official data releases are delayed or disputed, the proliferation of private indices becomes essential for transparency and economic analysis. It underscores the importance of accessible, diverse data sources to provide a comprehensive understanding of economic reality, allowing stakeholders to track trends, verify government claims, and make informed decisions when a single official metric is insufficient or unavailable.
