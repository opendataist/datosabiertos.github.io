Illana destaca el aumento interanual de población especialmente entre la población de 17 a 28 años

The article highlights a positive demographic trend in Cuenca, where the population has increased slightly compared to the previous year, reversing the historical trend of depopulation. This growth is particularly significant among young adults, who are staying in rural areas due to improved local opportunities. The administration attributes this shift to coordinated efforts across different government levels, emphasizing that sustained investment and policy support are crucial for consolidating this recovery and improving the quality of life in the countryside. This narrative is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how statistical outputs from national institutes serve as essential metrics for evaluating the effectiveness of public policies. Open access to such demographic data allows citizens, researchers, and policymakers to transparently monitor social changes, verify claims made by officials, and identify specific regional needs. It transforms raw numbers into actionable insights for combating rural decline. Furthermore, the article underscores the importance of granular data in targeting interventions. By analyzing specific age groups and localities, governments can allocate resources more efficiently, such as supporting youth retention or upgrading infrastructure in small municipalities. This case illustrates how open data fosters accountability and strategic decision-making, proving that transparent information is vital for designing successful long-term solutions to complex societal challenges like rural depopulation.
