Vox le exige al Gobierno de Navarra que haga algo que lleva haciendo desde hace 12 años

The Vox parliamentary group in Navarre has submitted a motion urging the regional government to publish all mandatory public domain information on its Transparency Portal. The stated goal is to ensure citizens understand how public resources are utilized. However, this request highlights a significant disconnect, as the legislation requiring the disclosure of financial reports, executive salaries, contracts, and audits has been in force since 2012. The initiative effectively demands the continuous enforcement of existing transparency laws rather than introducing new regulatory measures. This situation underscores the critical distinction between the legal existence of open data frameworks and their practical implementation. While the regional government has published this information for over a decade, the need for political pressure to maintain or improve access suggests potential gaps in compliance or visibility. Furthermore, the obligation extends to various public entities receiving funding, emphasizing that transparency is a systemic requirement across all levels of public administration, not just the executive branch. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates common challenges in the sector: the gap between statutory mandates and actual user accessibility. It reveals how political actors may use transparency demands as rhetorical tools even when legal obligations already exist. Consequently, it highlights the ongoing necessity for civil society to monitor not just the presence of data, but its usability and the true commitment of institutions to proactive disclosure and open governance standards.
