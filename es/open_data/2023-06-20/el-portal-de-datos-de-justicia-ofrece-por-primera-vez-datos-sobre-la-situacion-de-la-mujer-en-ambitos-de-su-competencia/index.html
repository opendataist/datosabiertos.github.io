El portal de datos de Justicia ofrece por primera vez datos sobre la situación de la mujer en ámbitos de su competencia

The Spanish Ministry of Justice has launched a new data section aimed at making women’s role in the legal sector visible while promoting evidence-based justice and digital transformation. By providing detailed indicators on critical social issues such as gender violence, crime, and civil registrations, the initiative allows for an objective comparison between genders. This granular analysis helps identify existing gaps and track progress since 2019, ensuring that decisions are grounded in concrete realities rather than assumptions. These datasets are explicitly designed to be public and reusable, aligning directly with European Union directives on data reuse. The publication serves as a practical example of open data in action, where raw statistics are transformed into accessible resources that foster transparency and civic collaboration. By breaking down information by demographics and geography, the portal empowers researchers and citizens to scrutinize how different groups are affected by judicial processes. This effort is crucial for the open data community because it demonstrates how government agencies can leverage transparency to protect vulnerable populations. It reinforces the strategic value of releasing machine-readable data to drive social equality and improve public policy quality. Ultimately, this initiative shows that open data is not just about technical access but is a powerful tool for social justice, enabling society to understand and address systemic inequalities effectively.
