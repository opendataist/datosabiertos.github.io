Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Argentina has initiated a proficiency exam for public sector workers, a measure intended to assess competency but one that has sparked significant labor tensions. While the government states the results are non-binding and designed to filter out those who fail three times, unions have reported extremely high pass rates. This unexpected outcome has led union leaders to argue that the policy has backfired, as the vast majority of evaluated employees now qualify for permanent status under constitutional stability guarantees rather than remaining in temporary positions. The core implication for the workforce is the conflict between administrative reform and job security. Union representatives insist that those who pass should be integrated into the permanent state staff, particularly those with long tenure who perform essential, ongoing functions. Consequently, the debate has shifted from mere competency testing to demands for the regularization of contracts. The unions view the exam not just as an assessment, but as a mechanism that inadvertently forces the state to fulfill its legal obligation to stabilize the employment of workers who have already proven their routine contribution to public service. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the friction between digital administrative reforms and the human capital required to implement them. Successful open government strategies depend on a stable, skilled, and secure civil service capable of managing transparent processes and data infrastructure. If labor policies create uncertainty or fail to properly integrate and train the workforce, the technical foundations of open data—such as data quality, system maintenance, and ethical governance—may be compromised by institutional instability and high turnover among experienced employees.
