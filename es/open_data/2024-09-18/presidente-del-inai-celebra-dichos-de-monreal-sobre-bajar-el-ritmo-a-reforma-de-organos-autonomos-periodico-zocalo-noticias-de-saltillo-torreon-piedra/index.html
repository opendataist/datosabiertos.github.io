Presidente del INAI celebra dichos de Monreal sobre bajar el ritmo a reforma de órganos autónomos | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection Protection (INAI) leadership has reaffirmed its commitment to dialogue rather than dissolution, aiming for consensus among new legislators and the incoming federal administration. Key stakeholders acknowledge that while the institute’s reform is not the immediate legislative priority, the proposed changes are being reconsidered through a collaborative approach, signaling a shift away from potential institutional disappearance toward constructive improvement of the transparency system. This openness to negotiation is crucial for maintaining the integrity of Mexico’s transparency framework, as it prioritizes systemic strengthening over drastic structural changes. By engaging proactively with political leaders, the INAI seeks to ensure that future modifications enhance rather than hinder access to information and data protection, reflecting a broader commitment to democratic values and institutional stability amidst political transitions. This development is highly relevant to open data because the robustness of transparency institutions directly impacts the availability and quality of public information. Stable, cooperative governance fosters an environment where data can be shared openly and effectively, ensuring that citizens and researchers have reliable access to government records. Consequently, successful dialogue safeguards the foundational mechanisms that support transparent, data-driven public administration and accountability.
