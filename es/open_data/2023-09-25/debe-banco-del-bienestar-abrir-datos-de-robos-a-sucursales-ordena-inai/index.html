Debe Banco del Bienestar abrir datos de robos a sucursales, ordena INAI

The National Institute for Transparency has ruled that the Banco del Bienestar must disclose detailed records of bank robberies occurring since 2019, rejecting the institution's five-year attempt to classify this data as confidential. This decision forces the bank to reveal the specific amounts stolen and the affected branches, dismantling legal arguments that claimed public disclosure would hinder ongoing federal investigations or compromise due process. By overriding the bank's secrecy claims, the ruling ensures that critical security metrics are no longer hidden from public scrutiny. This mandate is crucial for open data initiatives because it establishes a precedent that financial security statistics cannot be arbitrarily withheld under vague national security or operational secrecy justifications. When entities responsible for managing public funds and social welfare programs obscure transparency regarding asset protection and loss, it undermines accountability and prevents citizens from making informed decisions about the safety of state-managed resources. The ruling directly contradicts previous political assertions that institutions founded by the current administration were immune to insecurity issues. By compelling the release of this information, the decision highlights the necessity of verifying official narratives with empirical data, ensuring that transparency mechanisms can hold public institutions accountable for operational failures and mismanagement claims, thereby reinforcing democratic oversight.
