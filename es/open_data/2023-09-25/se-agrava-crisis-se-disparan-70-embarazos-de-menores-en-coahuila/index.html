Se agrava crisis: se disparan 70% embarazos de menores en Coahuila

The article highlights a sharp rise in adolescent pregnancies in Coahuila, revealing a significant demographic shift where a notable percentage of births are attributed to mothers under eighteen. This increase signals a deepening social crisis that requires immediate institutional and community attention, rather than being viewed merely as a statistical anomaly. The data underscores the vulnerability of young girls and the urgent need for effective public health and social support strategies. Beyond mere numbers, the text emphasizes that many of these cases are rooted in sexual violence and abuse, challenging the narrative that treats them solely as reproductive choices. Experts argue that societal sensitivity is lacking, often blaming the victim instead of investigating the perpetrators, which may be older individuals exploiting minors. This perspective reframes the issue as a matter of human rights and justice, necessitating comprehensive legal and psychological interventions to protect children from exploitation and harm. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistical information can expose hidden social injustices. When government agencies like INEGI release disaggregated birth records, civil organizations and journalists can analyze trends to advocate for policy changes and hold institutions accountable. Open data thus serves as a critical tool for identifying systemic failures in child protection, enabling society to transform raw statistics into actionable insights for protecting vulnerable populations.
