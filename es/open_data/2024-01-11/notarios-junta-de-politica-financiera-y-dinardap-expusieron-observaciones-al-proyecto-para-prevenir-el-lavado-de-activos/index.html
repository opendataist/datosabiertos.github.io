Notarios, Junta de Política Financiera y DINARDAP, expusieron observaciones al proyecto para prevenir el lavado de activos

The legislative discussion surrounding the reform of Ecuador’s anti-money laundering law highlights a critical tension between regulatory efficiency and institutional integrity. A primary concern raised by the notary sector is that classifying them as non-financial obliged subjects for routine acts, such as vehicle contract authentication, fundamentally distorts the essence of the notarial profession. This friction underscores the broader challenge of accurately defining scope in financial transparency frameworks without overburdening traditional legal intermediaries who lack direct financial oversight capabilities. Furthermore, the debate emphasizes the necessity of clarifying institutional competencies among regulatory bodies. Representatives stressed the need to distinguish clearly between the policy-setting roles of financial regulators and the supervisory functions of other agencies. This distinction is vital to prevent jurisdictional overlaps and ensure that mechanisms for detecting asset laundering are both effective and legally sound, particularly regarding credit card administrators and the integration of financial analysis units into notarial systems for real-time alert identification. This article is relevant to open_data because it explicitly addresses the requirements for information exchange systems and interoperability among public registries. The push for standardized reporting thresholds and integrated data flows between entities like the public records directorate and financial authorities illustrates the practical necessity of open, machine-readable data structures. Effective anti-money laundering efforts rely heavily on the seamless, open sharing of transactional and ownership data, making this legislative evolution a key example of how data governance policies directly impact financial transparency and systemic integrity.
