Proceso de selección de Correos en España: 11 candidatos compiten por cada plaza

The analysis of Correos’ recruitment data reveals a significant regional disparity in competition levels for civil servant positions. While the national average indicates substantial demand, southern provinces like Seville and Las Palmas face intense rivalry, whereas northern regions such as Gipuzkoa and Catalonia show comparatively lower applicant ratios. This geographic variation highlights how local population density and economic conditions directly influence job accessibility and candidate pool sizes. These findings underscore the critical role of transparency in public sector hiring processes. By making specific recruitment metrics and applicant volumes publicly available, organizations enable stakeholders to understand market dynamics and resource allocation. Open access to this granular data fosters accountability and allows for a more equitable assessment of employment opportunities across different territories, moving beyond aggregate statistics to reveal local realities. Ultimately, this article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how published datasets can drive informed decision-making for both job seekers and policymakers. The availability of detailed ratio analyses empowers individuals to tailor their preparation strategies based on regional competitiveness. Furthermore, it illustrates the value of standardized, machine-readable data in identifying trends and disparities, encouraging the continuous improvement of open government initiatives in human resources and public service management.
