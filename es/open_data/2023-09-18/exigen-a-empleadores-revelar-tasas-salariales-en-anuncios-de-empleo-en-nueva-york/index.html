This article provides a comprehensive directory of global geographic entities, listing United States states, Canadian provinces, international countries, and various territories with their official designations. The primary conclusion is that open and standardized data regarding geopolitical boundaries and administrative divisions is essential for accurate global information systems. By offering a complete enumeration of regions from Alabama to Zimbabwe, the text underscores the necessity of consistent classification schemes in geospatial databases. The relevance to open_data lies in the critical role of clean, comprehensive reference data for international interoperability. Such lists enable developers and researchers to standardize location-based analytics, ensuring that applications can correctly interpret and map entities across different political jurisdictions. Without these standardized mappings, data integration across borders becomes fragmented, leading to errors in reporting, shipping, and demographic studies. Ultimately, this resource highlights that open data must include granular geographic identifiers to support global utility. It demonstrates how accessible, well-structured lists facilitate transparency and accessibility in information management. By making these distinctions clear, the data empowers users to build more robust systems that respect the complexity of global geography, fostering better connectivity and understanding among diverse regions and populations.
