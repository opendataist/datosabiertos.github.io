Inegi: 96.8% de trabajadoras del hogar en Puebla viven sin prestaciones

Home care workers in Puebla face severe labor precarity, with a vast majority lacking statutory benefits because employers do not classify their employment as formal. This lack of protection highlights a critical gap in labor rights, where the workforce operates largely outside official social safety nets, resulting in significantly fewer opportunities and access to essential services compared to the national average. Despite high participation rates in domestic cleaning and eldercare, most workers remain unidentified and unprotected. The low rate of legal registration leaves them vulnerable to exploitation, evidenced by a substantial portion seeking conciliation to defend their rights. This systemic exclusion underscores the urgent need for broader societal and institutional recognition of domestic work as legitimate, formal employment deserving of standard protections. This data is relevant to open data initiatives because it reveals significant visibility gaps in official statistics regarding informal labor. Transparent, accessible data on worker demographics and legal status is essential for policymakers and activists to advocate for effective reforms. Furthermore, it supports public accountability by tracking the implementation of new social security registration campaigns, ensuring that digital governance tools can effectively monitor progress in formalizing these marginalized sectors.
