Tesis y título profesional de AMLO deben ser públicos, ordena el INAI justo antes de quedar ‘inactivo’

The National Institute for Transparency has mandated the UNAM to publicly disclose President López Obrador’s professional degree. While the institution initially withheld this document citing privacy concerns, the tribunal ruled that the document’s social significance and the need for government accountability outweigh individual confidentiality claims. This decision reinforces the principle that high-ranking officials are subject to stricter scrutiny regarding their personal records. This ruling directly addresses the tension between personal data protection and the public’s right to access information about those in power. By prioritizing transparency, the resolution strengthens democratic oversight mechanisms, ensuring that the credentials of leaders are accessible to citizens rather than kept hidden under the guise of privacy. It sets a critical precedent for how public figures’ academic histories are treated in legal and administrative contexts. This case is highly relevant to open data because it establishes that government accountability necessitates the release of specific personal information for top officials. It highlights the ongoing conflict between data privacy laws and the imperative for transparent governance, demonstrating how open data frameworks can be applied to ensure that leaders are fully accountable to the public they serve.
