Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The National Institute for Transparency has decisively ruled that the Secretariat of Foreign Affairs must publicly disclose the contract details, economic cost, and name of the private company hired for a recent presidential summit. This order overturns the government’s attempt to classify this financial information as reserved, establishing that public spending transparency outweighs administrative secrecy claims. The foreign ministry had previously justified withholding these details by asserting that revealing them could compromise national security, potentially inviting attacks or sabotages. They further argued that such disclosure might destabilize delicate diplomatic relations with other nations. However, the regulatory body found these arguments insufficient, noting that basic contracting data does not provide actionable intelligence for malicious actors or reveal security vulnerabilities. This case is highly relevant to open data because it reinforces the principle that all public expenditure must be accessible, regardless of political sensitivity or security justifications. By invalidating broad claims of non-usefulness for public interest, the decision strengthens accountability mechanisms. It ensures that citizens can scrutinize how public funds are managed, promoting trust in institutions and preventing the misuse of security narratives to hide financial information from public oversight.
