La inflación en la eurozona baja al 1,8% en septiembre, su nivel más bajo en más de tres años

The eurozone’s inflation rate has dropped to 1.8%, marking its lowest level since April 2021 and falling below the European Central Bank’s target for the first time in nearly three years. This decline is driven by significant reductions in energy prices, alongside moderating costs in services and food, signaling a continued cooling of economic pressures across the region. Crucially, core inflation, which excludes volatile sectors and serves as a key indicator for monetary policy, also decreased to 2.7%. Although this remains above the central bank’s goal, the consistent downward trend suggests that inflationary pressures are easing. The central bank continues to monitor this data closely, emphasizing that future interest rate decisions will depend on the sustained evolution of these indicators rather than short-term fluctuations. This development is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical role of transparent, real-time statistical releases from Eurostat in guiding monetary policy. Reliable and accessible data on inflation metrics allows markets, researchers, and citizens to accurately assess economic health, understand the rationale behind central bank decisions, and hold institutions accountable for their adherence to stability goals.
