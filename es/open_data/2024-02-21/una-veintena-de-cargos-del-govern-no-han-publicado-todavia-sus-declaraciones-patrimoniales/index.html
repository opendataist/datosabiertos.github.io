Una veintena de cargos del Govern no han publicado todavía sus declaraciones patrimoniales

The article highlights a political dispute over transparency reforms in the Balearic Islands, where the executive branch argues that eliminating the Anticorruption Office does not reduce oversight. Instead, functions are being transferred to other bodies like the General Inspection, with the government framing this as necessary administrative simplification rather than a deregulation measure. Opposition critics contend that these changes weaken internal and external controls, echoing past corruption scandals and suggesting a regression in governance standards. They argue that the government lacks commitment to true transparency, using the restructuring of specific commissions as evidence of an attempt to dismantle accountability mechanisms under the guise of efficiency. This issue is vital to open data discussions as it questions whether centralizing or redistributing public information truly enhances accessibility. The debate reveals how structural changes in government bodies can impact data availability and scrutiny, underscoring the importance of maintaining independent channels for public accountability and transparent information dissemination.
