La superficie visada para construcción en C-LM se mantiene estable en 2023, pese a la coyuntura económica

Spain’s construction sector is undergoing a significant transformation, prioritizing the rehabilitation and modernization of its aging building stock over new developments. This shift is driven by urgent needs for energy efficiency and accessibility, aligning with broader European goals to combat climate change. Although new construction volumes remain stable, rehabilitation has reached record highs, reflecting a strategic pivot toward sustainability rather than expansion. The surge in rehabilitation projects highlights the critical importance of transparent and accessible data regarding building conditions and subsidy distribution. Support offices handled a sharp increase in inquiries, revealing that citizens and professionals are heavily relying on public funds to upgrade inefficient housing. This interaction underscores the necessity of clear, open datasets to facilitate the effective allocation of resources and inform stakeholders about the actual state of the national housing infrastructure. This trend is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it demonstrates the need for robust, standardized information systems to track retrofitting progress and funding usage. By making detailed data on building stocks and rehabilitation outcomes publicly available, policymakers can better address the vast number of homes requiring urgent upgrades. Ultimately, accessible data is essential to guide future policy, ensuring that environmental targets are met through informed, data-driven decision-making rather than opaque administrative processes.
