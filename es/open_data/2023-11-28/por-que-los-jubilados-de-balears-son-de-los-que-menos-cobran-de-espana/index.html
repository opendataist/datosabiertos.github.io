¿Por qué los jubilados de Balears son de los que menos cobran de España?

The article highlights that Balearic Islands retirees receive significantly lower pensions than the Spanish average, a disparity driven by the local economy’s heavy reliance on low-wage, non-qualified service sector jobs. High rates of temporary contracts and part-time work mean many workers contribute less to the social security system, directly limiting their future benefits. Additionally, the prevalence of forced freelancers who contribute minimally further exacerbates this financial gap. This situation is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how macroeconomic indicators and labor market statistics can reveal structural inequalities. By analyzing accessible public data, such as pension amounts and employment types, citizens and researchers can identify the root causes of economic disparity rather than attributing them to individual circumstances. To address this, experts suggest diversifying the economy by attracting technology and healthcare sectors through targeted fiscal incentives. This case underscores the importance of using transparent data to inform policy decisions that aim to correct market imbalances and improve social welfare standards.
