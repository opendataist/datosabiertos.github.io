Radiografía de la pobreza en Arequipa: en 2023 se agravaron las crisis de los servicios básicos y la inseguridad | Diario Jornada

The publication of Peru’s 2023 poverty report underscores a critical regression in basic services and social welfare, revealing that political pressures failed to suppress data showing widespread deterioration. The findings highlight a severe decline in access to safe water, sanitation, and waste management, indicating that recent administrative challenges have directly impacted essential infrastructure. This regression suggests that governance failures are exacerbating fundamental living conditions for residents, moving the country away from previous developmental gains. Simultaneously, the data illustrates a deepening crisis in public safety and child welfare, where increased victimization rates correlate with worsening health and educational outcomes for vulnerable populations. The rise in crime victims reflects broader systemic instability, while the surge in childhood anemia and poor academic performance points to long-term developmental risks. These interconnected issues demonstrate how economic hardship and service failures create a domino effect that compromises the future potential of the younger generation. This article is vital to open data because it validates the necessity of transparent, accessible statistical institutions capable of resisting political interference. The ability to publish accurate, granular information despite external pressure ensures that citizens and policymakers can monitor real social indicators rather than relying on obscured narratives. Ultimately, it reinforces that reliable open data is not just a technical requirement but a fundamental safeguard for democratic accountability and effective public policy formulation.
