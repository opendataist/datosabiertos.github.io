El PP aprieta al "Gobierno zombi" de Urkullu por la imposición del euskera y la fallida ley de transparencia

The article highlights a critical standoff in the Basque Parliament, where the People’s Party (PP) vehemently opposes the incoming regional government’s push for stricter transparency and language regulations. By labeling the administration as a "zombie government," the opposition argues that these late-stage legislative moves are politically motivated impositions rather than genuine administrative reforms. Their strategy focuses on exposing procedural flaws and substantive deficiencies in the proposed laws, aiming to undermine the credibility of the new authority responsible for overseeing transparency and public participation. This political conflict has significant implications for the development of open data frameworks. The PP’s rejection of the transparency bill suggests a potential bottleneck in establishing robust mechanisms for public access to information and citizen engagement. If the legislative process is disrupted or the proposed regulations are diluted, the structural integrity of the new Transparency Authority could be compromised. This uncertainty risks hindering the consistent application of open data principles, as the legal foundation required to mandate proactive disclosure and ensure accountability remains contested and unstable during the final stages of the term. The controversy surrounding the mandatory use of the Basque language further illustrates the tension between linguistic policy and administrative accessibility. Critics argue that prioritizing language requirements over merit and openness creates barriers to public service and political dialogue, potentially excluding non-native speakers from full civic participation. This debate is relevant to open data because true transparency requires removing barriers to information access. When administrative decisions are perceived as tools for political imposition rather than service improvement, public trust in institutional openness erodes, complicating efforts to create inclusive, accessible, and truly open government datasets.
