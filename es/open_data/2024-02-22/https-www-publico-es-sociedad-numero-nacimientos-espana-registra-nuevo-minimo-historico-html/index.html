Spain’s birth rate has hit a historic low, marking five consecutive years of decline and reaching the lowest figures since 1941. This demographic shift signals a profound societal change, with most regions experiencing significant drops despite minor increases in isolated areas. In response, advocacy groups have launched provocative campaigns urging families to have more children, framing larger families as a solution to combat depopulation and existential pessimism. This highlights the urgent political and social debate surrounding demographic sustainability and the varying regional impacts of this trend. Concurrently, the death rate decreased, largely due to fewer fatalities among older age groups. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how publicly accessible government statistics, like those from the INE, are essential for identifying critical demographic patterns. Such transparency allows civil society and policymakers to accurately assess population dynamics and drive informed discussions on future social strategies.
