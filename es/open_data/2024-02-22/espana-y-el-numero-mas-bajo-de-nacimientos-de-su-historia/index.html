España y el número más bajo de nacimientos de su historia

Spain recorded its lowest birth rate in history, marking a significant demographic shift that highlights a sustained decline in fertility over the last decade. This trend is compounded by a negative natural balance, where deaths consistently outnumber births, reflecting broader societal changes such as delayed motherhood and the lowest fertility rates in the European Union. Despite this demographic contraction, the population continues to grow driven by substantial immigration from diverse countries. This dynamic demonstrates how migration effectively counterbalances local demographic declines, sustaining overall population numbers even when natural growth is absent. This data is relevant to open data initiatives as it underscores the necessity of high-quality, longitudinal statistical records. Reliable public data enables policymakers to understand complex interactions between birth rates, aging populations, and migration, facilitating informed strategies for social and economic planning.
