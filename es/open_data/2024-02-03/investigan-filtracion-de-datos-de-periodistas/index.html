Investigan filtración de datos de periodistas

The National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) has launched an official investigation into the illicit leak of personal data belonging to journalists covering the Mexican presidential morning briefings. This incident highlights a critical vulnerability in how sensitive professional information is managed by government entities, underscoring the urgent need for robust data protection standards in public administration. By opening this inquiry, the INAI aims to determine whether the breach resulted from a cyberattack or internal negligence, such as unauthorized access by former staff. This process is vital for establishing accountability and ensuring that any responsible parties face appropriate legal consequences, reinforcing the principle that transparency laws must be upheld to prevent abuse of power and protect individual privacy rights. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the tension between government transparency and the obligation to safeguard personal information. It serves as a cautionary example for organizations handling public data, demonstrating that even initiatives intended to increase openness can lead to significant privacy violations if security protocols are not strictly enforced, thereby eroding public trust in digital governance.
