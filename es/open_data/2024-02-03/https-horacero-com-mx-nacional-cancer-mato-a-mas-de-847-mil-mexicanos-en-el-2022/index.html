This article highlights the INEGI’s release of mortality statistics regarding malignant tumors, revealing a steady increase in death rates over the past decade. The data indicates that cancer remains a leading cause of death, with significant variations across different states and a clear correlation with aging, particularly showing higher risks for men over sixty. These trends underscore the growing public health burden and the need for targeted regional and age-specific interventions. The analysis further dissects the disease by gender and age groups, identifying specific cancer types that predominantly affect distinct demographics. For instance, while breast and cervical cancers are primary concerns for women, prostate and lung cancers heavily impact older men. Younger populations show different patterns, with leukemia being a major threat. This granular breakdown is essential for understanding the diverse nature of the disease and tailoring medical resources effectively to those most at risk. This report is relevant to open data because it exemplifies how national statistical agencies transform raw mortality records into accessible, structured public information. By providing detailed datasets on causes of death, demographics, and geographic distribution, the INEGI empowers researchers, policymakers, and the public to conduct evidence-based analyses. This transparency facilitates better health planning, fosters accountability in healthcare delivery, and enables society to monitor the effectiveness of cancer prevention and treatment strategies.
