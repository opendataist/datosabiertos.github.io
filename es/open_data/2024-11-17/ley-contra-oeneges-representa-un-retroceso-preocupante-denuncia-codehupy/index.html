Ley contra oenegés “representa un retroceso preocupante”, denuncia Codehupy

The article highlights a significant conflict in Paraguay regarding the promulgation of a new law targeting non-profit organizations, which critics label as a repressive tool rather than a transparency measure. Human rights groups argue this legislation aims to silence dissent and weaken civil society, representing a dangerous regression in democratic freedoms. They emphasize their historical commitment to accountability, asserting that internal oversight is sufficient without state-imposed restrictions. This dynamic is crucial for open data discussions because it illustrates how transparency policies can be weaponized to restrict information access and stifle investigative journalism. The concern is that excessive bureaucratic control may hinder organizations’ ability to investigate corruption and publish data about state organs. Consequently, the rule of law and the free flow of critical information are threatened by political motives disguised as regulatory compliance. Ultimately, the resistance against this law underscores the importance of protecting the autonomy of civil society groups. Open data ecosystems rely on independent entities that can operate without fear of punitive measures. When governments prioritize control over genuine transparency, the integrity of public information and the capacity for civic engagement are severely compromised, endangering democratic accountability.
