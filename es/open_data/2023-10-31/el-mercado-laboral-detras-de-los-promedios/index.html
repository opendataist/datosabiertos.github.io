El mercado laboral detrás de los promedios

The article highlights that while Uruguay’s labor market appears stable on average, open data reveals deep structural disparities across different population segments. By disaggregating employment statistics, the information exposes significant vulnerabilities hidden by aggregate trends, demonstrating how socioeconomic factors heavily influence individual labor outcomes. Age, education, and ethnicity create stark divides in labor participation. Young people face disproportionately high unemployment rates, while those with lower educational levels struggle compared to tertiary graduates. Furthermore, Afro-descendant populations experience higher unemployment and greater job informality, indicating that historical and social inequalities persist within the formal and informal labor sectors. This data is crucial for open_data initiatives because it promotes transparency and accountability. Access to granular, disaggregated datasets allows researchers and citizens to identify systemic inequities that general reports might obscure. Such openness facilitates targeted policy-making and public debate, ensuring that labor strategies address the specific needs of marginalized groups rather than relying on misleading averages.
