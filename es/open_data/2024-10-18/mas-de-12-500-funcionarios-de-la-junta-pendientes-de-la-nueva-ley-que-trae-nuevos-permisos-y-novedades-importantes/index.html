Más de 12.500 funcionarios de la Junta, pendientes de la nueva ley que trae nuevos permisos y novedades importantes

The draft Law of Public Function for Castilla y León represents a significant modernization of the region’s administrative framework, aligning it with current state legislation and societal needs. By consolidating regulations into a single, coherent text, the initiative aims to provide legal security for employees while enhancing the quality of public services. This comprehensive approach ensures that the administration’s structure reflects the reality of modern employment, moving beyond outdated norms to create a more efficient and responsive public sector. Key innovations include the expansion of parental leave rights and the formal reclassification of IT personnel from temporary staff to permanent civil servants. These changes prioritize employee well-being and professional stability, addressing specific demands for better work-life balance and adapting the workforce structure to contemporary technological requirements. Such updates not only improve conditions for staff but also signal a commitment to recognizing digital competencies and family-friendly policies as central pillars of modern public employment. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives because a modernized, stable, and digitally skilled civil service is essential for managing and leveraging open government information effectively. Clear, updated legal frameworks facilitate better data governance, ensuring that those responsible for public administration have the security and tools needed to handle information transparency. Furthermore, by integrating digital competence requirements, the law supports the technical capacity necessary for robust open data practices, ultimately fostering greater citizen engagement and trust through more accessible and reliable public services.
