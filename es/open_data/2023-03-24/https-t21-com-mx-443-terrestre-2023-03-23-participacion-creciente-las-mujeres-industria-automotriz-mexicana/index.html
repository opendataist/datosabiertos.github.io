The article highlights a critical disparity in gender representation within Mexico’s transport sector, where women remain significantly underrepresented in both general employment and operational roles. Despite global comparisons showing low female participation in Latin America, industry leaders observe a gradual increase in women occupying decision-making and expert positions within corporate structures. This shift suggests a positive trend toward professional integration, even as traditional roles, particularly driving, remain heavily male-dominated due to historical and structural barriers. A central theme is the strategic necessity of collecting granular, gender-disaggregated data to inform effective public policy and corporate strategies. Stakeholders emphasize that accurate statistics are not merely descriptive but serve as foundational tools for government officials to design equitable interventions and for companies to identify skill gaps. By tracking participation rates across different hierarchy levels, organizations can better address the talent shortage in autotransport while promoting inclusive growth, turning demographic insights into actionable business and policy advantages. This narrative is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the link between transparent, accessible statistics and tangible social progress. Without open, reliable datasets on labor participation, policymakers and industry associations cannot effectively monitor gender equity or measure the impact of diversity initiatives. The call for better data collection exemplifies how open data acts as a catalyst for accountability, enabling society to recognize the value of female contributors and dismantle systemic barriers in the logistics supply chain.
