Datos abiertos y fiscalización

The article argues that modern democratic governance increasingly relies on transparency and collaboration to manage the relationship between the state and society. The exponential growth of the internet has accelerated this shift, making real-time accountability essential. Consequently, public institutions must evolve their management dynamics to foster openness, moving beyond traditional administrative structures to engage citizens more directly through accessible digital channels. Open data serves as the critical mechanism for realizing these democratic ideals, defined as digital information freely available for reuse and redistribution. While international frameworks like the International Open Data Charter have established a global consensus on the right to access information, legal norms alone are insufficient. True progress requires implementing robust public policies and institutional practices that ensure data is not just published, but effectively utilized to enhance governance and public trust. The relevance of this text to open data lies in demonstrating its functional value for oversight and accountability. By providing accessible tools, such as audit consultation systems, institutions enable citizens to analyze fiscal performance and track enforcement actions. This interoperability allows for meaningful public interaction with government data, creating virtuous circles of transparency. Ultimately, the article highlights that the strategic application of open data is vital for strengthening democratic institutions and ensuring that public information translates into tangible civic engagement and improved governance outcomes.
