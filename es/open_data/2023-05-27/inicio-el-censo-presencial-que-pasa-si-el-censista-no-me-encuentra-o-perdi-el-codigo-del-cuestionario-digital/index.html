Inició el censo presencial: ¿qué pasa si el censista no me encuentra o perdí el código del cuestionario digital?

The 2023 Census in Uruguay has transitioned from its initial digital phase to a critical in-person stage, marking a significant milestone in national data collection efforts. The main conclusion is that the process now relies on a hybrid validation system: residents who completed the survey online must present a unique completion code to census takers during home visits. This step is essential to finalize the registration, as the digital responses are strictly linked to specific geographic coordinates and cannot be validated remotely or transferred to other locations. Relevance to open data lies in the methodological rigor of this hybrid approach, which ensures the accuracy and geospatial integrity of the national database. By requiring physical verification of digital submissions, the Institute of National Statistics (INE) mitigates risks of fraud or erroneous data entry, thereby enhancing the reliability of the information used for public policy and resource allocation. This dual-layer validation serves as a best practice model for maintaining high-quality, trustworthy datasets in large-scale demographic studies. The results highlight a successful digital adoption, with Uruguay leading Latin America in online participation rates and achieving a broad demographic reach. However, the ongoing in-person phase remains vital to capture households that missed the initial window or require alternative assistance. This strategy underscores the importance of accessible, multi-channel data collection methods, ensuring that no segment of the population is excluded from the final, comprehensive dataset that will shape future national planning.
