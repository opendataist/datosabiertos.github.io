Expertos en ciberseguridad desmontan el ‘pajaporte’: “es el sueño húmedo de un dictador”

The proposed "digital passport" has ignited fierce debate by imposing strict identity verification and usage limits for adult content access. Critics argue this measure fundamentally violates civil liberties, treating citizens like subjects of surveillance rather than empowering parents to manage their children’s digital education. The approach shifts responsibility from families and schools to the state, enforcing control over personal internet behavior under the guise of protection. Cybersecurity experts highlight that the plan fails to effectively block minors from accessing such content via international platforms while creating severe privacy risks. Storing sensitive data about who accesses specific websites increases the danger of leaks, potentially exposing high-profile individuals to embarrassment or blackmail. This infrastructure creates a dangerous precedent where the state collects intimate behavioral data, raising concerns about data security and the potential for misuse if systems are compromised. This article is crucial for open data discussions as it illustrates the tension between government data collection and individual privacy. It warns against creating centralized databases that aggregate sensitive personal information, emphasizing the need for transparency and security in digital policies. The controversy underscores how well-intentioned digital initiatives can inadvertently erode trust and expand state surveillance capabilities, challenging the principles of data minimization and user autonomy that are central to robust open data frameworks.
