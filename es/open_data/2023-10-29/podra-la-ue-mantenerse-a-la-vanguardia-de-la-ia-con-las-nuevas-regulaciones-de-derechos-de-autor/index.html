¿Podrá la UE mantenerse a la vanguardia de la IA con las nuevas regulaciones de derechos de autor?

The European Parliament’s proposed amendment to the AI Act requires developers to summarize copyrighted training materials, a mandate critics deem impractical and overly burdensome. This obligation would force companies to process vast amounts of internet content, creating significant compliance challenges due to the ambiguity of copyright statuses. Consequently, the rule could hinder innovation and drive AI specialists away from the region, undermining Europe’s global competitiveness in a critical technological sector. Open data thrives on the free flow and reuse of information to fuel innovation and research. By imposing strict, opaque barriers to data access for training AI models, this legislation directly contradicts the principles of open data transparency and accessibility. It prioritizes restrictive copyright control over the collaborative sharing of knowledge, potentially stifling the development of AI systems that rely on diverse, openly available datasets. Therefore, the debate surrounding this enmenda is highly relevant to open data advocates. It highlights the tension between protecting intellectual property and maintaining an open ecosystem necessary for technological progress. If adopted, this precedent could globally limit the availability of data for AI training, fragmenting the digital landscape and reducing the potential benefits of shared information resources.
