El INE eleva a 21,3 millones los ocupados y al 11,8% la tasa de paro al cierre de 2023

The revision of the Labor Force Survey data reveals that integrating the 2021 census results significantly adjusted previous estimates, increasing the number of employed and unemployed individuals while slightly raising the unemployment rate. This adjustment underscores the necessity of updating statistical bases to reflect demographic changes, ensuring that open labor market data remains accurate and representative of the actual population structure. A critical insight for policy analysis is the limited role of public employment services compared to temporary work agencies, with less than 2% of hires facilitated by public offices. This highlights a structural reliance on private intermediaries, suggesting that open data initiatives should focus on transparency regarding private sector recruitment channels to better understand labor market dynamics and potential disparities in access to employment resources. Furthermore, the data reveals persistent gender inequalities, particularly in caregiving responsibilities and job hierarchies, as well as a workforce predominantly characterized by subordinate roles without managerial authority. These patterns indicate that open data platforms must prioritize disaggregated metrics on gender, care duties, and job autonomy to effectively support research on social equity and labor conditions, driving more informed public debates and targeted interventions.
