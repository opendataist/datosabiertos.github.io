The article highlights a resurgence in inflation, driven primarily by rising costs in food, fuel, and leisure. This trend reverses recent declines and underscores a persistent disparity between corporate profits and the purchasing power of ordinary citizens. Unions argue that excessive margins in essential sectors, particularly the food industry, are transferring economic pressures onto workers, creating a critical gap between business earnings and household affordability. The core issue extends beyond mere price increases; it reflects structural problems in market competition and the unequal distribution of inflationary burdens. Essential goods, which cannot be substituted or avoided, are seeing disproportionate price hikes, disproportionately affecting low-income households. The narrative suggests that without intervention to curb excessive corporate margins and ensure fair pricing, the cost of living will continue to erode real wages, exacerbating social inequality and economic instability. This report is relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparent statistical indicators, like the Consumer Price Index, serve as vital tools for public accountability and policy advocacy. Open data allows civil society and labor organizations to independently verify economic trends, analyze sector-specific price behaviors, and demand evidence-based interventions. By making these metrics accessible, open data empowers citizens to challenge opaque market practices and push for regulations that prioritize social welfare over unchecked corporate profitability.
