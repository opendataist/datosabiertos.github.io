Fausto Cálix sobre boletos aéreos comprados en papelería: “Todo se publica en el portal de transparencia”

The Honduran Customs Administration claims full transparency by publishing its information on the official portal. However, investigative findings reveal a significant discrepancy between these assertions and the actual data quality. While the institution uploads purchase records, the documentation remains fundamentally incomplete, failing to provide essential supporting evidence such as invoices and competitive bidding details for several major transactions. Specifically, substantial funds were spent on air travel and lodging through a stationery supplier without the required public documentation for the procurement processes. Although some hotel services were properly recorded, the lack of transparent bidding records for flight tickets prevents external verification of cost-effectiveness or procedural compliance. This gap undermines the integrity of the financial disclosures and obscures whether public resources were managed efficiently or if potential irregularities occurred during these acquisitions. This case is critical for open data because it highlights that mere publication of files does not equate to true transparency. Effective open data requires completeness and accessibility of underlying evidence, not just the existence of summary logs. When official records admit to pending documentation, the dataset becomes unreliable for accountability purposes. Thus, the article demonstrates that robust open data standards must enforce comprehensive documentation to ensure genuine public oversight and trust in government operations.
