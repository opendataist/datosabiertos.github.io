El Geoportal del Ayuntamiento se consolida como referente de innovación con 180.000 usuarios al mes y 700 conjuntos de datos

The article highlights the strategic role of geospatial data in urban management, emphasizing Madrid’s commitment to open infrastructure through its Geoportal. This platform serves as a critical reference point for professionals, offering free access to vast amounts of geographic information. By prioritizing open data principles, the city enables the reutilization, modification, and commercial or non-commercial adaptation of spatial datasets, thereby fostering innovation and transparency in public service delivery. A key implication is the enhanced collaboration across municipal departments, which generates diverse, high-value datasets that address specific social and urban challenges. Projects ranging from fighting loneliness among the elderly to promoting public health through walking routes demonstrate how open geodata drives tangible societal benefits. This interdisciplinary approach not only enriches the data catalog but also ensures that technological tools directly support citizen well-being and efficient urban planning. The relevance to open data lies in the establishment of a robust, continuously updated framework that surpasses traditional periodicity standards. By integrating mobile mapping, drones, and collaborative efforts, the city maintains a dynamic digital twin of its urban environment. This case study illustrates how municipal governments can leverage open geospatial technologies to create transparent, accessible, and highly useful resources that empower both public administration and private innovation.
