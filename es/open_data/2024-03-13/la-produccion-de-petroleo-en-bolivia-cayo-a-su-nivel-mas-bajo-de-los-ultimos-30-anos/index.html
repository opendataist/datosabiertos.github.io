La producción de petróleo en Bolivia cayó a su nivel más bajo de los últimos 30 años

Bolivia’s oil production has plummeted to its lowest levels in three decades, driven by the natural depletion of wells and marking a stark decline from mid-2010s peaks. This sustained drop in domestic output forces the nation to rely increasingly on imports to maintain energy supply stability and meet internal demand for refined products. To mitigate costs and ensure adequate fuel supply, YPFB has initiated direct crude imports, a strategy projected to generate significant national savings. By securing raw materials through international shipping, the state aims to replenish domestic reserves and guarantee the availability of essential derivatives like diesel and gasoline despite the shrinking local extraction capacity. This narrative highlights the critical role of open data in monitoring resource depletion and informing public policy. Transparent statistical reporting from entities like IBCE and INE allows citizens and analysts to track economic trends, understand the urgency of infrastructure investments, and evaluate the government’s strategic responses to dwindling natural resources.
