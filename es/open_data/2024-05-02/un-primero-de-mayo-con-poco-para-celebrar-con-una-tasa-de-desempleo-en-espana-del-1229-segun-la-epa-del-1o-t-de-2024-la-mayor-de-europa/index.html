Un Primero de Mayo con poco para celebrar, con una tasa de desempleo en España del 12,29% según la EPA del 1º T. de 2024, la mayor de Europa

The latest Spanish labor data reveals a significant quarterly contraction in employment, particularly affecting women and full-time positions. This downturn highlights structural vulnerabilities, as the surge in permanent contracts was overshadowed by a substantial drop in temporary roles. Consequently, unemployment rose, placing increased pressure on households and signaling immediate economic instability despite long-term annual gains. The relevance of this report to open data lies in its demonstration of how granular, public statistics can expose nuanced societal trends that aggregate figures might obscure. By providing detailed breakdowns by gender, nationality, and sector, open datasets enable rigorous analysis of labor market disparities. This transparency is crucial for identifying which demographics and industries are most vulnerable, allowing for more targeted policy interventions and academic research. Ultimately, the article underscores the importance of accessible statistical transparency in holding political power accountable. When data is openly available, it empowers citizens to move beyond theatrical political discourse and focus on tangible socio-economic realities. Open data serves as a vital tool for democratic oversight, ensuring that decision-makers address actual labor crises rather than performing for public consumption.
