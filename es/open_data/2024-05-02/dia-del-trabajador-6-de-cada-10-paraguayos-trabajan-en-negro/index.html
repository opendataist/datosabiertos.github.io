Día del Trabajador: 6 de cada 10 paraguayos trabajan "en negro"

The article highlights the critical issue of informal labor in Paraguay, revealing that approximately 62% of employed individuals work without formal social security or tax registration. This prevalence is driven by economic realities where significant portions of the workforce earn below the minimum wage and lack IPS contributions. Understanding these dynamics is vital for open_data initiatives, as they demand transparent, granular datasets to accurately map labor conditions. By exposing the gap between official employment figures and actual social protection coverage, such data enables researchers and policymakers to analyze the structural weaknesses of the labor market effectively. Consequently, this information underscores the necessity for differentiated policy interventions to protect vulnerable workers. The transparency provided by official statistics supports public discourse on labor rights, facilitating evidence-based advocacy for improved social security systems and safer working environments across both urban and rural sectors.
