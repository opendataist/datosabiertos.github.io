Brindaron un taller vinculado a adolescentes y redes sociales: profundizaron en el ciberbullying

Health professionals at a primary care unit recently conducted workshops for adolescents focusing on online risks, digital footprints, and cyberbullying. The initiative aimed to empower students with strategies to handle harassment while emphasizing the critical connection between these digital threats and mental health. Participants demonstrated a clear understanding of the issues, utilizing dramatic performances to reinforce learning and highlighting the urgent need for awareness in navigating social media. The relevance of this initiative to open data lies in the necessity of transparent, accessible public health information to combat widespread digital violence. By making statistics and support protocols publicly available, communities can better recognize the scale of cyberbullying and identify available resources. This transparency encourages proactive engagement, allowing families and educators to access verified data rather than relying on anecdotal evidence, thereby fostering a safer digital environment through informed collective action. Ultimately, the project underscores the vital role of collaboration between healthcare providers and educational institutions. Rather than replacing teachers, health units provide specialized support, ensuring that professionals are equipped to guide students through complex social challenges. This cooperative model ensures that accurate health information is integrated into schools, promoting open dialogue and ensuring that adolescents have reliable channels for seeking help when facing online abuse.
