El creciente problema de vacantes en un país con 2,7 millones de parados: expertos y empresas urgen a reformar el SEPE

The article highlights a critical mismatch in the Spanish labor market, where hundreds of thousands of job vacancies remain unfilled despite high unemployment rates. This paradox underscores the inefficiency of the Public Employment Service, which fails to adequately connect job seekers with available positions. Consequently, many small and medium-sized enterprises do not rely on these public agencies, creating a vicious cycle that exacerbates labor shortages and hinders economic activity. The consequences of this structural dysfunction are severe, including reduced production, increased wage pressures, and the potential closure of family businesses. The lack of skilled workers is particularly acute in sectors like hospitality and construction, directly impacting corporate growth and operational costs. This situation reveals that current passive and active employment policies require urgent modernization and better coordination to address the growing disconnect between the workforce’s skills and market demands. This text is highly relevant to open data because the labor market’s inefficiency stems partly from poor information flow and analysis. Open data initiatives could improve the transparency and accessibility of labor statistics, enabling better matching algorithms between job seekers and employers. By making detailed employment data publicly available and standardized, policymakers and companies can diagnose mismatches more accurately, fostering a more responsive and efficient labor ecosystem that leverages data-driven decision-making.
