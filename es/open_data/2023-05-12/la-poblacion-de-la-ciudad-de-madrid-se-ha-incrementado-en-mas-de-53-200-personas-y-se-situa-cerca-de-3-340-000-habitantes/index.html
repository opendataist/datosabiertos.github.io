La población de la ciudad de Madrid se ha incrementado en más de 53.200 personas y se sitúa cerca de 3.340.000 habitantes

The annual revision of Madrid’s municipal register confirms that population growth is driven exclusively by international migration, reversing previous negative trends. While natural growth remains negative due to fewer births and increasing mortality, and internal migration results in a net population loss to other Spanish municipalities, the surge in foreign residents has offset these declines. This dynamic highlights that external migration is the primary factor determining the city’s demographic expansion. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it illustrates the critical importance of granular, longitudinal datasets for accurate urban planning and social policy. By breaking down statistics by district and nationality, policymakers can identify specific needs, such as infrastructure demands in high-growth areas like Carabanchel or social services for diverse communities. Open access to such detailed information allows for transparent monitoring of how different neighborhoods are affected by global mobility trends. Furthermore, understanding the distinction between natural growth and migratory balances helps evaluate the sustainability of current demographic models. The shift from a previously declining trend to one reliant on international inflows requires adaptive strategies in housing, education, and healthcare. Transparent reporting of these metrics fosters public accountability and ensures that municipal decisions are based on comprehensive, real-time evidence rather than aggregated generalizations.
