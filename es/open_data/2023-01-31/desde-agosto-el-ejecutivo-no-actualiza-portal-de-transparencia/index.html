Desde agosto el Ejecutivo no actualiza portal de transparencia

Many Honduran state institutions have consistently failed to comply with legal mandates requiring the monthly publication of transparency data, particularly concerning public procurement and expenses. While regulations stipulate that this information must be updated within ten days of any changes, major bodies like the Executive and Legislative branches have left their portals outdated for months. This systemic neglect means citizens cannot verify how public resources are being utilized, undermining the fundamental principle that transparency is essential for accountability and corruption prevention. The lack of timely data exposes significant gaps in public oversight, as essential details regarding government spending remain hidden from scrutiny. Although the Institute for Access to Public Information is currently verifying compliance and planning to issue sanctions for non-compliance, it faces operational challenges, including a shortage of auditors and recent changes to the digital platform that have confused some users. The authorities acknowledge the severity of these delays and are working to finalize reports that will compel institutions to rectify their omissions and fulfill their legal obligations. This situation is highly relevant to the open data community because it illustrates the critical gap between legal frameworks for data availability and actual institutional practice. Open data initiatives rely not just on the existence of platforms, but on the consistent, timely, and accurate release of machine-readable information. When the state fails to maintain these data streams, the potential for civic engagement and data-driven advocacy is severely limited, highlighting the urgent need for robust monitoring mechanisms and sustained pressure to ensure that transparency laws translate into accessible, living data for the public.
