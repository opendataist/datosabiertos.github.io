Una de cada seis viviendas habitadas en Menorca se utiliza menos de tres meses al año

Menorca exhibits a significant housing disparity, where nearly a third of properties are classified as low-consumption or sporadic-use homes. This reflects a market heavily skewed toward tourism, with coastal urbanizations concentrating the majority of these vacation-oriented residences. Consequently, traditional housing metrics often fail to capture the true extent of this seasonal dynamic. Simultaneously, high electricity consumption reveals a stark contrast between tourist properties and permanent residents. While many homes operate well below average energy thresholds, a substantial segment exceeds typical annual usage, indicating heavy reliance on utilities by non-primarily occupied dwellings. This consumption pattern underscores the structural difference between holiday homes and inhabited family residences. This analysis is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates how granular datasets can expose hidden urban trends. By cross-referencing habitation status with energy usage, policymakers can better understand housing availability and tourism impact. Such transparency enables more accurate urban planning and highlights the necessity of accessible, detailed statistics for effective governance.
