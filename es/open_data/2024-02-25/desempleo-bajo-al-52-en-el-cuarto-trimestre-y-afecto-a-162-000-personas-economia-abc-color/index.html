Desempleo bajó al 5,2% en el cuarto trimestre y afectó a 162.000 personas - Economía - ABC Color

The latest labor survey reveals a complex labor market landscape where traditional unemployment metrics improve while hidden underemployment rises. This divergence highlights a critical nuance in economic health: while fewer people are actively jobless, a growing segment of the workforce is working fewer hours than desired. This suggests that statistical gains in employment rates may not fully reflect the quality or stability of jobs available, pointing to structural vulnerabilities in the labor sector that pure headcount figures often obscure. The rise in partial unemployment indicates significant underutilization of human capital, challenging simplistic narratives of labor market recovery. When combined with standard unemployment, the total share of workers experiencing job insecurity or insufficient hours constitutes a substantial portion of the active population. This broader metric offers a more realistic assessment of labor distress, demonstrating that policy makers and economists must look beyond basic employment rates to understand true workforce well-being and economic resilience. This data is vital for open data initiatives as it underscores the necessity of granular, accessible labor statistics for comprehensive public scrutiny. By providing detailed breakdowns of employment types, such surveys empower researchers and citizens to detect emerging trends like underemployment early. Transparent access to these nuanced indicators fosters more informed public debate and ensures that policy responses address not just job creation, but also job quality and working conditions, thereby strengthening democratic oversight of economic management.
