¿Se puede vivir en València con un sueldo de 1.500 euros al mes?

The article concludes that living independently in Valencia on a net monthly salary of 1,500 euros is virtually impossible without external financial support. Experts and resident associations agree that housing costs have escalated to such an extent that this income level, which approximates local realities more than official statistics, is insufficient to cover rent and basic living expenses. Consequently, the majority of individuals in this income bracket are forced to rely on family savings, parental help, or shared living arrangements to remain in the city. This housing crisis extends beyond young people, affecting adults and seniors who can no longer afford the rising costs of both rental and purchase markets. The data reveals a significant portion of the population, particularly those between 20 and 40 years old, continues to live with their parents or returns to them after separation, highlighting a regression in living independence. This trend underscores a systemic failure where standard wages no longer correlate with the ability to sustain a autonomous household, leading to social immobility and increased economic vulnerability for all demographics. This analysis is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical gap between official statistical benchmarks and lived economic realities. It emphasizes the need for granular, transparent datasets regarding local housing markets and cost-of-living indices to drive effective policy. By exposing these discrepancies, the article advocates for greater data openness to hold institutions accountable and ensure that public services and minimum wage standards reflect the actual financial pressures faced by citizens.
