Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The government has introduced two pivotal legislative proposals aiming to modernize the national framework for digital rights and security. The first initiative establishes a comprehensive cybersecurity regime, creating a specialized Agency to define standards, monitor threats, and enforce compliance across all public entities and private organizations handling public resources. This shift addresses growing cyber risks by mandating robust protection measures and implementing significant sanctions for non-compliance, thereby strengthening institutional resilience against data compromises. Simultaneously, the second proposal introduces a robust law for the personal data protection, granting individuals extensive rights including access, rectification, and portability, while prohibiting the forced disclosure of sensitive information. By centralizing oversight under the same cybersecurity agency and repealing outdated access-to-information provisions, this legislation standardizes data treatment across both public and private sectors. It ensures that data sharing and international transfers adhere to strict security protocols, prioritizing citizen privacy and informed consent. These proposals are highly relevant to open data because they redefine the boundaries between transparency and privacy. While they enhance the security infrastructure necessary to protect public information systems, they also impose stricter constraints on how personal data can be processed and shared. This creates a balanced ecosystem where open data initiatives can operate securely, ensuring that the release of government information does not compromise individual rights or national security, thus fostering greater public trust in digital governance.
