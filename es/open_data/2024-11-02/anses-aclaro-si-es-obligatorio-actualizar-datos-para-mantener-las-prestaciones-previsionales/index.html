ANSES aclaró si es obligatorio actualizar datos para mantener las prestaciones previsionales

This article clarifies that the Argentine social security administration does not revoke benefits for citizens who fail to update their contact information. This distinction separates mandatory legal requirements from recommended administrative best practices, ensuring that access to public welfare remains protected regardless of data currency. The emphasis shifts from punitive compliance to voluntary engagement, prioritizing user rights over bureaucratic efficiency. The organization promotes updating details as a good practice to improve direct communication channels and protect individuals from potential fraud. By encouraging regular verification of email and phone numbers through secure digital platforms, the agency aims to enhance service delivery and security. However, this advice is framed as optional guidance rather than a strict condition for maintaining eligibility or continuing to receive pensions. This news is relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the tension between data utility and privacy rights in public services. It demonstrates that while governments benefit from accurate, updated datasets for operational efficiency, they must not leverage essential social rights as leverage for data compliance. Transparent communication about these policies is crucial for maintaining public trust and ensuring that open data efforts do not inadvertently compromise access to fundamental social guarantees.
