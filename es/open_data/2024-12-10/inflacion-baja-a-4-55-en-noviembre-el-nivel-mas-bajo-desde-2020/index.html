Inflación baja a 4.55% en noviembre, el nivel más bajo desde 2020

Mexico’s inflation moderated in November, stabilizing at 4.55 percent annually after a recent uptick. This decrease was driven significantly by falling prices for fresh produce and electronics, which offset spikes in electricity costs due to the end of seasonal tariff programs. The sub-inflation rate also reached its lowest level since April 2020, indicating a broader trend of price stabilization across less volatile sectors. The distinction between core and non-core inflation highlights that while food and energy prices remain volatile, the underlying economic pressure is easing. Services prices continue to rise, yet goods prices are declining, suggesting complex market dynamics. This bifurcation implies that while immediate consumption costs for staples may fluctuate, the fundamental inflationary pressures are cooling, allowing for more predictable economic planning. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, high-frequency statistical monitoring for economic policy. Reliable, accessible inflation metrics enable researchers and citizens to scrutinize government efficacy and market health. By making such granular data available, societies can foster informed debate on cost-of-living impacts, ensuring that economic indicators serve public accountability rather than remaining opaque institutional metrics.
