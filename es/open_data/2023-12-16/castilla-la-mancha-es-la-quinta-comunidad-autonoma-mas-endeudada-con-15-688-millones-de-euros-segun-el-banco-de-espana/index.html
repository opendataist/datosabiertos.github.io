Castilla-La Mancha es la quinta comunidad autónoma más endeudada con 15.688 millones de euros, según el Banco de España

The article highlights a record absolute increase in public debt, driven by pandemic and inflationary impacts, yet emphasizes a crucial positive trend: the debt-to-GDP ratio has moderated significantly. This decoupling suggests that economic growth is outpacing debt accumulation, positioning the administration to meet fiscal consolidation targets earlier than planned. This dynamic illustrates how aggregate figures can mask underlying stability when normalized against economic performance, a key metric in assessing sovereign solvency. Furthermore, the data reveals a clear hierarchy in debt distribution across administrative levels. The central government and Social Security account for the vast majority of the burden, while autonomous communities and local entities hold smaller but distinct shares. Notably, specific regions like the Valencian Community and Catalonia show higher relative debt-to-GDP ratios despite their economic weight. This granularity is essential for understanding structural fiscal disparities and ensuring that financial health assessments are not overly generalized. This report is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the necessity of multi-dimensional fiscal transparency. By providing disaggregated data across time, administrative sectors, and geographic regions, it allows for more nuanced policy analysis and accountability. Open access to such detailed, structured statistics empowers researchers and citizens to scrutinize government efficiency and equity, moving beyond simple headline numbers to evaluate the true sustainability of public finances.
