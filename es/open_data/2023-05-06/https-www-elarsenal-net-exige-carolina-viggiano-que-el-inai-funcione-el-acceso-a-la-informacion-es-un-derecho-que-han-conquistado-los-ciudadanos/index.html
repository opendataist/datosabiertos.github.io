The article highlights the critical gap between legal transparency guarantees and actual government accountability in Mexico. It emphasizes that while citizens possess a constitutional right to public information, institutional resistance often hinders its effective exercise. This discrepancy undermines democratic oversight and trust in federal governance. The narrative illustrates how opaque practices persist despite established legal frameworks, as seen in unanswered inquiries regarding significant public funds. These cases demonstrate that without robust implementation, transparency remains more aspirational than real. Consequently, citizens face barriers when attempting to verify how public resources are managed. This situation is relevant to open_data because it underscores the necessity of accessible, timely, and complete data releases for meaningful civic engagement. True transparency requires not just legal rights but active cultural shifts within institutions. Ensuring data openness helps bridge the trust gap and empowers society to hold officials accountable through verified information.
