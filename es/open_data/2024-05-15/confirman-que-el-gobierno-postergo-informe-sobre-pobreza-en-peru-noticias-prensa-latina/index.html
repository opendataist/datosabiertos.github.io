Confirman que el gobierno postergó informe sobre pobreza en Perú - Noticias Prensa Latina

The article highlights a political controversy regarding the delayed publication of Peru's 2023 poverty report, which officials deny was intended to suppress negative data. Despite the postponement, the study was eventually released, revealing a significant rise in national poverty rates alongside declining household incomes and rising food costs, affecting over twenty million citizens. This case is crucial for open data advocates as it underscores the importance of transparent, timely access to public statistics. Political pressures attempting to control the narrative of sensitive social indicators threaten data integrity and public trust. Ensuring that statistical agencies can release findings without undue interference is essential for accountability and informed civic engagement. Furthermore, the data exposes deep regional inequalities, with Andean and Amazonian areas suffering disproportionately high poverty levels. These figures illustrate the urgent need for policy interventions based on accurate, unrestricted information. Protecting the independence of national statistical institutes ensures that such critical socioeconomic realities are visible, enabling better resource allocation and social justice efforts.
