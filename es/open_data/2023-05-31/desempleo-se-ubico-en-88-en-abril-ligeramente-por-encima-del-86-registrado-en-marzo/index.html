Desempleo se ubicó en 8,8% en abril, ligeramente por encima del 8,6% registrado en marzo

The INE’s latest labor data reveals a slight rise in unemployment, marking the second consecutive monthly increase. This trend highlights ongoing volatility in the job market, contrasting with previous declines. Analyzing these shifts is crucial for understanding the broader economic health and labor dynamics within the country. Disaggregated analysis shows higher unemployment rates in the interior compared to the capital, underscoring regional disparities. Employment and activity rates also vary by location, indicating that economic participation is not uniform across the nation. Such granular insights help identify specific areas requiring targeted policy interventions to address uneven labor market performance. Furthermore, significant portions of the workforce face subemployment and lack social security registration. These metrics expose underlying structural vulnerabilities, such as informal labor practices and underutilization of skills. For open data, this article demonstrates how detailed, granular datasets are essential for diagnosing economic inequalities. Transparent access to such statistics empowers researchers and citizens to hold institutions accountable and drive informed public discourse on labor rights and economic policy.
