Los precios vuelven a bajar: la inflación cae al 3,2% y la subyacente se frena al 6,1% en mayo

Spanish inflation has reached its lowest level since mid-2021, driven primarily by falling fuel prices and a moderation in food costs. This significant decline places Spain among the European Union countries with the lowest inflation rates, highlighting a clear downward trend initiated in the first quarter of the year. Although underlying inflation remains elevated, the overall trajectory demonstrates that deflationary pressures are increasingly outweighing inflationary ones, signaling a gradual normalization of price levels across the economy. The persistence of high inflation in the services sector and certain food categories introduces uncertainty, yet experts project a continued progressive slowdown throughout the year. Forecasts suggest a steady convergence toward target levels by 2024, despite potential volatility caused by seasonal effects and environmental factors like drought. The contrast between the rapid initial transmission of energy costs and their slower reversal in production chains underscores the complexity of the current economic adjustment, with analysts expecting further deceleration in the coming months. This article is relevant to open_data because it illustrates how standardized, publicly available statistical indicators, such as the Consumer Price Index (IPC), serve as critical public goods for economic transparency. Reliable open data allows diverse stakeholders, from financial institutions to citizens, to monitor real-time economic health, verify policy effectiveness, and make informed decisions. By democratizing access to official metrics, open data fosters accountability and enables a deeper understanding of macroeconomic trends, ensuring that societal responses to inflation are grounded in verified, accessible evidence rather than speculation.
