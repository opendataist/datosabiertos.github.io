La población residente en España supera los 48,7 millones de habitantes y marca nuevo máximo histórico por los migrantes

Spain’s population has reached a historic high, driven primarily by significant migration rather than natural growth. This shift highlights how external demographic dynamics are reshaping the nation’s social fabric, with foreign nationals contributing disproportionately to recent increases compared to the declining number of native-born residents. Geographically, population growth is heavily concentrated in specific autonomous communities, creating a disparity between densely populated urban centers and regions with stagnating or declining numbers. This uneven distribution presents challenges for resource allocation and regional planning, as a significant majority of residents live in just a few key areas while others face depopulation pressures. For open data, this article underscores the critical need for accessible, granular demographic datasets. Understanding the interplay between migration, regional distribution, and total population growth requires transparent statistical sources. Reliable open data enables policymakers and researchers to analyze these complex trends effectively, ensuring informed decisions regarding housing, infrastructure, and social services across different regions.
