Itaipue también podría desaparecer por reformas federales

The proposed constitutional reform threatening the dissolution of the INAI and state transparency bodies like Itaipue signals a severe regression in Mexico’s transparency framework. The main conclusion is that removing these autonomous guarantor agencies dismantles the regulatory architecture essential for uniform access to information. Without a central regulator, obligated subjects may provide inconsistent responses, undermining the consistency and legal certainty that citizens previously relied upon when filing requests across different jurisdictions. This loss of uniformity poses a direct risk to the right of access to information and personal data protection. Instead of independent bodies ensuring accountability, internal control bodies would assume decision-making power, potentially lacking the necessary independence and expertise. The narrative highlights that the current system, which includes various national institutes, operates as an integrated network; breaking this link creates a fragmented landscape where transparency standards could vary arbitrarily, eroding public trust and effective oversight mechanisms. This development is critically relevant to open data because it threatens the institutional stability required to maintain reliable, standardized public data ecosystems. Open data initiatives depend on robust legal frameworks and independent entities that enforce data publication and quality standards. If transparency bodies disappear, the enforcement of mandatory data release diminishes, potentially leading to data silos or incomplete information. Consequently, the reform jeopardizes the infrastructure that supports transparency, making it harder for civil society and researchers to access consistent, high-quality public data for accountability and innovation.
