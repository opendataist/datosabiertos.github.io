Esto es lo que gana un maestro de primaria en la SEP | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

Primary teaching ranks as one of Mexico’s most popular professions, underscoring the sector's critical role in shaping future professionals. However, the financial reality for these educators reveals significant disparities, with average salaries falling short of broader labor market benchmarks and highlighting a persistent gender pay gap within the profession. Recent governmental initiatives aim to rectify this by proposing salary increases to align public school teachers’ earnings with those of formally registered workers under social security systems. This move seeks to improve job stability and benefits, particularly for those in the public sector, while acknowledging the precarious conditions often faced by educators in private institutions who lack comparable protections. This topic is vital to open data initiatives because it relies on transparent, accessible statistics from agencies like INEGI and Glassdoor to expose economic inequalities. By making workforce demographics and compensation details publicly available, stakeholders can advocate for evidence-based policy reforms, ensuring that educational labor conditions are measured, understood, and improved through data-driven accountability.
