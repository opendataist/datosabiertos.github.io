Tercer Sector C-LM busca blindarse en Estatuto, reclama Consejo de Gobierno abierto y pide diálogo también a nivel local

The Third Sector in Castilla-La Mancha is pursuing the constitutional protection of social rights within the new Regional Statute, ensuring these guarantees remain independent of political shifts. They advocate for robust civil dialogue structures that extend beyond the regional government to include local municipalities and provincial councils, recognizing that most social services are delivered at the community level. This strategic shift emphasizes the unique role of associative entities and aims to formalize their interaction with all tiers of administration to better serve citizens. Simultaneously, the CERMI is pushing to strengthen the Permanent Non-Legislative Commission on Integral Disability Policies within the regional parliament. The goal is to transform this body into a vital platform where government officials from various departments, such as health and employment, must explain their strategies and progress regarding disability policies. This initiative seeks to reactivate the commission’s momentum after the pandemic slowdown, ensuring it fulfills its original purpose of facilitating substantive oversight and accountability. This development is crucial for open_data because it demands institutional transparency regarding how public resources are allocated to disability services. By requiring the government to explain budgetary decisions before a parliamentary commission, the process creates a framework for public scrutiny of spending. This promotes the availability of detailed, accessible information on fiscal priorities, enabling civil society and data advocates to monitor whether funds effectively address the actual needs of the disability community, thereby fostering greater accountability and evidence-based policy making.
