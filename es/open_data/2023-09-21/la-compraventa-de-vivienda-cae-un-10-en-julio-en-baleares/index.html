La compraventa de vivienda cae un 10 % en julio en Baleares

The Spanish housing market experienced a sharp contraction in July, marking the most significant drop since early 2021. This decline is primarily driven by rising interest rates, which have made mortgage financing substantially more expensive, effectively cooling demand. The data reveals a sustained downward trend over six consecutive months, indicating that higher borrowing costs are severely limiting transaction volumes across the national real estate sector. A key insight is the disproportionate impact on the secondary market, which accounts for the vast majority of transactions and saw prices and volumes fall more steeply than new constructions. This suggests that existing inventory owners are facing greater difficulty adapting to the current economic environment compared to developers. The divergence highlights how financial constraints hit the broader, more accessible segment of the housing market harder, potentially reshaping buyer behavior and inventory availability in the medium term. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of timely, granular public statistics for economic analysis. By providing accessible metrics on transaction types, regional variations, and housing categories, official data enables researchers and policymakers to accurately diagnose market health. Transparent data allows for better identification of regional disparities and the specific effects of monetary policy, ensuring that decisions regarding housing affordability and financial regulation are grounded in empirical evidence rather than speculation.
