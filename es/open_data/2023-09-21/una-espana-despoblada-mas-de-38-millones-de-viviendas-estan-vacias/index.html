Una España despoblada: más de 3,8 millones de viviendas están vacías

Spain faces a significant housing challenge, with over three million vacant homes nationwide, highlighting a persistent mismatch between market supply and demand. Although the percentage of empty housing has remained relatively stable, the absolute increase underscores systemic inefficiencies in balancing availability with accessibility. Geographic disparities are stark, as major urban centers and coastal cities have seen significant reductions in vacant properties due to population shifts toward economic hubs. Conversely, rural inland regions suffer from high vacancy rates driven by depopulation and the obsolescence of stock from past real estate booms, revealing how migration patterns outpace new construction in desirable areas. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates how granular, public census information can expose complex socio-economic inequalities. By making these detailed housing metrics accessible, stakeholders can better analyze market dysfunctions, such as illegal occupations and regional imbalances, fostering transparent policy discussions and evidence-based solutions for housing accessibility.
