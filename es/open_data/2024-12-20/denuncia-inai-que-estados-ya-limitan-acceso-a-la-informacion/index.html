Denuncia Inai que estados ya limitan acceso a la información

Mexican transparency authorities warn that the dissolution of the National Institute of Transparency (INAI) has already led to increased restrictions on access to information. Despite the legal mandate for the state to guarantee these rights, local officials in some states are refusing requests, citing the imminent extinction of autonomous bodies. This abrupt shift threatens the fundamental right to know, as bureaucratic transitions lack the institutional safeguards previously provided by independent regulators. The controversy highlights a critical vulnerability in Mexico’s governance structure: the removal of the autonomous watchdog creates a vacuum where accountability is compromised. While the government suggests the Secretariat of Anti-Corruption will assume responsibility, there is significant concern that this new arrangement will not adequately protect citizens against information denials. The narrative underscores how institutional design directly impacts the effectiveness of democratic oversight. This situation is vital to open data discourse because it illustrates the risks of dismantling independent oversight mechanisms. Autonomous bodies ensure that data is not just published, but is accessible and accountable to the public. When political interference or bureaucratic ambiguity replaces these checks, the quality and reliability of open government data deteriorate. Protecting institutional independence is therefore essential for maintaining the integrity and utility of open data ecosystems.
