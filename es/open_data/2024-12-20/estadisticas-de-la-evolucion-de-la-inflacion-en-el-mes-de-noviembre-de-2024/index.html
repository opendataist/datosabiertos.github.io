Estadísticas de la evolución de la inflación en el mes de noviembre de 2024

The article reports that Guinea Equatorial’s inflation rate reached zero percent in November 2024, marking a significant deceleration from the previous month. This stability masks underlying pressures, particularly in food, transportation, and housing, which continue to drive costs upward for consumers. While headline inflation stabilized, essential goods such as meat, fish, and fresh produce experienced substantial price increases, highlighting the persistent burden on household budgets for basic necessities. Further analysis reveals that underlying inflation, which excludes volatile energy and fresh food prices, remained at 2.4%. This trend was primarily fueled by rising costs for manufactured goods and imported products. The data indicates that economic pressures are widespread across different sectors, including health, alcohol, and tobacco, suggesting that cost-of-living challenges are structural rather than isolated to specific commodity fluctuations. City-by-city comparisons show varying degrees of impact, yet the common thread is the strain on essential spending categories. This data is highly relevant to open_data initiatives as it exemplifies the critical need for transparent, accessible, and timely statistical releases by national institutes like INEGE. Open access to granular inflation metrics allows researchers, journalists, and citizens to verify official narratives and understand local economic realities. By making this detailed breakdown available, the government supports evidence-based public debate and fosters trust in official statistics, which are essential for informed decision-making in both public policy and private investment.
