Partidos políticos ocultan sueldos de sus dirigentes, ante la extinción del INAI

The article reveals a stark contradiction among major Mexican political parties regarding transparency. Although these parties championed the defense of transparency institutions while facing the potential extinction of the INAI, their actual behavior remains opaque. When requested to provide specific data on salaries and benefits for their national leadership, they largely evaded disclosure through delays, outdated public records, or claims that their roles are unpaid and therefore exempt from such detailed reporting. This systemic lack of responsiveness undermines the very principles of open government they publicly endorse. The core implication for open data is that legal frameworks for transparency are insufficient without strict enforcement and accurate, real-time data management. The parties’ reliance on incomplete or obsolete information highlights a gap between the existence of transparency platforms and the actual accessibility of reliable public records. This suggests that simply mandating disclosure is not enough; data must be consistently maintained, updated, and verified to hold political entities accountable. Without this rigor, transparency mechanisms fail to serve their intended democratic function. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how institutional actors can exploit procedural loopholes to maintain secrecy. It demonstrates that open data initiatives must address not only the publication of information but also the integrity and timeliness of the datasets provided. The narrative serves as a warning that without robust mechanisms to ensure data quality and enforce timely responses, open data remains a theoretical ideal rather than a practical tool for citizen oversight and political accountability.
