Federación Salud Mental celebra el descenso de suicidio en C-LM, pero urge a prevenirlo reforzando atención psicológica

The article highlights that while suicide rates in Castilla-La Mancha and nationally decreased in 2023, the absolute numbers remain critically high. The main conclusion is that statistical improvements should not be celebrated as total success; rather, every suicide represents a preventable tragedy that demands continued, intensified prevention efforts. This perspective shifts the focus from mere data tracking to the urgent need for systemic intervention and emotional support infrastructure. The relevance to open data lies in how public statistics serve as a diagnostic tool for policy gaps rather than just metrics of performance. By making mortality data transparent, authorities can identify specific vulnerable demographics, such as men and middle-aged individuals, and geographic hotspots. However, raw data must be contextualized with qualitative insights about socioeconomic factors like labor precarity and mental health resource shortages. Without this deeper analysis, open data risks becoming a superficial indicator that overlooks the underlying structural causes of these deaths. Consequently, the article argues for integrating these statistical findings into political agendas to prioritize psychological care in primary healthcare and early emotional education. The implication is that data openness must drive concrete resource allocation and preventive strategies. Recognizing that suicidal thoughts often stem from transient suffering rather than a desire to die, society must leverage public information to foster supportive environments. Thus, open data is essential not for celebration, but for mobilizing the necessary societal and governmental response to address the root causes of suicide effectively.
