Cantabria aprueba el proyecto de ley de participación ciudadana y gobierno abierto

The regional government of Cantabria has approved a landmark law institutionalizing open government by establishing a comprehensive framework for citizen participation. This legislation moves beyond mere transparency, creating a structured environment where the public can actively influence policy design, execution, and evaluation. By legally recognizing five distinct participation instruments—such as deliberative processes and participatory budgets—the law aims to strengthen democratic engagement and rebuild public trust in institutions through more inclusive decision-making. The law mandates administrative obligations to ensure these mechanisms are accessible and effective, requiring the government to integrate participation into its daily operations and utilize digital platforms like a dedicated citizen portal. It emphasizes principles such as universal access, non-discrimination, and transparency, while establishing clear procedures for initiating and evaluating public consultations. The creation of a biennial participation program ensures that civic engagement becomes a routine, strategic component of governance rather than an ad hoc initiative. This development is highly relevant to the open_data movement as it codifies the infrastructure necessary for data-driven civic engagement. By legally requiring the government to facilitate public scrutiny and feedback on policies, the law creates a demand for accessible, high-quality public data. It underscores the necessity of transparent digital platforms and open administrative processes, ensuring that citizens have the tools and the right to monitor government actions, which is essential for fostering a culture of accountability and collaborative governance.
