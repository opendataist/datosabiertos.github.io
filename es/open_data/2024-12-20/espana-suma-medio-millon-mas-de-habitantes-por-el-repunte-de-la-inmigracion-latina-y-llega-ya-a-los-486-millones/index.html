España suma medio millón más de habitantes por el repunte de la inmigración latina y llega ya a los 48,6 millones

The 2024 census reveals a significant demographic shift in Spain, driven primarily by a substantial influx of foreign residents who now constitute a growing portion of the population. This surge in international migration contrasts sharply with the stagnant growth among native citizens, highlighting a structural transition where non-nationals are the main engine of demographic expansion. The increasing diversity in national origins reflects evolving migration patterns and suggests long-term societal changes that go beyond mere population counts. Simultaneously, the data illustrates a pronounced urbanization trend and an aging society, with population growth concentrated in major metropolitan areas and specific coastal regions, while rural provinces face decline. This geographic disparity impacts resource allocation and regional planning, as growth clusters in cities like Madrid and Valencia while other areas lose residents. The aging population further strains this dynamic, requiring updated social and economic policies to address the needs of an older demographic in a increasingly urbanized landscape. This article is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the utility of high-resolution, granular statistics in understanding complex social phenomena. By making detailed census data accessible, researchers and policymakers can move beyond aggregate figures to analyze local trends, migration flows, and demographic inequalities. Transparent access to such precise information fosters evidence-based decision-making, allowing for better urban planning, resource distribution, and social inclusion strategies tailored to specific municipal realities.
