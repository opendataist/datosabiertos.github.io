Ciudad del Carmen y Pachuca lideran lista de 14 ciudades en las que aumentó la percepción de inseguridad, según Inegi - Noticaribe

The recent National Public Security Survey highlights a significant rise in the perception of insecurity across fourteen major Mexican cities during the first quarter. This trend underscores a growing disconnect between citizens and public safety, with Pachuca and Ciudad del Carmen experiencing particularly sharp increases. These shifts reflect immediate social consequences, such as the lack of police presence in certain areas, signaling that safety perceptions are highly volatile and closely tied to local law enforcement capacity. Nationally, the data reveals a stark gender disparity, as women feel significantly more unsafe than men. The overall sentiment is pessimistic, with a majority of the population expecting crime levels to remain stagnant or worsen in the coming year. This widespread anxiety is further exacerbated by political violence, creating a climate where citizens feel increasingly vulnerable to both organized crime and electoral-related threats, fundamentally altering the social fabric of many urban centers. This article is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates how raw statistical figures translate into tangible human experiences and policy challenges. By making detailed demographic and geographic breakdowns of safety perceptions available, institutions like INEGI enable researchers and civil society to analyze the root causes of insecurity. Such transparency allows for more targeted interventions and holds authorities accountable, proving that open data is essential for diagnosing and addressing complex societal issues like public safety.
