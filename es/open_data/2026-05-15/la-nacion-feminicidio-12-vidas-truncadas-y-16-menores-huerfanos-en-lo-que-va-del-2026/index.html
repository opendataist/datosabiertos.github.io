La Nación / Feminicidio: 12 vidas truncadas y 16 menores huérfanos en lo que va del 2026

The Ministry of Public’s Open Data Program provides a critical transparency mechanism for monitoring gender-based violence in Paraguay. By publishing detailed statistics on feminicides and attempted attacks, the initiative highlights the urgent need for preventive actions. This data-driven approach allows society to move beyond isolated incidents and understand the systemic nature of the crisis, ensuring that official records are accessible and auditable. The analysis reveals that domestic environments and close relationships remain the most dangerous spaces for women, with victims ranging from young children to the elderly. Geographic data identifies central regions as hotspots, while temporal patterns show concentrated violence at the beginning of the year. Furthermore, the collateral damage extends to children who lose their mothers, underscoring the broader social impact of these crimes. This article is relevant to open_data because it demonstrates how publicly available government datasets can expose hidden social realities. By making crime statistics accessible, citizens and policymakers can identify vulnerabilities and demand accountability. This transparency empowers communities to advocate for targeted interventions, proving that open data is essential for effective public safety strategies and social justice.
