LIBROS DE AYER Y HOY: SIN RELIGIÓN

The article highlights a significant demographic shift in religious affiliation, particularly noting the decline in Catholicism in Mexico and the simultaneous rise of agnosticism and atheism. According to data from the National Institute of Statistics and Geography, the percentage of individuals who identify as agnostic or atheist has grown substantially over recent years, reflecting a broader global trend away from traditional religious adherence. This transition is characterized not just by a decrease in adherence to specific denominations but by an increase in people who do not affiliate with any organized religious group. This evolution underscores the diversification of belief systems and the growing complexity of spiritual identity. The data reveals that as Catholic influence wanes, it is not necessarily being replaced by other traditional religions, but rather by a spectrum of non-religious or non-affiliated identities. This includes a clear distinction between agnosticism, where the existence of a deity is considered unknowable, and atheism, which represents a definitive lack of belief. The trend suggests a move toward individualized belief structures rather than collective religious conformity. This information is highly relevant to open_data as it demonstrates the value of publicly available census and statistical records in tracking societal changes. Open data initiatives facilitate the analysis of such demographic shifts, allowing researchers and policymakers to understand emerging social trends. By making this data accessible, stakeholders can better address the needs of a secularizing population, ensuring that public policies and social services reflect the reality of a increasingly diverse and less traditionally religious society.
