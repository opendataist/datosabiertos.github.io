Salario promedio de trabajadores tardó 10 años en subir G. 700.000

The article highlights a stagnant economic reality in Paraguay, where average salaries have seen only marginal growth over a decade despite a proposed significant increase for legislators. This disparity underscores a critical tension between policy priorities and the widespread financial struggles of the general population, particularly highlighting the persistent gender wage gap that remains largely unaddressed. Such discrepancies emphasize the vital importance of open data in fostering political accountability. When government statistics regarding income, employment, and education are transparent and accessible, citizens and journalists can effectively scrutinize decision-making processes. This transparency reveals whether public resources are allocated equitably or if political benefits are prioritized over the economic well-being of the majority. Ultimately, this case demonstrates how open data serves as an essential tool for social justice. By making detailed demographic and economic indicators publicly available, society can hold institutions accountable for their policies. It empowers civil society to demand evidence-based governance, ensuring that legislative actions reflect the actual socioeconomic conditions of the populace rather than insulated political interests.
