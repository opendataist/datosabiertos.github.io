El PIB registró en el tercer trimestre un crecimiento del 0,3%, una décima menos

The Spanish economy experienced a slight deceleration in the third quarter, with growth falling below the levels seen in the previous period. While the GDP still expanded, the slowdown was notably milder than market expectations and consensus forecasts. This resilience suggests that despite broader economic headwinds, the domestic economy retained a degree of momentum, defying predictions of a more pronounced contraction. A key driver of this performance was the divergence between domestic consumption and external demand. Household spending, which had previously surged, cooled significantly, marking a return to more modest growth rates. Conversely, the external sector provided a positive boost, largely because the sharp decline in goods exports was offset by a strong surge in service exports and increased spending by non-residents. This shift highlights a changing structure in Spain’s trade dynamics, where services are becoming increasingly vital to maintaining economic stability. This data is highly relevant to the open data community as it underscores the importance of real-time, granular statistical indicators like the INE’s national accounts for economic analysis. By tracking detailed components of demand and employment, researchers and developers can build more accurate predictive models. Furthermore, understanding these specific trends aids in creating open datasets that better reflect the nuanced realities of economic recovery, supporting informed decision-making for policymakers and businesses alike.
