Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

This article illustrates how macroeconomic indicators directly impact social equity, a core concern in open data. By publicly disclosing salary adjustments alongside inflation rates, statistical agencies reveal the real erosion of purchasing power. This transparency allows citizens to move beyond nominal figures and understand the actual deterioration of their economic situation, highlighting the vital role of accessible, precise public statistics in holding governments accountable for living standards. The data underscores that wage growth has consistently lagged behind inflation across all employment sectors, including formal and informal workers. This widespread loss of purchasing power demonstrates that electoral concessions failed to protect income, particularly for the most vulnerable. When such granular socioeconomic data is available and analyzed, it exposes the true depth of inequality and the effectiveness—or lack thereof—of current economic policies. Relevance to open data lies in its capacity to transform raw statistics into actionable insights for democracy. The detailed breakdown of salaries by sector and time period enables researchers and journalists to verify official narratives and advocate for policy changes. Ultimately, this case shows that open, high-quality data is essential for diagnosing systemic crises and empowering society to demand solutions that address the real impact of economic decisions on daily life.
