Perú: solo 636 policías de más de 100,000 han recibido formación en violencia contra la mujer

This article highlights a critical gap in how police institutions handle violence against women, revealing that despite thousands of annual complaints, the vast majority of officers lack specialized training. The core issue is not a lack of policy awareness, but a systemic failure in implementation, where gender-focused education is deprioritized against operational or technological concerns, leading to subjective and potentially harmful interactions with victims who rely on these agencies for protection. The narrative underscores the necessity of evidence-based institutional reforms to ensure that legal frameworks are translated into consistent, professional action. Without adequate, mandatory specialized training, police forces risk perpetuating biases and failing to provide the secure environment required for justice, emphasizing that technical proficiency in gender dynamics is as vital as traditional law enforcement skills in addressing domestic and gender-based crimes effectively. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it demonstrates how transparency laws can expose structural inefficiencies within public institutions. By making training statistics accessible, civil society can hold governments accountable for resource allocation and service quality. This case illustrates how open data serves as a diagnostic tool, allowing researchers and advocates to verify official narratives against ground realities and push for tangible improvements in public service delivery and human rights protections.
