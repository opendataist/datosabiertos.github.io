Un anticuerpo puede ralentizar los síntomas motores del párkinson en algunos pacientes - Ciencia - ABC Color

The analysis of prasinezumab reveals that this experimental antibody significantly reduces motor symptom worsening in patients with rapidly progressing Parkinson’s disease after one year, while showing no effect on those with slower disease progression. This suggests that clinical efficacy for this treatment is strictly tied to the rate of disease evolution, highlighting the importance of patient stratification in therapeutic outcomes. Experts caution that the observed benefits are minimal at this stage, emphasizing the need for longer-term studies to determine if the drug remains effective in slower-progressing cases over extended periods. The current limited follow-up duration makes it difficult to predict long-term results, urging a careful evaluation of the balance between costs, benefits, and potential adverse effects before widespread adoption. This research is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent, granular clinical trial datasets. By making detailed patient-level data accessible, researchers can independently verify subgroup analyses and improve the reproducibility of medical findings. Open data practices enable the scientific community to better understand heterogeneous treatment responses, ensuring that future healthcare decisions are based on robust, shared evidence rather than isolated studies.
