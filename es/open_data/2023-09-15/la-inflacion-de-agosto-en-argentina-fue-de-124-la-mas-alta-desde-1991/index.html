La inflación de agosto en Argentina fue de 12,4 %, la más alta desde 1991

The article highlights Argentina’s record-breaking inflation, which reached unprecedented levels driven by a sharp currency devaluation and external economic pressures. This surge has severely eroded purchasing power, leaving salaries consistently behind rising prices, particularly affecting essential goods like food and healthcare. The situation underscores the urgent need for transparent and reliable data to monitor the economic health of a population facing extreme volatility and loss of real income. This context is directly relevant to open data because independent, high-frequency, and accessible statistical information is crucial for accountability in such a volatile environment. When official metrics reflect extreme fluctuations, as seen in this report, open access to the underlying methodologies and raw data allows citizens, economists, and journalists to verify claims and understand the distributional impacts of policy decisions. Transparency becomes a tool for resilience against misinformation and political narratives that may obscure the true extent of the crisis. Ultimately, the focus on open data here extends beyond mere publication; it implies the necessity for data to be reusable and understandable by the public. In a climate where economic policies are heavily debated and contested, ensuring that inflation indices and related economic indicators are freely available promotes informed civic engagement. It enables stakeholders to assess the effectiveness of interventions, such as tax adjustments, and holds authorities accountable for managing the economy during periods of significant structural stress.
