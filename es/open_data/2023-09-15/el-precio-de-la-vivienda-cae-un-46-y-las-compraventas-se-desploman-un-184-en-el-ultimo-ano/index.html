El precio de la vivienda cae un 4,6% y las compraventas se desploman un 18,4% en el último año

Spain’s housing market is experiencing a significant contraction, characterized by substantial declines in both transaction volumes and property prices across the majority of regions. This downturn indicates a cooling demand, with significant drops in sales and meter-square costs recorded in numerous autonomous communities, while only a few areas managed to post modest price increases. The broad reduction suggests a systemic shift rather than isolated regional anomalies, reflecting a broader economic adjustment in the real estate sector. Simultaneously, mortgage lending has tightened considerably, with a sharp drop in the number of new loans and a decrease in average borrowing amounts. Since nearly half of all purchases rely on financing, this credit scarcity acts as a primary driver of the reduced market activity. The consistent decline in mortgage operations across all regions highlights a constrained financial environment for buyers, further suppressing market liquidity and purchase power. This data is crucial for the open data community as it underscores the importance of accessible, granular, and timely public statistics for economic analysis. Transparent records from official bodies like the General Council of Notaries enable researchers, policymakers, and developers to monitor market trends accurately. Open access to such detailed datasets fosters evidence-based decision-making and allows for deeper investigations into the structural factors influencing housing affordability and availability.
