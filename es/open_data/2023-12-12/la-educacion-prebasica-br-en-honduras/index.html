La educación prebásica <br />en Honduras

The article highlights a critical deficit in early childhood education and care infrastructure, revealing that access to structured educational services remains severely limited for young children. This insufficiency disproportionately affects families, particularly mothers, whose labor participation is hindered by the lack of accessible, high-quality childcare options. The situation is exacerbated by urbanization and the decline of traditional multi-generational support systems, leading to a growing demand for formal care. Without adequate structural support, many families rely on precarious care arrangements, which fail to provide the necessary developmental benefits and safety standards required for early childhood growth. This issue is vital to open data because transparent, accessible datasets on coverage rates and service quality are essential for evidence-based policy making. By making this information publicly available, stakeholders can better identify gaps, advocate for resource allocation, and monitor the effectiveness of interventions aimed at improving social welfare and educational equity.
