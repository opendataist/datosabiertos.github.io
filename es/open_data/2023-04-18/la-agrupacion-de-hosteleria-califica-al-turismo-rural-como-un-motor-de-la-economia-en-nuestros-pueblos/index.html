La Agrupación de Hostelería califica al turismo rural como un motor de la economía en nuestros pueblos

Recent statistics reveal that rural tourism in Cuenca has become a vital economic engine for depopulated villages. The significant surge in overnight stays, which now exceeds pre-pandemic levels, demonstrates that the region successfully meets the growing demand for tranquil, spacious environments. This growth is attributed to strategic investments in new rural establishments, proving that such sectors are not only recovering but thriving in the current market landscape. Crucially, these tourism initiatives are directly combating rural depopulation by creating employment and opportunities that encourage people to settle in these areas. With domestic travelers driving the majority of this increase, local communities are seeing tangible benefits from visitors seeking authentic experiences. This trend underscores the potential of rural hospitality to sustain local populations and revitalizes the social fabric of small towns that might otherwise continue to decline. This article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how public statistical records can validate local economic narratives and support policy-making. By making granular data on tourist behavior accessible, governments and organizations can identify successful trends, justify infrastructure investments, and monitor the effectiveness of anti-depopulation strategies. Transparent data empowers stakeholders to make informed decisions that sustainably develop rural areas based on actual usage patterns rather than assumptions.
