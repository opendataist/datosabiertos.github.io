La economía se derrumbó en julio y augura un tercer trimestre sombrío

Argentina’s economic activity experienced a significant contraction in July, marking the sharpest decline since early 2018 and raising serious concerns about a potential recession in the third quarter. While export-oriented sectors like mining and hydrocarbons showed growth, the broader economy, particularly industry, commerce, and construction, suffered severe drops. This divergence highlights a critical disconnect where booming external sectors fail to stimulate domestic demand, leaving consumers with reduced purchasing power unable to drive internal recovery. The data suggests that the government’s current economic strategy has not generated the necessary confidence to boost consumption or investment. Economists argue that without a substantial shift in policy and expectations, the country is unlikely to find a new engine for growth. The persistence of this trend implies a scenario of stagnation, where the internal market remains depressed despite positive indicators in specific industries, creating a challenging political and economic environment as electoral cycles approach. This article is relevant to open_data because it underscores the importance of accessible, transparent statistical information from national institutes like INDEC for economic analysis. Reliable public data allows independent consultants and researchers to identify sectoral disparities and forecast trends accurately, fostering accountability and informed public debate. When official data contradicts political narratives, it highlights the vital role of open, verifiable statistics in holding leadership accountable and ensuring that economic policy decisions are based on evidence rather than optimism.
