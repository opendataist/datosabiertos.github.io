Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The National Statistics Institute introduced “healthy life expectancy” to measure quality of life beyond mere longevity. Data reveals that while lifespans extend, significant health limitations often emerge only after age eighty, indicating a compressed morbidity period for many seniors. This metric is vital for open data as it transforms raw demographic statistics into actionable insights about societal well-being. By distinguishing between years lived and years lived in good health, public datasets enable more nuanced policy planning regarding elderly care and resource allocation. Consequently, healthcare systems must adapt to an aging population with complex needs. Improved patient-doctor communication, shared decision-making, and better care coordination are essential to manage chronic conditions effectively, ensuring that extended life expectancy translates into genuine quality of life for citizens.
