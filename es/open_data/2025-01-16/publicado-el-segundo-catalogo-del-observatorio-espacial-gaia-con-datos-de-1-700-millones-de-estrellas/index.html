Publicado el segundo catálogo del observatorio espacial Gaia, con datos de 1.700 millones de estrellas

The European Space Agency has released the second Gaia catalog, representing the most precise map of our galaxy to date. This monumental release includes data on nearly 1.7 billion stars, encompassing positions, distances, and motions. It also provides detailed information on stellar brightness, color, and variability, significantly enhancing our understanding of the Milky Way’s structure and evolution. Beyond stellar data, the catalog incorporates precise orbits for thousands of asteroids and coordinates for half a million quasars. These quasars serve as a crucial celestial reference frame for all objects within the catalog. By providing visible light data for quasars alongside traditional radio observations, this release offers a multi-wavelength perspective that deepens our ability to map the universe accurately. This dataset is highly relevant to open data because it exemplifies the transformative power of releasing massive, high-quality scientific records to the global community. By making such vast amounts of precise astronomical data freely available, researchers worldwide can derive new insights without barriers. This practice demonstrates how open access to complex datasets fosters innovation and allows for spectacular scientific discoveries that would otherwise remain hidden.
