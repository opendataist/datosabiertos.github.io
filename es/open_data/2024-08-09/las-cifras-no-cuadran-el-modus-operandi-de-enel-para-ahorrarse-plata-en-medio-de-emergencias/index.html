Las cifras no cuadran: el modus operandi de Enel para "ahorrarse plata" en medio de emergencias

The article highlights a systemic failure in data transparency by Chile’s electricity distributors, specifically Enel, which prioritized financial savings over accurate crisis reporting. Regulatory bodies found that the company submitted false or duplicated information regarding the number of customers without power during outages. This deliberate misreporting allowed the firm to reduce necessary emergency expenditures, such as deploying adequate repair crews, thereby generating significant economic benefits at the expense of public service quality and accountability. This case underscores the critical importance of open data in public utility management, as the state lacks the capacity to independently verify emergency metrics on the ground. When companies control the narrative by providing erroneous statistics, it hampers effective governance and leaves citizens vulnerable during crises. The repeated instances of non-compliance suggest a structural issue where data integrity is compromised to maximize profits, challenging the foundational principles of transparent and accessible public information. Relevant to open data advocates, this incident demonstrates how opaque data practices can mask corporate negligence and evade regulatory scrutiny. It serves as a stark reminder that without strict adherence to accurate, real-time data sharing, oversight mechanisms become ineffective. The article illustrates the tangible consequences of data manipulation, emphasizing the need for robust verification protocols and the ethical obligation of utilities to provide truthful information to ensure public trust and operational accountability.
