The article highlights a significant gender pay gap in Mexico, where men consistently earn more than women across all age groups and family structures. This persistent inequality underscores systemic biases in the labor market, demonstrating that women face financial disadvantages regardless of their experience level or number of dependents. The data reveals that these disparities are not limited to specific demographics but are widespread, affecting both young workers and those nearing retirement. This issue is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the critical role of public, high-quality statistics in exposing social inequities. When organizations like INEGI release granular, accessible datasets, researchers, journalists, and policymakers can independently verify claims and identify hidden patterns in income distribution. Transparency in such data allows for accurate analysis of how factors like family size intersect with gender to create economic disparity. Ultimately, open access to these detailed economic records empowers civil society to demand accountability and drive policy changes. By making this information freely available, governments enable the public to understand the scope of the problem beyond aggregated averages. This approach fosters informed debate and ensures that efforts to close the wage gap are based on evidence rather than assumption, promoting a more equitable society through data transparency.
