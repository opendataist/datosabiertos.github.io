El Gobierno niega el problema del pluriempleo pese a que más de 900.000 afiliados tienen al menos dos trabajos: "Es el 4% hace años"

The Spanish government argues that the rising trend in multi-job holders does not reflect increasing precariousness but rather mirrors the overall growth in employment. While independent statistics from the INE and Social Security show record highs in workers holding two or more jobs, officials claim this proportional stability indicates a healthy labor market where individuals are simply taking advantage of expanded opportunities rather than being forced into unstable situations due to economic hardship. However, experts and economic institutes dispute this interpretation, characterizing the government's narrative as misleading. They highlight that the rise in multiple jobs is closely linked to inflation and the erosion of purchasing power, forcing families to seek additional income to survive. Furthermore, analysts point out that the real issue lies not just in simultaneous jobs but in the cycle of contract creation and destruction, which creates a distinct form of labor instability that standard metrics fail to capture accurately. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical importance of context and source selection when interpreting statistical information. It demonstrates how different datasets—such as those from the INE versus Social Security—and varying methodological interpretations can lead to conflicting conclusions about the same social phenomenon. Understanding open data requires recognizing that raw numbers often hide complex realities, necessitating a deeper analysis of the underlying causes and definitions behind the metrics to avoid political or technical misrepresentation of socioeconomic conditions.
