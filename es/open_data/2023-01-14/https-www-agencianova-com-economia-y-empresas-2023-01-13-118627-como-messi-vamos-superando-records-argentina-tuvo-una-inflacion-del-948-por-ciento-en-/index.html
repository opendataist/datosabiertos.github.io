Argentina’s December inflation reached 5.1 percent, contributing to an annual rate of 94.8 percent. This figure represents the highest yearly increase since 1991 and significantly surpasses the accumulation seen during the previous administration. The result exceeded expectations set by the Minister of Economy, highlighting a persistent upward trend in consumer prices that reflects severe economic instability and loss of purchasing power across the country. Food and beverage prices emerged as a primary driver of this inflation, with the category recording a notable rise that disproportionately affects households. This trend is particularly concerning because food expenses constitute a fundamental component of daily life for most citizens. The impact was felt nationwide, with staples such as fruits, cereals, and beverages seeing sharp increases, thereby exacerbating the cost of living crisis and intensifying pressure on vulnerable populations. This article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparency in statistical reporting serves as a critical accountability mechanism for citizens. When government agencies publish official inflation metrics, they provide essential, verifiable evidence of economic performance. This accessible data empowers researchers, journalists, and the public to analyze fiscal policies, compare historical trends, and hold elected officials responsible for the real-world impact of their economic decisions on society.
