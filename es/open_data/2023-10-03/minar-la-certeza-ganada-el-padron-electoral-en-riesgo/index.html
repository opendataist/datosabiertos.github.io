Minar la certeza ganada: el padrón electoral en riesgo

The proposed reform to Mexico’s General Population Law threatens democratic stability by transferring the responsibility for the national identity and voter registry from the autonomous National Electoral Institute (INE) to the Secretariat of the Interior (Segob). This centralization risks compromising the impartiality required for free and fair elections. Since the INE has historically ensured high voter coverage and public trust through independent management, moving this function to a government agency introduces significant concerns regarding political manipulation and the erosion of electoral integrity. The narrative highlights that consolidating identity and voting records under a single autonomous body has been crucial for maintaining the legitimacy of Mexican democracy. Splitting these functions or placing them under executive control could reduce registry coverage and undermine the principle of one person, one vote. The article argues that the current system, which benefits from decades of institutional investment and public confidence, should not be disrupted by a change that prioritizes administrative centralization over democratic safeguards and citizen rights. This proposal is critically relevant to open data discussions as it touches upon the governance, privacy, and accessibility of sensitive biometric and personal information. The centralization of identity data raises serious questions about data security, the potential for misuse in political campaigning, and the lack of transparency in how such a national register is managed. Protecting open data principles requires ensuring that public databases serve transparency and accountability rather than consolidating power, making this reform a focal point for debating data sovereignty and institutional trust.
