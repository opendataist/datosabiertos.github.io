INAI ha recibido 6 denuncias de periodistas tras hackeo en Presidencia | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The INAI has launched an investigation into the illegal extraction of journalists’ accreditation files from Palacio Nacional, treating the incident as a serious breach of data protection laws. By initiating official proceedings and accepting multiple complaints, the institute aims to determine the extent of the violation and impose appropriate administrative or economic sanctions. This proactive stance underscores the institution’s commitment to safeguarding personal information against unauthorized disclosure by state entities. The leaked data places journalists at significant risk, highlighting the critical importance of strict compliance with privacy regulations in government operations. The INAI’s impartial review of the presidential office’s technical report will determine necessary actions to mitigate harm and ensure future accountability. This process emphasizes that even sensitive operational data must adhere to established legal frameworks for personal data protection. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the vital boundary between transparency and privacy. While governments must publish information for public scrutiny, they must simultaneously protect individuals’ personal data from misuse. The incident serves as a cautionary example that the open data movement requires robust legal safeguards to prevent violations of citizens’ rights, ensuring that openness does not come at the cost of individual security and trust.
