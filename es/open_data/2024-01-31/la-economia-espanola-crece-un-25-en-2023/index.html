La economía española crece un 2,5% en 2023

The Spanish economy demonstrated resilience in 2023, growing by 2.5% despite a broader slowdown compared to previous years. This performance surpassed government forecasts, driven primarily by robust private consumption supported by improved household purchasing power and strong export demand. The final quarter specifically showed a 0.6% increase, highlighting that external demand played a crucial role in sustaining growth as domestic consumption moderated. Official data from the National Statistics Institute confirms this trajectory, revising earlier quarterly figures upward to reflect a more dynamic recovery than initially anticipated. The labor market and exports emerged as the key engines of this growth, contributing significantly to the overall economic expansion. While inflation saw a slight uptick to 3.4% in January, largely due to electricity prices, core inflation continued to decline. This mixed picture indicates that while price pressures persist in specific sectors, the underlying economic activity remains stable and positive. The combination of job creation and export strength suggests a solid foundation for future economic stability. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical role of official statistical institutions like the INE in providing transparent, accessible, and verifiable economic indicators. Open data enables researchers, policymakers, and citizens to independently analyze these metrics, fostering trust in public administration and facilitating evidence-based decision-making. By making such detailed GDP and inflation data publicly available, it empowers the community to monitor economic health, challenge narratives, and drive innovation in economic analysis without relying solely on government interpretations.
