Descubren arqueólogos una ciudad maya en Campeche

Archeologists uncovered thousands of hidden Maya structures in Campeche by repurposing LiDAR data originally collected for environmental carbon monitoring. This finding highlights the immense value of open scientific data, demonstrating how publicly available datasets can yield unexpected archaeological breakthroughs when analyzed through different disciplinary lenses. The discovery reveals a densely populated ancient urban landscape, including a major city near modern settlements, fundamentally changing our understanding of the region’s historical significance. It proves that open data transcends its initial purpose, serving as a critical resource for diverse research communities and fostering interdisciplinary collaboration. This case is vital for the open data movement as it illustrates the potential for secondary use of published information. By making environmental data accessible, scientists enabled this cultural heritage discovery, reinforcing the argument that open access accelerates knowledge creation and maximizes the societal impact of funded research initiatives.
