El IEEG, desinformación y debates

The article argues that electoral institutions must prioritize civic education and transparency to ensure a meaningful democratic process. It critiques the failure to inform voters about the legal and ethical implications of vote-buying, as well as the complex mechanics of local elections, such as the distinction between majority and proportional representation. This lack of information undermines voter autonomy and reduces the election to a superficial choice rather than an informed exercise of rights. Transparency is presented as the essential tool for citizens to evaluate past governmental performance, particularly in the context of reelection. The text highlights how bureaucratic focus on regulatory minutiae overshadows the duty to educate the public, leading to a misunderstanding of executive power and the role of municipal councils. Without access to accurate data on how officials have governed, voters cannot make rational decisions, rendering the electoral process ineffective. Furthermore, the quality of candidate debates is criticized for failing to foster genuine discussion of ideas, instead relying on structured formats that disadvantage participants. This superficiality prevents voters from comparing actual proposals and competencies. The article is relevant to open_data because it underscores the necessity of accessible, transparent information about government actions and electoral rules. It suggests that without robust data dissemination and educational efforts, citizens remain disempowered, unable to hold authorities accountable or make informed choices.
