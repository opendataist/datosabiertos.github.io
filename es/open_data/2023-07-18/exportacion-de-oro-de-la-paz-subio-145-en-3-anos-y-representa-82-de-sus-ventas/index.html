Exportación de oro de La Paz subió 145% en 3 años y representa 82% de sus ventas

La exportación de oro ha eclipsado otras actividades económicas en Bolivia, particularly in La Paz, where it accounts for the vast majority of external sales. This surge creates a significant dependency on a single commodity with low value-added processing, highlighting structural vulnerabilities in the national economy. The concentration of production in specific regions raises concerns about equitable wealth distribution and long-term industrial development. The fiscal framework governing this sector remains contentious, as miners have historically paid minimal royalties compared to their massive revenues. Negotiations for a unified tax aim to increase state contributions, yet the current system fails to adequately capture the true value extracted from natural resources. This disparity underscores the urgent need for transparent revenue management and regulatory oversight to ensure that resource wealth benefits the broader population rather than remaining concentrated. Relevance to open data lies in the necessity to monitor environmental and social impacts alongside economic metrics. The documented cases of mercury contamination and deforestation reveal critical gaps in public information regarding mining activities in protected areas. Improving data transparency allows civil society to track health risks and ecological damage, fostering accountability and informed policy decisions that balance economic growth with sustainable practices.
