El diputado de Vox que vetó el PP para entrar en el Gobierno valenciano cuestiona su nueva ley de transparencia

The article highlights a critical tension in Valencian governance regarding the proposed reform of transparency laws. The ruling coalition seeks to lower the voting majority required to renew the Transparency Council, a move critics argue could centralize power and undermine the institution's independence by allowing the majority to dictate appointments without broader consensus. Carlos Flores, originally a Vox candidate for the regional government, publicly questioned this legislative change. Paralleling his opposition to similar judicial reforms at the national level, he argued that reducing consensus requirements creates a "double-edged sword." He emphasized that control institutions must remain above temporary political majorities to maintain legitimacy and effective oversight of public administration. This debate is highly relevant to open_data because the integrity of transparency bodies directly impacts data accessibility and accountability. If the council’s renewal becomes subject to simple majority rule rather than broad agreement, there is a significant risk that checks on administrative data usage could be weakened. Ensuring that these oversight mechanisms are insulated from partisan shifts is essential for maintaining reliable, open, and trustworthy government information systems.
