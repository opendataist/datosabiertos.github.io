Renovarse o morir

The article highlights that Mexico’s transparency law, enacted nine years ago, was designed to democratize access to public information, transforming the regulatory body into a guarantor of human rights. This foundational framework established equality and robust operational bases, ensuring that all citizens could access government data. However, the text argues that maintaining this status quo is insufficient in today’s rapidly evolving landscape. Current challenges demand a strategic evolution rather than mere survival. The institute must now address complex societal needs, including leveraging digital technologies and artificial intelligence to improve decision-making, prevent corruption, and protect privacy. These new realities require the organization to adapt its methods significantly, moving beyond traditional structures to meet the expectations of a modern, digital society. This piece is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for institutional agility in maintaining transparency systems. It warns against reducing budgets at the expense of core functions, emphasizing that true transformation requires rational resource use and structural redesign. For open data initiatives, the implication is clear: sustainable access relies on a well-resourced, adaptable authority capable of addressing modern technological and social complexities.
