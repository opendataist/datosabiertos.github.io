The article highlights a concerning upward trend in general mortality, driven primarily by population aging and exacerbated by the pandemic’s impact between 2020 and 2022. A significant gender disparity persists, with men experiencing higher death rates throughout the period, although this gap narrowed slightly by the end of the analyzed timeframe. This data underscores how external shocks like public health crises can disrupt long-standing demographic patterns, revealing vulnerabilities in societal health structures. Simultaneously, the report notes improvements in maternal health, evidenced by a sharp decline in maternal mortality rates, alongside a decrease in marriage rates and a growing preference for civil over religious ceremonies. In contrast to these positive shifts, infant mortality has risen, with neonatal deaths accounting for the majority of cases. The data reveals regional inequities, as certain provinces exhibit significantly higher infant death rates than the national average, pointing to localized challenges in healthcare access and quality. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how publicly available demographic statistics can uncover critical social insights, such as health inequalities and the long-term effects of pandemics. By making vital statistics accessible, researchers and policymakers can identify disparities across regions and genders, facilitating targeted interventions. The availability of granular data allows for a deeper understanding of population dynamics, ensuring that decision-making is informed by evidence rather than speculation, which is essential for improving public health outcomes.
