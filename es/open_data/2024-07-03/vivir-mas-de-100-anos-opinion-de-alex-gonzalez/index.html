Vivir más de 100 años | Opinión de Álex González

The article projects a significant rise in Spain’s centenarian population, highlighting the societal challenge of balancing increased longevity with quality of life. It argues that while mortality gives meaning to existence and experience protects against physical decline, an aging society faces the dilemma of prolonged life without mental or physical vitality. This tension forces a critical reflection on whether current advances merely extend life or genuinely improve it. This topic is crucial for open data because it underscores the need for high-quality, accessible datasets that track health and aging metrics beyond simple headcounts. To help society navigate the balance between quantity and quality, policymakers require granular, transparent data on well-being, mobility, and cognitive health. Without such open information, it becomes impossible to design effective interventions or evaluate the true impact of demographic shifts on social infrastructure. Ultimately, the piece calls for scientific and social strategies that prioritize meaningful living over mere survival. By leveraging open data, researchers can better understand the nuances of healthy aging, enabling communities to prepare for a future where living longer does not mean enduring unnecessary suffering. This transparency is essential for fostering equitable and humane policies in an increasingly aged world.
