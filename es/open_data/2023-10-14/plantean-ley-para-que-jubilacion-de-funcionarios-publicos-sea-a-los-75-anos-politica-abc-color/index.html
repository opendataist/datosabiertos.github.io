Plantean ley para que jubilación de funcionarios públicos sea a los 75 años - Política - ABC Color

A proposed legislative amendment in Paraguay seeks to raise the mandatory retirement age for public officials from 65 to 75 years. Sponsored by a cross-party group of senators, the bill argues that the current age limit is discriminatory and unjust, particularly since many workers reach this milestone without having contributed enough to qualify for a dignified pension. By allowing those who wish to continue working to do so until 75, the proposal aims to resolve constitutional tensions and provide a viable path for achieving adequate retirement benefits without forcing early exit from the workforce. The justification highlights inconsistencies in current regulations, noting that other high-ranking positions, such as Supreme Court justices and military personnel, already operate under different retirement frameworks based on the premise that physical and mental capacity declines slowly. Proponents emphasize that this change is primarily driven by the demands of public servants themselves, who seek the flexibility to delay retirement solely to accumulate sufficient contributions. Importantly, the measure is voluntary; officials wishing to retire at 62 or earlier would remain free to do so, ensuring that no worker is penalized or coerced into remaining active against their will. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it underscores the critical role of accessible, accurate statistical information in shaping public policy. Reliable data on life expectancy and demographic projections, such as those from the National Institute of Statistics, provide the empirical foundation necessary to debate retirement ages effectively. Open data transparency allows citizens and policymakers to scrutinize these assumptions, ensuring that legislative changes are grounded in objective evidence regarding population health and economic sustainability rather than anecdotal claims.
