CC advierte tintes políticos en boleta censal porque busca muertes desde la crisis política del 2019

The article highlights significant political tension surrounding Bolivia’s upcoming National Census, as lawmakers argue the questionnaire contains ideological bias. Critics, primarily from the opposition, contend that specific inquiries regarding mortality since 2019 and historical residency are deliberately designed to manipulate statistical outcomes related to the nation’s recent political crisis. This perception of manipulation stems from the timing of the census, which coincides with periods of intense social unrest and electoral disputes, raising concerns about the neutrality of state data collection efforts. Furthermore, the omission of questions on racial mestizage alongside the inclusion of sensitive demographic probes has fueled suspicions of political intent among legislators. The debate underscores a critical lack of transparency from the statistical institute, prompting calls for technical justification regarding the design of the survey instrument. Lawmakers emphasize that without clear, unbiased explanations for these methodological choices, the resulting data may be viewed as illegitimate or politically driven rather than scientifically objective. This situation is highly relevant to open data principles because it illustrates how the perceived political influence over statistical methodology can erode public trust in government datasets. When citizens and policymakers suspect that data collection tools are designed to support a specific political narrative rather than record objective reality, the foundational integrity of open data is compromised. Consequently, this case serves as a cautionary example of how opacity and political bias in census design can undermine the utility and credibility of public statistics, hindering evidence-based policy making.
