Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article highlights a critical tension between the rapid proliferation of connected vehicles and the severe erosion of user privacy. Connected cars act as sophisticated data collectors, aggregating intimate details about drivers' lifestyles, habits, and locations. With hundreds of millions of such vehicles on the road and numbers projected to rise drastically, the sheer volume of personal data being harvested poses significant risks, as most manufacturers share or sell this information with little to no control exercised by the owners. This scenario is particularly relevant to open data discourse because it underscores the ethical challenges inherent in the massive generation of personal information through ubiquitous digital infrastructure. The ease with which private data can be commodified and shared with third parties, including insurers, illustrates the dangers of unregulated data ecosystems. It serves as a stark reminder that open access or sharing mechanisms must be balanced with robust privacy protections to prevent the exploitation of citizens through pervasive surveillance and profiling. To mitigate these risks, experts advocate for implementing "privacy by design" and "privacy by default" principles in automotive technology. This approach requires that data protection standards be embedded into the initial development of vehicle systems, ensuring users retain full control over their information. Ultimately, the article calls for an ethical equilibrium where technological advancement does not come at the cost of fundamental rights, urging both manufacturers and users to prioritize transparency and voluntary consent in the digital age.
