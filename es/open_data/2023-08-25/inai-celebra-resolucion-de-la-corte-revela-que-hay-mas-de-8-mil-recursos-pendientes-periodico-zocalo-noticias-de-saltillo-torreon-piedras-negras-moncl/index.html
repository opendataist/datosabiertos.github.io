INAI celebra resolución de la Corte; revela que hay más de 8 mil recursos pendientes | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The Mexican Supreme Court’s decision to allow the INAI to operate with four commissioners effectively unblocks thousands of pending public information requests. This ruling resolves a prolonged quorum crisis, enabling the institute to resume its core function of addressing societal grievances regarding access to information. The move is hailed as a significant victory for citizens, restoring essential transparency guarantees that had been stalled since the departure of the fifth commissioner. Beyond immediate operational relief, the decision reinforces the constitutional separation of powers and validates the independent work of the oversight body. By confirming the institute's capacity to function under these constraints, the court affirms the rule of law and protects the public's right to accountability. The presiding commissioner retains a decisive vote in case of ties, ensuring that resolutions can continue despite the structural imbalance. This development is highly relevant to open data because it directly impacts the speed and reliability of public information disclosure mechanisms. When oversight bodies are paralyzed, citizens lose timely access to government data, undermining transparency efforts. Restaying the INAI’s workflow ensures that open data initiatives remain functional and that institutions are held accountable through efficient legal channels, preserving the integrity of the public’s right to know.
