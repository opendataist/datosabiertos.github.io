IA y los derechos de autor: la batalla que tiene Google con la industria musical

Streaming platforms enforce strict copyright control, yet open platforms like YouTube struggle with user-generated infringement. Universal Music Group has pressured Google to use AI to protect private intellectual property rights, creating a political dilemma for the tech giant. This pressure stems from the inability of current systems to effectively remove unauthorized content, highlighting the tension between open data accessibility and proprietary protection. Google must balance these demands to maintain operations and compete with rivals like TikTok, which operates under different copyright frameworks. While YouTube plans to develop an AI music incubator to improve moderation and prevent deepfakes, it faces significant challenges in training systems on vast web data without proper remuneration or clear licensing agreements for the underlying information. This situation is crucial for open data as it illustrates the conflict between unrestricted information flow and corporate ownership. It raises critical questions about how AI systems can legally train on copyrighted material and whether open data principles must adapt to accommodate strict intellectual property enforcement in the age of generative technologies.
