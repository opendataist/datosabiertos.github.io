Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article highlights a significant conflict between RTVE’s public obligations and its actual practices regarding transparency. Although the media obtained salary data for external presenters using transparency laws, RTVE initially refused to disclose similar information for its own staff employees, arguing that personal privacy rights outweighed public interest. This selective application created a double standard, where some salaries were visible while others remained hidden despite legal precedents suggesting otherwise. The transparency council ruled in favor of the public’s right to know, stating that understanding how public resources are allocated is crucial for institutional accountability. This decision underscores that the disclosure of remuneration for roles involving public trust and discretionary power serves the community better than individual privacy claims in this context. The ruling affirmed that citizens have a direct interest in monitoring the efficiency and fairness of public institution expenditures. This issue is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the persistent challenges in accessing public sector information, even when legally mandated. It demonstrates how administrative bodies can attempt to bypass transparency mechanisms through data protection arguments, requiring robust legal enforcement to ensure true openness. Ultimately, the case reinforces the necessity of continuous civic oversight and the importance of treating all public employee remuneration as accessible data to guarantee fiscal responsibility and democratic trust.
