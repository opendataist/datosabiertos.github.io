LOS CAPITALES: Millonaria demanda NYT a creadores de IA por derechos de autor - Almomento | Noticias, información nacional e internacional

The lawsuit filed by The New York Times against OpenAI and Microsoft highlights a critical conflict between intellectual property rights and the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence. By using millions of articles to train language models like ChatGPT, these tech giants face accusations of replicating not just content, but the distinctive style of journalistic expression. This legal battle serves as a pivotal moment for defining how copyrighted material is consumed and utilized in the digital age, potentially setting precedents that could limit or expand the future trajectory of AI development. From an economic perspective, the unauthorized use of creative work undermines the traditional revenue models of media companies, which rely heavily on subscriptions, licensing, and advertising. The core issue extends beyond legal violations to the erosion of financial sustainability for content creators. As AI capabilities grow, the risk of displacing human creativity and labor becomes more pronounced, challenging the established balance between technological innovation and the protection of professional livelihoods and artistic integrity. This case is profoundly relevant to open data because it forces a reevaluation of the principles governing data accessibility and ethical use. While open data promotes transparency and innovation, this lawsuit illustrates the dangers of ignoring copyright and ownership when aggregating large datasets. It underscores the necessity for clear frameworks that respect intellectual property while allowing for technological progress, ensuring that the openness of data does not come at the cost of creators' rights or the economic viability of information production industries.
