“Epidemia” de violaciones sexuales en una zona del sur de México

The article exposes a systemic crisis of sexual violence and impunity in Guerrero, Mexico, where the lack of judicial follow-through allows aggressors to continue offending. With a conviction rate nearing zero, the legal system effectively protects perpetrators, often through corruption and negligence, rather than delivering justice for victims. This environment creates a cycle of fear and trauma, particularly affecting indigenous communities where state protection is virtually nonexistent. Corruption within the justice system is highlighted as a key driver of this impunity, with reports of officials soliciting bribes from both victims to initiate cases and aggressors to close them. Furthermore, the arbitrary classification of crimes minimizes legal consequences; for instance, attempted rapes are often downgraded to lesser offenses, ensuring that violators retain their freedom and jobs. This institutional failure signals to offenders that they can act with total impunity, thereby exacerbating the scale of sexual abuse in vulnerable regions. This case is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the critical gap between reported statistics and the lived reality of justice. Transparent, accessible data on crime rates, conviction rates, and corruption allegations is essential to expose systemic biases and hold authorities accountable. Without open access to accurate information regarding legal processes and outcomes, civil society and international bodies cannot effectively advocate for policy reforms or measure the true impact of interventions aimed at reducing violence against women and children.
