El gasto medio de los hogares se disparó un 7,9% en 2022, hasta los 31.568 euros

The latest household budget survey reveals that Spanish families significantly increased their spending in 2022, driven primarily by a rebound in services such as dining out, tourism, and transportation. Although nominal figures appear high, adjusting for inflation shows that real purchasing power has not yet fully recovered to pre-pandemic levels. This distinction is crucial for understanding that while consumers are spending more, their actual standard of living remains constrained compared to 2019, highlighting the lingering economic impact of recent global disruptions. The data illustrates a clear shift in consumption patterns, with essential categories like housing, food, and healthcare exceeding pre-crisis benchmarks, while discretionary spending on leisure and clothing remains suppressed. Furthermore, significant disparities emerge across different household structures and geographic regions, indicating that economic recovery is uneven. Wealthier households and those in specific autonomous communities are recovering faster, whereas smaller families and those in less prosperous regions continue to lag, creating a fragmented economic landscape that policy makers must address. This report is highly relevant to open_data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and granular statistical indicators for informed decision-making. By making such detailed breakdowns available, governments and researchers can better identify inequalities and measure the true impact of inflation on diverse social groups. Open access to this data fosters accountability, allows for more precise economic analysis, and enables civil society to monitor whether recovery policies are effectively reaching the most vulnerable populations, ensuring that statistical insights translate into equitable social outcomes.
