La boleta censal incluye preguntas de covid y cuál fue su sexo al nacer

The preliminary census aims to measure the pandemic’s impact on mortality and households while introducing questions on gender identity at birth. This inclusion has sparked debate, with LGBTQ+ organizations arguing that the binary framing risks discrimination and bias. They advocate for broader, more inclusive categories and have proposed alternative surveys to better capture diverse sexual identities without compromising data integrity or social harmony. The document also expands on living conditions, inquiring about specific household amenities, family structures, and health insurance coverage. These details are intended to provide a comprehensive snapshot of socio-economic conditions, potentially guiding future public policies. By tracking variables such as housing quality and access to technology, the census seeks to identify inequalities that were exacerbated during recent global health crises. However, the collection of personal names and identification numbers has raised significant concerns regarding data confidentiality and potential misuse. Critics argue that such intrusive questions violate statistical privacy norms and may be used for purposes unrelated to demographic analysis, such as electoral list verification. This relevance to open data lies in the tension between comprehensive information gathering and the ethical obligation to protect individual privacy, highlighting the need for transparent, secure, and purpose-limited data practices.
