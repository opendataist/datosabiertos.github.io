Las PyMEs, el motor de la economía cada vez más abandonado: «Durante este tiempo el sector que más ganó fue el financiero»

The manufacturing sector for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) is experiencing a profound crisis, characterized by sustained production declines and a stagnant internal market driven by falling purchasing power. Despite minor monthly fluctuations, the annual trend reveals deep structural difficulties, exacerbated by a financial model that benefits a few rather than supporting productive industries. This economic stagnation threatens the viability of local businesses, as consumers reduce spending and companies deplete their inventory without recovery prospects. A critical concern is the impact of deregulation and open imports, which disproportionately harm national industry. Experts argue that the absence of protective measures allows foreign competition to overwhelm local producers, leading to closures and job losses without corresponding new investments. The current policy framework fails to address the unfair competitive landscape, leaving SMEs unable to compete against imported goods while simultaneously facing excessive tax burdens compared to regional peers. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the urgent need for transparent, granular economic indicators to track the real-time health of the private sector. Accurate, publicly accessible data on production, employment, and trade flows is essential to validate these claims, inform public policy, and support evidence-based advocacy. Without reliable data, the true cost of deregulation remains obscured, hindering efforts to create fairer economic conditions and fostering an informed debate on sustainable industrial development.
