La violencia contra la mujer persiste y sucede en casa como fuera de ella

The recent Encabih survey reveals that nearly half of Guatemalan women have experienced violence in at least one area of their lives, with sexual and psychological forms being the most prevalent. The data indicates that violence is most frequent in the community, such as in public spaces and transportation, while domestic violence within the immediate family reported the lowest incidence. This contrast raises concerns about potential underreporting in private spheres, as victims may fear retaliation from close relatives, highlighting the complex dynamics of privacy and safety in victim surveys. Critically, the study exposes that violence is not confined to specific demographics but is a generational issue affecting women across all age groups, with middle-aged adults showing the highest rates. The high prevalence of sexual violence suggests a systemic normalization of control and subordination, reinforcing harmful social imaginaries that view women as inferior. This widespread impact necessitates urgent government intervention to address prevention and protection, as the risks remain consistently high regardless of age or relationship status, underscoring the pervasive nature of gender-based aggression. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the importance of rigorous, ethical methodology in collecting sensitive social statistics. The INE’s approach, involving female specialists in psychology and social work to ensure confidentiality and emotional safety, serves as a best practice for handling vulnerable populations. By providing transparent, large-scale insights into the reality of violence, such open datasets empower policymakers, researchers, and civil society to analyze trends, challenge assumptions, and design evidence-based interventions to combat gender inequality effectively.
