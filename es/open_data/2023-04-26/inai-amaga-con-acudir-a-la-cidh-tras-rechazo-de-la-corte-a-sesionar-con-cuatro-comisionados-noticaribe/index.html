INAI amaga con acudir a la CIDH tras rechazo de la Corte a sesionar con cuatro comisionados - Noticaribe

The Mexican Supreme Court denied INAI’s request to operate with only four commissioners due to lack of quorum, forcing the transparency institute to halt decision-making until a final constitutional resolution. This ruling stems from President López Obrador’s veto of two Senate-approved candidates, creating a political deadlock that paralyzes the body’s core functions. The situation highlights the critical importance of institutional independence in open data governance. When transparency agencies are politically neutered, public accountability suffers, and the flow of information becomes restricted. The threat of appealing to the Inter-American Court underscores how legal mechanisms are essential to protect data access rights against executive overreach. This case is vital for open data advocates as it demonstrates how legislative and executive actions can compromise transparency infrastructure. It serves as a warning that without robust checks and balances, the very foundation of public data access can be undermined, jeopardizing democratic oversight and citizen trust in government operations.
