Manfred rechaza datos del censo y no irá a la reunión convocada por el INE

The Mayor of Cochabamba rejects the National Institute of Statistics' census results, arguing they underestimate the city's population compared to municipal projections derived from local health and territorial data. This divergence highlights a significant methodological conflict between official state figures and local administrative estimates, questioning the accuracy of current demographic tracking mechanisms in the region. An independent demographer suggests that while the municipality's higher estimate may be inflated, the official count could also be too low, indicating a need for transparent methodological review. The dispute underscores the complexity of rapid urban growth and speculative real estate development, which complicates accurate population assessment when counting methods do not adequately reflect actual residential occupancy in expanding vertical structures. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it exposes critical gaps in data transparency and public trust. When citizens and local authorities cannot verify how official statistics are generated, it erodes the utility of public datasets for planning and policy. The conflict illustrates the urgent need for accessible, understandable, and reproducible data methodologies to ensure that statistical information serves as a reliable foundation for governance rather than a source of political contention.
