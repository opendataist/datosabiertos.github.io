CEOE-Cepyme Cuenca señala que el último Directorio de Empresas avisa de la debilidad del tejido productivo provincial

The article highlights the fragility of Cuenca’s local economy, where the vast majority of businesses are micro-enterprises with few or no employees. This demographic structure renders the provincial business sector exceptionally vulnerable to economic instability, as evidenced by a significant decline in total company registrations. The high proportion of non-salaried enterprises means that broader economic downturns disproportionately impact the region’s productive capacity, leading to a sharp reduction in the overall number of active firms. A key finding is the substantial disappearance of businesses over the last year, with the majority being companies without any workers. This trend marks the lowest level of productive tissue in two decades, suggesting a systemic erosion of local entrepreneurship. The data implies that small-scale, individual-led enterprises are the most at risk, driving the aggregate decline in business density and reflecting a lack of robust corporate structures capable of weathering financial shocks. This report is relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how accessible statistical registries can reveal critical socioeconomic vulnerabilities. By analyzing publicly available enterprise data, stakeholders can identify at-risk sectors and demographic trends, informing policy decisions aimed at stabilizing local economies. Transparent data access allows for better monitoring of business health, enabling targeted interventions to support the most fragile segments of the market and foster sustainable economic growth.
