Las 13 cosas que debe saber sobre el día del Censo de Población y Vivienda

This article highlights the extensive logistical preparations for Bolivia’s upcoming national census, the first in over a decade, emphasizing its critical role in shaping future governance. The collected demographic, economic, and social data will directly inform public policy planning and assist the electoral body in redistributing political seats and redrawing constituencies. This underscores the census as a foundational tool for ensuring representative democracy and equitable resource distribution based on accurate population mapping. The operational framework involves a massive mobilization of hundreds of thousands of volunteers to interview residents at their doorsteps, with strict regulations enforced on census day to ensure complete coverage. Restrictions on movement and commercial activity are implemented to facilitate the enumeration of every household, reflecting the state’s commitment to capturing a comprehensive snapshot of the population. The inclusion of new data points, such as migration and mortality, indicates an evolution in statistical methodology to better understand societal dynamics. This event is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it generates the primary dataset required for transparent governance and evidence-based decision-making. High-quality, publicly accessible census data allows researchers, civil society, and the government to analyze inequalities, monitor development goals, and hold institutions accountable. By establishing a reliable baseline of information, the census enables the open community to advocate for policies that address real-world needs, promoting a more informed and engaged citizenry.
