INE presentó nuevo enfoque estadístico de la pobreza con mirada multidimensional

The introduction of the Multidimensional Poverty Index (IPM) in 2024 represents a significant shift in how Uruguay measures social deprivation, moving beyond traditional monetary metrics to include education, housing, and connectivity. This comprehensive approach allows for a deeper understanding of structural poverty, highlighting that nearly one-fifth of the population experiences deprivations across multiple essential life domains, not just economic ones. Relevant data reveals critical vulnerabilities in schooling completion, informal labor participation, and substandard housing conditions, with significant disparities between urban and rural areas. These findings underscore the urgent need for targeted policy interventions that address specific regional needs and structural inequalities, ensuring that social protection systems evolve to meet the complex realities of vulnerable households. This development is crucial for open data as it demonstrates the value of accessible, granular datasets in driving evidence-based governance. By making these multidimensional indicators public, the state empowers civil society and researchers to analyze social exclusion more rigorously, fostering transparency and enabling more effective, inclusive policy design that transcends simple income-based assessments.
