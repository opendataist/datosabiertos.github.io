Enero de 2025 inició con 0.50 por ciento de inflación

Guatemala’s statistical institute has implemented methodological improvements in its consumer price index to better capture seasonal variations and current consumption habits. By optimizing sampling blocks and updating product categories, the institution aims to eliminate distortions caused by specific times of the year, such as school supply demands or agricultural climate cycles. This ensures the economic data reflects the real purchasing behavior of society rather than outdated categories, thereby enhancing the accuracy of inflation monitoring. The evolution of the statistical basket involves removing obsolete items and adding modern alternatives, such as new types of printer inks and popular food vendors, while maintaining the overall structure of spending classes. These adjustments demonstrate a commitment to keeping statistical indicators relevant to technological and social changes. Consequently, the updated dataset offers a more precise reflection of the actual cost of living, allowing policymakers and citizens to rely on metrics that align with contemporary market realities rather than historical standards. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical importance of data governance and adaptability in public statistics. It illustrates how government agencies must continuously refine their data collection frameworks to maintain credibility and utility. Furthermore, the initiative to collaborate with the migration institute underscores the potential for inter-institutional data integration. Such efforts represent a move toward more interconnected and comprehensive public datasets, which are essential for fostering transparency and enabling better-informed public discourse on complex social and economic issues.
