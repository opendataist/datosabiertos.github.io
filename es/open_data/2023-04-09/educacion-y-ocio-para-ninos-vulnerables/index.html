Educación y ocio para niños vulnerables

The article highlights the persistent risk of social exclusion affecting a significant portion of Catalonia’s population, particularly children. By analyzing the economic profiles of families receiving aid, it reveals that a vast majority live in clear poverty. This data underscores the urgent need for societal intervention to ensure that vulnerable youth have access to dignified leisure and developmental opportunities, which are otherwise out of reach for these households. The Pere Tarrés Foundation addresses this gap by providing educational and recreational support, including summer camps and afternoon programs. These initiatives offer more than just entertainment; they provide essential nutritional support, socialization, and health checks. The narrative emphasizes that these services act as a critical safety net, allowing children from disadvantaged backgrounds to experience childhood activities that their peers take for granted, thereby mitigating the isolating effects of poverty. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how public statistics, such as those from Idescat, can directly inform and validate the operational needs of social organizations. The correlation between official poverty rates and the foundation’s beneficiary demographics demonstrates the power of transparent data in identifying vulnerable groups. Such alignment encourages the use of open data to drive targeted social policies, ensuring that resources are allocated efficiently to support inclusion and equality.
