The article highlights a severe crisis in food access for France’s lowest-income households, revealing that high inflation forces many to reduce grocery spending, skip meals, and decrease consumption of healthy produce. This directly impacts nutrition and public health, demonstrating how economic pressures disproportionately affect vulnerable populations. This situation is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent, high-quality statistical sources to monitor social precarity and food insecurity. Reliable data enables policymakers to identify systemic inequalities and design effective interventions, ensuring that government responses are based on accurate, accessible evidence rather than speculation. Ultimately, the study illustrates the real-world implications of macroeconomic trends on daily life. It emphasizes that open, rigorous statistical analysis is essential for understanding the human cost of inflation, allowing societies to better address structural issues that hinder equitable access to basic necessities.
