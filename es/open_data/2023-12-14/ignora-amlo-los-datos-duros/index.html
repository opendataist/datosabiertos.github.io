Ignora AMLO los datos duros

The article highlights a stark dissonance between the Mexican government’s dismissal of international educational metrics and the empirical reality of the country’s academic performance. Despite the administration’s refusal to heed bodies like the OECD, recent PISA results reveal that Mexican students score significantly below the average in mathematics, reading, and sciences. This deliberate neglect of standardized evaluations risks isolating the nation from global benchmarks, suggesting a political prioritization of ideological independence over verifiable educational quality and long-term competitiveness. Such disregard for data-driven insights is presented as a critical failure that jeopardizes the future development of Mexican society. By rejecting recommendations and minimizing standardized tests, the current approach undermines the foundational role of education in fostering civic responsibility and economic autonomy. The text argues that ignoring international progress leads to intellectual dependency, where the population remains ill-equipped to compete globally or solve complex domestic challenges, ultimately compromising constitutional guarantees regarding the right to education. This narrative is crucial for open data because it exemplifies the tangible consequences of rejecting transparency and evidence-based policy. When governments dismiss or ignore accessible, high-quality datasets from reputable international organizations, they obstruct the public’s ability to hold leaders accountable. The article serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of anti-intellectualism in governance, reinforcing the necessity of open data as a tool for democratic oversight and ensuring that educational policies are grounded in objective reality rather than political convenience.
