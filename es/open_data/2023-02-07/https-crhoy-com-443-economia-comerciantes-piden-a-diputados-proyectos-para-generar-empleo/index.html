ES EN

The Chamber of Commerce advocates for structural labor reforms in Costa Rica to boost formal employment and economic competitiveness. By supporting the "Exceptional Shifts" project, they aim to align production cycles with workforce needs, significantly improving women's labor participation. This legislative push seeks to address rigid working hours that currently hinder efficient business operations and inclusive hiring practices. Concurrently, the organization demands reductions in non-essential social security burdens. They welcome the Social Security Fund’s move to align minimum contribution bases with actual salaries, arguing this promotes fairer registration. Lowering these costs is viewed as essential for encouraging informal workers to join the formal sector, thereby enhancing labor market stability and reducing the financial strain on both employers and employees. This article highlights the critical intersection between policy advocacy and data-driven labor analysis. It underscores how accurate salary and employment data are vital for designing equitable social security systems. For open data, this case demonstrates how transparent, accessible labor statistics empower stakeholders to propose evidence-based reforms, fostering a more inclusive and formally integrated economy through improved policy transparency and public oversight.
