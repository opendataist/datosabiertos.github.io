Orden judicial urge al Senado a nombrar comisionados pendientes del Inai

The federal judiciary has repeatedly ordered the Senate to appoint pending commissioners to the National Institute of Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), highlighting a prolonged political gridlock that undermines institutional legality. This judicial intervention underscores the critical link between open data governance and the rule of law, as the inability to fill these vacancies compromises the autonomous body’s capacity to enforce citizens' rights to information and protect personal data effectively. The conflict reveals deep partisan divisions within the Congress, where legislative committees have failed to reach the necessary majorities to convene extraordinary sessions or approve appointments. This legislative inaction demonstrates how political maneuvering can obstruct transparency mechanisms, illustrating that the implementation of open data standards requires not just technical frameworks but also sustained political will and cooperation among diverse political forces. The Supreme Court’s recent ruling allowing the INAI to operate with fewer than the mandated commissioners offers a temporary procedural fix but does not resolve the fundamental democratic deficit. This situation is relevant to open data because it emphasizes that the integrity and effectiveness of transparency institutions depend on their full legal composition, ensuring that access to public information remains an independent, robust, and legally sound pillar of democratic accountability.
