Costa Rica faces rising electricity costs because its hydroelectric-dependent grid cannot meet demand during severe droughts, forcing the reliance on expensive thermal power plants fueled by bunker. This shift from renewable sources to fossil fuels significantly increases operational expenses, which are passed directly to consumers through tariff hikes to cover both current and past deficits. The situation highlights the vulnerability of relying heavily on climate-sensitive renewable energy when weather patterns become unpredictable. While the country strives for sustainability, environmental factors like El Niño can quickly undermine energy security and affordability, revealing the complex balance between green energy goals and immediate reliability needs. This case is relevant to open_data as it demonstrates how transparent access to climate, energy consumption, and pricing data is essential for public understanding and policy planning. Open datasets allow analysts to predict such financial impacts, enabling better resource management and helping stakeholders visualize the real-world consequences of environmental changes on essential services.
