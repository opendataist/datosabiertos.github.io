Pobreza: Una inadmisible deuda social con la población

The article concludes that despite significant natural wealth, the country suffers from persistent poverty stagnation, with roughly a quarter of the population remaining in deprivation since 2019. This lack of progress occurs alongside relatively high economic growth, revealing a critical disconnect where broader economic gains do not translate into improved living conditions for the majority. The situation is exacerbated by volatile reductions and a rise in inequality, which threatens social stability and governance. Crucially, the stagnation is driven by deteriorating labor and health indicators, including high informal employment and reduced access to healthcare. The Human Development Index also shows no improvement since 2014, suggesting that the current economic model fails to secure basic human capabilities. Instead of fostering sustainable development, the current labor market conditions often push workers deeper into chronic poverty, creating a cycle that undermines long-term economic resilience and social cohesion. This analysis is vital for open data initiatives because it highlights the necessity of monitoring not just monetary poverty rates, but multidimensional indicators like employment quality, health access, and social security coverage. Transparent, accessible data on these structural factors is essential for diagnosing the root causes of inequality and designing effective policies. Without granular visibility into these underlying variables, efforts to break intergenerational poverty and achieve genuine inclusive growth remain blind to the mechanisms that perpetuate economic exclusion.
