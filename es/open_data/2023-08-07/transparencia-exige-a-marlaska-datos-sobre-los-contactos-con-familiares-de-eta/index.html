Transparencia exige a Marlaska datos sobre los contactos con familiares de ETA

The Council for Transparency has compelled the Ministry of Interior to provide specific details regarding a meeting between penitentiary officials and ETA relatives, rejecting vague administrative evasions. This ruling emphasizes that public administration cannot use generic responses to obscure the actual content of discussions involving sensitive political and penal shifts, such as prisoner transfers. It underscores that citizens have a fundamental right to access information, which requires authorities to offer substantial answers rather than formalistic deflections. This case highlights the critical role of transparency bodies in enforcing accountability within government institutions. By challenging the Ministry’s refusal to disclose meeting specifics, the council reinforces the principle that administrative silence or indirectness does not satisfy legal obligations. The decision serves as a precedent for how public entities must handle requests for information, ensuring that procedural delays or broad disclaimers do not undermine the constitutional right to know what occurs within state functions. This article is highly relevant to open data as it illustrates the practical enforcement of data transparency laws. It demonstrates how legal frameworks protect the accessibility of public information against bureaucratic resistance, ensuring that data releases are substantive rather than perfunctory. For open data advocates, it confirms that institutional pressure is necessary to transform passive data availability into active, meaningful transparency, ensuring that citizens can hold power to account through concrete, verified information.
