Inflación persistente, por Editorial

The article highlights that Spain’s inflation has entrenched itself, with underlying rates rising significantly despite modest overall figures. This persistence indicates that price pressures are no longer driven solely by volatile energy or perishable food costs but have spread across the broader economy, complicating recovery efforts and underscoring the limitations of relying exclusively on monetary policy to address structural cost increases. A critical focal point is the sharp rise in food prices, which fuels labor tensions and threatens purchasing power. While temporary measures like VAT reductions provided slight relief, they failed to offset the fundamental lack of a coordinated pact between unions, employers, and the government. This absence of consensus on income distribution exacerbates the crisis, as both sides pursue conflicting strategies that risk either worsening inflation or eroding worker welfare. Relevance to open_data lies in the necessity of granular, transparent metrics to design effective interventions. Understanding specific sectoral drivers—such as the distinct spikes in sugar or eggs—requires accessible, detailed datasets that go beyond aggregate indices. Open data enables policymakers and researchers to analyze these localized supply chain issues and evaluate the impact of targeted subsidies, fostering evidence-based solutions that monetary policy alone cannot achieve.
