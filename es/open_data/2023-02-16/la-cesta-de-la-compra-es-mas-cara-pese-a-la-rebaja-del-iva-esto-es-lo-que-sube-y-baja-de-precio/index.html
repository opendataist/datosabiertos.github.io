La cesta de la compra es más cara pese a la rebaja del IVA: esto es lo que sube y baja de precio

The article analyzes recent fluctuations in food prices, highlighting a mixed trend where certain staples saw short-term decreases despite significant annual increases. Notably, beef, lamb, poultry, eggs, milk, oils, and fresh fruits experienced price drops in January. This monthly relief contrasts sharply with substantial interannual rises across these categories, indicating that while immediate costs for some essentials have lessened, the long-term pressure on household budgets remains high due to inflationary trends accumulated over the past year. Conversely, several other food groups continued to rise in cost, including cereals, bread, pork, fish, sugar, and various dairy and legume products. These increases suggest persistent inflationary pressures across diverse segments of the diet. The divergence between items that became cheaper and those that grew more expensive underscores the complex nature of current market dynamics, where temporary relief for specific goods does not necessarily offset broader economic challenges affecting food accessibility and affordability. This report is relevant to open data initiatives as it illustrates the critical importance of transparent, accessible consumer price indices for public analysis. By providing granular data on price changes alongside the impact of temporary tax exemptions, such information empowers citizens, researchers, and policymakers to monitor economic well-being. Open datasets that capture these detailed fluctuations enable better scrutiny of how fiscal policies, like reduced VAT on basic foods, influence market realities and help identify systemic trends in cost of living adjustments.
