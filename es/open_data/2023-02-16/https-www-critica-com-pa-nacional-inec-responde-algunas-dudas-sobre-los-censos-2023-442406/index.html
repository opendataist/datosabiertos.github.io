Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The recent national census in Panama, executed by the INEC, serves as a critical mechanism for generating reliable demographic and housing data after a twelve-year gap. This initiative is vital because accurate population statistics are the foundation for effective public policy; without clear insights into the demographic structure and distribution, the state remains unable to design specific programs that address the actual needs of its residents. To ensure data quality and inclusivity, the census utilized a prolonged, technology-assisted field methodology rather than a one-day event or an online-only platform. This approach allows for better supervision, accounts for diverse housing structures, and ensures the inclusion of vulnerable populations, such as those in shelters or living on the streets. Furthermore, strict confidentiality protocols guarantee that personal identifiers are protected, fostering trust and encouraging participation from both citizens and foreign residents who contribute to the local economy and society. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights how primary, high-quality statistical data drives transparency and governance. By systematically collecting and cross-referencing this information, the government creates a robust dataset that can be analyzed to identify social gaps and improve resource allocation. Understanding this rigorous collection process demonstrates how open, trustworthy data infrastructure is essential for holding institutions accountable and enabling evidence-based decision-making in democratic societies.
