The agreement limits price increases in major supermarkets, yet broader inflation remains high due to unregulated sectors like fresh produce and smaller stores. This discrepancy highlights how averages mask distribution inequalities, revealing the critical need for granular data to understand true market dynamics. Market expectations slightly cooled, anticipating lower inflation, driven partly by new broader agreements on meat prices. However, the persistent gap between controlled and uncontrolled sectors demonstrates that partial data points are insufficient for accurate economic analysis. This situation is vital for open data because it exposes the limitations of aggregated statistics. Transparent, detailed datasets are essential to identify where agreements succeed or fail, allowing policymakers and citizens to see the reality beyond official averages.
