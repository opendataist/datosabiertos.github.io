EEUU sufre su peor temporada de gripe en al menos 15 años

The United States is currently experiencing its most intense winter virus season in fifteen years, driven primarily by a surge in influenza cases. While COVID-19 and RSV are showing declining trends, the high volume of flu-related clinic visits has forced school closures in several states and resulted in significant hospitalizations and deaths. This data highlights the severe public health burden of seasonal respiratory viruses and underscores the urgency of monitoring real-time health metrics to understand epidemic scales. This situation is particularly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical importance of transparent, accessible public health information for accurate situational awareness. When government agencies restrict access to data or silence experts, it impedes the ability of researchers and the public to verify claims, assess risks, or make informed decisions. The current administration’s pause on health communications serves as a stark reminder that without guaranteed data transparency, societal resilience against health crises is compromised, and trust in scientific institutions erodes. Ultimately, the crisis emphasizes that open data is not merely a technical asset but a fundamental component of effective crisis management. Accessible statistics on vaccination rates, hospitalization trends, and viral strains allow for better resource allocation and public adherence to preventive measures. Protecting the flow of health data ensures that communities can respond swiftly to outbreaks, while obstructing this flow leaves populations vulnerable to misinformation and delayed interventions during public health emergencies.
