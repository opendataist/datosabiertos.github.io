Segado: 'El PSOE no ha hecho ni una sola propuesta en las siete reuniones celebradas por la Ponencia para modificar la Ley de Transparencia' - murcia.com

The article highlights a legislative shift in the Region of Murcia aimed at strengthening institutional transparency by replacing the existing Council with an independent Commissioner. This key change ensures the appointing authority is a qualified majority of the regional assembly rather than the government, thereby enhancing democratic accountability and reducing executive influence over oversight bodies. By consolidating the roles of the Transparency Commissioner and the Ombudsman, the proposal seeks to create a more technical and efficient mechanism for citizens. This structural integration allows individuals to directly appeal when their rights are ignored, making the oversight process more accessible and responsive to public demands for genuine administrative openness. This development is highly relevant to open_data initiatives as it addresses the fundamental governance structures required to ensure data integrity. Independent oversight is critical for maintaining public trust in government information, and reforming the appointment process to require broad political consensus helps safeguard the impartiality necessary for effective open data policies and citizen engagement.
