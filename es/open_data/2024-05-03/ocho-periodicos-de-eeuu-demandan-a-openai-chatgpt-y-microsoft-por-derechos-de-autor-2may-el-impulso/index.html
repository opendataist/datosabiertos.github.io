▷ Ocho periódicos de EEUU demandan a OpenAI (ChatGPT) y Microsoft por derechos de autor #2May - El Impulso

Eight US newspapers have filed a federal lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging that these technology giants infringed on copyright laws by scraping millions of articles without permission or compensation to train generative AI models like ChatGPT and Copilot. The plaintiffs argue that the companies appropriated protected journalistic content to drive commercial success, demanding proper consent and fair value for such use. This legal action highlights a critical tension between traditional media and AI developers regarding the ethical use of public data. While OpenAI claims its use of publicly available information constitutes legitimate fair use, the lawsuit emphasizes the necessity of obtaining explicit authorization from content creators. This reflects a broader industry struggle to balance innovation with respect for intellectual property rights and journalistic labor. This case is highly relevant to open data because it challenges the assumption that publicly accessible information is freely usable for training AI systems without restriction. It underscores the evolving legal landscape where open access does not necessarily equate to open licensing, forcing developers to reconsider data governance practices. The outcome may establish precedents that significantly impact how open datasets can be legally utilized in machine learning, potentially requiring more rigorous attribution and licensing protocols in future AI developments.
