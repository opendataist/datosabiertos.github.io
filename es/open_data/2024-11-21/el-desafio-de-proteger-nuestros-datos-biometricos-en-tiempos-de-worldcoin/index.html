El desafío de proteger nuestros datos biométricos en tiempos de Worldcoin

The article argues that biometric data poses unique, irreversible security risks because, unlike passwords, it cannot be changed if compromised. In developing nations like the Dominican Republic, urgent socioeconomic issues often push data privacy to the back burner, creating a vulnerable environment where personal information is easily exploited. This lack of prioritization allows companies to collect sensitive data with little oversight, as citizens frequently underestimate the permanent value of their biological identifiers. Regulatory frameworks currently fail to provide effective protection due to weak enforcement mechanisms and a lack of dedicated supervisory authorities. Existing laws establish principles for consent and processing but lack significant sanctions or independent oversight bodies to ensure compliance. Consequently, private entities can operate with impunity, transferring sensitive data across borders without respecting local sovereignty or ensuring adequate security standards, leaving citizens exposed to surveillance and identity theft. This situation is critical for open data initiatives because true transparency and utility of data depend on trust and security. Without robust legal frameworks and citizen awareness, the collection of biometric data undermines public confidence in digital systems. Therefore, protecting data sovereignty and establishing clear accountability are prerequisites for any open data ecosystem, ensuring that digital innovation does not come at the cost of individual privacy and fundamental rights.
