Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The publication of the first residential center census in Spain marks a significant step toward transparency in social services, yet it highlights critical challenges in data reliability. While the report aims to provide a comprehensive overview of facilities for the elderly and disabled, independent associations argue that the methodology lacks rigor. Significant discrepancies exist between official figures, including the unexplained removal of over 15% of initial records and inconsistencies regarding total facility counts and available bed numbers. The most pressing implication of this flawed data is the stark disconnect between statistical availability and real-world needs. The census claims tens of thousands of vacant beds, while thousands of individuals with severe dependency wait in limbo for placement. Furthermore, the metrics used to calculate coverage ratios are questioned for not accurately reflecting the demographics of users, obscuring the true scale of the shortage and potentially misleading policymakers about the sufficiency of the current infrastructure. This article is crucial for the open data community because it illustrates the dangers of publishing datasets without sufficient transparency regarding their creation and validation processes. It underscores that simply making data public is insufficient if the underlying methodology is opaque or if the results contain obvious logical contradictions. To be useful for public accountability and effective policy-making, open data must be accompanied by clear documentation on cleaning processes, source verification, and logical consistency checks.
