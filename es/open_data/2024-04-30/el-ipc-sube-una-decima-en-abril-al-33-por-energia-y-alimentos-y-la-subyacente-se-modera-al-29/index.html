El IPC sube una décima en abril, al 3,3%, por energía y alimentos, y la subyacente se modera al 2,9%

The article highlights a recent resurgence in inflation, with the consumer price index rising to 3.3% annually due to increased costs for gas and food. This trend marks a shift after several months of moderation, suggesting that external factors and base effects are temporarily reversing previous downward pressures. Understanding these fluctuations is crucial for policymakers and economists who rely on timely, transparent data to assess economic health and implement appropriate monetary or fiscal strategies. Notably, underlying inflation fell below the general rate for the first time in nearly two years. This divergence indicates that volatile components like energy and unprocessed food are driving the headline numbers, while the core economy experiences more stable pricing. This distinction is vital for analyzing the true purchasing power of households and the competitiveness of businesses, separating temporary shocks from sustained inflationary trends that influence long-term financial planning. This report is relevant to open data initiatives because it exemplifies how publicly accessible, high-frequency statistical indicators enable real-time economic monitoring. By making detailed breakdowns of inflation components widely available, institutions empower researchers, journalists, and citizens to verify official narratives and conduct independent analyses. This transparency fosters informed public debate and ensures that data serves as a reliable tool for accountability and decision-making across various sectors.
