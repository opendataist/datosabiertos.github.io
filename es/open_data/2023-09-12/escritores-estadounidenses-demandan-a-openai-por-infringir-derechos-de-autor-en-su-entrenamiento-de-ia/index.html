Escritores estadounidenses demandan a OpenAI por infringir derechos de autor en su entrenamiento de IA

Writers, including Pulitzer winner Michael Chabon, have sued OpenAI, alleging unauthorized use of their copyrighted works to train ChatGPT. This lawsuit highlights the growing tension between AI developers and creators, with multiple collective actions recently filed against OpenAI and other tech giants like Microsoft and Meta. These companies argue that training AI on publicly available data constitutes fair use, a claim authors strongly contest. The relevance to open data is profound, as this conflict underscores critical ethical and legal debates regarding data sourcing and intellectual property rights. While Open Data promotes the free sharing of information, this lawsuit illustrates the potential for such practices to infringe on individual creators' rights. It questions whether the open nature of web data justifies its commercial exploitation by AI models without explicit consent or compensation. Ultimately, this legal battle may redefine how AI companies handle public datasets. The outcome could establish precedents that either protect creative works from unauthorized AI training or validate the current methods of data harvesting. This decision will significantly impact the future of open data usage in artificial intelligence development, balancing innovation with copyright protection.
