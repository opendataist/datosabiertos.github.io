Central, Asunción y el Alto Paraná concentran el 66% de las empresas

The article highlights the significant geographical and structural disparities in Paraguay’s formal business landscape, revealing that economic activity is heavily concentrated in the Central Department, Asunción, and Alto Paraná. This uneven distribution indicates that employment opportunities and formal labor markets are predominantly accessible only in these specific zones, while other departments remain largely devoid of active companies, thereby limiting economic development and job creation in those regions. A critical insight regarding the composition of the market is that micro-enterprises dominate the sector, comprising the vast majority of both active firms and the national labor force. While large corporations exist, they are few and located in major urban centers. This concentration of micro-businesses underscores their vital role in sustaining the local economy, yet it also suggests that the formal private sector is primarily driven by small-scale operations rather than industrial or corporate giants, shaping the overall economic resilience and vulnerability of the country. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it exposes the scale of informality, with a majority of economic units operating outside the formal system. By providing transparent, geo-referenced statistics on where businesses are located and their sizes, the initiative empowers citizens and policymakers to identify regions with low economic activity and high informality. Such visibility is essential for designing targeted policies that encourage formalization, reduce regional inequalities, and foster inclusive economic growth across all departments.
