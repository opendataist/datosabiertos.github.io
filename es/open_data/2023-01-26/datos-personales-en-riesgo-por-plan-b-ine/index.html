Datos personales, en riesgo por plan B: INE

The detailed report by the Executive Secretary of the INE concludes that the electoral reform plan B produces a regressive effect incompatible with constitutional rights. By restructuring the electoral institute, eliminating key bodies, and reducing personnel, the reforms jeopardize essential citizen rights such as voting secrecy, political equality, and transparency. This structural dismantling threatens the very foundation of free and authentic elections, creating a high risk of institutional instability and compromising the integrity of the electoral process. Furthermore, the document highlights a severe loss of institutional autonomy, as the INE would be subjected to federal administrative and budgetary norms controlled by the Executive Branch. This subordination undermines the necessary independence of the electoral authority regarding political actors it regulates. Additionally, the integration of citizen service modules into government offices introduces significant security and data protection risks, potentially exposing sensitive voter information to unauthorized access or manipulation by third parties. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of institutional independence in safeguarding data integrity and privacy. The proposed reforms suggest a dangerous intersection between electoral administration and executive control, which could compromise the transparency and security of personal data management. For open data advocates, this serves as a stark warning that without autonomous governance, the fundamental principles of data protection and public accountability are at risk of being eroded by political influence.
