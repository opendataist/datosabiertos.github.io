ES EN

This article presents demographic trends regarding fatherhood in Costa Rica, highlighting significant shifts over the past two decades. The average age of cohabiting fathers has risen notably, reflecting broader societal changes in family structure. Concurrently, the proportion of fathers present at childbirth has decreased, suggesting a transformation in traditional engagement during the birthing process. Educational attainment and employment patterns further characterize this demographic. Most fathers hold primary education degrees, with a smaller segment possessing university titles. The majority of these men are active in the labor force, predominantly occupying elementary occupations or roles such as mechanics and security officers, indicating the economic pressures and vocational distribution within father households. Crucially, the data reveals a substantial increase in domestic participation and child care responsibilities among fathers. A vast majority engage in household chores and dedicate significant weekly hours to caring for their children. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how statistical agencies can use transparent, accessible datasets to challenge outdated stereotypes, providing empirical evidence of evolving gender roles and enabling researchers to track social progress regarding paternal involvement.
