Buscan transparentar el registro de licencias de importaciones para agilizar las solicitudes y evitar discrecionalidades

The proposed legislation aims to establish an Open Data Portal for Importation, requiring the publication of all import license applications in accessible, open digital formats. This initiative seeks to eliminate the significant corruption risks and discretionary abuses currently inherent in Argentina’s import authorization systems, such as SIRA. By making data on products, amounts, and application statuses publicly available, the proposal ensures that administrative procedures are no longer opaque, thereby reducing the potential for arbitrary decision-making by officials. A central implication of this measure is the enforcement of strict procedural standards to guarantee predictability and fairness. The bill mandates that resolution times must not exceed thirty days and that approvals should follow a chronological order of receipt. Crucially, it introduces the principle of "silence implies approval," meaning that if authorities fail to respond within the established timeframe, the license is automatically granted. This structural change shifts the burden of efficiency onto the state, protecting importers from delays and unreasoned rejections that have historically distorted the market. This proposal is highly relevant to the open_data movement because it demonstrates how transparent data access serves as a primary mechanism for accountability and anti-corruption. By treating public administrative processes as data sets that must be open by default, the project aligns with international best practices that link regulatory transparency to reduced governance risks. It highlights that true openness goes beyond mere publication; it requires structured, machine-readable data that allows citizens and oversight bodies to actively monitor state performance and demand compliance with legal standards.
