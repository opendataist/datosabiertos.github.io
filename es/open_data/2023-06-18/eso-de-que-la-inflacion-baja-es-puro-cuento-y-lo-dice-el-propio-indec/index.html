Eso de que la inflación baja es puro cuento, y lo dice el propio INDEC

The article concludes that recent economic indicators reveal an unyielding inflationary spiral and a severe erosion of purchasing power, demonstrating that the government lacks effective control over these structural issues. Although monthly inflation rates have slightly decreased, the annual rate remains at historic highs, indicating that any perceived deceleration is merely statistical noise rather than a genuine economic recovery. This persistent instability undermines confidence in the administration’s capacity to stabilize prices or restore public trust. Relevance to open data lies in the critical necessity of transparent, independent statistical monitoring to verify official narratives and expose the true scale of the crisis. When official figures highlight record-high costs for basic food baskets while wages stagnate, open data frameworks allow for the cross-verification of these discrepancies. This transparency is essential to move beyond political spin, ensuring that the actual impact on household budgets and poverty lines is accurately documented and accessible for public scrutiny. Furthermore, the analysis highlights how fiscal maneuvers and debt restructuring, often obscured by complex financial instruments, exacerbate inequality and social deterioraion. The data shows a significant gap between inflation and salary growth, proving that workers are losing substantial buying power. By making this information openly available, society can demand accountability regarding resource allocation and policy effectiveness, ensuring that the consequences of economic mismanagement are visible and cannot be dismissed through ambiguous political rhetoric.
