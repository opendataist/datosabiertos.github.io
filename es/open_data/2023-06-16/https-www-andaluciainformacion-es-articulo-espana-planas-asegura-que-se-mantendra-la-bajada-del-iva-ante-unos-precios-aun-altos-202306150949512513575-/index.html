Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The Spanish government’s decision to extend the reduced VAT on food highlights the critical role of transparency in fiscal policies affecting consumer prices. This temporary tax cut aims to alleviate the burden on households amidst persistently high inflation, demonstrating how legislative adjustments can directly influence market accessibility. By addressing these economic pressures, the state seeks to restore balance between production costs and retail affordability, ensuring that essential goods remain within reach for all citizens. The ongoing debate over price responsibilities underscores the need for clearer data chains within the agri-food sector. While officials acknowledge that neither primary producers, industry, nor distributors are solely to blame for the surge in costs, the complexity of the supply chain requires better visibility. Enhancing open data practices could help stakeholders identify bottlenecks and cost drivers more accurately, fostering a more accountable and equitable distribution of expenses across the entire food ecosystem. Furthermore, the legislative efforts surrounding food waste reduction and regulatory reforms emphasize the importance of accessible information for policy success. Although some initiatives were delayed by political cycles, raising public awareness remains a key achievement. Open data initiatives can support these goals by providing robust evidence for decision-making, enabling continuous improvement in sustainability and resource management. Ultimately, transparent data flows are essential for building trust and driving effective, long-term solutions in the food industry.
