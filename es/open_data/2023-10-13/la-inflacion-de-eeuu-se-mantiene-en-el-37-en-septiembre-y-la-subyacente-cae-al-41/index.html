La inflación de EEUU se mantiene en el 3,7% en septiembre y la subyacente cae al 4,1%

The United States inflation rate remained stable at 3.7% annually in September, slightly exceeding market expectations, while the core inflation index showed a notable decline. This trend indicates that the disinflationary process continues despite some upward pressures from specific sectors like housing and energy services. The stabilization of prices suggests that the economy is gradually moving away from the high-inflation dynamics seen in previous years, reinforcing the view that underlying price pressures are easing. Market experts interpret this data as evidence that inflationary drivers are diminishing, reducing the likelihood of further interest rate hikes by the Federal Reserve. Although certain components such as shelter and used vehicles show mixed signals, the overall trajectory points toward a sustained cooling of the economy. Analysts emphasize that labor market softening and decelerating wage growth support this outlook, allowing monetary authorities to adopt a patient stance rather than aggressively tightening policy. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the critical role of transparent, real-time statistical indicators in shaping macroeconomic policy and market expectations. Accessible and accurate public data, such as the Consumer Price Index, allows researchers, economists, and citizens to monitor economic health objectively. By making these datasets available, governments foster accountability and enable informed decision-making, ensuring that financial policies reflect actual economic conditions rather than speculation.
