Escritores estadounidenses demandan a OpenAI por infringir derechos de autor en su entrenamiento de IA

Multiple authors have filed a federal lawsuit against OpenAI, accusing the company of unauthorized copyright infringement by using their literary works to train ChatGPT. The plaintiffs argue that their high-quality writing was essential for the AI’s development and that the system can now accurately summarize and mimic their styles without permission. This legal action highlights the growing conflict between content creators and artificial intelligence developers regarding intellectual property rights. These lawsuits represent part of a broader wave of litigation against major tech firms utilizing copyrighted material for AI training. While companies claim their data usage constitutes legitimate fair use, creators maintain that the extraction of protected works without consent causes significant harm. The legal battles seek substantial damages and an injunction to halt these alleged unfair business practices, challenging the current norms of AI development. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it directly impacts the legal framework governing the use of public and private information for training machine learning models. As open data initiatives often involve aggregating and repurposing large datasets, these rulings could redefine what constitutes acceptable data usage. The outcome may enforce stricter compliance measures, potentially limiting the accessibility and reusability of open resources for AI innovation while protecting creator rights.
