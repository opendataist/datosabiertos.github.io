136 funcionarios municipales registraron 15.417 predios sin derecho propietario

The Municipality of La Paz has uncovered a systemic corruption scheme where municipal officials, complicit with neighborhood juntas, registered thousands of properties in the tax registry without legal ownership. This irregular practice allowed individuals to pay property taxes and facilitate transactions like sales or subdivisions, creating a false sense of legitimacy that violated constitutional principles regarding property rights. To address this crisis, the administration blocked all administrative procedures for the affected properties, preventing further illegal transfers or registrations. Although previous regulatory resolutions were annulled and officials were initially dismissed, labor authorities have mandated their reinstatement. Consequently, a comprehensive audit is underway to determine individual responsibilities and legal liabilities among the involved public servants. This situation highlights critical issues in open data governance, specifically the integrity of public registries and the transparency of municipal fiscal processes. It demonstrates how data manipulation can facilitate fraud and underscores the necessity for rigorous data verification mechanisms. By exposing how administrative records were exploited, this case emphasizes the vital role of open data initiatives in ensuring accountability, protecting civic rights, and maintaining the reliability of government information systems.
