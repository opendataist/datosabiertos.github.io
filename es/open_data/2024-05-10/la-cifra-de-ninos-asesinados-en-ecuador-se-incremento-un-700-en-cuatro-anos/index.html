La cifra de niños asesinados en Ecuador se incrementó un 700% en cuatro años

The article highlights a catastrophic escalation in violence against children and adolescents in Ecuador, driven largely by the infiltration of criminal gangs into vulnerable communities. The core finding is that this surge in lethal violence is not an isolated phenomenon but is structurally linked to the coercive recruitment of minors, which simultaneously triggers a crisis in educational retention and disrupts social stability. This trend reveals a dangerous cycle where state abandonment and normalized domestic violence create fertile ground for criminal organizations to exploit youth. The data indicates that exposure to violence profoundly impacts cognitive and emotional development, leading to long-term economic fragility and increased burdens on public health and education systems, thereby trapping affected individuals in a persistent state of vulnerability. This report is critical to open data discussions as it demonstrates how transparent, disaggregated statistics can expose systemic societal failures beyond mere crime rates. By making visible the intersection between organized crime, educational dropout, and domestic abuse, such data enables policymakers and civil society to move beyond reactive measures and develop holistic, evidence-based interventions that address the root causes of violence against minors.
