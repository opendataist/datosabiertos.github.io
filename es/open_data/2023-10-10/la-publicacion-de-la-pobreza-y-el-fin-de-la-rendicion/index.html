La publicación de la pobreza y el fin de la Rendición

The article critiques the Uruguayan government’s selective use of socioeconomic data during the approval of the Account Rendition law. Officials deliberately highlighted the improved poverty figures from the second semester of the previous year to showcase progress, while ignoring the full-year annual average or the more recent first-semester data of the current year. This strategic framing created a misleading narrative of recovery, contrasting sharply with the latest official statistics which reveal a resurgence in poverty levels back to pre-pandemic baseline figures, despite economic growth. The analysis exposes a pattern of political opportunism where data is manipulated to serve immediate administrative interests. When recent indicators showed an increase in poverty, particularly among children, officials resorted to denial or claimed ignorance of the newly available microdata. This tactic undermines democratic accountability, as it prevents a comprehensive assessment of the actual welfare situation. The discrepancy between the favorable half-yearly reports and the broader annual reality suggests that the current economic performance has failed to deliver sustainable improvements for the most vulnerable populations. This debate is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how releasing raw data, such as household survey microdata, does not automatically ensure transparency or accurate public understanding. Without independent verification and critical analysis by civil society and academia, policymakers can cherry-pick subsets of data to obscure negative trends. True open data practices require not just publication, but also mechanisms to prevent the instrumentalization of statistics, ensuring that all stakeholders have access to the full, unfiltered context of socio-economic conditions.
