¿Cuál es la alcaldía más segura de CDMX? Esto reporta el INEGI

The INEGI’s latest report reveals a statistically significant rise in citizens’ perception of insecurity across Mexico’s urban centers, marking a troubling shift from previous quarters. This data highlights a growing disconnect between official security metrics and public sentiment, emphasizing that safety is not just a function of crime statistics but heavily influenced by community trust and local policing strategies. The findings serve as a critical benchmark for evaluating the efficacy of municipal security policies nationwide. In contrast, Benito Juárez stands out as the safest borough, maintaining its top position for consecutive quarters through targeted local initiatives. This success underscores the value of decentralized, hyper-local security strategies that prioritize continuous community engagement and coordinated efforts between municipal and federal authorities. The borough’s sustained improvement demonstrates how focused resources and transparent communication can effectively reverse negative safety trends over time. Meanwhile, Miguel Hidalgo has achieved second place by transforming its reputation from high insecurity to a model of improvement. This turnaround illustrates the potential for rapid policy adjustment and increased surveillance infrastructure to restore public confidence. These case studies are vital for open data initiatives, as they provide comparable, granular metrics that allow researchers and policymakers to analyze the direct impact of specific interventions on public perception, fostering evidence-based governance and accountability.
