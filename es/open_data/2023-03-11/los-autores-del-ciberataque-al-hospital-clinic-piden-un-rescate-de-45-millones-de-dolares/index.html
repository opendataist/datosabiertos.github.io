Los autores del ciberataque al Hospital Clínic piden un rescate de 4,5 millones de dólares

The Generalitat of Catalonia firmly rejected negotiating with the attackers behind the cyberattack on Hospital Clínic de Barcelona, explicitly stating that no ransom will be paid. This decisive stance aims to prevent a cycle of extortion where paying rewards criminal organizations with increased capacity to launch more sophisticated attacks. By refusing to fund these operations, the government seeks to deter future incidents and uphold ethical security standards, despite the high risk that perpetrators might publish the stolen data. The incident severely disrupted hospital operations, forcing the cancellation of thousands of procedures and consultations. While the recovery process is gradual, the leadership emphasized that no data was permanently lost and is actively restoring services. The situation highlights the critical vulnerability of essential healthcare infrastructure to digital threats, demonstrating how cyber incidents can immediately impact patient care and public health systems, necessitating robust emergency response and resilience strategies. This event is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it underscores the imperative for transparency and public protection in digital crises. Authorities are actively warning citizens against fraudulent requests for personal information that may arise from the attack. This reinforces the importance of clear communication protocols and security awareness in open data contexts, ensuring that public institutions maintain trust and safety while managing sensitive information and mitigating the societal impact of cybersecurity breaches.
