Marco Lavagna’s appointment highlights the deep political connections between Argentina’s statistical agency and ruling parties. His background, rooted in family ties to former ministers and close links to current leaders, suggests that institutional independence may be compromised by partisan allegiances. This proximity raises concerns about the neutrality of official data, as personnel decisions appear driven by political loyalty rather than purely technical merit. The recent controversy over delaying the consumer price index release underscores the tension between electoral norms and statistical transparency. Although the delay was ultimately reversed due to public backlash, the incident reveals how administrative decisions can be perceived as politically motivated. Such actions risk eroding public trust in official statistics, especially when changes align conveniently with electoral calendars or political sensitivities. This case is critical for open data advocates because it illustrates how political interference can undermine data integrity. Reliable, unbiased statistics are essential for democratic accountability and informed public debate. When statistical agencies operate under perceived political pressure, the resulting data may lose its credibility, hindering evidence-based policy-making and compromising the fundamental principles of transparency and openness.
