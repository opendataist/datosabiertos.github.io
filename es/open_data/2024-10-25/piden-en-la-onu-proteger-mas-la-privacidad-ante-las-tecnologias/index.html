Piden en la ONU proteger más la privacidad ante las tecnologías

The United Nations Special Rapporteur on the right to privacy urges nations to fundamentally update data protection frameworks, arguing that existing regulations from the early 1990s are obsolete in the face of contemporary technological advancements. With the rise of artificial intelligence, big data, and neurotechnologies, the digital landscape has evolved significantly, creating inherent risks that older laws fail to address. Consequently, there is an urgent need to harmonize international standards by incorporating a human rights-based approach that ensures legality, transparency, and accountability in data processing. A central focus of this proposal is the imperative for explainability in automated decision-making systems. Individuals affected by algorithmic decisions deserve clear, accessible explanations regarding how their data influences outcomes, ensuring that technology serves humanity rather than operating opaquely. This shift requires developers and institutions to prioritize transparency, protecting sensitive information and vulnerable groups, including children, while maintaining confidentiality. These principles are essential for restoring trust in digital ecosystems and safeguarding fundamental freedoms in an increasingly interconnected world. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the delicate balance between leveraging large datasets for innovation and protecting individual privacy rights. For open data initiatives to remain ethical and sustainable, they must adhere to rigorous standards of transparency and human rights compliance. By advocating for updated legal frameworks that accommodate new technologies, this report emphasizes that open data must not come at the cost of personal security, ensuring that data utilization supports, rather than undermines, democratic values and individual dignity.
