La precarización laboral juvenil toma fuerza en la última década

The article highlights a structural crisis in Panama’s labor market, characterized by a sharp decline in formal private sector jobs and a corresponding rise in informal work and public administration roles. Young people are disproportionately affected, facing lower average wages and being concentrated in low-salary positions, which signals a systemic failure to generate quality employment opportunities. This trend suggests that economic growth driven by public spending and informality does not translate into sustainable, high-quality jobs for the younger workforce. A significant disconnect exists between educational output and market needs, with an oversupply of graduates in commercial and administrative fields while key sectors like technology and engineering face talent shortages. This misalignment exacerbates the difficulty for young professionals to find dignified employment, leading to increased alienation from the productive sector. The data indicates that the current educational model does not adequately prepare workers for the specific demands of high-growth industries, worsening the mismatch between skills available and those required. This analysis is crucial for open data because it demonstrates how granular, publicly accessible labor statistics can reveal deep socioeconomic inequities. By making wage disparities, employment sectors, and educational outcomes transparent, stakeholders can move beyond aggregate trends to identify specific structural bottlenecks. Such data-driven insights are essential for designing targeted policies that align education with market demands and foster private investment, ensuring that transparency leads to actionable solutions for youth employment challenges.
