El invierno 'enfría' la economía de Menorca: el turismo toca techo y el calzado destruye empleo

Menorca’s economy demonstrated recovery in 2023, marking a normalization after the pandemic, yet it remained the least dynamic Balearic territory. Although annual growth was positive, it lagged significantly behind neighboring islands, particularly in the final quarter due to reduced air connectivity. This persistent slowdown highlights regional disparities in economic resilience and the fragility of an economy heavily reliant on seasonal tourism and external access. Structural shifts are evident as tourism dominates recovery while manufacturing, especially footwear, contracts sharply. Conversely, service and construction sectors show modest gains, though industrial employment remains below pre-pandemic levels. This divergence underscores a broader trend of deindustrialization and increased dependence on the service sector, challenging efforts to diversify the local economic base beyond hospitality-related activities. This report is relevant to open data initiatives as it illustrates how public economic indicators reveal underlying structural vulnerabilities. Accessible data allows stakeholders to identify specific sectors needing support, track regional inequalities against broader archipelago averages, and inform policy decisions aimed at sustainable development and economic diversification in smaller islands.
