La violencia machista que no cesa: Mónica y Mainca, las asesinadas en Castilla y León en 2024

The article highlights a tragic and persistent trend of femicide in Castilla y León, emphasizing that the violence does not cease despite ongoing awareness efforts. It underscores that recent deaths in 2024 have contributed to a rising total of victims since records began in 2003, illustrating a systemic failure that continues to claim lives annually. This steady increase demonstrates the urgency of addressing gender-based violence as a persistent social crisis rather than an isolated series of events. The narrative reveals that official statistics often exclude certain victims, such as those killed outside the region, indicating a limitation in how data is captured and reported. By contrasting the official count with broader estimates, the text exposes discrepancies in how mortality from machismo is measured. This suggests that current tracking mechanisms may underrepresent the true scale of the issue, thereby affecting the accuracy of resource allocation and policy effectiveness. This content is highly relevant to open data because it reveals critical gaps in data completeness and transparency. For open data initiatives, this case illustrates the importance of integrating diverse data sources to ensure comprehensive coverage and the necessity of standardized definitions to capture all victims consistently. Improving data quality and accessibility can empower researchers and policymakers to identify patterns more accurately and implement more effective interventions to combat this violence.
