El precio de la vivienda sube todavía en 16 capitales y 24 grandes municipios más que la inflación

The housing market in many major Spanish cities has surged at a pace significantly exceeding general inflation, creating a disconnect between property value growth and the broader economic cost of living. This trend indicates that housing is becoming increasingly unaffordable relative to general price increases, particularly in coastal and tourist-heavy areas where price records are being broken. The report highlights that for numerous residents, rising property prices are not just reflecting normal market adjustments but represent a structural shift that outpaces wage and income growth. This disparity reveals a fragmented national market where specific locales experience double-digit percentage increases, driven by demand in high-desirability zones, while other interior regions see modest declines. The concentration of these rapid increases in certain metropolitan areas and coastal towns suggests that investment and demographic pressures are localized, exacerbating regional inequalities in wealth and access to housing. Consequently, the average citizen finds themselves in a scenario where asset inflation moves faster than the general inflation index, complicating financial planning for homebuyers and renters alike. This article is relevant to open data because it exemplifies the critical need for transparent, granular, and timely public datasets to monitor socio-economic health. Without open access to detailed real estate statistics, policymakers and researchers cannot accurately assess housing crises or design effective interventions. Reliable open data enables the tracking of these inequalities, fostering accountability and allowing stakeholders to understand the specific geographic and economic dynamics driving market distortions beyond aggregate national figures.
