El PIB creció un 2,7% en 2023, dos décimas más, por el mayor avance del consumo público y las exportaciones y pese a una caída más fuerte de la inversión

The National Statistics Institute (INE) has revised Spain’s economic data for 2021 to 2023, revealing that the economy performed significantly better than initially reported. The final figure for 2023 growth stands at 2.7%, higher than the previously estimated 2.5%, driven primarily by stronger-than-expected external demand and increased public administration spending. This upward revision highlights a more robust recovery following the pandemic crisis, contrasting with a sharper decline in corporate investment that was more severe than originally calculated. These adjustments result from the implementation of the RE-2024 update, which integrates new statistical sources and refined estimation methodologies to ensure greater accuracy. The INE emphasizes that such periodic revisions are essential for providing a precise measurement of the national economy. This methodological improvement aligns with international practices, as seen in similar updates across other EU countries, underscoring the dynamic nature of statistical compilation and the continuous effort to capture economic reality with higher fidelity. This article is highly relevant to open data as it demonstrates the iterative and transparent nature of public statistical processes. It illustrates how open datasets are not static but evolve as new information and improved methods become available. By publishing these revisions, the INE provides users with updated, more accurate information, enhancing the reliability of public sector data. This transparency allows policymakers and researchers to make better-informed decisions based on the most current understanding of economic performance, reinforcing the value of open, dynamic official statistics.
