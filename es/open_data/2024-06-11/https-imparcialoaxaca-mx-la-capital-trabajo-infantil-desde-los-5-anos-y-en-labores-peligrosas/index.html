Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Oaxaca faces a severe and normalized crisis of child labor, ranking among the worst in the nation with hundreds of thousands of minors engaged in dangerous or prohibited work. Despite official recognition of the problem by state authorities, social organizations highlight that this exploitation remains largely invisible and ignored by the community. The situation is exacerbated by the influx of child laborers from other entities into tourist centers, indicating a systemic failure in protection and vigilance that requires urgent, coordinated intervention beyond local borders. In response to this emergency, the state government has operationalized one hundred local commissions dedicated to preventing child labor and protecting working adolescents. These bodies aim to dismantle the apathy of previous administrations by establishing a concrete network for monitoring and eradicating exploitation across municipalities. The strategic focus is not only on legal enforcement but also on cultivating a culture of prevention, ensuring that public, private, and social sectors collaborate effectively to safeguard children's rights and well-being. This situation is critically relevant to open data because the high incidence of child labor persists partly due to its invisibility and lack of accessible, transparent information. By mandating the creation of local commissions and publishing official statistics, the government moves toward greater data transparency. Open data initiatives are essential here to monitor the effectiveness of these commissions, expose the true scale of the problem, and empower civil society to demand accountability, ensuring that statistical records translate into tangible social justice and policy enforcement.
