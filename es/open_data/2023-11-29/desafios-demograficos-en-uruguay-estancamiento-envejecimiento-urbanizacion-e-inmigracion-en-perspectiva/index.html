Desafíos demográficos en Uruguay: estancamiento, envejecimiento, urbanización e inmigración en perspectiva

Uruguay’s recent census reveals a demographic landscape defined by stagnation and profound structural shifts, primarily driven by migration counteracting natural population decline. Without the influx of immigrants, the country would have experienced a decrease in inhabitants due to lower birth rates and pandemic-related mortality. This highlights a critical challenge: an aging society with a rising median age and shrinking household sizes, necessitating policies that support an older population and smaller family units. A significant turning point in this data is the modernization of the census methodology, which marked the first time residents could complete forms online. This digital adaptation not only streamlined data collection but also reflects broader societal trends, such as the rise of remote work and increased pet ownership. These details offer a granular view of how technology and lifestyle changes are reshaping daily life and household composition in Uruguay. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it demonstrates the successful transition to digital data collection, enhancing accessibility and timeliness. It underscores the importance of capturing non-traditional metrics, like telework prevalence, to provide a holistic understanding of societal evolution. By making these nuanced demographic shifts publicly available, stakeholders can better analyze the impact of migration, aging, and digital integration on public policy and resource allocation.
