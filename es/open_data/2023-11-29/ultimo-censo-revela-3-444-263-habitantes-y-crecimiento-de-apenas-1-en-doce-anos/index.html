Último censo revela 3.444.263 habitantes y crecimiento de apenas 1% en doce años

The preliminary data from Uruguay’s 2023 Census reveals a critical demographic shift characterized by a stark decline in population growth and rapid aging. With a mere 1% increase since 2011, the nation faces a structural challenge driven by a sharp drop in birth rates that has been overtaken by mortality. This trend signals a potential population decline unless significant migration occurs, prompting urgent calls for policy reforms focused on care, healthcare, and productivity to support the growing elderly population. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, high-quality statistical releases serve as the foundation for democratic debate and legislative action. The public availability of these disaggregated figures allowed for immediate political responses, such as proposals for tax incentives to stimulate birth rates, highlighting the essential role of accessible official statistics in shaping national strategy. It underscores the necessity of clear, reliable datasets for identifying societal trends and mobilizing governmental responses. Ultimately, the census data emphasizes the imperative for long-term planning based on empirical evidence. The concentration of population growth in specific coastal departments and the increasing percentage of international residents further illustrate the dynamic nature of Uruguay’s social fabric. By making these insights openly available, statistical institutes empower citizens and policymakers to understand current realities and design sustainable interventions for the future, ensuring that data acts not just as a record of the past, but as a tool for navigating future challenges.
