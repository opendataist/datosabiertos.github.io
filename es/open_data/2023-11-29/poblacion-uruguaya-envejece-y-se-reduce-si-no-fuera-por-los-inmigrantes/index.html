Población uruguaya envejece y se reduce si no fuera por los inmigrantes

Uruguay’s population growth has stagnated at merely one percent, driven primarily by declining birth rates and a rising natural death rate. Without the significant contribution of immigration, the country would be experiencing a population decline rather than minimal growth. This trend highlights how migratory inflows are currently offsetting demographic shrinkage, underscoring the critical role of foreign residents in maintaining current population levels. Concurrently, the society is undergoing rapid aging, with the average age rising significantly and the proportion of seniors increasing sharply. As the number of working-age individuals shrinks relative to retirees, Uruguay faces imminent challenges for its social security system. The demographic shift implies that a smaller workforce must support a larger elderly population, necessitating increased productivity to sustain economic stability. This data is vital for open data initiatives as it illustrates how public statistics can reveal underlying social crises hidden by aggregate numbers. Transparent access to granular demographic trends allows policymakers and citizens to anticipate structural challenges, such as pension sustainability and labor market shifts. Ultimately, open datasets empower evidence-based planning essential for adapting to profound societal transformations like aging and migration dependency.
