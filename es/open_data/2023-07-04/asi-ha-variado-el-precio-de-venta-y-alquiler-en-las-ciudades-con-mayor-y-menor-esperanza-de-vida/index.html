Así ha variado el precio de venta y alquiler en las ciudades con mayor y menor esperanza de vida

The article reveals a strong correlation in Spain between higher life expectancy and elevated housing costs, suggesting that healthier living environments are significantly more expensive. Municipalities in the Community of Madrid, which boast life expectancy figures above 85 years, also exhibit the highest prices for both buying and renting homes. This indicates that economic barriers to accessing areas with superior public health outcomes are substantial, effectively linking demographic well-being with real estate affordability. Conversely, regions with lower life expectancy generally offer more affordable housing, reinforcing the idea that cost acts as a filter for quality of life. While exceptions exist, the overall trend demonstrates that citizens seeking longer, healthier lives often face a premium in the housing market. This dynamic highlights a potential inequality where the benefits of better urban conditions are accessible primarily to those with higher purchasing power. This analysis is crucial for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the power of combining diverse datasets—statistical health records and real estate market trends—to uncover hidden social patterns. By making these links visible through open data, researchers and policymakers can better address socioeconomic disparities, promoting transparency in how urban resources and health outcomes are distributed across different communities.
