Transparencia, cimiento de la democracia, afirma Blanca Lilia Ibarra

The article highlights transparency as a foundational pillar of democracy, not merely an optional addition. This perspective is crucial for open data because it frames public information as an essential tool for citizens to access fundamental rights like health and education. Recognizing transparency’s central role shifts the discourse from administrative compliance to democratic necessity, empowering society to demand accountability and better policy explanations. Current challenges involve strengthening the regulatory body’s enforcement capabilities and improving the quality of information responses. By addressing evasion tactics and enhancing sanctioning powers, institutions can ensure that transparency mechanisms are effective and reliable. This improvement is vital for open data ecosystems, as it guarantees that published data is accurate, accessible, and truly useful for public scrutiny rather than being obscured by bureaucratic hurdles or inadequate replies. Ultimately, fostering the daily use of access to information rights by the general population is a key goal. Widening socialization ensures that transparency serves as a bridge to other civic rights and strengthens democratic oversight. For open data initiatives, this implies a need for broader educational efforts, making data not just available but understandable and actionable for diverse communities, thereby reinforcing the integrity and utility of public sector information.
