Según el Indec, el 50% de los ocupados en el país ganaba menos de $80.000 en diciembre | Análisis

The report reveals that despite economic growth, Argentina’s income distribution remained highly unequal, with half of the workforce earning within a low-wage bracket. This disparity, measured by a Gini coefficient consistent with previous years, highlights the persistent gap between the wealthy majority and the majority of workers who struggle to secure higher earnings amidst soaring inflation. A significant gender wage gap exists, alongside a stark divide between salaried workers with pension deductions and those without. The latter group faced even higher inflationary pressures, underscoring the fragility of informal or unprotected labor sectors. Furthermore, reliance on non-labor income disproportionately affects lower-income households, indicating that wages alone are insufficient to sustain livelihoods for vulnerable populations. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it provides a transparent, statistical basis for analyzing social equity and policy effectiveness. By making detailed income distribution metrics publicly available, citizens and researchers can scrutinize government interventions, such as minimum wage adjustments, to evaluate their actual impact on reducing inequality and protecting workers in an unstable economic environment.
