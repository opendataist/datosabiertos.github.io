Mujeres buscan más libros de autoayuda que los hombres, revela el Inegi | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The INEGI’s MOLEC 2023 report highlights a significant gender disparity in reading habits, revealing that Mexican women consume more self-help and literary content than men. This trend underscores how reading motivations and genre preferences diverge by gender, with women showing a distinct inclination toward personal development and entertainment, while also demonstrating a strong preference for physical formats over digital ones. These insights are crucial for open data, as they challenge uniform assumptions about literacy and promote a more granular understanding of user behavior. Furthermore, the data exposes a critical dependency on informal distribution channels, with the majority of readers accessing materials through gifts or free sources rather than purchases. This shift indicates that economic barriers remain a primary obstacle to consistent reading habits, suggesting that promoting free, open-access resources could significantly expand readership. Understanding these consumption patterns allows policymakers to design more effective interventions that address the specific structural barriers preventing broader engagement with literature. Ultimately, the study marks a decline in the overall national readership rate, reaching its lowest level since 2016. The primary drivers for this drop are a lack of domestic role models and limited access to non-academic books at home. For the open data community, this narrative emphasizes the necessity of integrating socioeconomic and cultural context into statistical analyses. It illustrates how transparent, accessible data can reveal the deeper societal factors influencing public behavior, enabling more informed strategies to reverse declining literacy trends and foster a culture of reading.
