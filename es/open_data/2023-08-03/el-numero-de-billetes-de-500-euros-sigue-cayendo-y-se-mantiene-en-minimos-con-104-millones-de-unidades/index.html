El número de billetes de 500 euros sigue cayendo y se mantiene en mínimos, con 10,4 millones de unidades

The article details the ongoing decline in the circulation of 500-euro banknotes, reflecting a strategic shift toward financial transparency. This trend is a direct consequence of the decision to cease their production, aimed at curtailing illicit activities that often utilize high-value, untraceable currency. Although these notes remain legal tender, their reduced presence signals a broader move to limit cash anonymity in the economy. Concurrently, the data highlights significant imbalances in the distribution and return of lower-denomination bills. The negative net balance for 50, 100, 20, and 10-euro notes suggests that a substantial volume of cash is being returned to central banks rather than staying in active commercial circulation. This phenomenon is largely attributed to tourism, where visitors deposit foreign or accumulated cash into the banking system, effectively removing it from the general public’s daily transactions. This information is relevant to open_data because it demonstrates how public sector financial statistics serve as indicators of economic behavior and policy effectiveness. By analyzing the flow of currency, researchers can infer trends in consumer habits, tourism impacts, and the success of anti-money laundering regulations. Open access to such granular monetary data enables transparent scrutiny of how monetary policy influences the real economy.
