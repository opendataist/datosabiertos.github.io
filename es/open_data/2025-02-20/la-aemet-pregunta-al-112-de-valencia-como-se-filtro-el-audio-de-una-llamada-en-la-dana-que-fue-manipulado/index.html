La Aemet pregunta al 112 de Valencia cómo se filtró el audio de una llamada en la dana que fue manipulado

The Spanish Weather Agency (AEMET) has formally questioned the Valencian Government regarding the illicit leakage of an internal conversation between meteorological experts and emergency services personnel during the devastating 2024 DANA flood. AEMET emphasizes that such communications are legally protected confidential data, accessible only to judicial authorities, and expresses shock that a trusted inter-administrative relationship was undermined by this breach. The agency highlights that the leaked audio was selectively edited by political figures to misrepresent the severity of the warning, whereas the full recording shows that extreme danger alerts were accurately maintained. This incident underscores the critical intersection between emergency response protocols and data integrity during crises. The manipulation of partial audio excerpts demonstrates how sensitive operational data can be weaponized for political narratives, potentially obscuring factual preparedness assessments. AEMET stresses the necessity for robust custodial measures for emergency logs, reinforcing that the safeguarding of institutional communication channels is fundamental to maintaining public trust and effective crisis management across different governmental levels. The article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the dangers of non-consensual data release and the ethical complexities surrounding public sector transparency. It highlights that "open data" principles must be balanced with privacy protections and legal frameworks governing emergency information. The case serves as a cautionary example for data governance, showing how incomplete or manipulated data releases can distort public understanding of scientific risks and undermine the institutional cooperation required to manage catastrophic events effectively.
