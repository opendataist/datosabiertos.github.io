El empleo autónomo crece ante la caída del trabajo formal - Diario El Sureño

Argentina’s labor market is undergoing a structural shift driven by economic crisis and austerity policies. The formal sector has seen significant job losses, while the informal and autonomous sectors are expanding rapidly. This transition indicates that traditional employment models are weakening, forcing workers to rely on self-employment and unregistered work for survival, highlighting a decline in job stability and social security. The government’s efforts to reduce the state’s footprint have accelerated this trend, creating a vacuum in formal job creation. As private enterprises struggle with economic deceleration, the lack of robust alternative employment opportunities drives individuals toward precarious informal arrangements. This dynamic underscores the limitations of current economic strategies in fostering sustainable, high-quality employment, thereby exacerbating income insecurity for a large portion of the workforce. This situation is crucial for open data initiatives, as it reveals critical gaps in official statistical coverage. Traditional metrics often fail to capture the nuances of the growing informal economy, leading to incomplete policy assessments. Enhancing open data infrastructure to better measure informal employment and real income distribution is essential for developing effective social policies and ensuring transparency in the face of labor market instability.
