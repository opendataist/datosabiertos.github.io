Duelo de gigantes: New York Times demanda a Microsoft y OpenAI por apropiarse de sus contenidos sin permiso

The New York Times has initiated a landmark legal battle against OpenAI and Microsoft, accusing them of copyright infringement by training their artificial intelligence models on millions of protected news articles without permission. This lawsuit represents a critical confrontation between traditional media business models and the rapid expansion of AI technologies, highlighting the tension between technological innovation and the protection of intellectual property rights. The core implication is a struggle over the legitimacy of using copyrighted data for AI training. While some publishers have recently reached lucrative licensing agreements with AI companies, the Times argues that this unauthorized use constitutes illegal appropriation that competes directly with their own content. This case is expected to set a pivotal precedent, potentially reaching the highest courts, and will determine whether AI developers can freely utilize web content or must compensate creators for the data that fuels their algorithms. This article is highly relevant to open data because it challenges the assumption that publicly available information can be freely mined for commercial AI development without restriction. It underscores the urgent need for clear frameworks regarding data provenance, licensing, and ethical usage in machine learning. As AI relies heavily on vast datasets, this legal conflict emphasizes that "open" access does not necessarily imply unrestricted commercial use, pushing the open data community to address the rights of original content creators in an automated world.
