Radiografía de la indigencia: la cruda realidad de vivir en la calle en Mendoza

The article highlights a critical socioeconomic crisis in Gran Mendoza, where nearly half the population lives in poverty and the province leads the country in indigence rates. This statistical reality is reflected in the growing number of homeless individuals, including vulnerable youth and families, who face severe hardships and safety risks. The data underscores a systemic failure where existing public assistance devices are insufficient to address the rapidly expanding scale of vulnerability. Volunteer organizations report a significant surge in demand for basic services, struggling to meet the needs of increasing numbers of homeless people, particularly young men. Meanwhile, government officials acknowledge the diversity of this demographic, noting a rise in informal workers who resort to sleeping on the streets as a survival strategy. The disconnect between the magnitude of the problem and the limited capacity of shelters illustrates the urgent need for more comprehensive and scalable support systems. This article is highly relevant to open_data because it demonstrates how official statistics often stand in stark contrast to ground-level realities. It emphasizes the necessity of complementing aggregated census data with qualitative, granular insights from civil society and frontline aid groups. Such integration is crucial for accurate needs assessment, ensuring that policy decisions are informed by both quantitative trends and the nuanced human impacts that raw numbers might obscure.
