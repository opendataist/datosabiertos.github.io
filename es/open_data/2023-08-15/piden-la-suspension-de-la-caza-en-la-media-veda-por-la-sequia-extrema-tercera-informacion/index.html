Piden la suspensión de la caza en la media veda por la sequía extrema - Tercera Información

The article highlights how publicly accessible meteorological and hydrological data reveal an extreme drought in Extremadura, Spain, leading to severe water scarcity and habitat degradation. This environmental crisis disproportionately impacts wildlife, forcing species to congregate at limited resources and weakening their natural defenses. The transparency of these public datasets is crucial, as it provides the objective evidence needed to demonstrate that current hunting regulations ignore severe ecological constraints, thereby challenging administrative decisions that proceed with hunting seasons despite evident environmental emergencies. Consequently, the text argues for the mandatory suspension of the mid-season hunting period based on legal provisions that prohibit hunting when animals are deprived of normal defense capabilities due to extreme weather conditions. The author criticizes the lack of regulatory development for these "days of fortune," arguing that relying on arbitrary administrative discretion rather than scientific indicators constitutes a dereliction of duty. This underscores the importance of using standardized, open scientific metrics to inform policy, ensuring that legal protections for biodiversity are triggered automatically by verified environmental data rather than subjective or delayed official assessments. Relevance to open data lies in the necessity of utilizing transparent, independent metrics from organizations like AEMET to enforce environmental laws and protect wildlife. The case illustrates how public data can bridge the gap between ecological reality and legislative action, holding authorities accountable for inaction. By demanding that data-driven triggers replace discretionary decisions, the article advocates for a governance model where open information serves as the foundation for responsible conservation, preventing legal abuses and ensuring that resource management adapts swiftly to visible climate emergencies.
