Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article highlights a significant disparity in census volunteer recruitment across Bolivia, revealing that affluent urban zones are severely underrepresented while poorer areas are fully staffed. This geographic imbalance threatens the accuracy and representativeness of national demographic data, as critical population segments in high-income districts remain unmapped. Such gaps compromise the integrity of the census, which is essential for equitable resource allocation and effective public policy. Open data initiatives depend on comprehensive and inclusive datasets to function correctly. When specific socioeconomic groups are systematically excluded from data collection due to low participation, the resulting information becomes skewed, leading to biased analytical outcomes. This scenario underscores the vulnerability of open data ecosystems to participation gaps, where the quality of accessible government information directly reflects the inclusivity of the underlying data collection processes. Municipal authorities emphasize that accurate census data is vital for local planning and budgeting, making full civic participation a civic duty. The relevance to open data lies in the imperative for transparency and completeness; reliable public datasets must reflect the entire population to support evidence-based decision-making. Ensuring broad volunteer engagement across all income levels is crucial for maintaining the trustworthiness and utility of open government data, thereby fostering more just and informed urban development strategies.
