Según el Indec, el ingreso promedio en la Argentina es de $123.782 | Análisis

The report highlights a significant increase in average nominal wages, yet these gains fail to outpace the high inflation rate, resulting in a real decline in purchasing power for workers. This economic reality underscores the critical importance of transparent, high-quality open data, as citizens and analysts require accurate, accessible statistical information to distinguish between nominal adjustments and actual loss of income, enabling informed public debate on fiscal and monetary policies. Furthermore, the data reveals persistent and widening structural inequalities, particularly the substantial gender wage gap and the disparity between socioeconomic strata. By making this granular distribution data publicly available, open data initiatives facilitate a deeper understanding of social equity issues, allowing researchers and policymakers to identify vulnerable groups and design more targeted social programs that address the root causes of economic exclusion and inequality. Finally, the rise in the Gini coefficient indicates that inequality has intensified, with high-income earners benefiting disproportionately compared to lower-income populations. This trend emphasizes the necessity of ongoing, transparent monitoring of economic indicators. Open access to such longitudinal data empowers civil society to hold institutions accountable and advocate for equitable reforms, ensuring that economic policies actively work to reduce the divide between the wealthiest and most marginalized segments of society.
