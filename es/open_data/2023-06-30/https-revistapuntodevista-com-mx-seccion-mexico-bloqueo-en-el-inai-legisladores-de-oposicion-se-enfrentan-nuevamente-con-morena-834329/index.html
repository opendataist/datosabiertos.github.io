The article highlights a critical political conflict in Mexico where the ruling coalition blocks the appointment of INAI commissioners, paralyzing the transparency institute for nearly three months. This obstruction defies a judicial order to convene an extraordinary legislative session, raising serious concerns about institutional decay and the erosion of checks and balances in the democratic framework. The refusal to proceed with the appointments is framed by opposition lawmakers as deliberate disobedience to the judiciary, characterized as an opaque and deceptive maneuver to avoid accountability. By denying the quorum and the vote, the government majority prioritizes partisan control over constitutional mandates, effectively silencing the mechanisms designed to guarantee citizens' right to access public information. This situation is highly relevant to open data because the INAI is the fundamental body responsible for enforcing transparency and protecting personal data. Without a functional board, the oversight of government information release collapses, leaving citizens without effective recourse to demand the publication of public records or to contest data handling violations, thereby weakening the entire open data ecosystem.
