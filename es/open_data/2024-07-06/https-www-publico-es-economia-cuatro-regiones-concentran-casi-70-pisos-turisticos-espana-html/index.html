Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article highlights the critical role of standardized, open statistical data in understanding Spain’s tourism housing market. By consolidating scattered regional records into a unified national registry, authorities can achieve a more accurate overview of the nearly 350,000 tourist properties. This centralization helps resolve discrepancies between autonomous communities and local figures, demonstrating how open access to reliable, comparable datasets is essential for effective governance and transparent monitoring of the real estate sector. Concentrations of these properties heavily influence coastal regions and major cities, significantly altering local housing dynamics. The data reveals that tourist rentals often constitute a substantial portion of the total housing stock in popular municipalities, impacting availability and affordability for residents. Understanding these geographic distributions through open data allows policymakers and citizens to visualize the scale of tourism’s impact on specific communities, fostering a clearer dialogue about sustainable urban development and housing rights. Recognizing the regulatory gaps exposed by unlicensed rentals, the government plans to enforce stricter authorization requirements, including community consent for new tourist units. This legislative move underscores the necessity of integrating statistical evidence with policy-making to address housing shortages. Ultimately, the availability of clear, publicly accessible information on tourist housing stock empowers democratic decision-making, ensuring that future regulations are grounded in reality rather than speculation, thereby protecting both residents and the integrity of the rental market.
