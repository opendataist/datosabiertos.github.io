La inflación en Bolivia rozó el 10% en 2024: es la más alta de los últimos 16 años

The article highlights that Bolivia ended 2024 with its highest inflation in sixteen years, driven by a combination of domestic demand, imported inflation, and severe external shocks like drought and political blockades. A critical underlying cause was a chronic shortage of US dollars and fuel, which crippled the economy and led to a parallel exchange market and widespread scarcity of basic goods. This crisis resulted in a significant loss of purchasing power for families, with prices for essential items continuing to rise despite government interventions such as export bans and price controls. This context is highly relevant to open data because transparent, timely, and granular statistical information is essential for diagnosing complex economic phenomena like the one described. Accurate data on inflation components, currency exchange rates, and supply chain disruptions allows citizens, researchers, and policymakers to verify official narratives and understand the true impact of policies. Without accessible open data, it is difficult to hold institutions accountable for projections that drastically miss reality or to analyze the root causes of hyperinflation beyond surface-level explanations. The main conclusion is that the economic turmoil cannot be understood solely through aggregate inflation rates; it requires deep, open access to detailed datasets covering specific factors like fuel subsidies, import costs, and the impact of social unrest. Providing such data empowers independent verification of government claims and fosters informed public debate. Ultimately, open data serves as a vital tool for transparency, enabling a clearer understanding of how structural issues and political decisions directly affect the livelihoods of citizens during times of crisis.
