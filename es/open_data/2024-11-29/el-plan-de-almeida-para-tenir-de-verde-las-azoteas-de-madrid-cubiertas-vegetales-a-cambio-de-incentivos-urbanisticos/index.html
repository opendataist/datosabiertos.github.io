El plan de Almeida para teñir de verde las azoteas de Madrid: cubiertas vegetales a cambio de incentivos urbanísticos

Madrid’s municipal register reveals a stark reality: only a negligible fraction of the city’s over one hundred thousand buildings feature green roofs. The vast majority remain devoid of vegetation, contributing to urban heat islands and higher carbon levels. This data underscores the urgent need for structural environmental interventions to improve air quality and thermal comfort in the capital. To address this, the City Council has proposed a special plan to transform rooftops into naturalized zones, aiming for approval in early 2025. The strategy relies heavily on incentivizing private property owners through urbanistic benefits, such as direct licensing for rooftop attic constructions. These incentives allow for new functional spaces like storage or communal areas, effectively encouraging the integration of green infrastructure without exceeding maximum buildable limits. This initiative is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how publicly accessible municipal registries can expose critical urban deficits. By making building characteristics visible, open data enables policymakers to identify specific areas needing intervention. Furthermore, it facilitates transparent public debate and planning, ensuring that environmental solutions are grounded in accurate, verified information rather than assumptions.
