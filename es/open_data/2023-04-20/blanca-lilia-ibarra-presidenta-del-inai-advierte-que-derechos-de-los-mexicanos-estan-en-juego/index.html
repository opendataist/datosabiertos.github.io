Blanca Lilia Ibarra, presidenta del INAI, advierte que derechos de los mexicanos “están en juego”

The article highlights a critical political crisis in Mexico where the President’s refusal to appoint commissioners has left the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) without a legal quorum. This impasse effectively paralyzes the institution, preventing it from resolving citizen demands or protecting personal data. The core issue is not merely administrative but represents a fundamental threat to two essential human rights: the right to know and the right to data privacy. The practical implications of this shutdown are severe, as the INAI can no longer compel public agencies to disclose information or adjudicate violations of data protection laws. Thousands of pending appeals remain unresolved, undermining the transparency mechanisms that have historically supported investigative journalism and exposed significant corruption cases. Without the ability to function as a guarantor, the system risks becoming ineffective, leaving citizens without recourse against government opacity and privacy breaches. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how political will directly impacts the viability of data infrastructure. When the bodies responsible for enforcing transparency standards are dismantled or stalled, the entire ecosystem of open government data suffers. The reliance on judicial intervention and legislative consensus underscores the fragility of open data frameworks when they lack institutional independence and consistent enforcement, making the preservation of such institutions vital for accountability and public trust.
