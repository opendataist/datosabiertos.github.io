Consulta el número de residentes que viven en Guadalajara y han nacido en tu tierra. EL 57% LO HAN HECHO FUERA

The article reveals that Guadalajara is a highly dynamic province, with more than half of its residents born outside the region, driven primarily by migration from neighboring Madrid and the Corredor del Henares. This demographic shift is not merely a statistical update but a reflection of significant urban and social transformation, indicating that the area has become a major hub for internal mobility within Spain. Understanding these migration patterns is crucial for open data initiatives, as it highlights the need for granular, localized data to manage regional growth and infrastructure planning effectively. Furthermore, the province exhibits a diverse international population, with Romanians, Moroccans, and Colombians forming the largest foreign communities. The data underscores a trend of younger, working-age immigrants integrating into local municipalities, particularly in larger towns. This diversity presents an opportunity for open data advocates to demonstrate how disaggregated information can support social cohesion policies and better serve multicultural communities by revealing specific demographic needs that aggregate national statistics might obscure. Ultimately, the relevance of this article to open data lies in the complexity of the sources and the necessity of transparency in demographic reporting. The mention of data gaps due to anonymization in smaller municipalities illustrates the challenges of balancing privacy with public access to detailed information. It emphasizes the importance of robust, interoperable datasets that can accurately capture migration flows and diversity, enabling researchers and policymakers to make informed decisions based on reliable, accessible evidence rather than estimated or aggregated figures.
