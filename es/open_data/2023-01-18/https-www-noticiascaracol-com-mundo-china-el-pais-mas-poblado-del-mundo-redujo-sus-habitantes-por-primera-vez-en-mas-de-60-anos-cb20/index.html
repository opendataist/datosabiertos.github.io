Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

China experiences a historic demographic shift with its first population decline in decades, signaling a critical turning point driven by an aging society and plummeting birth rates. This reversal poses severe long-term risks to economic stability and public finances, forcing authorities to confront the limitations of current incentive policies. The widespread adoption of subsidies and extended maternity leaves has failed to significantly boost fertility, as these measures remain insufficient against the structural barriers preventing young families from expanding. The core issue lies in the impossible combination of demanding labor markets and high societal expectations for child-rearing. Young parents face a precarious dilemma where career advancement is jeopardized by the need for childcare, while the costs of private education and nurseries consume a disproportionate share of household income. Without robust, accessible infrastructure and genuine support from partners or extended families, the financial and emotional burden effectively discourages many from having additional children, highlighting a deep disconnect between policy intent and lived reality. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it underscores the urgent need for transparent, granular datasets to diagnose and address complex socioeconomic challenges. Open data can reveal hidden disparities in care access, employment discrimination against parents, and the true economic impact of demographic changes. By making such information accessible, policymakers and researchers can move beyond superficial metrics to design effective, evidence-based interventions that truly support family sustainability and mitigate the risks associated with a shrinking workforce.
