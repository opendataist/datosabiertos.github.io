Bilbao presenta su memoria "antiabusos": "Cualquiera, por leve que parezca, es insoportable e intolerable"

The Diocese of Bilbao’s Commission for the Protection of Minors has made its activity data public, emphasizing that no abuse is acceptable and prioritizing victim autonomy in handling cases. This transparency demonstrates a commitment to moving away from secrecy, ensuring that victims’ specific needs for privacy, recognition, or justice guide the response. By openly sharing these metrics, the institution aligns with open data principles, fostering trust through accountability and clear communication about how it addresses historical and ongoing safeguarding failures within the church structure. The commission’s operational framework balances support for victims with the presumption of innocence for the accused, while actively collaborating with judicial authorities when necessary. It focuses on prevention through extensive training programs and independent investigations into high-profile incidents. This holistic approach highlights the importance of multidisciplinary collaboration and rigorous procedural standards. By documenting and analyzing these cases, the diocese contributes valuable qualitative data to the broader conversation on institutional accountability and child protection mechanisms within religious organizations. This report is relevant to open data because it illustrates how public institutions can use data disclosure as a tool for social reform and trust-building. By releasing detailed statistics on case types, outcomes, and preventive measures, the diocese provides concrete evidence of its engagement with safeguarding issues. This transparency allows external stakeholders to monitor progress, hold leadership accountable, and identify systemic trends, thereby transforming internal administrative records into a resource for public oversight and continuous improvement in human rights protections.
