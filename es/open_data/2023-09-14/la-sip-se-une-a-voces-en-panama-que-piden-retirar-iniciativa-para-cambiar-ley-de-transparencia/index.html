La SIP se une a voces en Panamá que piden retirar iniciativa para cambiar Ley de Transparencia

The Inter-American Press Society opposes Panama’s proposed amendment to its Transparency Law, arguing it will hinder public access to information and protect corruption rather than combat it. This stance highlights the critical role of robust legal frameworks in safeguarding the right to information, a cornerstone of open data ecosystems that enable journalists and citizens to hold governments accountable. Despite government recognition of the need for civil society input, critics emphasize that the current law has been exemplary in the region. Replacing a functioning mechanism with one that lacks broad consultation risks weakening anticorruption efforts. The debate underscores how legislative changes can directly impact the availability and usability of public data, potentially creating barriers for researchers and activists relying on transparent records. This conflict is relevant to open data because it illustrates how political will and legal structures determine whether public information remains a shared resource or becomes restricted. Protecting existing transparency laws ensures that data stays accessible, fostering accountability and informed civic participation, which are essential for healthy democratic societies and effective data-driven governance.
