Percepción de inseguridad en México bajó a 64.2% al cierre de 2022: INEGI

The article highlights a slight but statistically significant decrease in the perception of insecurity among Mexicans, indicating that a substantial majority still feels unsafe in their cities. Despite a concurrent decline in homicide rates, the persistence of high fear levels reveals a complex reality where objective safety metrics do not fully align with citizens' subjective experiences. This disconnect underscores that violence statistics alone are insufficient to capture the full societal impact of crime and public safety conditions. Furthermore, the data exposes deep regional and gender disparities, with women and residents of specific high-crime municipalities reporting significantly higher levels of fear. The sharp contrast between wealthy areas and zones affected by organized crime illustrates how local contexts drastically shape public sentiment. These variations emphasize that safety is not uniform across the country, requiring nuanced, localized analysis rather than broad national generalizations. This report is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the value of granular, timely statistics in monitoring social trends and policy effectiveness. By making detailed demographic and geographic data publicly available, institutions empower researchers and citizens to identify inequalities and hold authorities accountable. Open data facilitates a deeper understanding of the gap between statistical outcomes and lived experiences, fostering more informed public debate and targeted interventions for community safety.
