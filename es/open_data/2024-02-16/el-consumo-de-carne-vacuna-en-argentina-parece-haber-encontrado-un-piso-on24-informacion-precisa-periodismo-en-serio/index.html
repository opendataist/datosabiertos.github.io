El consumo de carne vacuna en Argentina parece haber encontrado un piso | ON24 | Información Precisa. Periodismo en serio

The article highlights a counterintuitive economic phenomenon in Argentina, where beef consumption remained resilient despite a severe decline in real wages and significant inflation. While consumers are typically expected to reduce spending on expensive goods when purchasing power drops, beef demand showed unusual inelasticity. This suggests that essential cultural or dietary habits may override strict economic constraints, allowing consumption levels to stabilize even when prices surge faster than salaries. A closer analysis reveals that while total meat consumption increased slightly, this growth was driven by beef as its real price has effectively decreased compared to three years ago, despite high nominal increases. This stabilization is attributed to temporary market factors, such as drought-related supply fluctuations and weakened demand. However, this equilibrium is fragile; as supply normalizes, prices are expected to rise, potentially testing the true limits of consumer resilience and exposing the sustainability of current consumption patterns. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how analyzing public statistics from institutions like INDEC can uncover hidden social dynamics that contradict standard economic theories. By making wage, price, and consumption data accessible, researchers can identify structural shifts in public behavior. It underscores the importance of using open data to monitor not just aggregate trends, but the nuanced interplay between macroeconomic indicators and daily household decisions, providing a more accurate picture of societal well-being.
