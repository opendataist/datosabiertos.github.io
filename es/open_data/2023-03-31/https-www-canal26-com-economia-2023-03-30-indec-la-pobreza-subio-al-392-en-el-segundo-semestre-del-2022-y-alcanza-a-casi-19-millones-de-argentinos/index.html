Argentina’s rising poverty and indigence rates reveal a severe socio-economic crisis, driven by persistent inflation and drought. This deterioration signals that vulnerable populations are increasingly unable to secure basic subsistence, highlighting systemic failures in economic stability. The shrinking middle class and surging poverty, particularly among children, demonstrate a deepening social fracture. These trends indicate a long-term regression in living standards since 2015, suggesting that current policies are insufficient to protect citizens from economic shocks. This data is crucial for open data advocates as it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible statistics for accountability. Reliable public data enables civil society to monitor inequality, challenge official narratives, and demand evidence-based policy reforms to address such profound structural challenges.
