Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Zoom faced significant backlash after revealing its updated policies allowed the use of customer data to train artificial intelligence models. The controversy arose because activating these features required users to agree to broad data licensing terms, effectively granting the company perpetual rights to utilize their content. This lack of clarity triggered widespread criticism on social media, with experts and academics condemning the move as a potential abuse of user trust and privacy. In response to the viral outrage, Zoom was forced to revise its terms of use and publicly assure users that their audio, video, or chat content would not be used for AI training without explicit consent. The company emphasized that control over these AI tools remains with account administrators and that any data sharing is part of a transparent consent process. Crucially, they confirmed that even if users agree to share data, it will not be used to train third-party models, addressing core concerns about exploitation. This incident is highly relevant to the open data community as it highlights the critical tension between proprietary AI development and user privacy rights. It demonstrates how opaque data practices can quickly erode public trust in technology platforms. For open data advocates, this serves as a powerful reminder that transparency and genuine user agency are essential. If major platforms continue to obscure how personal information fuels their algorithms, the movement toward ethical, open, and accountable data ecosystems faces significant challenges in establishing necessary standards for consent and ownership.
