Pagan $1,400 millones a proveedores sin registro en el padrón

This report exposes a severe transparency failure in municipal procurement, revealing that the majority of funds were paid to suppliers excluded from the official roster. This implies systemic illegality, where mass contracting bypasses legal requirements for registered vendors, undermining public accountability and financial integrity. The significance for open data lies in exposing how published datasets can mask corruption through selective reporting. By highlighting discrepancies between paid amounts and registered providers, it demonstrates the critical need for data verification to detect irregularities. Ultimately, the article argues that open data is not just about publication but about enabling scrutiny. It urges officials to verify their own numbers, showing that accessible, accurate information is essential for identifying fraud and restoring trust in government financial operations.
