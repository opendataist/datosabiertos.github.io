The legislative session in San Luis Potosí approved key reforms aimed at enhancing legal protections and administrative integrity. Most notably, a new provision allows victims of domestic violence to directly sue aggressors for both material and moral damages, shifting from previous limitations to provide financial recourse for those escaping abusive situations. This judicial avenue acknowledges the emotional and economic burdens victims face, ensuring they have concrete tools for restitution and recovery. Concurrently, the state strengthened transparency governance by mandating specific training for personnel in Transparency Units and restructuring the Council of Transparency to include substitute members, preventing operational gaps. These measures ensure that officials handling public information and data are properly qualified and that oversight bodies remain fully functional. The reforms also updated civil registry procedures to allow for easier administrative corrections of clerical errors, providing citizens with greater legal certainty regarding their personal records. This article is relevant to open_data because it explicitly links transparency obligations with professional capacity building and institutional stability. By requiring certified training for those managing information and data protection, the reforms underscore that effective open data practices depend on skilled personnel and robust organizational structures. Furthermore, simplifying civil registry corrections demonstrates how accessible, well-maintained public data systems directly impact individual rights and administrative efficiency, reinforcing the importance of accurate and user-friendly public records in a democratic framework.
