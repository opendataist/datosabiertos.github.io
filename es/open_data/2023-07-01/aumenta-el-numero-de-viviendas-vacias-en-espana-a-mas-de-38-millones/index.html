Aumenta el número de viviendas vacías en España a más de 3,8 millones

The Spanish National Statistics Institute has released provisional data revealing that over three and a half million homes remain vacant, highlighting a significant inefficiency in housing stock distribution. This analysis distinguishes between empty residences, sporadic-use properties, and primary homes by leveraging granular data on electrical consumption. The methodology demonstrates how administrative data can be repurposed to offer a more precise classification of housing status, providing insights that traditional counts alone might miss. The findings reveal a stark geographic disparity, with vacant homes concentrated in smaller municipalities rather than major urban centers. Conversely, primary residences dominate metropolitan areas, while sporadic-use properties are prevalent in coastal and tourist destinations. This spatial distribution underscores the complex interplay between demographic shifts, tourism, and housing availability, offering a nuanced picture of where housing needs are most acute or where resources are underutilized. Simultaneously, the data indicates a structural shift in tenure, with homeownership declining and rental housing gaining ground for the first time in decades. This trend reflects broader socioeconomic changes and increasing reliance on the rental market. This article is highly relevant to open_data initiatives because it exemplifies the power of integrating diverse datasets, such as utility consumption records, with traditional censuses. By making these analytical methods and results transparent, it showcases how open data can drive evidence-based policy decisions regarding urban planning, social housing, and resource allocation.
