The article highlights the constitutional crisis paralyzing Mexico’s transparency institute due to the Senate’s failure to appoint missing commissioners. The main conclusion is that legal mechanisms, including potential economic sanctions for non-compliance, are being considered to force the legislative body to resolve the vacancies. This situation underscores the fragility of institutional oversight when political branches ignore judicial orders, threatening the agency's ability to function. Furthermore, the text points to a concerning precedent regarding gender-based political violence, where a sanction was deemed excessive compared to unpunished severe cases against other women. This discrepancy raises questions about the impartiality and effectiveness of electoral justice systems in addressing discrimination, suggesting that current regulatory frameworks may fail to protect vulnerable figures equally. This is relevant to open data because a paralyzed transparency institute cannot effectively guarantee public access to government information. When the body responsible for enforcing data rights lacks a quorum, citizens lose a critical channel for accountability. Moreover, unequal treatment in legal sanctions regarding political violence reflects broader governance issues that impact the integrity and trustworthiness of the data ecosystem and institutional fairness.
