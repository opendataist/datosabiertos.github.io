Desde agosto el Ejecutivo no actualiza portal de transparencia

Honduras faces a significant transparency crisis, as evidenced by extensive delays in updating the official portals of the Executive, Legislative, and other state institutions. Rather than complying with legal mandates to publish monthly data on procurement, spending, and contracts, many government bodies have left critical information outdated for months. This systemic failure prevents citizens from verifying how public resources are managed, thereby undermining the fundamental principle of government accountability and the legal framework designed to combat corruption through open access to information. The implications for open data in Honduras are profound, as the lack of timely, machine-readable, and current data restricts effective civic oversight. Without accurate digital records, journalists, researchers, and the general public cannot analyze government efficiency or detect irregularities in real-time. This digital gap creates an environment where opaque decision-making thrives, as the absence of verifiable evidence makes it nearly impossible to hold officials accountable for their financial actions or to evaluate the performance of public services delivered to the population. This situation is highly relevant to the open data movement because it highlights the critical difference between merely possessing data and making it accessible and reliable. True open data requires not only publication but also regular maintenance and user-friendly formats to ensure usability. The article demonstrates that without strict enforcement and institutional commitment to maintaining these digital infrastructure standards, open data initiatives fail to deliver their promised societal benefits, leaving transparency as a legal formality rather than a practical tool for democratic engagement.
