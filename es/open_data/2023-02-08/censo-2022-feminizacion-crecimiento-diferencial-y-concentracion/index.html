Censo 2022: feminización, crecimiento diferencial y concentración

The preliminary analysis of Argentina's latest census data reveals a population nearing 46 million, marked by a consistent annual growth rate and a significant process of feminization where women outnumber men. This demographic shift, linked to increased female life expectancy, highlights evolving social structures and introduces new categories for gender identity, reflecting a more inclusive understanding of the population. The data underscores that despite pandemic-related delays, these statistics remain a vital tool for quantifying and qualifying the nation's current reality and future projections. Geographically, growth is highly unequal, driven by regional disparities and specific economic activities. While traditional hubs show modest increases, provinces in Patagonia and areas bordering metropolitan centers or specialized in tourism and mining have experienced substantial expansion. This differential growth challenges historical patterns of internal migration and suggests that demographic dynamics are increasingly influenced by local economic opportunities and the appeal of specific territories, such as tourist destinations or resource-rich zones. However, the fundamental territorial concentration of the population remains unchanged. A tiny fraction of the country’s surface area continues to house nearly a third of the total population, maintaining the dominance of the Buenos Aires metropolitan area alongside vast sparsely populated regions. This persistence of unequal distribution emphasizes the urgent need for open data to analyze spatial planning and policy effectiveness. For the open data community, these findings illustrate the critical importance of accessible, granular demographic information in diagnosing regional inequalities and guiding sustainable development strategies in Argentina.
