Las compraventas de vivienda se desploman un 17,8% en diciembre y los precios se frenan al 0,5%

The Spanish real estate market experienced a significant cooling effect at the end of the year, characterized by a sharp deceleration in both transaction volumes and price growth. After reaching fifteen-year highs in total annual activity, the sector faced increasing pressure from rising interest rates, causing transaction declines across most regions and slowing price increases to near-zero levels by the final months. This trend indicates a clear normalization after the pandemic-driven boom, shifting from rapid expansion to a more balanced, albeit sluggish, equilibrium. Despite the late-year slowdown, the full-year data reveals a robust recovery compared to previous years, with sales volumes and mortgage amounts reaching their highest levels in 15 years. While prices rose nationally, the disparity between regions widened, with major urban centers and tourist hubs like Madrid and the Canary Islands seeing substantially higher value increases compared to inland areas. This divergence highlights how economic factors and local demand are reshaping property values unevenly across the country, influencing investment strategies and affordability for different demographic groups. This analysis is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates the necessity of granular, transparent, and accessible housing statistics to understand complex market dynamics. By providing detailed breakdowns by region and property type, such datasets empower researchers, policymakers, and citizens to move beyond aggregate averages. Accessible open data allows for better evaluation of regional inequalities, the impact of monetary policy on specific demographics, and the overall health of the housing sector, fostering informed public debate and evidence-based decision-making.
