El paro de Reino Unido escala al 4,2% y los salarios marcan récord desde 2001

UK’s labor market shows slight deterioration, with unemployment rising and employment falling due to reductions in full-time and self-employed workers. Despite these declines, wages have seen their highest annual growth in decades, driven by strong nominal increases that are only partially offset by inflation. Real wage growth remains minimal, indicating that workers’ purchasing power has barely improved despite record nominal salary hikes. This discrepancy highlights the ongoing pressure of inflation on household finances, suggesting that economic benefits are not yet fully translating into tangible living standards improvements. This data is relevant to open data initiatives as it underscores the importance of transparent, timely economic statistics. Access to such granular labor metrics allows for better policy analysis and public scrutiny, ensuring that decisions regarding wage policies and employment strategies are grounded in accurate, verifiable facts rather than obscured trends.
