The article highlights a significant erosion of traditional electoral support among women for the PSOE, driven by dissatisfaction with policies led by the Ministry of Equality under Irene Montero. Key concerns include the handling of the Trans law and a perceived alignment with Podemos that alienates feminist socialists. This discontent suggests a potential mobilization gap in upcoming elections, as activists and non-activist voters alike feel marginalized by gender policies they view as contradictory to equality principles. Relevant to open data, this political tension underscores the critical need for gender-disaggregated election statistics. The absence of public data specifically segmented by sex during municipal elections hinders the accurate assessment of these demographic shifts and the underlying causes of voter disenchantment. Without such granular transparency, political parties and analysts cannot effectively diagnose the specific drivers of electoral decay or measure the true impact of controversial legislation on female voting behavior, leaving the sector reliant on fragmented indicators like general CIS surveys. Ultimately, the PSOE faces a strategic challenge in re-engaging female voters beyond mere calls for tactical voting against the right-wing. The narrative suggests that without substantive policy shifts and transparent accountability regarding gender equality initiatives, the party risks further alienating its core base. This situation illustrates how opaque data environments can obscure the nuanced relationship between legislative decisions and specific voter demographics, making it difficult to formulate effective, evidence-based political strategies to address growing ideological divides.
