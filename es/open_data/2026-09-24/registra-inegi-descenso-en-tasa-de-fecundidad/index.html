Registra INEGI descenso en tasa de fecundidad

The INEGI’s intercensal survey reveals that Mexico’s fertility rate has dropped to 1.2 births per woman, significantly below the replacement level of 2.1. This decline, driven by factors like increased female education and labor participation, signals a long-term structural shift toward population aging and challenges the stability of traditional demographic models. This trend mirrors a global movement where many nations now face sub-replacement fertility, with regional disparities evident across Mexican states. The inverse correlation between women’s schooling and birth rates highlights how social progress directly impacts demographic dynamics, shifting the focus from quantity to quality of life and human capital development. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, high-quality statistical repositories enable policymakers to detect and respond to critical societal changes. By making such granular data publicly available, institutions like INEGI empower researchers and citizens to analyze complex trends, ensuring that public services and infrastructure planning align with the country’s evolving demographic reality rather than past assumptions.
