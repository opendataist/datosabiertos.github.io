Internet, la herramienta de doble filo que gana más terreno en la capital

The article highlights a critical tension between technology’s potential to expand knowledge versus its capacity to hinder it. It reveals that while internet access is widespread, the majority of users prioritize entertainment over educational applications. This imbalance suggests that digital connectivity alone does not guarantee learning; instead, it often displaces traditional pedagogical methods, potentially weakening students' logical thinking and critical analysis skills when technology replaces rather than supports formal education. Experts warn that without careful parental supervision and proper guidance, digital platforms can act as socializing agents that compete with family and school values. The ease of accessing content means minors are exposed to both constructive resources and manipulative influences. Consequently, the role of families is pivotal in regulating content consumption during adolescence to prevent the adoption of skewed life concepts and ethical violations, emphasizing that access must be accompanied by active oversight. This case is relevant to open_data because it demonstrates the necessity of contextualizing statistics beyond raw access numbers. Data indicating high connectivity masks deeper issues regarding usage patterns and digital literacy. Understanding these disparities allows policymakers and educators to design interventions that prioritize meaningful digital inclusion over mere connectivity, ensuring that open data insights drive strategies that foster critical thinking and equitable educational opportunities rather than simply increasing screen time.
