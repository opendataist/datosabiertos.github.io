INAI analiza una investigación de oficio por filtración de datos personales de 300 periodistas: ‘Es importante la denuncia’ | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI) is actively investigating a data breach involving journalists covering the Mexican presidency. The authority has granted the government seventy-two hours to file an official report; failure to comply will trigger an automatic inquiry lasting up to fifty days. This rigorous procedural timeline underscores the institution’s commitment to enforcing legal standards regarding personal information security. Victims and the administration are urged to submit formal complaints through various accessible channels, including virtual centers operating continuously. The INAI emphasizes that personal data owners can initiate proceedings directly, ensuring their specific grievances are documented. By facilitating direct access to justice, the agency empowers individuals to seek redress and ensures that all relevant facts are captured for a comprehensive legal review. This case is critically relevant to open_data as it highlights the tension between government transparency and the mandatory protection of personal privacy. It demonstrates how robust regulatory frameworks operate when data breaches occur, serving as a practical example of how oversight bodies balance the public’s right to know with the legal obligation to safeguard individuals’ sensitive information against misuse.
