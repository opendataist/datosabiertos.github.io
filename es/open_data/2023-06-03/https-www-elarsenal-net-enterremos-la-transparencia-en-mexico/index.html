The article highlights the tension between President Andrés Manuel López Obrador’s efforts to dismantle institutions like INAI and the essential role these bodies play in ensuring government accountability. By weakening independent oversight mechanisms, the administration risks obscuring the misuse of public funds. This dynamic underscores the critical importance of protecting transparency authorities that empower citizens and journalists to scrutinize state expenditures, particularly when political narratives prioritize institutional weakening over ethical governance. Specific instances of alleged fiscal irresponsibility are detailed, such as excessive spending on political events under governor Indira Vizcaíno Silva. The disclosed contracts reveal inflated costs for logistics and security, while allegations suggest that primary payments to performers may occur in unrecorded cash transactions to evade audit trails. These practices illustrate how opacity facilitates potential corruption, diverting resources from essential public services like healthcare infrastructure toward politically motivated expenditures. This narrative is deeply relevant to open data because it demonstrates how the suppression of accessible information directly enables financial mismanagement and erodes public trust. When governments avoid publishing detailed records of contracts and actual payments, citizens lose the ability to verify accountability. Sustaining robust open data frameworks is therefore vital to counteract attempts at secrecy, ensuring that fiscal decisions remain transparent and that public resources are used responsibly rather than for political gain.
