Datos personales, en riesgo por reforma: Adrián Alcalá Méndez, comisionado del INAI

The potential extinction of the INAI poses a severe threat to the privacy and security of Mexican citizens, creating a legal vacuum that could leave personal data vulnerable to organized crime. This reform removes the primary institutional guarantee for data protection rights without establishing an adequate successor, thereby exposing sensitive information and undermining fundamental human rights. The absence of a clear authority to manage these critical protections represents a regression in the digital rights framework, leaving citizens in a state of total insecurity regarding their personal information. The proposed transition plan lacks clarity and operational coherence, raising significant concerns about administrative continuity and financial obligations. Key aspects such as the management of the National Transparency Platform, the status of nearly 800 employees, and the handling of long-term financial leases remain ambiguous. Furthermore, assigning transparency oversight to a federal anti-corruption agency ignores the necessity of independent oversight over other branches of government, such as the judiciary, potentially compromising the impartiality and effectiveness of information access mechanisms across all levels of government. The replacement of an independent, user-centric body with administrative tribunals would drastically hinder citizens' ability to claim their rights due to increased costs, slower procedures, and reduced accessibility. Unlike the current model, which offers free, rapid, and online resolutions, alternative judicial processes are lengthy and require legal representation, effectively limiting access to justice for vulnerable populations. This shift not only diminishes the efficiency of transparency mechanisms but also erodes the autonomy necessary to prevent conflicts of interest when citizens request information from the state. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical importance of independent institutions in safeguarding data privacy and ensuring equitable access to public information. It demonstrates how legislative changes can directly impact the infrastructure of transparency, potentially weakening the legal and technical frameworks that protect individual rights and maintain trust in open government initiatives.
