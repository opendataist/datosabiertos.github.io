Liberan a ocho militares acusados de sabotear las presidenciales de 2018 - La Voz

The release of eight Venezuelan military personnel highlights the persistent politicization of justice, where soldiers are detained for alleged coup attempts but labeled political prisoners by human rights observers. This case underscores the severe lack of transparency in legal proceedings, as the government remains silent while families report health crises and hunger strikes among the detainees. This situation is critical for open data because it reveals how state secrecy and opaque judicial processes hinder independent verification of human rights abuses. Reliable, accessible data on prison populations and legal charges is essential for civil society to monitor political repression and hold authorities accountable for arbitrary detentions and due process violations. Without transparent, standardized datasets regarding judicial cases and inmate statuses, verifying claims of political persecution remains difficult. Open data initiatives are vital to bridge this information gap, enabling researchers and journalists to track patterns of abuse, validate witness testimonies, and ensure that the true scale of political imprisonment is accurately documented and publicly known.
