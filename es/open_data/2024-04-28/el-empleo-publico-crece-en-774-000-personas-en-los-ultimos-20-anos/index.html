El empleo público crece en 774.000 personas en los últimos 20 años

The article highlights a persistent expansion of the public sector in Spain, noting that employment in administrations has significantly increased since 2004. While private sector growth dominates in most regions, several autonomous communities exhibit a stronger reliance on government jobs, with some seeing more public than private job creation. This shift indicates a structural imbalance in the labor market, where the state remains the primary engine of employment in specific territories rather than the private enterprise sector. Relevance to open data is profound, as this analysis depends entirely on the availability and transparency of statistical sources like the Active Population Survey. Access to granular, high-quality demographic and economic datasets allows for the verification of such regional disparities. Without open and standardized government data, it would be impossible to accurately measure the weight of the public sector or identify these divergent employment trends across different jurisdictions. Furthermore, the ability to compare historical data against current figures demonstrates the importance of longitudinal open data availability. Such transparency enables policymakers and citizens to assess the efficiency of public spending and the effectiveness of labor policies. Ultimately, the article underscores that robust open data infrastructure is essential for holding governments accountable and ensuring that decisions regarding public employment are based on clear, accessible, and verifiable evidence rather than opaque reporting.
