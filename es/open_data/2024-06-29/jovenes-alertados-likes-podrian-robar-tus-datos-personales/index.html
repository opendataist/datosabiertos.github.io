Jóvenes Alertados: Likes Podrían Robar tus Datos Personales

The article underscores that digital interaction is not free, as users exchange personal data for connectivity. This implicit trade-off allows algorithms and artificial intelligence to infer sensitive details about individuals, transforming simple actions like giving "likes" into a comprehensive digital profile. The core conclusion is that users must recognize they are the currency of the online world, necessitating a shift from passive participation to active awareness of what information is being surrendered. This perspective highlights a critical gap in public understanding regarding data sovereignty. By revealing how innocuous online behaviors can expose deep personal attributes, the content challenges the assumption that social media usage is harmless. The emphasis is on educating citizens, particularly youth, to distinguish between safe self-expression and actions that compromise their privacy, thereby fostering a more cautious and informed digital citizenship. This narrative is highly relevant to open_data initiatives because it contextualizes the necessity of robust data protection frameworks alongside transparency. While open data promotes access to information, this article illustrates the parallel need to safeguard personal data from misuse. It supports the argument that effective open government requires balancing the right to information with the fundamental right to privacy, ensuring that the benefits of transparency do not erode individual security in the digital age.
