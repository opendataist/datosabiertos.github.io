La inflación se modera al 2,8% en febrero por la bajada del precio de la electricidad

Spanish inflation has moderated to its lowest level since late 2023, driven primarily by a significant drop in electricity prices and stable costs for food and beverages. This deceleration in the consumer price index allows households to regain purchasing power while enabling companies to restore competitiveness, supporting a policy approach that balances economic stabilization with social protection for vulnerable families. The easing of underlying price pressures presents a positive signal for the European Central Bank, indicating that core inflation continues to soften. Although industrial price expectations have fallen, persistent high inflation in the services sector and the removal of energy subsidies pose upward risks. Consequently, analysts project that inflation will likely remain around three percent this year, preventing a quick return to the two percent target due to sticky service costs and broader supply-demand dynamics. This data is relevant to open_data initiatives as it relies on transparent, standardized statistical reporting from national institutes like the INE, ensuring public access to reliable economic indicators. High-quality open datasets enable independent researchers, journalists, and citizens to verify official narratives, analyze sector-specific trends, and assess the real-world impact of fiscal and monetary policies. By making raw inflation data freely available, society can foster informed debate on cost-of-living issues and hold institutions accountable for managing economic stability effectively.
