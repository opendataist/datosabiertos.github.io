Seguridad Social cifra en 560.000 hogares y 1,7 millones de personas los beneficiarios del IMV

The Spanish Ministry of Social Security has shifted from reporting accumulated historical data to publishing the current number of Ingreso Mínimo Vital (IMV) recipients. This transparency allows for a more accurate assessment of the aid’s real-time reach, revealing that approximately 557,000 families are currently supported. By distinguishing between current beneficiaries and past claimants, the government aims to provide a clearer "radiography" of who is actually receiving assistance now, rather than just tracking administrative approvals over time. Despite the high number of recipients, the data highlights a significant gap in coverage, with estimates suggesting that a majority of eligible households do not apply. This underutilization raises critical questions about the effectiveness of social safety nets and the barriers preventing vulnerable populations from accessing rights. The complexity of the application process and the specific profile of potential beneficiaries present implementation challenges, indicating that merely making funds available is insufficient without ensuring they are accessible to those most in need. This disclosure is vital for open data as it underscores the importance of granular, real-time metrics over aggregated figures in public policy evaluation. High-quality, detailed data enables researchers, journalists, and civil society to analyze equity, identify systemic gaps, and hold institutions accountable for the actual impact of social programs. Without this level of transparency, the true efficacy of poverty alleviation strategies remains obscured, limiting informed debate and necessary reforms in social welfare.
