Cartografía establece que número de viviendas creció 50% en relación a 2012

The recent completion of Bolivia’s Statistical Cartographic Update reveals a dramatic expansion in the nation’s housing stock, showing a growth exceeding 50% compared to 2012. This surge indicates significant demographic shifts and urbanization patterns that were not captured in previous census cycles. By identifying over a million new units across major departments, the data suggests that current population estimates may need substantial revision to reflect the true scale of habitation and resource demand. The findings highlight critical variations in occupancy rates, with significant portions of housing in departments like La Paz and Cochabamba remaining unoccupied or used seasonally. These insights into housing utilization are vital for understanding the effectiveness of current urban planning and social policies. The discrepancy between the sheer volume of structures and their actual occupancy offers a clearer picture of living conditions,空置 housing availability, and the potential for real estate speculation versus genuine residential need. This update is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it demonstrates the necessity of maintaining accurate, updated geospatial and statistical registries for effective governance. The process underscores how transparent, granular data collection supports better allocation of public resources and infrastructure development. Furthermore, it emphasizes the value of integrating cartographic updates with demographic censuses, providing a robust foundation for future research, policy making, and civic engagement based on precise, accessible information.
