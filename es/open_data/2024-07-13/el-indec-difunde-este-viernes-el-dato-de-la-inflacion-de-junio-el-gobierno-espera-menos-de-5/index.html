El INDEC difunde este viernes el dato de la inflación de junio: el Gobierno espera menos de 5%

Argentina’s June inflation marks a reversal of the recent downward trend, driven primarily by the pass-through of regulated tariff hikes rather than broad monetary pressure. This deceleration halt suggests that while initial stabilization efforts have had some effect, the underlying economic structure remains volatile. For the open data community, this highlights the critical need for transparent, high-frequency indicators to distinguish between temporary administrative shocks and fundamental inflationary pressures, ensuring policy analysis is based on accurate, real-time data rather than delayed official reports. Concurrently, political narratives emphasize macroeconomic stability and fiscal discipline, yet private metrics and household cost-of-living surveys reveal a starkly different reality. The divergence between government projections and independent estimates underscores the importance of data pluralism. Open data initiatives are vital here, as they empower researchers and citizens to cross-validate official statistics, exposing gaps in economic storytelling and preventing the monopolization of truth by a single institutional source. Most critically, the rapid deterioration in purchasing power has doubled the number of people unable to afford basic food baskets in just seven months. This human impact demonstrates that aggregate macroeconomic indicators often mask severe micro-level suffering. Open data is essential to make these invisible inequalities visible, allowing for targeted social policies. By tracking granular price movements in vulnerable neighborhoods, society can hold institutions accountable and advocate for measures that address the real cost of survival, not just abstract economic targets.
