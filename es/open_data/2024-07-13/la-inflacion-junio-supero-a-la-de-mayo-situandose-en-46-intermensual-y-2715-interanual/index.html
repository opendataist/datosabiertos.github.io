La inflación junio superó a la de mayo, situándose en 4,6% intermensual y 271,5% interanual

Argentina’s June inflation showed signs of moderation, reaching 4.6% monthly and accumulating nearly 80% in the first half of the year. While the national consumer price index rose significantly year-over-year, the monthly increase remained within government expectations and below market forecasts. The acceleration was primarily driven by regulated utility tariffs and seasonal adjustments, rather than broad-based demand pressures. Crucially, the core inflation rate, which excludes volatile and regulated components, hit its lowest level since early 2022. This suggests that the current disinflationary process is gaining traction, with moving averages indicating a sustained deceleration in price growth across the economy. Regional disparities highlight varying impacts, with Cuyo experiencing the highest inflation due to housing and utility costs, while the Pampean region saw more moderate increases driven by food items. These geographical variations underscore how localized economic factors and subsidy policies influence individual household expenses differently across the country. Despite these differences, the overall trend points toward a stabilization effort that has successfully outperformed analyst predictions for consecutive months. The government emphasizes that excluding external shocks reveals a healthier underlying economic dynamic, reinforcing confidence in the stabilization plan's efficacy. This data is relevant to open data initiatives as it demonstrates the value of transparent, official statistical releases in verifying economic narratives. By publishing detailed breakdowns by region, category, and core metrics, institutions enable researchers and citizens to move beyond headlines and analyze the true drivers of inflation. Such granularity fosters accountability, allows for independent verification of government claims against market expectations, and empowers data-driven public discourse on monetary policy and cost-of-living crises.
