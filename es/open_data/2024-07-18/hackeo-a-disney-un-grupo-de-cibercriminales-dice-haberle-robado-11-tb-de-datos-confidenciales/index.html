Hackeo a Disney: un grupo de cibercriminales dice haberle robado 1,1 TB de datos confidenciales

The leak of internal Disney communications highlights critical vulnerabilities in cloud-based collaboration tools, demonstrating how easily sensitive corporate data can be exposed through platform-specific weaknesses. This incident serves as a stark reminder for organizations to rigorously audit their open data policies and secure API integrations, as even protected enterprise environments are susceptible to significant breaches involving intellectual property and internal infrastructure details. Furthermore, the hackers’ motivation underscores the growing tension between corporate efficiency and ethical AI usage, revealing how internal discussions about artist rights and automation can inadvertently become public liabilities. This situation emphasizes the need for transparent governance regarding generative AI and labor practices, encouraging companies to align their technological strategies with broader ethical standards to prevent such high-profile exposures. Ultimately, this event is relevant to open data as it illustrates the double-edged nature of digital transparency: while open principles foster innovation, they also amplify security risks when data management protocols are insufficient. It urges the community to prioritize robust security frameworks alongside openness, ensuring that the benefits of collaborative platforms do not come at the cost of compromised privacy or exposed sensitive operational data.
