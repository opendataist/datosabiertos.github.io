C-LM registró 212.838 pernoctaciones en alojamientos turísticos extrahoteleros durante el mes de diciembre

The article highlights a significant recovery and growth in Spain’s extrahotel tourism sector in 2023, marked by increased occupancy rates and rising prices across categories. This trend underscores a shift in consumer behavior where international visitors drive substantial gains, particularly in popular destinations like the Canary Islands and campsites in Catalonia. The data reveals that non-resident stays outperformed domestic ones, indicating a robust demand from abroad that compensates for slight declines or stagnation in local tourism. The distribution of stays shows a clear dominance of tourist apartments, which account for half of all extrahotel overnight stays, followed by campsites. This concentration of activity in specific accommodation types suggests evolving travel preferences towards flexible and self-catering options. The growth in these sectors, alongside increases in rural tourism and hostels, points to a diversified market that appeals to various traveler segments, from budget-conscious youth to families seeking nature experiences. This article is highly relevant to open data because it exemplifies the critical role of standardized, publicly accessible statistical indicators in monitoring economic sectors. By analyzing open datasets from the National Institute of Statistics, policymakers and researchers can track tourism trends, assess regional disparities, and inform infrastructure planning. Such transparency enables evidence-based decision-making, ensuring that public resources align with actual market dynamics and supporting sustainable tourism development through data-driven insights.
