«Los datos del INE no son ciertos» sobre el comercio minorista

The article reveals a stark disconnect between official statistics and the real-world experience of Spanish retailers, who report significant sales losses despite government figures showing growth. This discrepancy highlights a broader crisis where data manipulation obscures the true state of the economy, affecting both retail and employment metrics. Such contradictions are highly relevant to open data because they demonstrate how unreliable or opaque official datasets can hinder transparency and public trust. When statistical agencies provide numbers that directly contradict ground-level evidence, it undermines the foundational principle that open data should empower stakeholders with accurate, verifiable information for decision-making. Ultimately, the situation calls for greater accountability in how data is collected and presented. Ensuring that open data initiatives prioritize factual accuracy over political narratives is crucial for restoring confidence in public institutions and enabling civil society to effectively advocate for economic realities rather than accepted falsehoods.
