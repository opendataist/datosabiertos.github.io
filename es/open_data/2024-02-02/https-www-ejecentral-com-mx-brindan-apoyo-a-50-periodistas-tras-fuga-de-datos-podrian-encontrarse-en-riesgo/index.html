Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Following a significant data breach involving the personal information of accredited journalists at the Presidential offices, the Mexican government has initiated protective measures through the Secretary of the Interior. Recognizing that the illicit extraction of data from the Accreditation System places these media professionals in potential danger, the state has activated a specialized support framework. This response underscores the critical link between digital privacy and physical safety for those exercising their right to inform. The government is actively assisting journalists who feel at risk, facilitating their access to a federal protection mechanism designed for human rights defenders and media workers. Through dedicated modules, authorities are helping individuals file criminal complaints regarding data violations and strengthening monitoring plans for those already enrolled. This coordinated effort highlights the necessity of institutional safeguards when sensitive personal information is compromised, ensuring that vulnerabilities arising from data theft are addressed with immediate and structured interventions. This situation is highly relevant to open data discussions as it illustrates the severe real-world consequences of inadequate data governance. When public or quasi-public databases containing sensitive personal details lack robust security, the result is not merely a digital glitch but a tangible threat to individual safety. It emphasizes that open data initiatives must be balanced with stringent privacy protocols and accountability measures, demonstrating that data protection is a fundamental human rights issue rather than just a technical compliance matter.
