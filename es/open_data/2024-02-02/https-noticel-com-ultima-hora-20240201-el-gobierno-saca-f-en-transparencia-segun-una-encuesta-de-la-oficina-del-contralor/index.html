Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

A recent survey by the Office of the Comptroller of Puerto Rico highlights a significant gap between the public’s desire for government transparency and their actual engagement with open data resources. While nearly all participants recognized the importance of accessing public management information, a vast majority perceived the administration as lacking transparency. Crucially, only a small fraction of the population is aware of or actively visits the dedicated open data portal, revealing a disconnect between available digital tools and citizen awareness. The study identifies bureaucratic hurdles and a lack of legal knowledge among both officials and citizens as primary obstacles to accessing information. Although a majority of respondents successfully obtained requested data, many faced partial deliveries or delays. The findings suggest that current platforms do not adequately meet user needs for clarity, reliability, or ease of access. Consequently, there is an urgent need to improve information technology systems and enforce data quality standards to bridge this trust deficit. This report is vital to the open data community as it empirically validates the challenges of data usability and discovery in public sector initiatives. It underscores that simply publishing data is insufficient; without proactive promotion, user-friendly interfaces, and strict adherence to transparency laws, open data portals risk remaining underutilized. The insights provide actionable recommendations for policymakers to enhance government accountability and foster meaningful civic participation through better-designed open data ecosystems.
