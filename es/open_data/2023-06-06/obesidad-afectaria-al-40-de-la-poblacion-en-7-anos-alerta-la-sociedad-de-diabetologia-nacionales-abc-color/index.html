Obesidad afectaría al 40% de la población en 7 años, alerta la Sociedad de Diabetología - Nacionales - ABC Color

The article highlights a critical public health crisis in Paraguay, where rising rates of obesity, diabetes, and prediabetes are driven by poor dietary habits and sedentary lifestyles. Experts emphasize that lifestyle factors, particularly the consumption of ultraprocessed foods and insufficient physical activity, outweigh genetic predispositions in triggering these non-communicable diseases. The rapid increase in obesity is outpacing projections, signaling a future burden on healthcare systems if immediate behavioral changes are not adopted. Crucially, the transition toward cheaper, processed foods has replaced traditional diets, creating an environment where unhealthy options are economically accessible to the majority. This shift, compounded by the allure of fast food delivery and high alcohol consumption, has created a "double load" on health systems through interconnected issues like fatty liver and cardiovascular risks. The situation underscores how economic constraints and modern consumption patterns directly influence population health outcomes. This analysis is relevant to open data because it relies on national statistical surveys, such as those from the INE, to identify risk factors and track disease prevalence. Understanding these datasets allows policymakers and researchers to correlate socioeconomic variables with health metrics. By making this data accessible and transparent, stakeholders can better advocate for evidence-based interventions, monitor the efficacy of public health strategies, and address the systemic inequities that make healthy living less affordable for the general population.
