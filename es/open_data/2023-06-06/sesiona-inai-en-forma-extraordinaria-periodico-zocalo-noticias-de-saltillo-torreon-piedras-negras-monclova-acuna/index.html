Sesiona INAI en forma extraordinaria | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The Mexican Federal Institute for Transparency (INAI) recently held its first session since April, resolving a critical quorum dispute through judicial intervention. Courts determined that the existing four commissioners are sufficient to execute specific orders, overriding the Senate’s failure to fill three vacant positions. This decision validates the institute's ability to function despite incomplete staffing, ensuring the continuity of essential judicial mandates. However, this authority is strictly limited. The judges explicitly restricted the session to resolving only three specific amparo cases from 2021 and 2022, refusing to grant general authorization for the commissioners to address other review resources. This narrow mandate highlights the fragility of the current operational framework, where institutional capacity is contingent upon isolated legal rulings rather than a fully constituted body. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocates as it demonstrates how political inertia can jeopardize the enforcement of transparency rights. It underscores the necessity of robust legal safeguards to maintain functional oversight mechanisms. When key institutions lack full membership, the timely and comprehensive access to public information is at risk, emphasizing the need for proactive measures to protect data availability and institutional integrity.
