A Mexican legislator has initiated impeachment proceedings against the Secretary of the Interior for allegedly orchestrating the paralysis of the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI). The complaint centers on accusations that the official colluded with senators to block the appointment of commissioners, thereby preventing the institute from reaching the quorum necessary to operate. This strategic obstruction is framed as a direct violation of the separation of powers and an authoritarian attempt to undermine independent constitutional bodies. The inoperability of the INAI has severe implications for public accountability, as it leaves the institution unable to resolve citizen complaints regarding access to public information or the protection of personal data. By disrupting the functioning of this key oversight body, the political actions described effectively neutralize a critical check on government power. The legislator argues that this intentional neglect constitutes a fundamental breach of democratic norms, prioritizing political convenience over the institutional integrity required to safeguard civil liberties and ensure good governance. This case is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the fragility of the legal and institutional frameworks that guarantee transparency. When oversight bodies are rendered ineffective, the mechanisms for enforcing the right to information collapse, threatening the broader ecosystem of open government data. The episode underscores the critical importance of maintaining fully operational autonomous agencies to protect data rights and ensure that transparency remains a functional reality rather than a theoretical promise in democratic societies.
