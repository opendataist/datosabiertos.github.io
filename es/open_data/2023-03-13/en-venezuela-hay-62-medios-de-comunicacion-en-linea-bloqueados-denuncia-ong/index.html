En Venezuela hay 62 medios de comunicación en línea bloqueados, denuncia ONG

Venezuela maintains extensive censorship through the arbitrary blocking of numerous websites, primarily targeting media outlets. This restriction severely limits citizens' ability to access independent information, undermining fundamental digital rights and freedom of expression. The systematic nature of these blocks highlights a broader state strategy to control information flow and silence critical voices online. Censorship extends beyond technical blocks to include online intimidation, cyberattacks, and infrastructure failures like electricity outages. These tactics collectively create an environment where accessing the internet as a fundamental right becomes difficult. The situation reflects a sustained policy restricting citizen rights, placing Venezuela near the bottom in regional press freedom indices. This case is critical for open data because it illustrates how digital infrastructure and connectivity are manipulated to suppress transparency. Open data initiatives rely on unrestricted access to information; when governments block domains and degrade network reliability, they obstruct the flow of public information. This undermines accountability and demonstrates the need for global advocacy to protect digital openness and ensure citizens can freely access unbiased data.
