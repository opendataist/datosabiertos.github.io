Un inquilino que alquila y una gestora que factura 19.400 euros en un mes. Dos historias del bum de los pisos turísticos en Vigo

The article highlights a dramatic surge in short-term tourist rentals in Vigo, driven by higher profitability compared to traditional housing and improved year-round demand due to cultural management. This shift reflects a broader trend where property owners prioritize tourism to maximize returns, fundamentally altering the local rental market dynamics and incentivizing the conversion of residential units into commercial hospitality spaces. Simultaneously, this growth exacerbates housing affordability crises and neighborhood degeneration, as indicated by rising vacancies in residential sectors and increased demand for traditional housing. While some owners argue that market forces should regulate this sector, local associations and residents are calling for policy interventions to limit tourist units and prevent the displacement of the local community, creating a tension between economic gain and social sustainability. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how accessible, transparent statistical records from official bodies like INE and regional tourism agencies are essential for monitoring urban trends. By making this data publicly available, citizens and policymakers can critically assess the socioeconomic impact of the sharing economy, fostering informed debates and evidence-based decisions on urban planning and housing regulation.
