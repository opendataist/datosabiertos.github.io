Oportunidades reales para todas y todos

The publication of the latest ENIGH data by INEGI offers crucial insights into Mexico’s socioeconomic landscape, serving as a primary tool for evaluating poverty levels and income distribution. The main conclusion is that there is a positive post-pandemic recovery characterized by reduced economic inequality and lower extreme poverty rates, driven by minimum wage increases, labor reforms, and expanded governmental support programs. This data demonstrates the tangible impact of current public policies on improving household living conditions and highlights the effectiveness of state intervention in mitigating poverty. Despite these advancements, significant disparities persist that demand targeted attention. The report reveals deep-seated gender gaps, where women earn substantially less than men, with the divide widening for older, less educated women or those with multiple children. Similar inequities affect indigenous communities, individuals with disabilities, and rural populations. These figures underscore the urgent need for structural changes, such as implementing gender perspectives in all policy planning and establishing new political agreements that recognize the rights and territorial integrity of indigenous peoples to ensure their integral development. This article is highly relevant to open_data because it exemplifies how high-quality, publicly available statistical information is essential for holding governments accountable and guiding democratic decision-making. By making detailed household income and expenditure data accessible, institutions like INEGI enable researchers, civil society, and citizens to independently verify progress, identify systemic inequalities, and advocate for evidence-based policies. Open access to such granular data transforms abstract political promises into measurable outcomes, fostering transparency and allowing for the precise design of interventions aimed at achieving social justice and equitable development.
