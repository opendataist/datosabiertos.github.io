Mexico’s gold production is currently facing a significant downturn, driven by restrictive mining policies, labor strikes, and regional insecurity. The article highlights that limitations on granting new concessions have stalled exploration efforts, preventing the industry from replenishing its reserves. Concurrently, social unrest and operational halts, such as those at major mines in Zacatecas, have severely disrupted output and deterred foreign investment, creating a challenging environment for sustained industrial growth. The long-term implications of this crisis extend beyond immediate economic losses, threatening national supply chains and global competitiveness. Experts warn that depleting reserves without new discoveries will force production cuts, leading to job losses and higher costs for consumer goods and technology components that rely on these metals. This decline also impacts community development, as reduced tax revenues diminish funding for local infrastructure and social services in mining regions, exacerbating regional inequalities. This case is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent, accessible, and standardized statistical reporting in extractive industries. Reliable data from institutions like INEGI is essential for monitoring trends, informing policy reforms, and restoring investor confidence. By making production metrics and reserve statuses more open and understandable, stakeholders can better diagnose regulatory bottlenecks, track the real-time impact of socio-political factors, and advocate for evidence-based decisions that balance economic development with social and environmental responsibilities.
