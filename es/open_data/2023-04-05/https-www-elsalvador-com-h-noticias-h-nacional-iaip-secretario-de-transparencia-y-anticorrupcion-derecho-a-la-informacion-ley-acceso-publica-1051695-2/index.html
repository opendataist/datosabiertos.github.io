Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article argues that the Institute for Access to Public Information (IAIP) acted as an accomplice to the Salvadoran government’s intentional expulsion from the Open Government Partnership. Rather than a simple administrative oversight, the exclusion is characterized as a deliberate political strategy by President Bukele to escape external scrutiny regarding transparency and anti-corruption standards. The IAIP, an institution created through previous commitments to the alliance, failed to implement required action plans, effectively serving the executive branch’s goal of isolating itself from international democratic oversight mechanisms. This situation highlights a significant regression in the practical application of open data and public access to information. Although legal frameworks initially improved transparency, current administrative practices exploit loopholes to withhold data, rendering public portals obsolete and silencing civic engagement. The shift demonstrates how institutional spaces designed for accountability have been dismantled, suggesting that the government prioritizes political control over the democratic principles of participation and public record availability that are central to open government initiatives. The relevance of this case to open data lies in its demonstration of the fragility of institutional transparency when subjected to authoritarian tendencies. The expulsion signals a broader trend of international isolation, potentially leading to economic repercussions and moral sanctions from global organizations that prioritize governance standards. This underscores that open data is not merely a technical obligation but a critical safeguard against corruption; when states abandon these commitments, they risk losing credibility and access to international cooperation, negatively impacting both governance quality and citizen welfare.
