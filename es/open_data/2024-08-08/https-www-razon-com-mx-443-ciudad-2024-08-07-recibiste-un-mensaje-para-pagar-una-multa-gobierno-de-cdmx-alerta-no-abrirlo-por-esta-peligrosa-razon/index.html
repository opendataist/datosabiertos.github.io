Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Cybercriminals exploit public trust by sending fraudulent text messages that mimic official traffic fine notifications. These scams rely on urgency and fake links to phishing sites, aiming to steal personal and financial data. The Government of Mexico City warns citizens against clicking unknown links or providing information via unsolicited texts, highlighting the vulnerability of digital identity in everyday administrative interactions. Legitimate communications are strictly routed through official email addresses and verified electronic court portals. Authorities advise verifying the sender’s authenticity, checking for grammatical errors, and ignoring urgent payment demands from unknown numbers. Citizens are encouraged to report such messages as spam and contact cyber police for guidance, emphasizing the need for skepticism toward unsolicited digital requests that bypass standard verification channels. This alert is relevant to open data because it underscores the importance of authentic, publicly accessible official platforms for government services. When citizens rely on verified open data sources, like official infracciones portals, they are protected from malicious impersonation. Promoting transparency and easy access to legitimate digital channels reduces the attack surface for fraudsters who exploit confusion about official communication methods.
