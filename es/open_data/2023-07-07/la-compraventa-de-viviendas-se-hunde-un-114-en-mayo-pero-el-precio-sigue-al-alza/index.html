La compraventa de viviendas se hunde un 11,4% en mayo, pero el precio sigue al alza

The Spanish housing market is experiencing a significant downturn, characterized by sharp declines in both sales volume and mortgage lending. Transaction volumes have fallen substantially across most autonomous communities, indicating reduced consumer confidence and liquidity. This contraction is exacerbated by a dramatic drop in mortgage approvals, which has persisted for six consecutive months without growth, severely limiting access to financing for potential buyers. Despite the volume drop, national average prices remain relatively stable, yet the market is highly fragmented regionally. While some areas like Castilla-La Mancha and the Balearic Islands see notable price increases, others such as Navarra and Galicia report significant depreciation. This divergence highlights the lack of a uniform national trend, with local economic factors driving distinct housing dynamics rather than a cohesive national direction. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it underscores the necessity of transparent, granular real estate metrics. By publishing detailed, localized information on volumes, prices, and credit availability, institutions empower citizens and researchers to analyze market disparities accurately. Such accessibility fosters informed decision-making and exposes regional inequalities, ensuring that housing data serves as a tool for social and economic insight rather than just commercial statistics.
