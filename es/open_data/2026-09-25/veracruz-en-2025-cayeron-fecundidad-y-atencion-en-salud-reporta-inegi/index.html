Veracruz en 2025: Cayeron fecundidad y atención en salud, reporta INEGI

The 2025 Intercensal Survey reveals a profound demographic transition in Veracruz, characterized by a significant decline in fertility rates and substantial improvements in educational access. As the average number of children per woman decreases and literacy rates rise, the state is shifting towards a smaller, more educated population. This structural change implies long-term socioeconomic implications, as families invest more resources in fewer children, potentially enhancing future human capital and labor productivity across the region. Simultaneously, the healthcare landscape is undergoing a major restructuring, marked by a notable shift from public to private service utilization. While enrollment in public health institutions has diminished, usage of private clinics and pharmacy-based medical consultations has surged, now constituting a significant portion of patient demand. This migration towards private care suggests evolving user preferences and access patterns, indicating that public health policies must adapt to a market where private alternatives play a dominant role in meeting the population's medical needs. This data is critically relevant to open data initiatives as it highlights the necessity of granular, timely statistical transparency for effective public policy. By making these detailed demographic and health indicators accessible, governments and researchers can better track social progress, identify regional disparities, and evaluate the impact of healthcare reforms. Open access to such comprehensive datasets fosters evidence-based decision-making, ensuring that resource allocation aligns with the actual behaviors and needs of the population rather than outdated models.
