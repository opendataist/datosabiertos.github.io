Baja el desempleo en el país, pero no se crean más puestos de trabajo: el mercado laboral en cinco gráficos

The article reveals that while the official unemployment rate has decreased, this improvement is misleading. The reduction in unemployed figures is primarily driven by a shrinking labor force rather than the creation of new jobs. Consequently, the main conclusion is that the apparent progress masks a deeper stagnation in job generation, as many individuals have exited the workforce entirely, rendering the lower unemployment statistic an artifact of disengagement rather than economic health. Furthermore, the data highlights a persistent decline in both formal and informal employment, alongside a significant gap in labor participation rates between genders. Women and young people are disproportionately affected by these challenges, with women experiencing a substantial drop in labor force involvement. The recovery in employment levels has been sluggish and has not returned to pre-pandemic standards, indicating structural weaknesses in the market that fail to support vulnerable demographics effectively. This analysis is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical importance of contextualizing raw statistics with complementary indicators. Solely presenting the unemployment rate can lead to incorrect policy conclusions or public misinterpretation. Access to multidimensional datasets—such as those including labor force participation, informal employment trends, and demographic breakdowns—allows analysts to uncover the true state of the economy and prevent the obscuring of negative trends behind seemingly positive numbers.
