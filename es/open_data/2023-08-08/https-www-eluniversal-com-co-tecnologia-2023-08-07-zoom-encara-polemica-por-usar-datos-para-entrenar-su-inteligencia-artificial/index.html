Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Zoom faced significant backlash after users discovered that its updated terms of use allowed the platform to collect client data for training artificial intelligence models. This revelation, which spread rapidly across social media, sparked concerns about privacy violations and the potential misuse of user content. Critics argued that the company was seeking perpetual rights to utilize personal information without clear, immediate acknowledgment from the individuals providing it. In response to the controversy, Zoom promptly modified its consent policy and publicly clarified its stance. The company emphasized that using audio, video, or chat data for AI training would strictly require explicit user consent. Furthermore, Zoom stressed that this data would only be utilized to improve the performance of its own services and would never be shared with third parties for their proprietary model development, thereby addressing fears of unauthorized data exploitation. This incident is highly relevant to open data discussions as it highlights the critical importance of transparency and informed consent in data ecosystems. It illustrates how opaque terms can erode trust and demonstrates the necessity for platforms to adopt clear, user-centric governance when handling personal information. The case serves as a cautionary example for the broader tech industry, underscoring the need for ethical standards that prioritize user agency over unchecked data aggregation in the age of AI.
