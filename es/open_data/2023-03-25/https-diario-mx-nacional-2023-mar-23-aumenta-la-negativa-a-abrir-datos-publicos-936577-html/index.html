Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The INAI reported a significant rise in disputes regarding the government’s refusal to provide public information, highlighting a growing challenge to transparency. This increase underscores a systemic resistance from state entities to comply with disclosure obligations, often citing confidentiality or non-existence of records. The high rate of successful appeals demonstrates that these refusals are frequently unjustified, indicating a critical gap between legal mandates and administrative practices in Mexico. This trend is particularly relevant to open data initiatives, as it reveals structural barriers that prevent the free flow of public information essential for civic oversight and digital innovation. When government data remains inaccessible, it undermines the foundational principles of an open society and limits the ability of civil society to hold authorities accountable. The struggle for transparency directly impacts the availability of reliable datasets needed for research, journalism, and technological development. Furthermore, the political tension surrounding the INAI’s autonomy reflects broader democratic anxieties. Defending the institute’s independence is crucial for maintaining mechanisms that ensure data accessibility. Ultimately, strengthening these institutions is necessary to foster a culture where openness is the norm, enabling citizens to fully exercise their rights and participate effectively in democratic processes through verified information.
