Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article underscores the critical role of the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) in safeguarding democratic rights and combating corruption through transparency. The institution’s primary function is to ensure citizens’ right to access public information and protect their personal data, acting as an independent guardian rather than an opponent to government powers. By facilitating the disclosure of information, the INAI has been instrumental in uncovering major corruption cases, thereby enhancing accountability and strengthening public trust in democratic institutions. However, the institute faces a severe operational crisis due to political disagreements over the appointment of its commissioners, which has led to a lack of quorum and potential paralysis. Without a full panel, the INAI cannot resolve appeals, enforce transparency obligations, or address complaints regarding personal data protection. This institutional weakness poses a significant threat to citizens' rights, as the inability to function effectively could result in widespread impunity and the erosion of fundamental human rights guaranteed by law. This situation is highly relevant to the open_data community because it highlights the fragility of the legal and institutional frameworks that make data accessible and usable. Open data initiatives rely on robust autonomy and operational capacity to enforce transparency and protect privacy. When key oversight bodies are compromised by political interference, the flow of reliable, public-interest data is jeopardized, undermining efforts to promote digital rights and ensuring that transparency mechanisms fail to serve the public effectively.
