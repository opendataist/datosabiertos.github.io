Inai urge al Senado a designar dos cargos pendientes

The article highlights the critical need for the Senate to appoint pending commissioners to the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI). Without a quorum, the institution cannot function effectively, leaving citizens without recourse to enforce their right to know or protect their personal data. This legislative gap threatens the agency's ability to resolve national-level appeals and verify compliance with transparency obligations, undermining its operational autonomy and mission. Transparency is presented as the backbone of a functioning democracy, enabling the uncovering of major corruption cases and ensuring legitimate elections through reliable candidate information. By documenting significant financial irregularities, the INAI demonstrates how open data mechanisms hold power accountable. The narrative emphasizes that democratic integrity relies on verifiable information, asserting that justice and governance cannot survive in secrecy. This report is relevant to open data because it illustrates the institutional framework necessary for data accessibility and accountability. It underscores that open government principles are not merely technical requirements but fundamental democratic safeguards. The article argues that strengthening transparency institutions empowers civil society to demand accountability, proving that access to information is essential for mitigating abuses of power and fostering a truly open society.
