Los escándalos en el INAI: Desde ataques de AMLO, hasta tarjetas para table dance y acoso

The article highlights a severe institutional crisis at Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection, stemming from political pressure and internal ethical failures. The executive branch attempted to dismantle the autonomous body by withholding new council appointments, leading to a quorum shortage that temporarily paralyzed decision-making. This standoff demonstrates how external political interference directly undermines the operational capacity of transparency institutions, creating a bottleneck that delays thousands of public information requests and weakens governmental accountability mechanisms. Internally, the organization faces allegations of sexual harassment and the misuse of public funds for personal entertainment. The institute’s initial response to investigate these serious misconduct claims involved withholding relevant records under the guise of protecting procedural integrity. This contradictory behavior, where the guardian of transparency itself practices secrecy, erodes public trust and reveals significant governance flaws within the very systems designed to ensure ethical public service and information access. This narrative is crucial for open data advocates because it illustrates the fragility of transparency frameworks when political will and internal ethics collapse. It shows that open data initiatives are not merely technical projects but depend heavily on the independence, stability, and ethical conduct of the institutions managing public information. Without robust protection against political interference and strong internal accountability, the fundamental right to access public data remains vulnerable to manipulation and obstruction.
