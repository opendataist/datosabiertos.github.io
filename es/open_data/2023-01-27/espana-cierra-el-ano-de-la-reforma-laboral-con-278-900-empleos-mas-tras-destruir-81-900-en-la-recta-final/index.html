España cierra el año de la reforma laboral con 278.900 empleos más tras destruir 81.900 en la recta final

The article highlights a significant deceleration in the Spanish labor market during the final quarter of 2022, marked by job losses in the private sector and a rise in unemployment, which raises concerns about economic slowdown. Despite this recent negative trend, the government emphasizes that the primary achievement of the first year under the new labor reform is a substantial structural reduction in temporary employment rates, aligning Spain more closely with European averages and establishing indefinite contracts as the standard. This shift represents a fundamental transformation in labor relations, aiming to provide greater stability to workers and reduce precarity through generalized fixed-discontinuous contracts. While macroeconomic uncertainties and inflationary pressures have dampened short-term hiring momentum, particularly affecting sectors like services and self-employment, the long-term goal of the reform remains securing stable, permanent positions over temporary ones to create a more resilient workforce.
