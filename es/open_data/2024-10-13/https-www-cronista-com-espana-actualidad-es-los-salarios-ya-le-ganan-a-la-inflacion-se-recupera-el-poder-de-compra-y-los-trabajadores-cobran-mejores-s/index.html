Salaries agreed in collective bargaining have risen above inflation, restoring workers' purchasing power and ensuring that wages outpace the cost of living. This significant gap between nominal wage growth and price increases marks a positive shift for employee welfare and economic stability. The article highlights the importance of transparent wage data in assessing labor market trends and the effectiveness of social agreements. Understanding these dynamics allows stakeholders to evaluate how well compensation structures adapt to economic conditions. This is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the value of publicly accessible statistics in monitoring social equity and labor rights. By making such data available, researchers and citizens can better analyze the real impact of policy recommendations and collective negotiations on everyday life.
