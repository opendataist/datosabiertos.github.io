PLN propone excluir del Sicop las contrataciones de la Fiscalía para dar protección a víctimas y testigos

Public procurement transparency laws mandate the disclosure of detailed service data, which inadvertently exposes the locations and routines of individuals under witness protection. This lack of confidentiality creates a severe security risk, as criminal organizations could exploit public contracts to track, threaten, or extort protected victims and their families. Consequently, maintaining the secrecy of specific contracts is deemed necessary to safeguard lives and ensure the integrity of judicial investigations against organized crime. The proposed initiative argues that standard transparency platforms like Sicop should exclude sensitive protection services, justifying confidentiality in these unique cases. By obscuring who provides lodging or transport for witnesses, the government can prevent criminals from identifying targets through logistical records. This approach prioritizes the physical safety of vulnerable individuals over the general public’s right to detailed financial transparency, acknowledging that exposure could lead to direct harm or intimidation. This issue is critical for open data discourse as it highlights the tension between transparency principles and fundamental security rights. It demonstrates that not all public data should be automatically accessible; certain datasets require strict access controls to prevent misuse by malicious actors. The debate underscores the need for nuanced open data policies that balance accountability with the protection of high-risk individuals, ensuring that transparency initiatives do not compromise personal safety or judicial effectiveness.
