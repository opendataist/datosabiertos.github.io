Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The INDEC’s latest report reveals a severe national deterioration in living standards, with over half the population now living below the poverty line. This sharp rise highlights the urgent need for transparent, accessible statistical data to monitor socioeconomic shocks. Open data is crucial here, as it allows researchers and citizens to verify government claims and understand the real-time impact of policy decisions on vulnerable communities. The crisis is geographically uneven, with northern regions like Chaco and Formosa experiencing catastrophic increases in both poverty and indigence. These areas bear the brunt of the economic shift, demonstrating that aggregate national figures often mask deep regional disparities. By making granular, location-specific data open and available, stakeholders can better identify where intervention is most critical, moving beyond broad averages to address localized hardships effectively. While Buenos Aires remains the least affected region, its own metrics show a worrying upward trend, suggesting the crisis is nationwide. The relevance to open data lies in the ability to track these changes across all 31 urban agglomerates continuously. This transparency fosters accountability and enables evidence-based advocacy. Ultimately, accessible statistics empower civil society to demand precise, targeted solutions rather than generic responses to complex, multifaceted social challenges.
