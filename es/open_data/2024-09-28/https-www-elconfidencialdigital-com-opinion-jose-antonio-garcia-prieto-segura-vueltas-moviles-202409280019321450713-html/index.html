The article highlights a recent study by the Spanish Data Protection Agency regarding the use of digital devices in educational settings, recommending their avoidance when alternative pedagogical resources suffice. However, the core argument extends beyond regulatory compliance to emphasize the profound societal and psychological impacts of mobile technology on children. It presents these devices as a double-edged sword, offering significant advancements while simultaneously posing severe risks such as social isolation, attention deficits, and exposure to harmful content, which necessitate urgent protective measures. The text asserts that technical safeguards are insufficient without active parental involvement and moral leadership. It argues that education in moderation and austerity must begin at home, with parents serving as primary role models. The narrative critiques the tendency to seek state intervention instead of embracing parental responsibility, illustrating how adults often fail to practice the digital hygiene they expect from their children. Thus, the solution lies in a cultural shift where families prioritize real-world interactions and self-regulation over screen dependency. This discussion is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical intersection between digital access and personal data privacy. The argument that unrestricted access to devices exacerbates vulnerabilities in data protection highlights the need for ethical frameworks in technology design and usage. It suggests that open data initiatives must account for the protective needs of minors, advocating for policies that prioritize privacy and dignity alongside digital inclusion, thereby framing data rights as an integral part of broader societal well-being and child development.
