Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The rejection of the proposal to convene the Senate for an extraordinary session highlights a critical paralysis within Mexico’s transparency framework. By failing to address the vacancy at the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), legislative bodies are allowing the institution to remain incomplete, thereby impairing its ability to function effectively. This inaction contradicts judicial mandates and deepens the institutional crisis, leaving the autonomous body unable to fulfill its constitutional obligations. The implications for transparency are severe, as the lack of a full commissioning staff results in a significant backlog of public information requests and unresolved administrative cases. Citizens are increasingly forced to resort to the judiciary to enforce their rights, indicating that the normal legislative and administrative channels have broken down. This situation undermines public trust and delays justice, demonstrating how legislative delays can directly hinder the protection of fundamental rights to information and data protection. This event is highly relevant to open_data because it illustrates the fragility of the legal infrastructure required to ensure transparency and accountability. An operational INAI is essential for enforcing access to public information, a cornerstone of open data initiatives. When political maneuvering or structural reform debates prevent the filling of key positions, the mechanism that guarantees data openness and government transparency is compromised, affecting both civil society and the integrity of public information systems.
