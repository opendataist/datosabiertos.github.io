Ministra de Bolivia pide despolitizar datos de censo poblacional - Noticias Prensa Latina

The article highlights how the Bolivian government combats political polarization and disinformation surrounding census results by prioritizing transparency and the immediate public availability of data. By making these statistics open and accessible, authorities aim to allow citizens and institutions to verify, analyze, and contrast the information independently. This approach transforms raw data into a tool for truth, directly countering narratives that question the legitimacy of the official counts without evidence. This strategy is crucial for open data because it establishes a precedent where governance relies on verifiable evidence rather than political rhetoric. When data is open, it empowers civil society and international bodies to validate findings, as demonstrated by the UN and CEPAL’s confirmation of the data’s coherence. Consequently, openness serves as the primary defense against accusations of fraud, reducing the space for unfounded claims to spread in a polarized environment. The relevance to open data lies in its ability to restore trust in public institutions through accountability. Instead of relying on opaque bureaucratic processes, the state uses published datasets to invite scrutiny and dialogue. This case illustrates that robust open data practices are not just technical necessities but vital democratic tools that mitigate conflict, ensure factual accuracy, and protect the integrity of national statistics against partisan manipulation.
