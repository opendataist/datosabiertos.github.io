INE: La tasa de fecundidad bajó en Bolivia de 7,5 en 1970 a 2,1 en 2023

The 2024 Census in Bolivia reveals a significant demographic shift, characterized by a sharp decline in fertility rates approaching the replacement level and a slowing population growth. This transition, driven by changing social dynamics such as delayed parenthood and evolving family structures, indicates that the country is no longer expanding as rapidly as previously projected. These findings highlight a fundamental change in reproductive behavior and household composition across the nation. Official analysis attributes these changes largely to recent societal transformations, including the impact of the pandemic and shifting attitudes among young people. While some experts question whether short-term health crises alone explain long-term demographic trends, the data underscores the urgent need to account for factors like increased mortality, migration, and urban empty homes. The discrepancy between projected and actual population figures suggests that earlier estimates failed to capture these complex, multifaceted drivers of decline. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how census information serves as a critical tool for evidence-based policy-making. By making these demographic insights publicly available, authorities can better understand societal changes, such as aging populations or urbanization patterns, to adjust social services and infrastructure planning. Transparent access to this data allows for more effective governance and ensures that public policies reflect the current realities of the population rather than outdated assumptions.
