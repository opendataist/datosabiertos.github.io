Ingresos seguros en asentamientos informales surge como gran desafío para el proceso del Censo 2024 | Puranoticia.cl

The article highlights that successfully census-taking informal settlements relies on strategic collaboration rather than isolated government action. By establishing informal contacts with community representatives and coordinating with NGOs like Techo and Deficit Zero, the National Institute of Statistics ensures enumerator safety and gains access to critical data. This cooperative approach addresses the historical challenges of reaching populations living in precarious conditions, transforming potential hostility into a structured dialogue based on trust and prior engagement with local leaders. This partnership is vital for accurate urban planning and resource allocation. Obtaining precise data on population density and housing conditions in these camps allows the state to properly dimension essential public services such as health and education. Without this specific information, the government cannot effectively address overcrowding or plan infrastructure, making the inclusion of these marginalized communities fundamental for equitable policy-making and understanding the true scale of social housing demands. This case is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how sharing granular, contextual information between civil society organizations and state agencies improves the quality of national statistics. It underscores that open, accessible data regarding informal settlements is not merely an administrative technicality but a tool for social justice. When data flows freely between NGOs, technical committees, and statistical bodies, it enables better monitoring of human rights and ensures that statistical processes reflect the reality of vulnerable populations.
