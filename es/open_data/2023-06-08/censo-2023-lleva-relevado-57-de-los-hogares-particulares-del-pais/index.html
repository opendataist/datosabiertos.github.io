Censo 2023 lleva relevado 57% de los hogares particulares del país

The 2023 census in Uruguay has successfully initiated its in-person phase, leveraging a hybrid approach that combines digital submissions with field visits to ensure comprehensive coverage. A significant majority of households have already been processed, highlighting the effectiveness of integrating technology with traditional methods. This strategy not only accelerates data collection but also ensures that residents who are not found at home can easily complete the census digitally, reducing barriers to participation and improving the accuracy of population records. Operational transparency is a core component of this initiative, with clear distinctions drawn between census takers conducting the national count and surveyors handling separate, sample-based inquiries. By standardizing identification through uniforms and verified credentials, the statistical agency aims to build public trust and prevent fraud. This clarity ensures that citizens can confidently engage with the process, knowing that the data collected is secure and intended solely for official demographic and policy planning purposes, rather than general research. This article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the foundational importance of rigorous, transparent data collection for public governance. The census generates the baseline dataset upon which many open government initiatives, resource allocation models, and public service planning rely. Furthermore, the emphasis on verifying data sources and protecting citizen privacy reflects current best practices in data ethics, ensuring that the resulting open data ecosystem is both trustworthy and usable for researchers and policymakers aiming to drive evidence-based decision-making.
