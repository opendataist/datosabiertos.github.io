The National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) has elected Adrián Alcalá Méndez as its new president for the 2023–2026 term, a move that underscores the institution's resilience amid complex political challenges. This leadership transition is pivotal for maintaining the stability required to defend the constitutional design of the agency. By securing a full three-year mandate, the new leadership aims to consolidate internal consensus and resist polarization, which are essential for preserving the INAI’s autonomy and its capacity to enforce transparency standards without interruption. A central theme of this appointment is the reaffirmation of democratic processes within the oversight body. The successful election demonstrates that the INAI remains a functional and democratic institution capable of resolving leadership disputes through established legal frameworks rather than external pressure. The outgoing president emphasized the necessity of stability to continue the fight for citizens' rights, while candidates highlighted the need for institutional reengineering and improved processes. This focus on internal reconstruction and inter-institutional dialogue is critical for ensuring that the agency can effectively serve the public interest and adapt to evolving societal demands. This development is highly relevant to open data because a stable, autonomous transparency authority is the backbone of effective data governance. Open data ecosystems depend on rigorous enforcement of access to information laws and robust protection of personal data rights. By prioritizing institutional unity and legal defense, the new leadership ensures the regulatory framework remains strong enough to guarantee public access to government information. Consequently, this continuity supports the broader open data agenda by safeguarding the mechanisms that allow citizens and researchers to hold power accountable through accessible, reliable, and secure information.
