INE asegura que desde 1900 los censos registran el nombre de los entrevistados

The National Statistics Institute clarifies that requesting citizens' full names during the census is a longstanding practice rooted in statistical secrecy and confidentiality. This approach aligns with regional standards, ensuring data integrity while protecting individual privacy against the unnecessary public uproar regarding personal identification requirements. The insistence on using pencil rather than pen aims to preserve the accuracy of the collected information, preventing errors or marks that could compromise the validity of the results. This procedural detail underscores the rigorous standards maintained in modern census operations to ensure reliable demographic data collection across all households. This transparency is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how statistical agencies balance public trust with rigorous data quality protocols. By adhering to international best practices and maintaining strict confidentiality, the INE ensures that the resulting datasets are both legally protected and scientifically robust, facilitating responsible public access and analysis of national demographic trends.
