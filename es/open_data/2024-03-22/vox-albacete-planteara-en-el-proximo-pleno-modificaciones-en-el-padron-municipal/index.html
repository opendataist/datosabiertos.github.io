Vox Albacete planteará en el próximo pleno modificaciones en el Padrón Municipal

Vox Albacete proposes modifying the municipal registry procedures, arguing that the current administration incorrectly allows individuals occupying properties without legal title to register. The party contends that the official interpretation conflates the registration of homeless individuals in social centers with that of illegal occupants. They assert that while administrative bodies must verify residence, they cannot adjudicate private property rights, and thus, the mere fact of living in a house is insufficient for registration without proper legal justification or owner authorization. The central conclusion is that the current practice represents a misapplication of national statistical institute regulations, which prioritize proving actual habitation over ownership disputes but do not explicitly legitimize squatting. Vox emphasizes that legal residents face strict documentation requirements, whereas those in illegal occupations often bypass them, creating an unfair paradox. By insisting on scrupulous adherence to existing norms, the motion seeks to prevent the administrative recognition of illegal occupancy, ensuring that registry benefits—such as voting rights and public services—are restricted to those with legitimate residential status. This issue is relevant to open_data because it highlights critical tensions in how public administrative data is classified and accessed. The debate underscores the importance of precise data definitions and metadata transparency in public registries. It questions whether open data policies inadvertently enable the misuse of personal and demographic information when legal ambiguities regarding "habitual residence" are not rigorously distinguished. Ensuring data integrity requires clear, publicly accessible standards that prevent the administrative validation of illegal status, thereby maintaining the reliability of public statistics and legal governance.
