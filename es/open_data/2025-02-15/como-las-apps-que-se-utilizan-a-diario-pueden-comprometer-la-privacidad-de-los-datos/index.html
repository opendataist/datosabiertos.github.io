Cómo las apps que se utilizan a diario pueden comprometer la privacidad de los datos

The article reveals that everyday mobile applications, from social media to health trackers, silently harvest vast amounts of sensitive personal data. This pervasive tracking, amplified by artificial intelligence and predictive analytics, creates detailed user profiles without explicit consent. The main conclusion is that the convenience of digital tools comes at the cost of privacy, exposing users to significant security risks and the potential for malicious actors to exploit their behavioral patterns. This issue is critically relevant to open data because it highlights the urgent need for transparency and ethical standards in data collection practices. While open data initiatives often promote the sharing of information for public good, this research underscores the danger when data extraction occurs opaquely and unregulated. It challenges the open data community to advocate for frameworks that respect user autonomy, ensuring that the flow of data does not infringe upon individual privacy rights or create surveillance environments where privacy becomes a privilege rather than a norm. To mitigate these risks, users are urged to audit app permissions, employ privacy-focused tools like VPNs, and demand transparency from developers. The report suggests that without proactive measures, the proliferation of smart devices will further obscure who accesses our data and for what purpose. Ultimately, it calls for a cultural shift where users actively examine and control their digital footprints, reinforcing the principle that data sovereignty should remain a fundamental aspect of the open data ecosystem.
