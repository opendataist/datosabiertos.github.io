Radiografía a un mercado laboral mexicano en crisis: ¿Qué nos espera en 2025? - ebizlatam.com

Mexico’s high unemployment stems from a complex interplay of economic volatility, skill mismatches due to limited education, and technological automation. These factors reduce labor demand and displace workers, particularly in sectors reliant on routine manual tasks, creating a structural barrier to employment that transcends simple cyclical downturns. Addressing this crisis requires comprehensive policies focusing on infrastructure investment, support for small businesses, and workforce upskilling. Reforming labor markets to increase competitiveness and fostering innovation are essential to align worker skills with market needs. Specific attention must be given to stimulating growth in manufacturing, construction, and services, which are critical for job creation, while also implementing targeted programs for youth employment to ensure inclusive economic recovery. This article is relevant to open data because understanding and solving these multifaceted economic challenges relies on transparent, accessible, and granular statistical information. Open data enables policymakers and researchers to identify specific sectors suffering from labor shortages or surpluses, measure the impact of educational gaps, and track the efficacy of technological displacement. By making labor market, educational, and economic data freely available, stakeholders can develop evidence-based interventions that effectively target the root causes of unemployment rather than merely treating symptoms.
