Proponen revivir el padrón de telefonía; iniciativa en seguridad

The article highlights a controversial proposal to mandate mobile phone user registration for public security agencies, despite a previous Supreme Court ruling declaring such databases unconstitutional due privacy violations. This tension underscores critical challenges in open data governance, particularly the balance between state surveillance and individual rights. A secondary initiative aims to create a national intelligence system that integrates disparate public databases to combat high-impact crimes. By forcing the interoperability of federal, state, and municipal data, the proposal prioritizes administrative efficiency over data sovereignty and local control. This is highly relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates the risks of opaque government data aggregation. It serves as a cautionary tale about the need for robust legal frameworks and transparency when developing national digital infrastructure to prevent authoritarian overreach under the guise of security.
