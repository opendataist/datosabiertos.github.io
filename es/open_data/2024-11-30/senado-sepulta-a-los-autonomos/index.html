Senado ´sepulta´ a los autonómos

The Mexican Senate has approved a constitutional reform that extinguishes seven autonomous bodies, including the Institute of Transparency and Access to Information (INAI), and transfers their functions to state secretaries. This move centralizes power within the executive branch, effectively dismantling the institutional checks and balances designed to prevent government overreach and corruption. Critics argue this shifts the state away from a pluralistic model toward a centralized, unitary government structure. The elimination of independent regulatory and evaluation agencies undermines specialized oversight in critical areas such as telecommunications, economic competition, and energy. By consolidating these roles, the reform reduces the capacity for impartial monitoring and accountability, potentially allowing the ruling party to control previously independent adjudications. This consolidation raises concerns about the weakening of democratic institutions and the concentration of authority. This development is highly relevant to open data because the INAI was the primary guarantor of citizens' rights to access public information. Its removal jeopardizes transparency initiatives and the legal frameworks ensuring data openness. Without an autonomous body to enforce transparency laws, the reliability and accessibility of public data may significantly decline, impacting civic engagement and accountability efforts essential for an informed society.
