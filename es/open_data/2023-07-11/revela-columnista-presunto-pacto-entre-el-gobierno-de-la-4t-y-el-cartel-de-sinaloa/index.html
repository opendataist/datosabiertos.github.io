Revela columnista presunto pacto entre el Gobierno de la 4T y el Cártel de Sinaloa

The article alleges that the Mexican government has engaged in a controversial agreement with the Sinaloa Cartel to curb the production and trafficking of fentanyl. This alleged arrangement emerged amidst intense pressure from the United States, which blames Mexico for the opioid crisis driving significant fatalities in the US. The report suggests that cartel leadership issued orders to execute traffickers involved in fentanyl distribution, portraying this as an unprecedented negotiation facilitated by the current administration to address international demands. However, the text emphasizes that such a pact is unlikely to succeed in stopping the flow of chemical precursors from China or halting the proliferation of narcolaboratories within Mexico. Furthermore, it highlights that the growing involvement of the Jalisco Nueva Generación Cartel poses a significant challenge to any localized agreements. The narrative underscores the limitations of relying on criminal organizations to enforce state-level security policies, suggesting that structural issues and rival criminal factions will persist regardless of temporary truces. This story is highly relevant to open_data advocates as it illustrates how critical, yet often opaque, information regarding governance and security strategies impacts public policy. It demonstrates the necessity for transparent, verifiable data to scrutinize government claims and counter narrative speculation. Without access to reliable, open sources of information, citizens cannot effectively assess the validity of such high-stakes allegations or hold authorities accountable for the true effectiveness of their anti-drug initiatives.
