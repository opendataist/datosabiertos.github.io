El comportamiento homosexual está muy extendido y es hereditario en los macacos

The study concludes that same-sex behavior in non-human animals is not rare or abnormal, but rather a common and evolutionarily advantageous trait. By analyzing genetic and observational data from a wild macaque colony, researchers demonstrated that this behavior is widespread and heritable, effectively refuting the idea that it contradicts natural selection. This challenges the long-held "Darwinian paradox" by showing that such activities are integral to biological diversity and survival strategies. The primary implication is that these behaviors foster social alliances, providing tangible evolutionary benefits. Machos engaging in same-sex interactions were found to form stronger cooperative bonds, which helped them win conflicts and ultimately increased their reproductive success. Thus, the behavior is not an outlier but a functional mechanism for social cohesion and strategic advantage within animal groups. This research is highly relevant to open data because it relies on the integration of diverse, long-term datasets—genetic, genealogical, and observational—to uncover complex biological truths. It exemplifies how transparent, comprehensive data sharing allows scientists to challenge dogmatic beliefs with empirical evidence. By making such rigorous analyses accessible, the scientific community can better understand the natural basis of diverse behaviors, promoting informed, evidence-based perspectives against prejudice.
