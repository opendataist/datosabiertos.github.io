Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The investigation reveals a significant transparency failure within Chihuahua’s Judicial Power, as key personnel like Victoria de León Jáquez lack verifiable public records. Despite extensive searches across official directories, curricula, and financial archives, essential data regarding her employment history and official status remains absent. This absence prevents public verification of who manages public resources or exercises authority, undermining the fundamental principle of accountability that open data aims to enforce. The limited evidence found consists only of a single, seemingly validated expense report for per diems from 2022. The inability to confirm her entry date or prior roles within the judiciary highlights a systemic opacity. When government entities cannot provide basic workforce data, citizens cannot effectively monitor potential irregularities or verify the legitimacy of public servants, creating a dangerous gap in democratic oversight. This case is critically relevant to open data because it illustrates the consequences of incomplete or non-existent digital transparency. True openness requires not just the publication of isolated financial transactions but comprehensive, accessible records of personnel and their functions. Without such robust data availability, the potential for misuse of public funds and authority remains unchecked, emphasizing the urgent need for comprehensive, standardized, and searchable government datasets.
