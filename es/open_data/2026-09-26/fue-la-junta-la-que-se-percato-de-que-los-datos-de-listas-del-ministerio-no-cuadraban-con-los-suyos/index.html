Fue la Junta la que se percató de que los datos de listas del Ministerio no cuadraban con los suyos

The article highlights data integrity challenges within public health reporting, emphasizing that even minor statistical discrepancies in regional submissions can significantly alter national health rankings and averages. Castilla-La Mancha proactively corrected its waiting list figures, revealing that initial reports underestimated patient volumes and delay times. This self-correction shifted the region’s ranking and slightly increased the national average waiting time, demonstrating the fragility of aggregated open data. This case is crucial for open data as it illustrates the necessity for rigorous validation processes and transparency mechanisms. It underscores how accurate, publicly accessible administrative data is vital for maintaining trust in government performance metrics and ensuring reliable decision-making.
