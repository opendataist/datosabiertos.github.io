El intercambio de datos sobre el COVID-19 en China está lejos de ser "abierto y transparente"

The article highlights a stark contrast between Beijing’s official claims of data transparency and the overwhelming evidence of systematic underreporting of COVID-19 cases and deaths. International health authorities, including the WHO, have repeatedly criticized China for using narrow definitions of mortality and withholding critical epidemiological data. This lack of reliable statistics has led many nations to impose travel restrictions, citing the inability to accurately assess the global risk posed by the virus’s spread within China’s borders. The implications for global health security are significant, as accurate disease tracking relies entirely on open, standardized data sharing. When a country as populous as China limits the scope of reported deaths and restricts viral sequencing, it hinders the international community’s ability to monitor variants and model future outbreaks. Consequently, independent analysts are forced to rely on estimates rather than facts, creating uncertainty and potential blind spots in the global effort to understand the pandemic’s trajectory and impact. This situation is highly relevant to open data principles because it demonstrates how state-level opacity directly undermines global public health responses. Open data requires not just the existence of information, but its accessibility, completeness, and adherence to international standards. By restricting data flow, China exemplifies the dangers of non-compliance, showing that without transparent, real-time data sharing, the world cannot effectively coordinate risk assessments or protect public health, regardless of a government’s stated intentions.
