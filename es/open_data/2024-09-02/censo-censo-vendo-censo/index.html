Censo, censo…vendo censo

The publication of Bolivia’s 2024 census data has triggered widespread skepticism regarding the credibility of national statistical institutions. Critics argue that the reported population figures significantly contradict previous projections and vital records, such as birth and death statistics. This discrepancy suggests that the official numbers do not reflect the country’s demographic reality, fostering deep public distrust and highlighting a systemic lack of transparency in state institutions. A critical analysis reveals substantial inconsistencies between the census results and administrative data, particularly concerning population growth rates and internal migration. Key regions, especially Santa Cruz, show population figures that defy known migration patterns and vital statistics, implying potential undercounting or methodological flaws. These analytical gaps raise serious questions about the accuracy of the data, suggesting that the actual population growth is higher than officially reported and that specific departments may have been unfairly disadvantaged by the results. This lack of data integrity is highly relevant to open data because it undermines the foundational trust required for civic engagement and policy-making. When official statistics are perceived as politically motivated or unreliable, it hinders evidence-based decision-making and exacerbates social conflict. Ultimately, the controversy illustrates how opaque or questionable data management can erode institutional legitimacy, emphasizing the urgent need for transparent, verifiable, and methodologically sound statistical practices to restore public confidence.
