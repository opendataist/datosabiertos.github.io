Castilla-La Mancha es la quinta comunidad autónoma más endeudada con 15.688 millones de euros, según el Banco de España

Public debt has reached historical highs in absolute terms, driven by pandemic aftermath and energy crises, yet the debt-to-GDP ratio shows signs of stabilization and moderation. The government anticipates that sustained economic growth will allow for a gradual reduction of this ratio in the coming years, effectively accelerating targets to bring public finances under control without immediate austerity measures. This trajectory suggests a shift from emergency spending towards sustainable fiscal consolidation, prioritizing macroeconomic stability over rapid debt elimination. The composition of this debt reveals significant disparities across administrative levels, with the central government holding the vast majority of the burden, while regional and local debts remain comparatively lower. Although all regions saw an increase in absolute debt, those with larger populations naturally concentrate more liabilities. However, when adjusted for regional economic output, certain autonomous communities exhibit higher leverage, indicating varying degrees of fiscal health and resilience against external economic shocks. This report is relevant to open data because it underscores the importance of transparent, granular public financial datasets. By disaggregating debt figures by administration level and region, such data enables policymakers and citizens to monitor fiscal responsibility, identify structural imbalances, and evaluate the effectiveness of economic policies. Openly accessible and detailed government debt metrics are essential for fostering accountability, facilitating independent analysis, and ensuring that fiscal strategies are evidence-based and publicly scrutinized.
