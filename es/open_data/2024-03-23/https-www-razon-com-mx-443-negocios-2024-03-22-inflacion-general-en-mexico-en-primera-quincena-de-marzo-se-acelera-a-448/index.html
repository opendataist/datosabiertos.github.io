Mexico’s general annual inflation rose to 4.48 percent in the first half of March, marking a slight acceleration from the previous period and reaching the highest annual rate since early February. This increase was primarily driven by underlying inflation pressures, specifically in housing and other services, rather than merchandise prices. The data confirms that inflationary risks persist, with core inflation accelerating for a second consecutive period, suggesting that price stability remains an ongoing challenge despite the rise being partly attributed to seasonal effects ahead of Holy Week. Notably, merchandise inflation fell below four percent, a significant milestone after oscillating above this threshold for nearly two years. However, essential services such as air transport and tourism packages experienced substantial price hikes, indicating complex, segmented market dynamics. This divergence highlights that while goods have seen relative deflation, service costs continue to exert upward pressure on consumer prices, complicating efforts to achieve broad economic stability. This information is relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of transparent, high-frequency statistical monitoring for effective policy-making. As institutions like the INEGI publish detailed indices, open data ecosystems allow researchers and citizens to analyze granular trends beyond headline figures. Understanding these nuanced shifts in underlying versus non-underlying inflation ensures that public discourse and economic decisions are based on accurate, accessible, and comprehensive digital records of national performance.
