Bolivia realizó 11 censos desde su fundación y su población creció 10 veces

Bolivia’s demographic history, chronicled through twelve censuses since 1825, reveals a complex interplay between population dynamics and the nation’s political and economic stability. Early records indicate significant fluctuations tied to territorial changes and warfare, while later decades demonstrate steady growth driven by urbanization. However, periods of severe economic crisis, such as the hyperinflation of the 1980s, directly hindered data collection efforts, highlighting how macroeconomic challenges can disrupt fundamental state functions and the continuity of essential public records. The evolution of these censuses illustrates the transition toward modern statistical standards, particularly after 1992, which established a decennial routine. These consistent efforts have provided invaluable data for understanding social structures and guiding policy decisions. By offering a detailed panoramic view of diversity and growth, the historical sequence of censuses serves as a critical tool for analyzing long-term trends, allowing the government to adapt to shifting demographic realities and address the needs of an evolving society. The upcoming national census is particularly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible demographic information for informed governance. As this dataset approaches, its public availability will empower researchers, developers, and citizens to verify claims, analyze resource distribution, and hold institutions accountable. Reliable open data on population and housing forms the backbone of democratic oversight, ensuring that planning remains evidence-based and that public services align with the actual composition of the citizenry rather than opaque estimates.
