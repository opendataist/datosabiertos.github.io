AMLO no Quiere Eliminar la Transparencia, el Acceso a la Información y Protección de Datos: Ana Laura Gómez

The article discusses a debate in Mexico regarding the future of transparency institutions. Legislator Ana Laura Gómez Calzada argues that President Andrés Manuel López Obrador aims to strengthen anti-corruption efforts by transferring oversight responsibilities to the Supreme Audit Office or an Anti-Corruption Prosecutor’s Office, rather than eliminating transparency. This proposal responds to concerns that the current Institute for Transparency (INAI) has become ineffective due to leadership vacancies. The core argument suggests that robust financial auditing by specialized agencies would prevent superficial compliance and enhance accountability. By shifting these powers, the government seeks to address perceived inefficiencies in the existing framework. This structural change is framed not as a reduction in public access to information, but as a method to ensure more rigorous and effective scrutiny of public spending and governance. This situation is relevant to open data because it highlights the tension between administrative transparency mechanisms and data-driven accountability. The debate underscores how institutional design directly impacts the quality and usability of public information. For open data advocates, the key implication is that effective transparency requires not just the release of data, but robust, independent mechanisms to analyze and act upon it, ensuring that information leads to tangible anti-corruption results.
