The article highlights significant delays by the Itait in resolving transparency complaints, demonstrating how bureaucratic inertia undermines citizens' fundamental right to access public information. When an institution fails to respond within legal deadlines, it not only frustrates individuals seeking accountability but also erodes trust in public transparency mechanisms. The situation escalated to the national INAI, which intervened to enforce compliance with statutory timeframes. This external pressure underscores the necessity of robust oversight bodies to ensure that local transparency institutes adhere to their legal obligations and do not operate with impunity. This case is crucial for open data because it illustrates the real-world impact of opaque governance. Effective open data initiatives rely on timely responses to information requests; without accountability and strict adherence to deadlines, the potential for transparency and civic engagement remains severely limited.
