El Estado asistió a más de mil víctimas de violencia de género por mes

The article highlights a significant strengthening of local gender institutionalities across Argentina, which has facilitated the tracking and assistance of gender-based violence cases. This decentralization of efforts explains regional disparities in assistance rates, demonstrating that robust provincial and municipal frameworks are crucial for effective case management. The availability of this data underscores the importance of transparent, localized governance in addressing social issues. Open data plays a pivotal role in this context by making detailed statistics publicly accessible through interactive dashboards. This transparency allows for a nuanced understanding of violence patterns, such as the predominance of psychological aggression and the involvement of ex-partners. By providing granular data on risk levels and perpetrator characteristics, open data enables policymakers and researchers to identify trends and vulnerabilities that might otherwise remain hidden, fostering evidence-based decision-making. Despite improved accessibility, the data reveals systemic failures, particularly in judicial delays and non-compliance with protection orders. The relevance to open data lies in its ability to expose these gaps, highlighting where institutional support falls short. Ultimately, the article argues that while executive branch programs provide immediate aid, true progress requires judicial reform, a goal supported by the clear, open evidence of current systemic inefficiencies.
