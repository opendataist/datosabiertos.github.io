Crece percepción de inseguridad en México en segundo trimestre de 2023

Mexico’s perception of urban insecurity remains persistently high, affecting more than six out of ten adults despite a measurable decline in actual homicide rates. This divergence highlights a critical gap between statistical trends in violent crime and the public’s lived experience, suggesting that visibility of organized crime attacks significantly influences subjective safety feelings even when underlying violence metrics improve. A striking gender disparity underscores the crisis, as women report feeling unsafe at substantially higher rates than men. This widening gap indicates that insecurity is not uniformly distributed but disproportionately impacts female populations, reflecting deeper societal vulnerabilities and varying exposure to fear-inducing phenomena across different demographics within the country. This data is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how combining official crime statistics with subjective survey metrics provides a more holistic view of public security. Transparently publishing both objective violence indices and perception surveys allows for better policy design, enabling governments to address not just the reduction of crime, but also the restoration of public trust and social cohesion through data-driven transparency.
