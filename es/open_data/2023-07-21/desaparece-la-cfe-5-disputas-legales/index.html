‘Desaparece’ la CFE 5 disputas legales

The article highlights a significant discrepancy in how the Federal Electricity Commission (CFE) discloses its international arbitration cases. While annual financial reports claim sixteen ongoing processes, transparency requests reveal only eleven recognized cases, raising concerns about inconsistent public reporting. CFE justifies this limited disclosure by citing confidentiality, arguing that revealing specific causes or financial amounts could harm its legal defense strategy. By withholding details, the state-owned enterprise aims to prevent competitors from gaining strategic advantages or using public data to initiate new claims, thereby maintaining control over the arbitration narrative. This situation is crucial for open_data advocates because it demonstrates how public entities use legal secrecy as a barrier to transparency. The lack of accessible data on high-stakes government disputes undermines accountability, making it difficult for citizens and researchers to assess the true financial and legal risks associated with state administration.
