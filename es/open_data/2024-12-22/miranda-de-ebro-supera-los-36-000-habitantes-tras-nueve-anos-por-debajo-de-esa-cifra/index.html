Miranda de Ebro supera los 36.000 habitantes tras nueve años por debajo de esa cifra

The article highlights Miranda de Ebro’s demographic recovery, surpassing 36,000 inhabitants for the first time since 2015. This growth signals a potential economic and social resurgence driven by the arrival of new workers, particularly in the technology and data sectors. The increasing population is fueling demands for expanded public infrastructure, such as additional secondary schools, to accommodate rising enrollment and families. A significant portion of this growth stems from international migration, primarily from Iberoamerica and Morocco. While this influx underscores the city’s hospitality and ability to attract talent, it exacerbates housing shortages and rising costs. The trend also reflects a generational shift where retirees settle in the city and new labor replaces departing workers, reinforcing the necessity of external migration to sustain local businesses. This data is relevant to open data initiatives because municipal census figures often diverge from official national statistics, as seen when local registries showed higher numbers than the INE. Such discrepancies highlight the critical need for transparent, accessible, and harmonized data sources. Accurate demographic tracking is essential for urban planning, resource allocation, and addressing the social challenges, such as housing affordability, that accompany rapid population growth.
