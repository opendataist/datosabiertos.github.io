Avances en pobreza, pero se mantiene la vulnerabilidad

The article highlights the shift from a narrow monetary view of poverty to a multidimensional perspective, revealing that significant portions of Paraguay’s population remain deprived despite having sufficient income. This broader approach exposes hidden vulnerabilities, particularly in rural areas, and underscores that reducing poverty requires more than just covering basic services; it demands substantial improvements in the quality of education, health, and clean water to build genuine human capital. Relevance to open_data lies in the necessity of granular, high-quality datasets to track these intersecting deprivations and monitor progress over time. By identifying specific gaps, such as inadequate waste management or reliance on biomass for cooking, policymakers can design targeted interventions. The data reveals that a large segment of the population, though currently out of poverty, remains highly vulnerable to economic shocks, emphasizing the need for continuous, reliable monitoring to prevent relapse and ensure long-term social stability. The conclusion warns that while poverty rates have decreased, long-term structural barriers persist. Without addressing issues like pension exclusion and the rapid aging of the population, the country risks trapping individuals in future economic dependence. Therefore, sustainable development requires breaking the cycle of vulnerability through comprehensive, data-informed strategies that prioritize resilience and quality of life over mere statistical reduction of poverty metrics.
