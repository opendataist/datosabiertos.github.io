El número de billetes de 500 euros sigue cayendo y se mantiene en mínimos, con 10,4 millones de unidades

The article highlights the ongoing reduction of 500-euro banknotes in circulation, reflecting the European Central Bank’s strategic decision to cease their production to combat illicit financial activities. Although these notes remain legal tender and can still be exchanged indefinitely, their decreasing presence signals a broader shift toward transparency in cash transactions. This trend underscores the importance of monitoring high-denomination currency flows to understand societal trust in physical money and potential vulnerabilities in financial systems. Concurrently, significant discrepancies exist between the distribution and withdrawal of smaller denominations, particularly 100, 20, and 10 euros. This gap is largely attributed to tourism, as foreign visitors bring substantial amounts of cash into the country, which eventually returns to central banks through commercial entities. Such dynamics reveal how external factors like tourism heavily influence domestic cash liquidity and circulation patterns, complicating accurate monetary supply assessments. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates how public financial statistics can expose hidden economic trends, such as the impact of tourism on national liquidity. By analyzing these open datasets, researchers and policymakers can better understand currency flow inefficiencies and inform decisions regarding digital payment adoption and anti-money laundering strategies.
