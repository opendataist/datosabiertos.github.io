¿Quiénes son los que menos ganan en el país?

The ENIGH data reveals persistent economic disparities in Mexico, where indigenous people, women, and individuals with disabilities earn significantly less than the national average. This inequality is not merely statistical but reflects deep structural barriers that prevent vulnerable populations from achieving financial stability. Understanding these trends is crucial for open data initiatives, as it highlights the urgent need for accessible, disaggregated datasets to monitor social equity and inform inclusive policy-making. Gender and disability further compound these income gaps, with women and those facing functional difficulties experiencing the most severe financial exclusion. The correlation between linguistic identity and earnings underscores how cultural marginalization translates into economic disadvantage. These findings emphasize that transparency in public statistics must go beyond aggregate figures to expose intersectional inequalities, ensuring that marginalized voices are accurately represented in national economic narratives. Ultimately, this report demonstrates how robust open data frameworks can drive social accountability and targeted intervention. By making detailed income and expenditure information publicly available, researchers and policymakers can identify specific regions and groups requiring support. This transparency fosters a more equitable society, allowing civil society to advocate for reforms that address the root causes of poverty and promote inclusive economic growth for all citizens.
