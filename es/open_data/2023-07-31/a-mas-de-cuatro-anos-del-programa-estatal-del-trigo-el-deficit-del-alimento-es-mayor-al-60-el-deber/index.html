A más de cuatro años del programa estatal del trigo, el déficit del alimento es mayor al 60% | El Deber

The article highlights that Bolivia’s state-led program to boost wheat production has failed to address the significant deficit in the domestic market, leaving the country heavily dependent on imports from Argentina. This reliance undermines national food sovereignty, as external sources and contraband cover a large portion of annual demand. Despite substantial government investment in seed subsidies and insurance, local production remains insufficient to meet the needs of the population, creating a persistent vulnerability in the food supply chain. To overcome this challenge, producers are urging the government to approve the biotechnological event HB4, a drought-tolerant transgenic wheat variety. Since climate-induced droughts are the primary obstacle to increasing yields, this technology is presented as the most viable solution to secure harvests and expand cultivation areas. Although the application was submitted over a year ago and has been approved in neighboring countries, the lack of a response from Bolivian authorities delays a critical step toward reducing import dependency and stabilizing local supply. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible agricultural statistics to inform public policy and technological adoption. Reliable data on production levels, import flows, and the socio-economic impact of droughts are essential for evaluating the effectiveness of current programs and justifying investments in innovation. Without open and standardized data systems, stakeholders cannot accurately assess food security risks or measure the potential impact of biotechnological interventions on national sovereignty.
