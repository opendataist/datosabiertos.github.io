Alcanza economía informal participación récord en 2022

The informal economy reached its highest recorded contribution to Mexico's GDP in 2022, marking a significant shift in the national economic structure. This growth highlights a persistent reliance on unregistered micronebusinesses and unprotected labor, suggesting that formal employment sectors are struggling to absorb the workforce or generate proportional value. The data reveals a structural imbalance where informal workers contribute significantly to output yet lack legal protections, indicating deep-seated vulnerabilities in labor relations and economic security. This trend underscores critical challenges for social equity and fiscal planning, as a substantial portion of the population operates without formal safety nets. The shift in sectoral participation, particularly in retail and construction, reflects changing dynamics within the informal market. These fluctuations suggest that economic policies must address not just employment rates, but the quality and legality of work to ensure sustainable development and fair income distribution across all demographics. The article is highly relevant to open_data initiatives because it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, accessible statistical records for tracking informal economic activities. Reliable data is essential for policymakers to design effective interventions that transition workers into the formal economy. By making such granular insights publicly available, governments and researchers can better understand labor market complexities, fostering evidence-based decision-making that promotes social inclusion and economic stability.
