Tres comisionados se postulan a la presidencia del órgano de Transparencia de México - Mundo - ABC Color

The Mexican National Institute for Transparency (INAI) has announced three commissioners as candidates for its presidency for the 2023-2026 term. This leadership transition occurs amidst significant political tension, as the current president is stepping down after her full term, while the government has refused to appoint pending commissioners, rendering the institute legally operational only through judicial injunctions. The election process requires candidates to submit work plans and secure a minimum vote threshold, with potential multiple rounds of voting to ensure a decisive outcome. This internal procedural rigor contrasts sharply with the external pressure from the executive branch, which has publicly criticized the institution as useless and challenged its financial and structural integrity, highlighting a deep crisis in Mexico’s democratic oversight mechanisms. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the fragility of institutional independence required to guarantee information access. When political actors undermine transparency bodies, the fundamental right to public information is at risk, making the stability of these organizations crucial for maintaining accountability and ensuring citizens can effectively exercise their open data rights.
