¿Qué países consumen más agua? Esto dice el informe de Datacommons

The article highlights that rising global population has intensified pressure on natural resources, particularly water, exposing critical vulnerabilities in regions like Latin America. It reveals alarming consumption patterns where countries with abundant water supplies, such as Colombia and Peru, exhibit some of the highest per capita usage rates globally, surpassing even water-stressed nations like Mexico. This disparity underscores a systemic misalignment between resource availability and human consumption habits, indicating that abundance does not guarantee sustainability. The narrative emphasizes that water is not an unlimited asset but faces severe risks from environmental and climate pressures. Most Latin American countries are identified as facing medium vulnerability to droughts, signaling an urgent need for improved management strategies. The data demonstrates that high consumption levels persist despite these growing threats, suggesting that current practices are insufficient to protect vital water reserves against future scarcity. This content is highly relevant to open data because it relies on accessible, cross-referenced datasets from organizations like the OECD, WRI, and Statista. By aggregating and visualizing such diverse global statistics, open data platforms enable transparency in tracking resource usage and environmental risks. This approach facilitates better public understanding and supports evidence-based policy-making for sustainable water management worldwide.
