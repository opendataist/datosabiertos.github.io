Crece percepción de inseguridad en México en segundo trimestre

Public perception of insecurity in Mexico has risen significantly, with a majority of citizens now viewing their cities as unsafe. This data highlights a growing gender disparity, as women report feeling considerably more vulnerable than men. These subjective experiences contrast with official statistics showing a recent decline in homicide rates, suggesting that objective safety metrics do not fully align with the population's lived reality. This discrepancy is crucial for open data initiatives because it underscores the importance of combining diverse datasets to understand complex social issues. Relying solely on crime statistics provides an incomplete picture, whereas integrating survey data reveals the emotional and societal impact of violence. Open data frameworks must therefore accommodate both quantitative records and qualitative public sentiment for accurate analysis. Ultimately, transparent access to these varied data sources allows policymakers and researchers to address the root causes of perceived insecurity. It enables a more holistic approach to public safety strategies that consider citizen trust and well-being. By making such comprehensive information publicly available, society can foster informed debates and develop more effective interventions to restore confidence in urban environments.
