Registran más heridos por mordeduras de perros que por armas en Coahuila

Hospital admissions for dog bites have surpassed those for gunshot or sharp-object injuries, marking a significant shift in public health statistics. This trend highlights the growing burden on healthcare systems, as bite-related cases increased substantially over two years while weapon-related injuries declined. The data reveals that animal aggression is now a more frequent cause of hospitalization than firearms, underscoring the urgent need to address this emerging safety concern. The surge in incidents is closely linked to the rising number of abandoned street dogs, with reports indicating a dramatic increase in neglect. Animal welfare organizations attribute this to a lack of public awareness regarding the responsibilities of pet ownership. The concentration of stray animals in specific areas exacerbates the risk of attacks, creating a cycle of abandonment and aggression that affects community safety and animal well-being. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how epidemiological bulletins can expose hidden social crises through comparative analysis. By making health metrics accessible, such data allows researchers and policymakers to identify correlations between animal abandonment rates and public health outcomes. Transparent reporting enables targeted interventions, promoting better resource allocation for sterilization campaigns and adoption programs to mitigate future risks.
