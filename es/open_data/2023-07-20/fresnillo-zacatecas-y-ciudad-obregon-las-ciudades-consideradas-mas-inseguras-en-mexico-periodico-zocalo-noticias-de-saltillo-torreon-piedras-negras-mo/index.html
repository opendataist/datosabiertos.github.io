Fresnillo, Zacatecas y Ciudad Obregón, las ciudades consideradas más inseguras en México | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

Recent public security surveys highlight a significant decline in citizen safety perceptions across major Mexican cities, with Fresnillo, Zacatecas, and Ciudad Obregón identified as the most dangerous. This widespread insecurity forces residents to drastically alter their daily routines, often restricting children’s mobility and avoiding public spaces, which underscores the urgent need for data-driven policy interventions to address these safety crises. The data reveals that ATMs and public transportation are perceived as the most hazardous areas, reflecting broader societal fears about crime and violence. Citizens report frequent encounters with theft, drug use, and vandalism, leading to a pervasive sense of vulnerability. Understanding these patterns is crucial for open data initiatives, as transparent access to such granular insights allows communities and developers to build tools that enhance personal safety and awareness. Consequently, the survey notes a distinct preference for the Navy’s performance in crime prevention, suggesting that effective security strategies can be measured and replicated. The relevance of this information to open data lies in its potential to empower civic tech solutions. By making these safety metrics publicly accessible, stakeholders can create applications that help citizens navigate urban environments more securely, fostering a more informed and resilient society through transparency.
