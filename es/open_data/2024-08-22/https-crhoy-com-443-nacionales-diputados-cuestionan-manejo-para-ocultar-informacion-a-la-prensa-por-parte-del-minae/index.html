Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The revelation that officials within Costa Rica’s Ministry of Environment actively instructed staff to manage and restrict information shared with the press highlights a troubling disregard for transparency in public administration. This directive, aimed at downplaying a controversial water park project, demonstrates how governmental entities may prioritize political convenience over accountability. By treating public data as a controlled asset rather than a right of citizens, the government undermines the fundamental principle that information owned by the state belongs to the public. Opposition legislators have strongly condemned this behavior, arguing that such secrecy prevents effective political oversight and hides the true use of public resources. The incident exposes a systemic issue where internal communications are manipulated to obscure decision-making processes, potentially favoring specific political interests over community welfare. This lack of openness raises serious concerns about whether public funds are being utilized for genuine environmental benefit or as instruments of personal or partisan agendas, thereby eroding trust in democratic institutions. This case is critically relevant to open data because it illustrates the gap between formal data availability and actual government openness. Merely having data does not constitute transparency if institutional culture encourages its suppression or manipulation. For open data initiatives to succeed, governments must commit to proactive disclosure and resist the impulse to gatekeep information. Without such cultural shifts, digital transparency tools remain ineffective, as the underlying barriers of secrecy and political control persist.
