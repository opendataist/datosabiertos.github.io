Pide INAI a Bienestar transparentar recursos de programas sociales

The National Institute for Transparency (INAI) has ruled that the Ministry of Welfare must disclose detailed budgetary spending data for programs targeting children and adolescents. This decision corrects the Ministry’s initial refusal, which claimed the information did not exist or belonged to other units. The INAI emphasized that the Ministry failed to conduct a thorough search across all relevant administrative bodies, as required by law, and confirmed that the necessary records must exist within their archives given their internal budgeting protocols. This case underscores the critical link between increased public funding and the obligation for transparency. As government programs expand significantly, especially those classified as constitutional rights, there is an imperative for rigorous accountability mechanisms. The ruling reinforces that the delivery of social benefits without intermediaries cannot come at the cost of obscuring financial flows; instead, transparency must accompany every stage of resource allocation to ensure public trust and proper oversight. The relevance to open data lies in establishing a precedent that public entities cannot evade disclosure by citing internal bureaucratic fragmentation. By mandating a comprehensive search across specific departments, the INAI ensures that granular financial data is accessible, preventing silos that hinder public scrutiny. This approach promotes a culture where budgetary details are not just archived but actively made available for societal verification, strengthening the integrity of open government initiatives and ensuring that taxpayer money is tracked effectively through accessible, structured data.
