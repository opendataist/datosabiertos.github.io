Población ocupada en Lima sumó 5.2 millones de personas y creció 4.5%

The latest labor market data for Lima Metropolitan reveals a nuanced recovery characterized by significant demographic and sectoral shifts. While the overall employed population has grown compared to pre-pandemic levels, this positive trend masks a concerning rise in underemployment, particularly when measured against 2019 baselines. The market shows resilience in formal job creation, yet the quality of employment remains a critical challenge for many workers seeking stability in the current economic climate. Key drivers of this employment growth include substantial gains in the services and commerce sectors, alongside a notable surge in participation among women and individuals with higher education levels. Conversely, traditional industries like manufacturing and construction have contracted, and job losses are evident among younger demographics and those with lower educational attainment. These divergent trends highlight a labor market undergoing structural transformation, where specific sectors and educational profiles are disproportionately benefiting from current economic conditions. This information is vital for the open_data community as it underscores the importance of accessible, granular statistical indicators in tracking socioeconomic dynamics. Transparent data allows researchers and policymakers to move beyond aggregate headlines, identifying specific vulnerabilities such as rising underemployment and sectoral disparities. By making such detailed metrics available, stakeholders can better analyze the effectiveness of employment policies and ensure that data-driven decisions address the complex realities of workforce development and equity.
