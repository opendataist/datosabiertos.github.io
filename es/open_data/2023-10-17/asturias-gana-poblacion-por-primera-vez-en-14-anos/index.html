Asturias gana población por primera vez en 14 años

Recent provisional data suggests that Asturias may experience a demographic turning point in 2023, potentially halting years of population decline with a net gain of nearly 3,000 residents. This shift is attributed primarily to intense internal migration, which offsets a deeply negative natural growth rate where deaths significantly outnumber births. If confirmed by official statistics, this would mark the first increase since the peak around 2009, challenging previous projections that anticipated the region falling below one million inhabitants. However, this recovery is highly uneven across the region. While a few specific municipalities see genuine growth driven by migration, the majority of towns suffer from negative real population growth because their migratory gains are insufficient to counterbalance natural loss. Furthermore, historical demographic maps reveal that most parishes have been in decline for decades, highlighting a long-term structural challenge despite the temporary statistical uptick. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how accessible government statistics from the National Institute of Statistics, combined with local institutional analysis, can reveal nuanced social trends. It illustrates the importance of transparent, granular data in understanding complex phenomena like migration patterns and rural depopulation, allowing researchers and citizens to visualize and interpret the real impact of demographic changes on local communities.
