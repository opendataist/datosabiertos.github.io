Recuerdan al INE que puede tener datos para escaños 3 meses después del censo

The article highlights the critical timeline for obtaining updated population data from Bolivia’s National Statistics Institute to ensure a fair redistribution of parliamentary seats for the upcoming elections. Experts emphasize that within two or three months after the census, the necessary demographic details—such as total population, geographic distribution, and gender ratios—will be available to support the electoral tribunal’s work. This information is essential not only for political representation but also for equitable tax sharing, making precise and timely data delivery a matter of institutional integrity rather than mere administrative procedure. The reliability of this process depends heavily on political will and the INE’s commitment to completing its cartographic updates across the remaining departments. While officials confirm high completion rates for preliminary work, the final submission deadline is strict, requiring the electoral body to receive the data by September to prepare for the 2025 general elections. The focus remains on ensuring that the final numbers accurately reflect the current reality, avoiding delays that could compromise the democratic redistribution of power. This case is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and timely public statistics for maintaining democratic accountability. Open data principles require that such vital information be available in a usable format to citizens and institutions alike, enabling independent verification and informed public debate. Ensuring that census results are open and verified helps prevent manipulation, fosters trust in public institutions, and guarantees that resource allocation and representation reflect the true needs and demographics of the population.
