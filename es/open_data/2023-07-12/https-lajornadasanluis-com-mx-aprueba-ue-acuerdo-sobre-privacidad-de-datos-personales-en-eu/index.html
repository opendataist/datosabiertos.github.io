Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The European Union has approved a new data privacy framework with the United States, declaring that US protections are adequate for personal data flows. This agreement allows companies to transfer data from Europe to the US freely, eliminating the need for additional security measures and resolving long-standing uncertainty for major technology firms operating across the Atlantic. However, privacy activist Max Schrems strongly rejects this arrangement, arguing that fundamental rights issues remain unaddressed. He intends to challenge the deal in the highest EU court, highlighting the persistent tension between US surveillance practices and European privacy standards. This dispute reflects the ongoing difficulty in aligning the US’s flexible regulations with the EU’s strict data protection requirements. This development is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical role of legal frameworks in determining how information can be shared globally. While facilitating data movement is essential for innovation and transparency, it must be balanced against privacy concerns. Understanding these regulatory dynamics is crucial for policymakers and practitioners aiming to promote open data initiatives that respect user rights while enabling cross-border information exchange.
