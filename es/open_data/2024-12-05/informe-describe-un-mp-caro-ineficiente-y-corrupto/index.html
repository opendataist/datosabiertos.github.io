Informe describe un MP “caro, ineficiente y corrupto”

The report concludes that the Guatemalan Public Ministry under Consuelo Porras has suffered severe regression, characterized by systemic inefficiency, mismanagement of resources, and political interference that undermines the rule of law. Rather than delivering justice, the institution has become marked by an inability to address serious crimes, leading to widespread impunity and a significant erosion of public trust in the justice system. This analysis is critically relevant to open_data because it demonstrates how publicly available statistical information can be leveraged by civil society to hold government institutions accountable. By critically examining official data rather than accepting institutional narratives at face value, human rights organizations exposed a stark discrepancy between reported achievements and actual outcomes, proving that transparency is a powerful tool for detecting administrative failure and corruption. Key findings indicate that despite substantial budget increases, effective case resolutions have plummeted while costs have skyrocketed, with the vast majority of processes ending in dismissal rather than justice. This disconnect highlights the danger of relying solely on self-reported metrics and underscores the necessity of independent verification of open data to expose institutional capture and protect democratic integrity from those who claim to serve the public interest.
