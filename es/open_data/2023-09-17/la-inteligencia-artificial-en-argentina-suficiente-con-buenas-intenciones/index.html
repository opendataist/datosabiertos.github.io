La Inteligencia Artificial en Argentina: ¿suficiente con buenas intenciones?

Argentina has initiated a regulatory framework for artificial intelligence through Resolution 161/2023, which establishes a program dedicated to transparency and the protection of personal data. By aligning with international standards and embedding principles such as fairness, privacy, and human oversight, the state aims to balance technological advancement with citizen rights. This move signals a commitment to designing robust, safe, and impartial AI systems within both public and private sectors. However, the article argues that intention alone is insufficient to address the ethical and security challenges posed by AI’s rapid integration into daily life and economic structures. There is a critical gap between regulatory goals and practical implementation, particularly regarding data protection management. Without robust operational programs and proactive governance, well-meaning guidelines risk becoming empty shells that fail to prevent data breaches or mitigate algorithmic biases in real-world scenarios. This discussion is vital to open data advocates because it highlights the tension between making data accessible and ensuring its secure, ethical use. True open data governance requires more than transparency declarations; it demands concrete investments in management and technical capacity. For data ecosystems to thrive, regulations must evolve from aspirational statements to enforceable practices that secure data integrity while fostering innovation and accountability.
