Más del 70 por ciento de población de Guatemala en economía informal - Noticias Prensa Latina

The article highlights that only a small fraction of the population works in the formal sector, with informal labor dominating among those over 24, particularly in rural areas and agriculture. This prevalence indicates that informal work is not an isolated choice but a symptom of insufficient quality employment, creating a vicious cycle of low consumption and reinforcing social inequalities. The main conclusion emphasizes that combating informality requires generating better jobs through economic diversification and competitiveness rather than punitive measures. By fostering an environment that supports fair wages and diverse opportunities, governments can address the root causes of informal labor and its associated migratory pressures, ultimately strengthening the local economy’s internal dynamics. This content is vital for open data because it demonstrates how granular labor statistics can drive evidence-based policy making. Transparent access to such detailed socioeconomic indicators allows analysts to visualize disparities, monitor the effectiveness of fiscal strategies, and advocate for structural reforms. Open data transforms these raw figures into actionable insights for fostering inclusive growth and reducing poverty.
