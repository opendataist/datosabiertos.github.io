Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The publication of Spain’s first comprehensive census of residential centers highlights the critical importance of accurate, transparent open data for public policy and social welfare. By aggregating information on facilities for the elderly and people with disabilities, the report aims to provide a reliable baseline for managing a sector serving hundreds of thousands of citizens. However, significant inconsistencies in the data undermine its utility as a tool for informed decision-making, revealing gaps between claimed and actual facility counts. The report faces serious scrutiny regarding its methodological rigor and internal contradictions. Critics point out unexplained exclusions from the initial database, discrepancies in total center counts over time, and implausible figures where public-funded beds exceed total available capacity. These anomalies suggest potential flaws in data collection and processing, raising questions about the reliability of the official statistics used to allocate resources and monitor service delivery across different autonomous communities. Most critically, the census fails to adequately address the gap between available beds and the needs of those with severe dependency. While the data suggests a large number of vacant facilities, thousands of individuals remain on waiting lists, creating a misleading narrative of sufficient coverage. This discrepancy emphasizes the need for open data standards that not only count resources but also accurately reflect access barriers and unmet social needs. Ensuring data integrity is essential to bridge the gap between statistical reporting and the reality faced by vulnerable populations, allowing for more effective and equitable policy interventions.
