Se divorcian 33 de cada 100 matrimonios reporta INEGI; las mujeres a los 36 años y los hombre a los 38, en promedio - Noticaribe

The article reveals a significant rise in Mexico’s divorce rate over the past twelve years, nearly doubling to approximately 33 divorces per 100 marriages. This trend highlights shifting family dynamics and suggests that marital stability is becoming increasingly precarious, with the average marriage lasting only 17.7 years before dissolution. Such demographic shifts underscore the importance of tracking societal changes in relationship structures. Furthermore, the data indicates that while the vast majority of unions remain heterosexual, a small but notable percentage involves same-sex couples. The report also correlates marital status with age, showing higher marriage rates among adults and older populations compared to youth. These statistics provide a comprehensive snapshot of how civil status evolves across different generations, reflecting broader cultural and economic influences on union formation. This information is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how official, transparent statistics enable public understanding of social trends. By making such detailed demographic data freely available, governments empower researchers, policymakers, and citizens to analyze societal health. Accessible data fosters evidence-based discussions on family policy, social services, and the evolving nature of marriage in modern society.
