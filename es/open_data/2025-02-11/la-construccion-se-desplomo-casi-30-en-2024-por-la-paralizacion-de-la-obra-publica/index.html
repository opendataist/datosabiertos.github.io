La construcción se desplomó casi 30% en 2024 por la paralización de la obra pública

The construction sector in Argentina experienced a severe contraction during the first year of Javier Milei’s administration, driven by public works suspensions and deepening economic recession. This decline, described as the sharpest since the end of the convertibility regime, highlights the immediate and profound impact of austerity measures on industrial activity and employment. Employment in the sector plummeted due to the halt in government projects, while construction costs surged significantly in nominal terms, reaching levels not seen in over a decade. Although the sector shows tentative signs of stabilization through consecutive monthly increases, the industry leaders argue that the anticipated rapid economic rebound is not imminent. Consequently, the Chamber of Construction has urged the government to implement an alternative policy plan, emphasizing that without immediate investment and structural stability, improving living standards remains impossible. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how official statistical indicators, such as those from INDEC, serve as critical evidence for validating economic narratives and holding policymakers accountable. By transparently tracking metrics like activity indices and employment rates, open data frameworks enable independent analysts and civil society to verify claims about economic health, fostering informed public debate on the real-world consequences of fiscal adjustments and the effectiveness of government recovery promises.
