El año que dejamos atrás la crisis de la covid y nos olvidamos de las mascarillas

The article analyzes the post-pandemic phase in Spain, marking the transition from an emergency state to managed endemic presence. It highlights that while global and national health crises have subsided, the virus remains a seasonal threat driven by human behavior rather than viral mutation. The persistence of outbreaks during high-mobility periods underscores that public health strategies must adapt to social patterns, ensuring that risk management focuses on vulnerable populations rather than general restrictions. A critical finding is the independent evaluation of the Spanish National Health System’s pandemic response, which identifies significant structural flaws alongside successes like vaccination campaigns. The report emphasizes the urgent need for better coordination and specific protocols for elderly care facilities. This retrospective analysis serves as a foundational lesson for future health emergencies, advocating for proactive integration of social and health services to prevent recurring systemic failures. This content is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates how transparent, independent analysis of public health metrics can drive policy reform. By making evaluation reports and epidemiological trends accessible, stakeholders can move beyond reactive crisis management toward preventive infrastructure. The emphasis on learning from past data errors and coordination gaps illustrates the necessity of open, reliable datasets to build resilient public health systems capable of handling future biological threats.
