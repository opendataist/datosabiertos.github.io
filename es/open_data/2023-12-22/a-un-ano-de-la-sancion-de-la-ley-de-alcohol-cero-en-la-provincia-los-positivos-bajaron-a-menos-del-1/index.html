A un año de la sanción de la Ley de Alcohol Cero en la Provincia, los positivos bajaron a menos del 1%

The implementation of the Zero Alcohol Law in Buenos Aires has significantly transformed road safety dynamics by drastically reducing positive alcohol test results among drivers. This legislative shift demonstrates how strict regulatory frameworks can effectively alter behavioral patterns and lower accident rates linked to alcohol consumption, proving that legal enforcement combined with cultural change yields substantial public health benefits. The data indicates a dramatic decrease in both the percentage of positive tests and the total number of monthly violations compared to previous years. These statistics highlight the immediate impact of intensified control operations and clear penalties, suggesting that consistent application of the law serves as a powerful deterrent. The reduction in positive cases reflects a successful transition toward safer driving habits across the province. This case is crucial for open data initiatives as it illustrates the value of transparent, accessible government statistics in validating policy effectiveness. By publishing granular operational and enforcement data, authorities enable independent analysis, fostering accountability and providing a model for how open information can drive evidence-based decision-making and public trust in safety measures.
