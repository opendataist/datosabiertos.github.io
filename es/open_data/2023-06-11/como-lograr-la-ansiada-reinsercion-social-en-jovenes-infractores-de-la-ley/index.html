¿Cómo lograr la ansiada reinserción social en jóvenes infractores de la ley?

The article argues that public discourse on crime is often driven by prejudice and simplistic scapegoating rather than technical rigor. It emphasizes the urgent need for the academic community to break its silence and provide evidence-based explanations, challenging the narrative that attributes criminality solely to specific groups or socioeconomic factors. This call to action highlights a critical gap between public perception and scientific understanding, demanding a shift toward data-driven policy. Research indicates that delinquency is a multivariate phenomenon linked to specific behavioral factors and life trajectories, rather than being an inevitable consequence of poverty or substance abuse. Crucially, while some background factors are immutable, attitudes and behaviors conducive to crime can be modified through targeted, sequenced, and measured interventions. This demonstrates that rehabilitation and social reinsertion are achievable goals when based on proven psychological and sociological insights, offering a hopeful alternative to punitive measures. This topic is relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and high-quality scientific information to inform public policy. By supplying the state with the best available science and technology, academia can ensure that penal system practices are continuously reviewed and optimized. The article advocates for using rigorous data and social science research to guide decision-making, thereby fostering a more just and effective society where policies are rooted in verified knowledge rather than bias.
