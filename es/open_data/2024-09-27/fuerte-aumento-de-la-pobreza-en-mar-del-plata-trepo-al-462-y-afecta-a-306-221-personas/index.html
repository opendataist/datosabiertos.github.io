Fuerte aumento de la pobreza: en Mar del Plata trepó al 46,2% y afecta a 306.221 personas

The article highlights a significant rise in poverty and indigence in Argentina, particularly in Mar del Plata, driven by high inflation and economic contraction. While the government attributes these increases to inherited structural issues and hyperinflation, the data reveals a sharp decline in real wages for public and informal workers, alongside rising unemployment. This indicates that current economic adjustments have exacerbated vulnerability, leaving a substantial portion of the population unable to meet basic nutritional needs despite nominal salary increases for some private sector employees. This report is crucial for open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of accessible, granular socioeconomic statistics to monitor policy impacts accurately. Transparent data allows researchers and civil society to verify official narratives, analyze regional disparities, and assess the real-world effects of macroeconomic decisions. Without reliable, publicly available datasets, it becomes impossible to hold institutions accountable or to design effective social interventions. Ultimately, the narrative demonstrates how open data serves as a vital tool for democratic oversight. By making detailed indicators on poverty, inflation, and employment available, stakeholders can engage in evidence-based debates rather than relying solely on political rhetoric. The release of such data empowers citizens to understand complex economic trends and fosters informed public discourse on the country’s recovery trajectory.
