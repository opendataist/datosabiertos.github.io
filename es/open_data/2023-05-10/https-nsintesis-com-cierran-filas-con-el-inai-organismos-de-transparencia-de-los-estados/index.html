Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The paralysis of Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) due to a lack of quorum critically jeopardizes the enforcement of citizens' rights to access public information and protect their personal data. This institutional blockage stems from political opposition in the Senate, preventing the appointment of necessary commissioners and leaving the federal body unable to resolve pending demands. The implications extend beyond federal operations, as state transparency agencies rely on the INAI for resolving specific cases, particularly regarding data protection. Without a functioning federal body, local organizations face increased administrative burdens and reduced capacity to safeguard citizens' privacy rights, creating a systemic vulnerability across the national transparency framework. This situation is highly relevant to open data because the INAI serves as the essential guarantor that ensures public institutions actually publish and share information effectively. When the oversight mechanism fails, the core principles of open data—accountability, transparency, and citizen access—are undermined. Sustaining a operational INAI is therefore crucial for maintaining the integrity and utility of open government initiatives in Mexico.
