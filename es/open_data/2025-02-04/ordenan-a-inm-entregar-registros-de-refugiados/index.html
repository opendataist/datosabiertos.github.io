Ordenan a INM entregar registros de refugiados

The INAI ruled that migrants must be granted free, certified copies of their entry and exit records without being forced to visit offices in person. This decision eliminates administrative barriers that hinder the exercise of personal data access rights, ensuring equitable treatment for vulnerable populations seeking their own information. By mandating delivery options such as postal mail, the ruling prioritizes convenience and safety over bureaucratic convenience. It establishes a clear precedent that public institutions cannot obstruct data access through logistical obstacles or unclear fee exemptions, reinforcing the principle that access to personal information should be straightforward and non-discriminatory. This case is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical intersection between individual privacy rights and institutional transparency. It demonstrates how regulatory bodies enforce the obligation of agencies to make personal data accessible and usable, ensuring that data protection laws are actively implemented to empower citizens against opaque administrative practices.
