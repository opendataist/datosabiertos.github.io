Once mujeres son víctimas de feminicidio cada día en Latinoamérica y el Caribe, dice Cepal

The article highlights that despite universal legal frameworks in Latin America and the Caribbean to prevent gender-based violence, femicides remain a persistent human rights violation. It underscores a stark contrast between the existence of laws and the reality of widespread violence, noting that the majority of these deaths occur within intimate partner relationships, predominantly affecting women of reproductive age. This data reveals a critical gap between legislative progress and effective protection for women, emphasizing that legal measures alone are insufficient to eradicate the crisis. The report also quantifies the severe collateral impact on dependents, illustrating how femicides create new victims through indirect consequences. By tracking these ripple effects, the analysis moves beyond individual cases to show the broader social devastation, highlighting the urgent need for comprehensive support systems for families affected by this violence. This holistic view is essential for understanding the full scope of gender-based homicide and informing more robust public policy interventions. This article is relevant to open data because it relies on standardized, comparative statistics from the CEPAL Gender Observatory to track trends across multiple nations and years. Such open, harmonized datasets enable researchers and policymakers to identify patterns, evaluate the effectiveness of interventions, and hold governments accountable for reducing violence against women. Transparency in these figures is crucial for driving evidence-based advocacy and resource allocation in the fight for gender equity.
