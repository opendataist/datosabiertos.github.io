El INDEC prepara cambios en el índice de inflación con respaldo del FMI: qué se modifica

The article highlights the INDEC’s ongoing transition under its previous leadership, focusing on the planned launch of a new Consumer Price Index (IPC). This update replaces outdated statistical bases with recent household expenditure data, a move designed to capture price changes with significantly greater precision. By aligning the basket of goods with current consumption patterns, the government aims to enhance the reliability of inflation measurements during periods of economic volatility. This methodological shift is not merely administrative but represents a critical improvement in statistical transparency. The revised index incorporates detailed data on housing, transport, and food expenses, ensuring that policy decisions are based on accurate indicators of citizens’ living conditions. Although the official release date remains pending, the inclusion of a trial period underscores a commitment to validating results before full implementation, thereby reducing the risk of distorted inflation figures. Relevance to open data lies in the fundamental necessity of high-quality, accessible, and updated statistics for public trust. Reliable open data enables citizens, researchers, and institutions to scrutinize economic policies effectively. By modernizing the IPC with recent, granular data, the INDEC contributes to a more robust information ecosystem. This transition exemplifies how open, accurate, and methodologically sound statistics are essential for democratic accountability and informed public debate in an open society.
