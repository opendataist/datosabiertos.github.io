¿Si ya me censaron, puedo salir de mi casa? INE Santa Cruz responde a 10 dudas sobre el censo | El Deber

The article clarifies that the National Institute of Statistics holds exclusive authority for official population data, dismissing local estimates as non-binding projections. This distinction is vital for open data advocates, as it underscores the necessity of recognizing sole official sources to prevent the proliferation of unverified statistics in public discourse and decision-making processes. Transparency and confidentiality are emphasized to ensure the integrity of the census. By deploying residents as enumerators and establishing secure emergency protocols, the process aims to remain open yet protected. The prohibition on photographing survey sheets highlights the importance of legal frameworks that safeguard individual privacy, a critical component for maintaining public trust in any statistical system. Relevance to open data lies in the balance between accessible information and protected rights. While the goal is comprehensive coverage, including marginalized groups and transit passengers, the strict confidentiality rules demonstrate that openness does not equate to public exposure of personal details. Understanding these boundaries helps stakeholders appreciate how modern data collection balances transparency with necessary privacy protections.
