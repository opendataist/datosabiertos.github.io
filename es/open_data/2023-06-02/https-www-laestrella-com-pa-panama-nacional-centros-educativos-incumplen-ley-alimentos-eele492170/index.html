Panama faces a surging crisis of childhood obesity and overweight, with prevalence rates tripling over the last decade. This alarming public health trend highlights the critical responsibility of parents to foster healthy habits and active lifestyles within their households, rather than relying solely on external interventions. Consequently, inspections of various schools revealed widespread non-compliance with national laws mandating healthy food standards in educational environments. These findings indicate that while parental influence is paramount, schools are also failing to provide adequate nutritious options, exacerbating the unhealthy trends among students. This situation underscores the vital role of open data in addressing public health challenges. Transparent, accessible statistical information is essential for holding institutions accountable and driving policy enforcement, ensuring that data not only informs the public but also compels systemic change in how communities and schools promote wellness.
