The article highlights Jaén’s profound demographic aging and population loss, driven by declining birth rates and youth migration. Over fifteen years, the province has lost a significant portion of its child and young adult populations, reversing any hopes for sustainable innovation and investment. This shift toward an older, shrinking populace indicates a lack of future opportunities, causing many to leave for better prospects elsewhere. Relevant to open data, this report exemplifies how accessible, standardized government statistics can diagnose structural regional crises. By analyzing census data over time, the narrative moves beyond raw numbers to reveal clear social implications: the collapse of local demographics threatens the social fabric and economic viability of the region. The data serves as critical evidence for understanding the mechanisms of rural depopulation and aging. Furthermore, the granular breakdown of municipal data exposes the disproportionate impact on smaller towns, with some losing nearly half their residents. This level of detail is essential for policymakers to design targeted interventions rather than generic solutions. The article underscores the necessity of using open data to identify specific vulnerability hotspots, enabling evidence-based strategies to reverse depopulation trends and restore economic equilibrium to affected areas.
