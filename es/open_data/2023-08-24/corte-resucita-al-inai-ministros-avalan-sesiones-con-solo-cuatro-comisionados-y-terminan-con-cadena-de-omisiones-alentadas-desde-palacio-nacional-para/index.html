CORTE 'RESUCITA' AL INAI: Ministros avalan sesiones con solo cuatro comisionados y terminan con cadena de omisiones alentadas desde Palacio Nacional para sabotear la transparencia - Noticaribe

The Supreme Court authorized the National Institute for Transparency to operate with only four members, resolving a months-long paralysis caused by the Senate's failure to appoint replacement commissioners. This critical legal adjustment allows the INAI to resume its mandate, previously hindered by strict quorum requirements that rendered it unable to process thousands of pending transparency appeals. This development is vital for open data because it restores the primary institution responsible for enforcing citizens' right to access public information. Without a functioning INAI, citizens and researchers lose an essential mechanism to challenge government secrecy, undermining the practical application of transparency laws and limiting accountability in public administration. The ruling counters political efforts to dismantle or weaken the institute, ensuring continued oversight of executive branch data practices. By maintaining the body's operational capacity, the decision protects the infrastructure necessary for verifying government actions, thereby sustaining the integrity of Mexico’s open data ecosystem and preventing regulatory vacuums that could obscure public spending and decision-making processes.
