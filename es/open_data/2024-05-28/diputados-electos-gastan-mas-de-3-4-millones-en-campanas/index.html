Diputados electos gastan más de $3.4 millones en campañas

The article highlights the mandatory disclosure of campaign expenditures by newly elected National Assembly deputies, revealing that total spending exceeded $3.4 million. Nine candidates individually spent over $100,000, with several surpassing $150,000, though all remained within the statutory cap of $300,000 per candidate. This transparency is crucial for open data initiatives, as it allows citizens to monitor financial flows and ensure compliance with electoral regulations. Legal frameworks require these reports to be submitted under oath within fifteen days of the election, enforced by financial penalties for late submissions. The data underscores the balance between allowing substantial campaign funding and preventing excessive spending that could distort democratic processes. By making these figures public, authorities provide a clear view of the costs associated with securing political office, fostering accountability. This information is relevant to open data because it exemplifies the importance of accessible, verified government records in promoting electoral integrity. When financial data is freely available and structured, it empowers researchers and the public to analyze trends, detect anomalies, and assess the health of democratic institutions. Such transparency serves as a foundation for civic engagement and ensures that political financing remains subject to public scrutiny rather than hidden opacity.
