The article highlights a political crisis in Mexico where President López Obrador advocates for abolishing the National Institute for Transparency (INAI). He argues the agency fails to combat corruption and should instead be dismantled, with its functions transferred to the Federal Auditor General’s office to reduce costs. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it threatens the independence of the primary institution safeguarding access to public information. The proposed structural change risks centralizing transparency functions within an auditing body, potentially undermining the autonomy and effectiveness of data openness in the country.
