The Senate rejected the appointment of a new commissioner, preventing the INAI from reaching a quorum. This failure highlights the deep political fragmentation and obstructionism threatening the institute's ability to function as an independent oversight body, leaving it unable to deliberate on critical transparency matters. The rejection coincided with a controversial initiative to abolish the INAI, underscoring a broader political effort to weaken institutions dedicated to public accountability. The tense atmosphere, marked by protests within the chamber, illustrates the intense conflict surrounding the protection of civil rights and the rule of law in Mexico. This situation is highly relevant to open data because the INAI is the primary guarantor of citizens' right to access public information. Without a functional commission, the enforcement of transparency laws stalls, potentially hindering the availability and quality of open data for research, journalism, and civic engagement.
