Congreso retrasa dar información sobre actas vía transparencia, pero Redondo mostró los datos en sesión

The Honduran Congress failed to provide requested data regarding legislative acts ratified and unratified by the legal deadline, highlighting a significant transparency gap. Although the legislative president eventually presented internal statistics revealing that some decrees were processed with insufficient vote counts, the institution did not respond to formal information requests from the press. This delay underscores systemic issues in accountability and the accessibility of official records, which are fundamental to open data initiatives. The controversy centers on the controversy surrounding Honduras’s admission to the Andean Development Corporation, a decision marred by procedural irregularities and political opposition. The legislature ultimately rejected the ratification of the session that approved this controversial financial integration, leading the executive branch to halt the process. The lack of clear, timely documentation regarding how and why these decrees were transmitted to the Executive Power without proper ratification emphasizes the need for robust, accessible datasets to track governmental decision-making. This situation is relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical importance of proactive information publication and the consequences of opacity in democratic institutions. When governments withhold or delay essential legislative data, it hinders public scrutiny and prevents citizens from understanding the validity of state actions. Ensuring that all legislative activities, including vote tallies and ratification statuses, are openly available is essential for maintaining trust and enabling effective civic oversight.
