Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The 2022 report on marital dissolutions reveals a significant return to pre-pandemic trends, marked by a notable decline in divorce rates. Following a sharp post-confinement surge in the previous year, this decrease suggests a normalization of family stability metrics and provides crucial insights into long-term societal shifts. Understanding these fluctuations is essential for analyzing the resilience of social structures and the varying impacts of health crises on household formations. The data highlights that the vast majority of separations occur through mutual agreement, predominantly via divorce rather than separation or nullity. With half of the cases involving minor children, most resulting in shared custody or maternal sole custody, the findings underscore the complex interplay between legal proceedings and child welfare. This emphasis on cooperative resolution points to a cultural shift toward less adversarial legal frameworks, influencing how family law evolves to support collaborative co-parenting arrangements. This information is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistics enable researchers to track societal well-being and policy effectiveness. By making granular details on custody, duration, and demographics publicly available, governments foster accountability and allow for evidence-based social planning. Such open datasets empower citizens and academics to scrutinize trends, ensuring that public resources are allocated efficiently to support families navigating these critical life transitions.
