Diputados inició el debate para modificar la ley de protección de datos personales

Argentina is updating its personal data protection law to address modern technological challenges and safeguard individual autonomy. This legislative revision aims to harmonize current regulations with contemporary realities, ensuring that citizens retain control over their information in an increasingly digital society. By reinforcing these rights, the initiative strengthens the democratic framework and responds to evolving societal needs. The proposed law introduces critical protections for vulnerable groups, specifically children and adolescents, who were previously underrepresented in existing statutes. It also establishes new institutional mechanisms, such as a Federal Council for Transparency, and regulates international data transfers. These measures reflect a broader human rights perspective, emphasizing that data protection is essential for personal dignity and societal structure in the information age. This development is highly relevant to open data because it defines the boundaries between public transparency and private privacy. While open data initiatives promote government accountability through information sharing, they must operate within strict legal frameworks that prevent misuse of personal information. This balance ensures that accessing public records does not infringe upon individuals' rights, allowing for responsible data reuse while maintaining ethical standards and legal compliance.
