La actividad industrial creció 1,4% en noviembre y la construcción cayó 1,5%

The report reveals a divergent economic trend where industrial activity experienced consistent growth, particularly in manufacturing sectors like automotive and basic metals, while the construction industry faced a notable decline. This contrast highlights a structural imbalance in the economy, with industrial production accumulating significant gains year-to-year despite monthly fluctuations, whereas construction struggled with reduced activity across various material inputs, signaling weaker domestic investment in this specific sector. Business expectations for the near future remain largely cautious, with nearly half of the respondents predicting stability rather than significant changes in demand or exports. However, a substantial portion of business leaders anticipate a contraction in internal demand, and sentiment is particularly negative within the private construction segment, where a majority foresee a downturn. This mixed outlook suggests uncertainty regarding market resilience and consumer purchasing power, complicating immediate strategic planning for companies dependent on domestic consumption. This article is relevant to open data because it exemplifies the critical role of official statistical institutions, such as INDEC, in providing transparent, granular, and timely macroeconomic indicators. By making detailed data on sectoral performance, input consumption, and business surveys publicly available, open data fosters accountability and enables stakeholders to independently analyze economic health. This transparency supports evidence-based policy-making and allows civil society and researchers to verify governmental claims, such as those regarding employment and inflation, against objective metrics.
