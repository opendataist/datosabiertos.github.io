Nuevo récord de dióxido de carbono emitido a la atmósfera en 2023 llega a 40.900 millones de toneladas

The Global Carbon Budget report concludes that global carbon emissions have reached unprecedented levels, significantly widening the gap with international climate goals. The data indicates that fossil fuel emissions continue to rise globally, driven largely by increased consumption in major economies like India and China, which outweighs reductions in Europe and the United States. This persistent upward trajectory confirms that current efforts are insufficient to curb the primary driver of the climate crisis, highlighting an urgent need for accelerated decarbonization strategies worldwide. Furthermore, the report warns that the world is on track to surpass the critical 1.5°C warming threshold within the next seven years, a milestone likely to render the Paris Agreement’s primary target unattainable without immediate and drastic action. While natural sinks still absorb a portion of emissions, the remaining CO2 continues to accumulate in the atmosphere, exacerbating extreme weather events such as record-breaking wildfires. The disparity between emission sources and the minimal capacity of current technological removal solutions underscores the inadequacy of existing mitigation technologies compared to the sheer volume of greenhouse gases released annually. This article is highly relevant to open data because it relies on a massive, collaborative effort involving over 120 scientists and dozens of institutions to aggregate, verify, and publish transparent climate metrics. The publication of such comprehensive, peer-reviewed datasets in open-access journals like *Earth System Science Data* enables independent verification, fosters global scientific cooperation, and provides the essential evidence base required for policymakers to make informed decisions. It exemplifies how open, standardized data is crucial for monitoring planetary health and holding stakeholders accountable to climate commitments.
