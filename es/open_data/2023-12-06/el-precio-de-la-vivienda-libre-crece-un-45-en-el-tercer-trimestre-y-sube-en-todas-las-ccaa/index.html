El precio de la vivienda libre crece un 4,5% en el tercer trimestre y sube en todas las CCAA

Spain’s housing market demonstrates remarkable resilience, with free-market prices rising across all autonomous communities for thirty-eight consecutive quarters. New home costs surge to their highest annual rate in sixteen years, while the secondary market continues its steady climb since 2014. This sustained growth contradicts previous expert predictions of a downturn, indicating a robust underlying demand capable of absorbing current market pressures. The core driver of this trend is a critical imbalance between supply and demand. Despite financing becoming more expensive and transaction volumes dropping, the available housing stock remains insufficiently replenished. Consequently, solvent buyers continue to push prices upward, suggesting that price increases will persist, albeit at a more moderated pace as the year concludes. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it underscores the necessity of transparent, timely, and granular housing statistics. Accurate data from institutions like the INE allows stakeholders to monitor market health, identify regional disparities, and understand structural imbalances. Such transparency is essential for policymakers and researchers to develop effective strategies addressing housing affordability and supply constraints in real time.
