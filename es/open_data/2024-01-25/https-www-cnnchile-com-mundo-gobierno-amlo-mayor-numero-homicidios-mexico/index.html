The article reveals that while the current Mexican administration has managed to stabilize and slightly reduce the rate of homicides compared to the sharp increases seen in previous terms, the absolute number of murders remains historically high. This data highlights a critical disconnect between political narratives of success and the reality of persistent violence, underscoring the necessity for transparent and accurate statistical reporting to truly assess public safety trends. Experts emphasize that the underlying issue is not just national totals but a severe lack of coordination between federal and state authorities, particularly in high-violence regions. This insight demonstrates that open government data is essential for identifying specific geographic failures and governance gaps, allowing for targeted policy interventions rather than broad, ineffective national strategies. For open data enthusiasts, this case illustrates the importance of scrutinizing official statistics to separate short-term trend stabilization from long-term structural failure. It serves as a reminder that data transparency must go beyond simple counts to include contextual analysis, ensuring that citizens and policymakers can hold leaders accountable for genuine improvements in security rather than just relative reductions in growth rates.
