INEGI reconoce que tuvo que pagar «derecho de piso» para realizar el Censo Agropecuario 2022 - Plumas Libres

The article reveals that INEGI personnel were forced to pay extortion fees and navigate complex local power dynamics imposed by organized crime groups to conduct the 2022 Agricultural Census. This admission highlights a critical intersection between state data collection and criminal control, demonstrating that the production of official statistics in certain regions is contingent on illicit negotiations rather than sovereign authority. Concurrently, survey data indicates that a significant majority of the population perceives their living environment as unsafe, with extreme levels of fear reported in specific urban areas. Public sentiment reflects deep distrust in the public security system, with most citizens expecting conditions to worsen rather than improve in the immediate future. This widespread perception of insecurity underscores the severe challenges the state faces in maintaining order and public trust. This information is highly relevant to open data because it exposes how corruption and violence can compromise the integrity and accessibility of statistical processes. It raises urgent questions about the transparency of data collection methodologies and whether official statistics fully account for the coercive environments in which they are generated, potentially affecting the reliability and representativeness of national datasets.
