The article details the intensification of Tropical Storm Polo into a hurricane, forecasting potential category 4 strength and erratic movement along Mexico’s Pacific coast. It warns of severe risks, including landslides and floods, prompting coordinated vigilance between Mexican and US authorities. This case highlights the critical role of open data in meteorology. Transparent, real-time sharing of atmospheric metrics enables accurate modeling and early warning systems, which are essential for disaster preparedness and protecting vulnerable communities from natural hazards. Accessible environmental data bridges the gap between scientific forecasting and public safety. By leveraging open datasets, organizations can improve coordination and response efficiency, demonstrating how shared information infrastructure directly supports societal resilience against climate-related threats.
