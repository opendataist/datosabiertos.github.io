Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The article argues that traditional democratic models, limited to free elections, have failed to address modern societal challenges like inequality and corruption. Consequently, there is a critical need to shift from a state-centric view to a new governance paradigm that recognizes citizens as active participants. This transition aims to rebuild trust and legitimacy by treating public problems as shared responsibilities, moving beyond the historical pattern where the state slowly negotiated rights while remaining opaque and unaccountable. The proposed solution is "Open Government," a philosophy that centers on transparency, citizen participation, accountability, and social innovation. Rather than viewing society as passive or incapable, this model leverages collective knowledge and technological tools to co-create solutions for complex public issues. By opening state operations to public scrutiny and collaboration, governments can overcome internal bureaucratic stagnation and foster a more responsive, adaptive administration that values public input in decision-making processes. This shift is vital for open data because it establishes the legal and cultural necessity for transparent information flow as a foundation of modern governance. Open data is not merely an administrative tool but the essential infrastructure that enables the transparency and accountability required by Open Government. By making public information accessible, societies can empower citizens to monitor government actions, collaborate in policy design, and ensure that data serves as a mechanism for social value creation rather than just state control, thereby strengthening democratic institutions in the twenty-first century.
