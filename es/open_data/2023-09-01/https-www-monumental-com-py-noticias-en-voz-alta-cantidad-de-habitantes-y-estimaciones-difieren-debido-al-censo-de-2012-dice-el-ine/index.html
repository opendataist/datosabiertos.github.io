Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

The disparity between previous projections and the preliminary census data in Paraguay stems from the reliance on outdated baseline information. Projections based on the 2002 census accumulated significant errors over two decades, as the missed 2012 count prevented timely updates. This highlights the critical necessity for accurate, real-time demographic baselines to ensure reliable population estimates. The resulting one-million-person difference underscores the instability of long-term forecasting without recent validation. Frequent censuses are essential to maintain data integrity, as extended intervals lead to substantial deviations in fertility, mortality, and migration models. This case demonstrates how structural gaps in data collection compromise the validity of future planning and resource allocation. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it illustrates the direct impact of data timeliness on public trust and policy effectiveness. Transparent access to raw census results allows independent verification, fostering accountability. Moreover, it encourages governments to prioritize regular, high-quality data releases, ensuring that open data ecosystems remain robust tools for informed decision-making and societal development.
