¿Veto parcial? - Opinión - ABC Color

The article concludes that the transparency law is fully constitutional and essential for public accountability, refuting claims by NGO coalitions that it infringes on privacy or press freedom. It emphasizes that financial transactions impacting public policy cannot remain secret in a republic, and existing regulations fail to adequately address foreign funding, leaving a necessary gap for this legislation to fill. This topic is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical political struggle to establish mandatory transparency in non-governmental financing. By arguing for the disclosure of foreign fund flows, the text supports the principle that data regarding public-impacting entities should be accessible, challenging opaque governance models that resist civic scrutiny and accountability through data openness.
