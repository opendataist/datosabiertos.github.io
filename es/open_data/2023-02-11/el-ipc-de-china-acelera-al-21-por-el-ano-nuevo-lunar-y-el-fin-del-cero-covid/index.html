El IPC de China acelera al 2,1% por el Año Nuevo Lunar y el fin del 'cero covid'

China’s consumer prices rose moderately in January, driven by strong demand following the end of pandemic restrictions and seasonal holidays. Conversely, industrial producer prices continued their decline due to falling global energy costs. This divergence creates a unique economic environment where retail costs increase while manufacturing input costs decrease. The contrast between these indicators offers the central bank room to ease monetary policy to support economic recovery. Analysts suggest that unlike developed economies, Chinese households did not receive significant financial transfers during the pandemic, which limits the potential for a sharp inflation surge despite reopening efforts. This structural difference dampens inflationary pressures compared to other global markets. This scenario is relevant to open data because transparent, real-time statistical releases from national offices like China’s NBS enable precise economic modeling. Open access to these metrics allows researchers and policymakers to accurately track the lagging effects of policy shifts and consumption patterns, ensuring that data-driven decisions reflect the nuanced reality of post-pandemic inflation dynamics.
