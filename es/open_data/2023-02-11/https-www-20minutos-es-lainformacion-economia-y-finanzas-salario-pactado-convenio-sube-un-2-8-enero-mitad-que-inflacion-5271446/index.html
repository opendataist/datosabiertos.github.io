The article highlights a critical disconnect between recent wage increases and soaring inflation, revealing that negotiated salary adjustments fall significantly short of the rising cost of living. This gap exacerbates the erosion of workers' purchasing power, underscoring the urgent need for more robust collective bargaining outcomes that can effectively protect employees against economic volatility and ensure real income stability. Furthermore, the data illustrates a substantial decline in the number of active collective agreements across all sectors, suggesting a slowdown in labor negotiations and potentially weakening the social dialogue framework. The scarcity of new contracts and the prevalence of modest raises indicate a labor market struggling to adapt to post-crisis economic pressures, raising concerns about the long-term resilience of worker protections and the overall health of industrial relations. This situation is highly relevant to open data as it demonstrates how transparent, publicly available statistics are essential for validating policy effectiveness and fostering accountability. By making detailed labor market indicators accessible, citizens, researchers, and policymakers can critically assess whether government appeals for negotiation truly translate into tangible improvements. Open data thus serves as a vital tool for holding institutions accountable and driving informed debates on social equity and economic justice.
