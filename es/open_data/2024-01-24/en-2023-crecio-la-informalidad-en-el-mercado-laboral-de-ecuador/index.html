En 2023 creció la informalidad en el mercado laboral de Ecuador

Despite seasonal retail boosts during the holiday season, Ecuador’s labor market failed to reflect broader economic recovery in late 2023. The rise in informal employment indicates a lack of sustainable job creation, suggesting that previous gains have not adequately replaced positions lost during recent socioeconomic crises. This structural weakness highlights that temporary commercial incentives are insufficient without a stable environment that fosters genuine economic dynamics and full-employment conditions. The economy faces ongoing deceleration driven by deflation and reduced sales, exacerbated by security measures that disrupt commercial chains. These factors create a critical employment climate, particularly affecting youth and tourism sectors. Consequently, metrics such as underemployment and unemployment have worsened, demonstrating that the labor market remains fragile and disconnected from the festive spending activity. This analysis is relevant to open data as it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible statistics to inform effective public policy. Reliable labor indicators allow stakeholders to identify structural inefficiencies and advocate for targeted interventions. Open data facilitates this scrutiny, enabling a clearer understanding of how economic shocks impact employment quality and guiding decisions needed to restore sustainable growth and formal job opportunities.
