El Inegi refrenda la leve tendencia a la baja de los asesinatos en México

The National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) confirms a slight downward trend in intentional homicides in Mexico during the first half of 2023, marking the lowest figures since 2017. This data validates the government’s security strategy, which relies heavily on the deployment of the National Guard and armed forces. While these statistics provide political support for current policies, they also highlight that the annual total is expected to exceed 30,000 killings, maintaining a consistent pattern over the last six years and preventing any triumphant narrative about complete safety restoration. Crucially, this report underscores the importance of independent data verification within the open data ecosystem. Unlike preliminary reports from security secretariats or state-level aggregations, INEGI’s methodology integrates records from forensic services and civil registries nationwide. This multi-source approach significantly enhances accuracy by cross-referencing different official channels, offering a more reliable and comprehensive picture of violence than unilateral government updates or isolated state statistics. The article is highly relevant to open data advocates because it illustrates the critical value of standardized, transparent statistical agencies in a fragmented information landscape. By providing granular details, such as distinguishing between firearm injuries and other causes of death, the data enables deeper sociological analysis rather than mere political messaging. It demonstrates how robust, multi-source open data can challenge narratives, ensure accountability, and facilitate a more nuanced understanding of public safety trends based on empirical evidence rather than partial or unverified reports.
