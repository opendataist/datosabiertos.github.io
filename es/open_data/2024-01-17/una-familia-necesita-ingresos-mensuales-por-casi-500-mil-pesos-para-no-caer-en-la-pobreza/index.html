Una familia necesita ingresos mensuales por casi 500 mil pesos para no caer en la pobreza

The recent data reveals a severe deterioration in Argentina’s economic landscape, where the cost of living has surged dramatically, pushing millions below the poverty line. This situation underscores the critical role of open statistical data in monitoring social welfare, as transparent metrics allow society to grasp the scale of hardship and hold institutions accountable. Without accessible, accurate data, policymakers and citizens would remain blind to the extent of the crisis and the urgent need for intervention. The core implication is that basic survival costs have outpaced inflation, necessitating significantly higher incomes to avoid both poverty and indigence. As prices for essential goods and services continue to rise, the gap between wages and living costs widens, exacerbating multidimensional poverty. This trend highlights how open data serves as an early warning system, revealing structural inequalities and the disproportionate impact on children and vulnerable families. Ultimately, this article demonstrates that open_data is not merely a technical exercise but a vital tool for democratic oversight and social justice. By making inflation and poverty metrics publicly available, stakeholders can analyze trends, predict future challenges, and advocate for evidence-based policies. The continuous publication of these indicators ensures that the severity of the social debt remains visible, fostering informed public debate and driving necessary reforms to protect the most affected populations.
