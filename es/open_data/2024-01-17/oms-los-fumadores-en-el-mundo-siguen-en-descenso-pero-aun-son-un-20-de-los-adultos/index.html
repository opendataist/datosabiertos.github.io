OMS: los fumadores en el mundo siguen en descenso, pero aún son un 20 % de los adultos

Global smoking prevalence has decreased significantly, with adult smokers now comprising one in five individuals worldwide. Although this decline reflects substantial progress in tobacco control, the pace remains insufficient. The persistence of high consumption rates in specific regions, particularly Europe and Southeast Asia, indicates that current efforts are unevenly distributed. Crucially, these gains are fragile due to aggressive counter-strategies by the tobacco industry, which actively manipulates health policies to protect its market share. The trend is not uniform across genders or age groups. Europe exhibits concerning spikes in smoking among women and adolescents, with female consumption rates more than double the global average. This demographic vulnerability necessitates targeted interventions, especially since adolescent use persists through both traditional cigarettes and emerging products like e-cigarettes. The fact that smoking rates have increased in only a handful of nations highlights that effective policy frameworks exist, yet political will and regulatory enforcement vary wildly across borders. This report is vital for the open data community as it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and comparable health statistics to drive evidence-based policy. Reliable data allows researchers and advocates to monitor industry interference and assess intervention effectiveness in real-time. By making these insights widely available, stakeholders can better hold governments accountable and coordinate global strategies to dismantle the tobacco industry’s influence, ensuring public health remains prioritized over corporate profit.
