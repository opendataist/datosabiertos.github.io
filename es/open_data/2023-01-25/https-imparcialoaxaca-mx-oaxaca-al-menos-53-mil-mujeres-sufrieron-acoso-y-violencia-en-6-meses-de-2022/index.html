This article reveals a profound discrepancy between statistical surveys on gender-based violence and official legal reports in Oaxaca. It highlights how vast numbers of women report harassment and sexual violence, while formal justice systems process significantly fewer cases, exposing systemic gaps in recording and addressing these crimes. The narrative underscores the severity of femicides in the region, contrasting official counts with higher figures from civil society organizations. This divergence points to challenges in data accuracy and the urgent need for reliable mechanisms to track violence against women, which is critical for effective public policy. Relevance to open_data lies in the urgent need for transparent, interoperable, and trustworthy datasets. By integrating diverse sources and ensuring accessibility, open data initiatives can help reconcile these discrepancies, fostering accountability and enabling better-informed strategies to combat gender violence and protect vulnerable populations.
