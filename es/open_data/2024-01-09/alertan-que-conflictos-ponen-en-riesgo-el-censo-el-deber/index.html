Alertan que conflictos ponen en riesgo el Censo | El Deber

Territorial disputes, exemplified by the Cochabamba-Santa Cruz highway blockade, threaten the March census schedule. Legal experts warn that delays could prevent results from influencing 2025 electoral redistribution, underscoring the critical intersection between accurate demographic mapping and political representation. The process faces additional scrutiny regarding methodological weaknesses, particularly concerning self-identification variables and enumerator training, as highlighted by United Nations recommendations. These concerns emphasize the necessity for transparent and scientifically rigorous data collection standards to ensure the census yields valid, internationally recognized statistics. Simultaneously, the initiative demonstrates significant civic engagement, with a majority of recruited volunteers being youth. This broad participation highlights the social dimension of data gathering, while the influx of young enumerators signals a shift in demographic involvement that will ultimately shape the integrity and scope of the national dataset. This case is relevant to open_data as it illustrates how spatial controversies and methodological transparency directly impact the validity of public administrative data. It shows that open, accurate datasets are essential for democratic accountability and equitable resource distribution, highlighting the real-world stakes of precise geographic information systems.
