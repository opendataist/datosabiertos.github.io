La inflación de CABA fue del 21,1% en diciembre y acumuló 200%

The recent surge in Buenos Aires’ Consumer Price Index highlights a critical intersection between economic instability and the reliability of statistical data. The sharp rise in inflation, driven by currency devaluation and soaring costs in essential sectors like food, housing, and transport, underscores the necessity for accurate, independent metrics. This context explains why the city initiated its own autonomous measurement system in 2012, diverging from national indices to restore credibility and ensure transparency in tracking real economic conditions for residents. The data reveals that specific categories, particularly food, utilities, and hospitality, are the primary drivers of this historical inflation peak. By isolating these trends, the report provides granular insights into how external economic shocks translate into daily living costs. This level of detail is vital for analyzing purchasing power and understanding the specific burdens on households, demonstrating how precise sectoral breakdowns can offer a clearer picture of economic health than aggregated national figures alone. This article is highly relevant to open data because it exemplifies the value of local, transparent statistical governance. It shows how accessible, independently verified data empowers citizens and policymakers to make informed decisions amid financial uncertainty. The contrast between official and local measurements illustrates a broader open data principle: trust in public information depends on methodological integrity and accessibility, ensuring that economic realities are not obscured by political narratives.
