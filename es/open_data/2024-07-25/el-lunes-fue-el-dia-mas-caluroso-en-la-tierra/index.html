El lunes fue el día más caluroso en la Tierra

The article highlights unprecedented global temperature records, establishing that Earth is currently as warm as it was 125,000 years ago. This extreme heat, driven by human-induced climate change and fossil fuel consumption, indicates that weather events are increasingly surpassing human tolerance levels. The recurrence of such intense heat waves suggests that natural climate variations alone cannot explain the current trajectory, pointing instead to an urgent need for systemic transformation to prevent irreversible damage to lives and livelihoods. This narrative is crucial for open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible satellite data from Copernicus empowers scientific consensus and public awareness. By making provisional temperature data immediately available, open datasets allow researchers and citizens to verify climate trends independently, fostering trust in scientific findings. The rapid dissemination of these figures underscores the necessity of open data infrastructure in monitoring planetary health and holding policymakers accountable for environmental decisions. Ultimately, the article reinforces the importance of open data in driving policy changes toward renewable energy sources like solar and wind. When data is freely accessible, it facilitates broader understanding of the climate crisis, enabling effective advocacy for national policies that support this transition. The intersection of open data and climate science illustrates how transparency can mobilize global action, turning raw information into a tool for survival and sustainable development in the face of escalating environmental challenges.
