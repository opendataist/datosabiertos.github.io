Spain’s recent demographic shift highlights a critical reliance on immigration to counteract the declining native birth rates and an aging population. While the total population has reached historical highs, this growth is driven almost entirely by foreign residents, who compensate for the stagnation of the Spanish-born demographic. This trend underscores a structural dependency on external human capital to sustain economic and social systems, marking a pivotal change in the country’s demographic foundation. The resulting inversion of the population pyramid poses significant challenges for the sustainability of the pension system and labor market. With low natality rates and a growing elderly cohort, the traditional support structure is becoming increasingly strained. Consequently, the influx of working-age immigrants is essential to maintain social security contributions, illustrating how demographic data directly influences long-term fiscal policy and societal stability. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the value of continuous statistical records in tracking complex societal trends. Transparent, accessible datasets allow researchers and policymakers to identify these critical demographic shifts, enabling evidence-based planning for public services. Open data facilitates the analysis of immigration patterns and their socioeconomic impacts, ensuring that government responses are grounded in accurate, real-time population insights rather than assumptions.
