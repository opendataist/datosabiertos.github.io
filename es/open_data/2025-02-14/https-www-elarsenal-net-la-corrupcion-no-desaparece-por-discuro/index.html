Datos Abiertos (Open data)
ES EN

Mexico’s historical low score in the Corruption Perceptions Index underscores the urgent need for independent oversight mechanisms, a core principle of open data. This stark reality challenges political narratives claiming the eradication of corruption, highlighting how transparency metrics serve as crucial accountability tools. Without robust, publicly accessible data, governments risk masking institutional failures behind rhetoric rather than addressing systemic issues through verifiable evidence. The impending extinction of the INAI, Mexico’s transparency institute, poses a direct threat to data integrity and citizen rights. Transferring oversight of personal data protection to the executive branch undermines the separation of powers essential for honest governance. This structural shift diminishes the availability of objective information, eroding public trust and limiting the ability of civil society to monitor state actions effectively. The recent dismissal of INAI officials amid extortion allegations illustrates the tension between political control and institutional independence. When access to information and data protection are weakened, the capacity to expose irregularities and demand accountability is severely compromised. For open data advocates, this situation emphasizes that technological access to information must be protected by strong legal frameworks and autonomous institutions to ensure genuine transparency.
