La inflación se modera en septiembre hasta el 1,5%, la tasa más baja en tres años y medio

Inflation in Spain has slowed significantly, dropping to its lowest level in three and a half years. This moderation is primarily driven by decreases in food, transport, and housing costs, pushing the annual rate below the European Central Bank’s target. The decline reflects a broader stabilization in consumer prices, offering a welcome relief for households after a period of sustained economic pressure. The relevance to open data lies in the critical role of transparent, standardized metrics in tracking these economic shifts. Reliable indicators like the Consumer Price Index allow analysts to verify trends and compare regional variations across autonomous communities. Access to such granular, high-quality datasets enables researchers to understand the specific local drivers of inflation, such as energy price fluctuations or seasonal retail patterns, ensuring that policy responses are grounded in accurate, publicly available evidence. Furthermore, harmonized data facilitates cross-border comparisons within the Eurozone, highlighting how national trends align with broader European economic contexts. By maintaining open access to these statistical sources, institutions support accountability and informed public discourse. This transparency is essential for validating the effectiveness of monetary policies and ensuring that the population can trust the official narratives surrounding their purchasing power and the overall health of the economy.
