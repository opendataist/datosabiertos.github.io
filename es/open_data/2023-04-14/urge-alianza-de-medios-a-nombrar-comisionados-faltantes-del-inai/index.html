Urge Alianza de Medios a nombrar comisionados faltantes del INAI

The article highlights a critical democratic rupture in Mexico as the INAI fails to convene for the first time in twenty years. This paralysis stems from unfilled vacancies, preventing the institute from resolving millions of citizen requests for information. The absence of a quorum effectively stalls essential oversight mechanisms, leaving the public without recourse for their transparency claims. This situation underscores the fragility of open data infrastructure when political appointments are blocked. The inability to process appeals demonstrates how governance gaps directly impact citizens' rights to access public information. It reveals that legal frameworks alone are insufficient without the necessary personnel to enforce them, creating a vacuum in accountability. Relevance to open data lies in this systemic risk: transparency institutions require operational continuity to function. When these bodies cease activity, the ecosystem of open information collapses, eroding trust and hindering civic engagement. The event serves as a stark warning that maintaining active, fully staffed regulatory bodies is fundamental to preserving the integrity and accessibility of open data in a democracy.
