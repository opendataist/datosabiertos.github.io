The article argues that the rise of artificial intelligence significantly amplifies global surveillance capabilities, thereby increasing the value and necessity of privacy-preserving infrastructure like Nym. As AI tools become more accessible and powerful, they enable adversaries to easily de-anonymize network communications, shifting the threat landscape. Consequently, robust encryption and anonymity networks are not merely optional enhancements but critical defenses required to protect individual freedoms against increasingly sophisticated digital threats. Furthermore, the piece emphasizes that safeguarding personal data is a collective societal responsibility rather than an individual burden. It calls for a fundamental redesign of internet infrastructure to prioritize privacy by default, citing hardware-level security innovations as successful precedents. This approach contrasts with the current model where users are forced to navigate insecure systems, highlighting the need for systemic changes that ensure devices and networks are inherently secure, much like physical locks on doors. This discussion is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the tension between data utility and the imperative for privacy. It challenges the open data community to consider how transparency and accessibility must be balanced with strong technical safeguards to prevent the abuse of information by states and corporations. The narrative suggests that for open initiatives to be ethical and sustainable, they must coexist with decentralized, user-controlled privacy technologies that empower individuals against mass surveillance.
