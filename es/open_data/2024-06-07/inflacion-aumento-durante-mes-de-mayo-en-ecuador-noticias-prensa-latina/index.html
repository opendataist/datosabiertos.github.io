Inflación aumentó durante mes de mayo en Ecuador - Noticias Prensa Latina

Ecuador faces rising inflation and increased costs for the basic family basket, driven by the recent hike in VAT from 12 to 15 percent. The government implemented this tax increase to fund security operations amid internal armed conflict, arguing that essential items like food, medicine, and public transport remain exempt. However, critics point out that fuel and gas prices also rose, undermining the official narrative and exacerbating economic pressures on households. Economists warn that this fiscal policy significantly reduces purchasing power, particularly for low-income citizens, making the country less competitive. The discrepancy between the president’s claims and the actual impact on essential goods highlights the vulnerability of the population. Consequently, cities with higher basket values are experiencing sharper declines in economic stability, reinforcing the burden on the most vulnerable sectors of society. This situation is highly relevant to open data because transparent, granular statistical monitoring is crucial for verifying government claims and assessing the real-world impact of policy changes. Open datasets on pricing and inflation allow civil society and researchers to independently track the cost of living, exposing gaps between official rhetoric and economic reality, thereby fostering accountability and informed public debate regarding social welfare and fiscal decisions.
