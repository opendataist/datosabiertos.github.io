Desaparición del INAI: así votaron los diputados poblanos

A recent legislative vote aimed to eliminate key autonomous bodies, including the National Institute for Transparency (INAI), Federal Telecommunications Institute (IFT), and Federal Economic Competition Commission (Cofece). This move, driven by the ruling party and its allies, seeks to dismantle institutions that ensure government accountability and protect citizens' rights to access public information and personal data protection. The INAI plays a crucial role in guaranteeing transparency in public resource management and resolving disputes when these rights are violated. By allowing citizens to monitor government actions and safeguard their personal data, the institute fosters a more informed and accountable society. Its removal represents a significant shift away from open governance principles. This development is highly relevant to open data because it directly threatens the infrastructure that enables public access to information. Eliminating such autonomous bodies undermines the mechanisms required for transparency and citizen oversight. Consequently, it risks increasing opacity in government operations, making it more difficult for the public to scrutinize decisions and hold authorities accountable, thereby weakening the foundations of an open data ecosystem.
