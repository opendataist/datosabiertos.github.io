Impulsan en el Congreso un proyecto de ley para garantizar el derecho al “olvido digital”

This proposal introduces the digital right to be forgotten, allowing individuals and entities to request the removal or de-indexing of outdated or false personal data that harms their privacy. Crucially, it excludes information of public, historical, or educational interest, balancing individual protection with transparency. The bill mandates that digital platforms implement accessible systems for processing these requests, supervised by the public access agency. It places special emphasis on protecting minors, whose vulnerability is recognized under international conventions, ensuring their best interests are prioritized in the digital environment against irreversible reputational damage. This is relevant to open data as it highlights the tension between data transparency and individual privacy rights. It urges a reevaluation of current data frameworks to prevent static, inaccurate information from causing harm, while maintaining access to historically significant or public interest data, thereby refining how open datasets handle personal information ethics.
