El número de mujeres docentes en la universidad ha aumentado más de cinco puntos en la última década - Tercera Información

The report highlights a significant shift toward gender parity in the Spanish education sector, particularly among university faculty and school leadership, where women now constitute the majority. Notably, younger academic cohorts are led by women, signaling a generational change that challenges traditional gender dynamics in higher education. However, substantial disparities persist in specific fields, with women remaining underrepresented in technological and scientific disciplines despite their overall academic success. Beyond representation, data reveals that female students consistently outperform males in graduation rates and literacy engagement across various educational stages. While girls demonstrate higher enthusiasm for reading and achieve better academic outcomes, they remain significantly less present in STEM-related vocational and university tracks. This segregation in career choices underscores a persistent structural barrier, indicating that academic equality does not automatically translate into equal professional distribution across all sectors. This information is crucial for the open data community as it illustrates how public datasets can expose nuanced inequalities that simple aggregate metrics might obscure. By making granular educational statistics accessible, policymakers and researchers can design targeted interventions to address specific bottlenecks in STEM participation. Furthermore, the transparency surrounding these gender gaps fosters accountability and encourages evidence-based strategies to promote equity, demonstrating the societal value of publishing comprehensive, disaggregated public data.
