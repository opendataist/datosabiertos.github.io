Le intentan robar el móvil y explica el método para que no te pase: "Llevaba el número en un papel"

A new social engineering tactic is emerging where thieves exploit empathy by asking pedestrians to borrow their phones to call a relative in an alleged emergency. This method relies on creating a sense of urgency and confusion, allowing criminals to snatch the device during the interaction. The widespread nature of this scheme highlights a growing vulnerability in public safety, where criminals manipulate social norms to bypass traditional security measures and execute quick thefts with minimal resistance. The relevance to open data lies in the critical relationship between physical device possession and digital security. Modern smartphones serve as central hubs for personal information, often containing direct access to banking, social media, and private communications. When a device is stolen, the immediate risk is not just the hardware loss but the potential breach of sensitive data stored locally or accessible through active sessions. Public awareness is essential to mitigating these risks. By recognizing such scams, individuals can protect their devices and, by extension, the personal data they hold. This aligns with open data principles by emphasizing the importance of safeguarding individual privacy and preventing unauthorized access to personal information in an increasingly connected world.
