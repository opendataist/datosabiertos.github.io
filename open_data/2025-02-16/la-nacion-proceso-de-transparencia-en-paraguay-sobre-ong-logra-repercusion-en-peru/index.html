La Nación / Proceso de transparencia en Paraguay sobre ONG logra repercusión en Perú

The article exposes a widespread pattern in which non-governmental organizations misuse international cooperation funds—ostensibly allocated to promote transparency and democracy—to instead finance professional fees and political agendas. This scandal reveals that such entities often operate as profitable networks for elites rather than delivering genuine humanitarian aid to marginalized communities. The narrative highlights how these organizations leverage media credibility to maintain public trust while diverting resources away from their stated social missions. This dynamic is particularly relevant to open data, as it underscores the critical need for mandatory financial transparency and accessible public records. Without robust data governance, citizens and oversight bodies cannot verify whether aid reaches its intended beneficiaries or if it fuels corruption. Open data serves as an essential mechanism to dismantle these opaque structures by enabling journalists and researchers to cross-reference funding flows, project outcomes, and beneficiary lists against reality. The case illustrates that relying on self-reported information or media narratives is insufficient for accountability. True transparency requires verifiable, machine-readable data regarding organizational finances and project execution. By advocating for laws that enforce the disclosure of how international funds are managed, society can ensure that cooperation serves the public interest. Ultimately, open data empowers civil society to challenge powerful narratives and demand accountability from those who profit under the guise of charitable work.
