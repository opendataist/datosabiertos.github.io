Población ocupada en Perú creció en 0,8 % en 2024, y la tasa de desempleo llegó al 5,6 % - Mundo - ABC Color

The report reveals a divergent labor market trend in Peru, characterized by urban job growth and rural decline. This shift highlights significant regional disparities, with major metropolitan areas gaining employment while smaller rural centers struggle. Understanding these geographic imbalances is crucial for open data initiatives, as it underscores the need for granular, location-specific datasets to accurately reflect national economic realities and guide targeted policy interventions. Labor market outcomes show distinct inequalities across demographic groups, particularly affecting women, youth, and university graduates. These groups face disproportionately higher unemployment rates and income challenges, revealing structural vulnerabilities within the workforce. For open data advocates, this emphasizes the necessity of intersectional data analysis. Making demographic breakdowns accessible allows researchers and policymakers to identify systemic biases and develop inclusive strategies that address the specific barriers faced by marginalized populations. Furthermore, the data exposes wide variations in job informality across different cities, correlating with local unemployment levels. High informality rates in certain regions indicate economic instability and a lack of formal protections for workers. This relevance to open data lies in the potential to create transparent, interactive maps that visualize these informal economies. Such tools can drive accountability, encourage civic engagement, and support the development of robust datasets that help governments and NGOs better understand and mitigate the challenges of precarious work.
