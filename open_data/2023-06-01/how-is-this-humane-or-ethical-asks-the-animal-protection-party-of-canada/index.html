How is this humane or ethical, asks the Animal Protection Party of Canada

Animal welfare groups condemn Ontario’s planned expansion of sporting dog compounds, arguing that training methods involving live wild animals constitute inherent cruelty. The release of an internal association letter highlights the intense stress placed on coyotes, foxes, and hares exposed to numerous dogs and participants. Critics assert that such practices violate fundamental animal welfare standards, as the environment is designed for dog training rather than the safety of the wildlife victims used as bait. The article argues that humane alternatives, such as scent-based training, are readily available and would eliminate the need to chase or kill wild animals. Experts question the feasibility of claims that organizers maintain complete control over hounds, suggesting that the current system relies on exploiting wildlife for sport. There is a strong call to ban the use of live animals in both compounds and general landscapes, emphasizing that pitting multiple dogs against a single wild victim is barbaric and inhumane. This issue is critically relevant to open data because the exposure of the Ontario Sporting Dog Association’s internal letter demonstrates the power of Freedom of Information requests in revealing hidden institutional realities. It underscores how transparent access to government and organizational records allows activists to challenge opaque legislation, like Bill 91, and mobilize public outrage against policies that prioritize industrial interests over ecological and ethical concerns.
