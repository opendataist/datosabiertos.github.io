The Senate’s Political Coordination Board has unanimously approved two slates of candidates to fill critical vacancies at the National Institute for Transparency, Information Access, and Personal Data Protection (INAI). This move fulfills a judicial mandate, aiming to restore the institution’s full operational capacity through an upcoming extraordinary session. The process highlights the tension between legal obligations and political resistance, as one seat remains unfilled due to a provisional suspension requested by the executive branch. This development is vital for open data governance because the INAI serves as the guardian of citizens’ rights to access public information and protect their personal data. Filling these positions is essential for maintaining institutional independence and ensuring that transparency laws are enforced without political interference. The composition of the new commissioners will significantly influence how strictly the government adheres to data protection standards and public accountability measures. The relevance to open data lies in the stability of the regulatory framework. When key oversight bodies are left vacant or their leaders suspended, the enforcement of transparency becomes precarious, potentially weakening public trust and limiting citizens’ access to essential government information. Ensuring these roles are filled promptly guarantees that mechanisms for data openness remain robust and effective, safeguarding democratic transparency against executive overreach.
