¿Tus apps protegen tu privacidad o te espían? El informe que revela riesgos ocultos en Colombia - Pulzo

¿Tus apps protegen tu privacidad o te espían? El informe que revela riesgos ocultos en Colombia - Pulzo

The article highlights a critical disparity in Colombia between the adoption of digital technology and the protection of data privacy. It reveals that numerous popular mobile applications request excessive permissions unrelated to their core functions, often collecting personal information without users’ explicit consent. This practice creates significant risks of data leaks and unauthorized commercial or illicit use, a problem exacerbated by the fact that most users do not carefully read terms and conditions before installation. This situation underscores a broader challenge within the open data ecosystem: the tension between the convenient data collection practices of developers and the fundamental right to privacy. Although Colombia has a legal framework designed to protect personal data, its effectiveness is undermined by insufficient oversight and the rapid pace of technological evolution. The gap between legislation and everyday reality means that companies frequently operate with impunity, failing to provide the transparency and genuine user control necessary for ethical data handling. For open data advocates, this report is relevant as it illustrates the necessity of robust governance and public awareness in the digital age. It emphasizes that data sovereignty cannot rely solely on legal frameworks; it requires active digital education and user vigilance. The narrative stresses that true open data integrity depends on establishing trust through transparency, ensuring that individuals understand how their information is used and retain the power to control their digital footprint.
