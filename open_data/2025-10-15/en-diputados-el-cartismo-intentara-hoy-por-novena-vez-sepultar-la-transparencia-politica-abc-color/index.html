En Diputados, el cartismo intentará hoy, por novena vez, sepultar la transparencia - Política - ABC Color

En Diputados, el cartismo intentará hoy, por novena vez, sepultar la transparencia - Política - ABC Color

Paraguay faces a critical legislative decision regarding its proposed Personal Data Protection Law, which has been repeatedly delayed due to controversy over Article 24. This specific provision threatens to undermine existing transparency laws by allowing any public official to independently withhold public data, shifting the authority to disclose information from institutions to the discretion of individual officials. This shift jeopardizes the mandatory publication of essential public information, effectively weakening proactive transparency mechanisms that require regular, automatic disclosure of basic institutional data. The bill’s original intent was to safeguard citizens’ sensitive personal information, such as biometric and financial data, a noble goal that has been compromised by political maneuvering. By prioritizing executive control over data release, the current proposals conflict with established legal frameworks designed to ensure public accountability. The situation reflects a broader pattern of political hostility toward citizen rights to access public records, with the ruling party’s majority holding the decisive power to either ratify the contested version or accept Senate modifications. This development is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the tension between data privacy protection and the fundamental principle of government transparency. If the law restricts the automatic disclosure of public sector information, it significantly hinders the availability of open data for civic engagement and oversight. Ultimately, the outcome will determine whether Paraguay strengthens digital rights while maintaining transparency or sacrifices open access in the name of data protection, setting a dangerous precedent for future information governance.
