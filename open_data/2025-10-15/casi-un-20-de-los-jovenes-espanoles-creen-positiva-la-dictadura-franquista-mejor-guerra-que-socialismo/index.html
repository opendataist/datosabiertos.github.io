Casi un 20% de los jóvenes españoles creen positiva la dictadura franquista: "Mejor guerra que socialismo"

Casi un 20% de los jóvenes españoles creen positiva la dictadura franquista: "Mejor guerra que socialismo"

Recent data reveals a troubling resurgence of favorable perceptions regarding the Franco dictatorship among Spanish youth, many of whom did not live through the regime. A significant minority views those years positively and considers contemporary democracy inferior, often romanticizing the era’s perceived stability and economic improvements over civil liberties. This sentiment highlights a dangerous historical amnesia where repression is minimized in favor of authoritarian efficiency, suggesting that young voters are increasingly susceptible to nostalgic narratives that distort democratic values. The normalization of authoritarian rhetoric extends beyond mere opinion, influencing political behavior and even academic contexts. Support for the dictatorship correlates strongly with voting for right-wing parties, indicating that historical revisionism is actively shaping the current political landscape. Furthermore, the casual use of "dictatorship" as a symbol of strength in creative exercises demonstrates how deeply embedded these distortions have become, potentially undermining the foundational principles of freedom and pluralism essential to a healthy society. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent, accessible historical statistics to combat misinformation. When official surveys and cultural indicators reveal such stark discrepancies between reality and public perception, open data initiatives become vital tools for education and accountability. By making reliable sociological data widely available, citizens can better understand their collective history and resist narratives that seek to dismantle democratic institutions through historical distortion.
