The case highlights the critical intersection of open government data collection and public safety. It underscores the inherent risks faced by statistical personnel conducting fieldwork in remote areas, emphasizing the need for robust safety protocols within national data institutions. It suggests that current monitoring systems may be insufficient, prompting calls for improved risk assessment and real-time tracking mechanisms to protect those who gather essential public information. The relevance to open data lies in the continuity of reliable statistical generation.
