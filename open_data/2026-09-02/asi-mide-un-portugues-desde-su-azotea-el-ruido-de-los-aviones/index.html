Así mide un portugués desde su azotea el ruido de los aviones

The Lisbon Finals case stands out as a prominent example of citizen-led open data, demonstrating how independent technological solutions can democratize access to environmental information. By automating the collection of flight metrics, noise levels, and vibrations, the platform provides transparent, freely accessible data to the public, activists, and aviation enthusiasts. This initiative highlights the power of open data in empowering communities to monitor and understand local impacts that official statistics may obscure or lack detail. The data reveals critical discrepancies regarding nocturnal flight restrictions, identifying thousands of potential violations and high-noise events affecting tens of thousands of residents. By quantifying the extent of noise pollution and flagging non-compliant flights, the platform challenges the adequacy of current regulatory measures. This objective, neutral dataset enables citizens to validate concerns about health and quality of life, shifting the debate from anecdotal complaints to evidence-based discourse. This case is significant for open data because it showcases a scalable model for civic technology that operates independently of political agendas. It demonstrates that low-cost, automated systems can generate high-quality, verifiable data to support environmental justice movements. Furthermore, it illustrates how open information can drive policy discussions, such as the urgent calls to close the city-center airport, by providing the concrete evidence needed to advocate for stronger protective measures and future infrastructure planning.
