Desafíos en el registro de nacimientos en Panamá: 24,636 menores sin identidad legal

Desafíos en el registro de nacimientos en Panamá: 24,636 menores sin identidad legal

The article emphasizes that the absence of birth registration in Panama constitutes a critical barrier that undermines the legal identity of minors, particularly in indigenous communities. This lack of documentation prevents access to fundamental rights such as healthcare and education, perpetuating cycles of social exclusion and discrimination. The main conclusion is that ensuring civil registration is an indispensable requirement for inclusion and the full enjoyment of citizenship rights, recognizing that legal identity is the gateway to other basic services. The relevance of this issue to open data lies in the importance of transparency and the quality of public information. Discrepancies between official civil registration figures and census data highlight the need to integrate diverse and standardized data sources. By using demographic variables shared among institutions, more accurate statistics can be generated, enabling the design of targeted public policies. This demonstrates how interconnecting government databases enhances the state’s capacity to identify and address invisible population gaps. Furthermore, the decentralization of registration services and the implementation of new digital systems illustrate how technology can facilitate access to citizenship. By connecting hospitals and district offices with centralized systems, under-registration is reduced and the integrity of national statistical data is strengthened. This approach reinforces the need for governments to maintain up-to-date and accessible data, not only for administrative management but also as a fundamental tool for accountability and the protection of the most vulnerable groups.
