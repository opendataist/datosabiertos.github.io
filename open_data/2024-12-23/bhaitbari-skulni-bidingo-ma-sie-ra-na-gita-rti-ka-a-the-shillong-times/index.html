This incident highlights systemic opacity in school governance, where officials evaded Right to Information requests regarding management committee elections and student admissions. Such behavior undermines transparency in educational institutions, demonstrating how public bodies can obstruct accountability for financial and administrative decisions. It underscores the critical need for open data policies in education. Mandating accessible records for admissions and committee operations ensures democratic oversight, prevents corruption, and empowers communities to hold schools accountable for public resources. Open data serves as a vital tool against administrative secrecy. By enforcing disclosure of key institutional records, society can verify electoral integrity and fair resource distribution, fostering trust and ensuring that public education serves the collective good rather than hidden interests.
