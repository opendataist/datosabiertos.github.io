Oaxaca de luto - El Imparcial de Oaxaca

The article argues that the disappearance of key autonomous bodies, particularly the INAI, constitutes a severe blow to democratic institutions and public trust. It emphasizes that transparency is fundamental to democracy, serving as a guarantee that the state acts in the public interest rather than consolidating unilateral executive power. The text suggests that eliminating these checks and balances leaves citizens vulnerable and undermines the essential separation of powers required for a functional democratic system. Central to this argument is the historical significance of the Declaration of Oaxaca, which established citizen participation as the cornerstone of access to information laws. The narrative highlights that true democracy relies on the assumption that citizens must actively defend their right to information, as this engagement ensures government accountability and legitimacy. Without independent oversight, the state loses the credibility needed to justify its actions and management of public resources, eroding the social contract between the government and the governed. This content is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical role of institutional independence in sustaining data accessibility regimes. It warns that removing autonomous regulators does not merely change administrative costs but fundamentally alters the power dynamic, potentially allowing the executive branch to control or withhold critical public information. For open data advocates, this underscores that technical standards for data publication are ineffective without robust, independent legal frameworks that protect the right of citizens to access and utilize government data without fear of repression or censorship.
