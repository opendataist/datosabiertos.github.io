India's Nonprofit Sector Still in Early Stages of AI Adoption; Almost 30 Per cent of Nonprofits not Yet Using AI, says Survey

India's Nonprofit Sector Still in Early Stages of AI Adoption; Almost 30 Per cent of Nonprofits not Yet Using AI, says Survey

The report reveals a significant digital divide within India’s nonprofit sector regarding artificial intelligence adoption. Early adopters, typically larger and urban-based, possess the necessary technical infrastructure and leadership engagement to leverage advanced AI tools beyond basic generative applications. In contrast, smaller, rural-focused organizations lag behind, showing minimal interest in expanding their use of AI and lacking the internal expertise required for deeper integration. This disparity highlights that organizational capacity, rather than just willingness, dictates AI readiness. Early adopters benefit from formal data policies, cloud storage, and dedicated technical staff, which collectively enable effective technology implementation. Conversely, the majority of late adopters operate with limited resources and unclear strategic goals, creating a barrier that prevents them from understanding or utilizing AI’s potential to enhance their social impact effectively. This analysis is critical to open data because it demonstrates how data literacy and infrastructure directly influence equity in technology access. Without targeted support and shared resources, the social sector risks widening the gap between well-resourced institutions and those serving marginalized communities. Ensuring equitable AI adoption requires open collaboration on data use cases and infrastructure, ensuring that technological advancements serve mission needs rather than exacerbating existing inequalities.
