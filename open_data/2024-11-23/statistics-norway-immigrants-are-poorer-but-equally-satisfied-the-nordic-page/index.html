Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly disadvantaged living conditions compared to the general population, encompassing lower incomes, poorer housing, and inferior employment opportunities. This economic and social inequality is accompanied by distinct health challenges, including higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness, indicating that structural barriers create tangible burdens on their well-being despite their integration efforts. Surprisingly, this objective hardship does not translate into subjective dissatisfaction. Immigrants report levels of life satisfaction and trust in state institutions—such as the judiciary and police—that match or exceed those of the native population. This paradox suggests a strong psychological resilience and a genuine affiliation with Norwegian society, highlighting that material deprivation does not necessarily erode social cohesion or faith in the state among immigrant communities. This findings are highly relevant to open data because they demonstrate the critical need for disaggregated datasets to capture nuanced social realities. Without specific demographic breakdowns, systemic inequalities in health and income might be masked by aggregate statistics that show overall high satisfaction. Access to such detailed, segmented data allows policymakers to target support effectively and ensures that vulnerable groups are not overlooked in broader societal analyses.
