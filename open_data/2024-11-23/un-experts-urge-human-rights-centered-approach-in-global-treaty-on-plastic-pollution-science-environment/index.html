UN Experts Urge Human Rights-Centered Approach in Global Treaty on Plastic Pollution | Science-Environment

UN Experts Urge Human Rights-Centered Approach in Global Treaty on Plastic Pollution | Science-Environment

UN experts urge that the final plastic pollution treaty must embed robust accountability and human rights principles to prevent the burden from shifting unfairly onto developing nations. This approach demands that producer countries and corporations bear equitable responsibility for the global crisis, ensuring that vulnerable states are not left to manage the impacts without adequate support or infrastructure. Transparency serves as a cornerstone for this framework, requiring public access to comprehensive data on chemicals and pollution volumes throughout the plastic lifecycle. Such openness enables effective monitoring of state and corporate responsibilities, while a global fund operationalizing the polluter-pays principle would finance waste management and sustainable transitions. This financial mechanism ensures that producers contribute directly to addressing the toxic legacy of plastics and supporting cleaner technologies. Relevance to open data lies in the explicit demand for accessible, detailed information on plastic production and pollution. Open data on chemicals, pollutant quantities, and manufacturing volumes is essential for enforcing reduction targets and safeguarding human rights. Without transparent, publicly available datasets, stakeholders cannot verify compliance, hold entities accountable, or evidence-based the just transitions required to mitigate environmental and health harms globally.
