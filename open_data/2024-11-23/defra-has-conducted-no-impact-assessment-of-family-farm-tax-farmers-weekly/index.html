Defra has conducted no impact assessment of family farm tax - Farmers Weekly

Defra has conducted no impact assessment of family farm tax - Farmers Weekly

The recent revelation that Defra conducted no impact assessment of the new inheritance tax reforms on British agriculture highlights a critical governance gap regarding open data and policy transparency. By failing to analyze the financial, food security, and mental health implications before implementation, the government demonstrated a lack of accountability and reliance on evidence-based decision-making. This absence of pre-policy analysis suggests that vital data required for informed public discourse was either not collected or not made available, undermining the principle that government actions should be supported by robust, accessible research. This case underscores the necessity of proactive data sharing and comprehensive impact studies in agricultural policy. Without empirical evidence to substantiate claims that only a small number of estates will be affected, stakeholders are left to speculate on the true scale of the disruption to farming families and rural communities. The failure to gather and publish this data raises serious concerns about the government’s understanding of the sector’s realities, suggesting that policy decisions were made in isolation from the communities most affected by them. Ultimately, this incident is relevant to open data because it illustrates the risks of operating without transparent, verified data trails. It emphasizes that for democracy to function effectively, citizens and industry bodies must have access to the evidence underpinning major legislative changes. The reliance on unverified assertions rather than publicly available, rigorous impact assessments weakens trust in institutional processes and highlights the urgent need for standardized, open data practices in all government departments, particularly those dealing with significant economic and social reforms.
