Aug 2022: High processing fees for freedom of information requests slammed

The article highlights a significant controversy where Manitoba Justice requested nearly $32,000 in processing fees for freedom of information requests regarding a secret RCMP investigation into former Winnipeg Mayor Sam Katz. Experts and politicians condemned these fees as outrageous, arguing they represent a political strategy to obstruct public access to information. This approach is viewed as violating both the spirit and letter of freedom of information laws, which are designed to ensure transparency rather than serve as barriers for citizens seeking accountability. Critics emphasize that requiring the public to pay for government-held documents is fundamentally unjust, as taxpayers have already funded the collection of this data. The excessive fees are interpreted as a deliberate tactic to discourage inquiries into sensitive matters, reflecting a broader national pattern where governments misuse access-to-information frameworks. Advocates argue that such practices undermine democratic principles and suggest that the government’s resistance to providing details indicates an intent to hide information rather than maintain openness regarding past investigations. This case is critically relevant to open data because it illustrates how administrative barriers can stifle the flow of public information, even when the data exists. It underscores the tension between institutional opacity and the public’s right to know, particularly in high-profile cases involving potential corruption. The incident serves as a cautionary example of how fee structures and procedural hurdles can be weaponized to suppress transparency, reinforcing the need for robust policies that prioritize accessible, uncensored open data for civic engagement and oversight.
