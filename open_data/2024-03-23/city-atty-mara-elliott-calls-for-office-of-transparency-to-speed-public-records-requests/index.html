City Atty. Mara Elliott Calls for 'Office of Transparency' to Speed Public Records Requests

City Atty. Mara Elliott Calls for 'Office of Transparency' to Speed Public Records Requests

San Diego City officials are considering a new Office of Transparency to overhaul the city’s fragmented and inefficient public records response system. A two-year study revealed that the current decentralized approach leads to inconsistent applications, technical failures, and significant legal liabilities due to non-compliance with public records acts. By centralizing request management, the city aims to eliminate communication gaps between departments, reduce litigation risks, and ensure accurate, timely responses to public inquiries. While council members express strong initial support for this modernization, they have mandated a thorough cost-benefit analysis before proceeding. The primary concern involves validating the proposal’s claim to be budget-neutral by reorganizing existing staff rather than creating new positions. The committee will collaborate with the Independent Budget Analyst to confirm the financial viability and determine the optimal implementation strategy, ensuring that anticipated savings from reduced legal payouts and improved efficiency can actually fund the necessary structural changes. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates as it highlights the systemic barriers to information accessibility in large municipal governments. The current cumbersome infrastructure not only discourages public engagement but also fosters mistrust due to unpredictable and delayed disclosures. By moving toward a coordinated, centralized model, the city addresses critical issues of transparency and accountability, setting a potential precedent for how major urban centers can effectively manage high volumes of data requests while maintaining public trust and operational integrity.
