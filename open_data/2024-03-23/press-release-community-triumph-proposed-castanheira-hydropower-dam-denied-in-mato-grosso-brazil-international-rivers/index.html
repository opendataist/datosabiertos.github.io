PRESS RELEASE | Community Triumph: Proposed Castanheira Hydropower Dam Denied in Mato Grosso, Brazil - International Rivers

The suspension of the Castanheira hydroelectric dam project in Mato Grosso marks a significant victory for Indigenous rights and environmental protection, demonstrating that community mobilization can successfully challenge state-backed infrastructure. This decision highlights the critical importance of rigorous environmental licensing processes that genuinely respect local populations and adhere to legal standards, rather than prioritizing rapid energy expansion at the expense of human rights and ecological integrity. This case underscores the necessity of transparency and public participation in data-driven decision-making, particularly when dealing with large-scale projects affecting vulnerable communities. By exposing technical flaws and cumulative impacts, civil society organizations proved that accessible, accurate, and scrutinized environmental data is essential for holding authorities accountable. It reinforces the principle that open, participatory licensing procedures are vital for preventing irreversible damage to biodiversity and respecting the sovereignty of Indigenous lands. For the open data community, this outcome serves as a powerful reminder that data must serve as a tool for empowerment and oversight, not just bureaucratic compliance. The struggle demonstrates how robust data collection and analysis, when combined with active civic engagement, can prevent harmful developments and promote sustainable alternatives. Ultimately, it advocates for a future where energy policies are informed by comprehensive, accessible information that prioritizes long-term ecological health and social justice over short-term industrial gains.
