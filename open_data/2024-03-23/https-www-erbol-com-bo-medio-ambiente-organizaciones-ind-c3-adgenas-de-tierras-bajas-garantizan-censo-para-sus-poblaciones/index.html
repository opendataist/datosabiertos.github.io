Indigenous organizations in Bolivia’s lowlands are proactively organizing census logistics due to operational gaps at the National Institute of Statistics. By securing their own transport, supplies, and enumerators, these groups ensure accurate demographic coverage in remote areas. This self-reliance highlights a critical infrastructure failure in public data collection, where state agencies cannot reach marginalized populations without indigenous intervention. The census serves as a vital tool for securing rights and resources. Accurate data allows communities to substantiate demands for basic services such as health and education, while also informing territorial management plans. However, the absence of specific categories for Autonomous Indigenous Governments creates a data vacuum, undermining the planning capacity of these newly empowered entities and obscuring the reality of their residents. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates that official statistics are often incomplete unless complemented by community-generated information. The reliance on external support and local organization reveals systemic biases in national data systems. True open data integrity requires acknowledging these gaps and integrating indigenous-led data collection methods to ensure no population is statistically invisible.
