Adobe wants to make it easier for artists to blacklist their work from AI scraping

Adobe wants to make it easier for artists to blacklist their work from AI scraping

Adobe is introducing a public beta for content authentication, enabling creators to digitally sign their media regardless of the creation tool. This initiative promotes transparency by allowing artists to clarify the origin and usage terms of their work, effectively acting as a modern signature. The move supports a growing movement where creators defend against unauthorized AI training by embedding explicit restrictions. By making it easier to attach these digital credentials, the technology empowers artists to control how their intellectual property is used and consumed by automated systems. This development is crucial for open data initiatives, as it establishes verifiable provenance standards for digital assets. It encourages the responsible inclusion of human-created data in AI training sets, fostering a more ethical and sustainable ecosystem for media sharing and data accessibility.
