The Heritage Foundation’s massive use of the Freedom of Information Act exemplifies how public records tools can be weaponized against privacy rather than enhancing accountability. Instead of fostering transparency, this strategy uses voluminous, targeted requests to identify and scrutinize specific government employees, effectively turning a democratic safeguard into an instrument of political intimidation. This approach shifts the focus from institutional oversight to individual harassment, creating a chilling effect on civil servants. By seeking communications based on keywords rather than specific wrongdoing, the campaign aims to map and potentially remove perceived ideological obstacles, thereby undermining the neutrality and stability of the public service. For open data advocates, this case highlights the critical importance of intent in data access. It demonstrates that unfettered information retrieval can erode trust and safety within government systems. Protecting the integrity of open data requires recognizing that excessive, malicious scraping harms the very transparency it claims to serve, necessitating ethical boundaries in how such tools are deployed.
