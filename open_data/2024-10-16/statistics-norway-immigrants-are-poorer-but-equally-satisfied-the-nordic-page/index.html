Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly disadvantaged living conditions compared to the native population, including lower incomes, poorer housing, and employment gaps. Despite these structural inequalities, immigrants report equal satisfaction with life in Norway and maintain strong institutional confidence. This paradox highlights that subjective well-being does not always correlate with objective socioeconomic status, suggesting that cultural integration and perceived security play crucial roles in immigrant resilience. Mental health disparities remain a critical concern, with immigrants experiencing higher rates of psychological distress and loneliness than the general population. However, this group exhibits notably high trust in the political system, judiciary, and police, surpassing native Norwegians in institutional reliance. This trust may serve as a protective factor against social alienation, indicating that strong civic engagement can mitigate the negative impacts of material disadvantage and social isolation. This data is vital for open data initiatives as it challenges simplistic narratives that equate socioeconomic metrics with overall life satisfaction. By providing granular insights into mental health, trust, and social connection, the report enables policymakers and researchers to develop nuanced interventions. Open access to such comprehensive datasets encourages cross-sectoral collaboration, ensuring that future housing, health, and integration policies are informed by a holistic understanding of immigrant experiences rather than economic indicators alone.
