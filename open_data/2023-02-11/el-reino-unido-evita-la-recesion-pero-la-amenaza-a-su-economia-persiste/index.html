El Reino Unido evita la recesión, pero la amenaza a su economía persiste

The British economy avoided a technical recession thanks to the stagnation in the last quarter of 2022, although this escape is precarious given that GDP fell significantly in December. While the government highlights the resilience of the system, critical risks persist, such as runaway inflation and high interest rates, which are already pressuring consumption and triggering widespread strikes. The outlook indicates that the country continues to operate below pre-pandemic levels, with a structural slowdown that deeply affects social well-being and commercial activity. This situation is relevant to the field of open data because it underscores the need to monitor economic indicators in real time with high resolution. Official data, although published with some delay, allow for the rapid identification of critical monthly deviations that quarterly projections might overlook. Transparency in these indicators enables analysts and citizens to understand the depth of economic distress, promoting more informed civic oversight of fiscal and monetary policies that directly impact their quality of life. Moreover, the accuracy of national statistics is essential for anticipating future consequences, such as the likely economic contraction projected for the following year. Free and detailed access to this information allows various stakeholders to evaluate the effectiveness of government measures in addressing inflation and energy crises. In a context where the country faces unique challenges within the G7, open data not only document the present but also serve as an essential tool for accountability and the design of evidence-based solutions.
