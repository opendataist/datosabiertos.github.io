Remueve INAI a funcionarios acusados de extorsión por FMF | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The Mexican Transparency Institute has dismissed two officials from their positions to facilitate ongoing investigations into allegations made by the Mexican Football Federation. These officials were accused of allegedly requesting World Cup tickets and influencing fine calculations, raising serious questions about institutional integrity and the potential misuse of public data mechanisms. This situation is highly relevant to open data, as it stems from previous penalties imposed on the Football Federation for violating personal data protection laws. The incident highlights the critical importance of safeguarding sensitive information and maintaining ethical standards in data handling. It underscores how failures in data governance can lead to legal consequences and damage public trust in regulatory bodies. Ultimately, the decision aims to ensure impartiality and protect the institution during its dissolution phase. The case serves as a reminder that accountability and transparency are essential, even in temporary or transitioning government structures. It emphasizes the need for robust internal controls to prevent abuse and ensure that data protection principles are respected, reinforcing the broader commitment to open and secure data practices in Mexico.
