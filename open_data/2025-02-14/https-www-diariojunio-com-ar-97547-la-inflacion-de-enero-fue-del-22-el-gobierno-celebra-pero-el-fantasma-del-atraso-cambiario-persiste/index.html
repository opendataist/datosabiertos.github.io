The report highlights significant variations in consumer prices across sectors and regions, with hospitality and housing costs driving substantial monthly increases. This granular breakdown reveals how inflation impacts different economic activities and geographic areas unevenly, particularly affecting food expenses in several major regions. Such detailed sectoral data is essential for understanding the structural drivers of inflation beyond aggregate figures. The cost of the basic food basket increased modestly in January, though it remains significantly higher than the previous year. These metrics provide critical insights into household affordability and poverty thresholds. Tracking these specific costs allows for a more accurate assessment of social welfare and the real economic pressure on families, rather than relying solely on broad monetary indicators. This data is relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the value of transparent, accessible statistical information for public accountability. By making detailed inflation reports publicly available, the government enables independent analysis and debate. This transparency allows experts and citizens to verify official claims about economic stabilization, fostering trust through verifiable evidence rather than political narratives.
