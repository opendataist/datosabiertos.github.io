Northern Ireland's proposed conversion therapy ban likely to target ordinary parents and Christians

Northern Ireland's proposed conversion therapy ban likely to target ordinary parents and Christians

Freedom of Information requests indicate that complaints regarding medical conversion therapy in Northern Ireland are virtually nonexistent. This scarcity suggests that proposed legislation targeting such practices would not address genuine clinical harms but instead criminalize ordinary religious expressions, biblical study, and parental guidance for children questioning their gender identity or sexual orientation. The relevance to open_data lies in how FOI processes can expose the disconnect between legislative premises and empirical reality. By making institutional data transparent, stakeholders can verify that the problem the law aims to solve is largely fictional. This transparency allows citizens to challenge regulations based on unfounded fears rather than actual evidence of abuse, highlighting the power of data in democratic oversight. Consequently, the proposed ban risks infringing on fundamental freedoms by punishing innocent actions like prayer or conversation. The narrative demonstrates that without solid data supporting the need for such laws, regulations may serve to suppress specific religious and ideological viewpoints rather than protect patients, urging a critical examination of how data informs public policy.
