Tlalpan has significantly advanced its transparency and accountability standards, positioning itself as a leader in open government within Mexico. This achievement reflects a strategic shift toward preventing corruption through joint alliances and robust institutional frameworks. The local administration has achieved top rankings in national metrics, demonstrating that high compliance with information obligations correlates directly with effective governance and public trust. The municipality’s success stems from intensive staff training, with nearly all transparency units and archives achieving full qualification. By transforming employees into certified trainers, Tlalpan fosters a sustained culture of transparency rather than relying on temporary measures. This internal capacity building ensures that public servants can effectively manage data protection and respond to citizen inquiries, thereby institutionalizing openness at every administrative level. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how technical compliance and cultural training converge to improve civic access. It proves that structured transparency programs enable better service delivery and data management. For open data initiatives, Tlalpan’s model highlights the necessity of empowering public employees with the tools and knowledge to facilitate information flow, ensuring that government data remains accessible, secure, and usable for society.
