This article highlights how Freedom of Information requests can uncover the hidden cultural and political narratives within government offices. By revealing which artworks adorn the walls of top politicians, it demonstrates the practical application of transparency in accessing government-held information. This case study shows that open data mechanisms can expose subtle biases and preferences, such as the exclusion of female prime ministers or the symbolic choice of art reflecting specific political ideologies. The findings suggest that the selection of parliamentary art is not merely decorative but serves as a reflection of institutional values and political identity. For open data advocates, this illustrates how seemingly mundane datasets, like inventory records, can yield significant insights into organizational culture. It emphasizes the importance of scrutinizing non-traditional data sources to understand the broader context of political decision-making and resource allocation. Relevance to open data lies in its demonstration of how public access to government information can enrich public discourse and hold institutions accountable. It encourages further exploration of diverse data types to uncover hidden stories within the public sector. Ultimately, this reinforces the value of transparency in ensuring that all aspects of government operations are subject to public scrutiny, fostering a more informed and engaged citizenry.
