Aprueba Congreso de Puebla reforma al artículo 480 y la Ley de Transparencia

Aprueba Congreso de Puebla reforma al artículo 480 y la Ley de Transparencia

The Congress of Puebla approved a comprehensive Transparency and Access to Information Law, establishing a clear framework for open government data. By designating the anti-corruption body as the guarantor of transparency and personal data protection, the legislation ensures that public entities, including municipalities and those managing public resources, must adhere to strict standards regarding the disclosure and safeguarding of information. This move significantly enhances accountability and facilitates citizen access to state-held data. Simultaneously, lawmakers reformed the Criminal Code to address modern digital threats, specifically introducing penalties for "cyber siege" and illegal land invasions. The recognition of cyber violence as a distinct crime highlights the urgent need to protect individuals in digital spaces, while stricter sanctions for property invasions aim to secure territorial rights. These legal updates reflect a broader effort to adapt judicial responses to contemporary social challenges and technological realities. These reforms are highly relevant to the open data movement because they institutionalize transparency as a legal obligation rather than a voluntary practice. By legally mandating the protection of personal data alongside the right to information, the state creates a balanced ecosystem where data openness promotes good governance without compromising individual privacy. This legal foundation is essential for building trust and enabling the effective use of public data for civic oversight and innovation.
