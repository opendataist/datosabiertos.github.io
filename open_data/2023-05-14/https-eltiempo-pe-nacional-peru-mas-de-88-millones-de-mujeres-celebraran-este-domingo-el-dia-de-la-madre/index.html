The article presents demographic and socioeconomic insights from Peru’s official statistical agency, highlighting significant disparities in fertility rates and maternal characteristics. It reveals that rural populations, women with lower educational attainment, and those from indigenous backgrounds tend to have more children and start families earlier compared to their urban, highly educated counterparts. These patterns underscore deep-seated social and economic inequalities affecting family planning and maternal health across different sectors of society. The data also illustrates the strong labor force participation among Peruvian mothers, with the majority actively engaged in employment, predominantly in informal or self-employed work categories. This high level of economic activity coexists with varying levels of educational achievement and family size, reflecting complex socioeconomic realities. The findings serve as a critical reference for policymakers aiming to address issues related to gender, education, healthcare, and labor rights in a diverse national context. This content is relevant to open data because it relies on transparent, publicly accessible government statistics to inform public policy. Such open data enables researchers and civil society to analyze social trends, advocate for equitable reforms, and monitor progress toward sustainable development goals, ensuring that decisions are evidence-based and inclusive.
