Tesla contrata trabajadores por USD 48 la hora para usar trajes de captura de movimiento y recopilar datos para entrenar su robot humanoide

Tesla contrata trabajadores por USD 48 la hora para usar trajes de captura de movimiento y recopilar datos para entrenar su robot humanoide

Tesla is ramping up the development of its Optimus humanoid robots by hiring operators to collect motion data using motion-capture suits and virtual reality. This strategy aims to feed the artificial intelligence needed to automate repetitive or hazardous tasks in its factories, leveraging labor shortages as a key driver pushing the industry toward automation. Despite initial skepticism and delays in promised deliveries, Tesla has made progress toward operational autonomy, with plans for internal implementation and external sales. However, competition has intensified with well-capitalized rivals, raising significant challenges regarding the technical feasibility and exorbitant costs of scaling robot production to a large scale, without any guarantee of technological success. This case is relevant to the open-data community because it illustrates the growing importance of human motion datasets as critical assets for training robotics models. The intensive collection of physical data to train AI highlights the need for ethical frameworks, standardization, and potentially the sharing of these datasets to accelerate responsible innovation and lower barriers to entry in the development of general-purpose artificial intelligence.
