Potential Destruction of Public Records by LA City Exposed: A Threat t

The article highlights a critical transparency failure in Los Angeles, where the city may be automatically deleting electronic records generated through Google Workspace tools, such as internal chats. This potential destruction of data, possibly within 24 hours, violates state laws mandating the retention of public records for at least two years. The lack of confirmed archival protocols raises serious ethical concerns about institutional integrity and the deliberate obscuring of evidence. This situation is particularly alarming because it undermines legal accountability and public trust. If the city is configured to delete these communications, it risks violating discovery obligations in litigation, including civil rights and land use cases. The ambiguity creates two disturbing possibilities: either records are being actively destroyed to hide information, or they are being saved but withheld from the public and litigants, both of which constitute severe breaches of open data principles. For the open data community, this case serves as a vital warning about the hidden risks of cloud-based government collaboration tools. It emphasizes that adopting modern technology does not exempt agencies from their legal duty to preserve public records. The article urges citizens and officials to demand clear answers regarding data retention settings, ensuring that government transparency is not sacrificed for operational convenience or secrecy.
