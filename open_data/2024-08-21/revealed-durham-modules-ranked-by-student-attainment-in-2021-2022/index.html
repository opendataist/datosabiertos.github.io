Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

This article highlights the critical role of Freedom of Information Requests in democratizing academic performance data. By leveraging FOI tools, independent platforms can access and publish institutional metrics that are often opaque to the public. This transparency allows students and researchers to scrutinize grading standards, fostering a more accountable educational environment where data serves as a public good rather than internal administrative secrecy. The findings reveal significant disparities in attainment across different disciplines and academic levels, underscoring how open data can expose systemic variations in grading rigor. For instance, certain departments consistently achieve higher average marks and first-class honors compared to others, while foundational and specific language modules show notably lower success rates. These insights allow stakeholders to question whether grade inflation or differing assessment complexities drive these outcomes, encouraging a deeper, data-informed dialogue about academic standards. Relevance to open data lies in its capacity to validate institutional claims with empirical evidence. When academic statistics are made accessible, it empowers users to compare programs, understand student experiences, and identify areas needing policy review. This case demonstrates that releasing granular assessment data not only satisfies legal transparency requirements but also enriches public understanding of higher education quality, proving that open data is essential for meaningful scrutiny and improvement in academic institutions.
