Ex-Michigan staffer Connor Stalions did not file any expense reports, AP's FOIA request finds

The case highlights significant challenges in verifying athletic compliance through transparent record-keeping. By demonstrating that no expense reports existed for a key staffer during a critical period, the investigation reveals gaps in financial transparency. This absence of documentation complicates efforts to trace expenditures related to scouting activities, underscoring the importance of open administrative data in holding institutions accountable. Furthermore, the incident illustrates how digital footprints and purchasing patterns can serve as proxies for uncovering prohibited conduct. The reliance on ticket purchases and third-party video acquisition demonstrates that modern recruiting violations often leave indirect traces. For open data advocates, this emphasizes the value of accessible transactional records and the necessity of robust data governance to prevent hidden operational practices within high-profile organizations. Ultimately, this article is relevant to open data because it exposes the tension between institutional secrecy and public oversight. When critical evidence is derived from fragmented or missing records, it hinders true accountability. Ensuring that financial and operational data from educational institutions is accessible and auditable is essential for maintaining integrity in competitive environments, allowing stakeholders to verify claims of ethical conduct rather than relying on unverified denials.
