Retired Bureaucrats and Military Officials Living Abroad Get Pension in US Dollars

Foreign affairs and defense ministries withheld critical data on pension payments to expatriate civil and military retirees, initially refusing transparency despite Right to Information requests. This secrecy highlights systemic failures in government accountability, where citizen inquiries are met with institutional resistance rather than openness. The case underscores the vital role of activists in challenging opaque administrative practices. By persistently seeking information through legal channels, individuals can expose discrepancies between domestic grievances and international benefits, forcing authorities to justify their information management strategies. This narrative is relevant to open data as it demonstrates how citizen-led transparency efforts can break down barriers to public sector information. It emphasizes that accessible data is not automatic; it requires active engagement and legal pressure to ensure equitable information flow for all stakeholders.
