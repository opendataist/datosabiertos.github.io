Banco Nacional le ofreció al BCCR su propio indicador de riesgo climático anonimizado, dijo gerente del banco

The National Bank’s refusal to provide individualized credit data to the Central Bank of Costa Rica highlights the tension between regulatory transparency and privacy protection. While other banks complied with the request, which included sensitive information without anonymization, the National Bank argued that its own climate risk analysis methodology, developed using aggregated data over decades, already satisfied the objectives of the International Monetary Fund. This stance demonstrates that it is possible to manage complex systemic risks without exposing citizens’ identities, offering an alternative model that prioritizes the public good over the mass collection of personal data. The relevance of this case for open data lies in its challenge to the supposed need for identifiable data for technical and policy decision-making. By rejecting anonymization requirements, the National Bank emphasizes that analytical utility does not necessarily depend on the exposure of individual identities, but rather on the quality and context of aggregated data. This sparks a crucial debate about how institutions should balance their operational requirements with the right to privacy, suggesting that interoperability and robust statistical analysis can achieve the same public policy goals through the responsible use of non-personal information. Finally, the crisis illustrates the legal and ethical implications of reusing financial data, where the lack of explicit consent for purposes not initially agreed upon can lead to significant legal conflicts. The event underscores the importance of defining clear frameworks for information exchange between public and private entities, ensuring that open data or analytical initiatives do not violate data protection laws. This situation serves as an important precedent for establishing limits on requests for sensitive information, promoting a culture of responsibility in data management that protects both institutional efficiency and citizens’ fundamental rights.
