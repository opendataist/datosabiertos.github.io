The National Gallery in Prague has initiated a comprehensive five-year reconstruction of Albrecht Dürer’s Renaissance masterpiece, aiming to reverse centuries of deterioration caused by mishandling and inferior historical restorations. This extensive effort is necessary because the painting’s physical condition has been severely compromised since the late sixteenth century, with original paint detaching and previous repairs actively damaging the artwork. The restoration seeks to preserve the cultural integrity of this significant piece, ensuring it survives for future generations by addressing the cumulative effects of time and poor conservation practices. Advanced scientific techniques, including macro-XRF scanning and 3D modeling, will be employed to analyze the painting’s composition and structural stability. These methods allow experts to map the material layers and understand the condition of the wooden base without causing further harm. By utilizing technology similar to that used in other major European art restorations, the project aims to reveal hidden details and carefully remove non-original additions. This precise, data-driven approach ensures that the visual rehabilitation respects the artist’s original intent while correcting earlier aesthetic interventions. This project is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it highlights the value of transparent, scientific documentation in cultural heritage preservation. The detailed scans and digital models generated during the reconstruction will likely become public resources, offering researchers and the general public unprecedented insights into Dürer’s techniques and the painting’s history. By making these technical findings accessible, the project demonstrates how open data can democratize art history, allowing anyone to study the intricate layers of a masterpiece remotely.
