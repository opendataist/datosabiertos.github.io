El número de viajeros por autobús aumentó un 4,4% en C-LM en noviembre hasta alcanzar las 2.031.000 personas

Spanish transport data reveals a robust recovery in high-speed rail and intercity mobility, signaling increased public reliance on efficient transit options. This surge in specific segments highlights a growing preference for speed and connectivity over traditional, slower modes, reflecting broader trends in urban planning and infrastructure investment. Conversely, certain local services, such as metro and school transport, experienced declines, suggesting shifting mobility patterns or demographic changes. These contrasts emphasize the need for nuanced data analysis to understand varying user behaviors across different regions and transport types, rather than relying solely on aggregate national figures. This dataset is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the value of granular, disaggregated statistics. By breaking down performance by mode, region, and service type, policymakers and researchers can identify specific inefficiencies and opportunities. Such detailed, accessible information enables evidence-based decisions to optimize public transport networks, improve service equity, and respond dynamically to changing travel demands across the country.
