Three reported UFO sightings across greater Belfast in 2024 | BreakingNews.ie

Three reported UFO sightings across greater Belfast in 2024 | BreakingNews.ie

In 2024, the Police Service of Northern Ireland recorded only three reported UFO sightings, a figure experts consider surprisingly low compared to historical trends. These incidents, described as silent flying objects with strange lights, were merely noted for information without formal investigation. This minimal official count contrasts sharply with the hundreds of reports previously handled by the Ministry of Defence, suggesting a significant disconnect between public perception of the sky and official record-keeping. The scarcity of reports likely stems from societal hesitation rather than an absence of events. Many witnesses avoid reporting sightings due to fears of ridicule, disbelief, or concerns that authorities are concealing the truth. Additionally, confusion regarding the appropriate jurisdiction—whether the police or defense ministries handle such cases—further discourages submissions. Consequently, the actual number of aerial anomalies is believed to be far higher than the documented cases, driven by a cultural tendency to dismiss or ignore unexplained phenomena. This data is pertinent to open data initiatives as it highlights the limitations of official transparency and the gap between perceived and actual events. When public reporting is suppressed by stigma or bureaucratic indifference, open data sets fail to reflect reality, undermining their utility for analysis and public understanding. Examining these gaps encourages a broader inquiry into how institutions manage unverified citizen inputs, emphasizing the need for open datasets that capture not just official outcomes, but also the nuances of public engagement and trust in data collection processes.
