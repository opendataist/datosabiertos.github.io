En el primer año de Milei, el presupuesto a las universidades cayó un 30% y los salarios docentes un 23%

The drastic reduction in funding for public universities has triggered a profound institutional crisis and widespread social backlash, as evidenced by massive student and academic protests. This tension reflects a significant rupture in democratic governance, where budgetary transparency and university autonomy are being contested by the executive branch, which justifies its measures under narratives of control and efficiency—claims that educational institutions refute with verifiable, publicly available data. The central dispute revolves around the legitimacy of data concerning university management and teaching quality. While the government promotes external audits and questions the integrity of student records, universities defend the robustness of their internal information systems and their track record of regulatory compliance. This conflict exposes the fragility of accountability mechanisms when the management of public resources becomes politicized, complicating institutions’ ability to maintain academic independence in the face of external political pressures. This case is crucial for the open data movement because it demonstrates how opacity or manipulation of fiscal information can serve as a tool of power rather than facilitating citizen scrutiny. The struggle to promote clear and accessible funding legislation is not merely an economic demand but a fundamental requirement to ensure that public data are open, understandable, and auditable by society, thereby guaranteeing the sustainability and genuine transparency of the state education sector.
