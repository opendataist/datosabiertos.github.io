Therese Bottomly will retire from The Oregonian/OregonLive after 42 years

Therese Bottomly’s upcoming retirement highlights the critical role of newsroom leadership in safeguarding democratic values. Throughout her distinguished career, she has championed governmental transparency, using legal expertise to protect journalists’ access to public records. This dedication ensures that accountability reporting remains robust, serving as a model for how editorial leaders can actively defend the public’s right to know against barriers to information. Beyond external transparency, Bottomly fostered internal accountability through initiatives like the clean slate committee and diverse representation efforts. By encouraging ethical journalism and confronting institutional biases, she demonstrated that open data and open newsrooms must also address historical injustices. Her approach underscores the importance of continuous self-examination within media organizations to maintain credibility and serve all community members fairly. This article is relevant to open_data because it illustrates the symbiotic relationship between transparent institutions and effective data journalism. Bottomly’s legacy shows that accurate, accessible information is not just a technical asset but a moral imperative. Her commitment to exposing inequalities and protecting record access reminds the open_data community that true transparency requires both technical openness and a steadfast commitment to justice and community trust.
