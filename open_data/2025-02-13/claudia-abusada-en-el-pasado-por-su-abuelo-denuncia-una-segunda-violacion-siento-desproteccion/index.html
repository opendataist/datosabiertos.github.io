Claudia, abusada en el pasado por su abuelo, denuncia una segunda violación: "Siento desprotección"

Sexual violence in Spain remains a critical crisis, exacerbated by a justice system that fails to protect victims. High rates of unreported assaults stem from profound fear and institutional distrust, creating a cycle of impunity. Structural reforms in judicial and educational frameworks are urgently needed to ensure victim safety and hold perpetrators accountable. This context highlights a significant transparency gap in open data initiatives regarding gender-based violence. While official statistics exist, they only capture a fraction of the reality due to widespread underreporting. Accurate public data is essential to reflect the true scale of the issue, yet current reporting mechanisms often obscure the full extent of the crisis, hindering effective policy formulation. Relevant to open data, this situation underscores the necessity of complementary, high-quality datasets that capture social dynamics and victim experiences beyond official crime records. Without comprehensive and accessible information, policymakers cannot address the root causes of violence or measure the impact of interventions. Transparent data is crucial for driving the societal and legislative changes required to support survivors and prevent future abuses.
