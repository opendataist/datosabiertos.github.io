IN STATS: Why did Sweden reject Brits' applications for post-Brexit residency?

The document reveals a significant discrepancy in how the Swedish Migration Agency processed post-Brexit residency applications, challenging claims of strict but fair legal interpretation. Statistical analysis demonstrates that a vast majority of rejected applicants had actually submitted their requests before the deadline, with most denials based on disputed interpretations of EU law requirements regarding employment and savings. This suggests the agency may have applied overly rigid standards that do not align with established European regulations. The consequences for these applicants have been severe, involving high rates of rejection and subsequent deportation orders, despite many having applied promptly. Many individuals cited ignorance, health issues, or misinformation from other Swedish agencies as reasons for any delays, rather than intentional non-compliance. The high number of rejections for on-time applications indicates that the barrier to residency was often substantive rather than procedural, leaving many UK citizens in a state of legal limbo or facing removal from the country. This case is critically relevant to open data because it highlights how transparent access to government statistics can expose systemic administrative failures. By utilizing Freedom of Information requests to obtain and analyze raw database records, journalists and citizens can hold public agencies accountable for unjustified decision-making. It underscores the power of data transparency in uncovering biases in immigration enforcement and ensuring that legal protections are applied equitably rather than arbitrarily.
