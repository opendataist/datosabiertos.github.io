¿Cuántos puestos de trabajo se perdieron en la construcción?

The article concludes that the new government’s strict policy of halting public works has caused a severe contraction in the construction sector, resulting in the loss of approximately 100,000 formal jobs. This drastic reduction highlights the immediate economic impact of freezing state-led infrastructure projects, demonstrating that such fiscal austerity measures directly translate into significant employment shortages rather than efficient market adjustments. A key implication is that relying on private investment to replace public infrastructure spending is unrealistic in the current economic context. Industry leaders emphasize that private funding typically covers only a small fraction of public works globally, and without state support, the sector cannot sustain its previous workforce levels. Consequently, the collapse in employment stems primarily from the absence of government projects, revealing a capacity gap that private actors alone cannot fill due to current market conditions and lack of security. This situation is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent, timely, and accessible statistical information to monitor public policy outcomes. The article notes a significant lag in official data, which delays the confirmation of these economic losses and hinders informed public debate. Reliable open data systems are essential to verify claims, assess the real impact of governance decisions on employment, and ensure accountability regarding the trade-offs between fiscal austerity and social welfare.
