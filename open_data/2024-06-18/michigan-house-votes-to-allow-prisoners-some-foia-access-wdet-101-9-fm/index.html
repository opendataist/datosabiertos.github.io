Michigan House votes to allow prisoners some FOIA access - WDET 101.9 FM

A proposed Michigan bill would grant incarcerated individuals limited access to the state’s Freedom of Information Act. This legislation allows prisoners to directly request records concerning their own arrest, prosecution, or that of their minor children, removing the current legal ban on such access. The measure aims to recognize the humanity of prisoners by providing them with the same rights to transparency as those outside of prison. Proponents argue that direct access enables inmates to potentially uncover evidence that could correct wrongful convictions, emphasizing that existing third-party requests are insufficient for ensuring justice and due process. This development is highly relevant to open_data because it challenges the exclusion of a marginalized population from data transparency frameworks. By extending information rights to those behind bars, the bill highlights how equitable access to public records is essential for accountability, particularly in criminal justice contexts where data can significantly impact individual liberty and legal outcomes.
