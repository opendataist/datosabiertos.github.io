Henrico envelope company cited, fined for safety failures after employee crushed to death in machine

Henrico envelope company cited, fined for safety failures after employee crushed to death in machine

This case highlights the critical role of open data in enforcing workplace safety standards. By leveraging Freedom of Information Act requests to access public records from the Virginia Department of Labor and Industry, journalists uncovered a pattern of regulatory non-compliance that might otherwise have remained hidden. The transparency provided by these government citations allows the public and the media to scrutinize corporate accountability, demonstrating how accessible official data serves as a vital check on industrial safety practices. The investigation revealed that the company faced multiple serious violations following a fatal accident, including failures to implement proper lockout procedures and inadequate employee training. Crucially, these were not isolated incidents but part of a recurring history of neglect, with previous citations dating back to 2018 for similar hazards. This longitudinal view of safety data underscores the severity of ignoring established protocols and illustrates how open records can trace persistent systemic risks rather than just single-point failures. Ultimately, this story emphasizes that open data is essential for protecting workers by exposing repeated negligence. When regulatory findings are publicly accessible, it empowers stakeholders to demand stricter adherence to safety laws. The ability to review past violations alongside new incidents provides a comprehensive picture of corporate behavior, ensuring that companies cannot easily obscure their safety records from public scrutiny and regulatory oversight.
