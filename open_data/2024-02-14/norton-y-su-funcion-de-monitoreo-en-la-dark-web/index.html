Norton y su función de monitoreo en la Dark Web

Norton’s expansion of its Dark Web monitoring service to India underscores the urgency of protecting digital identity in markets with high rates of data breaches. The widespread exposure of sensitive information, such as national identifiers and passports, has made proactive surveillance a critical necessity to prevent identity theft and the fraudulent use of personal data by cybercriminals. This tool provides an additional layer of security by actively scanning hidden networks for compromised credentials, enabling users to respond quickly to security breaches. Although free alternatives exist, the paid offering highlights the perceived value of continuous, specialized protection, differentiating itself from basic solutions by integrating this monitoring into comprehensive security packages tailored to various user profiles. In the context of open data, this article is relevant because it sheds light on the risks associated with the interoperability and centralization of public information. The availability of government data in open or poorly managed sources can facilitate its access on the Dark Web, demonstrating that transparency should not come at the expense of privacy. This underscores the need to develop governance frameworks that balance information openness with robust anonymization and individual protection mechanisms, ensuring that the democratization of data does not undermine personal security.
