La inflación de EEUU baja al 3,1% en enero y la subyacente se mantiene en el 3,9%

The Consumer Price Index in the United States showed a deceleration in its year-over-year rate during January, although overall figures, and especially core inflation, exceeded market expectations. This persistent inflation, driven primarily by rising housing costs and certain food items, undermines the narrative of an immediate and sustained slowdown, suggesting that the fight against inflation is still far from being successfully concluded. The economic implications are significant for monetary policy, as these data weaken expectations of an imminent interest rate cut by the Federal Reserve. Experts agree that the Fed is likely to maintain a cautious "wait-and-see" stance, postponing rate-cut decisions until May or June. Recent volatility indicates that markets must adjust their forecasts, as inflation continues to exhibit resistance that could delay the anticipated "soft landing" of the economy. This analysis is relevant to the field of open data because it illustrates how the availability and transparency of official statistical data, such as those published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics, are essential for informed, real-time decision-making. The ability of analysts and citizens to access disaggregated metrics enables a nuanced understanding of economic reality, fostering an evidence-based public debate and facilitating accountability of financial institutions. Moreover, it underscores the importance of maintaining open and up-to-date indicators to detect early trends and mitigate uncertainties in global markets.
