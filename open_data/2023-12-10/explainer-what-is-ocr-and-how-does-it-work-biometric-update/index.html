Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical Character Recognition (OCR) transforms physical documents into editable digital text, addressing critical challenges in data storage and management. As digitalization accelerates, businesses increasingly rely on OCR to process print media, enabling efficient storage, searching, and analysis of information that was previously difficult to handle electronically. The technology fundamentally enhances operational efficiency by eliminating manual intervention and allowing seamless integration with other business software. This automation streamlines workflows, facilitates advanced analytics, and boosts productivity, making it an essential tool for modern enterprises seeking to optimize their data handling processes without human error. This process is particularly relevant to open data initiatives involving digital identity and document verification. By converting sensitive identity documents into machine-readable formats, OCR supports biometric identity binding and secure digital interactions, thereby enabling open, standardized data exchange while maintaining rigorous security and accuracy standards in public and private sectors.
