Crisis regional: La economía de Cusco se contrae dramáticamente por más de nueve meses

The Cusco region is undergoing a severe economic contraction lasting more than nine months, primarily driven by declines in agriculture and construction. This downturn has significantly reduced production volumes, directly impacting family incomes and limiting employment opportunities. The situation underscores a critical need for strategic interventions to reverse this trend, emphasizing that sustainable recovery depends on revitalizing productive activities rather than relying solely on existing sectors. While most industries struggle, the mining sector demonstrates resilience, with significant production increases in copper, molybdenum, and silver. This positive performance is vital because mining generates formal employment, providing stable incomes and social protection for thousands of families. The sector’s contribution extends beyond raw output, supporting downstream industries such as electrical cables and specialized steel, thereby anchoring regional economic stability amidst broader regional hardships. This data is relevant to open data because transparent, accessible statistics from institutions like the National Institute of Statistics and Informatics (INEI) are essential for validating these economic disparities. Open access to such granular regional data enables economists and policymakers to identify sector-specific vulnerabilities and advocate for evidence-based investments. Ultimately, making this information public fosters accountability and guides the necessary infrastructure and policy changes required to support vulnerable communities and stimulate a diversified, robust economic recovery.
