Liberia: Iic & Cental Train Key Agencies On Foi, Anti-Corruption

Liberia: Iic & Cental Train Key Agencies On Foi, Anti-Corruption

Liberia’s Independent Information Commission and CENTAL have convened a critical workshop for senior government officials to strengthen adherence to the Freedom of Information Act. The core conclusion is that compliance must evolve from a mere legal obligation into an institutional culture driven by proactive disclosure and efficient record-keeping. Leaders are urged to move beyond reactive responses to information requests, recognizing that true transparency requires embedding openness into daily operations to foster efficiency and reduce secrecy. The initiative highlights the direct link between information access and anti-corruption efforts, emphasizing that withholding data fuels public speculation and undermines trust. By providing clear details on government projects and funding, institutions can mitigate misinformation and demonstrate integrity. The training stresses that ethical leadership shapes the organizational climate, making the active dissemination of information a vital tool for combating corruption and ensuring accountable governance. This article is highly relevant to open data because it frames data availability as a fundamental human right and a prerequisite for democratic accountability. It underscores the necessity of robust information management systems and proactive sharing practices, which are the operational foundations of effective open data ecosystems. By advocating for structural changes in how public bodies handle information, the piece illustrates the broader global push for governments to leverage open data to build citizen trust and enhance public service delivery.
