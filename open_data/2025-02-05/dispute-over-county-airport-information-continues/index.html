Dispute over county airport information continues

Dispute over county airport information continues

This dispute highlights critical tensions between individual transparency rights and government secrecy, specifically regarding whether legal counsel’s notes constitute public records. Former Airport Manager Andrea Nummilien contends that Delta County withheld investigation materials, alleging retaliation for a harassment complaint. The core conflict centers on Commissioner’s assertion that the county attorney personally owns his notes, a claim directly challenged by court precedents establishing that such files are public when performed in an official capacity. The relevance to open data lies in the evolving definition of public records within legal and administrative contexts. By invoking legal counsel files as private, the county risks creating opaque systems where significant investigative actions remain invisible to the public. Nummilien’s argument that staff interactions prove information was withheld underscores the difficulty of verifying transparency when institutions claim non-existence of records that later appear in alternative forms, such as public rebuttals. Ultimately, the county’s decision to seek a ten-day extension to locate these documents emphasizes the fragility of accountability mechanisms. This case serves as a cautionary tale for open data initiatives, illustrating how ambiguous interpretations of record ownership can stall public access. It demonstrates that without strict adherence to legal precedents regarding public body files, citizens may face systemic barriers in challenging official narratives or understanding the full scope of governmental investigations.
