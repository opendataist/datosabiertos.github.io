Resolving the Unemployment Disaster?

Namibia faces a catastrophic unemployment crisis, with official figures revealing a rate that exceeds half the working-age population, effectively rendering the majority jobless and impoverished. This statistic underscores a profound social emergency where extreme poverty coexists with national wealth, highlighting a systemic failure where nearly all potential employees have ceased seeking work due to the impossibility of finding employment. The data suggests that the current governance structure, having suppressed these figures prior to elections, lacks the seriousness required to address the magnitude of the disaster. The article argues that mass unemployment is an inherent feature of global capitalism, functioning to suppress wages and maintain control over the labor force in the Global South. Economists cited suggest that full employment is unattainable within the current system because it triggers uncontrollable inflation and contradicts the needs of global finance. Consequently, individual agency is eroded by economic coercion, and state-led interventions to boost employment are hindered by the dominance of international financial powers that punish nations attempting to transcend capitalist logic. To resolve this, the text advocates for transcending capitalism through socialism with Namibian characteristics, potentially facilitated by joining BRICS to counter global financial pressure. It emphasizes that sustainable solutions require delinking from exploitative global systems and prioritizing domestic agricultural development alongside wealth redistribution. This perspective is relevant to open data as it demonstrates how transparent, independent statistical reporting can expose structural economic failures that governments may obscure, thereby empowering civil society to demand policy shifts toward equitable employment and social justice.
