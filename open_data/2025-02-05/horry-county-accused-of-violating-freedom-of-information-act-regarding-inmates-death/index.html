Horry County accused of violating Freedom of Information Act regarding inmate’s death

Horry County accused of violating Freedom of Information Act regarding inmate’s death

A lawsuit against Horry County highlights the critical tension between public transparency and law enforcement exemptions under South Carolina’s Freedom of Information Act. The plaintiff alleges that the county failed to provide essential records regarding the death of an inmate, arguing that broad denials based on ongoing investigations violate statutory obligations. This case underscores how local governments may use investigative status as a blanket justification to withhold information, potentially obscuring accountability in sensitive incidents involving detainees. The dispute centers on the scope of document release, with the plaintiff asserting that the county provided scant data and improperly redacted materials. By refusing to produce logs, surveillance footage, and medical reports without specific justification, the county is accused of neglecting its duty to disclose non-exempt content. This approach challenges the principle that FOIA requests should yield relevant factual records unless a clear, specific harm to an investigation is demonstrated, rather than relying on generalized claims of interference. This legal battle is highly relevant to the open data movement because it tests the boundaries of what constitutes "public data" in correctional settings. It illustrates how institutional opacity can prevent families and the public from understanding the circumstances of deaths in custody. The outcome will determine whether open data standards require granular, itemized explanations for exemptions, ensuring that transparency is not sacrificed for vague administrative convenience.
