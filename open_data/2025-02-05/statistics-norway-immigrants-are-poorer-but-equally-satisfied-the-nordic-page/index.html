Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Norway’s comprehensive living conditions report reveals a paradox where immigrants face significantly poorer socioeconomic circumstances, including lower income, worse housing, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Despite these objective challenges, immigrants report satisfaction levels and institutional trust comparable to or exceeding the native population. This suggests that subjective well-being in Norway is not solely determined by material conditions but is heavily influenced by strong affiliation to the country and confidence in its political and judicial systems. This finding is crucial for open data because it highlights the importance of holistic metrics beyond standard economic indicators. By providing detailed, disaggregated data on social and psychological well-being, such reports enable researchers and policymakers to challenge assumptions that link poverty directly to dissatisfaction. It demonstrates how open access to nuanced survey data can uncover complex societal dynamics, fostering a deeper understanding of integration and quality of life. Ultimately, this dataset serves as a vital resource for promoting evidence-based social policy. It allows for the identification of specific demographic groups, such as those from Iraq or Iran, who may require targeted mental health support despite their high institutional trust. The transparency of this information encourages further academic inquiry and public discourse, ensuring that future interventions address both material disparities and the unique social contexts of immigrant communities.
