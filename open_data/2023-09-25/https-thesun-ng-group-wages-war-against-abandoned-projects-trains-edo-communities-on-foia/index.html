Social Action and EDOCSO recently trained Edo State stakeholders on utilizing the Freedom of Information Act to demand transparency in government contracting. The initiative empowers citizens to bypass opaque procurement processes by formally requesting details from agencies, thereby ensuring public funds are used efficiently and reducing opportunities for official corruption. The training emphasized strict adherence to procedural rules, such as submitting written requests with specific details and proper identification. Participants were advised that courts are a last resort, urging them to exhaust administrative channels first. This structured approach ensures that information requests are legally sound and less likely to be dismissed, making the act a practical tool for accountability rather than a theoretical right. This article is vital to the open data movement because it illustrates the legal mechanisms required to access government-held information. By teaching citizens how to successfully request data from state agencies, it fosters a culture of transparency. This practical application of open data principles allows communities to monitor public spending directly, transforming abstract data rights into tangible oversight capabilities for marginalized groups.
