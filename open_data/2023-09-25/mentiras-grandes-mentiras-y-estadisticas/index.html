Mentiras, grandes mentiras y estadísticas

The Spanish National Statistics Institute (INE) revised upward the GDP growth figures for 2020–2022 by 1.3 percentage points, correcting previous estimates while preserving its statistical integrity in the face of political pressure to report higher numbers. This adjustment aligns with broader European trends, as most EU countries similarly recalculated their pandemic-era economic data upward. Consequently, Spain’s relative economic standing remains unchanged, disproving government claims of exceptional national resilience and highlighting that the country still lags behind its peers in both real and nominal growth during the post-2019 period. This case underscores the tension between technical accuracy and political narrative management within public administration. While INE professionals resisted attempts to manipulate the results, the article suggests a broader pattern of using statistics to distort economic reality for political gain. The discrepancy between official optimistic portrayals and independent analyses reveals a disconnect between governmental messaging and actual socioeconomic conditions, prompting calls for greater transparency and adherence to rigorous statistical standards. For open data advocates, this situation illustrates the critical need for data integrity and independence from political interference. The potential manipulation of official statistics undermines public trust and hinders accurate policy evaluation. Ensuring that statistical agencies operate free from executive pressure is essential for maintaining reliable open data ecosystems, enabling citizens and experts to access truthful information necessary for holding governments accountable.
