El sueldo promedio de los argentinos es 87.310 pesos, es decir 116 dólares

The article highlights a critical disconnect in Argentina’s economy, where record-low unemployment fails to prevent soaring poverty rates. Despite the labor market reaching levels akin to full employment, the prevalence of low-quality, informal jobs means that holding a job no longer guarantees escape from poverty. This paradox suggests that current employment figures reflect a crisis of subsistence rather than economic vitality, as inflation continues to erode wages faster than they can be adjusted. This situation is particularly concerning because it reveals how high inflation and stagnant productivity have decoupled labor participation from income stability. While the availability of jobs acts as a partial buffer, preventing poverty from spiking even further, it does not resolve the underlying issue of real income decline. The data indicates that the majority of new jobs are insufficient to cover basic needs, trapping a significant portion of the population in financial hardship despite the absence of joblessness. Relevant to open data, this case illustrates the limitations of relying on single indicators to assess social welfare. It emphasizes the necessity of multidimensional datasets that correlate employment quality, wage value, and inflation rates to provide an accurate picture of economic health. Without transparent, integrated data, policymakers and the public may misinterpret low unemployment as a success, masking the severe reality of widespread poverty and the need for structural economic reforms.
