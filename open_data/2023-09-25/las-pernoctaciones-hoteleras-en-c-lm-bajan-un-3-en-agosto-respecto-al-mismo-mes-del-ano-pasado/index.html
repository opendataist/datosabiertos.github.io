Las pernoctaciones hoteleras en C-LM bajan un 3% en agosto respecto al mismo mes del año pasado

The report reveals a solid recovery in Spanish tourism during the first eight months of the year, surpassing even pre-pandemic levels, although August showed a slight slowdown in the number of travelers. Despite this minor reduction, revenue per room increased significantly, driven by tourists’ greater purchasing power, indicating that the hotel industry is prioritizing profitability over pure volume. This trend suggests a resilient and professionalized sector, capable of maintaining high revenues even with a slight decline in overall occupancy. The structure of demand confirms the duality between domestic and international tourism. Spanish residents remain crucial for destinations such as Andalusia and Catalonia, while foreign visitors, particularly from the United Kingdom and Germany, concentrate their spending in the Balearic and Canary Islands. This diverse geographic distribution not only spreads economic benefits across the peninsula but also reduces dependence on a single source market, strengthening the structural stability of Spain’s tourism model against external fluctuations. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the availability and utility of the INE’s tourism business cycle data. These data, being public and standardized, enable the analysis of macroeconomic trends, the assessment of the impact of public policies, and the generation of added value through integration with other databases. Free access to this information facilitates transparency, academic research, and strategic decision-making in the tourism sector, demonstrating how open data can transform the management of a key economic sector.
