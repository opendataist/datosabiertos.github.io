Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022 - Palatinate

Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022 - Palatinate

This article demonstrates how Freedom of Information requests can unlock valuable institutional data, transforming opaque university performance metrics into accessible public knowledge. By analyzing undergraduate attainment across various departments, the disclosure reveals significant disparities in academic outcomes, highlighting which subjects consistently produce top grades and which struggle. This transparency allows students, educators, and policymakers to scrutinize the quality and rigor of different courses, fostering a more informed academic community. The data indicates that Physics and English lead in overall attainment, while foundation years and certain language modules lag behind. Such granular insight into module-specific performance challenges the notion of uniform academic standards across disciplines. It suggests that factors like assessment methods, student demographics, and subject nature heavily influence results. Understanding these variations is crucial for evaluating the effectiveness of curricula and ensuring equitable educational experiences for all students regardless of their chosen field of study. Relevance to open data lies in the power of transparency to drive accountability and improvement. When universities disclose such detailed performance metrics, they encourage constructive dialogue about teaching standards and resource allocation. This case illustrates how open data initiatives can empower stakeholders to make evidence-based decisions. By making hidden institutional statistics visible, the university promotes a culture of openness, enabling external validation of its educational claims and supporting data-driven reforms in higher education.
