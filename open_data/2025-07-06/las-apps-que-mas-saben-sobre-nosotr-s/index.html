Las ‘apps’ que más saben sobre nosotr@s

Las ‘apps’ que más saben sobre nosotr@s

Major digital players, led by Meta and ride-hailing services, engage in aggressive collection of personal data, often exceeding what is necessary for their business operations. This practice not only facilitates the creation of detailed advertising profiles but also involves the massive sharing of information with third parties, revealing a significant gap between the stated privacy policies and the actual behavior of applications. The inherent vulnerability of these platforms exposes users to growing cybersecurity risks, such as fraud and identity theft, exacerbated by the sophistication of cyberattacks. Although most consumers do not read terms of use, global concern over digital privacy has driven behavioral changes, such as deleting apps and refusing to share data, signaling an urgent demand for greater control and security within the digital ecosystem. This issue is central to open data because it highlights the critical tension between informational transparency and the protection of individual privacy. Promoting open standards that require clarity about what data is collected and how it is used is essential to fostering public trust. Without robust regulatory and technical frameworks that ensure data minimization and security, any initiative aimed at opening up information risks eroding citizens' privacy and compromising ethical standards in data handling.
