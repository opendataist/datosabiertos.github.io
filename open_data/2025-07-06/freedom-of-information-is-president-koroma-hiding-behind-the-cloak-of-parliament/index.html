Freedom of Information: Is president Koroma hiding behind the cloak of parliament?

Freedom of Information: Is president Koroma hiding behind the cloak of parliament?

The article urges the government of Sierra Leone to immediately enact its pending Freedom of Information Bill, emphasizing that timely legislative action is crucial for establishing transparency and accountability. Advocacy groups argue that this law is vital for addressing historical patterns of corruption and human rights abuses that contributed to the nation’s past armed conflict. By allowing public access to previously sensitive information, such as government contracts, the legislation serves as a foundational tool for good governance and the rule of law. The delay in passing the bill is attributed to political deadlock between the executive and legislature, despite prior presidential endorsements and party manifestos prioritizing anti-corruption measures. The article highlights that this inaction undermines democratic principles and prevents citizens from accessing information necessary to understand their rights. Consequently, the lack of transparency hinders the realization of other human rights, as individuals cannot effectively identify violations or hold officials accountable without knowing how public institutions operate. This case is directly relevant to open data because it illustrates the legal framework required to make public information accessible to citizens. Open data initiatives rely on strong legislative support to ensure that government-held data is not just available but usable for public scrutiny and civic engagement. The Sierra Leonean example demonstrates how open data legislation acts as a mechanism for preventing abuse of power and fostering trust between the state and its populace, reinforcing the idea that access to information is a prerequisite for meaningful democracy.
