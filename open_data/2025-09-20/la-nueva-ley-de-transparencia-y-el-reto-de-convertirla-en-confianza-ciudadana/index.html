La nueva Ley de Transparencia y el reto de convertirla en confianza ciudadana

La nueva Ley de Transparencia y el reto de convertirla en confianza ciudadana

Baja California has enacted new legislation to modernize its transparency framework, aligning it with the principles of open government and inclusion in the digital age. This legal advancement represents a crucial step toward consolidating democracy by recognizing access to public information as a fundamental human right, rather than merely a state concession. The reform aims to address current demands for connectivity and data protection, establishing a new chapter in governmental accountability. However, the effectiveness of this legislation faces significant challenges, primarily the need to ensure the independence of oversight bodies to avoid conflicts of interest. It is also vital to precisely define exceptions to access to information, ensuring that broad concepts are not arbitrarily used to restrict transparency. To overcome these obstacles, swift avenues for appeal and constant public oversight are required to certify that decisions are objective and respect the principle of maximum disclosure. This article is relevant to open data because it links access to information with the genuine openness of government data. The true measure of success will be the ability of citizens to use this information as a tool to report abuses and improve public management. Effective transparency depends on data being accessible and understandable, transforming the right to know into a concrete mechanism for social participation and the strengthening of civic freedoms.
