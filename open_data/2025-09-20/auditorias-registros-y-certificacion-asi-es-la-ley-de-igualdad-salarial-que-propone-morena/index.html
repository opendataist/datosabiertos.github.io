Auditorías, registros y certificación, así es la Ley de Igualdad Salarial que propone Morena

Auditorías, registros y certificación, así es la Ley de Igualdad Salarial que propone Morena

The proposed General Law on Wage Equality aims to formally mandate transparency and accountability in compensation structures, directly addressing the persistent gender pay gap. By requiring employers to implement objective salary criteria and conduct regular audits, the legislation moves beyond mere anti-discrimination statements to enforce measurable equity. This shift implies a structural change in how organizations value labor, prioritizing skill and performance over gender, which is essential for dismantling systemic inequalities that have historically marginalized women in the workforce. This regulatory framework emphasizes that data transparency is not just a compliance metric but a tool for empowerment. Employers must provide accessible information about pay policies and allow workers to understand their rights regarding remuneration. The introduction of a certification for companies demonstrating wage equality creates a competitive incentive for adherence. Consequently, this fosters a market environment where equitable practices become a standard of operational excellence, encouraging institutions to proactively correct internal disparities rather than reacting to legal violations. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it institutionalizes the collection and disclosure of sensitive compensation statistics. By legally requiring regular audits and public transparency regarding pay structures, the law generates standardized, comparable datasets that can reveal broader socioeconomic trends. This mandated openness transforms wage data from proprietary secrets into public information, enabling researchers, policymakers, and civil society to monitor progress effectively. It establishes a precedent where granular economic data serves as a fundamental mechanism for social justice and continuous improvement in labor equity.
