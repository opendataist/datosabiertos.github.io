Configuración del delito en México cambió; fraude, extorsión y amenazas los que más repuntaron - La Jornada San Luis

Configuración del delito en México cambió; fraude, extorsión y amenazas los que más repuntaron - La Jornada San Luis

The article reveals a significant shift in Mexico’s criminal landscape, characterized by a transition from violent physical crimes to non-violent offenses such as fraud, extortion, and threats. This change in the configuration of crime highlights the growing complexity of security challenges, where traditional robbery rates are declining while financial and psychological intimidation crimes are surging. This evolution impacts how victimization is measured and understood, shifting the focus beyond physical safety to economic and procedural security. A critical finding is the persistent and high rate of “hidden crime,” in which the vast majority of offenses go undetected and uninvestigated. The low reporting rate, driven by the time-consuming nature of the process and distrust in authorities, creates a substantial data gap. This lack of transparency hinders accurate risk assessment and policy-making, as official statistics fail to capture the true scale and nature of the insecurity affecting citizens’ daily lives. This report is highly relevant to open data because it exposes the limitations of relying solely on reported incidents for public security analysis. It underscores the necessity of integrating victimization surveys, such as ENVIPE, with official records to correct biases and provide a holistic view of crime. For developers and analysts, this highlights the importance of designing tools that account for underreporting and diverse data sources to support more effective, evidence-based public policies.
