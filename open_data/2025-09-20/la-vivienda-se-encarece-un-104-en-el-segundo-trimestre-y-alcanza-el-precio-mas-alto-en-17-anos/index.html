La vivienda se encarece un 10,4% en el segundo trimestre y alcanza el precio más alto en 17 años

La vivienda se encarece un 10,4% en el segundo trimestre y alcanza el precio más alto en 17 años

The Spanish housing market has reached historic price levels, with free-market residential property values hitting their highest point since the 2008 financial crisis. This surge is driven by a critical shortage of housing supply struggling to meet growing demand, which places significant barriers to access for young and vulnerable populations. Concurrently, lower mortgage interest rates are stimulating transaction activity, creating a tension between market liquidity and affordability that threatens social stability. This price escalation reflects a profound structural imbalance where real estate assets are rapidly appreciating, nearly matching the peaks of the previous real estate boom. The disparity between new and existing homes indicates broad-based inflation across the sector, while protected housing prices, though rising more modestly, are also at record highs. The implication is a dual crisis: investors see substantial asset growth, while average buyers face unprecedented exclusion from the market, exacerbating socioeconomic inequality and housing insecurity. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity for transparent, standardized, and accessible real estate statistics. Reliable open data allows researchers, policymakers, and civil society to monitor market trends, identify regional disparities, and advocate for evidence-based housing policies. Without such visibility, the magnitude of the affordability crisis remains obscured, hindering effective interventions and public understanding of the systemic challenges affecting housing rights.
