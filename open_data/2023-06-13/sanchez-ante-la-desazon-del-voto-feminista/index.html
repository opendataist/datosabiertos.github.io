Sánchez, ante la desazón del voto feminista

The article highlights a growing concern within the PSOE regarding a potential erosion of female voter support, driven by dissatisfaction with the Ministry of Equality’s recent legislative agenda. Key issues include the controversial Trans Law and the management of gender identity policies, which critics argue have alienated feminist voters by prioritizing LGBTQ+ rights over traditional gender equality principles. This disconnect is seen as a significant risk to the party’s electoral cohesion. The core implication is that calls for "useful voting" to prevent right-wing ascendancy are no longer sufficient to maintain mobilization among this demographic. Instead, there is palpable fatigue and disillusionment among socialist feminists who feel their core concerns have been sidelined. The narrative suggests that the party’s failure to address these structural debates has created a gap between its leadership and its base, threatening to demobilize a crucial segment of the electorate. This case is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical gap in public, disaggregated electoral information. Without transparent data on voting patterns by gender, analysts cannot accurately quantify the scale of this internal dissent or predict its impact on election outcomes. The reliance on general trends rather than specific, accessible datasets hinders a comprehensive understanding of how policy decisions directly influence gendered voting behaviors.
