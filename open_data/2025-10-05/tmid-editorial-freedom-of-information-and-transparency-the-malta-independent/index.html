TMID Editorial - Freedom of information, and transparency - The Malta Independent

TMID Editorial - Freedom of information, and transparency - The Malta Independent

Malta’s Freedom of Information framework suffers from systemic inefficiencies that severely undermine public transparency. Delays are rampant, with authorities frequently exploiting extension loopholes to postpone responses until they eventually reject requests. This bureaucratic sluggishness effectively blocks citizens and journalists from accessing critical information, defeating the core purpose of the law which is to ensure accountability through timely disclosure of public data. The appeal process is similarly flawed, as informal complaints are often handled by the same individuals who issued the initial rejections. This lack of independent oversight renders the grievance mechanism ineffective, forcing applicants into lengthy legal battles with the Information and Data Protection Commissioner. Consequently, valid requests for publicly funded data, such as specific contract costs or ministers' wealth, are often denied under vague pretenses like non-disclosure agreements or incomplete record-keeping, rather than being openly shared. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the gap between legal frameworks and actual data availability. When public entities treat information as proprietary rather than public assets, it creates significant barriers for open data ecosystems. Ensuring that information is not only legally accessible but also practically available in usable formats is essential for fostering genuine transparency, enabling journalists to hold power accountable, and allowing citizens to engage meaningfully with government activities.
