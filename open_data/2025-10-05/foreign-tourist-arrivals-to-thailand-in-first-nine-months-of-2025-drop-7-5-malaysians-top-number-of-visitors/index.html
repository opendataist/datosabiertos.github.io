Foreign tourist arrivals to Thailand in first nine months of 2025 drop 7.5%; Malaysians top number of visitors

Thailand’s tourism sector experienced a decline in foreign visitor arrivals and overall revenue during the first nine months of 2025. Despite this downturn, average expenditure per tourist increased, suggesting that remaining visitors are contributing more significantly to the economy. Domestic tourism also showed growth, partially offsetting the drop in international travel. This shift highlights a transition toward higher-yield tourism rather than mass volume. The data reveals changing market dynamics, with notable increases from India, Russia, and Western nations, while traditional markets like China saw sharp declines. These fluctuations indicate evolving global travel preferences and the need for targeted marketing strategies to sustain economic benefits. This information is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, publicly accessible tourism statistics enable stakeholders to analyze economic trends and policy impacts. By making such detailed metrics available, governments and researchers can better understand sector resilience, support data-driven decision-making for future tourism initiatives, and promote accountability in national economic reporting.
