El Estado, metiendo mano al teléfono

El Estado, metiendo mano al teléfono

The article argues that Mexico’s mandatory cellular registration policy is not a security measure but a tool for social control and political discipline. By forcing universal identification through personal data, the state shifts from a presumption of innocence to generalized suspicion, effectively turning communication devices into instruments of surveillance. This approach prioritizes administrative obedience over actual crime prevention, targeting common citizens rather than the criminal organizations the policy claims to combat. The initiative represents a deliberate disregard for constitutional jurisprudence, as it reinstates a measure previously declared unconstitutional by the Supreme Court. The author highlights the immense risk of data breaches, noting that the government lacks the capacity to secure such sensitive information. Consequently, this creates a vulnerable database that normalizes fear and encourages self-censorship, undermining fundamental rights by associating identity with the freedom to speak and organize. Relevant to open data, this case illustrates the dangers of centralized data collection without robust privacy safeguards or judicial oversight. It serves as a critical warning that when states prioritize surveillance capabilities over data protection, they erode civil liberties and trust in digital infrastructure. The policy demonstrates how data initiatives can be weaponized for social control, highlighting the urgent need for transparency, accountability, and strong legal frameworks to protect individual rights against state overreach.
