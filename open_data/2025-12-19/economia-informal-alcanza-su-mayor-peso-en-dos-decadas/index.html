Economía informal alcanza su mayor peso en dos décadas

Economía informal alcanza su mayor peso en dos décadas

The article highlights a significant rise in Mexico’s informal economy, which now contributes over a quarter of the national GDP and employs more than half the workforce. This trend underscores a stark productivity gap, where formal sectors generate the majority of economic value with a smaller share of workers, indicating deep structural inefficiencies in labor distribution. Informal activities, particularly in construction and commerce, are expanding rapidly, driven by the growth of unregistered micro-businesses and jobs lacking legal protection. This surge suggests that economic growth is increasingly reliant on low-productivity, unprotected labor rather than stable, formal employment opportunities for the population. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it reveals systemic vulnerabilities and the true scale of unrecorded economic activity. By making such statistics accessible, policymakers and researchers can better analyze the informal sector’s impact, design more effective social protections, and understand the limitations of traditional economic metrics that often overlook this vast segment of the workforce.
