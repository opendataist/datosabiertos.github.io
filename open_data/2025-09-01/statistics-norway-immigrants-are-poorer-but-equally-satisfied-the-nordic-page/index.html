Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly worse living conditions than the native population, including lower incomes, poorer housing, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Despite these material and psychological disadvantages, this group reports satisfaction levels with life in Norway that are equal to the general population. This paradox highlights a complex relationship where objective hardship does not necessarily translate to subjective dissatisfaction or national alienation. Furthermore, immigrants demonstrate remarkably high confidence in public institutions, such as the judiciary, police, and political system, often exceeding the trust levels found among native Norwegians. This strong institutional affiliation suggests a resilient sense of belonging and civic integration, contrasting sharply with the challenges encountered in daily employment and social welfare. The data reveals that social cohesion is not solely dependent on material parity but is also fueled by trust in the state’s fairness and protection. This report is vital for open data because it provides a transparent, evidence-based foundation for policy-making. By making comprehensive indicators on well-being and integration accessible, it allows researchers and advocates to challenge simplistic narratives about immigrant struggles. It enables a nuanced dialogue on how societies can improve material conditions while maintaining high levels of civic trust and social satisfaction, proving that data transparency is key to fostering inclusive urban planning and social policy.
