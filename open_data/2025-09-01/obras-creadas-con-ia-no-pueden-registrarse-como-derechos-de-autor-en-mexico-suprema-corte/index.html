Obras creadas con IA no pueden registrarse como derechos de autor en México: Suprema Corte

Obras creadas con IA no pueden registrarse como derechos de autor en México: Suprema Corte

The Mexican Supreme Court ruled that works created solely by artificial intelligence cannot be registered under copyright law, affirming that authorship is exclusive to natural persons. This decision emphasizes that copyright stems from human intellect and creativity, requirements that autonomous AI systems inherently lack. By denying the appeal, the Court established a clear legal precedent in Mexico, rejecting arguments that artificial entities should possess rights under international treaties. The ruling reinforces that the law objectively limits protection to human creators, ensuring compatibility with existing national legislation and global agreements. This case is vital for open data advocates as it clarifies the legal status of AI-generated content. It confirms such outputs fall into the public domain without copyright restrictions, potentially facilitating their unrestricted use, sharing, and integration into open datasets and research initiatives without licensing barriers.
