The Municipality of Quilmes is launching an Open Government Forum at the National University of Quilmes, emphasizing civic participation and administrative transparency. This initiative aims to create spaces where residents can actively engage in public issues, propose local improvements, and access key governance information through various thematic commissions covering areas from environmental governance to social rights. By facilitating direct dialogue between citizens and officials, the forum strengthens democratic engagement and ensures that community voices influence municipal decision-making processes. A significant outcome of this event will be the public vote for the name of the new Quilmes Sur railway station, symbolizing the integration of citizen choice into urban development. The forum coincides with the presentation of a comprehensive hydro-social and environmental plan and highlights existing digital tools like the Open Data Portal and Infrastructure Map. These resources allow residents to monitor public works and understand municipal management, reinforcing the link between open governance and tangible urban planning. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how municipalities can operationalize transparency policies through accessible digital platforms. By standardizing over 150 administrative procedures and making them searchable, the government enables citizens to navigate bureaucracy more efficiently. Furthermore, the availability of reusable data formats encourages civic innovation and accountability, demonstrating that open data is not merely a technical requirement but a foundational tool for fostering trust and active citizenship in local governance.
