Freedom of information fumble a missed opportunity for Bulgaria’s new government

The new Bulgarian government risks undermining its promises of transparency and anti-corruption resilience by advancing legislative amendments that restrict the right to information. Proposed changes would limit public access to official documents by imposing vague "legitimate interest" criteria and excluding primary accounting records, effectively shielding state entities from scrutiny. This threatens journalistic oversight and citizen accountability, contradicting the coalition’s stated commitment to rule of law and openness. Such restrictions directly impact open_data initiatives by creating legal barriers to accessing essential public sector information. By narrowing who can request data and excluding critical financial records, the amendments hinder the availability of raw government data necessary for transparency analysis and civic engagement. This regulatory uncertainty discourages the development of data-driven solutions that rely on unrestricted access to public records. The relevance to open_data lies in the fundamental tension between legal frameworks and data accessibility. When laws restrict access to primary documents, the ecosystem for open government data suffers, reducing the potential for public oversight and innovation. Ensuring unrestricted access to public information is crucial for maintaining trust in governance and supporting the open_data movement’s goal of empowering citizens through transparent, available data.
