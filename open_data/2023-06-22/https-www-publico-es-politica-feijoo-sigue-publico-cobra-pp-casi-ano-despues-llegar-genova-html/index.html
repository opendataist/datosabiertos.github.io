The article highlights a significant transparency deficit within the People’s Party, as its president, Alberto Núñez Feijóo, has failed to disclose his specific remuneration from the party headquarters since taking office. This omission persists despite legal mandates requiring political parties to publish the annual salaries of their high-ranking officials. Feijóo’s defense relies on submitting his asset declaration to the Senate, which only reflects his previous income as President of Galicia, thereby obscuring his current earnings derived from his political leadership role. This raises serious questions about compliance with transparency laws and the public’s right to know how much public funds or party resources are allocated to party leadership. The core issue extends beyond mere secrecy to the broader implications for political integrity and the standardization of party funding. Unlike his predecessors, who openly declared their salaries as party presidents, Feijóo’s refusal to clarify these figures prevents an assessment of whether he has set his own pay or limits his compensation to mere expenses. This lack of clarity contradicts the spirit of the Law on Transparency, which aims to prevent conflicts of interest and ensure that high-level positions in political entities are funded transparently. Without this data, it is impossible to evaluate the proportionality or appropriateness of his compensation relative to other political leaders. This case is highly relevant to the open data community as it exemplifies how legal obligations for public disclosure are often ignored or circumvented by major institutions. The situation underscores the gap between existing transparency legislation and its practical enforcement in the political sphere. For open data advocates, this incident illustrates the urgent need for stricter monitoring mechanisms and digital platforms that enforce real-time, accessible publication of financial data. It serves as a critical reminder that transparency laws are ineffective without robust, automated systems that ensure compliance and make sensitive financial information readily available to citizens and watchdogs.
