Rumors in Monterrey

The article highlights how Monterrey’s authorities attribute the rise in citizens’ trust in the police and the perceived reduction in insecurity to their current management, using these indicators to bolster their political image. This performance is presented as a central strategy for consolidating alliances and preparing for future electoral campaigns, demonstrating the instrumentalization of security statistics for propagandistic purposes. The relevance to open data lies in the critical need to verify the methodology behind surveys such as those conducted by INEGI. By observing how governments selectively interpret results to justify their management, the urgency becomes evident for citizens and analysts to access raw data. This enables independent scrutiny, preventing the official narrative from concealing biases or necessary contexts required to genuinely assess public safety. Furthermore, the text reflects a polarized political environment where government messages intertwine with personal dynamics and local conflicts, such as opposition to urban projects. This complexity underscores the importance of radical transparency: when data are neither accessible nor clearly explained, it facilitates the manipulation of public perception. Open data serves as an essential counterweight to these fragmented narratives, ensuring that public decisions are based on verifiable evidence rather than partisan interpretations.
