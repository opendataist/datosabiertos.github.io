The article highlights the current mental health crisis in Spain, characterized by a significant rise in depressive disorders and chronic dependence on psychotropic medications. While these drugs are often necessary, they can cause side effects and lead to addiction. Personal testimonies illustrate how conventional pharmacological treatment frequently only masks symptoms without addressing the underlying causes, leaving patients in a state of passivity or “zombification.” This underscores the limitations of the healthcare system in providing non-invasive, personalized therapies on a large scale. The relevance of this text to open data lies in the need for transparency and access to epidemiological information. The figures cited, drawn from health surveys and public databases such as the BDCAP, are essential for assessing the scale of the problem and designing effective policies. However, the public availability of these data enables citizens and researchers to compare clinical realities with official statistics, fostering a deeper understanding of gaps in healthcare provision and inequalities in access to innovative treatments, such as transcranial magnetic stimulation. In conclusion, the evidence suggests that public healthcare must evolve toward models that rely less on long-term medication, integrating more humane and technologically advanced therapeutic alternatives. The visibility of open data not only justifies the urgent need to invest in research and new therapies but also empowers patients to demand more dignified and effective treatments, reducing stigma and improving quality of life for those living with chronic mental health disorders.
