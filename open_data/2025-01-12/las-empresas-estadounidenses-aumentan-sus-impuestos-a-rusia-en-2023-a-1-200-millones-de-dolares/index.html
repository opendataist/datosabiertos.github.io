Las empresas estadounidenses aumentan sus impuestos a Rusia en 2023 a 1.200 millones de dólares

In 2023, U.S. multinational corporations increased their tax payments to the Russian state, demonstrating that remaining in the market continues to generate significant revenue for Moscow despite international pressure. This reality contradicts the narrative of total economic isolation, revealing the resistance of some corporations to sever ties due to commercial interests, even when facing sanctions and boycotts from pro-Ukraine activists. The complexity of the situation is exacerbated by Russia’s strict divestment rules, which require companies wishing to exit to sell their assets at steep discounts and pay additional taxes. These structural barriers, combined with difficulties in repatriating capital, make withdrawal a costly and bureaucratic operation, thereby discouraging immediate exits and keeping hundreds of companies operating under conflict conditions. This case is relevant to open data because it underscores the need for transparency in transnational financial flows and corporate governance during geopolitical crises. The availability of verifiable information on tax contributions and divestment strategies enables civil society and legislators to monitor the ethical alignment of companies. Moreover, it fosters debate on how the lack of access to clear data can hinder the assessment of the real impact of economic sanctions on the global economy.
