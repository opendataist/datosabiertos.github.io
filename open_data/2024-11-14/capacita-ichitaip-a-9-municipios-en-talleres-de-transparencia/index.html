Capacita Ichitaip a 9 municipios en talleres de transparencia

The article highlights a critical debate regarding the potential elimination of local transparency bodies in Mexico. It argues that maintaining autonomous state organs is essential for guaranteeing citizens' right to information and data protection, regardless of federal decisions. The text warns that even if these local guarantees disappear, existing fines for non-compliance would remain enforceable, leaving citizens with fewer mechanisms for recourse. This scenario underscores the urgent need for continuous training of public officials. Ensuring that staff at all levels can respond appropriately to information requests is vital for maintaining transparent governance. The workshops aim to strengthen these competencies, emphasizing that adequate preparation is necessary to uphold democratic standards and legal obligations in the face of institutional changes. This topic is relevant to open data because it questions the stability of the institutional framework required to manage and release public information. If local guarantee bodies are abolished, the accountability and enforcement mechanisms that ensure data quality and accessibility could be weakened. Preserving these autonomous structures is crucial for sustaining a robust ecosystem where open data is not just available, but actively protected and properly utilized by the public.
