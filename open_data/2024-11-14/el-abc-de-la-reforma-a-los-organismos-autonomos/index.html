El ABC de la reforma a los organismos autónomos

El ABC de la reforma a los organismos autónomos

The proposed reform eliminates key autonomous constitutional bodies such as COFECE and IFT, transferring their powers to secretariats of the federal public administration. This move represents a regression in the democratic architecture, as courts have established that the autonomy of these bodies is essential to the system of checks and balances. By eliminating institutional independence, the separation of powers is undermined and authority is concentrated in the Executive branch, weakening the mechanisms that prevent abuse of public power and ensure the constitutional separation of functions. Furthermore, this change undermines the regulatory state model designed to manage complex markets through specialized technical criteria. Autonomous bodies ensure efficiency and fairness in sectors such as telecommunications and economic competition, protecting consumers from monopolistic practices without political interference. The absorption of these functions by government agencies introduces risks of regulatory capture and politicization, which could result in higher prices, lower service quality, and unfair barriers to free economic competition. This article is relevant to the open data movement because transparency and competition are interconnected pillars of an informed society. The elimination of INAI and the disconnection of telecommunications regulation threaten universal access to public information, which is crucial for accountability. Without independent bodies guaranteeing both data availability and the openness of digital markets, citizens lose fundamental tools to actively participate in democracy and verify the quality of the information services they use daily.
