En 2021, en Veracruz murieron 490 en accidentes viales en zonas urbanas y suburbanas

Veracruz recorded a notably low traffic fatality rate in 2021, ranking second to last nationally. However, officials emphasize that statistics should never normalize death, as the ultimate goal is zero fatalities. While the data highlights progress, it serves only as a baseline; the ideal remains preventing all accidents, rendering low mortality rates insufficient justification for complacency in public safety strategies. The majority of accidents concentrate in specific urban municipalities, revealing distinct patterns of collisions involving cars, motorcycles, and alcohol. Crucially, these figures exclude rural highways, meaning the true scope of danger is likely higher than reported. This partial visibility underscores the urgent need for comprehensive data collection that encompasses all travel environments to accurately assess risk and allocate resources effectively. For open data advocates, this article illustrates the critical importance of granularity and coverage in public statistics. Without complete datasets that include rural areas and detailed causal factors, policymakers cannot design effective interventions. Furthermore, the gap between international safety goals and national compliance highlights how transparent, holistic data is essential for accountability and for meeting global humanitarian targets by 2030.
