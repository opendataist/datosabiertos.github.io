The National Institute for Transparency has filed a complaint with the Supreme Court against a recent decree that classifies major infrastructure projects, such as the Mayan Train, as matters of national security. This action asserts that the decree violates an ongoing injunction granted in a previous constitutional controversy. The INAI argues that by reclassifying these public works, the executive branch is attempting to circumvent legal protections regarding information transparency, effectively restricting citizens' right to know. The core implication is that labeling infrastructure as a security threat creates a mechanism to withhold vital data from public scrutiny. This undermines the principle that government projects, funded by public money, must remain transparent to ensure accountability and proper oversight. The legal battle highlights the tension between state security claims and the fundamental democratic necessity of open access to information about public spending and planning. This case is critically relevant to open data because it establishes a precedent for how governments might weaponize "national security" declarations to block data publication. If upheld, such decrees could set a dangerous precedent, allowing authorities to arbitrarily exclude significant amounts of public interest data from open government initiatives. It emphasizes the urgent need for robust legal frameworks to protect open data policies against executive overreach that prioritizes secrecy over transparency.
