An AI glossary: the words and terms to know about the booming industry

This article provides a foundational glossary of key artificial intelligence concepts, addressing the complex jargon that often obscures understanding of the technology. It clarifies distinctions between narrow AI, which performs specific tasks, and generative AI, which creates new content like text or images. By defining terms such as Large Language Models, neural networks, and training data, the piece demystifies how these systems function and learn, offering essential literacy for navigating the rapidly evolving AI landscape. The text also highlights critical challenges and nuances, such as algorithmic bias resulting from flawed training data, and the phenomenon of "hallucinations" where models produce false information. It introduces advanced concepts like alignment, which seeks to ensure AI goals match human values, and multimodal capabilities that allow processing of diverse information types. Understanding these risks and features is vital for evaluating the reliability, safety, and ethical implications of deploying AI technologies in real-world scenarios. This summary is relevant to open data because the integrity and accessibility of training datasets are central to AI development and governance. High-quality, open, and unbiased data are prerequisites for reducing algorithmic bias and improving model accuracy, directly impacting the fairness of AI systems. Furthermore, transparent access to training methodologies and data sources fosters trust and accountability, enabling researchers and the public to scrutinize how AI models are built and identify potential sources of error or prejudice, thereby supporting responsible innovation in the open data ecosystem.
