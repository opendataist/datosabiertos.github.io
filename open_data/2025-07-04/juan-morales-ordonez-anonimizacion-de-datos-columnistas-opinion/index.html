Juan Morales Ordóñez: Anonimización de datos | Columnistas | Opinión

Juan Morales Ordóñez: Anonimización de datos | Columnistas | Opinión

The article emphasizes that personal data is integral to individual identity and privacy, necessitating robust state protection against commercial exploitation and criminal threats. It highlights that while laws like the 2021 Organic Law on Personal Data Protection aim to safeguard this information, the sheer volume of personal details available online creates significant vulnerabilities. The core tension lies between the societal demand for security and the aggressive monetization of data by various actors, including financial and political entities, which often operate without genuine consent. Anonymous data is legally distinguished from personal data, theoretically allowing its use without connecting it back to specific individuals. The central conclusion is that anonymization serves as a legal fiction to enable data utility while preserving privacy. However, this protection is precarious. The author argues that true anonymity is increasingly difficult to maintain due to advanced computer engineering and sophisticated data analysis techniques that can re-identify individuals by correlating disparate datasets, thereby undermining the legal assumption that anonymized data is safe for unrestricted use. This discussion is critical for open data initiatives because it challenges the foundational premise that anonymized datasets are entirely free from privacy risks. For open data to be both ethically sound and legally compliant, stakeholders must recognize that technical anonymization alone is insufficient. The narrative warns against treating anonymized data as completely private, urging a re-evaluation of how open data protocols balance transparency with the realistic threats of data re-identification and surveillance in an increasingly technologized society.
