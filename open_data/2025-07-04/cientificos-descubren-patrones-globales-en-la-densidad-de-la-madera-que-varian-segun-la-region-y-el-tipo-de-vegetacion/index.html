Científicos descubren patrones globales en la densidad de la madera que varían según la región y el tipo de vegetación

Científicos descubren patrones globales en la densidad de la madera que varían según la región y el tipo de vegetación

A global research project led by experts from forty countries, with the crucial participation of Argentina’s National Institute of Agricultural Technology (INTA), has revealed that wood density varies significantly depending on the region and vegetation type. This finding, published in a prestigious scientific journal, establishes that environmental factors such as temperature and humidity are key determinants in the evolution of this property, thereby enhancing our understanding of how tree species adapt to their respective ecosystems. The relevance of these data extends beyond ecology, directly impacting the forestry industry and climate management. Knowing wood density is essential for predicting its strength and durability, optimizing its industrial use, and is also fundamental for more accurately calculating biomass and the amount of carbon stored in forests. This global quantification improves carbon storage models, providing more precise tools for climate change research. This study is particularly relevant to the field of open data, as it demonstrates the power of international collaboration in generating scientific knowledge. By integrating data from more than one million forest plots across diverse regions, including specific contributions from Argentina, the work illustrates how access to and interoperability of large volumes of ecological data can address critical information gaps. It thus promotes transparency and the sharing of environmental data for the global benefit of science and public policy.
