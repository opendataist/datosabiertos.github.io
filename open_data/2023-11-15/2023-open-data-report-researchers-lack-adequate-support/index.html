2023 Open Data Report: Researchers Lack Adequate Support

The State of Open Data report reveals that despite growing global mandates, the majority of researchers still lack adequate support to share their data openly. This persistent gap highlights a critical infrastructure failure in the scientific community, where informal internal networks, rather than institutional or funder-backed resources, remain the primary source of assistance. This disconnect undermines the transition toward sustainable open science, leaving researchers to navigate complex data sharing processes without necessary professional guidance. A significant barrier to open data adoption is the perceived lack of professional recognition, with a majority of researchers feeling they receive insufficient credit for their contributions. This recurring issue creates a disincentive for transparency, eroding trust and collaboration essential for reusable research. Furthermore, the survey indicates that awareness of emerging tools like artificial intelligence has not yet translated into practical application, suggesting a need for better training and integration of these technologies into daily research workflows to facilitate smoother data management. This report is highly relevant to the open_data movement because it exposes the human and structural challenges behind technical compliance. It argues that achieving true open science requires more than just policy mandates; it demands a culturally supportive ecosystem that recognizes contributions, provides accessible support tailored to diverse regional and disciplinary needs, and effectively integrates new technologies. Understanding these nuances is essential for building equitable and effective open data frameworks.
