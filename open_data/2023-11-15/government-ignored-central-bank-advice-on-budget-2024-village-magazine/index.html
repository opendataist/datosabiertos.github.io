Government ignored Central Bank advice on Budget 2024 - Village Magazine

The government implemented a mortgage-interest relief scheme in Budget 2024 despite explicit warnings from the Central Bank against such a broad approach. The central bank argued that the policy was inherently regressive, disproportionately benefiting older, higher-income homeowners rather than those most vulnerable to rising living costs. By providing universal tax relief, the strategy risked exacerbating inflation and overheating the economy instead of offering timely, targeted support to households in genuine financial distress. Critically, the intervention raises significant concerns about market distortion and fiscal responsibility. The central bank highlighted that similar policies historically inflate house prices without increasing homeownership rates and may inadvertently incentivize lenders to maintain higher interest rates to preserve profitability. This suggests that public funds were being used to subsidize lender margins rather than effectively alleviating the burden on borrowers, a move that undermines the goal of sustainable economic stability. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency mechanisms, specifically Freedom of Information requests, can expose discrepancies between official policy decisions and expert regulatory advice. It highlights the importance of accessible government records in holding institutions accountable, revealing how political motivations can override technical guidance when internal communications are made public. Such transparency is essential for understanding the true impact of fiscal policies and ensuring that public resources are allocated based on evidence rather than electioneering.
