CDC Claims on Vaccination and Natural Immunity Made Without Seeing Underlying Data: FOIA Document

CDC Claims on Vaccination and Natural Immunity Made Without Seeing Underlying Data: FOIA Document

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) acknowledged in a recent Freedom of Information Act response that it issued critical public health recommendations regarding COVID-19 vaccines for previously infected individuals without ever examining the underlying data supporting those claims. The CDC relied on a study authored by individuals with significant pharmaceutical conflicts of interest, yet subject matter experts never received the raw data prior to the contract’s termination. Consequently, the agency made sweeping assertions about reinfection risks without independent verification, leaving the foundational evidence inaccessible to both the public and regulators. This incident highlights a critical failure in scientific transparency and data governance, which are central concerns in open data initiatives. When public health institutions release findings that drive national policy, the integrity of those decisions depends on the availability and scrutiny of source data. The inability to access or audit the specific dataset undermines trust in institutional science and demonstrates how opaque data practices can lead to recommendations that contradict official vaccine authorizations and established medical principles regarding natural immunity. The relevance to open data lies in the urgent need for mandates requiring public access to all research data that informs government policy. Without transparent, reusable, and verifiable data, stakeholders cannot validate conclusions or detect potential biases, such as those stemming from corporate conflicts of interest. Ensuring that raw data remains accessible is essential for maintaining accountability, allowing independent analysis, and preserving the credibility of public health institutions in an era where evidence-based decision-making is paramount.
