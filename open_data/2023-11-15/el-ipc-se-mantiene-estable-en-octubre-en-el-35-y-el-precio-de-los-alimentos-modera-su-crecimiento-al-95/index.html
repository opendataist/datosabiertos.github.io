El IPC se mantiene estable en octubre en el 3,5 % y el precio de los alimentos modera su crecimiento al 9,5 %

The article highlights a divergent inflationary trend in which general price stabilization masks a persistent surge in food costs. While energy prices have declined, essential groceries remain significantly more expensive, forcing households to alter their consumption habits and cut back on items such as fish and fresh produce. This disparity underscores the fragility of family budgets, particularly for low-income groups, who must rely on savings or informal credit to bridge the gap between income and expenses, revealing a critical vulnerability in current economic resilience. The persistence of high food inflation is particularly relevant to open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how granular, sector-specific datasets are essential for identifying uneven economic impacts. General aggregate indices often obscure these critical disparities, making high-resolution, open statistical releases vital for policymakers and researchers. Without access to detailed breakdowns of price changes across different product categories, stakeholders cannot accurately assess the true cost-of-living crisis or design targeted social protections for the most affected populations. Furthermore, the significant deficit in financial literacy identified in the data calls for greater transparency and educational use of public information. When nearly half of the population misunderstands basic concepts such as inflation, the value of accessible, clear, and open economic data becomes crucial for empowering citizens. This scenario emphasizes that open data is not merely a technical requirement but a social tool necessary to improve financial decision-making and address the structural inequalities exacerbated by current macroeconomic trends.
