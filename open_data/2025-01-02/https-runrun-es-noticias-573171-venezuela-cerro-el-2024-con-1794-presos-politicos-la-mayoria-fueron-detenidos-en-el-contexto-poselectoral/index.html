Open Data
ES EN

This article highlights the critical role of non-governmental organizations in documenting human rights violations when state data is incomplete or biased. The Foro Penal’s detailed breakdown of political prisoners demonstrates how independent entities provide verified, granular data that official narratives often obscure. This contrast underscores the necessity of civil society monitoring to ensure accountability and transparency in post-electoral contexts. The discrepancy between official releases and NGO counts reveals systemic opacity within judicial processes. By exposing the lack of comprehensive official lists, the report illustrates how fragmented information hinders accurate assessment of justice mechanisms. Such data gaps emphasize the urgent need for standardized, accessible public records to validate claims of due process and fair treatment. The relevance to open data lies in exposing how fragmented, non-standardized, or withheld government information creates significant barriers to understanding civic realities. Open data initiatives can bridge these gaps by promoting transparent, interoperable datasets from various sources. This enables researchers and citizens to cross-reference official claims with independent reports, fostering a more accurate and verifiable public record of political detention and judicial outcomes.
