Boeing and FAA put the US Freedom of Information Act in Danger

This article highlights a critical struggle for transparency in aviation safety, focusing on the conflict between Flyers Rights, Boeing, and the Federal Aviation Administration regarding the Boeing 737 MAX. The narrative underscores how essential public access to information is for ensuring consumer protection and accountability in high-stakes industries where government oversight intersects with corporate interests. The core issue involves allegations of a criminal conspiracy surrounding the aircraft’s certification and a systematic refusal by regulators and manufacturers to disclose known defects. By hiding critical safety data under the guise of secrecy, authorities prioritized maintaining the fleet’s operational status over informing the public of genuine risks, leading to catastrophic consequences that were preventable had the information been shared openly. Relevance to open_data lies in the failure of Freedom of Information requests to compel disclosure, demonstrating how opaque systems can endanger lives. This case serves as a stark reminder that mandatory transparency is not merely a bureaucratic formality but a fundamental requirement for trust. It illustrates the urgent need for open data initiatives to challenge institutional secrecy and ensure that safety-related information is accessible to all stakeholders, thereby preventing future tragedies.
