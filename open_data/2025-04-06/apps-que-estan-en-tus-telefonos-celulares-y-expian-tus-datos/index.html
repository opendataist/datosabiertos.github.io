Apps que están en tus teléfonos celulares y expían tus datos

Apps que están en tus teléfonos celulares y expían tus datos

Mobile applications collect far more extensive personal data than users might imagine, ranging from real-time geolocation to technical device details and social contacts. This massive data collection enables companies to personalize the user experience and optimize services, but it also facilitates the creation of detailed profiles for targeted advertising, often without users’ explicit or fully informed consent. The inherent risk of this practice is the violation of privacy and exposure to threats such as data breaches, identity theft, or commercial manipulation. By sharing sensitive information, individuals face the possibility that their data may be sold or used by third parties for inappropriate purposes, creating a power asymmetry in which transparency about the ultimate use of the information remains hidden. This issue is crucial for the open data movement because it underscores the need for ethical governance and transparency in the management of digital information. While mass data collection can drive innovation, it also demands robust regulatory frameworks that protect individual autonomy and prevent the commercial exploitation of personal data. Citizen awareness of these risks is essential to advocate for fairer systems that balance technological utility with the fundamental right to privacy and control over one’s own information.
