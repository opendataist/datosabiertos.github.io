Ejecutivo reglamenta ley garrote contra las ONG

Ejecutivo reglamenta ley garrote contra las ONG

The Paraguayan government has officially enacted regulations for the controversial Law 7363, imposing strict transparency controls and accountability requirements on non-profit organizations. This move intensifies oversight, raising concerns among international bodies and civil society that the measure stifles dissent and undermines democratic freedoms rather than ensuring genuine openness. The legislation’s focus on monitoring entities dealing with state corruption and transparency highlights a tension between regulatory compliance and the fundamental right to free association. Critics argue this excessive scrutiny acts as a deterrent against civil society watchdogs, potentially hindering their ability to operate independently and report on public administration issues without fear of persecution. This development is crucial to open data discussions as it illustrates how legal frameworks can manipulate transparency mechanisms to suppress information flow. It serves as a warning that data disclosure mandates, when weaponized by the state, can erode the trust and operational space necessary for independent organizations to contribute to a healthy, informed public sphere.
