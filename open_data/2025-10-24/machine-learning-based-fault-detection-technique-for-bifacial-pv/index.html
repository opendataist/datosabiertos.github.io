Machine learning-based fault detection technique for bifacial PV

Machine learning-based fault detection technique for bifacial PV

Researchers have developed a robust machine learning framework for detecting faults in bifacial photovoltaic systems, addressing a critical gap in renewable energy maintenance. By integrating a one-dimensional convolutional neural network with a random forest classifier, the study establishes a powerful tool for identifying specific issues such as dust accumulation, shading, aging, and physical cracks. This hybrid approach significantly outperforms standalone models, achieving near-perfect accuracy in distinguishing general operational states and high precision in pinpointing individual fault types. The study highlights the unique resilience of bifacial technology, demonstrating that these systems can maintain superior power output even under severe shading or fault conditions. The developed model leverages current-voltage curve analysis to detect anomalies with minimal false positives, ensuring reliable diagnostics. This capability is essential for maximizing the efficiency and longevity of solar installations, as early and accurate fault detection prevents significant energy losses and reduces costly manual inspections. This research is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it advances the automation of solar monitoring through sophisticated, replicable computational methods. By providing a validated framework for fault classification, it encourages the sharing of standardized datasets and algorithms within the scientific community. Such transparency fosters collaborative innovation, enabling other researchers to build upon these models to create more accessible, data-driven solutions for global renewable energy infrastructure management.
