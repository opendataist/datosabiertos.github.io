El mapa de ingresos de los barrios asturianos: Somió y San Martín del Rey Aurelio, dos extremos de un mismo territorio

El mapa de ingresos de los barrios asturianos: Somió y San Martín del Rey Aurelio, dos extremos de un mismo territorio

The publication of income data by the National Statistics Institute (INE) reveals a profound economic divide in Asturias, where wealth is disproportionately concentrated in the central metropolitan axis and specific residential enclaves, while western rural areas and former mining basins remain at a disadvantage. This division not only separates regions but also manifests with extreme harshness within cities themselves, creating starkly contrasting realities separated by mere meters or streets, underscoring that urban geography is a key determinant of either exclusion or prosperity. The analysis shows that inequality is structural and territorial, particularly affecting working-class neighborhoods and areas with post-industrial economic fragility. When comparing figures between high-income-density zones and those with minimal income levels, it becomes evident that geographic proximity does not guarantee social equity. On the contrary, the economic distance between adjacent neighborhoods illustrates how access to resources and opportunities is highly stratified, creating pockets of poverty that coexist with immediate luxury—a phenomenon that demands a nuanced understanding of poverty that goes beyond regional averages. This study is crucial for open data because it demonstrates how detailed statistical transparency enables the identification of micro-inequalities invisible in national or regional aggregates. By democratizing access to high-resolution geolocated data, it becomes possible to investigate the root causes of exclusion and design more precise public policies. Without this capacity for data-driven public scrutiny, social gaps between neighbors would remain hidden behind misleading averages, hindering effective intervention and accountability in the distribution of wealth.
