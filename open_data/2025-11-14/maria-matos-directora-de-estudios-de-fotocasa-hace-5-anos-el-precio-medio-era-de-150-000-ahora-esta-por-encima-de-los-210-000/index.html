María Matos, Directora de Estudios de Fotocasa: "Hace 5 años el precio medio era de 150.000, ahora está por encima de los 210.000"

The housing market in Spain has reached a critical state of inaccessibility, driven by a severe structural imbalance where demand significantly outpaces supply. This dynamic has transformed housing from a basic right into an unattainable luxury for many citizens, highlighting a systemic failure in market regulation and urban planning that prioritizes speculative gains over social welfare. Relevant to open data, this crisis underscores the necessity of transparent, real-time datasets to understand complex socio-economic trends. Without accessible information on pricing, availability, and demographic shifts, policymakers and researchers cannot accurately diagnose the root causes of such disparities or develop effective interventions. Open data serves as a crucial tool for holding institutions accountable and ensuring that market dynamics are visible to the public. Ultimately, the situation demands immediate policy responses grounded in robust evidence. By leveraging open data, stakeholders can foster informed debates and create equitable solutions that address the disconnect between wages and property costs. Ensuring data openness is not just a technical requirement but a fundamental step toward restoring fairness and stability in the residential sector, allowing society to effectively monitor and correct these alarming trends.
