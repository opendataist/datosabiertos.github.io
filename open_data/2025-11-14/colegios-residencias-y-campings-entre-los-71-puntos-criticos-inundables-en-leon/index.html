Colegios, residencias y campings entre los 71 puntos críticos inundables en León

Colegios, residencias y campings entre los 71 puntos críticos inundables en León

The report reveals that, although Castilla y León has a lower relative percentage of flood threat compared to other regions, there are critical infrastructures—such as schools, residential facilities, and essential services—located in high-risk zones. This finding is significant for open data, as it highlights the gap between the availability of detailed geospatial information and its accessibility or comprehension by local authorities and citizens. Transparency in risk data is essential for residents and managers to make informed decisions and demand appropriate preventive measures. The current situation shows a widespread lack of awareness among mayors regarding the level of danger their municipalities face, underscoring the need for clear and accessible communication of hydrogeological data. The use of risk labels and the implementation of early warning systems based on open data could help bridge this information gap. This would ensure that vulnerable populations and operators of essential services are aware of the threats and can act proactively in the face of potential catastrophic floods. Finally, the study advocates for nature-based solutions and the relocation of sensitive infrastructure to safer areas as urgent measures. The societal relevance of open data lies in its capacity to visualize and analyze these risks, thereby prioritizing investment in resilience. Democratizing this information not only upholds principles of transparency but also serves as an indispensable tool for sustainable urban planning and the protection of lives and public services in the face of climate change.
