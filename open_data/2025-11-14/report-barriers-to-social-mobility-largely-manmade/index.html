Report: Barriers to social mobility largely manmade

Report: Barriers to social mobility largely manmade

A new report by the Archbridge Institute argues that barriers to social mobility are primarily artificial, stemming from restrictive government policies rather than inherent personal limitations. By identifying these "man-made" obstacles, the study suggests that policymakers can significantly improve economic mobility through targeted reforms. These changes include reducing occupational licensing, lowering business taxes, deregulating housing markets, and expanding educational choices. This perspective shifts the narrative from one of inequality to a proactive framework for removing impediments that hinder individual flourishing. The analysis distinguishes between external policy constraints and personal challenges, highlighting that artificial barriers in areas like housing and education have profound impacts on long-term outcomes. Interestingly, the findings indicate that high social mobility is not strictly tied to traditional political ideologies, with top-ranking states including both conservative and liberal jurisdictions. This bipartisan nature underscores that effective policy environments, characterized by freedom from excessive regulation and support for entrepreneurship, are crucial for creating equitable opportunities across different political landscapes. This research is highly relevant to the open data community as it demonstrates how measurable, transparent datasets can drive tangible policy improvements. By quantifying the impact of specific regulations on mobility, the report provides a model for using public data to identify and address systemic inefficiencies. It encourages the open data movement to move beyond mere observation, advocating for data-driven solutions that empower communities to advocate for legislative changes that enhance economic opportunity and transparency.
