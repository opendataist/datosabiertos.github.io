Bruselas acusa a TikTok y Meta de violar las reglas de transparencia de la Ley de Servicios Digitales

Bruselas acusa a TikTok y Meta de violar las reglas de transparencia de la Ley de Servicios Digitales

The European Commission has launched a sanctioning procedure against TikTok and Meta for failing to comply with the Digital Services Act (DSA) regarding transparency and content moderation. It is argued that these platforms impose artificial barriers that hinder researchers’ access to public data, thereby compromising their ability to audit risks such as illegal or harmful content. Furthermore, it is pointed out that the “notice and action” and appeal mechanisms are confusing or misleading, which limits the effectiveness of citizen intervention and the protection of fundamental rights. This case is crucial for the open data movement because it establishes a legal precedent on the mandatory nature of access to information generated by algorithms and users. The regulation not only seeks to ensure that data are accessible, but also that they are available in formats and under conditions that enable genuine and effective public scrutiny. The requirement for transparency acts as a counterweight to the power of major tech companies, ensuring that independent research can verify the social impact of these platforms without being obstructed by deliberately complex designs. The significance lies in the fact that the DSA transforms data openness from a technical issue into a binding legal obligation, with serious economic consequences for violations. By compelling companies to provide verifiable information, it promotes a safer and more responsible digital democracy. This decision drives the creation of open standards for monitoring systemic risks, demonstrating that the real availability of data is essential for accountability and public health, and paving the way for future regulations on access to non-public data.
