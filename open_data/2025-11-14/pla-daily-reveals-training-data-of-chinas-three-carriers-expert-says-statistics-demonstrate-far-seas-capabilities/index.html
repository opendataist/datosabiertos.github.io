PLA Daily reveals training data of China’s three carriers; expert says statistics demonstrate far seas capabilities

China’s navy has officially entered a new era of blue-water power with the commissioning of the Fujian, bringing its total carrier count to three. This milestone signifies a strategic shift from regional defense to global power projection, demonstrating that Beijing now possesses the hardware and personnel to conduct sustained maritime operations far from its shores. The integration of electromagnetic catapult technology on the newest vessel highlights a significant leap in logistical efficiency and operational tempo compared to previous models. Operational data reveals that these ships are no longer merely experimental platforms but are actively engaged in rigorous, combat-ready training. High frequencies of sea trials and air sorties indicate a maturation in crew proficiency and system reliability. The successful launch of advanced aircraft types from the Fujian, including stealth fighters and early warning systems, proves that the complex electromechanical systems are functional and that pilots are prepared for high-intensity conflict scenarios. This development is highly relevant to open_data initiatives as it underscores the growing importance of transparent, verifiable evidence in analyzing military capabilities. Reliable, publicly available data allows independent researchers and defense analysts to track geopolitical shifts, assess security risks, and understand the technological trajectory of major naval powers. Without such open metrics, understanding the true scale and readiness of modern fleets remains opaque, making open-source intelligence critical for informed global discourse.
