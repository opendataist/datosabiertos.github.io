Opinion | Need a unified data repository

The article argues that data is the fundamental driver of the modern digital economy, capable of generating substantial social and economic benefits when accessed and shared effectively. This perspective highlights how open data practices directly fuel innovation and productivity across sectors, transforming how governments and businesses operate. The core implication is that unrestricted data flow is not merely a technical convenience but a critical engine for growth, making the availability of information a prerequisite for competitive development. For nations like Nepal, realizing these benefits requires overcoming weak digital foundations by prioritizing data centricity and interoperability. The text emphasizes that robust infrastructure and open Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) are essential for making government data actionable and transparent. By adopting open standards, public institutions can enhance accountability and combat corruption, ensuring that digital governance structures are resilient enough to support a middle-income transition. Open_data is relevant here as it serves as the practical mechanism for achieving these governance and economic goals. Collaborative efforts with private entities demonstrate how open APIs can bridge the gap between raw government data and user-friendly applications, empowering citizens and researchers alike. This synergy proves that open data ecosystems are vital for fostering inclusive growth, enabling evidence-based decision-making, and ultimately building a transparent, digitally literate society.
