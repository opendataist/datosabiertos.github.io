Pescadores reclaman depuración de padrón y vigilancia contra pesca furtiva en Tamaulipas

Fishers in Tamaulipas are calling for improved budget planning and stricter enforcement to combat illegal fishing, which severely reduces production in the Gulf of Mexico. The sector highlights systemic inequities, noting that bureaucratic distortions have prevented legitimate small-scale fishers from obtaining essential equipment, while opportunistic actors hoard resources. As a result, there is an urgent need for accurate censuses to correct these historical exclusion errors and ensure that federal support reaches those who actually practice the trade. The situation is worsened by environmental changes and persistent poaching, making daily operations increasingly difficult and threatening the economic viability of fishing cooperatives. Addressing these challenges requires transparency in resource distribution and effective monitoring to protect sustainable yields. This narrative underscores the critical role of open data in public administration. Transparent, accurate datasets are essential for identifying fraud, allocating funds fairly, and monitoring ecological impacts. Without reliable information flows, governments cannot effectively enforce regulations or support vulnerable communities, highlighting that data openness is foundational to justice and sustainability in resource management.
