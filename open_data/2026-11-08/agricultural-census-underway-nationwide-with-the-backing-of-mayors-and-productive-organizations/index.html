Agricultural Census underway nationwide with the backing of mayors and productive organizations

Agricultural Census underway nationwide with the backing of mayors and productive organizations

The 2026 Agricultural Census in Bolivia is in its final phase, with an execution process that prioritizes the absolute confidentiality of producers’ data. The National Institute of Statistics (INE) emphasizes that information is strictly anonymized under statistical secrecy, ensuring that individual data are not linked to tax obligations or property disputes. This protective approach is essential to build public trust, guaranteeing that voluntary participation carries no negative legal consequences. The relevance of this process for open data lies in the commitment to publish progressive and disaggregated results. While general indicators will initially be disseminated, the INE plans to release detailed data at the community level after several months of processing. This structured transparency enables citizens and researchers to access granular information that reveals the true productive structure, fostering independent analysis and accountability. The primary objective of the collected information is to guide public policies and improve food security. By identifying new productivity potential and characteristics of family farming, municipal and departmental governments will be able to design more precise strategies. Consequently, the openness of these data not only meets ethical standards but also transforms census statistics into key tools for sustainable socioeconomic development and evidence-based decision-making.
