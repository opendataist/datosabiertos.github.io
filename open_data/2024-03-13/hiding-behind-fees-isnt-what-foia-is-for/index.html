Hiding behind fees isn’t what FOIA is for

Government transparency is essential for accountability, yet excessive Freedom of Information Act fees often obscure public records. In Detroit, journalists investigating the controversial awarding of mural contracts faced prohibitive costs. These barriers prevent citizens from accessing information about how public funds are utilized, effectively shielding government actions from scrutiny. The high expense of retrieving emails highlights a systemic failure where administrative burdens outweigh the duty to inform the public. This issue extends beyond journalism, affecting businesses, individuals, and legal firms who seek government data. When agencies charge high rates for searching and separating records, they discourage the use of open data mechanisms. This widespread practice in Michigan demonstrates a prevailing attitude among officials that transparency is a burden rather than a core service. Consequently, the public is kept in the dark, undermining trust in democratic institutions and hindering informed civic participation. Open data initiatives must address these access barriers to remain effective. If citizens cannot afford to request information, the promise of government openness is hollow. This article illustrates the urgent need to reform fee structures and improve agency responsiveness. By treating transparency as an essential service, governments can ensure that data remains a tool for empowerment rather than a shield for opacity, fostering a more accountable society.
