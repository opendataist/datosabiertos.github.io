La inflación de EEUU repunta al 3,2% en febrero y la subyacente cae al 3,8%

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) in the United States experienced a rebound in February, surpassing expectations in both its headline and core variants. The energy and housing sectors led the monthly increase, generating more persistent inflation than analysts had anticipated. This outcome suggests that the disinflation process has progressed very slowly, complicating the achievement of the Federal Reserve’s two percent target. The reassessment of these data directly impacts expectations regarding monetary policy. Experts agree that it is likely the Federal Reserve will maintain its “wait and see” strategy, delaying rate cuts until June or later, although they rule out an adverse scenario in which rates would need to be raised again. The persistence of inflation forces policymakers to monitor the evolution of future data before making definitive decisions on monetary easing. This article is relevant to the open data sector because it highlights how official macroeconomic indicators, such as the CPI, are fundamental to economic transparency and evidence-based decision-making. The accurate and timely availability of these datasets enables analysts, investors, and citizens to understand real economic dynamics. Moreover, it promotes accountability among central authorities, as the continuous comparison between forecasts and published results helps assess the effectiveness of implemented public policies.
