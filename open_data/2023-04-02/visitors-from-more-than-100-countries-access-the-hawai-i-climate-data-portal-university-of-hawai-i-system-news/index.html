Visitors from more than 100 countries access the Hawaiʻi Climate Data Portal | University of Hawaiʻi System News

The Hawaiʻi Climate Data Portal successfully demonstrated its value during its inaugural year by attracting significant global engagement. This high level of usage highlights a critical need for accessible, reliable climate information in an era of increasingly extreme weather conditions. The platform’s ability to draw users from nearly two hundred countries underscores the urgent demand for centralized data resources to support informed decision-making across diverse communities. Stakeholders and researchers collaborated to refine the portal’s functionality, ensuring it addresses practical challenges such as drought, fire, and flood risks. By integrating feedback from local organizations and government agencies, the team is developing targeted tools that help residents and managers adapt to environmental changes. This collaborative approach ensures that the data remains relevant and actionable for those on the front lines of climate resilience. This article is vital to the open data community as it exemplifies how interdisciplinary partnerships can transform raw scientific data into usable public infrastructure. It illustrates the importance of open access in building societal resilience and fostering trust among citizens and institutions. By prioritizing user-centric design and community involvement, the portal serves as a model for how open data initiatives can effectively address complex, real-world environmental issues.
