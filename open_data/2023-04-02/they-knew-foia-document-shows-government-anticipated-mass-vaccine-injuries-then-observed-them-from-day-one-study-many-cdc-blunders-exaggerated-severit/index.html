They Knew: FOIA Document Shows Government Anticipated Mass Vaccine Injuries, Then Observed Them From Day One; Study: Many CDC Blunders Exaggerated Severity of COVID-19, and other C-Virus related stories

The article contends that U.S. government agencies anticipated widespread adverse reactions to vaccines from the outset, using Freedom of Information Act documents to suggest they prepared for record-breaking report volumes rather than investigating safety concerns. This narrative argues that officials prioritized maintaining the vaccination campaign despite early awareness of potential issues, fostering a perception that public health authorities concealed risks while dismissing legitimate safety reports as fraudulent or anomalous. Furthermore, the text highlights a study alleging that the CDC frequently made significant numerical errors during the pandemic, predominantly exaggerating the severity of the disease, particularly regarding children. These inaccuracies are presented as evidence of a broader pattern of misinformation and a failure to maintain data integrity, which the author claims undermines the agency’s credibility and suggests a deliberate attempt to mislead the public about the true impact of the virus and interventions. This content is relevant to open data because it relies on the transparency provided by FOIA requests to scrutinize government decision-making processes and data management. It underscores the critical importance of accessible, verifiable records in holding institutions accountable. By exposing discrepancies between internal preparations and public narratives, it reinforces the argument that open data mechanisms are essential for validating official claims and ensuring public trust in scientific and health policies.
