***INAI, INOPERANTE

***INAI, INOPERANTE

The article highlights a critical institutional failure in Mexico, where the National Institute for Transparency (INAI) has been rendered inoperative because the Senate has failed to appoint the required number of counselors. This legislative inaction effectively paralyzes the body responsible for safeguarding citizens' right to information, demonstrating how political maneuvering can directly undermine transparency mechanisms and the rule of law. Simultaneously, the piece underscores the vital role of independent data channels in human rights advocacy, specifically citing the recovery of a Twitter account used to track missing persons. The disruption of this verified data source illustrates how digital infrastructure is essential for accountability, while the ongoing manipulation of electoral council selections reveals attempts to politicize institutions that should remain neutral and impartial. This content is highly relevant to open data because it exposes the fragility of public information systems when political interests override institutional mandates. It demonstrates that open data is not merely a technical issue but a fundamental democratic necessity; when data access is obstructed by legislative delays or digital security breaches, the public’s ability to monitor government actions and seek justice is severely compromised.
