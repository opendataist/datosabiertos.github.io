Ontario university instructor spills the beans on how he wins 80% of his Tim Hortons Roll Up to Win tries | CBC News

Researcher Michael Wallace used public data to determine that participating in Tim Hortons’ “Roll Up to Win” campaign during early morning hours significantly improves winning odds. His mathematical analysis reveals that the contest’s structure allows for strategic timing, demonstrating how hidden patterns in consumer promotions can be uncovered through data tracking. This approach is highly relevant to open data because it validates the power of accessible, public datasets. By leveraging information freely available on the company’s website, Wallace proved that citizens can independently analyze corporate transparency initiatives to gain tangible advantages. This example encourages public engagement with open datasets, showing that such data is not just academic but practical and immediately usable. Wallace uses this real-world case to teach statistics, illustrating that the world is filled with data worth exploring. The study highlights how open data can transform dry academic subjects into exciting, applicable skills. It underscores the importance of data literacy, proving that understanding how to interpret open information can lead to successful, informed decision-making in everyday scenarios.
