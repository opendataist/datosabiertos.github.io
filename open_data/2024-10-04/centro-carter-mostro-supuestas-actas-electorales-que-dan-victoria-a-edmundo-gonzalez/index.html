Centro Carter mostró supuestas actas electorales que dan victoria a Edmundo González

Centro Carter mostró supuestas actas electorales que dan victoria a Edmundo González

The Carter Center presented original Venezuelan voting records to the Organization of American States (OAS), asserting that opposition candidate Edmundo González won by a significant margin, in contrast to the official claim of Nicolás Maduro’s victory. The opposition argues that these documents, which feature verifiable codes, constitute physical proof of electoral fraud, while the Electoral Council’s lack of transparency in releasing disaggregated data fails to meet international standards. The OAS Secretary-General condemned the process as neither free nor fair, highlighting violations of signed agreements and the issuance of a fraudulent result. This diplomatic intervention underscores the deep political crisis and the international community’s skepticism regarding the legitimacy of the current administration’s narrative, amplifying calls for accountability and democratic restoration in Venezuela. This case is vital for open data, as it highlights the necessity of accessible, verifiable electoral records to ensure transparency and trust. The discrepancy between official statistics and documented evidence illustrates how opaque data practices can obscure election results, reinforcing the argument that open, standardized data release is essential for credible democratic processes and for preventing disputes over electoral integrity.
