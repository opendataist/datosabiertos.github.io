The article announces the launch of the registration phase for the “Mujeres Bienestar” program, a federal initiative that provides financial support to Mexican women aged 60 to 64 who reside in municipalities with a high indigenous population. By fulfilling previous commitments, the government has established a specific schedule and procedural guidelines to ensure that eligible beneficiaries can access the bimonthly stipend intended to improve their economic stability and social welfare. The registration process is systematically organized over several weeks, with entry times scheduled according to the alphabetical order of applicants’ surnames to manage flow and enhance administrative efficiency. Participants are instructed to consult official government portals for precise dates and required documentation, such as identification and proof of residence, while using designated digital tools to locate the nearest registration centers within their jurisdictions. This initiative is relevant to open data because it underscores the importance of transparent, publicly accessible government information regarding social programs. The reliance on official web platforms for calendars, requirements, and geographic locator tools demonstrates a commitment to digital transparency, ensuring that citizens can independently verify eligibility criteria and access services without intermediary barriers. This approach fosters trust in public administration and supports data-driven governance.
