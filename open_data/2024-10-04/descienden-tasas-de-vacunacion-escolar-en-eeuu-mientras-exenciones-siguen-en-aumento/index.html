Descienden tasas de vacunación escolar en EEUU mientras exenciones siguen en aumento

The decline in childhood vaccination rates and the record number of non-medical exemptions reveal a critical vulnerability in public health. Although the figures appear stable on the surface, falling below the threshold for herd immunity allows preventable diseases, such as measles, to resurge. This phenomenon highlights how small aggregated variations can result in thousands of unprotected children, significantly increasing the risk of community outbreaks in school settings. The relevance of these data lies in the importance of transparency and access to public information. Official health statistics are essential for understanding the geographic distribution of vaccine hesitancy, which often clusters in specific communities. Without open, real-time access to these indicators, authorities and society lack the capacity to identify high-risk areas and design precise, effective interventions. Finally, this analysis underscores that open data is not merely an administrative exercise but an essential tool for global health security. Clear visibility of exemptions enables the assessment of the impact of state policies and combats misinformation through verifiable evidence. By making these data public, accountability is promoted and families are empowered to make informed decisions, thereby strengthening trust in public health systems.
