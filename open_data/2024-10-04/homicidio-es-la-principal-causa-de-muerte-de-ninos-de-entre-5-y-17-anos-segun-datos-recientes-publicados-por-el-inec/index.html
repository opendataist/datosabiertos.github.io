Homicidio es la principal causa de muerte de niños de entre 5 y 17 años, según datos recientes publicados por el INEC

The article highlights a dramatic surge in intentional homicides, which have evolved from a minor cause of death into the second leading cause of mortality, surpassing even major cardiovascular diseases. This shift underscores a severe deterioration in public safety, indicating that violent crime is no longer an anomaly but a central factor driving mortality statistics. The data reveals that aggressive violent acts have fundamentally altered the health profile of the population, displacing traditional causes of death in the national rankings. Demographically, the crisis disproportionately impacts younger demographics, particularly males between the ages of five and forty-nine. While mortality rates for older adults have improved, likely due to reduced post-pandemic effects, the youth demographic faces heightened risks from criminal violence. This specific concentration of fatalities among productive age groups suggests deep-rooted social and security issues, contrasting sharply with the relative stability or improvement seen in non-violent causes of death for other segments of the population. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how transparent statistical access allows for precise identification of emerging societal crises. By making granular demographic and cause-of-death data publicly available, authorities and researchers can analyze trends, such as the specific rise in short-gun violence or regional disparities, to inform policy. Open data transforms raw mortality figures into actionable insights, enabling a deeper understanding of how violence reshapes public health landscapes and requiring continuous monitoring to address systemic failures.
