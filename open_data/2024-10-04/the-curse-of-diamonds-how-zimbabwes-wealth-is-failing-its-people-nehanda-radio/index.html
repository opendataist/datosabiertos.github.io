The curse of diamonds: How Zimbabwe’s wealth is failing its people – Nehanda Radio

The curse of diamonds: How Zimbabwe’s wealth is failing its people – Nehanda Radio

Zimbabwe’s diamond sector, despite ranking among the world’s largest producers and generating hundreds of millions in revenue, has failed to improve the lives of local communities in Marange. Instead of economic prosperity, residents face forced displacement, poverty, and a severe human rights crisis. The disparity between national export figures and local suffering highlights a systemic failure where resource wealth benefits foreign entities and the state elite rather than the people who bear the environmental and social costs of extraction. The article underscores critical issues of transparency and accountability in natural resource governance, which are central to open data initiatives. It reveals that a significant portion of diamond revenue remains unaccounted for, and companies pay minimal taxes while violating labor and environmental laws. This opacity allows impunity for foreign investors and prevents citizens from tracking how public resources are managed. Without accessible, standardized, and verifiable data on revenues, payments, and environmental impact, civil society cannot effectively advocate for justice or hold institutions responsible. Relevance to open data lies in the urgent need for transparent data systems to monitor extractive industries. Open data can empower communities to verify claims about production, revenue distribution, and environmental degradation. By making financial flows, contract terms, and regulatory compliance publicly available, stakeholders can demand accountability. Ultimately, the case of Marange demonstrates that data transparency is not merely a technical requirement but a fundamental tool for protecting human rights and ensuring equitable development in resource-rich nations.
