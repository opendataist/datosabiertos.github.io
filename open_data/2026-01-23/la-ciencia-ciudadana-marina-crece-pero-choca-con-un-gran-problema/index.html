La ciencia ciudadana marina crece, pero choca con un gran problema

An international study reveals that marine citizen science is far more diverse than expected, with over a thousand global initiatives. However, its impact is severely constrained by a significant lack of data openness. Contrary to the belief that the marine environment hinders participation, existing documentation shows a considerable volume of involvement. Yet, most of these projects do not allow access to the information they generate, which prevents their effective use for scientific research and environmental management in the face of challenges such as climate change. The relevance to open data lies in the application of FAIR principles, which require that information be Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable. The analysis demonstrates that only a small minority of projects meet these basic standards, highlighting the scarcity of standardized vocabularies and clear licenses that ensure semantic interoperability. This technological and legal gap fragments knowledge, making it difficult to integrate citizen-generated data into advanced tools or public policies, thereby drastically reducing the added value of community participation. In conclusion, to maximize the potential of citizen science for ocean sustainability, it is urgent to transition toward robust open data practices. The implementation of persistent identifiers, standardized licenses, and uniform metadata will help overcome current fragmentation. This shift will not only improve the quality of marine research but also strengthen the capacity to respond to environmental crises, turning citizen initiatives into a key and truly usable component for evidence-based decision-making.
