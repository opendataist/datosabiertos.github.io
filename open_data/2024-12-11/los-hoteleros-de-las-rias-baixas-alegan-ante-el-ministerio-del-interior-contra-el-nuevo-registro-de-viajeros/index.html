Los hoteleros de las Rías Baixas alegan ante el Ministerio del Interior contra el nuevo registro de viajeros

Los hoteleros de las Rías Baixas alegan ante el Ministerio del Interior contra el nuevo registro de viajeros

Tourism associations argue that the new traveler registration system creates operational chaos, urging the government to implement efficient telematic data collection. They propose shifting the responsibility for data verification to customers and significantly reducing the data retention period to six months. This shift prioritizes data minimization and enhances privacy protection by limiting the number of personal details collected, which are currently viewed as intrusive. The sector emphasizes that minimizing data points prevents unjustified privacy violations and simplifies management. By requesting clearer guidelines on specific requirements, businesses aim to avoid misinterpretations and ensure legal compliance. These changes would streamline operations while respecting individual rights, balancing security needs with practical hospitality management. This article is vital for open data discussions because it highlights the tension between state surveillance infrastructure and data privacy standards. It illustrates how rigid data collection mandates can conflict with principles of minimalism and user control. The debate underscores the importance of designing transparent, efficient, and privacy-preserving systems in public administration, affecting how citizen data is handled and shared.
