El "sencillo truco" de la Guardia Civil para que no te suplanten la identidad a través de las etiquetas de los paquetes

El "sencillo truco" de la Guardia Civil para que no te suplanten la identidad a través de las etiquetas de los paquetes

The Spanish Civil Guard has issued a warning about the growing risk of identity theft stemming from the indiscriminate use of shipping labels, which contain sensitive personal data such as names, addresses, and phone numbers. This problem is exacerbated during the holiday season due to the massive surge in online shopping, offering cybercriminals an opportunity to steal information for fraudulent purposes, such as opening bank accounts or applying for credit under the victim’s identity. To mitigate this threat, two practical methods are recommended: physically destroying the labels before recycling the boxes, or using hydroalcoholic gel to erase the written information. Additionally, users are urged to avoid posting images of their shipments on social media, reinforcing the need for individuals to adopt digital privacy habits in their daily lives to protect their identity against potential financial and administrative fraud. This warning is particularly relevant to the open data community, as it underscores the critical importance of protecting personal data in the digital age—a fundamental pillar for trust in the open data ecosystem. The responsible disclosure of public information must be balanced with strict individual privacy measures; this case illustrates how the accidental exposure of structured data in real life can have serious consequences, reminding data professionals that security and ethics are inseparable from the handling of information.
