Por qué las cuentas olvidadas de Gmail, Instagram y más redes sociales pueden ser un peligro de seguridad

Por qué las cuentas olvidadas de Gmail, Instagram y más redes sociales pueden ser un peligro de seguridad

The article highlights that abandoned online accounts, often neglected due to password fatigue, constitute a significant cybersecurity threat for both individuals and organizations. These inactive profiles typically retain sensitive personal and financial data while lacking essential security measures like two-factor authentication. Consequently, they serve as easy entry points for cybercriminals aiming to steal identities, commit fraud, or launch broader attacks against businesses through credential stuffing and malware. This issue is particularly relevant to open data initiatives because the breach of such accounts often exposes interconnected datasets, including email lists, contact directories, and behavioral metadata. When inactive accounts are compromised, the resulting data leaks not only harm private citizens but also pollute the information ecosystem, reducing the reliability and integrity of publicly available or shared information sources. Understanding these vulnerabilities is crucial for ensuring that open data remains trustworthy and secure. To mitigate these risks, users and enterprises must proactively audit their digital footprints by removing unused services and strengthening security protocols for active accounts. Implementing robust password management, enabling multi-factor authentication, and verifying data deletion policies are essential steps. Ultimately, treating account hygiene as a continuous security practice protects personal privacy and contributes to the overall resilience of the digital infrastructure upon which open data systems rely.
