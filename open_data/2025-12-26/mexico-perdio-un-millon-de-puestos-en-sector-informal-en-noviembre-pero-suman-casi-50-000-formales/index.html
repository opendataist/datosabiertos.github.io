México perdió un millón de puestos en sector informal en noviembre, pero suman casi 50.000 formales

México perdió un millón de puestos en sector informal en noviembre, pero suman casi 50.000 formales

Mexico’s November labor data reveals a complex shift: massive job losses occurred exclusively in the informal sector, while the formal economy absorbed only a small number of new positions. This disparity caused the overall unemployment rate to appear deceptively low, masking a significant decline in labor force participation. Consequently, the share of workers in informal employment decreased, highlighting a structural transition rather than a uniform market recovery. The statistics expose a severe gender bias, with women bearing nearly all the burden of job losses. This disproportionate impact drastically reduced female labor force participation, indicating that standard unemployment metrics fail to capture the depth of the crisis. The rise in discouraged workers further suggests underlying economic distress, as many individuals stopped seeking employment despite being available to work. This information is vital for open data initiatives because it demonstrates how traditional indicators can obscure critical social inequalities and economic realities. Transparent access to granular datasets allows analysts to uncover hidden disparities in participation and gender-specific impacts. Without detailed, open statistics, policymakers might overlook the true cost of economic adjustments, leading to ineffective interventions that fail to protect vulnerable groups or address the root causes of labor market fragmentation.
