LankaWeb – Actions of the Police Media Division threaten media freedom: Hiru responds to the police

This incident highlights the critical tension between state transparency and institutional secrecy in the context of open data. The police withheld crucial evidence that they themselves recorded, denying the public access to vital information until journalists uncovered it. This deliberate omission undermines democratic principles and the public’s right to know what their funded agencies are doing. By refusing to acknowledge the link between a specific assault and a police raid, the authorities attempted to control the narrative rather than share facts. This behavior demonstrates how withholding data erodes trust. When public entities fail to disclose evidence freely, they hinder accountability and prevent citizens from making informed judgments about law enforcement actions. Relevance to open data lies in the necessity of proactive disclosure. The case proves that without independent media pressure, key information remains hidden. True open data requires institutions to release evidence automatically, ensuring transparency is maintained without relying on external interventions to expose what the public is entitled to know.
