New law means digital news sites will be able to post public notices as traditional media retracts

Virginia lawmakers have passed significant legislation enhancing press access and government transparency, marking a notable shift toward protecting journalistic principles. Crucially, the state now allows qualified online news outlets to publish public notices, a move that levels the playing field between traditional newspapers and digital media. This change ensures that critical civic information reaches the public where they actually consume news, rather than being confined to declining print readership, thereby strengthening democratic engagement and providing new revenue streams for emerging local news organizations. Furthermore, reforms to the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) address the prohibitive costs that often hinder public access to records. By eliminating fees for the first hour of processing initial requests and capping subsequent charges, the legislation removes financial barriers for citizens and journalists. Additionally, new requirements mandate public meetings before interim appointments and prevent the redaction of employee names from credit card statements. These measures promote uniformity and accountability, ensuring that transparency is not just a theoretical right but a practically accessible one for all users. This legislative activity is vital to the open data movement because it demonstrates that legal frameworks must evolve alongside media consumption habits. True openness requires that data and records are not only available but also affordable and discoverable by the general public. By lowering costs and expanding digital channels for information distribution, Virginia sets a precedent that open government laws must actively facilitate public scrutiny, ensuring that transparency benefits both the media and the citizens they serve.
