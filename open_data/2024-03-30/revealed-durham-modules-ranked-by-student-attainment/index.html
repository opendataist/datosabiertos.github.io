Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

This analysis highlights the significant disparities in academic performance across different university disciplines, revealing that attainment levels vary drastically by subject. By utilizing Freedom of Information data, the study demonstrates that certain departments, such as Physics and English, consistently achieve higher average marks and first-class degree proportions compared to others. This variability suggests that institutional structures and departmental cultures play a substantial role in student outcomes, rather than performance being solely dependent on individual effort. The findings further expose notable gaps between different stages of undergraduate education and specific module types. Foundation year students show considerably lower attainment rates compared to final-year participants, indicating potential challenges in early academic transition or support systems. Additionally, specific modules, particularly in languages and arts, exhibit extreme polarization in success rates, with some seeing nearly all students achieve top grades while others record none. This inconsistency points to potential issues in assessment standards, module difficulty calibration, or teaching effectiveness across the curriculum. This information is critically relevant to open_data as it exemplifies how transparent access to institutional records can uncover systemic inequalities and drive accountability. When universities release granular performance metrics, stakeholders can scrutinize equity in education and identify areas requiring intervention. Such transparency empowers prospective students and policymakers to make informed decisions, ensuring that higher education institutions are held responsible for maintaining fair and effective learning environments across all departments.
