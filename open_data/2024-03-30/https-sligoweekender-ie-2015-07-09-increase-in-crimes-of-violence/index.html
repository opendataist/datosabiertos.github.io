Crime statistics in the Sligo-Leitrim area reveal a troubling rise in violent offenses, including sexual offenses, assaults, and robberies, alongside increases in property damage and arson. While theft and fraud rates declined, the surge in violent incidents highlights significant shifts in local security dynamics that demand immediate attention from community leaders and policymakers. These figures are presented with caution, as their publication resumed only after a comprehensive review of the Garda crime recording system. A previous suspension was due to identified irregularities in how incidents were logged, including misclassifications and unrecorded crimes. This context underscores the critical importance of data integrity, as flawed administrative records can obscure the true nature of public safety challenges. This article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the challenges of releasing transparent, high-quality government statistics. It emphasizes that open data initiatives must be underpinned by rigorous data governance and validation processes. Without accurate and reliably recorded information, public trust in official data is eroded, hindering effective policy-making and community engagement.
