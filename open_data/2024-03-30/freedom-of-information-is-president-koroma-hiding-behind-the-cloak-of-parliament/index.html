Freedom of Information: Is president Koroma hiding behind the cloak of parliament?

Major human rights organizations urgently urge the Sierra Leonean government to pass its pending Freedom of Information Bill. This legislation is critical for dismantling the culture of secrecy that fueled past corruption and conflict. By granting the public access to previously withheld government data, including sensitive contracts, the law would significantly enhance transparency and accountability. The delay in enactment undermines the country’s efforts to rebuild trust and adhere to democratic principles after its brutal civil war. The failure to enact the law highlights a disconnect between political rhetoric and action, despite high-level endorsements for transparency. Without legislative reform, citizens remain unable to effectively monitor public institutions or hold officials accountable for misuse of power. The organizations emphasize that true governance requires a united political will to open government operations to public scrutiny. This shift is essential not only for good governance but also for preventing the recurrence of human rights violations driven by hidden administrative practices. This article is directly relevant to open data because it advocates for the institutional mechanisms necessary to make public information accessible. Open data initiatives rely on legal frameworks that mandate the release of government records, which this bill provides. By enabling citizens to access essential data, the law supports the broader open data movement’s goal of empowering communities through information. Ultimately, it demonstrates how legal transparency is a prerequisite for meaningful public engagement with government data.
