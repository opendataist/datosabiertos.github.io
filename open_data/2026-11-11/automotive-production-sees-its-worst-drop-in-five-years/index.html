Automotive production sees its worst drop in five years

Automotive production sees its worst drop in five years

Mexico’s automotive production and exports suffered significant declines, marking the worst situation since 2021. This downturn is driven by internal strategic adjustments, such as technical stoppages to refresh models, and reduced operations at key plants, including those of Mercedes-Benz. Additionally, global competition and evolving market demands are forcing leading brands to adjust their manufacturing capacity. The external context plays a decisive role, particularly through U.S. tariffs that increase the cost of sales to the north. Historically strong brands have seen sharp declines in their international shipments due to this tax burden and competitive pressure from the Chinese automotive industry. This scenario highlights the vulnerability of the supply chain to trade barriers and geopolitical shifts. This report is relevant to the open data community because it underscores the importance of monitoring official statistical data in real time. The transparency enabled by national indices facilitates the analysis of industrial trends and the evaluation of trade policies. Understanding these variations helps identify economic risks and make informed decisions regarding investments and sectoral regulations.
