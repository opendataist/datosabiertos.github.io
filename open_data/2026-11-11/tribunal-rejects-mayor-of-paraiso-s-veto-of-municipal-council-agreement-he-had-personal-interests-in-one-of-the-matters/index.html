Tribunal rejects mayor of Paraíso's veto of Municipal Council agreement: he had personal interests in one of the matters

The Administrative Litigation Court rejected the mayor of Paraíso’s veto of a municipal agreement, establishing a crucial precedent for local transparency. The ruling affirmed that the administration cannot obstruct information requests from the City Council, especially when those requests concern matters of personal interest to the executive branch. This decision reinforces the principle that mayors must not interfere in legislative processes that directly affect their own administrative or financial situation, thereby ensuring a balance of powers. The court determined that the challenged agreement constituted merely an administrative procedure rather than a substantive decision, and therefore lacked grounds for a veto. Moreover, the court identified a conflict of interest in the inclusion of inquiries regarding retroactive payments to elected officials, a category that includes the mayor himself. Given the direct interest involved, the law expressly prohibits the head of the executive branch from intervening, invalidating any attempt to withhold relevant information about compensation and salary adjustments. This ruling is highly significant for the open data movement, as it validates the right of legislative bodies to demand access to administrative public information. By dismissing the veto, it ensures that financial and payroll records can be audited and disclosed, fostering more rigorous accountability. The judgment underscores that opacity in matters of public interest, particularly those linked to the interests of those in power, is unacceptable and must be remedied through full transparency of data.
