Inflation: Food prices showed a slowdown in the first week of November

Inflation: Food prices showed a slowdown in the first week of November

Early private measurements for November indicate a moderation in food and beverage prices, interrupting several consecutive weeks of increases and generating early signals of stabilization. However, this trend is influenced by transitory factors such as commercial promotions and seasonal variations, which prevent drawing definitive conclusions about a structural slowdown in inflation. The data suggest that, while there is some relief in specific categories, the overall inflation projected for October maintains a slight acceleration compared to the previous month. The persistence of inflation will continue to be conditioned by multiple variables, including the meat price cycle, increases in regulated public utilities, and the evolution of international oil prices. Although Brent crude oil prices remain at levels that could alleviate pressures, the seasonality of food prices and tariff adjustments represent risks that could hinder any downward trend. Therefore, private forecasts warn about the difficulty of achieving a sustained slowdown in the short term, highlighting the fragmented nature of price behavior. This analysis is relevant to the field of open data because it illustrates the importance of complementing official statistics with private sources and multiple indicators. The need to interpret data considering seasonal and structural contexts, beyond isolated figures, underscores the value of transparency and access to diversified information. Open access to these surveys allows citizens and specialists to be better informed, enabling a more precise and nuanced assessment of economic reality.
