The poor condition of Spanish roads causes more than a thousand annual accidents with fatalities or injuries

The poor condition of Spanish roads causes more than a thousand annual accidents with fatalities or injuries

The analysis reveals that the deterioration of road surfaces is a key factor in traffic accidents, accounting for a significant share of crashes and causing thousands of victims annually. This reality shows that the lack of maintenance is not merely a comfort issue, but a serious threat to public health and safety on the road network, where a considerable portion of the asphalt requires urgent interventions to prevent infrastructural collapse. The opacity of official data exacerbates the situation, as the General Directorate of Traffic does not specifically break down accidents caused by road conditions, often attributing them to causes such as inappropriate speed. This lack of transparency prevents the proper assignment of responsibility, since the Administration conceals the real impact of pavement deterioration, which is growing at an accelerated rate and accumulating a massive conservation backlog that current investments fail to cover. This article is crucial for the open data movement because it exposes how the omission of specific statistical information on infrastructure conditions hinders objective analysis and accountability. The demand for the publication of detailed and accessible data on accidents related to road conditions is essential to ensure administrative transparency, enable citizen oversight, and guarantee that public resources are managed effectively to protect users.
