Inflation in Guatemala: Why it fell to 4.38% in October, despite the rise in propane prices

The article highlights that, despite the recent slowdown in Guatemala’s annual inflation rate, the indicator remains above the official target of four percent. This situation is primarily due to the persistent influence of petroleum-derived products, especially liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), whose prices experienced a significant increase during the year. Although tax exemptions on fuels helped mitigate inflationary pressure in transportation, the high cost of LPG continues to push up the consumer price index. The relevance of this report to the field of open data lies in the critical importance of transparency in the statistical sources that underpin economic decisions. The figures published by the National Institute of Statistics and the General Directorate of Hydrocarbons not only inform about macroeconomic trends but also serve as a factual basis for urgent public policies. The detailed publication of these indices enables civil society and analysts to independently verify official information, fostering accountability based on verifiable evidence. Finally, the accuracy of these open data has direct implications for determining next year’s minimum wage. With no consensus between labor and business sectors, the President of the Republic will be compelled to use the published inflation indicators to set the final figure. This underscores how access to timely, clear, and public economic data is essential to ensure fair negotiation processes and government decisions that accurately reflect the country’s economic reality.
