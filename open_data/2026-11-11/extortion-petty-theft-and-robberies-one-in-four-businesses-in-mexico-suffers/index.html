Extortion, petty theft and robberies… one in four businesses in Mexico suffers

Extortion, petty theft and robberies… one in four businesses in Mexico suffers

The National Survey of Victimization of Businesses reveals that crime represents a critical operational and economic burden for Mexico’s business sector. With a significant percentage of businesses affected, these crimes not only disrupt commercial activity but also generate massive financial costs that directly impact the country’s stability. This situation underscores that insecurity is a structural obstacle to sustainable economic development. The phenomenon is characterized by an alarming gap between the reality of criminal activity and the institutional response. The overwhelming majority of incidents go unreported due to distrust in law enforcement and the perception that the judicial process is futile, leaving businesses without effective legal protection. This relative impunity fosters the recurrence of offenses, primarily extortion and minor thefts, creating a vicious cycle in which spending on prevention far exceeds any tangible benefits derived from justice. This document is vital for open data initiatives because it illustrates how official statistics can expose hidden systemic failures. By quantifying the dark figure of crime and security expenditures, an abstract social problem is transformed into verifiable data for research. Transparency in this information is essential for demanding evidence-based public policies, optimizing government resources, and designing security strategies that genuinely protect the business environment, demonstrating that open data is a key tool for accountability and institutional improvement.
