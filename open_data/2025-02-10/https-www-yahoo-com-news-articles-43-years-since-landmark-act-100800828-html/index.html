Open Data
ES EN

The article underscores that despite the Freedom of Information Act, lingering public skepticism about government secrecy persists. It emphasizes that true openness relies not just on laws, but on active citizen engagement and the exercise of rights to demand transparency at all administrative levels. This perspective is vital to open data because it highlights the human element in data accessibility. Laws like FOIA provide the legal framework, but their efficacy depends on citizens and organizations actively seeking and using information. Without this persistent demand, data remains inaccessible or underutilized, failing to serve its democratic purpose. Consequently, the piece argues that accountability requires continuous pressure from the public. By attending meetings and requesting records, individuals ensure that government operations are visible. This proactive stance transforms open data from a passive resource into a tool for meaningful participation and governance reform.
