LinkedIn cofounder Reid Hoffman, Hugging Face CEO Clement Delangue sign open letter calling for AI ‘public goods’

Prominent tech leaders and venture capitalists are urging governments and civil society to establish new global public goods in artificial intelligence. This initiative focuses on creating high-value, privacy-preserving public data sets that are accessible to researchers and non-profits. By democratizing access to critical information, particularly in medical and biological fields, this approach aims to accelerate breakthroughs in life-saving treatments and foster innovation among startups, ensuring that AI development benefits the broader public rather than remaining confined to a few large corporations. Simultaneously, the signatories advocate for incentives to develop smaller, open-source AI models that are environmentally friendly and tailored to specific community needs. This shift prioritizes transparency, safety, and accessibility over the current trend of increasingly massive, proprietary systems. Emphasizing that society must remain in charge of AI’s trajectory, the letter argues that open-source alternatives can provide robust tools for education and healthcare while reducing the environmental footprint associated with training enormous models. This move supports a more sustainable and equitable ecosystem where diverse actors can contribute to and benefit from technological advancements. This article is highly relevant to open data because it explicitly frames publicly accessible, safe data sets as essential public infrastructure for ethical AI progress. It highlights the critical link between open data availability and societal well-being, arguing that transparent resources are necessary to mitigate risks like discrimination and job displacement. By calling for data that enables non-profits and researchers to drive scientific and social good, the letter reinforces the open data community’s goal of leveraging shared information to maximize positive human impact.
