Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway’s latest report highlights a paradox in immigrant living conditions: despite facing systemic disadvantages in employment, housing, and income, immigrants report satisfaction levels comparable to the general population. This suggests that material hardships do not necessarily diminish subjective well-being, indicating that non-economic factors play a significant role in how this demographic perceives their quality of life. However, the data reveals critical health disparities, with immigrants experiencing significantly higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness than the native population. These challenges are not uniform, varying substantially by country of origin. This nuance is vital for understanding the complex social realities of integration, showing that broad categorizations often mask distinct needs and vulnerabilities within specific immigrant communities. Interestingly, immigrants demonstrate unusually high trust in state institutions like the judiciary and police, contrasting with lower interpersonal trust. This finding is relevant to open data because it underscores the importance of disaggregating statistics to reveal these contradictory trends. Access to such detailed, nuanced datasets allows policymakers to move beyond generic narratives, enabling targeted interventions that address both structural inequalities and the unique psychological resilience found in these groups.
