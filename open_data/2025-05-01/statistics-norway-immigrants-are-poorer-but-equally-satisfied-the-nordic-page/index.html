Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly worse living conditions regarding employment, housing, and health compared to the native population, yet they report equal life satisfaction. This paradox highlights that material disadvantages do not necessarily diminish subjective well-being, suggesting resilience or differing value systems that prioritize non-material aspects of life. Mental health disparities are stark, with immigrants experiencing higher rates of psychological distress and loneliness, although trust in state institutions like the judiciary and police remains notably high. This strong institutional confidence contrasts with lower interpersonal trust, indicating a unique social dynamic where faith in the system serves as a buffer against personal hardships. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it provides granular, accessible insights into social equity and integration success. By revealing these nuanced contrasts between objective hardship and subjective satisfaction, such datasets enable policymakers and researchers to move beyond simple metrics, fostering more targeted interventions that address specific mental health needs while leveraging existing trust in public services.
