Ireland saw 15% decrease in foreign visitors in March

Ireland saw 15% decrease in foreign visitors in March

Ireland’s tourism sector experienced a significant downturn in March 2025, with foreign visitor numbers dropping sharply compared to the previous year. Although there was a marginal improvement over the 2023 levels, the overall trajectory indicates a sustained decline in inbound tourism activity. This reduction affects both the volume of arrivals and the total economic contribution generated by international travelers during this period. The data reveals that expenditure by visitors also fell considerably year-on-year, highlighting a contraction in tourism revenue. While Great Britain and the United States remain the primary source markets, visitors from these regions saw reduced numbers. The primary motivation for travel remains leisure, and hotels continue to be the preferred accommodation, suggesting that the core leisure market is facing headwinds rather than a shift in traveler preferences. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how official statistical agencies provide transparent, granular datasets that allow for independent analysis of economic trends. By making detailed metrics on visitor origins, spending, and duration publicly available, the Central Statistics Office enables researchers, policymakers, and businesses to assess the health of the tourism industry. Such transparency is crucial for fostering accountability and supporting evidence-based decision-making regarding national economic strategies.
