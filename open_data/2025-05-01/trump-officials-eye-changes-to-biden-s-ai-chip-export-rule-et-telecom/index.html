Trump officials eye changes to Biden's AI chip export rule - ET Telecom

Trump officials eye changes to Biden's AI chip export rule - ET Telecom

The Trump administration is reconsidering a Biden-era rule that restricted access to advanced AI chips by categorizing countries into tiers. This proposed shift aims to replace the current system with a bilateral licensing model, allowing the U.S. to negotiate chip access directly with individual nations. Such a move would likely treat semiconductor availability as a flexible diplomatic tool, enabling the government to leverage technology exports in broader trade negotiations rather than adhering to a fixed, multilateral framework. Critics argue that the existing tiered structure inadvertently drives international buyers toward Chinese alternatives due to perceived inefficiencies and unfair classifications. By lowering the threshold for license exemptions, the new administration hopes to simplify compliance while potentially strengthening control. However, experts warn that removing established tiers could introduce greater complexity, as the current system attempts to balance national security concerns with the practical realities of global technology supply chains. This development is highly relevant to open_data because it illustrates how geopolitical policy directly shapes the infrastructure underlying data ecosystems. If access to computational power becomes a negotiable commodity rather than a standardized right, it may fragment the global internet and restrict cross-border data flows. Consequently, open_data advocates must monitor these regulatory shifts, as restrictions on hardware can effectively limit who has the capacity to generate, process, and share data openly, potentially exacerbating digital divides between allied nations and those of concern.
