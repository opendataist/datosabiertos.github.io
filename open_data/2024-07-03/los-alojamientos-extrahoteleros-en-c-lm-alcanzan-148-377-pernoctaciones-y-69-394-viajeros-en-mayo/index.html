Los alojamientos extrahoteleros en C-LM alcanzan 148.377 pernoctaciones y 69.394 viajeros en mayo

Los alojamientos extrahoteleros en C-LM alcanzan 148.377 pernoctaciones y 69.394 viajeros en mayo

Spain’s tourism sector showed a strong recovery in May, with bookings for non-hotel accommodations rising sharply compared to the previous year. This growth underscores a structural shift in traveler preferences, as visitors increasingly move away from traditional hotels toward alternatives such as apartments, campsites, rural stays, and hostels. The data points to a maturing market in which tourists seek diverse experiences and tend to stay longer, signaling sustained demand for alternative lodging that is reshaping the national hospitality landscape. This trend is fueled by robust performance across all alternative accommodation segments, with notable increases in both domestic and international guest numbers. Although apartment bookings grew only modestly, they remain popular for their flexibility, while campsites and rural tourism experienced double-digit growth, reflecting a growing desire for nature-based and authentic experiences. Rising occupancy rates and longer average stays indicate that these types of accommodations are becoming increasingly competitive, offering viable and attractive options for a broad range of tourists rather than serving only niche markets. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the need for granular, timely, and standardized tourism statistics. Accurately tracking these diverse accommodation types enables policymakers, businesses, and researchers to identify emerging trends and regional strengths. Open data frameworks facilitate the analysis of such detailed metrics, supporting better decision-making, more efficient resource allocation, and greater transparency in understanding how tourism behaviors are evolving across different geographic areas and traveler demographics.
