SAT debe explicar a contribuyente por qué no tomó en cuenta una factura de hospitalización para su declaración anual - Noticaribe

The National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI) ruled that the Tax Administration Service (SAT) violated access rights by refusing to deliver requested tax documents electronically. By requiring taxpayers to appear in person, the SAT disregarded modern verification methods such as video calls. This decision establishes that obligated entities must adapt to technological advances, ensuring that information is accessible without imposing unnecessary logistical burdens or costs on citizens. This case highlights the critical intersection of digital rights and administrative transparency within the open data ecosystem. It demonstrates how open data principles require agencies to provide information in easily accessible formats rather than restrictive ones. When institutions prioritize physical presence over digital convenience, they hinder the fluidity and immediacy that open data aims to promote. The ruling reinforces that accessibility is not merely about providing information, but also about the method of delivery. The precedent set here compels all federal entities to expand their delivery modalities to guarantee effective access to information. It implies that open data initiatives must evolve beyond mere publication to include user-friendly dissemination channels. This ensures that transparency is meaningful and practical for citizens. Consequently, this decision serves as a vital reminder that true openness requires removing barriers to access, leveraging technology to make data and information universally reachable and usable by the public.
