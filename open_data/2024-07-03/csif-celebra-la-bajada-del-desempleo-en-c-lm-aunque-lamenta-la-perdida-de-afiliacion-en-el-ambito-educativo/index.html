CSIF celebra la bajada del desempleo en C-LM aunque lamenta la pérdida de afiliación en el ámbito educativo

The article highlights a significant decline in unemployment driven primarily by the services sector, marking the lowest jobless rates in the region since 2008. While female unemployment has decreased proportionally more than male unemployment, it remains the predominant form of joblessness in absolute terms, underscoring persistent gender disparities in the labor market despite overall positive trends. A critical issue persists within the educational sector, where interim teachers face financial instability during the summer months. The current practice of canceling contracts while stripping seniority rights highlights systemic precarity. The union demands the restoration of historical agreements that guarantee summer wages and continuous career progression, arguing that current policies disproportionately harm interim staff by penalizing their professional development and economic security during periods of non-payment. This report is vital for open data initiatives because it exposes the gap between aggregate employment statistics and the lived realities of vulnerable groups. Public transparency regarding labor agreements, subsidy allocations, and seasonal contract patterns is essential to evaluate policy effectiveness. By making data on interim teacher contracts and union funding accessible, stakeholders can better assess equity in public services and advocate for structural reforms that protect workers' rights beyond simple employment metrics.
