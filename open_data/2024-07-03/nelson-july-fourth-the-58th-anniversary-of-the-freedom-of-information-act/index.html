NELSON: July Fourth — The 58th Anniversary of the Freedom of Information Act

NELSON: July Fourth — The 58th Anniversary of the Freedom of Information Act

This article highlights the enduring legacy of John Moss and Michael Ross Lemov, whose lifelong dedication established and expanded the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA). By championing this legislation against significant political opposition, they transformed government transparency into a fundamental democratic principle. Their story serves as an inspiring reminder of the persistent effort required to secure public access to government records, illustrating how individual advocacy can lead to systemic change that protects citizens' rights to know. FOIA remains a critical instrument for maintaining accountability, evidenced by record-breaking annual request volumes that demonstrate sustained public interest in government operations. As the first modern open government law of its kind, it has influenced policies in over a hundred countries and is recognized by the United Nations as an integral part of freedom of expression. The legislation empowers the public to scrutinize federal actions, acting as a vital check on power and ensuring that government activities remain visible and subject to democratic oversight. The article is highly relevant to open data because FOIA provides the legal mechanism through which structured government information becomes accessible to citizens and researchers. While the law is not without limitations, such as exemptions for national security, it establishes the precedent that data held by the state should generally be open to scrutiny. This framework underpins the modern open data movement, emphasizing that true transparency requires both technical accessibility and robust legal protections for the right to seek and receive information.
