Labour Market Statistics

Northern Ireland’s labor market demonstrates robust recovery, with employment levels reaching historic highs and unemployment rates returning to pre-pandemic standards. The data reveals a significant strengthening of the workforce, driven largely by the services sector, as job counts and total hours worked exceed previous benchmarks. This resurgence indicates a resilient local economy where more individuals are employed, and economic inactivity has notably declined, signaling improved access to work across the region. Despite the positive employment trends, compensation dynamics present a mixed picture for workers. While annual earnings have risen significantly, recent monthly fluctuations suggest slight short-term volatility in pay. Furthermore, while confirmed redundancies remain low compared to historical norms, the rise in proposed redundancies highlights emerging uncertainties within the labor landscape. These figures suggest that while stable employment is widespread, underlying business decisions may still be adjusting to broader economic pressures. This report is critical for open_data initiatives as it exemplifies the value of integrating multiple statistical sources, such as payroll records, business surveys, and household assessments, to create a comprehensive economic narrative. For data practitioners, it underscores the importance of contextualizing raw metrics with historical baselines and understanding the interplay between different labor indicators. Transparent publication of such diverse datasets allows researchers and policymakers to validate findings across methodologies, ensuring that economic insights are both accurate and actionable for the public good.
