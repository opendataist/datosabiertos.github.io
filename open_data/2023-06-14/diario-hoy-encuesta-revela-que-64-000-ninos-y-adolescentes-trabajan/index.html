Diario HOY | Encuesta revela que 64.000 niños y adolescentes trabajan

Paraguayan biochemists have denounced the collapse of the public health system, attributing it to a lack of political will that has led to shortages of supplies and degraded salaries, forcing poor citizens to incur costly travel expenses. This humanitarian crisis exposes the real consequences of state negligence in the management of essential resources. This situation is crucial for open data because it illustrates the gap between the available technological infrastructure and its actual applicability. Data must go beyond administrative records to highlight geographic and economic disparities, enabling citizens and legislators to visualize the structural failures affecting the most vulnerable populations. The mobilization aims to exert legislative pressure, demonstrating that transparency in public information is key to accountability. Without accessible and verifiable data corroborating the described crisis, demands for improved salaries and logistics lack empirical support, hindering the implementation of evidence-based solutions for social well-being.
