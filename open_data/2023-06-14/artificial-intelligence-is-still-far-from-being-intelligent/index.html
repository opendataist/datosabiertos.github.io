Artificial intelligence is still far from being ‘intelligent’

The article argues that current AI systems lack genuine intelligence, relying instead on the uncredited, uncompensated labor of human artists and biased historical data. This dependency raises significant ethical concerns regarding copyright and the perpetuation of societal prejudices, challenging the narrative that AI operates with independent creative or decision-making capabilities. Furthermore, calls for an immediate pause on AI development are portrayed as strategic moves by major tech corporations to protect their market dominance rather than genuine safety measures. By advocating for restrictive licensing, these entities aim to create barriers to entry that prevent smaller, open-source competitors from democratizing access to powerful technology, thereby consolidating wealth and control within a few large entities. For open data advocates, this context underscores the urgent need for regulations that prioritize transparency, open-source accessibility, and ethical standards. Protecting the open data ecosystem is essential to prevent AI from becoming a tool for monopolistic control and misinformation, ensuring that technological benefits remain distributed equitably rather than amplifying existing inequalities and biases inherent in the training data.
