¿No encuentras vivienda? Solo un 16% la vende frente al 78% que quiere comprar

The Spanish housing market is experiencing a severe structural imbalance, with intense buying demand far outstripping critically low supply. This situation has been worsened by pandemic-induced shifts in preferences and rising interest rates. The persistent shortage, stemming from years of inadequate construction, is pushing prices upward and significantly restricting housing access for the general population. Buyers remain motivated by the expectation that economic conditions will eventually stabilize, leading many to postpone purchases until interest rates improve. Younger demographics, in particular, show strong intent to acquire property, reflecting long-term confidence in the market’s eventual correction, even as older age groups display somewhat more cautious planning horizons. This article is highly relevant to open data, as it highlights the need for transparent, real-time information to analyze market dynamics. Accessible data enables policymakers and the public to grasp the severity of the supply-demand gap, facilitating informed decision-making and underscoring the critical need for improved housing production metrics to effectively address the deficit.
