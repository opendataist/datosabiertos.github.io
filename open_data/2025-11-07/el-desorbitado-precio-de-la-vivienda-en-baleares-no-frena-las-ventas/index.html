El desorbitado precio de la vivienda en Baleares no frena las ventas

The Spanish real estate market exhibits diverging trends, with new home prices rising significantly more than those of used properties. Despite this price surge, transactions for new homes have declined due to supply shortages, whereas sales of existing properties have increased. Meanwhile, mortgage approvals have grown, indicating sustained borrowing activity among buyers. Regional disparities are stark, with Madrid and the Balearic Islands being the most expensive areas. The Balearic Islands present an extreme case, where housing costs are vastly higher than the national average. In contrast, regions like Extremadura remain significantly more affordable, highlighting a deep geographical inequality in housing accessibility across the country. This article is relevant to open data as it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, standardized statistical reporting. By publicly sharing detailed metrics on prices and transaction volumes, authorities expose systemic issues like housing affordability crises. Such open datasets empower citizens, researchers, and policymakers to analyze market dynamics, advocate for equitable housing policies, and hold institutions accountable for the socio-economic well-being of residents.
