Para Desirée, no es un "mal menor" nueva ley de protección de datos que blinda a funcionarios

Para Desirée, no es un "mal menor" nueva ley de protección de datos que blinda a funcionarios

The recently enacted data protection law in Paraguay has ignited significant political controversy by restricting public access to information about public officials’ salaries and benefits. Critics argue that this legislative change undermines transparency efforts, effectively hindering citizens’ ability to monitor how public resources are allocated and managed by the government. This development is particularly concerning to open data advocates, as it reverses progress achieved by previous transparency laws. The new provisions are viewed as an unconstitutional setback that weakens essential tools for combating corruption. By creating barriers to accessing the personal financial data of public servants, the law potentially enables secrecy and reduces accountability, eroding trust in public institutions. The article highlights a critical tension between privacy protection and the right to information, a core issue in open data governance. Its relevance lies in demonstrating how legislative adjustments can compromise civic oversight mechanisms. When laws prioritize administrative secrecy over transparency, they hinder the open exchange of data necessary for democratic participation and effective anti-corruption strategies, ultimately limiting society’s ability to hold power accountable.
