Meta enfrenta una demanda por descargar películas para adultos para entrenar a su IA

The central controversy revolves around the illegal acquisition of copyright-protected content, specifically films and literary works, through the use of peer-to-peer (P2P) networks. These practices are intended to train artificial intelligence models, prompting a multimillion-dollar lawsuit filed by creators. The case highlights the tension between the need for large volumes of data to develop generative AI and the respect for intellectual property rights, raising ethical and legal questions about the methods employed by tech giants to access protected information. The relevance to open data lies in the stark exposure of how the technology industry collects information, challenging the principles of transparency and legality that should govern the public availability of knowledge. While open data promotes free access, this case underscores the crucial distinction between public data or data with open licenses and works subject to copyright, where technical naivety or legal deliberation can have serious consequences. Moreover, internal leaks reveal an organizational culture that prioritizes data accumulation over legal compliance, which undermines trust in information management. Despite a recent victory in a trial based on fair use, the ruling leaves the door open to future claims, highlighting legal uncertainty surrounding the training of AI with copyrighted data. This precedent is fundamental to the open data ecosystem, as it forces a reevaluation of the boundaries between technological innovation and creators' rights. The situation demonstrates that data availability is insufficient if not accompanied by rigorous ethics in its acquisition, marking a turning point for data policies in the technology sector.
