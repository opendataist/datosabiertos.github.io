Ghibli y otros estudios japoneses acusan a ChatGPT y Sora 2 de usar sus obras para que la gente cree contenido con IA sin su permiso

The growing popularity of AI-generated imagery that mimics Studio Ghibli’s distinctive aesthetic has triggered a significant backlash in Japan, culminating in a formal demand from the Content Distribution Association. Industry leaders, including major animation studios and broadcasters, have united to accuse OpenAI of utilizing copyrighted works without authorization to train its video generation models, asserting that the resulting outputs closely resemble protected Japanese cultural assets. This conflict highlights the urgent need for clear legal frameworks governing artificial intelligence training data. By demanding the immediate cessation of using unauthorized materials and requesting a sincere response from the tech giant, these entities emphasize that technological advancement must not come at the expense of creators' rights. The situation underscores a broader tension between rapid innovation and the preservation of intellectual property in the digital age. This article is highly relevant to open data discussions as it illustrates the critical intersection between proprietary datasets and the transparency of AI development. It raises essential questions about the necessity of consent, licensing, and ethical data sourcing, challenging the industry to balance open innovation with respect for individual and corporate ownership of creative content.
