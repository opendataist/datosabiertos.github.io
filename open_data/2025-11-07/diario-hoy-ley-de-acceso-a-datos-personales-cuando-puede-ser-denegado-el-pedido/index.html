Diario HOY | Ley de acceso a datos personales: ¿cuándo puede ser denegado el pedido?

Diario HOY | Ley de acceso a datos personales: ¿cuándo puede ser denegado el pedido?

The proposed law balances transparency with privacy by allowing the denial of public information access if it causes concrete harm to individuals' private interests. This mechanism prioritizes personal data protection over public disclosure when the potential damage outweighs the public interest, establishing a legal framework where privacy rights can supersede open government principles under specific, justified circumstances. The procedure grants data subjects the right to oppose information requests through a structured timeline involving state entities and a control authority. Although the final decision rests with the public entity, a non-binding opinion from the supervisory body ensures that requests are evaluated for compatibility with data protection laws. This process introduces checks and balances, aiming to prevent arbitrary denials while respecting individual rights in the handling of sensitive personal information. This legislation is crucial for open data as it defines the boundaries of accessible government information. By categorizing sensitive data such as health, genetics, and political affiliations, it creates necessary restrictions that data portals must respect. Understanding these exemptions is essential for developers and policymakers to design compliant open data platforms that fulfill transparency goals without violating fundamental privacy protections, ensuring ethical data sharing.
