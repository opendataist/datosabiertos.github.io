Inicia INAI investigación por filtración de números de Sheinbaum y Gálvez

The arrest of Juchitán’s former police commissioner highlights deep institutional corruption, as he allegedly protected criminal groups. This action is part of a broader security operation involving multiple state agencies, resulting in the detention of key local figures, including the mayor. These arrests aim to dismantle organized crime networks that have compromised local governance and public safety. The case underscores the urgent need for transparency in law enforcement agencies to prevent collusion with illicit actors. By exposing high-level involvement, authorities can begin to restore trust and ensure that police functions serve the community rather than criminal interests. This situation is relevant to open data because transparent access to arrest records, investigation details, and official communications allows citizens to monitor accountability. Open data mechanisms enable civil society to verify claims, track the judicial process, and demand rigorous oversight, ensuring that anti-crime measures do not simply rotate corrupt personnel but effect systemic reform.
