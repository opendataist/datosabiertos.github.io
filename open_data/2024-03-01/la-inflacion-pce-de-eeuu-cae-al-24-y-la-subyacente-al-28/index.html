La inflación PCE de EEUU cae al 2,4% y la subyacente, al 2,8%

The article reveals a slowdown in U.S. inflation, noting that both the PCE deflator and core inflation have declined, coming in below market expectations. This downward trend suggests that the Federal Reserve may consider interest rate cuts in the near future, although caution persists due to past experiences regarding the transitory nature of price movements. The spending structure shows an increase in services expenditure, offset by a decrease in goods spending, reflecting subtle shifts in consumption patterns and their macroeconomic implications. At the same time, the labor market displays mixed signals, with an increase in initial jobless claims and continuing claims, surpassing consensus forecasts. This rise in unemployment claims indicates a possible easing in the labor market, although the overall insured unemployment rate remains relatively low. The geographic distribution of these figures is also relevant, with significant variations across states that could influence both local and federal policy decisions. This content is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how economic and labor data, when available in accessible and standardized formats, enable analysts and citizens to monitor economic trends in real time. Transparency in the publication of these indicators fosters greater government accountability and helps researchers validate economic models, thereby contributing to informed decision-making in both public and private sectors.
