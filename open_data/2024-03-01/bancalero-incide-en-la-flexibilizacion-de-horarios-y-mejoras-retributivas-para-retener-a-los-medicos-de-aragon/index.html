Bancalero incide en la "flexibilización" de horarios y mejoras retributivas para retener a los médicos de Aragón

The Aragonese government has presented a plan to retain young doctors by offering flexible working hours, improved compensation, and logistical support such as housing. The strategy aims to mitigate the severe shortage of healthcare professionals, which worsens annually, by providing stable contracts and facilities for families or couples. The goal is to make local healthcare an attractive option, particularly for those completing their residency, thereby preventing the brain drain to other regions. The proposal includes redefining underserved areas and improving working conditions in these zones, ensuring stability and continuous training. The measures include short-term contracts for recent graduates and preferential authorization for practice periods in large hospitals, ensuring that travel time counts as working hours. These initiatives aim to address the needs of residents and facilitate their professional integration within the regional public healthcare network. This debate is relevant to open data because the controversial measures are based on drafts not publicly available, leading to criticism over lack of transparency. The opposition questions the absence of the document on open data portals and the exclusion of consultations with trade unions. This case highlights the critical need for public health policies, crucial for social well-being, to be developed with open access to information to ensure accountability and effective citizen participation.
