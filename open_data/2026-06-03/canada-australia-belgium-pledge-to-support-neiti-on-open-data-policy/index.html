Canada, Australia, Belgium, pledge to support NEITI on open data policy

The Embassy of Belgium, along with the Australian and Canadian High Commissions in Nigeria, has formally pledged support for the Nigeria Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative’s Open Data Policy. This commitment, announced during a visit to the newly completed NEITI Data Center, underscores a shared global determination to enhance transparency and accountability within the extractive sector through stronger diplomatic partnerships and technical cooperation. The data center serves as a comprehensive repository for historical and current industry information, designed to provide easy public access to aggregated and disaggregated data. By acting as a hub for evidence-based decision-making, the facility empowers civil society, media, and government bodies to scrutinize extractive industry activities, thereby fostering informed public discourse and supporting reforms aligned with international transparency standards. This initiative is crucial for open data because it transforms raw industrial information into accessible, usable assets for diverse stakeholders. It addresses the critical need for data science capacity building and supports Nigeria’s transition amidst global energy shifts. By ensuring that extractive data is openly available and well-understood, the project promotes sustainable advocacy for environmental and social issues, reinforcing the role of open data in driving good governance and equitable resource management.
