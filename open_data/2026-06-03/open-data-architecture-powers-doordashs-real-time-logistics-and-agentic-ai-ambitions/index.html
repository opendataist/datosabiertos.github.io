Open data architecture powers DoorDash’s real-time logistics and agentic AI ambitions

The shift from human-centric analytics to machine-driven workflows, particularly for agentic AI, necessitates a move away from monolithic data stacks toward open data architectures. As machine users increasingly outpace humans in consuming data, organizations must adopt compute-agnostic designs that decouple storage from processing. This structural evolution is critical for scalability, allowing platforms to support simultaneous real-time logistics, machine learning, and AI operations without the latency and rigidity inherent in traditional, closed systems. Implementing open standards like Apache Iceberg significantly reduces infrastructure costs and complexity by minimizing redundant data movement across disparate tools. More importantly, this approach enables centralized governance at the storage layer, ensuring that both human analysts and AI agents rely on a single, consistent source of truth. By defining data quality and access policies once, enterprises prevent the hallucinations and inconsistent decisions that arise when multiple platforms interpret entity data differently, thereby securing the reliability required for automated, agent-based decision-making. This article is highly relevant to the open_data community because it demonstrates that open standards are no longer just about accessibility but are fundamental to the stability and performance of modern AI systems. The DoorDash case study validates that open data architectures provide the necessary flexibility and governance framework to support complex, multi-agent environments. It highlights how interoperable formats empower data engineers to focus on business logic rather than plumbing, proving that open data is the enabler of scalable, trustworthy AI integration in enterprise settings.
