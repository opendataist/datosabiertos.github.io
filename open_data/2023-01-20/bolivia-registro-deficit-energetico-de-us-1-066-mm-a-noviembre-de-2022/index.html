Bolivia registró déficit energético de $us 1.066 MM a noviembre de 2022

Bolivia has transitioned from being a traditional hydrocarbon exporter to a net importer, a shift driven by declining domestic production and soaring fuel costs. This structural change has generated a substantial trade deficit, directly affecting the national economy by slowing GDP growth. The situation underscores the severe consequences of underinvestment in exploration and the failure to replenish dwindling natural gas reserves, transforming what could have been an energy hub into a vulnerable importer. The crisis is further intensified by state subsidies that, while maintaining stable domestic prices, impose heavy fiscal burdens and inadvertently stimulate illegal cross-border trade. Experts emphasize that reversing this trend requires significant foreign investment and legal reforms, both of which encounter considerable political and economic obstacles. As a result, the country faces long-term dependence on fuel imports, with no immediate prospects for energy self-sufficiency despite announced exploration initiatives. This case is particularly relevant to discussions on open data, as it highlights the importance of transparent and accessible energy statistics for accurate economic forecasting. When data on production, reserves, and trade balances is publicly available, analysts and policymakers can identify structural vulnerabilities early. Without such transparency, the societal and economic costs of energy insecurity remain obscured, hindering the implementation of effective, evidence-based public policies.
