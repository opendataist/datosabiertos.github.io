MORE EVIDENCE: FOIA Requests Reveal There Were No DOJ Investigations on Election Fraud After 2020 Election as Bill Barr Claimed

Freedom of Information Act requests submitted by political figures reveal that the Department of Justice conducted no investigations into alleged widespread voter fraud following the 2020 election. These records directly contradict previous public statements by former Attorney General Bill Barr, who claimed there was no evidence of fraud but also suggested investigations had occurred or were considered. The absence of documentation across most critical jurisdictions indicates that no formal inquiries were initiated, challenging the narrative that the department actively sought and dismissed widespread criminal activity. This transparency underscores the critical role of FOIA requests in holding government officials accountable for their public assertions. When institutional records do not align with official rhetoric, it suggests a deliberate omission rather than an absence of criminal conduct. The discrepancy highlights how access to government documents can expose potential misrepresentations by leadership, ensuring that the public record reflects administrative actions rather than political convenience. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how the systematic release of government records serves as a vital check on power. It illustrates that open data is not merely about volume, but about verifying claims through accessible, verifiable evidence. By making internal communications and investigation logs available, society can detect gaps between stated policies and actual practices, reinforcing the necessity of transparency in maintaining democratic integrity.
