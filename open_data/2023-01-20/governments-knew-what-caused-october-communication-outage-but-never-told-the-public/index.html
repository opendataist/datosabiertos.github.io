Governments knew what caused October communication outage but never told the public

The article exposes significant failures in government communication regarding the Shefa-2 subsea cable outage in Shetland. Although both the Scottish Government and the Maritime and Coastguard Agency confirmed early on that a fishing vessel caused the damage, they withheld this information from the media. This silence allowed unfounded speculation about Russian sabotage to spread unchecked on social media, leading to public condemnation of the authorities for their irresponsibility and lack of transparent engagement during the crisis. This incident highlights the critical intersection of open data and public trust, as the truth remained hidden behind bureaucratic delays and non-disclosure until revealed through freedom of information requests. The failure to proactively share known facts demonstrates how the absence of accessible, timely information can exacerbate public anxiety and erode confidence in institutional integrity. It underscores the necessity for proactive data transparency, ensuring that citizens are not left to speculate when clear facts are available to officials. Furthermore, the event reveals vulnerabilities in critical infrastructure, prompting calls for more resilient and secure communication systems. By exposing the inefficiencies in handling crisis information and the fragility of current connectivity, the case serves as a vital lesson for policymakers. It emphasizes that open access to factual data is not just a procedural requirement but a fundamental element of effective governance and community security in the digital age.
