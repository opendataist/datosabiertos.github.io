Preocupante nivel de necesidades básicas insatisfechas en Paraguay

The analysis of Unmet Basic Needs (NBI) serves as a critical tool for multidimensional poverty reduction by examining structural factors such as education, housing, sanitation, and economic security. Improvements in these areas not only enhance immediate quality of life but also help break the intergenerational transmission of poverty, ensuring long-term social stability and economic autonomy for vulnerable populations. These indicators are particularly valuable because they are directly actionable through public policy, as they correspond to constitutionally established rights. By leveraging census data, authorities can design, monitor, and evaluate interventions with high territorial granularity, allowing for targeted responses that address specific local inequalities and demographic disparities. This data-driven approach transforms abstract social goals into measurable, accountable policy outcomes. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores how accessible, high-quality demographic statistics empower governments to make informed decisions and hold institutions accountable. Open data facilitates the transparency required to identify urgent gaps, such as the lack of basic infrastructure, thereby enabling civil society and policymakers to demand effective solutions and ensure that economic progress translates into universal access to essential human rights.
