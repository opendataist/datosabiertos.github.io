Resultados INEGI sirven autoridades verificar políticas públicas

Resultados INEGI sirven autoridades verificar políticas públicas

Mexico has achieved a significant reduction in poverty rates between 2022 and 2024, driven largely by favorable trade dynamics with the United States, minimum wage increases, and social programs. However, this progress is nuanced by a simultaneous rise in the number of people experiencing social deprivations. This divergence highlights that while extreme economic hardship has decreased, fundamental barriers to fully exercising rights persist among those no longer classified as poor. The data underscores a critical complexity in measuring well-being: statistical exit from poverty does not guarantee the fulfillment of social needs. Experts argue that these findings offer vital feedback for policymakers, urging a shift toward addressing deeper structural deficiencies rather than focusing solely on income metrics. The emergence of new social demands among those entering the middle class further illustrates that traditional poverty metrics may overlook ongoing vulnerabilities and unmet social requirements. This report is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, granular statistics to evaluate policy effectiveness. By contrasting official poverty metrics with indicators of social deprivation, the article emphasizes that open data initiatives must capture multidimensional well-being. Such transparency allows citizens and experts to verify government claims, hold authorities accountable, and ensure that development strategies address the full spectrum of citizen needs beyond simple economic thresholds.
