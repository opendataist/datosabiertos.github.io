Advocacy Group Writes Nigerian Broadcasting Commission To Disclose Details Of 302 Sanctioned Media Stations

Media Rights Agenda has utilized the Freedom of Information Act to demand transparency from the National Broadcasting Commission regarding the sanctioning of over three hundred media outlets. This request seeks comprehensive data on the specific complaints, evidence, and administrative correspondence that led to these punitive actions, challenging the opacity often surrounding regulatory decisions. The advocacy group highlights the significant volume of sanctions issued during a critical period leading up to the 2023 elections. By demanding details on both penalized and absolved stations, they aim to expose potential inconsistencies or biases in how broadcasting offenses are adjudicated, ensuring that regulatory power is not exercised arbitrarily or as a tool for political suppression. This effort is vital to open data principles because it asserts the public’s right to access government-held information concerning media governance. Transparent disclosure of enforcement records allows civil society and citizens to scrutinize regulatory bodies, fostering accountability and ensuring that broadcasting regulations are applied fairly, consistently, and in accordance with democratic norms rather than hidden behind bureaucratic secrecy.
