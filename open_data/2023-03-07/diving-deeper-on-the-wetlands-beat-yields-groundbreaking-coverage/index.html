Diving Deeper on the Wetlands Beat Yields Groundbreaking Coverage

The article highlights how journalists leveraged Freedom of Information Act requests, data analysis, and on-the-ground reporting to expose the federal government's role in protecting wetlands against climate change. Their award-winning series revealed how policy decisions and scientific warnings were often ignored, underscoring the critical importance of transparent, accessible government records in holding agencies accountable for environmental stewardship. A key finding was the disproportionate impact of wetland degradation on minority and low-income communities, illustrated through reporting on historic flooding in a majority-Black Michigan city. This focus demonstrates that open data and document access are not merely technical exercises but essential tools for uncovering environmental injustices and amplifying the voices of marginalized populations affected by policy failures. This case is relevant to the open data movement because it proves that rigorous investigative journalism depends on the public’s right to access government information. By challenging initial denials and persisting through appeals, the reporters secured the internal documents necessary to tell a comprehensive story, illustrating that robust open data practices enable deeper scrutiny of environmental risks and drive meaningful public discourse.
