Elon Musk blames AI scraping for Twitter problems

Elon Musk attributes recent Twitter outages to aggressive artificial intelligence data scraping, arguing that corporations and startups are overloading infrastructure to fuel their valuations. By implementing strict usage limits, Musk aims to deter these entities from harvesting vast amounts of data without contributing to the platform's operational costs. This stance highlights the growing tension between social media platforms hosting public discourse and AI companies seeking unrestricted access to that content for model training. The implementation of these restrictions has sparked widespread user frustration, as both logged-in and anonymous users faced significant barriers to accessing information. While Musk claims the measures are necessary to manage server loads caused by scrapers, critics suggest the instability may stem from internal management decisions, such as staff reductions and service cuts. This ambiguity raises important questions about the reliability of platforms when commercial interests conflict with the need for stable, open access to information. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocates because it illustrates how private platforms can arbitrarily restrict access to publicly available information under the guise of security or cost management. When companies prioritize protection against AI scraping over transparency, it hinders public scrutiny and independent research. The incident underscores the fragility of open information ecosystems, where access is contingent on the profit motives and technical strategies of a single entity rather than being a guaranteed public utility.
