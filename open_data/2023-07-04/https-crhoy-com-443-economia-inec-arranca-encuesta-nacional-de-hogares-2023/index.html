The National Household Survey serves as a vital tool for evaluating public policies and guiding private investment by monitoring living conditions and access to services. Its inclusion of a specific module on communication technologies highlights the growing need to assess digital skills and infrastructure, providing essential data for formulating science and technology policies while enabling the reporting of international statistical indicators. The reliability of these insights depends on strict methodological rigor and public trust, ensured through rigorous verification processes for field personnel and the legal protection of respondent anonymity. This confidentiality framework guarantees that individual data remains secure from misuse, fostering the willingness of citizens to participate openly in the information-gathering process. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how official statistical bodies transform raw household information into actionable public intelligence. By balancing transparent operational procedures with robust privacy safeguards, the survey illustrates the foundational principles necessary for generating high-quality, trustworthy data ecosystems that support both government accountability and evidence-based societal development.
