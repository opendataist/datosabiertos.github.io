2022 RTI Report Presented To Parliament | - Ghanamma.com

The recent parliamentary presentation of the 2022 Right to Information Act report highlights Ghana’s accelerating journey toward institutional transparency and accountability. The Minister underscored that the government is moving beyond mere legislative frameworks to enforce substantive compliance across public sectors. By integrating RTI adherence into the performance agreements of Chief Directors, the state ensures that transparency becomes a measurable obligation for leadership, rather than an optional courtesy. This structural shift signals a cultural change within civil service, where information access is viewed as a fundamental duty essential for democratic governance. A critical development in this trajectory is the enhancement of the RTI Commission’s regulatory power, specifically through the acquisition of prosecutorial authority. This empowerment allows the commission to take legal action against entities that flout the act, transforming the framework from a guideline into an enforceable mandate. Combined with increased logistical support and expanded field offices, these measures aim to reduce barriers for citizens seeking information. The initiative reflects a broader commitment to ensuring that the right to know is not just a theoretical concept but a practically accessible reality for all citizens, regardless of geographic location. Looking forward, the focus is shifting toward digital modernization and sustained sensitization efforts. The planned operationalization of an online records management system promises to streamline request processes, making information more accessible and efficient. This technological advancement, paired with ongoing recruitment of information officers, addresses historical gaps in compliance and capacity. For open data advocates, this report is significant because it demonstrates how robust legal enforcement, combined with digital infrastructure, creates a sustainable ecosystem for transparency. It serves as a model for how governments can actively dismantle opacity, thereby empowering citizens through genuine access to public records.
