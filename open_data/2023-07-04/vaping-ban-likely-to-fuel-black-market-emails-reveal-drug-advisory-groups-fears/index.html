Vaping ban likely to fuel black market: emails reveal drug advisory group’s fears

Government advisors warned that Australia’s strict prescription-only model for vapes exacerbates public health issues by fueling a black market. Experts argue that prohibitive regulations inadvertently make nicotine more potent and accessible to minors than legally controlled products, ultimately criminalizing users rather than addressing the root causes of addiction. This approach contrasts with international jurisdictions that allow easier access, suggesting that severe restrictions may increase harm rather than reduce it. Concerns were raised that policymakers were influenced by media-driven moral panics and anecdotal evidence rather than robust scientific data. Advisors cautioned that excessively harsh controls could make traditional cigarettes more attractive to young people, given their higher toxicity. The advice emphasized the need for evidence-based policy that considers harm reduction and demand reduction strategies, which were notably absent from the initial regulatory proposals despite their established role in drug policy. This disclosure is highly relevant to open data as it demonstrates how freedom of information requests can reveal the conflict between internal expert consensus and public political decisions. It highlights the importance of transparency in government advisory processes, showing how confidential communications can shed light on the evidence base—or lack thereof—underpinning major health reforms. Such openness allows the public to scrutinize whether policy is driven by scientific reality or political narrative.
