Revealed: How much has your county councillor spent on petrol and diesel?

Oxfordshire councillors have incurred significant fuel costs while attending meetings, revealing a tension between official "active travel" agendas and the practical realities of governing a largely rural region. High-mileage claims from several leaders highlight that reliance on private vehicles remains necessary for those representing distant or underserved communities, suggesting that current public transport infrastructure is insufficient to support efficient democratic participation across all areas. Councillors defend these expenses by emphasizing that many rural locations lack viable public transport options or timely connections, making alternative modes of travel impractical for fulfilling multiple daily duties. This reliance on cars is not merely a preference but a functional necessity to ensure representatives can attend numerous parish and district meetings, thereby maintaining a visible and accessible presence for residents who might otherwise be disconnected from local government. This situation is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent disclosure of expense claims can expose systemic infrastructure gaps and inform policy debates on sustainable mobility. By making these financial details publicly accessible, it forces a conversation on how data can reveal the hidden costs of democracy and whether current transport investments adequately support equitable civic engagement, rather than simply highlighting individual spending habits.
