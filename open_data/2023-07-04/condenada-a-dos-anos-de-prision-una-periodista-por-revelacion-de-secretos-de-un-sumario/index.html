Condenada a dos años de prisión una periodista por revelación de secretos de un sumario

This ruling establishes a critical precedent for transparency and access to public records, as it is the first case in Spain in which a journalist has been convicted for disclosing confidential judicial documents. The conviction highlights the tension between the public’s right to information and the protection of personal privacy, establishing that not all details contained in a criminal investigation carry sufficient social relevance to override the victim’s right to privacy. The court determined that publishing specific, granular data from the case files—such as forensic autopsy details and toxicology reports—exceeded the bounds of legitimate public interest. By emphasizing that such information was irrelevant, gratuitous, and unnecessary for public understanding, the judgment clarifies that freedom of the press does not shield the disclosure of legally protected secrets, even in high-profile cases that attract intense media attention. For open data initiatives, this decision serves as a cautionary benchmark regarding the scope of accessible judicial information. It implies that while case outcomes are public, the raw, sensitive data contained in preliminary investigations may remain legally restricted to safeguard individual rights. Consequently, developers and researchers must carefully navigate legal frameworks to distinguish between publicly available administrative data and protected procedural secrets in order to avoid legal liability.
