¿No se darán los nombres de los imputados? El Poder Judicial responde sobre su política de datos

¿No se darán los nombres de los imputados? El Poder Judicial responde sobre su política de datos

The Judicial Power Council (CPJ) has clarified that its new data protection policy aims to balance judicial transparency with individual privacy, rather than restricting access to information. By implementing a general rule of dissociating or anonymizing personal data in court documents, the CPJ argues that this measure protects the dignity and security of vulnerable parties, such as minors and victims of violence. This approach aligns with international standards, emphasizing that true transparency involves making relevant legal information accessible without causing unnecessary harm to individuals. Crucially, the CPJ asserts that high-profile cases of public interest will remain fully accessible to journalists and the public. The policy does not limit media access to courtrooms or public hearings, nor does it hide essential case details like accusations or verdicts. Instead, it only excludes non-essential personal identifiers, such as home addresses or identification numbers, from published decisions. This ensures that the press can continue its investigative work and the public stay informed about significant legal outcomes without exposing sensitive private data that could jeopardize the safety or reputation of those involved. This development is highly relevant to open data because it highlights a critical shift in how public sector information is managed. It illustrates the evolving challenge of maintaining open government principles while complying with modern data protection regulations. For open data advocates, this case underscores the importance of clearly defining "public interest" versus "private data," ensuring that datasets remain usable for journalism and research without violating fundamental human rights. It demonstrates that ethical data governance can coexist with transparency, provided that anonymization techniques are robustly applied.
