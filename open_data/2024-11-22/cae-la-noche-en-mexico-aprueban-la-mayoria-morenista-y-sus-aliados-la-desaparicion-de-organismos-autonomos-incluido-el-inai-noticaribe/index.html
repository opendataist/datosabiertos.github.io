CAE LA NOCHE EN MÉXICO: Aprueban la mayoría morenista y sus aliados la desaparición de organismos autónomos, incluido el INAI - Noticaribe

CAE LA NOCHE EN MÉXICO: Aprueban la mayoría morenista y sus aliados la desaparición de organismos autónomos, incluido el INAI - Noticaribe

The Mexican Chamber of Deputies approved a significant constitutional reform that eliminates several key autonomous bodies, including the National Institute of Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI). This legislative initiative, driven by the ruling party’s majority, aims to centralize functions within standard government ministries, such as the Secretariats of Economy and Energy. Proponents argue that this change streamlines administration, while opponents warn that it dismantles critical checks and balances established to prevent political interference in regulatory and data protection matters. The most profound implication for open data is the erosion of institutional guarantees for information transparency and personal privacy. By abolishing the INAI, the legal framework that ensures public access to government records and protects citizens’ data from state overreach is effectively dissolved. This shift suggests that transparency will now depend on political goodwill rather than independent oversight, creating a vulnerable environment where data rights are subordinate to executive authority instead of being protected as fundamental public interests. This development is crucial for the open data community, as it highlights the fragility of data infrastructure in the absence of autonomous safeguards. It underscores that the mere technological availability of information is meaningless without independent institutions to enforce accountability and prevent misuse. The reform serves as a stark reminder that sustainable open data ecosystems rely on robust, legally protected autonomy to ensure integrity, fostering a global discourse on the necessity of separating data governance from direct political control to maintain public trust.
