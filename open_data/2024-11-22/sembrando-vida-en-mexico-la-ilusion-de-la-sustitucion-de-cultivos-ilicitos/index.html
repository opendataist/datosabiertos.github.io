Sembrando Vida en México: ¿la ilusión de la sustitución de cultivos ilícitos?

The analysis concludes that the Mexican government program "Sembrando Vida" has been ineffective in replacing illegal crop cultivation with legal alternatives. Although political discourse frames the initiative as a tool for environmental preservation and poverty alleviation, operational rules lack explicit objectives for drug substitution. Consequently, there is no structured strategy to monitor progress, rendering the program’s impact on illicit agriculture unverifiable through official channels. Empirical evidence from agricultural statistics and beneficiary data contradicts the program’s stated goals. Instead of observing an increase in legal crop surfaces as farmers transition away from illicit activities, the data reveals a decrease in legally planted areas. Interviews with farmers and officials further suggest that decisions regarding cultivation are driven primarily by market prices and economic incentives rather than government incentives, indicating that the financial support provided by the program has minimal influence on these agricultural choices. This study is critical for open data advocates because it highlights the severe lack of transparent, accessible, and systematic data regarding government social programs. The inability to evaluate policy effectiveness stems not only from ambiguous objectives but also from the government’s failure to provide comprehensive, publicly available datasets. This opacity prevents independent verification of results and underscores the urgent need for improved data governance to ensure accountability and assess the true impact of public spending.
