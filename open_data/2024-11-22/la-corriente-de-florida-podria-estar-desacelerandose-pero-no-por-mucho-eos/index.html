La corriente de Florida podría estar desacelerándose, pero no por mucho - Eos

The article concludes that, following a rigorous reanalysis, the Florida Current has remained stable over the past four decades. Initially, a slight weakening had been reported and attributed to human activity, but the study identifies a systematic error in the historical processing of data related to Earth’s geomagnetic field. Correcting this two-decade bias eliminates the direct evidence of a significant slowdown, thereby lowering the overall estimate of the decline in the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation. This technical revision highlights the fragility of scientific conclusions based solely on limited temporal records. The natural variability of ocean currents, combined with methodological errors that went undetected for a long time, can generate spurious trends. Consequently, the current results underscore that the previously perceived instability was largely artifactual, suggesting that oceans have maintained a more constant dynamic than previously thought, until more refined and extended data become available. The relevance to open data lies in its crucial role as a tool for scientific auditing and transparency. This case demonstrates how public access, analysis, and reuse of original datasets enable the correction of misinterpretations and improve the accuracy of global climate models. Moreover, it illustrates the critical need to maintain long-term, clear, and well-documented records, which are essential for distinguishing between genuine climate change signals and statistical fluctuations, thereby facilitating a more robust and reliable understanding of Earth systems.
