EL sector hotelero denuncia falta de transparencia y comunicación del Gobierno con el nuevo registro de viajeros

The Spanish Hotel Confederation (Cehat) criticizes the government’s lack of transparency and dialogue regarding Royal Decree 933/2021, which mandates the documentation of hotel reservations. This administrative silence creates significant uncertainty for hotels and travelers, who remain unclear on how to implement the regulation effectively. The absence of clarifying ministerial orders has led to widespread frustration, as the sector feels ignored despite repeated requests for clarification and collaboration. The core concern lies in the disproportionate bureaucratic burden and potential privacy risks associated with the new data collection requirements. Hotels argue that the obligation to store sensitive traveler information is excessive and contradicts European privacy directives, threatening both tourist trust and the competitiveness of the Spanish tourism industry. There are legitimate fears that improper handling of this data could compromise personal security and damage Spain’s reputation as a safe, privacy-respecting destination. This situation is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical tension between mandatory government data collection and the principles of data minimization, transparency, and user privacy. The industry’s call for a simplified, compliant system underscores the need for regulations that balance security with civil liberties. Ultimately, the case demonstrates how opaque policy-making and excessive data hoarding can hinder economic sectors, emphasizing the importance of clear, privacy-by-design frameworks in public administration.
