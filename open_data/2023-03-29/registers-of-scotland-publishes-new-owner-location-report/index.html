Registers of Scotland publishes new owner location report

Registers of Scotland’s latest report highlights how international ownership shapes the nation’s property landscape, revealing that a small but distinct segment of titles is held by entities and individuals based outside the United Kingdom. This data illustrates the growing intersection of global capital and local land markets, showing that foreign investment is not merely anecdotal but statistically significant and geographically concentrated. The findings indicate that corporate ownership from Crown Dependencies and overseas territories dominates the international sector, while individual buyers frequently originate from the United States. Urban centers like Edinburgh and Glasgow attract the highest volume of these foreign titles, driven by specific commercial interests such as infrastructure linked to major transport hubs. This concentration underscores how global players target specific economic zones rather than dispersing assets evenly across the country. This information is vital for open_data initiatives because it demonstrates the utility of publishing granular, location-based ownership records to track cross-border capital flows. By making such detailed statistics accessible, authorities enable researchers and journalists to monitor transparency in land ownership. The report serves as a key example of how open government data can illuminate economic dependencies and support informed public debate regarding property regulation.
