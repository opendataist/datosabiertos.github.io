Clearview CEO claims company's database of scraped images is now 30 billion strong | Engadget

Clearview AI has removed billions of images from its database while maintaining significant demand among law enforcement agencies. This highlights a critical tension in open data ecosystems: the conflict between the availability of publicly scraped information and the urgent need for privacy protections and regulatory oversight. The scale of usage reveals how opaque surveillance tools operate with minimal transparency. Without clear data on accuracy or error rates, it is nearly impossible to verify claims of safety or accountability, leaving citizens vulnerable to unchecked algorithmic bias and potential wrongful arrests. This situation underscores the necessity of open standards in biometric technology. To protect civil liberties, policymakers must move toward legislation that restricts the commercial sale of biometric data and mandates greater transparency, ensuring that public safety does not come at the cost of fundamental privacy rights.
