La pobreza en Uruguay cayó al 9,1 % tras el segundo semestre de 2022

The article reports a decline in poverty in Uruguay during late 2022, highlighting significant disparities across demographics and geography. It identifies children, female-headed households, and Afro-descendant populations as the most vulnerable groups, alongside specific departments with higher incidence rates. This narrative underscores how socioeconomic data reveals structural inequalities that require targeted policy interventions. This content is crucial for open_data because it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, accessible statistical information for public accountability. When governments release granular demographic data, citizens and researchers can identify marginalized communities and advocate for equitable resource distribution. Open datasets empower civil society to verify official claims and push for social justice. By making these findings publicly available, institutions facilitate informed public debate, ensuring that data serves not just as a record, but as a tool for driving meaningful social change and reducing inequality.
