The article highlights the INE’s official statistical data on Spanish tourist housing, revealing that nearly seventy percent of these accommodations are concentrated in coastal and island regions such as Andalusia and the Balearic Islands. This data serves as a crucial national benchmark, despite discrepancies with autonomous communities that often maintain their own, sometimes conflicting, registries. The distribution underscores a significant disparity between tourist-heavy zones and inland provinces, where some small municipalities exhibit a disproportionately high share of housing dedicated to tourism relative to their total housing stock. This concentration impacts local real estate markets and housing availability, particularly in smaller coastal communities where the prevalence of tourist accommodations is most intense. This information is vital for the open data community, as it demonstrates the importance of standardized, accessible government statistics. Reliable official datasets enable researchers and citizens to analyze urban planning trends, assess housing affordability, and hold authorities accountable for transparency in managing the growing tourism sector’s impact on residential areas.
