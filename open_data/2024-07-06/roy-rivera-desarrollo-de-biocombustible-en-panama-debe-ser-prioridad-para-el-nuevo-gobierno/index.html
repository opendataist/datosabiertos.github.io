Roy Rivera: ‘Desarrollo de biocombustible en Panamá debe ser prioridad para el nuevo gobierno

Roy Rivera: ‘Desarrollo de biocombustible en Panamá debe ser prioridad para el nuevo gobierno

The article highlights the growing economic burden on Panama due to the rising costs of importing fossil fuel derivatives. In response, the government and business sectors are prioritizing the development of a domestic biofuel industry to reduce reliance on foreign energy sources. This strategic shift aims to retain capital within the national economy, stimulate investment in the interior regions, and generate significant employment opportunities by substituting expensive imports with locally produced, cleaner alternatives. The transition to biofuels is legally mandated through recent legislative reforms requiring specific percentages of bioethanol in gasoline, starting with a 5% mixture and gradually increasing to 10%. Stakeholders, including union leaders and private sector representatives, strongly support this initiative, viewing it as a vital step for economic development and environmental sustainability. They emphasize the need for government agility in implementing these policies to ensure the private sector can effectively create jobs and leverage free trade agreements. This content is highly relevant to open data because the transition relies heavily on the collection and analysis of transparent economic and production metrics. Institutions like the National Institute of Statistics and Census (INEC) and the Observatory of Economic Complexity (OEC) provide the essential datasets needed to track import costs, monitor production efficiency, and evaluate the project's economic impact. Accessible, accurate open data allows policymakers and citizens to verify progress, ensure regulatory compliance, and make informed decisions regarding energy security and sustainable development.
