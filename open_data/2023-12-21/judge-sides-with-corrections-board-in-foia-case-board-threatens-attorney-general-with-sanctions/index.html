Judge sides with corrections board in FOIA case; board threatens attorney general with sanctions

A Pulaski County judge ruled against Attorney General Tim Griffin, declaring his lawsuit against the Board of Corrections legally and ethically flawed. The core issue was a conflict of interest: Griffin sued board members individually for alleged Freedom of Information Act violations while his office simultaneously represented those same individuals in other litigation. By suing his own statutory clients, Griffin violated his mandatory duty to act as legal counsel for all state agencies, creating an unresolvable procedural conflict that prevented the court from addressing the substantive claims. This decision highlights the critical importance of transparent legal representation and adherence to statutory duties in government operations. The judge emphasized that the Attorney General cannot bypass legal obligations by refusing to invoke procedures for hiring outside counsel when conflicts arise. By failing to secure proper representation for the board or dismiss the conflict, Griffin effectively deprived his clients of legal defense, undermining the integrity of the judicial process and the rule of law within state governance structures. This case is highly relevant to open data and transparency advocates because it exposes how legal maneuvering can obstruct public accountability. When government officials engage in retaliatory lawsuits or ignore ethical guidelines, it creates barriers to public access to information and fair administrative processes. The ruling reinforces that transparency mechanisms, such as freedom of information laws, must be applied consistently and without conflict of interest to ensure that the public’s right to know is not compromised by internal political disputes or procedural malpractice.
