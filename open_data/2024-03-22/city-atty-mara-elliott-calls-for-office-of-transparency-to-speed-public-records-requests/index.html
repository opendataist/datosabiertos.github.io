City Atty. Mara Elliott Calls for 'Office of Transparency' to Speed Public Records Requests

San Diego’s City Attorney’s Office proposes establishing a centralized Office of Transparency to overhaul the city’s fragmented and inconsistent public records response system. This initiative aims to resolve longstanding issues where siloed departments and outdated technology lead to errors, delays, and increased litigation risks, ultimately fostering greater public trust through more accurate and efficient service. While the proposal addresses critical inefficiencies, council members emphasize the need for a thorough financial review to ensure the new structure remains budget-neutral. By potentially reorganizing and training existing staff rather than hiring new personnel, the city seeks to mitigate costs. The Rules Committee has ordered an analysis to confirm whether these operational improvements can offset expenses without requiring additional tax revenue, balancing transparency goals with fiscal responsibility. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates because it highlights the structural barriers to effective information access. A unified, technology-driven approach would not only reduce the administrative burden on employees but also ensure that the growing volume of public requests is handled systematically. Centralizing this function sets a precedent for modernizing government transparency, ensuring that open data initiatives are supported by robust, coordinated infrastructure rather than ad-hoc departmental efforts.
