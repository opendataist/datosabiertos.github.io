The article highlights the critical vulnerability of personal data in virtual spaces, emphasizing that everyday digital interactions often expose individuals to significant risks such as identity theft and fraud. It argues that current preventive measures and reporting mechanisms are insufficient, as the anonymity of cybercriminals and the organized nature of these attacks make detection and justice elusive. The lack of comprehensive statistics further obscures the true scale of the problem, creating a false sense of security among citizens who may not realize they are targeted. This lack of transparency extends to official data, where reported cases represent only a fraction of actual incidents due to a low culture of reporting and judicial inefficiencies. The narrative underscores the broader social implications, including threats to minors, women, and migrants, while noting the economic burden placed on individuals for cybersecurity tools. The core issue is the disconnect between the magnitude of cybercrime and the availability of reliable, public information to address it effectively. This content is highly relevant to open data because it exposes a significant gap in the publication of accurate, granular statistics regarding cybercrime. Without open, accessible data on the real volume and types of digital offenses, society cannot accurately assess risks, governments cannot formulate effective policies, and citizens remain uninformed about the true state of their digital security. Pushing for transparent data would empower better decision-making and accountability.
