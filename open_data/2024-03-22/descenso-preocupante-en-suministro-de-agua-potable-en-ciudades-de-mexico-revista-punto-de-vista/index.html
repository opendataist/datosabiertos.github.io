Descenso preocupante en suministro de agua potable en ciudades de México | Revista Punto de Vista

The article highlights a critical decline in the continuous supply of drinking water and in satisfaction with public services in Mexico’s urban areas. This trend underscores significant regional disparities and growing public distrust in water quality, exacerbated by perceived waste and infrastructure failures. These findings are vital for open data initiatives, as they demonstrate how transparent government statistics can expose systemic inefficiencies and regional inequalities. By making such detailed survey data accessible, stakeholders can better monitor service delivery trends and hold institutions accountable for the provision of basic utilities. Ultimately, this data reveals a broader crisis in urban governance, linking service failures directly to citizen dissatisfaction. It emphasizes the necessity of rigorous, open monitoring of public resources to address the widening gap between population needs and actual service outcomes in key states.
