IPPR - New figures show child poverty targets at-risk of being missed | Think Tanks

Scotland’s latest poverty data reveals a troubling upward trend, indicating the government is on track to miss its legally binding interim targets for reducing child poverty. This statistical reality highlights a critical disconnect between political rhetoric and the lived experiences of hundreds of thousands of children, signaling that current policy interventions are insufficient to reverse the rising tide of disadvantage. The analysis suggests that while the Scottish Child Payment offers some relief, it cannot single-handedly solve the crisis. Meaningful progress requires a broader, more ambitious strategy that simultaneously addresses structural barriers in childcare, housing, and employment markets. Relying on a single fiscal lever ignores the complex, multidimensional nature of poverty and risks perpetuating systemic inequality if not complemented by significant, coordinated investments across these key sectors. This situation underscores the vital role of open data in holding public institutions accountable. By making poverty statistics transparent and accessible, organizations can rigorously evaluate policy effectiveness against statutory goals. Open data empowers citizens and researchers to identify gaps between official targets and actual outcomes, fostering a data-driven dialogue that is essential for demanding the evidence-based reforms necessary to achieve genuine social equity.
