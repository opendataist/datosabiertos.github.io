Frutas y verduras: con una brecha de más del 300%, cuáles son las zonas más baratas del Gran Mendoza

The article highlights significant price disparities for fruits and vegetables in Mendoza, driven by logistical costs and subjective valuation within a free market. Despite uniform inflation rates reported by official statistics, consumers face massive variations depending on the purchase location, with differences exceeding three hundred percent between neighborhood markets and city centers or supermarkets. This phenomenon illustrates how supply chain efficiency and local operational expenses directly impact final consumer prices, rather than just broad macroeconomic indicators. This situation is particularly relevant to open data initiatives, as it underscores the critical need for granular, accessible, and standardized price transparency tools. When official aggregates mask extreme local volatility, citizens lack the objective information required to navigate the market efficiently. Open data solutions, such as real-time price tracking platforms or public repositories of consumer goods costs, can democratize access to this information, empowering individuals to identify fair pricing and hold vendors accountable for unreasonable markups. Ultimately, the article suggests that in the absence of transparent market data, consumers must rely on manual effort to mitigate costs. The recommendation to "walk and compare" reflects a failure of systemic data infrastructure to inform purchasing decisions. Bridging this gap through open data not only supports economic equity but also enhances market efficiency by reducing information asymmetry, allowing consumers to make informed choices based on factual price comparisons rather than uncertainty.
