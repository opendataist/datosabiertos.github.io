Canada, Australia, Belgium, pledge to support NEITI on open data policy

Diplomatic missions from Belgium, Australia, and Canada have formally pledged support for Nigeria’s Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative, specifically backing its new Open Data Policy. This commitment underscores a growing international consensus on the necessity of transparent resource management, signaling that global partners are willing to provide essential technical assistance and skills development to bolster local governance frameworks. The newly established Data Center serves as a comprehensive repository for extractive sector information, designed to facilitate easy public access for civil society, media, and government bodies. By aggregating decades of industry reports, the initiative aligns with global EITI standards, moving Nigeria toward systematic disclosure. This structure ensures that evidence-based decision-making is supported by robust data analysis, thereby enhancing accountability within the oil, gas, and mineral industries. This development is crucial for open data advocates as it demonstrates how centralized, accessible information infrastructure can combat corruption and sustain public knowledge. The collaboration highlights the critical role of partnerships between developing nations and developed countries in tackling complex challenges like the transition to renewable energy. Ultimately, it reinforces the principle that open data is not just a technical tool, but a foundational element for democratic oversight and sustainable economic reform in resource-rich nations.
