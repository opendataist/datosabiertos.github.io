El paradigma que supone la Inteligencia Artificial para los artistas y derechos de autor

The rise of artificial intelligence has sparked intense legal and ethical conflicts over copyright, as companies face lawsuits for training models on unauthorized creative works. This clash highlights a critical gap in current intellectual property frameworks, which often fail to distinguish between AI as a tool and the humans or entities responsible for feeding it data. The central tension lies in determining liability when algorithms generate derivatives of protected content without explicit consent, challenging traditional notions of authorship and ownership. The core issue extends beyond litigation to the fundamental structure of open data and knowledge sharing. If training data is strictly gated by copyright restrictions, it creates barriers to innovation and the democratization of technology. Conversely, unrestricted access risks undermining the livelihoods of creators. This dilemma underscores the urgent need for new regulatory approaches that balance the protection of original artists with the open exchange of information necessary to advance AI capabilities and ensure equitable technological evolution. This scenario is vital to open data discussions because it questions the sustainability of using openly available or scraped data for commercial AI development. It emphasizes that true progress requires not just technological adoption, but ethical frameworks that recognize human creativity. Moving forward, the open data community must advocate for models where data usage respects provenance, ensuring that the democratization of AI tools does not come at the expense of exploiting unprotected creative commons or individual intellectual rights.
