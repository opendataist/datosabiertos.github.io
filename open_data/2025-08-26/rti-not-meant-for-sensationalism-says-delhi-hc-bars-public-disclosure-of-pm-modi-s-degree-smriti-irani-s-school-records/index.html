RTI not meant for sensationalism, says Delhi HC; bars public disclosure of PM Modi's degree, Smriti Irani's school records

RTI not meant for sensationalism, says Delhi HC; bars public disclosure of PM Modi's degree, Smriti Irani's school records

The Delhi High Court ruled that academic records, including degrees and marksheets, constitute protected personal information under the Right to Information Act. The judgment establishes that holding public office does not automatically strip individuals of privacy rights regarding their educational history, rejecting claims that such data should be freely accessible merely due to its age or the holder’s status. This decision highlights a critical tension in open data frameworks between transparency and privacy. By emphasizing the fiduciary relationship between educational institutions and students, the court reinforces that data custodians must safeguard sensitive information against indiscriminate demands. It signals a stricter interpretation where public interest must clearly outweigh individual privacy concerns before personal records are disclosed. This ruling is highly relevant to open data governance as it sets a precedent for balancing disclosure mandates with data protection laws. It challenges the assumption that all government-linked information is public, urging organizations to rigorously assess the sensitivity of personal data. Ultimately, it encourages a more nuanced approach to open data, ensuring that transparency does not come at the cost of fundamental privacy rights.
