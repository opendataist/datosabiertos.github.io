Land Development Agency seeks to block FOI request at High Court

Land Development Agency seeks to block FOI request at High Court

The Land Development Agency’s attempt to withhold sensitive risk data via legal action highlights the ongoing tension between government transparency and commercial confidentiality. By seeking to block the release of its corporate risk register, the agency argues that disclosing internal strategies would disadvantage its negotiations with private developers, potentially increasing costs for affordable housing projects. This case underscores the difficulty public bodies face in balancing their duty to provide open information with claims that such disclosure could harm their financial interests or market position. The Information Commissioner’s initial rejection of these claims emphasizes that transparency does not necessarily equate to commercial harm. The ruling suggested that the agency’s fears were exaggerated, as the core risks were already visible, implying that withholding additional details served more to obscure accountability than to protect state funds. This decision reinforces the principle that FOI laws should prioritize public access to information, limiting the ability of semi-state bodies to use vague commercial sensitivities as a blanket excuse for secrecy. This dispute is highly relevant to open data advocates because it tests the boundaries of what constitutes genuinely sensitive information versus routine operational data. If successful, the LDA’s precedent could empower other public entities to obscure critical financial and strategic details, undermining efforts to monitor how public funds are managed. Ultimately, the outcome will determine whether open data principles can withstand arguments that transparency impedes effective market competition for social goods.
