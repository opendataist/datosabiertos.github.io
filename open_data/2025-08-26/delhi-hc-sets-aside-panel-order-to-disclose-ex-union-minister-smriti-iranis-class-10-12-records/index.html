Delhi HC sets aside panel order to disclose ex-Union minister Smriti Irani’s Class 10, 12 records

Delhi HC sets aside panel order to disclose ex-Union minister Smriti Irani’s Class 10, 12 records

The Delhi High Court recently overturned an order requiring the disclosure of academic records for prominent political figures, emphasizing that privacy rights outweigh general public curiosity. The court ruled that educational qualifications do not constitute a statutory requirement for holding public office. Consequently, revealing such personal data without a direct link to official duties infringes upon the fundamental right to privacy established by the Supreme Court. This decision reinforces the principle that the Right to Information Act is intended to ensure transparency in government functioning, not to satisfy sensationalism or idle curiosity. By protecting personal data unconnected to public responsibilities, the judiciary prevents the creation of a precedent where privacy is eroded merely because an individual holds a public position. This balance safeguards individuals from unwarranted intrusion while maintaining the Act’s core purpose of promoting accountability in administrative processes. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates as it clarifies the boundaries between public interest and personal privacy in data disclosure. It serves as a critical legal reference for determining when personal information should remain protected versus when it must be made accessible to the public. Understanding these distinctions helps organizations and policymakers navigate data sharing protocols, ensuring that openness initiatives respect constitutional privacy rights while still fostering governmental transparency and accountability.
