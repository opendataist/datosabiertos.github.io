Modi degree case: Delhi HC sets aside panel order to disclose PM’s BA certificate

Modi degree case: Delhi HC sets aside panel order to disclose PM’s BA certificate

The Delhi High Court overturned a directive forcing Delhi University to reveal Prime Minister Modi’s academic records, accepting the institution’s argument that such data falls under fiduciary trust rather than public disclosure. This ruling significantly impacts transparency efforts by establishing a precedent that educational records held by public universities can be classified as personal information, thereby restricting citizen access to these details via Right to Information requests. This decision highlights the critical tension between institutional privacy claims and the public’s right to scrutinize the credentials of elected officials. It underscores the limitations of current open data frameworks when legal interpretations prioritize confidentiality over accountability, potentially shielding high-profile figures from necessary public examination of their backgrounds. The case is highly relevant to open_data advocates because it demonstrates how legal definitions can obstruct data accessibility. It serves as a cautionary tale for maintaining robust, unambiguous open standards that prevent institutions from easily circumventing transparency obligations through broad interpretations of privacy or fiduciary duties, ensuring that public data remains truly accessible for democratic oversight.
