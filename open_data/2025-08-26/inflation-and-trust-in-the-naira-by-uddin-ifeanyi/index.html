Inflation and trust in the naira, By Uddin Ifeanyi

Inflation and trust in the naira, By Uddin Ifeanyi

The article highlights a critical dilemma for Nigeria’s Central Bank, where the persistence of negative real interest rates severely undermines monetary policy effectiveness. By keeping nominal rates below inflation, the state effectively taxes domestic savers while transferring wealth to borrowers and the government. This environment encourages capital flight and speculative investment in volatile assets like cryptocurrencies, as citizens seek to preserve purchasing power, leading to long-term economic instability and the crowding out of productive private sector credit. Relevance to open_data lies in the necessity of transparent, accessible inflation metrics to understand these hidden economic transfers. When data such as the divergence between core inflation and treasury bill rates is readily available, citizens and analysts can accurately assess the erosion of wealth and the true cost of government borrowing. This transparency is essential for holding institutions accountable and fostering public trust, which is foundational for the credibility of any open government initiative aimed at improving macroeconomic governance. The core conclusion is that without anchoring inflation expectations, the central bank risks triggering an inflationary spiral that destroys confidence in the national currency. The choice remains between maintaining accommodative policies that fuel asset bubbles and capital flight, or raising rates to stabilize prices, despite the risk of fiscal stress. Ultimately, sustainable economic recovery requires prioritizing credible, transparent data-driven policies over short-term political gains to restore trust in national financial institutions.
