Canada, Australia, Belgium, pledge to support NEITI on open data policy

Canada, Australia, Belgium, pledge to support NEITI on open data policy

The Embassies of Belgium, Australia, and Canada have formally pledged support for Nigeria’s Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative open data policy. This commitment, demonstrated during a visit to the new NEITI Data Center, signals strong diplomatic backing for transparent resource governance. By offering skills development and technical assistance, these nations aim to reinforce the integrity of Nigeria’s extractive sector and promote accountability in oil, gas, and mineral management. The newly established Data Center serves as a comprehensive repository for industry data, ensuring easy public access for civil society, media, and government agencies. This infrastructure aligns with global EITI standards, transforming raw information into actionable insights for evidence-based decision-making. By centralizing aggregated and disaggregated data, the initiative fosters a robust public understanding of resource revenues, which is crucial for sustaining democratic oversight and reducing corruption risks. This development is significant for open data advocates as it illustrates the practical application of transparency frameworks in resource-rich developing nations. It highlights how international partnerships can strengthen local capacities to manage data effectively amid the global energy transition. Such collaboration ensures that critical information remains accessible, empowering stakeholders to hold governments and corporations accountable while supporting sustainable economic reforms in the extractive industries.
