Cegaip, principal violador de la Ley de Transparencia

Cegaip, principal violador de la Ley de Transparencia

The article criticizes the Commission for Guaranteeing Access to Public Information (CEGAIP) for systematically rejecting citizen complaints concerning information withheld by local authorities. This negligence occurs just before the agency is scheduled to be dissolved and replaced by an entity under the control of the State Comptroller, raising concerns that outgoing officials are evading accountability by dismissing grievances rather than enforcing transparency laws. The core implication is that institutional opacity has reached critical levels, with multiple municipalities and state departments refusing to publish basic financial data. By dismissing valid requests as unfounded, the commission effectively shields non-compliant entities, demonstrating how weak oversight mechanisms allow public information rights to be eroded through administrative inaction and deliberate obstruction. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the fragility of data availability when legal guardians lack political independence or moral commitment. True open data requires not just publication platforms, but robust, impartial institutions that enforce compliance. Without such safeguards, open data initiatives remain vulnerable to political manipulation and administrative neglect, undermining public trust and the fundamental right to information.
