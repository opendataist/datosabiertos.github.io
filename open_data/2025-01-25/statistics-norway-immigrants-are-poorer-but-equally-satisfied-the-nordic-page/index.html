Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway experience significantly poorer living conditions, including lower incomes, worse employment prospects, and inferior housing compared to the native population. Despite these material disadvantages, they report equal satisfaction with life in Norway, highlighting a complex resilience that contradicts assumptions linking objective hardship directly to subjective well-being. The data reveals a troubling disparity in mental health, with immigrants facing twice the rate of mental problems and loneliness as the general population. Certain groups, particularly from the Middle East, show especially high levels of distress, underscoring the urgent need for targeted mental health support systems that address the unique stressors faced by diverse immigrant communities. However, this demographic exhibits remarkably high trust in state institutions, such as the judiciary and police, exceeding native confidence levels. This paradox is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistics can reveal nuanced social dynamics, challenge simplistic narratives about integration, and inform evidence-based policies that respect both societal challenges and institutional trust.
