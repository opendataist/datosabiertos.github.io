Revealed: what the London Overground lines could have been called

This article highlights a failed 2015 initiative by former Mayor Boris Johnson to rebrand London’s Overground lines with distinctive names like "Suffragette" and "Windrush." The project involved significant spending on customer research before being abandoned in favor of traditional geographic identifiers. This historical context illustrates the complex decision-making processes behind public infrastructure branding, revealing how political visions can clash with practical implementation. The relevance to open data lies in the transparency achieved through Freedom of Information requests. These documents expose the specific costs, research methodologies, and proposed alternatives that were never publicly debated due to the project's cancellation. Such records demonstrate the value of accessible government data in understanding past administrative choices and evaluating the efficiency of public spending on urban rebranding efforts. Ultimately, this case serves as a cautionary tale regarding the allocation of public funds for symbolic changes. It underscores the importance of scrutinizing taxpayer money spent on controversial or abandoned projects. By making these records available, open data enables citizens to hold authorities accountable, fostering greater trust in municipal governance and encouraging more thoughtful consideration of future infrastructure communications.
