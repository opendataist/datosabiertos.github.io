Se frenan motores de la economía: inversión se estanca y consumo retrocede

Mexican economic indicators revealed a concerning trend in late 2022, characterized by stagnant physical investment and declining private consumption driven by high inflation. Although gross fixed investment remained slightly above pre-pandemic levels, it continues to lag significantly behind its peak from 2018, highlighting structural vulnerabilities within the construction and machinery sectors despite minor monthly fluctuations. Simultaneously, the private consumption index broke a four-month growth streak, dipping in November due to persistent inflationary pressures. While total consumption remains elevated compared to early 2020 figures, the notable drop in imported goods consumption suggests diminishing household purchasing power, reflecting broader macroeconomic instability that constrains internal demand. This article is relevant to open data as it underscores the critical importance of accessing granular, real-time statistical sources like those from the National Institute of Statistics and Geography. Openly available, high-quality datasets empower analysts, researchers, and policymakers to move beyond headline figures, enabling a deeper understanding of specific sectoral dynamics and informing more precise economic interventions and academic research.
