IA amenaza derechos de autor de artistas

IA amenaza derechos de autor de artistas

The article highlights a significant convergence of voices from the creative industries, where thousands of artists and authors warn that using their copyrighted works to train generative AI without permission constitutes an unfair threat to their livelihoods. This collective resistance underscores a critical ethical boundary in the development of artificial intelligence, emphasizing that artistic creation should not be treated as free raw material for commercial algorithms. This conflict is particularly relevant to open data because it challenges the current norm of scraping publicly available or unlicensed information for model training. It forces a re-evaluation of data provenance and ownership, arguing that transparency and consent are essential components of responsible data usage. The debate extends beyond copyright law to the fundamental principles of ethical data collection, questioning whether access to data should imply the right to use it without compensation or attribution. Furthermore, the narrative reveals a power imbalance where tech giants, backed by massive capital, often overshadow individual creators in policy discussions. The urgency lies in establishing frameworks that ensure data subjects are compensated, thereby promoting a sustainable ecosystem. This is vital for the open data community, as it advocates for models that respect intellectual property while fostering innovation, ensuring that the benefits of AI do not come at the expense of human creativity and fair economic practices.
