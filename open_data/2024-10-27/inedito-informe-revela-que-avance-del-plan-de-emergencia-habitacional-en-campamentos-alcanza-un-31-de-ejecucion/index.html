Inédito informe revela que avance del Plan de Emergencia Habitacional en campamentos alcanza un 31% de ejecución

Inédito informe revela que avance del Plan de Emergencia Habitacional en campamentos alcanza un 31% de ejecución

The report reveals that the programs of the Emergency Housing Plan targeting camps have achieved limited and largely incomplete progress. Although interventions have been carried out in a fraction of the planned settlements, most projects are in the early stages of diagnosis or design, with only a minimal percentage of solutions truly finalized. This means that the vast majority of affected families continue to wait for state assistance without receiving tangible improvements in their living conditions. The gap between the goal and reality is concerning, especially in light of the growing trend of new informal settlements and the proposal to cut funding for these programs. The organization highlights that precarious housing remains a structural problem neglected by public policies, as the allocated investment is insufficient to address the magnitude of the housing deficit. There is an urgent call for the government to accelerate not only permanent construction but also to provide temporary solutions that alleviate the immediate emergency faced by families awaiting assistance. This news is relevant to the open data sector because it demonstrates the power of transparency in making social realities visible. Through requests under the Transparency Law, citizens were able to access state information that was previously non-public, allowing them to verify the actual status of projects and question the effectiveness of housing policy. This case exemplifies how access to official data is essential for citizen oversight and the demand for accountability in public management.
