Failure to Notify penalties for CGT have doubled in past two years - IFA Magazine

Failure to Notify penalties for CGT have doubled in past two years - IFA Magazine

HM Revenue & Customs has significantly increased the issuance of Failure to Notify penalties for capital gains tax, driven by substantial reductions in the annual exemption threshold. This policy shift forces more individuals into tax liability scope, resulting in a doubling of penalties as authorities intensify efforts to recover unpaid revenue. The financial burden on taxpayers has escalated, reflecting a broader government strategy to boost state coffers by targeting previously overlooked investment gains and income sources. Critics argue that these official figures likely represent only a fraction of the actual non-compliance, suggesting systemic gaps in HMRC’s data tracking and investigative capabilities. While the administration aims to improve transparency and recovery, evidence points to significant challenges in identifying high-value tax evaders and effectively prosecuting complex cases. The reliance on incomplete data systems raises questions about the true extent of the tax gap and the efficiency of current compliance measures, highlighting the disparity between reported penalties and actual unreported activity. This development is crucial for open data advocates, as it underscores the necessity for comprehensive, accessible, and accurate government datasets to hold tax authorities accountable. Without full transparency into penalty issuance and enforcement outcomes, it remains difficult to assess the true impact of tax policy changes on both revenue generation and public trust. Open data initiatives can help reveal these hidden disparities, ensuring that claims about tax collection efficiency are grounded in complete evidence rather than fragmented statistics.
