La vivienda se encarece en el segundo trimestre un 12,7%, la mayor subida de precio desde 2007

La vivienda se encarece en el segundo trimestre un 12,7%, la mayor subida de precio desde 2007

Spanish housing prices have surged to their highest levels since 2007, driven by a critical imbalance between robust, growing demand and severely limited supply. This shortage creates intense market pressure, preventing price stabilization and ensuring that affordability remains a significant challenge for prospective buyers in the near future. Experts emphasize that despite the acceleration in price increases, the current market dynamics do not constitute a speculative bubble. Financial conditions remain prudent and compliant with strict European solvency criteria, suggesting that the upward trend is fundamentally structural rather than risky. The sustained increase is largely attributed to consecutive years of strong sales activity that have not allowed inventory to recover adequately. This situation highlights the urgent need for transparent, accessible data to monitor housing market health and inform policy interventions. Open data is essential for tracking these supply-demand gaps and evaluating the effectiveness of housing strategies. Without granular, real-time information, stakeholders cannot accurately assess market pressures or design solutions to improve availability and accessibility for the general public.
