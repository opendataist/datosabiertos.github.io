Agricultura en Bolivia: ¿Sembramos futuro o lo perdemos en silencio?

Agricultura en Bolivia: ¿Sembramos futuro o lo perdemos en silencio?

The article argues that Bolivia’s industrial agriculture model, while generating significant export revenue, causes severe environmental and social damage. It highlights how illegal burning and monocultures are destroying forests and harming community health through pesticide exposure. The core conclusion is that the true cost of this system is hidden, as economic balances fail to account for the destruction of habitats, water cycles, and public health crises caused by agrochemicals. The current approach prioritizes short-term gains by supporting industrial expansion through subsidies and market access, while marginalizing small producers and traditional farming practices. This shift not only displaces communities but also erodes cultural heritage and ancestral knowledge. The text emphasizes that the displacement of local families represents a profound human and cultural loss, weakening the social fabric in favor of an extractive model that depletes natural resources. This narrative is crucial for open data because it exposes the limitations of standard economic metrics and the lack of comprehensive, accessible data on externalities like health impacts and ecological degradation. To foster transparent policy debates, there is a need for open datasets that reveal these hidden costs. By making such information available, society can move beyond short-term productivity metrics to evaluate agricultural models based on long-term sustainability, public health, and social equity, enabling more informed decisions about the country’s future.
