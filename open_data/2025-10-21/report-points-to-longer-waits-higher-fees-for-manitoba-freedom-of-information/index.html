Report points to longer waits, higher fees for Manitoba freedom of information

Report points to longer waits, higher fees for Manitoba freedom of information

Manitoba’s freedom of information system is facing a dual crisis of declining efficiency and rising costs, threatening the core principle of public transparency. The government’s on-time completion rate has dropped significantly, with only a majority of requests fulfilled within the legal deadline. This delay, attributed to recent administrative centralization, suggests that efforts to standardize processes have inadvertently created bottlenecks, reducing the state’s ability to provide timely access to public records. Concurrently, the financial barrier to accessing government information has surged dramatically, with fee collections tripling year-over-year. Experts warn that these higher costs disproportionately affect individual citizens who lack discretionary income, potentially chilling civic engagement and limiting the diversity of those able to exercise their right to know. While the government argues this reflects a more consistent application of existing rules, the steep fees risk turning public transparency into a luxury service reserved for well-resourced organizations. This trend is highly relevant to the open data movement because it highlights the tension between administrative efficiency and equitable access. True open data requires not just publication, but low-friction access mechanisms. If governments impose significant financial and temporal hurdles on information requests, they undermine the democratic purpose of transparency. This case serves as a critical reminder that open data initiatives must prioritize user accessibility and affordability to ensure that information remains a public good rather than a commodified resource.
