French Evangelical leaders assert that the traditional biblical family remains the essential foundation for social stability and the protection of vulnerable individuals. This stance emerges against a backdrop of shifting demographics in France, where marriage rates have declined significantly while civil partnerships and divorce rates have risen. Evangelicals argue that maintaining distinct marital structures is crucial for learning social relations and safeguarding children, even as societal norms increasingly diversify relationship models. The relevance of this discourse is heightened by recent trauma within the French religious landscape, particularly the widespread sexual abuse scandal exposed in the Catholic Church. In response, Evangelical bodies have committed to transparency and active combat against abuse, establishing dedicated services to support victims. This contrast underscores a broader religious effort to rebuild trust and demonstrate ethical responsibility, emphasizing that faith communities must actively protect the vulnerable rather than prioritizing institutional self-preservation. This article highlights the evolving role of religious institutions in shaping public policy and social values. For open data, it illustrates the importance of tracking diverse metrics beyond simple marriage statistics, such as partnership types, abuse reports, and social support initiatives. Understanding these nuanced trends helps researchers and policymakers analyze how different societal groups respond to demographic changes, providing a richer context for evaluating social welfare and community resilience in an increasingly secular and varied society.
