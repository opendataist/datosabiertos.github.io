¿Alguien se metió en mi celular? - Diario Río Negro

The unsolicited integration of Meta AI into messaging apps raises a fundamental concern about privacy, as this tool operates in a directive manner, guiding users toward interaction and a continuous flow of personal data. Although presented as a useful assistant, its design aims to ensure that consumers proactively engage in search and communication activities, thereby facilitating the mass collection of information without clear or explicit consent. The primary interest of major technology companies lies in the impending scarcity of publicly available data for training artificial intelligence models. By accessing user information through these features, companies seek to “feed” their algorithms with unique content—such as posts, photos, and private conversations—that no longer benefit from traditional privacy exemptions. This effectively turns users into a direct source for model training, prioritizing technological development over individual security and privacy. This article is crucial for the open data community because it illustrates the risks of opacity in data management and the lack of transparency in the use of emerging technologies. The absence of effective mechanisms to opt out of or remove these services in many regions, coupled with uncertainty about how information is processed, underscores the urgent need for robust regulatory frameworks. This case exemplifies the tension between open innovation and the protection of digital rights, demanding greater corporate accountability and active oversight by data protection authorities.
