Woman denied govt job by mistake in score calculation during evaluation

A calculation error by university staff during the recruitment of assistant professors at Keladi Shivappa Nayaka University of Agricultural and Horticultural Sciences deprived a highly qualified candidate of a reserved position. Dr. Shruthi, whose post-graduation marks were significantly under-assessed due to administrative negligence, would have ranked first had her score been calculated correctly. This case highlights how internal procedural failures can directly impact equitable access to academic careers, particularly for candidates from marginalized communities. The incident underscores the critical need for transparent and accurate data processing in public sector recruitment. When institutions fail to verify numerical data rigorously, they risk excluding top talent based on artificial deficits rather than merit. For the broader open data community, this serves as a stark reminder that accessibility alone is insufficient if the underlying datasets used for decision-making are flawed. Trust in public systems relies heavily on the integrity and verifiability of the information guiding selection processes. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the tangible human cost of data opacity and error. It emphasizes the importance of auditing official records and advocating for the release of detailed scoring criteria. By exposing how a simple mathematical mistake altered a career trajectory, it reinforces the argument that governments and universities must publish granular, checkable data. This enables external scrutiny, ensures accountability, and prevents administrative oversights from undermining fair competition and social justice initiatives.
