Court orders Lai Mohammed to disclose FG’s agreement with X, formerly Twitter

A Nigerian court has ruled that the agreement between the Federal Government and X, formerly Twitter, must be disclosed to civil society groups. This decision prioritizes transparency and public interest over claims of national security or business confidentiality. The judgment validates the right of citizens to access government records regarding digital platform regulations, reinforcing accountability in how state data interactions are managed. This case highlights the critical role of open data initiatives in holding governments accountable for their digital policies. By compelling the release of such agreements, the ruling supports the principle that public interest outweighs third-party commercial interests or vague security concerns. It establishes a legal precedent where government-digital platform contracts are subject to public scrutiny, ensuring that citizen rights online are protected through accessible information rather than opaque administrative processes. The relevance to open data lies in its affirmation that digital governance contracts are public records. When governments engage with private technology firms, the resulting agreements impact societal norms and individual freedoms, making them essential for public understanding. This case demonstrates that open data frameworks can effectively challenge secrecy, empowering organizations to verify compliance with human rights standards. Ultimately, it reinforces that transparency in digital agreements is a fundamental component of democratic oversight and information freedom.
