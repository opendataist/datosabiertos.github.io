Questions persist about falsification of 100,000 mail-in ballots in Michigan as Gov. Whitmer changes election laws to make fraud harder to expose – NaturalNews.com

Questions persist about falsification of 100,000 mail-in ballots in Michigan as Gov. Whitmer changes election laws to make fraud harder to expose – NaturalNews.com

Recent FOIA documents suggest that federal agencies identified serious election integrity issues in Michigan’s 2020 election, including allegations of ballot tampering and procedural irregularities, yet largely deferred investigation to state authorities. Despite evidence of potential fraud and specific claims of falsified ballot packaging, the complaint was handled internally by local officials, raising questions about the effectiveness of oversight mechanisms when conflicts of interest or inter-agency coordination issues arise. In response to these concerns, Governor Whitmer recently enacted legislation that significantly alters how election discrepancies are handled. The new laws remove investigative powers from local boards of canvassers, requiring them to refer fraud allegations to prosecutors instead of conducting recounts. Furthermore, the statutes raise financial barriers for filing recount petitions and limit the circumstances under which recounts are permitted, ostensibly to streamline processes but critics argue to hinder transparency. This article is critically relevant to the open data community because it highlights the intersection of legal transparency, public records access, and electoral trust. It underscores the importance of FOIA requests in uncovering hidden administrative practices and serves as a stark warning about legislative changes that may restrict future access to election data. The reduction in investigative mandates and the tightening of legal pathways for challenges demonstrate how policy shifts can directly impact the ability of citizens and journalists to verify data integrity, emphasizing the need for robust, accessible datasets to safeguard democratic processes.
