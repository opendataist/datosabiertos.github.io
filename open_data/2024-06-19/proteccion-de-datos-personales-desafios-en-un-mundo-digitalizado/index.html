Protección de datos personales: desafíos en un mundo digitalizado

The article highlights the critical intersection between humanitarian crises and data privacy, emphasizing that the protection of personal information is vital for safeguarding the dignity and security of refugees and victims of conflict-related violence. With millions of displaced individuals facing heightened vulnerabilities, including digital exploitation and human trafficking, ensuring the confidentiality of their sensitive data becomes a fundamental component of international human rights and humanitarian law. The narrative underscores the necessity of preventing revictimization and discrimination through rigorous data protection standards. By treating personal data as a human right rather than merely a technical asset, authorities can empower vulnerable populations in the digital sphere. This approach not only mitigates specific risks associated with information leakage but also supports the social reintegration and dignity of those forced to flee their homes due to persecution or armed conflict. This topic is highly relevant to open data because it defines the necessary boundaries between transparency and privacy in humanitarian contexts. It illustrates how open data initiatives must incorporate strict ethical guidelines and technical safeguards to avoid harming beneficiaries. The discussion promotes a framework where data sharing for development and crisis management coexists with robust privacy protections, ensuring that openness does not compromise the safety of the most at-risk populations.
