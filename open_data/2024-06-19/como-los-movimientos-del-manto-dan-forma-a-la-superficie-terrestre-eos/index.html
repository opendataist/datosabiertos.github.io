Cómo los movimientos del manto dan forma a la superficie terrestre - Eos

This study highlights how the Earth's mantle, rather than just tectonic plate boundaries, significantly shapes surface topography through residual features. Researchers developed extensive datasets combining global crustal thickness measurements with laboratory-derived seismic velocity estimates based on temperature and density. By distinguishing crustal influences from mantle-driven variations, they identified how chemical and thermal anomalies in the mantle create vast swellings and basins deep within plates, independent of edge tectonics. These mantle-induced topographical changes, reaching up to two kilometers in elevation, directly influence long-term geological processes like erosion and sedimentation patterns. The findings suggest that hot mantle regions correlate with high swells, while cooler areas correspond to deep basins, potentially explaining magmatic activity far from plate boundaries. This challenges previous models that focused primarily on plate interactions, offering a more comprehensive view of how internal Earth dynamics sculpt the landscape over millions of years. This research is crucial for open data because it demonstrates the value of integrating diverse, large-scale datasets to solve complex geophysical problems. By making such comprehensive measurements and analytical methods available, scientists can better model Earth's interior dynamics and surface evolution. Open access to these foundational data empowers broader scientific inquiry, fostering reproducibility and encouraging interdisciplinary collaboration to understand the subtle, yet powerful, forces shaping our planet beyond visible plate boundaries.
