La inflación de la eurozona repunta al 2,6% en mayo y la tasa subyacente sube al 2,9%

Eurozone inflation rose in May, driven by food and energy costs, reaching its highest level since February. This rebound signals a potential shift in price dynamics after months of decline, complicating the path toward the European Central Bank’s stability targets. The rise in underlying inflation suggests persistent pressures beyond volatile sectors, as services also hit recent highs. These trends indicate that disinflation is not uniform, requiring careful monitoring of domestic demand and wage growth across member states. This data is crucial for open data initiatives, highlighting the need for transparent, timely economic indicators. Accessible statistical records allow researchers and policymakers to analyze inflation drivers accurately, fostering better-informed decisions regarding monetary policy and economic stability within the European Union.
