Uzbekistan’s Anti-Corruption Agency visited Georgia to learn from its successful Open Government Partnership membership, highlighting the value of international knowledge exchange in strengthening governance frameworks. By studying Georgia’s reforms, Uzbek officials gained insights into effective strategies for enhancing transparency and accountability, crucial steps for robust open data ecosystems. The collaboration underscores how sharing best practices in digital governance and public procurement can significantly reduce corruption risks. This peer-to-peer learning encourages governments to adopt more open, data-driven approaches to policy-making, ensuring that public information is accessible and usable by citizens. This initiative is highly relevant to open data as it promotes the institutional commitment to transparency and citizen engagement. By integrating open government principles, Uzbekistan aims to improve how public data is managed, fostering a culture where open data serves as a fundamental tool for anti-corruption efforts and democratic accountability.
