RAW Exempted Under RTI Act Unless Information Relates To Human Rights Or Corruption Related Issues: Delhi High Court

The Delhi High Court reaffirmed that the Research and Analysis Wing is statutorily exempt from disclosure under the Right to Information Act. This ruling clarifies that such intelligence agencies generally withhold information unless the request specifically involves human rights violations or corruption. Consequently, general administrative details regarding officials are not subject to public scrutiny under current legal frameworks. This precedent significantly impacts the accessibility of information concerning national security entities. It establishes a high threshold for transparency, limiting public oversight to serious ethical or legal breaches rather than routine administrative conduct. For citizens and activists, this means that obtaining details about the internal operations or personnel of exempted bodies remains exceptionally difficult without substantiating claims of misconduct. This case is relevant to open data because it defines the boundaries of what constitutes "open" information in critical governance sectors. It highlights how legal exemptions can effectively restrict data availability, creating gaps in public knowledge about state institutions. Understanding these limitations is crucial for advocates who seek to expand transparency and hold powerful agencies accountable through information rights.
