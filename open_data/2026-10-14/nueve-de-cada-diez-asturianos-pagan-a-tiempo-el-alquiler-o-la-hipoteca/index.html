Nueve de cada diez asturianos pagan a tiempo el alquiler o la hipoteca

Nueve de cada diez asturianos pagan a tiempo el alquiler o la hipoteca

Most of Asturias’ population remains able to meet their day-to-day financial obligations, such as mortgages and utility bills, although the rate of payment delays has risen to concerning levels similar to those recorded after the pandemic. This uptick signals growing economic pressure affecting a significant portion of the community, even though the Principality continues to show better indicators than other regions facing more severe structural difficulties. Moreover, vulnerability to unforeseen events has created a scenario of financial fragility for nearly one-third of households, which lack the resources to cover extraordinary expenses without jeopardizing their stability. This figure reflects a progressive decline in households’ savings capacity and economic resilience, showing that although regular payments are generally met, the margin for maneuver in the face of unexpected crises has notably shrunk in recent years. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency in public statistics, such as those from Eurostat, enables comparison of economic health across territories and detection of hidden trends. By combining data on solvency and resilience, an integrated view is obtained that facilitates informed decision-making by governments and citizens, highlighting the importance of opening and linking these datasets to analyze social well-being with greater precision and depth.
