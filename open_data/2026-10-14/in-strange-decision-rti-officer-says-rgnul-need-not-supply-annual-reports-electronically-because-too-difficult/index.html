In strange decision, RTI officer says RGNUL need not supply annual reports electronically because too difficult

In strange decision, RTI officer says RGNUL need not supply annual reports electronically because too difficult

RGNUL Patiala was criticized for refusing to digitize annual reports, citing resource constraints despite other national law universities successfully publishing theirs online. The institution argued that maintaining electronic records was not mandatory due to financial limitations, a stance later adjusted to charge applicants for mailing physical copies. This dispute highlights a significant gap in open_data practices within legal academia. While peer institutions demonstrate that digital transparency is feasible and compliant with right to information standards, RGNUL’s reliance on physical records creates unnecessary barriers for public access to financial accountability. The case underscores the importance of proactive digital publication in the open_data movement. It reveals that administrative convenience or alleged resource issues should not impede the availability of public records, emphasizing that true transparency requires removing logistical obstacles for citizens seeking institutional information.
