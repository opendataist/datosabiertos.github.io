Edomex lidera la movilidad estudiantil interestatal: 15.8% sale de su estado para estudiar

The INEGI Intercensal Survey reveals significant educational inequality, highlighting that young people in the State of Mexico face the greatest challenges in accessing post-compulsory education. The need to migrate temporarily or permanently outside their home state to continue their studies indicates a lack of local educational infrastructure, which stands in stark contrast to states like Sinaloa, where the internal academic offerings allow the vast majority of students to remain in their place of residence. This disparity suggests that the spatial distribution of upper secondary and higher education institutions is not uniform across the national territory. For residents of the State of Mexico, mobility becomes an economic and social barrier, whereas in other regions, the proximity of these services facilitates academic development without additional relocation costs. This analysis is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how access to high-resolution official statistical data enables the identification of structural gaps in social rights. By making these geodemographic indicators publicly available, transparency and public debate are enhanced, allowing civil society and policymakers to analyze territorial inequalities and demand public policies that ensure equitable access to education.
