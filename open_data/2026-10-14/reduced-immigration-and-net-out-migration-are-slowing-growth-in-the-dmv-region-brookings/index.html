Reduced immigration and net out-migration are slowing growth in the DMV region | Brookings

Reduced immigration and net out-migration are slowing growth in the DMV region | Brookings

The second Trump administration has precipitated a significant demographic and economic slowdown in the Washington D.C. metropolitan area. Job losses have surged, particularly affecting Black workers, while population growth has decelerated due to a sharp decline in international immigration. This contraction is compounded by increased domestic out-migration from core jurisdictions, driven by federal job cuts and a broader uncertainty that has deterred talent from moving to the region. Immigration policies have had an outsized negative impact on the region, which relies heavily on foreign-born labor, especially in the construction sector. The expiration of Temporary Protected Status for El Salvador, the largest immigrant group in the area, poses a unique risk. Enforcement actions have not only reduced the immigrant workforce but also triggered broader economic disruptions, leading to job losses for both immigrant and native-born residents through decreased spending and business instability. This analysis is crucial for open data because it demonstrates how demographic tracking and labor statistics serve as early warning systems for policy impacts. The data reveals that the region’s economic health is inextricably linked to its immigrant population, highlighting the need for data-driven advocacy. By understanding these shifts, stakeholders can better predict economic vulnerabilities and advocate for inclusive growth strategies that mitigate the negative consequences of restrictive immigration and federal workforce reductions.
