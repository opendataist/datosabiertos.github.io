Trump’s Dirty Energy Double Whammy: Pay More for Fuel & Power—and Use More of It - CleanTechnica

Trump’s Dirty Energy Double Whammy: Pay More for Fuel & Power—and Use More of It - CleanTechnica

The article argues that current energy policies are severely undermining the transition to sustainable power, creating significant barriers for open data initiatives that rely on transparent, reliable, and publicly accessible energy metrics. By reversing clean energy incentives and prioritizing fossil fuels, the administration has disrupted the infrastructure needed for accurate energy tracking and reporting. This shift not only increases financial burdens on households but also obscures the true costs of energy production, making it difficult for citizens and researchers to analyze environmental and economic impacts effectively. Furthermore, the cancellation of substantial clean energy projects has eliminated vital datasets regarding renewable energy deployment, job creation, and grid modernization. The loss of planned solar, wind, and battery storage projects means fewer opportunities to study the efficacy and scalability of green technologies. This absence of high-quality, open data hampers efforts to benchmark progress against climate goals and limits the ability of independent analysts to hold policymakers accountable for energy decisions. Finally, the resulting increase in air pollution introduces severe public health costs that remain difficult to quantify without comprehensive data sharing. The rollback of clean air standards leads to higher rates of illness and premature deaths, yet the mechanisms linking these health outcomes to specific energy policies are often obscured. For open data advocates, this highlights the critical need for rigorous, accessible datasets that connect energy choices to their long-term societal and environmental consequences, ensuring that public health and ecological impacts are visible and actionable.
