Revisar el censo, y hacerlo ya

Revisar el censo, y hacerlo ya

The article questions the reliability of Bolivia’s 2024 Population and Housing Census, highlighting a significant divergence between official data and demographic projections, particularly in the city of La Paz. It argues that the lack of transparency in the methodology—such as the omission of updated cartography and verifiable evidence regarding the margin of error—has fostered institutional distrust. This situation underscores the critical need for open access to census data and processes to validate their technical integrity. The relevance of these inaccurate data extends beyond statistics, as they directly influence the distribution of public resources, tax revenue sharing, and national development planning. Without precise figures, political and budgetary decisions are based on uncertainty, with long-term economic and administrative consequences. Therefore, there is a call for an independent technical audit and the full publication of census information to enable rigorous social scrutiny. The text concludes that the current government should urgently review the results of the population census before proceeding with the Agricultural Census. Implementing a new process is justified only if the audit reveals structural flaws, thereby ensuring that the next demographic count is reliable. This stance is fundamental to the open data movement, advocating for radical transparency and public verification as pillars for building trust in state statistics and evidence-based decision-making.
