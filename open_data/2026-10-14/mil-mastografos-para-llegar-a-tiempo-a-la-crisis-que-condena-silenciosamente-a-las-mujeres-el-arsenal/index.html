Mil mastógrafos para llegar a tiempo a la crisis que condena silenciosamente a las mujeres - El Arsenal

Mil mastógrafos para llegar a tiempo a la crisis que condena silenciosamente a las mujeres - El Arsenal

The rise in breast cancer mortality in Mexico reveals a systemic crisis that goes beyond the mere shortage of medical equipment. Although the deficit of mammography machines is real, the data indicate that women with the greatest caregiving burdens, particularly those in the informal sector, face structural barriers that delay their access to healthcare. The lack of infrastructure is compounded by a lack of time and viable alternatives, creating an environment where late diagnosis becomes the norm rather than the exception. The relevance of this case for open data lies in the critical absence of a population-based national cancer registry and the lack of transparency regarding the system’s response times. Without public metrics that measure the delays between suspicion, diagnosis, and treatment, it is impossible to identify bottlenecks or demand accountability. Opacity in the management of programs prevents an assessment of whether investments in hardware actually translate into effective coverage and trained personnel to operate the equipment. The new government strategy, although ambitious, risks disconnecting the timelines for acquiring goods from actual health outcomes. Promising reductions in mortality in the same year that machines are delivered ignores the biological latency required for screening to impact survival rates. To transform this discussion, it is urgent to establish public, verifiable quarterly indicators that monitor not only the installation of equipment but also the real efficiency of the care process, ensuring that data serve to save lives rather than merely to meet political schedules.
