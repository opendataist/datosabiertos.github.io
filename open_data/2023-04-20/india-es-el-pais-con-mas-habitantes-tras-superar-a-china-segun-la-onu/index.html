India es el país con más habitantes tras superar a China según la ONU

India is projected to surpass China as the world’s most populous nation this year, marking a historic shift since the UN began tracking demographic data in 1950. This transition reflects India’s continued positive population growth, driven by a young demographic profile and fertility rates that, although declining, remain above replacement levels. In contrast, China faces its first negative growth in decades, indicating a divergent trajectory between the two Asian giants regarding their future workforce and societal structures. The uncertainty surrounding the exact moment of this milestone highlights significant challenges in data collection, particularly in India, which has not conducted a comprehensive census since 2011. This gap underscores the limitations of relying on estimates rather than verified statistical records, demonstrating how outdated or missing administrative data can obscure precise demographic realities. Consequently, projections from international bodies, while authoritative, are subject to interpretation and potential inaccuracy when based on incomplete national datasets. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical need for timely, transparent, and accessible government statistics to inform global discourse and policy. The discrepancy between estimates and actual census data emphasizes how data availability—or the lack thereof—directly impacts our understanding of major societal shifts. Ensuring robust, open data infrastructure is essential for accurate monitoring of population dynamics, resource allocation, and sustainable development planning on a global scale.
