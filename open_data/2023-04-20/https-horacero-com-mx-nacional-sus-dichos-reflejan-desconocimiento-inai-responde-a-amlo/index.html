The INAI expressed deep concern over the federal government’s recent dismissive rhetoric, which it argues undermines the institution’s constitutional autonomy. Rather than reflecting political bias, the agency asserts that its two-decade role has been vital for democratic transparency. This political pressure threatens not only the institute’s existence but also the fundamental rights to access information and personal data protection enshrined in the Constitution. By eroding these safeguards, the government jeopardizes citizens’ ability to exercise other human rights. The INAI highlights that access to information enables people to verify social programs and health services, while data protection allows individuals to secure pensions, correct medical records, and demand accountability for identity theft. These tools are essential for holding both public and private entities accountable for the misuse of personal data. Ultimately, this situation underscores the critical role of independent oversight bodies in maintaining an open society. The INAI emphasizes its unique authority to protect data across all sectors, noting that its decisions have exposed cases of significant public interest. This conflict highlights the fragility of data transparency mechanisms when faced with executive hostility, making it a pivotal issue for defenders of open data principles and democratic integrity.
