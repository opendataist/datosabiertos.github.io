Bruselas critica al Gobierno por no explicar el cese de Rodríguez Poo y avala su labor como presidente del INE

Eurostat has validated the statistical rigor of Spain’s National Statistics Institute (INE), affirming its strict adherence to European best practices. This official endorsement effectively rehabilitates the reputation of former president Juan Manuel Rodríguez Poo, contradicting political narratives that suggested his departure was necessitated by data quality issues. By recognizing the high level of compliance across the national statistical system, the European office reinforces the scientific integrity of the data previously released under his leadership. Crucially, the report explicitly criticizes the government for failing to provide transparent justifications for the president’s removal. Eurostat mandates that the public statistical service law must clearly document the reasons for such dismissals to ensure institutional independence. This intervention highlights a significant concern regarding political interference, urging authorities to respect procedural norms that safeguard statistical autonomy from executive pressure. This update is vital for open data advocacy, as it underscores the necessity of transparent governance and freedom from political manipulation in official statistics. It demonstrates that international bodies actively monitor and correct attempts to undermine data credibility through opaque administrative decisions. Consequently, the case serves as a powerful reminder that trustworthy open data relies not only on accurate metrics but also on institutional protections that prevent the “tezanización” or politicization of essential economic information.
