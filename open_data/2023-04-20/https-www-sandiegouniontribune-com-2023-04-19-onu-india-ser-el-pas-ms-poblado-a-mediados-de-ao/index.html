India is poised to become the world’s most populous nation, surpassing China as fertility rates decline there while India maintains a younger demographic profile. This shift marks a significant global demographic transition, altering the balance of power and resource distribution between the two Asian giants. The implication for India lies in the potential for a demographic dividend, where a large workforce could drive economic growth. However, this opportunity hinges on job creation and social stability; without adequate employment, the youth bulge could transform into a social burden rather than an economic asset. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights how transparent, accessible demographic statistics from institutions like the UN and World Bank enable rigorous public analysis of societal trends. Open data empowers citizens to understand complex issues like aging populations and economic potential, fostering informed public debate and accountability. By making such critical information freely available, open data transforms raw population counts into actionable insights for policy-making and civil society engagement, ensuring that demographic shifts are managed with clarity and foresight rather than anxiety.
