Open Data
ES EN

The article highlights the critical intersection of open data and public security in Nigeria, where the absence of reliable statistics on kidnapping and other crimes hampers effective policy-making and accountability. Without comprehensive data on victims, fatalities, and regional trends, it becomes nearly impossible to evaluate the success of interventions or understand the evolving motives of criminal groups, ranging from political grievances to purely financial ransom demands. This data vacuum prevents the government from restategizing based on evidence, leaving security agencies to operate without the empirical foundation necessary for strategic planning and resource allocation. The narrative further illustrates how measurable outcomes, such as the temporary decline in kidnapping during specific cashless initiatives, underscore the value of data-driven security measures. However, the lack of systematic recording means these successes are not leveraged to create sustainable solutions. The article argues that to combat the escalating insecurity in northern regions, authorities must adopt rigorous intelligence gathering and enforce accountability mechanisms, which are fundamentally dependent on accurate, transparent data regarding crime incidents and jurisdictional performance. Furthermore, the piece connects internal police politics and recruitment biases to operational inefficiencies, suggesting that professionalization and education are key to modern policing. The call for state police forces and stricter merit-based promotions implies a need for better organizational data to monitor effectiveness and fairness. Ultimately, the article asserts that resolving Nigeria’s security crisis requires a cultural shift toward transparency and data openness, enabling stakeholders to track progress, identify failures, and implement evidence-based reforms that protect citizens and restore public trust in law enforcement institutions.
