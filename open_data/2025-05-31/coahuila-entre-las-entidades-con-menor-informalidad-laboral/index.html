Coahuila, entre las entidades con menor informalidad laboral

Coahuila, entre las entidades con menor informalidad laboral

The article highlights significant regional disparities in labor informality and poverty in Mexico, noting that northern states such as Coahuila and Nuevo León maintain lower informality rates compared to southern regions, where rates exceed 75%. Although Coahuila has succeeded in keeping labor informality low, it remains in a cautionary yellow status for labor poverty, illustrating the complex interplay between employment formality and actual income adequacy. This distinction underscores that low informality does not automatically equate to reduced poverty, necessitating a nuanced analysis of socioeconomic indicators. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the necessity of accessible, granular datasets to track policy effectiveness and regional equity. The recent transfer of measurement responsibility from Coneval to INEGI introduces uncertainty regarding methodology and frequency. Transparent, standardized data publication is crucial for civil society to verify these metrics and ensure that transitions in governance do not compromise the continuity or comparability of vital social statistics, thereby maintaining trust in official figures. Ultimately, the findings reveal that only a minority of states achieve optimal green status for labor poverty, emphasizing the need for targeted regional interventions. The reliance on specific thresholds for color-coding states requires robust, publicly available underlying data to be meaningful. Open data advocacy is vital here to ensure that citizens and researchers can independently analyze these trends, question methodological changes, and hold institutions accountable for improving labor conditions across diverse geographic and economic contexts.
