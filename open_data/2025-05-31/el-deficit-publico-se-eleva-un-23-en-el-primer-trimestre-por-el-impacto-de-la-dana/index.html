El déficit público se eleva un 23% en el primer trimestre por el impacto de la DANA

The article reveals how extraordinary spending triggered by recent severe weather events temporarily widened public sector deficits, highlighting the fiscal fragility caused by climate-related disasters. This data underscores the critical need for transparent, open data on public expenditure and the allocation of emergency resources. By isolating baseline financial health from crisis-induced anomalies, authorities can better assess long-term economic stability. This distinction is vital for researchers and citizens analyzing government performance without the distortion of temporary external shocks. Ultimately, providing accessible, granular data on such fluctuations empowers democratic oversight. It enables society to understand how unexpected events impact fiscal balances, fostering informed debate on budget resilience and the necessity for robust financial planning mechanisms in the face of increasing environmental volatility.
