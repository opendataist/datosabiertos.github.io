China’s population has entered a historic decline, marking the first contraction in sixty years. This demographic shift is driven by a plummeting fertility rate that remains well below replacement levels, despite the government abandoning restrictive policies and offering incentives. The decline is attributed to cultural shifts toward smaller families, rising living costs, and demographic imbalances resulting from decades of preferential son bias, which has significantly reduced the pool of women of childbearing age. The long-term implications involve a severe contraction of the working-age population and a rapidly aging society, fundamentally altering the economic structure. With fewer workers supporting a growing elderly cohort, labor-intensive manufacturing is expected to relocate to countries with larger workforces, while domestic resources shift heavily toward healthcare and pensions. This structural change threatens slower economic growth and necessitates a reorientation of global supply chains, affecting international trade dynamics and investment patterns worldwide. This data is critical to the open_data community as it illustrates how public statistical records can expose profound socio-economic trends. Transparent access to demographic metrics enables accurate modeling of future workforce shortages, pension sustainability, and migration pressures. By analyzing these open datasets, researchers and policymakers can better understand the cascading effects of population decline, fostering evidence-based discussions on labor market adaptations and global economic resilience.
