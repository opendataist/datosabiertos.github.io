En el Perú 5 de cada 10 establecimientos de salud no tiene abastecimiento adecuado de medicamentos esenciales | RPP Noticias

The article reveals a severe crisis in the supply of essential medicines within Peru’s public health system, where most facilities lack the necessary stock. This structural deficiency forces a large portion of the population to seek care and resources in the private sector, resulting in a direct and disproportionate economic impact on households. Regional disparities are evident, with some areas achieving full coverage while others face critical shortages, highlighting historical failures in the management of the national supply chain. Political instability and high turnover among officials in key positions, particularly those responsible for procurement, are identified as determining factors that slow down budget execution and decision-making. This lack of continuity in public management prevents the development of efficient processes and generates administrative inertia, perpetuating the problem despite allocated funds. The report emphasizes the need to prioritize technical stability and governance over arbitrary political changes to ensure a consistent supply. This report is relevant to the field of open data because it illustrates the critical importance of transparency and access to reliable data for auditing the effectiveness of public policies. The availability of disaggregated information, such as that from systems like SISMED, enables the identification of regional inequalities and the measurement of institutions’ actual performance. However, opacity in procurement processes and the lack of clear data on budget execution hinder accountability, underscoring the urgency for governments to use technology to open up data that strengthens citizen oversight and improves public management.
