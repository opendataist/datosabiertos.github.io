Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

Data obtained via Freedom of Information requests reveals significant disparities in undergraduate academic performance across various disciplines at the University. By analyzing average marks and the distribution of degree classifications, the analysis highlights that subjects such as Physics and English achieved the highest attainment rates, while foundation year modules and certain foreign languages recorded notably lower outcomes. These variations suggest that factors like module type and cohort size heavily influence grading standards, offering a transparent view of how different departments evaluate student success. The breakdown of performance by year of study further illustrates these inconsistencies, with some first-year design modules failing to award any top grades, while others saw high concentrations of first-class results. Similarly, upper-year modules show extreme polarization, where specific Philosophy and Classics courses achieved exceptional success rates compared to peers in Theology or Language studies. This granular data exposes the lack of uniformity in assessment rigor and success metrics across the curriculum, indicating that a student’s likelihood of achieving top honors may depend more on their chosen subject than their individual effort. This investigation is highly relevant to the open data movement, as it demonstrates the public value of requesting and analyzing institutional transparency. By leveraging FOI laws to access internal grading statistics, stakeholders can scrutinize educational equity and accountability. This case exemplifies how open data initiatives can empower communities to question administrative practices, fostering a culture where universities are held accountable for their grading policies through publicly accessible, detailed performance metrics.
