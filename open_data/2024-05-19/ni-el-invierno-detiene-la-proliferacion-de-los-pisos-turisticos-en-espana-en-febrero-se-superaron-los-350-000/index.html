Ni el invierno detiene la proliferación de los pisos turísticos en España: en febrero se superaron los 350.000

Ni el invierno detiene la proliferación de los pisos turísticos en España: en febrero se superaron los 350.000

Spain has experienced an unprecedented surge in short-term rental properties, reaching record levels even during traditionally slow winter months. This growth is driven by the higher profitability of daily rentals compared to long-term leases, coupled with insufficient regulatory enforcement. Consequently, the availability of housing for residents is diminishing, exacerbating the national housing crisis and altering the social fabric of urban centers where locals are increasingly displaced. The concentration of these properties is particularly intense in major tourist destinations and coastal municipalities, often overwhelming local infrastructure and community life. While some regions have implemented restrictions with limited success, the trend continues to rise in key areas like Madrid and Barcelona. This shift indicates a professionalization of the sector, where intermediaries manage properties, further fueling the expansion regardless of existing municipal controls or seasonal expectations. This situation is crucial for open data because the availability and quality of housing statistics directly impact the transparency of urban policy. Accurate, accessible data on rental trends allows civil society and researchers to monitor the real impact of tourism on housing accessibility. Without robust open data frameworks, it becomes nearly impossible to evaluate the effectiveness of regulations or advocate for evidence-based solutions to protect residential rights.
