Inicios de construcción de viviendas en EEUU caen a menor nivel desde 2020 ante mayores costos de deuda | Diario Financiero

The US housing market is experiencing a stark divergence: current construction has plummeted due to prohibitive interest rates, yet indicators of future demand remain robust. This tension highlights how high borrowing costs suppress immediate access while creating scarcity that could benefit builders if affordability improves. This situation is critical for open data because transparent, timely metrics on permits and starts enable researchers to track market resilience and policy efficacy. Analyzing these indicators reveals the real-time impact of monetary policy on housing accessibility, helping stakeholders understand the gap between theoretical demand and actual supply. Ultimately, the article underscores the need for granular data to decode complex economic forces. By monitoring both current downturns and future permits, analysts can better predict whether builders will capitalize on limited inventory or if rates will continue stifling the market, providing essential insights for informed decision-making.
