AG: Mahomet Township Violated FOIA; Improper Designation Of Recurrent Requester –

The article highlights a significant conflict between government transparency obligations and administrative obstructionism. In Illinois, Mahomet Township spent excessive legal resources attempting to delay Freedom of Information Act responses rather than providing records. By misinterpreting statutory timeframes to classify a requester as "recurrent," the township sought extended deadlines. This approach directly contradicted the Illinois Supreme Court’s mandate that FOIA must be liberally construed to ensure easy public access to government information, prioritizing bureaucratic convenience over statutory intent. A core implication for open data governance is the critical importance of precise legal interpretation in access requests. The investigation revealed that the township erroneously calculated the look-back period for determining recurrent status, adding unnecessary time to the standard window. This technical error, combined with an incorrect assumption that multiple requesters acted in concert, led to a false designation. The ruling clarifies that statutory language must be applied strictly and accurately, preventing public bodies from manipulating definitions to restrict citizen access to public records through procedural delays. This case underscores the necessity for rigorous adherence to open data principles by local governments. The Public Access Bureau’s conclusion that the township improperly restricted access serves as a vital precedent for maintaining accountability. It emphasizes that without strict oversight, public entities may exploit ambiguous interpretations of access laws to withhold information. For the open data community, this reinforces that clear, consistent application of freedom of information statutes is essential to protect the public’s right to know and prevent administrative barriers from undermining transparency initiatives.
