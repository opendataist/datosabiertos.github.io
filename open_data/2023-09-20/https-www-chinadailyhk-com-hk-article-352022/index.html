Research utilizing the CMST 2.0 dataset predicts that 2023 is likely to become the hottest year on record, with 2024 potentially even warmer. This forecast stems from analyzing unprecedented global sea surface and land air temperatures, driven by El Niño and other climatic factors. The study highlights an accelerating warming trend that intensifies the risk of extreme weather events, necessitating immediate mitigation efforts to address their devastating impacts. The relevance of this article to open data lies in the CMST 2.0 dataset itself. Developed by Sun Yat-sen University, this resource integrates over a century of global land-air temperature data into a single, cohesive product. By providing such a comprehensive and accessible historical record, it serves as an invaluable tool for climate scientists and policymakers. Open data initiatives enable better aggregation and analysis of long-term environmental metrics, fostering more accurate climate modeling and decision-making. Furthermore, the availability of high-quality, merged datasets allows for more robust validation of climate predictions. When researchers can easily access standardized, long-term historical data, they can better identify trends and anomalies, such as the recent surge in global temperatures. This transparency supports scientific collaboration and ensures that urgent calls for policy action are grounded in reliable, publicly verifiable evidence, thereby strengthening the global response to climate change.
