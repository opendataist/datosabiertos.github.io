Gen Z hit hardest by cost of living crisis, hidden data shows

This analysis highlights that Generation Z is disproportionately suffering from the cost of living crisis, with young people facing significantly higher rates of poverty and financial insecurity compared to other demographics. The data reveals that a substantial portion of individuals under 25 are unable to afford essentials, are in arrears on utility bills, or are borrowing from support networks to survive. This economic strain is compounded by mental health challenges and housing instability, underscoring a severe crisis that affects the well-being and future prospects of young adults. The report emphasizes that current official statistics likely underestimate the scale of youth homelessness and deprivation due to fragmented and non-standardized data collection methods. Experts argue that this gap between recorded data and lived reality prevents resources from reaching those who need them most. By failing to capture the full scope of the issue, policymakers risk skewing funding and support away from vulnerable populations, rendering current interventions less effective in addressing the root causes of youth poverty. For the open data community, this article is highly relevant as it demonstrates how improving data infrastructure and adhering to open data standards can drive tangible social change. The Open Data Institute advocates for standardized data collection to fill existing gaps, arguing that transparent and comprehensive information allows for more effective targeting of support services. Ultimately, leveraging open data principles can help uncover hidden vulnerabilities, ensure equitable resource distribution, and empower policymakers to create more responsive and efficient social safety nets.
