Freedom of information? Activist questions redacted traffic study for Vancouver development - BC | Globalnews.ca

The near-total redaction of a major development’s traffic studies by the City of Vancouver highlights critical failures in governmental transparency. When residents requested data on a large-scale housing project, they received a heavily censored document package, sparking concerns about withheld information affecting community well-being. This incident underscores how freedom of information laws can be utilized to obscure, rather than clarify, public sector decision-making, leaving citizens in the dark about significant local impacts. This case reflects a broader cultural trend in Canada where government operations default toward secrecy. Experts argue that the ease of withholding information under various legal exemptions creates an opaque environment that undermines democratic accountability. Because challenging these rulings is a protracted and difficult process, the public often lacks the ability to verify whether governments are applying privacy rules appropriately or simply avoiding scrutiny of controversial projects. This situation is highly relevant to the open data movement because it demonstrates the gap between legal access to information and the practical availability of usable data. True open data requires more than just the existence of records; it demands that information be released in accessible, non-redacted formats to foster genuine public engagement. Without this transparency, citizens cannot effectively participate in planning processes or hold institutions accountable for potential adverse impacts on their neighborhoods.
