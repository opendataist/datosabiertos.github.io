REVEALED: The cost of policing Wrexham AFC matches and reasons for arrests

This article demonstrates the practical application of freedom of information requests, showing how citizens can access specific operational data from law enforcement agencies. By revealing detailed financial records, arrest statistics, and official justifications regarding the policing of Wrexham AFC matches, it highlights the transparency enabled by open data principles. The ability to scrutinize public spending and police conduct fosters accountability and allows the public to understand the balance between community safety and resource allocation. The core finding reveals a significant disparity between the substantial costs incurred by North Wales Police for match security and the limited fees recovered from the football club. Authorities emphasize that their primary obligation is wider public safety rather than full cost recovery, a stance supported by legal frameworks. This insight into the financial reality of policing public events underscores the complexity of budgeting for community services and the necessity of understanding how public funds are utilized for crowd control and safety measures. Ultimately, the data illustrates a robust approach to managing football-related incidents, with most years showing no arrests while significant interventions occurred during higher-risk periods. This narrative supports the relevance of open data by providing concrete evidence of how police forces assess threat levels and deploy resources. It empowers stakeholders to evaluate the effectiveness of policing strategies and ensures that decisions regarding public order are subject to informed public debate and oversight.
