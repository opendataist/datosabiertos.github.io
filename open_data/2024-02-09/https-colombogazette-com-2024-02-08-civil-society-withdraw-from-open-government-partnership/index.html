Civil society organizations in Sri Lanka have withdrawn from the Open Government Partnership to protest government efforts to suppress civic space through restrictive legislation. This decision underscores the critical role of genuine multi-stakeholder collaboration in maintaining democratic accountability and freedom of expression. The withdrawal highlights a fundamental conflict between authoritarian measures and the core OGP principles of transparency and citizen participation. It demonstrates that open data and governance initiatives require a safe, supportive political environment to be effective and meaningful for communities. This event is vital for the open data community as it illustrates how political repression directly undermines data accessibility and public engagement. It serves as a stark reminder that technical openness cannot survive without protecting the broader ecosystem of civil liberties and active citizen involvement in governance processes.
