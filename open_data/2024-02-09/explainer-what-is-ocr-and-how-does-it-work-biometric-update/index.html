Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical character recognition bridges the gap between static print media and dynamic digital workflows, enabling seamless data extraction from physical documents. This capability is essential for modern business operations, as it transforms uneditable image files into structured, machine-readable text. By eliminating manual data entry, organizations can significantly reduce errors and accelerate processes, turning raw visual information into actionable insights. The technology operates through a sophisticated analysis of light and dark areas, utilizing algorithms to identify characters via pattern matching or feature extraction. This technical foundation allows for the accurate conversion of various fonts and layouts into digital formats, supporting tasks like automated form completion. The reliability of this conversion depends on the system's ability to distinguish between background noise and meaningful text features. For open data, this automation is crucial because it unlocks vast amounts of previously inaccessible information stored in physical archives. By converting identity documents and other scanned materials into structured digital data, OCR facilitates broader accessibility and interoperability. This not only enhances transparency but also enables advanced analytics and biometric integration, ensuring that public and private data can be efficiently utilized for innovation and verification purposes.
