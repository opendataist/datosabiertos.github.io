Los números de la Eurocopa retratan a Tebas por su mal manejo de la Liga

The article highlights a stark contrast between the massive television audiences for open-access events like the Euro Cup and the declining viewership of the Spanish League. This disparity suggests that the primary challenge is not a lack of interest in football, but rather the structural barriers preventing fans from easily accessing matches. When viewing options are limited or prohibitively expensive, public interest shifts rapidly toward more accessible forms of entertainment. The core issue lies in the Spanish League’s adherence to traditional, exclusive broadcasting models that lock content behind high-cost subscriptions. Unlike other international competitions that have embraced streaming platforms and lower entry prices to maximize reach, the League maintains restrictive rights deals with specific providers. This approach fails to capitalize on new opportunities, such as the arrival of global stars, and ignores the successful strategies adopted by other sports that utilize affordable streaming services to engage broader, younger demographics. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the impact of data accessibility and transparency on public engagement. When information and media consumption are gated behind expensive, opaque systems, users naturally migrate to platforms that offer better value and openness. The article argues that sustainable growth requires aligning distribution models with modern consumer expectations for accessibility, demonstrating how digital openness correlates directly with audience retention in the media landscape.
