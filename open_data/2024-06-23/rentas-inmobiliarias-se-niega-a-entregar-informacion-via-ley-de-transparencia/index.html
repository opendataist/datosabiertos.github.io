Rentas Inmobiliarias se niega a entregar información vía Ley de Transparencia

Rentas Inmobiliarias, the operator of the Punta Arenas Free Zone, has denied transparency requests submitted by regional councilor Alejandro Riquelme concerning financial investments and audit reports. The company argues that disclosing such data would reveal strategic business secrets and operational details essential to its private enterprise model. It contends that this information is crucial for maintaining competitive advantages and protecting commercial rights against unauthorized disclosure to rivals or the public. This refusal underscores the tension between corporate confidentiality and public accountability in public–private concession agreements. The operator asserts that, although it has obligations to the state, the detailed execution of these contracts constitutes proprietary business intelligence. Consequently, it maintains that forced transparency could unfairly disadvantage it by exposing long-term strategies and financial structures that are normally shielded under commercial law protections. This conflict is highly relevant to open data debates, as it exemplifies the boundaries of transparency for entities managing public concessions. It raises critical questions about where to draw the line between legitimate trade secrets and information that the public has a right to access due to the involvement of state resources. Understanding this dynamic is essential for developing policies that balance economic competitiveness with the democratic principle of governmental accountability and open governance.
