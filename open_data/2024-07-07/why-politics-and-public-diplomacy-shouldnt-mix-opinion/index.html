Why politics and public diplomacy shouldn’t mix - opinion

Why politics and public diplomacy shouldn’t mix - opinion

The article argues that effective international public diplomacy for Israel requires advocates to strictly separate their political opinions from their advocacy efforts. By remaining nonpartisan, activists can avoid alienating potential supporters and maintain credibility with a diverse, global audience that extends beyond existing pro-Israel circles. Identifying with specific political parties, whether on Israel’s left, right, or center, undermines the universal appeal of the state’s message and fractures the broad coalition necessary for effective support. Furthermore, the piece highlights that maintaining a neutral stance is crucial for sustaining bipartisan support in key allied nations like the United States, the UK, and France. Engaging in domestic political fights distracts from the shared values that unite voters across the spectrum and risks turning Israel into a political football. Even in hostile regions like Iran, staying out of partisan politics allows advocates to focus on universal concerns, such as nuclear non-proliferation, rather than getting entangled in internal electoral dynamics that do not serve Israel’s strategic interests. This approach is vital for open data initiatives because it ensures that advocacy remains focused on factual accuracy and media accountability rather than partisan narratives. By avoiding political bias, organizations can build trust among younger, digitally native demographics who consume news through social media, thereby expanding their reach and impact. The implication for open data is that transparency and neutrality are more powerful tools for influencing public opinion and policy than political alignment, enabling a broader, more sustainable foundation for international support.
