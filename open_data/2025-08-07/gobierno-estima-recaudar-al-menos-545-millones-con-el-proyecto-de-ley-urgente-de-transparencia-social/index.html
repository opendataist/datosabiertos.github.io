Gobierno estima recaudar al menos $ 545 millones con el proyecto de ley urgente de ‘transparencia social’

Gobierno estima recaudar al menos $ 545 millones con el proyecto de ley urgente de ‘transparencia social’

The government’s new “social transparency” initiative aims to strengthen oversight of foundations and civil society organizations to prevent irregular capital flows. By tightening regulations on these entities, the state seeks to identify and control financial activities that could facilitate illicit operations, thereby enhancing the integrity of the national financial system. This legislative measure represents a significant shift toward stricter monitoring of the non-profit sector to ensure compliance with financial standards. The expected fiscal impact is substantial, with projections indicating permanent annual revenues of over half a billion dollars derived from these controls. Administrative costs are being managed through targeted adjustments, including the hiring of additional inspectors and leveraging existing resources within financial analysis units. This approach ensures that the enforcement mechanism is sustainable and does not impose an undue burden on the budget while maximizing the collection of funds linked to previously unreported or irregular transactions. This development is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical role of transparent financial reporting in combating money laundering and corruption. As banks and institutions generate extensive data on suspicious activities, making such information accessible and analyzable is essential for effective governance. Strengthening these oversight mechanisms promotes accountability and provides a clearer picture of economic flows, which is fundamental for maintaining trust in public institutions and facilitating data-driven policy decisions.
