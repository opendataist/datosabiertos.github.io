Reduce Transparencia para el Pueblo desecho de quejas y funciones clave

Reduce Transparencia para el Pueblo desecho de quejas y funciones clave

The replacement of INAI with Transparencia para el Pueblo marks a controversial shift in Mexico’s transparency framework, raising serious concerns about democratic oversight. This reorganization centralizes authority and drastically reduces resources, severely limiting the agency's capacity to process complaints and enforce information access rights effectively. Concentrating decision-making in a single individual rather than a collegial body undermines institutional independence and pluralism. Critics argue that this structural change prioritizes austerity over accountability, potentially shielding government misconduct by weakening the mechanisms designed to ensure public sector transparency and protect citizens' right to know. This case is highly relevant to open data advocacy, as it illustrates how political restructurings and budget cuts can actively hinder data accessibility. It highlights the critical need for independent, well-resourced institutions to maintain open data ecosystems, demonstrating that without robust oversight, open government initiatives can easily erode, compromising the fundamental principles of transparency and public accountability.
