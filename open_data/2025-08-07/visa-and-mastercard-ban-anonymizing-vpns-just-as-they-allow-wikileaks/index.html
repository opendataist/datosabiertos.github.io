Visa And Mastercard Ban Anonymizing VPNs… Just As They Allow Wikileaks

Visa And Mastercard Ban Anonymizing VPNs… Just As They Allow Wikileaks

Credit card giants Visa and Mastercard have reversed course by banning payments to VPN providers, despite previously restoring funding to WikiLeaks following a court order. This contradictory stance ignores the legal necessity of encryption for personal data security, effectively forcing users toward exposed data practices. By targeting anonymizing services, these financial institutions prioritize surveillance control over individual privacy rights and digital hygiene. The abrupt policy enforcement, leaving providers minimal notice to adapt, suggests an intent to circumvent legal scrutiny by assuming providers will not challenge the bans. This aggressive approach threatens legitimate privacy tools essential for secure remote work and travel. It undermines the global standard for anonymous, safe internet usage without valid cause, prioritizing corporate and state monitoring interests over user protection. This development is critical to open data because it erodes the infrastructure necessary for transparent, uncensored information exchange. By stifling anonymity, the credit card industry inadvertently facilitates data suppression and reduces accountability for powerful entities. Protecting open data ecosystems requires defending the very tools that allow whistleblowers and citizens to communicate securely, ensuring that financial networks do not become instruments of censorship rather than open commerce.
