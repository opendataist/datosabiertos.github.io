Elecciones en Venezuela: cómo la tecnología y los datos abiertos han defendido la democracia – Correo del Caroní

Elecciones en Venezuela: cómo la tecnología y los datos abiertos han defendido la democracia – Correo del Caroní

The article concludes that technology, far from being a unilateral threat to democracy, can serve as a powerful civic ally. Through an analysis of recent elections in Venezuela, it demonstrates that radical transparency and citizen-led audits can counteract disinformation and government manipulation. The open publication of electoral records enabled academics, media outlets, and ordinary citizens to independently verify the results, thereby validating the popular will even in the face of official opacity. This case underscores the crucial importance of open data for ensuring electoral integrity. By releasing the underlying databases, a collaborative verification ecosystem was enabled, in which diverse actors—from international agencies to individual citizens—cross-referenced information to detect inconsistencies. This accessibility transforms the election into an exercise in collective transparency, demonstrating that the availability of verifiable information is the best defense against false narratives and systemic fraud. The relevance for the open data movement lies in the fact that this phenomenon establishes a global precedent: the democratization of electoral data empowers civil society. The Venezuelan experience suggests that when data are accessible, citizens’ capacity to discern the truth increases, mitigating the impact of deepfakes and disinformation. This validates the thesis that data transparency is not merely a technical tool, but a fundamental requirement for democratic resilience in the digital age.
