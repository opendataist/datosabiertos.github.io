The article highlights the successful completion of a new public park in Querétaro, achieved through the city’s Open Government program. This initiative emphasizes continuous citizen engagement beyond electoral processes, fostering collaboration between local authorities and architecture students to strengthen social cohesion and provide safe recreational spaces for youth. This project demonstrates how transparent, participatory governance models can directly improve urban infrastructure and community well-being. By integrating local educational resources with municipal planning, the administration illustrates a practical application of open data principles in spatial planning and resource management. The relevance to open data lies in the transparency and collaborative nature of the process. It shows how accessible information and open collaboration channels enable communities to co-create public goods, ensuring that urban development responds effectively to social needs while promoting accountability and long-term civic trust in government institutions.
