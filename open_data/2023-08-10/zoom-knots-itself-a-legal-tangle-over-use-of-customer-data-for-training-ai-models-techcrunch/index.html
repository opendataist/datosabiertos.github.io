Zoom knots itself a legal tangle over use of customer data for training AI models | TechCrunch

Zoom faces potential legal repercussions in Europe after controversy over its terms of service, which originally suggested customer data could be used for AI training without an opt-out. While the company clarified that explicit consent is required for audio, video, and chat content, critics argue its implementation violates European privacy standards. The core issue lies in how Zoom manages user choice, particularly for meeting participants who are effectively forced to accept data usage or leave the call entirely, a practice that undermines the requirement for freely given consent. This situation highlights critical challenges for open data and privacy governance, specifically regarding the validity of consent mechanisms in an era of expansive AI development. Under regulations like the GDPR, consent must be specific, informed, and freely given, yet Zoom’s design often bundles non-essential data processing with essential service features or uses pre-selected options. This "bundling" and the lack of genuine alternatives for users suggest a mismatch between corporate data practices and regulatory expectations, raising questions about whether such models can withstand legal scrutiny in regions with strict data protection laws. The relevance to open data lies in the precedent this sets for how user-generated content is repurposed for artificial intelligence. If platforms can normalize ambiguous consent structures where users have no real power to prevent their contributions from training commercial algorithms, it erodes trust and transparency in digital ecosystems. This case serves as a cautionary tale for the broader open data community, emphasizing that ethical data handling requires robust, uncoerced user agency rather than technical compliance that masks exploitative practices.
