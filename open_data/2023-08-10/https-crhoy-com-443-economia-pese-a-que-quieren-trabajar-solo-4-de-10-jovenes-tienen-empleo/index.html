The article highlights a severe crisis in Costa Rica’s youth labor market, where a significant proportion of young people have exited the workforce despite their willingness to work. This disengagement points to a broader failure in aligning human capital development with market demands, as many youths lack the specific skills and experience employers require. The resulting exclusion creates a cycle of disadvantage that threatens social stability, potentially fueling poverty, inequality, and social unrest among the younger population. From an open data perspective, this report underscores the critical role of high-quality, accessible statistical indicators in diagnosing societal health issues. The analysis relies on rigorous surveys to reveal discrepancies between labor supply and demand, demonstrating how transparent data can expose hidden inefficiencies in the economy. By quantifying the gap between educational outcomes and employment requirements, public data serves as an essential tool for identifying structural barriers that prevent demographic groups from fully participating in the formal economy. Ultimately, the study emphasizes that without targeted policy interventions supported by reliable data tracking, the country risks entrenching long-term social and economic disparities. The relevance to open data lies in its capacity to drive accountability and inform evidence-based solutions. When statistical information is freely available and clearly interpreted, it empowers stakeholders to advocate for better educational frameworks and labor policies, ensuring that statistical transparency translates into tangible improvements in social mobility and employment opportunities.
