Marc y Martina sustituyen a Antonio y Cristina como los nombres más comunes en Baleares en los últimos 40 años

The article reveals how the National Statistics Institute tracks the evolution of naming conventions in the Balearic Islands, highlighting a shift from historical classics to modern preferences. This demonstrates the value of historical demographic records in understanding cultural trends and societal changes over time. The data shows that while names like Antonio and Juan remained dominant for decades, recent years have favored names such as Marc, Pau, and Leo for boys, and Martina, Julia, and Emma for girls. This transition reflects changing parental tastes, moving away from traditional Spanish names toward more international or minimalist options, illustrating how language and culture evolve through public data. This resource is relevant to open data because it provides accessible, longitudinal demographic statistics that allow researchers and the public to analyze sociological shifts. By publishing detailed naming patterns, statistical agencies enable deeper insights into identity, migration, and globalization, supporting data-driven studies on social behavior and community identity without restrictive barriers.
