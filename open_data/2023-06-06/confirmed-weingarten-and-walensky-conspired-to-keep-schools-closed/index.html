Confirmed: Weingarten and Walensky conspired to keep schools closed

Recent disclosures obtained through public records requests reveal that the CDC altered school reopening guidelines in early 2021 to accommodate political pressure from teachers' unions rather than scientific evidence. Text messages demonstrate that union leadership successfully influenced the CDC director to soften language regarding in-person instruction, prioritizing the ability to keep schools closed longer over health-based metrics. This exchange highlights a troubling intersection where institutional policy was shaped by organizational interests instead of impartial data. The implications of this incident suggest that pandemic mitigation strategies were often driven by political alliances rather than empirical outcomes. Evidence indicates that prolonged school closures, facilitated by such influence, resulted in significant negative impacts on students' educational achievement and mental health, with no corresponding benefit to virus control. This challenges the narrative that health agencies strictly followed scientific consensus, suggesting instead that external pressures compromised the integrity of public health recommendations during a critical period. This story is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical necessity of transparency in government operations. By leveraging freedom of information laws to access internal communications, citizens can uncover discrepancies between stated policies and actual decision-making processes. Such transparency is essential for holding public officials accountable and ensuring that future public health responses are based on verifiable facts and open scrutiny rather than opaque political negotiations.
