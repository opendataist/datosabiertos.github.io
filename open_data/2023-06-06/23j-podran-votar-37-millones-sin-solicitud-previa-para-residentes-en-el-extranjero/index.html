23J: podrán votar 37 millones, sin solicitud previa para residentes en el extranjero

This article highlights the logistical framework for the upcoming Spanish general elections, detailing the expansion of the electorate and streamlined voting processes. It emphasizes that over 37 million citizens will vote, including a significant number of first-time voters and millions of expatriates who no longer need to submit prior requests to cast their ballots. The text outlines specific windows for census verification, error correction, and opting out of political propaganda, ensuring administrative clarity for all participants. This information is crucial for open data initiatives, as it reveals how government entities publish and structure electoral datasets for public access. By outlining specific digital portals and physical locations for retrieving census data, the article illustrates the intersection of bureaucratic procedures and data transparency. It demonstrates how state institutions operationalize voter rights through accessible, structured information systems. Understanding these data flows helps analyze how democratic institutions manage and communicate vital public records. The reliance on electronic services alongside traditional channels reflects a hybrid approach to data dissemination. This context enables researchers to examine the efficiency, accessibility, and inclusivity of electoral data infrastructure in modern democracies.
