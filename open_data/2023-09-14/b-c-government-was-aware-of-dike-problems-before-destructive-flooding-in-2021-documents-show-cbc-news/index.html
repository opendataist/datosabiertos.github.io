B.C. government was aware of dike problems before destructive flooding in 2021, documents show | CBC News

The article reveals that provincial and municipal authorities in British Columbia were aware of critical failures in the Merritt dike system years before the devastating 2021 floods. Despite repeated professional engineering reports highlighting severe structural compromises and urgent maintenance needs, no corrective orders were issued by the provincial oversight body. This inaction underscores a significant gap in regulatory enforcement, where documented risks were ignored until disaster struck, resulting in massive property damage and community displacement. The core issue stems from a 2003 policy shift that transferred dike maintenance responsibility to local governments, a decision now widely criticized as unsustainable for small communities lacking the necessary financial resources. The Union of B.C. Municipalities has repeatedly called for the province to reclaim control, arguing that the current model places an unfair burden on municipalities. This systemic failure highlights the dangers of decentralizing critical infrastructure management without ensuring adequate provincial support or oversight, leaving vulnerable communities exposed to preventable hazards. This case is vital to the open data community because it demonstrates the tangible consequences of transparency mechanisms like freedom of information requests. By accessing and analyzing hidden government records, advocates were able to expose the disconnect between official reports and administrative action. The incident illustrates how open data can drive accountability, challenge entrenched policy failures, and push for systemic reforms in public infrastructure management, proving that accessible information is crucial for safeguarding public safety.
