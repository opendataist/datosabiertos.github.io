The Inter-American Press Society (SIP) is urging the Panamanian government to withdraw a new transparency law, arguing that it threatens journalists’ and citizens’ access to public information while potentially shielding corruption. This proposed legislation seeks to replace a long-standing, effective framework that has significantly contributed to regional anti-corruption efforts. The SIP views this substitution as counterproductive, emphasizing the importance of maintaining proven mechanisms that ensure governmental accountability. Critics highlight the absence of a consultative process for this controversial initiative, noting a growing trend of governmental resistance to transparency across the Americas. The SIP stresses that leaders have committed to open governance through regional declarations, which legally obligate authorities to provide timely and equitable access to public data. Restricting these rights contradicts established principles of free expression and democratic integrity. This situation is critical to open data, as it illustrates the fragility of legal frameworks ensuring data accessibility. Strong laws are essential for enabling civil society and the media to hold power accountable. Protecting robust transparency regulations prevents the erosion of public trust and ensures that information remains a tool for empowerment rather than a privilege restricted by state control.
