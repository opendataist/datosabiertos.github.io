US consumer prices continued to rise last month — but the Fed probably won’t raise rates - KTVZ

The article highlights the distinction between headline and core inflation, noting that while gas prices drove a recent acceleration in overall costs, core inflation continued its downward trend. This divergence suggests that the Federal Reserve’s aggressive interest rate hikes are effectively cooling demand-driven price pressures, even as volatile energy sectors create temporary noise in the data. For open data enthusiasts, this underscores the critical importance of using disaggregated metrics rather than aggregate figures to understand underlying economic health. Market participants and policymakers are primarily focused on core inflation as a reliable indicator of monetary policy success. The data supports a high probability of a rate pause, indicating that central banks can rely on robust, transparent economic datasets to navigate toward a "soft landing" without triggering a recession. This reinforces the value of timely, accessible statistical releases in allowing markets and the public to form accurate expectations about future economic stability and monetary decisions. Looking ahead, experts predict further disinflation driven by moderating shelter costs and a cooling labor market. This narrative emphasizes that open data serves as the foundational infrastructure for validating these long-term economic forecasts. By making detailed metrics like wage growth and shelter price trends publicly available, stakeholders can better assess whether structural imbalances are being resolved, ensuring that policy responses remain data-driven and aligned with the goal of sustainable price stability.
