Today’s SCOTUS controversy would be deja vu to Gil Cranberg

The article highlights the enduring relevance of long-standing calls for transparency and ethical codes within the U.S. judicial system, tracing concerns back to editorials published four decades ago. It contrasts the current Supreme Court’s lack of a binding ethical code and opaque recusal processes with Iowa’s more structured judicial qualification standards. This comparison underscores a critical gap in federal accountability, where justices operate under custom rather than uniform rules, raising significant questions about conflicts of interest and public trust. The narrative illustrates how historical advocacy for open government remains vital today, as modern controversies surrounding undisclosed gifts and political influence echo past warnings. By examining the specific ethical blind spots that allow such issues to persist, the piece argues that the absence of standardized guidelines creates a "ticking time bomb" for the Court’s integrity. The recurring theme is that judicial secrecy and inconsistent ethical application undermine the perceived legitimacy of the highest court, regardless of the specific justices involved. This content is highly relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates that access to information is not merely a technical issue but a fundamental component of institutional accountability. It shows how the lack of standardized, publicly available data regarding judicial conduct and decision-making processes hinders democratic oversight. The article serves as a powerful reminder that open data initiatives must extend to all branches of government to prevent secrecy from eroding public confidence in democratic institutions.
