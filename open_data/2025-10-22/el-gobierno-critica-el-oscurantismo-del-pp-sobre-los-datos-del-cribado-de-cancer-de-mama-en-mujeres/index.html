El Gobierno critica el "oscurantismo" del PP sobre los datos del cribado de cáncer de mama en mujeres

El Gobierno critica el "oscurantismo" del PP sobre los datos del cribado de cáncer de mama en mujeres

The Spanish central government accuses several regional administrations of withholding breast cancer screening data, characterizing this refusal as political obstruction and a violation of citizens' right to transparent health information. This conflict highlights the critical role of data openness in public health, as withholding such vital statistics prevents societal oversight and undermines trust in healthcare services. Simultaneously, severe issues in Andalusia, including alleged deletion of medical records, have triggered legal complaints and executive resignations, further illustrating the consequences of opaque information management. These incidents underscore how restricted access to health data not only impedes policy evaluation but also exposes populations to potential malpractice and security breaches within medical platforms. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates that mandatory transparency is essential for accountability and safety. When governments treat health statistics as exclusive regional competencies rather than public goods, they hinder the collective ability to monitor service quality. Ensuring open access to such data empowers citizens and enables accurate assessment of healthcare effectiveness, preventing systemic failures from remaining hidden.
