Castilla-La Mancha tiene el tramo de carretera más peligroso de España: su riesgo multiplica por 598 la media

Castilla-La Mancha tiene el tramo de carretera más peligroso de España: su riesgo multiplica por 598 la media

The report by Automovilistas Europeos Asociados highlights critical safety gaps in Spanish road infrastructure by identifying specific high-risk segments using official government data. This analysis challenges the prevailing narrative that accidents are solely caused by human error, demonstrating instead that physical road conditions significantly influence accident frequency. By pinpointing these dangerous zones, the study provides a factual basis for prioritizing infrastructure improvements and targeted maintenance, rather than relying exclusively on behavioral campaigns to enhance overall traffic safety. The findings reveal that certain stretches, including those on toll highways previously considered the safest, exhibit perilous accident rates that far exceed national averages. This unexpected trend in private infrastructure suggests that the distinction between public and private roads is not a guaranteed safety measure. The identification of these specific "black spots" allows authorities to allocate resources more effectively, addressing structural flaws that contribute to both the number of collisions and the resulting casualties, thereby improving the overall resilience of the transportation network. This article is highly relevant to open data because it relies on the transparency and accessibility of public administrative records to drive policy and public awareness. It exemplifies how leveraging open government data enables civil society organizations to independently verify safety trends, hold institutions accountable, and advocate for evidence-based decision-making. The study underscores the importance of making detailed, granular safety data publicly available to foster a culture of accountability and continuous improvement in public infrastructure management.
