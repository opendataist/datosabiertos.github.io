España es objeto de ciberataques masivos de origen ruso durante las elecciones del 23-J

A coordinated DDoS attack targeted Spanish government and corporate websites during the election, temporarily disrupting access to critical electoral and state platforms. While the Russian-linked group Noname057 claimed responsibility to protest Spain’s support for Ukraine, no data breaches occurred. The incident highlights the vulnerability of digital infrastructure to political cyber warfare, emphasizing the need for robust resilience measures in essential services during high-profile national events. National cybersecurity bodies, including the CCN and CNI, were activated to mitigate the impact and investigate the perpetrators. This response underscores the increasing sophistication of state-sponsored attacks and the importance of inter-agency coordination. The event serves as a stark reminder that public institutions must prioritize continuous monitoring and rapid incident response capabilities to maintain public trust and operational continuity amidst escalating geopolitical tensions. This article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical intersection between information availability and cybersecurity. As governments strive to provide transparent, accessible electoral data, they become prime targets for disruption. Ensuring the integrity and availability of open data portals is not just a technical challenge but a democratic necessity, requiring proactive security strategies to prevent interference with public information flows during sensitive political periods.
