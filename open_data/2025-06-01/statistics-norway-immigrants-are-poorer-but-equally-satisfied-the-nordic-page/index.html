Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway reveals a striking paradox in immigrant living conditions: despite facing significantly poorer economic, health, and social outcomes compared to the general population, immigrants report equal life satisfaction and higher confidence in state institutions. This divergence highlights that objective material deprivation does not necessarily correlate with subjective well-being, suggesting that immigrants may find stability and contentment through strong affiliation with Norway despite systemic disadvantages. The data indicates substantial disparities in mental health and social isolation, with immigrants experiencing higher rates of psychological distress and loneliness. However, these challenges exist alongside unusually high trust in the political system, judiciary, and police, reflecting the broader Nordic model’s appeal. This resilience implies that strong social safety nets and institutional reliability can mitigate the negative impacts of socio-economic inequality, offering a unique perspective on integration success. This report is vital to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, large-scale statistical surveys can uncover complex societal dynamics that contradict simple narratives. By making detailed insights into living conditions accessible, open data enables policymakers and researchers to move beyond aggregate metrics, fostering nuanced discussions on integration, mental health, and institutional trust that are essential for evidence-based social policy.
