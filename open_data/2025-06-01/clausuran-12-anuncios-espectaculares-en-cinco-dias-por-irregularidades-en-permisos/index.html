Clausuran 12 anuncios espectaculares en cinco días por irregularidades en permisos

The municipal government is enforcing strict compliance with regulations governing outdoor advertising to enhance urban order, safety, and aesthetics. By cracking down on structures that lack permits or proper registration, authorities aim to eliminate public hazards and ensure that only regulated entities operate in public spaces. This regulatory push compels owners to regularize their status or face closure, highlighting the critical need for accurate, up-to-date public records of private infrastructure. A significant portion of these signs operates illegally, often owned by companies with no local presence that have neglected fee payments for years. Simultaneously, a vast number of unregistered structures exist, ranging from small banners to large lighted displays. The sheer volume of non-compliant entities underscores a systemic failure in public accountability, where citizens are exposed to risks from poorly maintained or unauthorized installations without clear ownership or legal recourse. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the value of transparent, accessible municipal registries. If data on advertising permits, licenses, and infrastructure conditions were made open, it would allow for greater public oversight and easier verification of compliance. Open data could streamline regularization processes, reduce administrative burdens, and empower citizens to hold authorities accountable for urban management and safety standards.
