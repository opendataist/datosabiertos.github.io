Garda and court stats shows sex work laws are ‘complete failure’ — Sex Workers Alliance Ireland | BreakingNews.ie

The article argues that Ireland’s 2017 law criminalizing the purchase of sex has fundamentally failed its stated objective of protecting workers. Despite promises to shift liability away from sellers, statistics reveal that prosecutions of clients remain negligible while summons against workers increase. Consequently, the legislation has not reduced demand or improved safety, but instead has fostered a hostile environment where sex workers face heightened stigma, violence, and isolation. A critical implication of this legal framework is that it actively discourages victims from seeking help. By creating a climate of fear where both clients and workers risk prosecution, the law prevents the reporting of sexual violence and exploitation. This lack of reporting leaves sex workers significantly more vulnerable to abuse and trafficking, as they cannot access necessary support or legal protection without endangering themselves. This case is highly relevant to open data because it uses publicly released government statistics to debunk the efficacy of current policy. It demonstrates how transparent data can serve as a powerful tool for advocacy, allowing civil society groups to empirically prove that specific regulatory approaches are not only ineffective but detrimental. The article underscores the importance of using open datasets to hold policymakers accountable and to drive evidence-based reform rather than relying on political promises.
