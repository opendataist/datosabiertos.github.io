The feds and FOIA subversion: Delay-until-they’re-dead edition

Federal agencies are increasingly employing unreasonable document production limits to obstruct Freedom of Information Act requests, effectively stalling transparency efforts. By capping monthly releases to minimal thresholds, departments such as Justice and Defense are extending processing times from years to decades for critical records. This tactic transforms FOIA from a tool for accountability into a mechanism for indefinite concealment, preventing journalists and researchers from accessing timely information on significant public events. This issue is crucial for open data because it demonstrates how administrative inertia and strategic delays can nullify legal mandates for public access. When agencies treat low production volumes as a defense, they create massive backlogs that render data useless for investigative journalism and policy analysis. The resulting information asymmetry undermines public trust and hinders the ability to scrutinize government actions, particularly regarding law enforcement conduct and national security decisions during volatile political periods. To restore the integrity of open data systems, statutory reforms are urgently needed to enforce strict, short deadlines for full production, backed by financial penalties for non-compliance. Additionally, implementing automatic declassification for older records would alleviate processing burdens and make historical data accessible. These measures would ensure that FOIA fulfills its original purpose, guaranteeing citizens meaningful oversight and enabling the free flow of essential government information.
