Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

Data released through a Freedom of Information Request highlights significant disparities in undergraduate academic performance across various university disciplines. The analysis reveals that physics and English lead in attainment, while foundation courses and certain modern languages score considerably lower. These variations suggest that grading standards and module structures differ widely, indicating that a single grade may not hold uniform meaning across all fields of study. The breakdown by year of study further illustrates this inconsistency, with first-year modules showing minimal grade distribution variance, whereas upper-level classes exhibit extreme polarization. For instance, specific third-year modules see nearly universal top grades, while others in the same cohort receive none. This trend underscores how individual module design and assessment methods heavily influence student outcomes, potentially complicating fair comparisons between students in different academic tracks. This data is critical for open_data initiatives as it demonstrates the importance of transparent, granular institutional metrics. By making such detailed attainment records publicly available, universities can foster greater accountability and allow stakeholders to understand the nuances of grading practices. Furthermore, this transparency supports research into educational equity, enabling analysts to identify systemic biases or structural advantages inherent in specific subjects or assessment formats.
