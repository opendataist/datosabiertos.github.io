Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

The report reveals a striking paradox where immigrants in Norway experience significantly poorer living conditions, including lower income, employment, and health outcomes, yet report satisfaction levels equal to the general population. Despite facing substantial socioeconomic disadvantages and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness, immigrants demonstrate strong affiliation with Norway and notably higher trust in state institutions compared to native residents. This discrepancy highlights the complex interplay between objective hardship and subjective well-being, suggesting that structural integration factors, such as institutional trust, may buffer the negative impacts of material deprivation. The findings challenge assumptions that poor living standards inevitably lead to lower life satisfaction, pointing instead to the protective role of social capital and civic engagement within the Norwegian context. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of granular, disaggregated datasets to uncover nuanced social dynamics that aggregate statistics might obscure. By making detailed survey data accessible, researchers and policymakers can better understand the specific needs of diverse immigrant groups, enabling targeted interventions that address both material inequalities and the psychological factors contributing to social cohesion.
