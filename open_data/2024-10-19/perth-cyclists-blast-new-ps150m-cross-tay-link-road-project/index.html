Perth cyclists blast new £150m Cross Tay Link Road project

The article highlights a significant tension between large-scale infrastructure development and active travel priorities, directly impacting open data transparency. Campaigners utilized Freedom of Information requests to uncover traffic modeling data, revealing that the new Cross Tay Link Road is projected to increase car traffic rather than alleviate it. This incident underscores how accessible government data is crucial for holding public bodies accountable and challenging official narratives regarding transport planning outcomes. The core implication is that without transparent access to predictive modeling and impact assessments, citizens cannot effectively advocate for sustainable urban design. The debate illustrates that infrastructure projects often treat walking and cycling as secondary add-ons rather than primary mobility solutions. Consequently, open data serves as a critical tool for civil society to identify discrepancies between claimed environmental benefits and actual projected consequences, fostering more informed public discourse. Relevance to open data lies in its role as an enabling mechanism for democratic oversight. By making project-specific data publicly available, authorities empower communities to scrutinize long-term sustainability strategies. This case demonstrates that open data is not merely about publishing information, but about ensuring that the data reflects genuine community interests and enables evidence-based dialogue on shifting urban transport policies toward safer, greener alternatives.
