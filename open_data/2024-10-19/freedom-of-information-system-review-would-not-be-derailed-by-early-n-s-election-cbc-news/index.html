Freedom of information system review would not be derailed by early N.S. election | CBC News

Freedom of information system review would not be derailed by early N.S. election | CBC News

The review of Nova Scotia’s Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act will proceed despite potential early elections, ensuring that civil servants maintain necessary policy work. This continuity highlights the importance of sustained administrative effort in democratic processes, even during political transitions, preventing legislative stagnation and ensuring public records management remains a priority regardless of electoral outcomes. The review aims to modernize an act last significantly updated in 1999, addressing gaps revealed by technological advances and previous recommendations from the Information and Privacy Commissioner. By engaging with various public bodies and considering external legislation, the government seeks to enhance transparency and accountability. This process is crucial for aligning provincial laws with current digital realities and ensuring that privacy protections remain robust and relevant in a rapidly evolving information landscape. Relevance to open data lies in the potential strengthening of the Commissioner’s order-making powers, which have long been promised but delayed by successive governments. Enhancing these powers could compel greater compliance with data access requests, significantly impacting how open data is managed and disclosed. Ultimately, this review represents a critical opportunity to enforce stricter data governance standards, ensuring that open data initiatives are not merely aspirational but legally enforceable mechanisms for public accountability.
