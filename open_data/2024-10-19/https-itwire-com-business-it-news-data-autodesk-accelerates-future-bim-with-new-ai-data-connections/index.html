Open Data
ES EN

Autodesk is driving the architecture and construction industry toward outcome-based Building Information Modeling by prioritizing open, granular, and accessible data as the foundational element for sustainable design. This strategic shift addresses critical industry challenges such as infrastructure deficits and construction waste, arguing that unified data enables AI-powered insights and informed decision-making. By establishing data accessibility as a core principle, Autodesk aims to unite project teams across the entire lifecycle, fostering environments where carbon neutrality and reduced waste are achievable through better digital collaboration rather than isolated efforts. The company is enhancing this data ecosystem by connecting its new industry cloud, Autodesk Forma, with Autodesk Docs, serving as a central repository for secure and organized information. This integration facilitates fluid workflows between planning and detailed design tools, allowing stakeholders to access the right data at the right time. Furthermore, the introduction of immersive design review tools and expanded geographic context from Esri ensures that teams can visualize and analyze projects within their real-world location. These advancements underscore the importance of interoperability and shared data standards, which are essential for seamless information exchange and preventing costly rework. AI capabilities are being embedded directly into these data-rich workflows to accelerate productivity and creativity. Features like embodied carbon analysis and natural language assistants allow users to extract insights from complex specification documents quickly, reducing risk and saving time. These tools empower designers to test sustainability decisions early in the process, leveraging real-world data to create more resilient communities. For the open data community, this highlights a growing industry trend where robust, interconnected data frameworks are not just technical requirements but vital drivers for achieving broader social and environmental outcomes in the built environment.
