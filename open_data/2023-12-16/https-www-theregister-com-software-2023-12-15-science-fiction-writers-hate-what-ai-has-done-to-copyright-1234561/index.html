Open Data
ES EN

The Science Fiction Writers Association argues that the current unauthorized scraping of copyrighted works for AI training exploits the community’s historical willingness to share art freely for human readers. They distinguish between making work accessible and relinquishing ownership rights, asserting that technology companies are unfairly capitalizing on creators’ generosity by ingesting material without consent or compensation. This imbalance undermines the moral and legal rights of authors, turning a culture of open sharing into a resource for corporate profit without fair return. The submission highlights that the removal of Digital Rights Management (DRM) to enhance reader convenience has inadvertently made authors more vulnerable to data scraping. By prioritizing user experience over restrictive protections, many publishers have inadvertently facilitated the easy extraction of their content. The Association warns that this technical accessibility is being weaponized against creators, who must now face the consequences of having their labor used to build commercial products that mimic their style while bypassing traditional licensing channels. To rectify this, the authors advocate for an opt-in regime where AI developers must negotiate and pay for access to copyrighted material, ensuring creators retain control over their work. This approach shifts the dynamic from exploitation to voluntary partnership, allowing stakeholders to reach a balanced agreement that respects intellectual property rights. This issue is critical to open data because it challenges the foundational assumption that publicly available information can be freely harvested for commercial purposes without attribution or remuneration, potentially setting a precedent that restricts open access if creators feel their rights are disregarded.
