Public raise concerns over RTI payments

The Right to Information Act faces significant accessibility barriers, as high fees and institutional secrecy hinder citizens from exercising their constitutional rights. Despite establishing numerous information units and processing thousands of requests, the implementation is marred by a culture of opacity and inadequate public record-keeping. These challenges disproportionately affect marginalized groups, creating inequalities that undermine the law’s democratic purpose. Regulatory delays and the slow transition to digital platforms further impede effective implementation, causing frustration among stakeholders who demand transparent governance. The government acknowledges that while progress has been made, systemic issues rooted in fear of embarrassment and poor infrastructure continue to obstruct the free flow of information. This stagnation limits the act’s potential to empower citizens and hold institutions accountable. This article is crucial for open data advocates because it highlights the gap between legal frameworks and practical accessibility. It underscores the urgent need for digital transformation and proactive disclosure to ensure public records are not only available but easily retrievable. Addressing these structural and cultural barriers is essential for realizing true transparency and ensuring that information rights are accessible to all, regardless of socioeconomic status.
