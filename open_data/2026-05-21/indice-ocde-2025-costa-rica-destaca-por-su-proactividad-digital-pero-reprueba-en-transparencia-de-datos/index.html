Índice OCDE 2025: Costa Rica destaca por su proactividad digital, pero reprueba en transparencia de datos

The article highlights that while digital government initiatives significantly improve service efficiency and cross-entity collaboration, the true challenge lies in the strategic management of data. The OECD’s latest indices reveal that leading nations view digitalization not merely as a technical upgrade, but as a fundamental asset for resilience and decision-making. This shift underscores the necessity of robust data governance and interoperable infrastructure to meet citizen expectations and support economic investment, marking a transition from isolated operations to integrated, intelligent public administration. Regarding open data, the report emphasizes a critical gap between digital service provision and the transparency required for societal benefit. Although many countries show progress in digitizing services, there is a pronounced lack of efforts to evaluate the real-world impact of open data policies. The findings suggest that simply publishing information is insufficient; governments must actively design policies that ensure data is useful and reusable. This focus on measurable impact is essential for fostering trust and enabling the public and private sectors to leverage information for innovation and accountability. This content is highly relevant to the open data community as it exposes the disparity between digital access and data utility, particularly in regions like Latin America where scores remain low. The article illustrates that without strong support for data reuse and improved accessibility, open data initiatives fail to deliver their promised social value. It serves as a call to action for policymakers and data advocates to prioritize not just availability, but the structural and legal frameworks that allow data to be effectively repurposed for the public good.
