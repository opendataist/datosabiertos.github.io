Mesas 900 mil en el Perú: de 495 a 4,703 en 20 años y cómo votaron estas zonas en la primera vuelta

The exponential growth of voting tables in isolated areas, designated with the 900 series, demonstrates the institutional effort to reduce the gap in access to voting in the country’s most remote regions. These spaces, historically concentrated in the highlands and the jungle, have evolved from a marginal measure to a structural component of the electoral system, reflecting geographic dispersion and the need to bring democracy closer to populations traditionally excluded due to lack of infrastructure. Analysis of electoral results reveals that these communities vote significantly differently from the national average, favoring grassroots candidates and penalizing political figures with weaker territorial roots. This divergence in electoral behavior underscores the importance of considering regional particularities when interpreting the popular will, demonstrating that participation in these isolated contexts can act as a democratic counterweight that substantially alters overall results. This report is relevant to the open data field because it shows how publishing detailed and accessible electoral information enables data journalism that dismantles homogeneous narratives about the country. By making available details on the location, request, and results of these voting tables, it facilitates citizen scrutiny that validates institutional transparency and helps understand the real dynamics of political participation, moving beyond national aggregates to highlight specific local realities.
