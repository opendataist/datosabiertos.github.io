Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significant structural disparities, exhibiting lower incomes and poorer living conditions regarding employment and health compared to the native population. Despite these material challenges and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness, they report equal satisfaction with life in Norway. This paradox highlights that objective socioeconomic status does not solely dictate subjective well-being, suggesting that non-material factors may buffer against adverse conditions. Crucially, immigrants demonstrate higher confidence in state institutions, such as the judiciary and police, and a strong affiliation with the country. This contrasts with lower interpersonal trust but underscores a robust civic integration into the political framework. Understanding these distinct layers of satisfaction and institutional trust provides a more nuanced view of immigrant integration than traditional economic metrics alone. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it illustrates the value of granular, longitudinal surveys in revealing complex social dynamics. By making such detailed living condition data publicly available, researchers and policymakers can move beyond simplistic aggregates. Open access to this information enables better analysis of why satisfied populations endure poor conditions, fostering more effective, evidence-based integration policies that address both material and psychosocial needs.
