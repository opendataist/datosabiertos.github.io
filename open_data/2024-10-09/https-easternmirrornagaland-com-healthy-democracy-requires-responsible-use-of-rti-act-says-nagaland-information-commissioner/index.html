The Nagaland Information Commissioner urges citizens to exercise the Right to Information Act responsibly, emphasizing that its true purpose is fostering government transparency and accountability rather than being misused for personal grievances. This guidance aims to strengthen democratic health by ensuring that the tool serves its primary objective of promoting ethical governance and public trust in state institutions. The article highlights the critical link between citizen awareness and the prevention of corruption. It stresses that many individuals fail to hold power holders accountable due to a lack of understanding regarding their rights. By actively disseminating information and understanding this dynamic, the public can effectively check abuses of power, ensuring that duty bearers remain answerable to the right holders they serve. This topic is vital to open data because it underscores the ethical and practical framework required for information accessibility. Open data initiatives rely on an informed populace that uses transparency tools correctly. The commissioner’s warning against harassment and obstruction reinforces that sustainable open data ecosystems must be built on mutual respect, where both seekers and providers navigate the system with integrity to maximize democratic benefits.
