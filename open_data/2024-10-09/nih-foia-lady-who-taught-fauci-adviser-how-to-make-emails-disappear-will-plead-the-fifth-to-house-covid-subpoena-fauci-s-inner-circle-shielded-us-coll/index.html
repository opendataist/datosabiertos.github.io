NIH ‘FOIA Lady’ Who Taught Fauci Adviser How to ‘make emails disappear’ Will Plead the Fifth to House COVID Subpoena; Fauci's Inner Circle Shielded US Collaborator At Wuhan Lab, and other C-Virus related stories

The article highlights severe concerns regarding transparency and accountability within the National Institutes of Health during the early pandemic. It details allegations that staff, including a senior FOIA liaison and an adviser to Dr. Fauci, allegedly instructed colleagues on how to evade federal record-keeping laws by deleting emails and using personal accounts. This potential obstruction of records suggests a systemic effort to conceal critical information, raising serious questions about the integrity of federal data management during a global health crisis. Furthermore, the report reveals that NIH officials protected the Wuhan Institute of Virology’s US collaborators, such as EcoHealth Alliance, despite requests from other government bodies for crucial scientific data. By continuing to provide millions in funding and offering political cover, these officials hindered efforts to trace the virus’s origins and assess the risks associated with gain-of-function research. This internal shielding delayed the collection of vital genomic data, suggesting that institutional priorities may have superseded public health transparency. These revelations are particularly relevant to the open data movement, as they illustrate how opaque bureaucratic practices can actively suppress public access to vital scientific information. The incident underscores the necessity of robust FOIA compliance and data retention policies to ensure that critical health data remains accessible for public scrutiny and independent research. Without strict adherence to open data standards, the potential for hidden agendas to obstruct pandemic response and understanding remains a significant risk.
