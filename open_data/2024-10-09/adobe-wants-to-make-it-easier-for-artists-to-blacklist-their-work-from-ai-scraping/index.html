Adobe wants to make it easier for artists to blacklist their work from AI scraping

Adobe wants to make it easier for artists to blacklist their work from AI scraping

Adobe’s launch of Content Authenticity aims to democratize C2PA credentials, enabling creators to cryptographically label content origins. This initiative seeks to empower artists by establishing a cultural standard for verifying provenance, similar to a digital signature, thereby fostering transparency in media consumption. The tool aligns with growing efforts to protect intellectual property from unauthorized AI training. By facilitating easier credential attachment, it supports a broader movement where creators can assert control over their work, challenging tech companies’ scraping practices without relying solely on technical poison attacks. This development is crucial for open data ecosystems, as it introduces standardized, machine-readable metadata for media assets. Such interoperable protocols enhance data integrity, allowing datasets to carry provenance information that supports ethical sourcing and clearer attribution in the rapidly evolving landscape of AI-generated and authentic media.
