Aquí no hay quien alquile

Aquí no hay quien alquile

The article explains how Spain’s housing crisis has decoupled rental prices from wage realities, disproportionately affecting major tourist areas. Data show that while general inflation and wages have grown moderately, rents have skyrocketed, making access to decent housing an unsustainable burden for a large segment of the population. This structural discrepancy creates a social urgency that goes beyond economic concerns, directly impacting quality of life and family stability. The human consequences are devastating: young people are forced to share rooms, families return to their parents’ homes after middle age, and individuals forgo basic health services to afford rent. Permanent instability not only entails frequent moves but also deteriorating living conditions and the inability to save for home purchase, creating a vicious cycle of residential exclusion. The market reflects a prioritization of tourist and speculative profits over the basic right to housing. This analysis is crucial for open data because it illustrates how transparency in public information can reveal hidden inequalities. Comparing official data with private portals makes it possible to visualize the geographic concentration of the crisis and the gap between actual supply and affordability. By making these patterns visible, open data empower citizens and public officials to demand evidence-based policies, promoting structural solutions to the housing emergency.
