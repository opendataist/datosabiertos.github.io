Adopted Parenthood

The article highlights a significant shift in adoption practices in Kashmir, driven by declining fertility rates and increased social pressure on childless couples. Historically restricted to local guardianship under state laws, adoption has now been legalized and centralized under India’s CARA framework following the revocation of Article 370. This change transforms adoption from a regional custodial arrangement into a formal, legal parenthood process accessible through a national, online platform. The introduction of this centralized digital system aims to streamline procedures, increase transparency, and eliminate the need for prospective parents to navigate complex local bureaucracies. By connecting district-level monitoring units with a national database, the new system allows parents to view profiles of legally available children and track applications electronically. This digital integration theoretically reduces delays and provides a clearer pathway for couples who have exhausted medical fertility treatments and face societal stigma. This case is relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the impact of centralizing public welfare records into a unified, accessible digital infrastructure. The CARINGS portal exemplifies how open, standardized data platforms can modernize social services, ensuring equitable access and traceability across regional boundaries. However, the transition also raises questions about data privacy and the balance between administrative efficiency and the preservation of local community structures in sensitive demographic transitions.
