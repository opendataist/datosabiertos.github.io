Inegi señala que el mercado informal aportó el 24% del PIB

The article reveals that Mexico’s informal economy reached a twelve-year high in 2021, contributing significantly more to GDP than previously measured. This surge reflects a substantial portion of the workforce operating without basic legal registration, highlighting deep structural vulnerabilities in the labor market. These figures indicate that informal sectors are not just surviving but actively driving key economic segments, challenging standard growth metrics. The revision of the national accounting baseline underscores how data collection methods directly shape our understanding of economic health. By updating the reference year, statistical agencies captured a broader reality of unregistered micro-businesses and subsistence agriculture. This adjustment demonstrates that official statistics can underestimate economic activity when relying on outdated frameworks, thereby distorting policy responses and economic assessments. This finding is crucial for open data because it illustrates the necessity of transparent, frequently updated methodologies in national statistics. Open access to these revised datasets allows researchers and citizens to scrutinize how economic contributions are calculated, ensuring accountability. It emphasizes that accurate public policy requires not just raw numbers, but clear explanations of methodological changes that significantly alter the perceived size and nature of the economy.
