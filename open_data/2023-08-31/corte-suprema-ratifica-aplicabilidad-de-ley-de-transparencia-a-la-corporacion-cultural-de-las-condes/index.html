Corte Suprema ratifica aplicabilidad de Ley de Transparencia a la Corporación Cultural de Las Condes

The Chilean Supreme Court ruled that municipal corporations must comply with transparency laws, rejecting their attempt to evade oversight. This decision confirms that public funds cannot remain unaccounted for, regardless of independent entities created to circumvent scrutiny. Consequently, these organizations are now subject to control by the Transparency Council, ensuring they provide requested financial information or face investigation. This ruling definitively settles the issue, establishing that all municipal corporations are subject to open data requirements. It prevents legal loopholes that might shield public spending from scrutiny. The Supreme Court’s criterion reinforces the principle that common sense must guide governance, ensuring accountability in public administration. This development is highly relevant to open data, as it expands the scope of transparency obligations. It mandates greater accessibility of public financial data from municipal entities. By closing loopholes, it strengthens the fight against corruption. Furthermore, it aligns with broader national initiatives aiming to improve transparency in foundations and corporations, promoting a more open and accountable public sector.
