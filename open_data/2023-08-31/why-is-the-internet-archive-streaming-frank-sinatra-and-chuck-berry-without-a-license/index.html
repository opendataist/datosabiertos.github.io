Why is the Internet Archive Streaming Frank Sinatra and Chuck Berry Without a License?

The article highlights a critical tension between the Internet Archive’s mission of providing universal access to knowledge and existing copyright frameworks, specifically through its "Great 78s" project. Major music labels have sued the organization, arguing that streaming digitized versions of historic recordings infringes on rights, particularly for pre-1972 works now under federal protection. This legal conflict underscores the difficulty of balancing public interest in preserving cultural heritage with the legal and economic interests of rights holders, challenging the notion that digital accessibility automatically overrides intellectual property laws. Relevance to open data is profound, as this case tests the limits of "fair use" for archival institutions and public libraries. It demonstrates that simply owning a physical copy does not grant the right to create and distribute digital copies to a global audience. For open data advocates, this establishes a crucial precedent that digital preservation and access must strictly adhere to statutory exceptions, such as those for non-commercial preservation, rather than broad interpretations of fair use. The outcome will define whether digital archives can operate with the same flexibility as traditional libraries or if they face significant legal barriers to sharing data. Ultimately, the dispute suggests the Internet Archive may be pushing the boundaries of copyright law to effect systemic change, rather than strictly following established preservation protocols. This has significant implications for how open data projects handle copyrighted material, warning that aggressive expansion of access without legal compliance can lead to severe financial penalties and restrictive case law. It reinforces the need for open data initiatives to navigate legal landscapes carefully, ensuring that the pursuit of knowledge accessibility does not undermine the foundational rights of creators, which are essential for sustaining the production of data and creative works.
