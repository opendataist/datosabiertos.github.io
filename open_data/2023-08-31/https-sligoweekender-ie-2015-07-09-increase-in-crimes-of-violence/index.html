Crime statistics in the Sligo-Leitrim division reveal a complex picture of increasing violent offenses, including sex offenses, assaults, and robberies, alongside rising property damage and drink-driving incidents. Conversely, traditional thefts, fraud, and drug-related crimes have declined, suggesting a shift in criminal activity patterns rather than a uniform rise in overall crime. These conflicting trends highlight the need for nuanced analysis when interpreting raw police data to understand true public safety concerns. The resumption of official crime figures follows a significant pause caused by irregularities in how gardai recorded data on the PULSE system. A review confirmed that a substantial percentage of reported crimes were either missing from records, misclassified, or improperly categorized as non-crimes. This admission underscores the critical importance of data integrity in public safety metrics, as flawed recording methods can severely distort the perceived reality of crime rates and hinder accurate resource allocation. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how poor data governance and classification errors can undermine the reliability of public datasets. It serves as a cautionary example that publishing open statistics requires rigorous validation processes to ensure accuracy and consistency. Without addressing underlying structural issues in data collection and management, open data initiatives risk disseminating misleading information, thereby eroding public trust and limiting the utility of the data for researchers and policymakers.
