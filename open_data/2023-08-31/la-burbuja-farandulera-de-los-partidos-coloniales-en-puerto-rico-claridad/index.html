La burbuja farandulera de los partidos coloniales en Puerto Rico - Claridad

The article highlights an accelerating global climate crisis, characterized by unprecedented heat records and intensified extreme weather events. It argues that current environmental degradation is happening faster than anticipated, creating a vicious cycle where fossil fuel consumption drives warming, which in turn fuels more frequent and severe disasters. This urgent situation demands immediate adaptation strategies, yet many vulnerable populations, particularly in Puerto Rico, face a dilemma where protecting themselves from heat exacerbates their economic poverty. Relevance to open data lies in the critical need for transparent, accessible, and actionable climate information to support public safety and policy reform. Reliable data is essential for validating scientific warnings about extreme weather and for holding governments accountable for their lack of engagement in climate action. Open data platforms can bridge the gap between complex scientific forecasts and community needs, enabling citizens to understand risks and advocate for evidence-based environmental justice and democratic governance reforms. Ultimately, the piece criticizes political elites for operating in isolation, ignoring the urgent need for structural changes in energy and transport. It calls for a new national project grounded in transparency, professional public service, and global awareness. By making climate data and governance processes open, society can foster the collective awareness necessary to combat both the environmental catastrophe and the social inequities it disproportionately affects.
