Cuántos cádaveres llegaron a los anfiteatros en 2022, según el Inegi

The article highlights a significant challenge in Mexico’s forensic system: despite receiving over 130,000 bodies, nearly 38% remain unidentified, indicating systemic gaps in identification processes and resource allocation. This data reveals a critical need for improved forensic infrastructure and standardized procedures to ensure timely identification and closure for families. Relevant to open data, this statistical report demonstrates the importance of transparent, government-published datasets for monitoring public service efficiency. By making granular details on identification rates, storage capacity, and personnel distribution publicly available, citizens and researchers can analyze trends, hold institutions accountable, and advocate for evidence-based policy reforms in criminal justice and public health. The breakdown of resources by state and institution provides a clear picture of regional disparities in forensic capabilities. Such detailed, disaggregated data is essential for understanding where bottlenecks occur, such as in storage or laboratory accreditation, allowing for targeted interventions. Ultimately, the article underscores how accessible, high-quality public statistics are fundamental for diagnosing and solving complex societal issues.
