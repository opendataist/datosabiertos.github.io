The Azad Jammu and Kashmir High Court has accepted a critical petition seeking the enforcement of the Right to Information, a fundamental right embedded in the region’s constitutional amendments. By issuing notices to top government officials, the court acknowledges the urgent need to operationalize transparency mechanisms that have long been neglected. This legal move marks a significant step toward aligning regional governance with established constitutional mandates and international human rights standards regarding free expression and public accountability. The petition highlights that despite the constitutional declaration of information access as a fundamental right, the government has failed to enact necessary legislation or establish the required Information Commission and Public Information Officers. This legislative gap prevents journalists, lawyers, and citizens from legally obtaining data, thereby hindering transparency and fostering an environment where corruption can thrive. The lack of structured information dissemination mechanisms leaves the public uninformed and undermines the professional capabilities of media practitioners who rely on accurate data for their work. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates as it underscores the necessity of robust legal frameworks to support data accessibility and reuse. Without enforceable rights and dedicated oversight bodies, government data remains siloed and inaccessible, preventing its potential for public benefit. Ensuring the implementation of an Information Act not only empowers citizens to hold authorities accountable but also facilitates the open sharing of information, which is essential for fostering a transparent, informed, and democratic society.
