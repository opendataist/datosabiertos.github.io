Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

This report highlights a significant paradox in Norwegian society where immigrants face substantially poorer living conditions compared to the native population. Despite enduring challenges in employment, housing, and income, as well as higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness, immigrants report satisfaction levels and institutional trust comparable to or exceeding those of the general population. This suggests that subjective well-being does not always correlate directly with objective socioeconomic status. The findings are particularly relevant to open data advocates because they demonstrate the value of transparent, standardized statistics in revealing complex social dynamics that might otherwise remain obscured. By making this comprehensive survey data publicly available, researchers and policymakers can move beyond simplistic narratives about immigrant integration. The data allows for a nuanced understanding of how different demographic groups experience society, highlighting specific areas like mental health and loneliness that require targeted policy interventions. Furthermore, the high trust immigrants place in national institutions, despite their disadvantaged position, challenges assumptions about social alienation. This openness in data sharing fosters informed public discourse and supports evidence-based decision-making. Ultimately, the report underscores how accessible, high-quality open data is essential for identifying societal gaps and promoting equitable outcomes, ensuring that marginalized voices are represented in the broader conversation about national well-being and social cohesion.
