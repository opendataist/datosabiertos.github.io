De la gestión de los asuntos públicos al gobierno abierto: nuestro propósito

The text argues that citizen participation is a fundamental pillar of democracy, supported by international human rights, and that its modern exercise has been transformed through the concept of open government. This model seeks to replace isolated bureaucratic administration with an integrated networked structure, where co-innovation with civil society and access to previously restricted resources are central. The essence of this new approach lies in three interconnected principles: transparency through the opening of public data, active citizen participation in policy formulation, and the creation of collaborative spaces among the state, civil society, and the private sector. The goal is to overcome conceptual and operational challenges through dialogue, combining societal experience with institutional knowledge to address public problems more effectively. This article is relevant to open data because it positions the opening of information not as a technical end in itself, but as an essential mechanism for social oversight, accountability, and the economic reuse of data. By facilitating access to public information, it enables citizens to genuinely participate in governance, transforming open data into a tool for civic empowerment and improved government quality.
