Votará apenas 1 % fuera de Venezuela

Votará apenas 1 % fuera de Venezuela

The article highlights significant barriers faced by Venezuelan exiles in accessing electoral rights, illustrating how political exclusion creates data opacity. The short registration window and bureaucratic hurdles have effectively disenfranchised millions of refugees, raising serious concerns about the integrity and representativeness of the election. This situation underscores the critical need for transparent, accessible voter registries that accurately reflect the full population, including those displaced by crisis. Open data initiatives are vital for exposing such systemic gaps and ensuring that marginalized groups are not rendered invisible in public records. By analyzing discrepancies between refugee statistics and actual voter turnout, researchers can identify discriminatory practices and advocate for more inclusive policy-making. Reliable, open datasets empower civil society to monitor electoral fairness and hold governments accountable for equitable representation. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how the lack of standardized, publicly available information facilitates political manipulation. Ensuring that voter registration data is complete, transparent, and freely accessible allows for independent verification and promotes democratic accountability. Ultimately, open data serves as a tool to bridge the gap between official narratives and the lived realities of excluded populations, fostering greater trust in democratic processes.
