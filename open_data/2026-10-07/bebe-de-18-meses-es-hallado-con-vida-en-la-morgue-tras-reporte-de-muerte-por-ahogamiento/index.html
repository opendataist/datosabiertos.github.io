Bebé de 18 meses es hallado con vida en la morgue tras reporte de muerte por ahogamiento

An 18-month-old baby was rescued alive after being mistakenly declared dead at a Phoenix hospital. Although police officers observed vital signs before transporting the child to the morgue, medical staff declared him deceased. This serious incident has prompted a thorough review of hospital protocols and the recommendation of negligence charges against the parents, highlighting critical failures in the chain of care and communication during the emergency. The relevance of this case to open data lies in the need for transparency and public access to information about errors within the healthcare system. Police records and official statements enable auditing of the chain of command and clinical decisions. By making this data accessible, society can identify structural deficiencies, demand accountability from medical institutions, and promote evidence-based reforms rather than relying solely on partial or confidential corporate reports. Although such failures are rare, they reveal that the determination of death depends largely on staff training and institutional policies, rather than on death itself as an irreversible phenomenon. Comparisons with similar cases highlight variability in care standards. Therefore, the open dissemination of this data is crucial for educating the public, improving patient safety, and preventing human or procedural errors from having fatal consequences in the future.
