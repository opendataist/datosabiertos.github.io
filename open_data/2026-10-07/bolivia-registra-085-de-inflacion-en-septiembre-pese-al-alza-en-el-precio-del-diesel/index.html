Bolivia registra 0,85% de inflación en septiembre pese al alza en el precio del diésel

Bolivia registra 0,85% de inflación en septiembre pese al alza en el precio del diésel

Bolivia has undergone a significant economic transition following the elimination of diesel subsidies, a measure that has directly impacted September’s inflation, sparking complaints about rising transportation and basic service costs. Although the monthly increase was moderate, it affects multiple sectors such as food and health, reflecting widespread pressure on consumer prices beyond fuel. This adjustment is part of a package of government reforms aimed at addressing the economic crisis inherited from years of high inflation, seeking to stabilize the economy through exchange rate flexibility and fiscal adjustment. The relevance of this article to open data lies in the transparency and accessibility of official economic indicators. Institutions such as the National Institute of Statistics (INE) and the Central Bank of Bolivia (BCB) publish detailed data on price variations, regional breakdowns, and inflation projections. This information is crucial for citizens, researchers, and analysts to monitor the real impact of public policies, evaluate the effectiveness of austerity measures, and promote accountability through the analysis of macroeconomic trends. The contrast between current inflation and historical records of hyperinflation underscores the importance of monitoring these variables to prevent dangerous scenarios. The availability of data disaggregated by city and category enables the identification of regional inequalities in economic impact, which is essential for designing targeted social policies. By making these indicators public, an informed society is fostered, one capable of actively participating in public debate on the sustainability of structural reforms and their effect on citizens’ quality of life.
