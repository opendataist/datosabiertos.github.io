The INAI has established strict guidelines to protect consumers’ privacy during the Hot Sale, emphasizing that the intensified use of personal data in online shopping carries significant risks if not properly managed. The central recommendation is transparency and user control: consumers should demand the presentation of the Privacy Notice, understand their rights regarding their information, and ensure that only the data strictly necessary for the transaction is provided, rejecting any excessive or unjustified requests. To mitigate the risks of fraud and misuse, the implementation of technical security measures during browsing is emphasized. It is crucial to avoid public WiFi networks or to use a virtual private network (VPN), verify the authenticity of the website through security seals, and keep devices up to date. Additionally, consumers are advised to disable default marketing options and actively monitor their bank transactions, ensuring that the identity of the selling company is clear before sharing any financial information. This article is relevant to the field of open data because it illustrates the tension between facilitating digital commerce and protecting privacy. Although the focus is on individual security, it underscores the need for robust regulatory frameworks that guarantee the integrity and confidentiality of personal data—a fundamental principle also applicable to the ethical and secure handling of any dataset involving individuals’ sensitive information.
