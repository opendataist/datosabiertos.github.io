Pobreza en Costa Rica cae a niveles no vistos desde 2009

Costa Rica has reached its lowest poverty levels in fifteen years, marking a significant socioeconomic shift as the poverty rate falls below the historical twenty percent threshold. This reduction indicates that tens of thousands of families have successfully escaped poverty, reflecting improvements across both urban and rural areas. The decline in extreme poverty further underscores the broadening of economic stability, suggesting a tangible positive trajectory for the nation’s social indicators. The government attributes this progress to its targeted policy decisions, viewing the data as validation of its strategy to prioritize the most vulnerable populations. However, economic experts and citizens question the depth of this improvement, noting that macroeconomic gains may not align with daily realities. Critics argue that growth driven by special economic zones might not benefit the poorest groups, and rising living costs continue to strain household budgets despite favorable aggregate statistics. This report is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical importance of transparency and methodological clarity in official statistics. While headline numbers show improvement, the lack of granular detail regarding specific economic activities fosters public skepticism. Reliable open data requires accessible, detailed breakdowns that allow independent verification, ensuring that reported social progress accurately reflects the lived experiences of the population rather than just aggregate economic trends.
