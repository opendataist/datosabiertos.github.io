Ni Juan ni María: este el nombre más común en Baleares entre los milennials

The article highlights a significant cultural shift in naming preferences within the Balearic Islands during the 1980s and 1990s, marking a transition from traditional to modern identities. This period reflects broader societal changes influenced by globalization and the democratization of Spanish culture, where families began seeking names that signaled a break from historical conventions. The emergence of contemporary, internationalized names for both boys and girls demonstrates a move away from locally dominant traditional choices. This evolution illustrates how demographic trends and cultural openness reshaped regional identity, favoring names perceived as fresh and globally connected rather than rooted solely in local heritage. This data is relevant to open data because it exemplifies how public demographic records can reveal nuanced social trends over time. By analyzing freely available statistical data, we can track cultural evolution and understand the interplay between policy, globalization, and individual choices, showcasing the power of open datasets to contextualize human behavior beyond mere statistics.
