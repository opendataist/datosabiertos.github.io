Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions than the native population, including lower incomes, substandard housing, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Despite these tangible disadvantages, immigrants report satisfaction levels, institutional trust, and national affiliation that are comparable to or even higher than those of the general populace. This paradox suggests that well-being metrics cannot solely be determined by objective socioeconomic status, highlighting the complex interplay between structural barriers and subjective resilience among immigrant communities. This study is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates the value of disaggregated data in revealing nuanced social realities that aggregate statistics often obscure. By providing comprehensive, breakdown-specific insights into health, trust, and living standards, such datasets enable policymakers to identify vulnerable subgroups within broader demographic categories. This granularity supports more targeted and effective interventions, ensuring that resources address the specific needs of marginalized groups rather than relying on misleading averages. Furthermore, the findings emphasize the importance of including subjective well-being measures alongside objective indicators in public datasets. The disconnect between poor living conditions and high satisfaction challenges conventional assumptions about immigrant integration and highlights the need for multidimensional data frameworks. Open access to such detailed socio-economic and psychological data fosters greater transparency, allowing researchers and civil society to better understand the lived experiences of immigrants and advocate for policies that enhance both material conditions and social cohesion.
