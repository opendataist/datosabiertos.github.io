Empleo en Panamá ¿tocamos fondo?

Empleo en Panamá ¿tocamos fondo?

The article highlights a structural crisis in Panama’s economy, characterized by a significant rise in unemployment and informal labor between 2012 and 2024. Despite this deterioration in formal job markets, the public sector expanded rapidly, financed by accumulating substantial external debt. This shift has created an artificial economy where benefits became rights, leading to a situation where debt servicing costs now exceed revenue from the Panama Canal, indicating severe fiscal unsustainability driven by reliance on borrowed funds rather than productive growth. Relevant to open data, this narrative underscores the critical need for transparent, timely, and detailed statistical records from national institutes like the INEC. The analysis relies heavily on comparing employment figures, debt metrics, and consumption indices to reveal hidden economic imbalances, such as the disproportionate growth of informal jobs versus formal ones. Without accessible, granular data, it is impossible to accurately assess the true impact of policy decisions, verify the sustainability of public spending, or understand how external shocks have reshaped the labor market over more than a decade. The conclusion points to a potential turning point, signaled by recent increases in private credit and a decrease in risk premiums. However, the author cautions that these are early indicators rather than confirmed trends. The relevance to open data lies in the necessity of continuous monitoring; only through the ongoing publication and analysis of reliable economic indicators can policymakers and citizens distinguish between temporary recovery and a genuine, sustainable shift away from the previous cycle of debt-fueled, informal-driven expansion.
