25N: 52 de cada 100 mujeres de 15 a 49 años alguna vez fueron víctimas de violencia

The article presents alarming data on violence against women in Peru, highlighting that more than half have experienced abuse by a partner. It underscores a critical disconnect between the prevalence of violence and the lack of support, as nearly half of the victims do not seek help, often due to perceptions that the violence is unnecessary or social stigma. This low reporting rate contrasts with a high level of societal tolerance, where a significant majority of the population justifies such violence, indicating deep-rooted cultural issues that perpetuate the cycle of abuse. The relevance of this report to open data lies in its use of large-scale, official surveys like ENDES and ENARES to quantify social phenomena that are often hidden or underreported. By making this statistical information publicly available, open data initiatives enable researchers, policymakers, and civil society to analyze trends, identify at-risk populations, and hold institutions accountable. Transparency in these datasets is essential for moving beyond anecdotal evidence to evidence-based strategies for prevention and victim support. Ultimately, the main conclusion is that addressing gender-based violence in Peru requires more than legal reforms; it demands a fundamental shift in social attitudes and improved access to support services. The data reveals that while physical aggression is common, the barriers to seeking help—ranging from fear to indifference—are systemic. Open data serves as a vital tool to visualize these disparities and drive the necessary structural changes to protect women and reduce the high rates of violence and femicides documented in the country.
