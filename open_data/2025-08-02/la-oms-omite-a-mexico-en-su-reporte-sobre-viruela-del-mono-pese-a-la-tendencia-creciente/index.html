La OMS omite a México en su reporte sobre viruela del mono pese a la tendencia creciente

La OMS omite a México en su reporte sobre viruela del mono pese a la tendencia creciente

The article highlights a serious gap in health data transparency, pointing out that Mexico’s omission from recent World Health Organization (WHO) reports contrasts with an alarming trend of rising case numbers and a disproportionately high mortality rate in the region. This lack of international coordination not only hinders global epidemiological control but also obscures the true scale of the local outbreak, creating risks of virus exportation and potential mobility restrictions in the context of global events such as the 2026 World Cup. From a data science perspective, this incident underscores the critical importance of the availability, accessibility, and comparability of open health data. The discrepancy between national public information and international standards reveals how the failure to publish timely and standardized data can distort the real understanding of a health crisis, undermining evidence-based response capacity and perpetuating inequalities in the protection of vulnerable populations. The relevance to the open data movement lies in the urgency of establishing robust information-sharing systems that ensure government transparency. Without accurate and accessible data, authorities cannot implement effective vaccination strategies or adequately protect at-risk groups, highlighting that access to information is an essential social determinant of health for ensuring equity and global health security.
