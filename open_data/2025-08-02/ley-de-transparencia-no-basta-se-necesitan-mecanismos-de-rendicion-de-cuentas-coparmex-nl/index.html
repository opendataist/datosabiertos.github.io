Ley de Transparencia no basta, se necesitan mecanismos de rendición de cuentas: Coparmex NL

Ley de Transparencia no basta, se necesitan mecanismos de rendición de cuentas: Coparmex NL

The article argues that mere compliance with formal transparency laws is insufficient; instead, genuine accountability and an active civic culture are essential to combat corruption. This perspective highlights a critical shift in open data governance, where the mere release of information is not enough. True transparency requires accessible procedures and firm sanctions to ensure that data serves as a transformative tool for public life, fostering trust rather than just bureaucratic compliance. Furthermore, the text emphasizes the need to align local regulations with international standards, ensuring that data systems are robust enough to allow for the evaluation of public spending effectiveness. There is a strong warning against maintaining opacity, particularly concerning sensitive areas like human rights violations and government expenditures. By measuring the impact of public resources and ensuring media access to critical files, governments can prevent waste and uphold democratic integrity, treating public information as a shared asset rather than a restricted commodity. This discussion is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the importance of quality, utility, and accessibility over simple data publication. It advocates for policies that promote interoperability, standardization, and meaningful engagement with civil society. By prioritizing the practical application of information to verify government actions and protect human rights, open data becomes a vital mechanism for accountability, ensuring that digital openness translates into tangible improvements in governance and public trust.
