La brecha educativa entre extranjeros y españoles se acentúa en Baleares por el ‘boom’ migratorio

La brecha educativa entre extranjeros y españoles se acentúa en Baleares por el ‘boom’ migratorio

The data reveals a significant educational gap in the Islands, driven by recent economic migration from South America and Africa. This influx has drastically lowered the average schooling levels among foreigners compared to the local population, who retain higher rates of university and secondary education. This disparity highlights how migration types directly shape demographic profiles. Unlike leisure-focused tourism residents, economic migrants often arrive with lower educational backgrounds, creating distinct social strata that challenge local integration models. This case is relevant to open data as it illustrates the critical need for disaggregated, granular datasets. Understanding these specific nuances allows policymakers to design targeted social interventions rather than relying on aggregate statistics that obscure such vital regional inequalities.
