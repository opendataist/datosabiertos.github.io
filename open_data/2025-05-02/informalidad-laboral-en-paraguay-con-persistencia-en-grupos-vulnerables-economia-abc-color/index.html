Informalidad laboral en Paraguay con persistencia en grupos vulnerables - Economía - ABC Color

Informalidad laboral en Paraguay con persistencia en grupos vulnerables - Economía - ABC Color

The analysis reveals that labor informality in Paraguay persists as a structural problem, with more than six out of every ten workers in precarious conditions. Although there has been a slight annual improvement, the recent growth in the informal employed population, particularly in urban areas, indicates that this phenomenon is expanding. This trend underscores the fragility of the labor market and the critical dependence on unregulated sectors for job creation, challenging current formalization efforts. Inequalities are clearly manifested in demographic and economic distribution. Informality disproportionately affects young people, who face barriers to entering the formal labor market, and older adults, who rely on informal work due to a lack of alternatives or resources. Furthermore, there is a direct inverse correlation between income and precariousness; workers with low salaries are significantly more likely to lack social protection. This demonstrates that informality is not merely a labor issue but a symptom of structural inequality that primarily affects the most vulnerable sectors. This report is relevant to the open data movement because it illustrates how statistical transparency is crucial for identifying systemic inequities. By making these disaggregated data publicly available by age, geography, and income, citizen scrutiny and evidence-based policy advocacy are enabled. Access to accurate information allows civil society and policymakers to design targeted interventions that address the root causes of labor exclusion, promoting fairer and more efficient governance.
