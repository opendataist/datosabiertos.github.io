Fuerte caída del empleo público nacional: 35 mil trabajadores menos en un año

Public sector employment in Argentina has declined significantly, marking a substantial reduction in the national administrative workforce compared to the previous year. This contraction is driven by a combination of frozen vacancies, early retirements, and non-renewed contracts, reflecting a deliberate policy strategy aimed at reducing state expenditure. The data indicates that this downward trend is consistent across various ministries and state-owned enterprises, suggesting a systemic approach to downsizing the government apparatus. The adjustments are particularly evident in centralized administration and specific key ministries, highlighting the intensity of the current government’s fiscal consolidation efforts. Even excluding entities with reporting delays, the consistent decrease in personnel across different sectors underscores the breadth of these cuts. This widespread reduction points to a structural shift in public sector staffing, rather than isolated organizational changes, as the administration continues to implement measures to shrink its operational footprint. This information is crucial for the open data community as it demonstrates how official statistical institutions provide transparent, time-series evidence of policy impacts. By making employment metrics publicly available, these datasets allow researchers, journalists, and citizens to independently verify government claims regarding fiscal efficiency and administrative restructuring. Such transparency ensures accountability and supports informed public debate on the long-term implications of reducing the state workforce on service delivery and economic stability.
