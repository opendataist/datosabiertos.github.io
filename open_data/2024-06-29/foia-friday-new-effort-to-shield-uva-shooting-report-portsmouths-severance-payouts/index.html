FOIA Friday: New effort to shield UVA shooting report, Portsmouth’s severance payouts

FOIA Friday: New effort to shield UVA shooting report, Portsmouth’s severance payouts

The article highlights a persistent gap between Virginia’s legal presumption of public records and the common practice of officials treating government data as private by default. Despite the Virginia Freedom of Information Act, leaders frequently resist transparency, forcing the public to prove that information should be released rather than officials justifying secrecy. This systemic opacity undermines democratic accountability and suggests that the spirit of open governance is often ignored in favor of administrative convenience or political protection. Specific cases illustrate how this resistance impacts critical public interests. In Charlottesville, efforts to release a costly investigative report into a university shooting were delayed, with prosecutors arguing that criminal proceedings outweighed public right to know. Meanwhile, in Portsmouth, open records requests revealed nearly one million dollars in severance payouts to departing top officials, exposing financial practices that might otherwise have remained hidden. These examples demonstrate how withholding information can obscure both the handling of sensitive legal matters and significant fiscal decisions. Furthermore, the struggle for access to body camera footage in Chesterfield reveals how resistance to transparency can distort public understanding of police conduct. When video showing a mentally ill man being shot while backing away from officers was finally obtained through federal court documents, it contradicted police statements that the victim was advancing. This disparity underscores the vital role of open data in verifying official narratives, ensuring that citizens can accurately assess government actions and hold authorities accountable for their conduct.
