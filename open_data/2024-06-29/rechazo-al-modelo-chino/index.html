Rechazo al modelo chino

Rechazo al modelo chino

The article underscores the critical need to balance the benefits of state-collected biometric data against the risks of its misuse. While accurate data can significantly enhance public health responses and disaster management, the potential for authoritarian surveillance poses a severe threat to democratic societies. This duality necessitates a careful distinction between legitimate utility and harmful exploitation of citizens' information. A central concern is the institutional framework governing these data assets, particularly in Mexico. Debate centers on whether autonomous bodies like the INE, with their technical expertise, should manage biometric records or if new safeguards are required. There is genuine public anxiety that the incoming administration might exploit privileged information to harass dissenters, given recent government conduct. This fear highlights the urgency of establishing robust legal and institutional limits to prevent a slide toward authoritarianism. This discussion is highly relevant to open data, as it underscores the importance of transparency, accountability, and ethical governance in data management. Open data principles advocate for responsible stewardship, ensuring that information serves the public good without compromising privacy or civil liberties. The debate illustrates how open data infrastructure must be paired with strong democratic checks to ensure that technology enhances, rather than erodes, individual rights and societal trust.
