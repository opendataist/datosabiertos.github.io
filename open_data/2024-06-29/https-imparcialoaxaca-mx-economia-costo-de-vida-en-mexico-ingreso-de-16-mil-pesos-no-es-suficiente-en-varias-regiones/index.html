The 2023 Ensafi study reveals significant regional disparities in Mexico’s cost of living, demonstrating that a uniform national income standard fails to account for local economic realities. This data highlights how essential geographic variables directly impact household financial stability, challenging the validity of generic national averages in assessing economic well-being. Gender and location emerge as critical determinants of financial needs, with men and residents of high-cost metropolitan areas requiring substantially higher incomes to maintain a dignified lifestyle. The concentration of elevated expenses in specific states, driven by housing and inflation, underscores the complex interplay between infrastructure demand and personal financial health. This article is relevant to open data because it exemplifies the necessity of granular, disaggregated datasets to drive effective policy. Relying on aggregated figures obscures critical vulnerabilities, whereas accessible, detailed regional statistics empower stakeholders to identify precise gaps in social protection and design targeted, equitable financial inclusion strategies.
