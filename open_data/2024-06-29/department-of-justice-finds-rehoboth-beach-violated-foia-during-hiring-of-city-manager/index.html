Department of Justice finds Rehoboth Beach violated FOIA during hiring of city manager

The Delaware Department of Justice determined that the City of Rehoboth Beach violated open meeting laws during the hiring of its new City Manager. By excluding public discussion from negotiations regarding the official’s unexpectedly high compensation package, the local government failed to adhere to transparency standards. This ruling highlights the critical importance of maintaining accessible governmental processes, ensuring that significant fiscal decisions impacting public funds remain visible and subject to community scrutiny rather than being conducted behind closed doors. The investigation reveals that proper procedures, including adequate notice and opportunities for public comment, were bypassed in favor of private executive sessions. This breach underscores a common vulnerability in local governance where administrative efficiency can overshadow legal mandates for openness. For open data advocates, this case serves as a stark reminder that true transparency requires not just the availability of information, but the active inclusion of the public in the decision-making narrative, particularly when substantial resources are involved. Although the city leadership expressed disappointment, they acknowledged the need to improve their commitment to openness and agreed to follow the attorney general’s recommendations. They plan to hold a compliant meeting to ratify their previous actions, thereby restoring public trust. This outcome reinforces the necessity of robust open data frameworks that facilitate immediate, accurate access to governmental proceedings. It demonstrates how legal mechanisms can enforce transparency, ensuring that citizens retain the power to influence decisions made on their behalf.
