¿Cusco más peligroso que el Callao? Autoridades rechazan estadísticas de INEI sobre seguridad ciudadana | Inforegión

¿Cusco más peligroso que el Callao? Autoridades rechazan estadísticas de INEI sobre seguridad ciudadana | Inforegión

The Mayor of Cusco and local police authorities strongly reject the latest INEI report that classifies their city as highly insecure, arguing that the statistics distort the actual safety conditions. They contend that the data lacks current relevance and question the survey methodology, asserting that citizens can walk the streets calmly and without fear. This dispute highlights a significant disconnect between official metrics and the perceived reality on the ground, prompting requests for detailed explanations from the statistical institute. The disagreement stems from contrasting views on security perception versus reported indices, with officials emphasizing their efforts to improve police presence despite resource limitations. By challenging the validity of these national surveys, local leaders aim to protect their city’s reputation and ensure that policy decisions are based on accurate, locally reflective information rather than potentially flawed aggregate data. This incident is crucial for open data advocates as it illustrates the importance of transparency in statistical methodologies and the necessity of verifying data sources. It underscores how opaque or contested datasets can impact public trust and local governance, highlighting the need for clear, accessible, and verifiable public data to facilitate informed debate and effective policy-making in civic security matters.
