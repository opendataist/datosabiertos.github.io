When the data center comes to your town | Diario Sur

When the data center comes to your town | Diario Sur

The article describes the conflict between local governments in Aragon and the regional government, which is driving a massive deployment of data centers for artificial intelligence. Through the General Interest Plan mechanism, the autonomous community centralizes urban planning powers, enabling expropriations and tax exemptions that undermine municipal sovereignty and deprive town councils of essential revenues, thereby generating a sense of powerlessness and exclusion among affected communities. The socio-environmental impacts are the subject of intense debate, as these facilities require vast amounts of energy and water, threatening local sustainability and food security by occupying agricultural land. Moreover, the promise of job creation proves insufficient, since construction is temporary and operations require few workers, while residents face risks such as pollution, noise, and loss of their way of life, without having been properly consulted. This narrative is relevant to open data because it highlights the urgent need for transparency in technology-related decision-making. For genuine citizen participation and accountability to exist, data on critical infrastructure projects, their actual environmental impact, signed contracts, and financial compensation must be available, accessible, and provided in open formats. Without access to this information, citizens lack the basic tools to monitor, challenge, or negotiate the territorial transformations that directly affect their surroundings and rights.
