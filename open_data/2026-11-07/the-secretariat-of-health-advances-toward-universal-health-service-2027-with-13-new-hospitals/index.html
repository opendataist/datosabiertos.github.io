The Secretariat of Health advances toward Universal Health Service 2027 with 13 new hospitals

The Secretariat of Health advances toward Universal Health Service 2027 with 13 new hospitals

Mexico is advancing toward a Universal Health System to eliminate historical fragmentation and ensure equitable access, regardless of socioeconomic or geographic conditions. This transformation seeks to integrate public institutions through efficient coordination that prioritizes timely, free, and high-quality care for the entire population. Strategic investments have strengthened infrastructure by incorporating advanced medical technology, restoring operating rooms, and expanding the availability of medications. At the same time, the deployment of human resources in remote areas and improvements in supplies have driven a significant reduction in mortality rates from chronic diseases and critical conditions, demonstrating a favorable shift in public health indicators. This initiative is relevant to open data, as it shows how transparency in spending and the quality of health data enable the assessment of the real impact of public policies. The use of verifiable statistics, such as those published by the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI), highlights the importance of accessing accurate and up-to-date information to measure progress toward universal health goals and optimize the management of public resources.
