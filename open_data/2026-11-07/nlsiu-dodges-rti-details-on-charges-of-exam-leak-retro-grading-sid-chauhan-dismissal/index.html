NLSIU dodges RTI details on charges of exam leak, ‘retro’ grading, Sid Chauhan dismissal

NLSIU Bangalore faced scrutiny for allegedly evading transparency through its responses to Right to Information (RTI) requests concerning academic integrity and administrative conduct. The institution’s administration provided opaque answers regarding grade re-evaluations, failing to clarify whether inflated scores resulted from procedural adjustments or arbitrary intervention, while simultaneously withholding crucial details about the dismissal of former visiting faculty member Sidharth Chauhan. The core implications highlight significant governance failures, as the lack of accessible data obscures potential misuse of re-evaluation policies and compromises academic standards. By refusing to disclose evaluator identities or specific decision-making rationales, the university undermines accountability and trust, suggesting that internal mechanisms for ensuring fair evaluation and preventing examination leaks are neither transparent nor effectively enforced. This case is vital to the open data movement because it exemplifies how public institutions can exploit procedural ambiguities to withhold information essential for public scrutiny. It underscores the critical need for robust open data frameworks and independent appellate authorities to prevent administrators from using confidentiality claims as shields against legitimate questions about institutional fairness, ensuring that transparency remains a fundamental right rather than a privilege determined by those in power.
