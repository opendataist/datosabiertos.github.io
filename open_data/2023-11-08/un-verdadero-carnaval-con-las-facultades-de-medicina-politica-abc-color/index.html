Un verdadero “carnaval” con las facultades de medicina - Política - ABC Color

The research reveals a serious crisis of transparency and regulation in Paraguayan higher education, marked by an alarming disconnect between the programs officially listed by the Ministry of Education and operational reality. Universities such as Hernando Arias de Saavedra operate without the necessary authorization from the relevant regulatory bodies, while discrepancies between public records and statements made by the institutions themselves suggest that the system is artificially inflating the number of active academic programs. This lack of statistical rigor and state oversight creates an environment of administrative disorder that hinders the accurate assessment of educational quality. The problem is exacerbated by the proliferation of medical schools in border areas, primarily designed to attract Brazilian students. Many of these institutions operate without authorization from the National Council of Higher Education (CONES) or by relying on court injunctions that circumvent existing regulations. The pressure on the health sector has shown that the massification of these programs exceeds institutional capacity to train competent professionals, directly compromising the quality of medical care. This phenomenon illustrates how the absence of reliable, auditable data enables the existence of “ghost” or irregular institutions that evade public supervision. This case is crucial for the open data community because it highlights the urgent need to ensure the interoperability and accuracy of government data. The contradictions among different public databases—those of the Ministry of Education (MEC), CONES, and the judiciary—demonstrate that merely publishing information is insufficient without rigorous cross-validation. To protect the public interest, it is imperative to develop open data mechanisms that enable citizens and civil society to detect inconsistencies in real time, ensuring that official records accurately reflect the operational reality of educational institutions and preventing the misuse of information as a tool to legitimize unauthorized entities.
