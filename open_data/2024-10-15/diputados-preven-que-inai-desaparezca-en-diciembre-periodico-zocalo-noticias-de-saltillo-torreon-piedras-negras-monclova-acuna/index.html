Diputados prevén que Inai desaparezca en diciembre | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

Diputados prevén que Inai desaparezca en diciembre | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The proposed legislative agenda aims to eliminate several key autonomous institutions, including the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), before the end of the year. This radical restructuring represents a fundamental shift in Mexico’s governance model, replacing independent oversight bodies with centralized government control. The consolidation of these entities suggests a move toward a more streamlined, yet less check-driven, administrative state. Critically, this reform directly impacts the fundamental right to access public information and the protection of personal data. While government officials assert that citizens’ rights will remain guaranteed despite the INAI’s disappearance, the removal of this specific independent guarantor raises significant concerns about transparency and accountability. The future of data protection mechanisms is now tied directly to executive branch commitments rather than autonomous judicial or technical safeguards. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it threatens the institutional independence necessary for robust data governance. Without a separate, empowered body to enforce transparency laws and protect citizen data, the open data ecosystem becomes vulnerable to political influence. The potential dissolution of the INAI signals a broader trend that could undermine the technical integrity and democratic oversight essential for maintaining public trust in digital government services.
