Según un informe, el 47% de los estudiantes de la UNRN son pobres | ANBariloche

Según un informe, el 47% de los estudiantes de la UNRN son pobres | ANBariloche

The report highlights that nearly half of UNRN students come from low-income backgrounds, confirming a significant trend toward socioeconomic inclusion in higher education. This demographic shift indicates that public universities are successfully expanding access to historically marginalized sectors, serving as a crucial engine for intergenerational social mobility. However, this increased access coexists with persistent structural inequalities, where barriers to academic completion remain high for disadvantaged students despite their enrollment. Despite the success in recruitment, the analysis reveals a critical paradox: while entry is more equitable, graduation rates still favor higher-income groups. This discrepancy underscores the necessity of implementing robust support policies, such as scholarships and academic adjustments, to compensate for initial deficits in cultural and educational capital. The data suggests that merely guaranteeing open access is insufficient; targeted interventions are essential to ensure that students from poorer backgrounds can effectively navigate and finish their degrees. This information is vital for the open data community as it demonstrates how transparent, localized data analysis can drive evidence-based policy-making in education. By making socioeconomic metrics visible, institutions can identify gaps in equity and allocate resources more effectively. It emphasizes the importance of open datasets in monitoring the real impact of public investment, ensuring that the promise of inclusive education translates into tangible academic success for all social strata rather than just facilitating initial enrollment.
