Estos son los 11 accidentes mortales en el trabajo que no computa la estadística oficial

Estos son los 11 accidentes mortales en el trabajo que no computa la estadística oficial

The article highlights a significant discrepancy in how workplace fatalities are counted in Aragon, revealing that official statistics underreport deaths compared to union data. This gap stems from specific exclusions in the ISSLA’s methodology, which omits workers from companies not based in Aragon, even if accidents occur locally, and delays the inclusion of cases involving ambiguous causes like heart attacks or strokes due to conflicting interpretations between health insurers and social security systems. Union representatives argue that these omissions obscure the true scale of occupational risks, particularly affecting transport drivers and temporary agricultural workers. By failing to capture these instances immediately or at all, the official data fails to reflect the actual danger levels faced by certain vulnerable labor sectors. The unions emphasize that this lack of comprehensive tracking hinders effective policy-making and obscures the responsibility of employers in high-risk environments. This issue is crucial for open data advocates because it illustrates how methodological choices in data collection directly impact transparency and accountability. When public institutions exclude relevant categories based on jurisdictional or diagnostic ambiguities, it creates an incomplete picture of public safety. Ensuring inclusive, timely, and consistent data definitions is essential for accurate monitoring of labor conditions and for empowering civil society to demand better regulatory enforcement.
