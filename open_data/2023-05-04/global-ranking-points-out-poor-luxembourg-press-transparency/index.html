Global ranking points out poor Luxembourg press transparency

The annual World Press Freedom Index reveals a global decline in journalism quality, driven by government crackdowns, disinformation campaigns, and the disruptive emergence of artificial intelligence. While only a handful of nations currently enjoy a safe environment for journalists, the trend is worsening, with political volatility and state-sponsored propaganda becoming increasingly common worldwide. This context highlights the urgent need for robust legal protections and transparency mechanisms to safeguard independent reporting against both physical threats and digital manipulation. In Luxembourg, this global deterioration is reflected in significant domestic shortcomings, particularly regarding the lack of guaranteed access to state-held information. Despite constitutional guarantees of free expression, the legal framework fails to protect journalists’ right to information, and civil service circulars effectively censor direct communication between officials and the press. The journalists’ union argues that these practices constitute censorship and undermine democratic accountability, emphasizing that without enforceable freedom of information laws, true press independence remains unattainable in the country. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as the struggle for access to information is fundamentally a struggle for open data. When governments withhold documents and restrict communication channels, they actively impede the availability of public data necessary for investigative journalism and civic oversight. The case illustrates that technical openness is insufficient without legal mandates ensuring that data is not only generated but also accessible, transparent, and free from political interference, thereby serving as a critical reminder that open data policies must be backed by strong legal frameworks to protect both the data and those who seek to use it.
