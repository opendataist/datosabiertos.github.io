Opinion: CT shouldn't exempt UConn, other institutions from FOIA

Proposed legislation in Connecticut seeks to exempt state universities from open records laws, threatening to conceal taxpayer-funded activities behind closed doors. This move would strip citizens of the ability to monitor how public funds are allocated, particularly regarding controversial scientific research that often lacks public consent. Transparency has historically proven essential for exposing institutional misconduct and ensuring accountability. Previous revelations of severe animal welfare violations and legal infractions were made possible only through freedom of information requests, leading to necessary regulatory corrections and the closure of abusive facilities. This article underscores the critical role of open data in safeguarding democratic oversight. Without accessible public records, citizens cannot effectively scrutinize government actions, making robust freedom of information laws vital for preventing waste, fraud, and abuse in publicly funded institutions.
