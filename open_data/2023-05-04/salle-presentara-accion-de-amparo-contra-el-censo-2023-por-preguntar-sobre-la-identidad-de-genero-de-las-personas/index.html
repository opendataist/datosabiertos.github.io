Salle presentará acción de amparo contra el Censo 2023 por preguntar sobre la identidad de género de las personas

The article details a legal challenge against the 2023 Census for including a question on gender identity, arguing that such data collection infringes upon individual privacy and constitutes state overreach. The petitioner contends that demographic surveys should focus on household logistics rather than intimate personal realities, asserting that mandatory inquiry into psychological and sexual self-conception violates the principle that private information remains the sole property of the individual. This stance highlights a broader tension between state surveillance capabilities and civil liberties, suggesting that intrusive data gathering undermines democratic values by prioritizing ideological objectives over constitutional protections of intimacy. The controversy underscores the critical importance of transparency and consent in open data initiatives, particularly regarding sensitive personal attributes. When statistical agencies collect non-essential, highly personal information without clear public benefit or optional participation, it risks eroding public trust and legitimizing authoritarian data practices. The argument that such data serves political agendas rather than genuine civic needs illustrates how opaque or coercive data collection can fuel political polarization and skepticism toward institutional neutrality. This case is relevant to open data because it exemplifies the ethical boundaries of what should be made publicly accessible or statistically gathered. It serves as a cautionary tale for policymakers and data scientists to distinguish between necessary public health or economic metrics and intrusive ideological markers. Ensuring that open data frameworks respect human rights and maintain a strict separation between public utility and private sovereignty is essential to preserve both data integrity and democratic trust.
