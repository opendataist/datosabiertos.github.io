How India’s Data Protection Law Weakens Citizens’ Right to Information

The Right to Information Act in India marks a critical shift from a historical culture of bureaucratic secrecy to a regime of substantive accountability, empowering citizens to scrutinize state actions. By legally mandating transparency, the Act has redefined state-citizen linkages, fostering a democracy where individuals can demand explanations for public spending and service delivery. This legal framework serves as a vital mechanism for exposing corruption and ensuring that governance remains responsive to the public interest rather than hidden within official files. Despite its transformative impact, the Act faces persistent threats from political attempts to weaken its independence and scope. Recent amendments have placed the tenure and salaries of information commissioners under executive control, risking their autonomy and potentially shielding the government from scrutiny. Furthermore, new data protection legislation creates a conflict with existing transparency laws by placing the burden of proof on citizens to justify information requests, effectively making it easier for authorities to deny access to personal data under the guise of privacy. This tension is highly relevant to open data principles, illustrating the fragility of digital transparency when not safeguarded by strong legal frameworks. The struggle between the right to know and the impulse toward secrecy highlights how open data can be undermined by procedural barriers and conflicting regulations. It underscores the necessity of defending robust data access laws and institutional independence to ensure that technology and policy continue to serve public accountability rather than entrench state power.
