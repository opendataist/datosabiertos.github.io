Brett Murphy Wins Al Nakkula and Brechner Freedom of Information Awards

Brett Murphy’s award-winning investigation exposes the widespread use of unreliable "911 call analysis" within the U.S. justice system. By leveraging vast amounts of government records and internal emails, he revealed that law enforcement and prosecutors have used linguistic patterns to wrongly assess guilt for over a decade. This practice, rooted in junk science, has impacted countless cases, leading to wrongful convictions and prompting immediate legal appeals and legislative inquiries. The relevance to open data lies in the critical role public records played in uncovering this hidden misconduct. Murphy’s ability to analyze thousands of documents demonstrated how transparency initiatives allow journalists to hold powerful institutions accountable. Without access to these datasets, the systematic reliance on pseudoscientific methods would likely remain concealed. This case highlights that open data is not merely about information availability but serves as a vital tool for detecting systemic errors and protecting civil liberties. Ultimately, this story underscores the necessity of robust data transparency to ensure judicial integrity. The exposure of these practices has triggered reforms, including post-conviction reviews and warnings from district attorneys against such evidence. It proves that when government data is open, citizens and the media can identify and correct flaws in the justice system. Consequently, maintaining accessible public records is essential for preventing miscarriages of justice and maintaining public trust in law enforcement procedures.
