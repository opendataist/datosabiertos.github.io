Record labels demand streaming providers block AI music scraping

Universal Music Group is demanding that major streaming platforms actively police their users to prevent artificial intelligence developers from scraping music for large language model training. By threatening legal action against intermediaries like Spotify and Apple Music, UMG argues that these services have a responsibility to stop the unauthorized use of copyrighted material. This approach shifts the burden of enforcement away from the AI companies directly using the data and places it on the content providers, raising significant questions about intermediary liability in the digital age. The core controversy lies in whether AI models are merely learning from public data or infringing on specific artistic rights. UMG claims that training algorithms on melodies and lyrics constitutes copyright violation, particularly when models regurgitate plagiarized versions of existing works. However, critics question why rights holders target the platforms selling licensed access to music rather than the prominent, well-funded AI firms directly engaging in the alleged infringement. This strategy highlights a growing tension between traditional copyright enforcement and the emerging demands of generative AI development. This development is crucial for open data because it challenges the fundamental principle of access to information for research and innovation. If streaming services are compelled to restrict data scraping, it could create significant barriers for AI developers relying on public or licensed datasets. The outcome of this dispute will likely define the legal boundaries of data usage, potentially influencing how open datasets are protected or restricted. Understanding this shift is essential for stakeholders advocating for balanced copyright laws that support both creators and technological progress.
