The INAI’s Plenary is paralyzed due to a lack of quorum, preventing the resolution of hundreds of citizen appeals. This inoperability stems from the Senate’s failure to appoint three missing commissioners, leaving the institution unable to fulfill its mandate. Consequently, citizens face restricted access to public information and data protection services, undermining their fundamental rights. This situation highlights a critical vulnerability in Mexico’s transparency infrastructure. When oversight bodies cannot function, the rule of law and government accountability suffer immediate tangible impacts. The delay demonstrates how political bottlenecks in appointments can effectively halt democratic safeguards, leaving citizens without recourse against government opacity. This case is vital for open data advocates because it illustrates that technical data rights depend entirely on functional institutional governance. Without a fully staffed and operative regulatory body, legal guarantees for information access remain theoretical. Ensuring timely appointments is not merely administrative but essential for maintaining the integrity and availability of open government data systems.
