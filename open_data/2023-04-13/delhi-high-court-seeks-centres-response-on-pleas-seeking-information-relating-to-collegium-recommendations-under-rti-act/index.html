Delhi High Court Seeks Centre’s Response On Pleas Seeking Information Relating To Collegium Recommendations Under RTI Act

The Delhi High Court is currently reviewing petitions that challenge the non-disclosure of Supreme Court Collegium recommendations under the Right to Information Act. This legal proceeding highlights a critical tension within open governance: determining whether internal judicial deliberations constitute public records or protected personal privacy. The court’s decision will clarify if the transparency mandated for public authorities extends to the sensitive, deliberative processes involved in appointing judges, setting a precedent for accountability in high-level institutional decision-making. Central to this case is the interpretation of exemptions regarding personal information and privacy. While some records were previously denied based on these grounds, others have been partially disclosed, creating inconsistent legal standards. The ongoing litigation seeks to establish a uniform approach, weighing the individual’s right to privacy against the public’s right to know how judicial appointments are made. This balance is essential for ensuring that judicial appointments remain transparent without infringing on the confidentiality required for candid internal discussions among senior judges. This case is highly relevant to the open data movement as it addresses the accessibility of political and judicial data. If Collegium recommendations are deemed open, it would significantly enhance public scrutiny of judicial appointments, fostering greater trust in the legal system. Conversely, maintaining strict secrecy could limit civic oversight of power structures. The outcome will likely influence how other public bodies handle internal communications, potentially shaping future policies on releasing sensitive governmental and judicial data for public benefit and accountability.
