Patent Awarded To Clearview AI For Its Innovative, Web-Scraping Fuckery

The article critiques Clearview AI’s strategic pivot toward legitimacy, highlighting its recent receipt of a federal patent for facial recognition technology derived from massive, non-consensual web scraping. This development represents a critical turning point, as the government is effectively granting intellectual property protection to a system built on widely condemned privacy violations, thereby validating a business model that treats the entire public internet as a searchable database without consent. This legal approval underscores a disturbing intersection between state validation and unethical surveillance practices. By allowing such patents, the regulatory framework inadvertently shields aggressive data extraction methods from scrutiny, enabling Clearview to rebrand its controversial operations as innovative and respectable. The narrative suggests that this move signals a dangerous precedent where moral accountability is secondary to technical achievements and corporate lobbying, potentially normalizing invasive surveillance tools in future legal and commercial landscapes. For the open data community, this case serves as a stark warning about the consequences of unregulated data harvesting. It illustrates how scraped data, when aggregated into proprietary systems, can bypass ethical norms and gain institutional support, undermining principles of consent and transparency. The episode highlights the urgent need for robust data governance frameworks that distinguish between publicly available data and exploitative surveillance applications, ensuring that open data initiatives do not inadvertently facilitate human rights abuses or erode public trust in digital rights.
