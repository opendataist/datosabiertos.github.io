The disclosure of the remuneration packages of RTVE’s presenters was achieved solely through a formal request to the Transparency Council, highlighting the corporation’s usual reluctance to proactively release such data. This process underscores the importance of access-to-information mechanisms in breaking down the institutional secrecy that typically conceals the working conditions of external and artistic staff. The results reveal a significant salary hierarchy among the stars of public television, with individual fees reaching figures far higher than those of the rest of the team. This economic distinction reflects market dynamics and the bargaining power of the most recognized talents, implying a resource distribution that can spark debates about internal equity and the justification of costs in public funding. This case is relevant to the field of open data because it demonstrates how transparency serves as a necessary counterweight to the opacity of state bodies. By compelling the disclosure of sensitive information such as salaries, it fosters accountability and enables citizens to assess the efficient use of public funds, setting a key precedent for demanding openness in other similar institutions.
