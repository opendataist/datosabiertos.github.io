ICE might be breaking the law to stonewall reporters.

ICE might be breaking the law to stonewall reporters.

Journalists and transparency advocates report that U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement and Customs and Border Protection are systematically ignoring or denying Freedom of Information Act requests. This widespread stonewalling results in heavily redacted documents, indefinite delays, and outright refusals, effectively preventing reporters from obtaining critical data on deportation actions and detention facilities. The agencies appear to be operating with near-total opacity, failing to meet statutory deadlines and leaving most requests unanswered or unresponsive. This lack of transparency coincides with aggressive tactics against press coverage, including arrests and the use of force against journalists, further complicating accountability efforts. While some major news organizations have resorted to litigation to secure limited information, smaller outlets lack the resources for prolonged legal battles. Consequently, many reporters are forced to rely on alternative sources or report on the obstruction itself, undermining the public’s right to understand federal immigration enforcement operations. This crisis is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the failure of legal mechanisms to ensure government accountability when institutions refuse to comply with transparency laws. It demonstrates that open data initiatives depend not only on policy but on institutional willingness to share information. When key government agencies treat FOIA requests as nuisances rather than obligations, the ecosystem of public oversight collapses, leaving citizens uninformed about significant state activities.
