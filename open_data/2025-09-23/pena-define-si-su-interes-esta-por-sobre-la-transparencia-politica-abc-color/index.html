Peña define si su interés está por “sobre” la transparencia - Política - ABC Color

Peña define si su interés está por “sobre” la transparencia - Política - ABC Color

The article argues that the proposed Personal Data Protection Law in Paraguay poses a severe threat to existing transparency and freedom of information legislation. Rather than enhancing privacy, the bill introduces provisions that would effectively nullify current rights to access public information, potentially allowing government officials to withhold critical data under the guise of data protection. This legislative maneuver is criticized as a strategic effort to shield public servants from scrutiny and hide information regarding the use of public resources. The relevance of this issue to open data is profound, as it highlights the risk of regulatory frameworks being manipulated to obstruct transparency rather than protect it. The text emphasizes that the President of the Republic holds the decisive power to veto these restrictive articles. His decision will determine whether citizens retain their constitutional right to access public records or if these rights are effectively erased by executive interference and legislative alignment. Specifically, the article links this legislative risk to ongoing corruption investigations involving the president, suggesting a personal conflict of interest. If promulgated without changes, the law could legally justify denying access to information about visits to the presidential residence, thereby concealing potential misuse of public funds. This situation illustrates how open data initiatives can be undermined when political actors prioritize secrecy over accountability, turning data protection laws into tools for hiding irregularities instead of protecting individual privacy.
