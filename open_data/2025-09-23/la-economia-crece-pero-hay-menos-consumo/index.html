La economía crece, pero hay menos consumo

La economía crece, pero hay menos consumo

The article highlights a stark economic divergence in Panama, where modest GDP growth contrasts sharply with significant declines in consumption tax revenues and a spike in unemployment. This disparity indicates that while macroeconomic aggregates may show stability, the underlying health of the economy is deteriorating due to massive job losses and reduced household spending power, particularly affecting the middle class in key provinces. The core driver of this crisis was the closure of major mining operations and the subsequent loss of investment-grade rating, which triggered a chain reaction of formal job eliminations and reduced commercial activity. This event demonstrated that economic potential without sustained private investment leads to stagnation, as alternative sectors like tourism and logistics have failed to generate sufficient formal employment to replace those lost, leaving the country vulnerable to external shocks. This situation is critical for the open data community because it underscores the necessity of transparent, high-quality economic indicators to accurately track such structural shifts. Reliable data on employment, consumption, and investment flows enables policymakers and analysts to identify emerging crises early and advocate for evidence-based strategies. Without accessible and detailed datasets, it remains difficult to hold institutions accountable or to design effective interventions that can restore investor confidence and foster inclusive economic recovery.
