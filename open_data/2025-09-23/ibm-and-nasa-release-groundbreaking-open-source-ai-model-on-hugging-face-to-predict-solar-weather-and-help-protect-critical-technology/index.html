IBM and NASA Release Groundbreaking Open-Source AI Model on Hugging Face to Predict Solar Weather and Help Protect Critical Technology

IBM and NASA Release Groundbreaking Open-Source AI Model on Hugging Face to Predict Solar Weather and Help Protect Critical Technology

IBM and NASA have unveiled Surya, an advanced open-source foundation model that revolutionizes solar weather forecasting by analyzing high-resolution solar observations. This tool significantly enhances our ability to predict how solar activity impacts Earth’s critical infrastructure, including GPS, power grids, and telecommunications. By offering a "space weather forecast," Surya helps mitigate substantial economic and operational risks associated with solar storms, protecting both modern technological systems and deep space exploration efforts. The model’s effectiveness stems from its training on the largest curated dataset of heliophysics data, addressing previous limitations in accuracy and resolution. It achieves notable improvements in classifying solar flares and uniquely predicts their visual occurrence up to two hours in advance. This capability allows researchers and operators to anticipate disruptions with unprecedented precision, transforming reactive responses into proactive preparedness against events that can otherwise cause widespread technological failure. Surya’s release on Hugging Face exemplifies the open data movement by democratizing access to complex scientific tools. By making these advanced AI capabilities available to the global research community, IBM and NASA enable scientists to build upon a shared foundation for specialized applications. This collaborative approach accelerates scientific discovery and ensures that vital space weather insights are accessible to all, fostering innovation and resilience in the face of growing environmental vulnerabilities.
