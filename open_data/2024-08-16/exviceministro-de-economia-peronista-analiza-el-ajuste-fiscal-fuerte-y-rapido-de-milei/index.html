Exviceministro de Economía peronista analiza el "ajuste fiscal fuerte y rápido" de Milei

The article highlights the immediate release of July’s inflation data by Argentina’s INDEC, which recorded a significant decrease to four percent. This transparency in publishing high-frequency economic indicators exemplifies open data principles by making timely, granular statistics accessible to the public, allowing for real-time monitoring of macroeconomic trends rather than relying on delayed or aggregated reports. Ex-minister Gabriel Rubinstein attributes this decline not solely to recession but to the government’s rapid and severe fiscal adjustment. His analysis underscores the importance of accessible policy evaluation, where citizens and experts can directly correlate official statistics with specific governmental actions, fostering a more informed public discourse on economic stabilization efforts. However, the piece also reveals limitations in data utility, as Rubinstein critiques the program’s structural flaws and negative discourse impact. This demonstrates that while open data provides the necessary raw material for accountability, its full value is realized only when coupled with clear context and critical interpretation, enabling society to distinguish between statistical outcomes and policy effectiveness.
