The increasing lethality of traffic accidents highlights a critical public safety crisis, particularly affecting vulnerable road users such as pedestrians and cyclists. This trend underscores systemic failures in infrastructure design and enforcement, demanding immediate attention to prevent further loss of life in urban areas. Data reveals a disturbing rise in fatalities among motorcyclists and pedestrians, indicating that current safety measures are insufficient to protect users of non-motorized transport. The disparity in risk exposure points to the need to prioritize the safety of those most at risk, rather than focusing solely on vehicular traffic flow. This issue is relevant to open data because transparent, accessible statistics are essential for holding authorities accountable and driving evidence-based policy changes. Without reliable, publicly available data on the causes and locations of accidents, it is impossible to effectively monitor trends or implement targeted interventions to reduce these preventable deaths.
