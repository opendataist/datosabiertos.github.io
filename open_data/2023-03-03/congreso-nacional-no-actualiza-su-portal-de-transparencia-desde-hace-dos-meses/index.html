Congreso Nacional no actualiza su portal de transparencia desde hace dos meses

The article highlights a systemic failure in Honduras’ public administration to uphold transparency standards, with the National Congress serving as a prominent example of this deficit. Despite legal obligations, the legislature struggles to maintain timely updates on its transparency portal, specifically omitting critical data regarding legislators’ attendance and voting records. This gap undermines public accountability, as citizens are denied essential insights into how their representatives exercise power and make decisions. This issue is not isolated but reflects a broader national challenge, where approximately one-third of supervised institutions fail to meet the IAIP’s four criteria: veracity, timeliness, adequacy, and completeness. The root causes include high staff turnover, bureaucratic delays in obtaining necessary signatures, and a lack of proper certification for information officers. Consequently, even when updates occur, they often arrive after statutory deadlines, rendering the information less useful for real-time civic oversight and suggesting a structural inability to sustain rigorous transparency protocols. Relevance to open data lies in the demonstration of how poor data governance erodes democratic trust. The article illustrates that the mere existence of data portals is insufficient if the underlying processes for collecting, verifying, and publishing data are flawed. Without accurate, timely, and accessible data on legislative activities, open data initiatives fail to achieve their primary goal of enabling informed public participation. The persistent delays and omissions emphasize the urgent need for institutional reforms and technical capacity building to ensure that open data truly serves its purpose of fostering transparency and accountability in governance.
