The Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Information Commission is struggling with a growing backlog of over fifteen hundred pending cases due to critical staff vacancies and procedural bottlenecks. The Chief Information Commissioner highlighted that the inability to form a quorum has effectively paralyzed the commission’s operations. Furthermore, proposed legislative amendments aimed at strengthening the Right to Information Act have reportedly gone missing from government records, signaling a lack of administrative priority. Former commissioners and experts warn that the Right to Information movement has lost momentum, with bureaucratic hurdles preventing the appointment of key officials. They emphasize that true transparency requires covering all public sector bodies, including the judiciary, within the scope of access laws. The absence of functional commissions and weak legal frameworks across various provinces undermines citizens’ fundamental rights to information and accountability in public administration. This situation is highly relevant to open data because effective open data initiatives depend entirely on robust legal enforcement and institutional capacity. Without empowered commissions and clear legislative mandates, proactive disclosure of public records remains unachieved. The study calls for harmonized, stronger laws across all regions to ensure digital record maintenance and citizen access. Strengthening these legal structures is essential for moving beyond passive data release toward a culture of genuine transparency and public accountability in governance.
