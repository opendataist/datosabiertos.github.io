AI Trained on Copyrighted Material Without Permission Poses ‘Direct Threat’ to Film Industry, Says BFI

AI Trained on Copyrighted Material Without Permission Poses ‘Direct Threat’ to Film Industry, Says BFI

A recent report by the British Film Institute warns that generative AI models trained on copyrighted material without permission threaten the economic foundations of the UK screen sector. By allowing machines to replicate and create new works at a fraction of the cost of human effort, AI risks undermining the industry’s ability to generate value from intellectual property. This displacement of traditional value creation poses a significant risk to the sustainability of film, television, visual effects, and video game industries if legal frameworks do not adapt to protect creators' rights. To mitigate these risks, the report recommends establishing AI training licenses that ensure fair compensation for intellectual property holders. It emphasizes the need for the sector to build complementary skills, focusing on human oversight, creative direction, and technical fluency, rather than competing directly with automation. While AI offers opportunities to enhance efficiency and democratize content creation, the industry must proactively manage workforce adaptation to prevent the erosion of established business models and ensure long-term resilience against technological shifts. This discourse is critical to open data discussions as it highlights the tension between accessing data for innovation and protecting individual rights. The proposed Data (Use and Access) Bill, which favors broad data access unless creators opt out, contrasts sharply with the BFI’s call for explicit consent and licensing. Understanding this conflict is essential for developing open data policies that balance technological advancement with ethical considerations, ensuring that data usage respects the economic and creative integrity of original authors in an increasingly automated digital landscape.
