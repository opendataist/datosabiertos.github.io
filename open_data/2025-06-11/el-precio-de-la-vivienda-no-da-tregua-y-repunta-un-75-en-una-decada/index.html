El precio de la vivienda no da tregua y repunta un 75 % en una década

El precio de la vivienda no da tregua y repunta un 75 % en una década

The article highlights a severe housing crisis in the Balearic Islands, where prices have risen dramatically over the past decade, starting from already elevated levels. This surge, significantly higher than in other Spanish regions, has drastically reduced citizens' ability to save and purchase homes. Although the year-over-year increase was the lowest in Spain, it still reached double digits, underscoring the persistent pressure on affordability. This trend is not isolated; several other autonomous communities are experiencing similar price recoveries, suggesting the problem is expanding nationwide and threatening residents' access to housing. Relevance to open data lies in the role of the National Statistics Institute (INE) and its Housing Price Index (IPV). These public datasets are crucial for monitoring market evolution and measuring the impact of property transactions. By making such detailed statistical information publicly available, the INE enables researchers, policymakers, and citizens to track trends accurately. Open access to this data facilitates transparency, allowing society to understand the root causes of the housing shortage and the disproportionate impact on specific regions, thereby supporting evidence-based advocacy and policy interventions. The intersection of tourism saturation and housing scarcity further complicates the situation, prompting public protests and political urgency to approve measures for new housing supply. The narrative concludes by noting the government's rushed efforts to address the deficit, emphasizing that statistical evidence of rising costs is driving immediate political action. Ultimately, the article demonstrates how open data serves as a vital tool for identifying social issues, validating public concerns, and holding authorities accountable for implementing effective solutions to prevent the displacement of residents due to unsustainable market dynamics.
