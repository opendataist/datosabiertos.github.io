Promociones, comercios de barrios y menos stock: cómo cambió el consumo de los argentinos | Análisis

Promociones, comercios de barrios y menos stock: cómo cambió el consumo de los argentinos | Análisis

The article describes a significant transformation in the consumption habits of Argentines, driven by price stabilization and the anticipated decline in inflation. Consumers have moved away from stockpiling non-perishable goods, opting instead for more rational weekly purchases, actively seeking promotions, and favoring nearby stores to buy fresh products. This shift reflects greater planning and efficiency in spending, moving away from immediate need-based purchases toward a more strategic management of household budgets. However, this recovery of the domestic market is characterized by deep socioeconomic fragmentation. While middle- and high-income sectors are resuming their purchasing levels and diversifying their shopping baskets, lower-income households have not yet shown a similar rebound. This disparity reveals that improvements in economic stability have not been evenly distributed, leaving vulnerable families with restricted consumption capacity and lacking the same market vitality observed in other social strata. This situation is relevant to open data because it underscores the importance of having disaggregated and accessible data to accurately understand market dynamics. Information on purchasing behaviors, frequency, and channel selection helps identify structural gaps and assess the real impact of economic policies on different population groups. Transparent access to these indicators facilitates a more rigorous analysis of inequality and consumption efficiency, which are essential for formulating informed and equitable solutions.
