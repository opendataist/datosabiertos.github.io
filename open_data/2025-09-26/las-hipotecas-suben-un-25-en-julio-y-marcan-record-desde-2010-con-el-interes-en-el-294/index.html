The housing credit market is experiencing a robust recovery, characterized by a significant surge in mortgage signings and lending volumes. This trend indicates strong consumer demand and sustained confidence, driven by more affordable financing conditions and competitive offers from financial institutions. Activity has reached levels unseen since 2010, reflecting a positive shift in the housing sector’s cycle and a renewed emphasis on fixed-rate mortgages alongside a resurgence of interest in variable rates amid stable economic indicators. Concurrently, there is notable activity in modifying existing loan terms, particularly through subrogations and interest rate adjustments. This behavior demonstrates that borrowers are actively seeking to optimize their financial situations in response to current market opportunities. The prevalence of these modifications suggests that the current environment not only facilitates new purchases but also encourages homeowners to refine their debt structures, thereby injecting further dynamism into the real estate and credit markets. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the critical importance of transparent, high-quality statistical records in understanding macroeconomic trends. Publicly accessible, standardized data from institutions such as the INE allows for accurate analysis of housing market dynamics, consumer behavior, and financial health. Such transparency empowers researchers, policymakers, and citizens to make informed decisions, ensuring that the benefits of market openness extend to better economic forecasting and policy design.
