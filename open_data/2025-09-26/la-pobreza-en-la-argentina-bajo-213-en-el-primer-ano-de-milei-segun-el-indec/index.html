La pobreza en la Argentina bajó 21,3% en el primer año de Milei, según el INDEC

The recent statistical report highlights a significant reduction in poverty and extreme poverty rates in Argentina during the first half of 2025, attributing this improvement primarily to a substantial increase in household income relative to the cost of basic food baskets and total consumption baskets. This correlation demonstrates how economic policy impacts the material well-being of the population, offering a clear metric for assessing the effectiveness of government strategies in combating social inequality. By isolating salary evolution from other variables such as hours worked, the data provides a clearer view of real wage growth across public and private sectors, revealing distinct disparities in recovery between formal and informal employment. These findings are particularly relevant to open data initiatives because they underscore the critical importance of transparent, systematic, and granular statistical production. The report exemplifies how standardized, publicly accessible datasets allow for rigorous independent analysis, enabling researchers and citizens to verify official narratives and understand complex socioeconomic dynamics. The detailed breakdown by region, age, and sector illustrates the power of open data to expose structural inequalities, such as the higher vulnerability of children or specific geographic areas, which might otherwise be obscured in aggregate figures. Furthermore, the inclusion of methodological notes regarding what the measurement excludes, such as chronic structural poverty, serves as a vital example of data transparency and integrity. It reminds users that statistical indicators have defined boundaries and limitations, fostering a more nuanced public debate. Ultimately, this article demonstrates how open, high-quality government statistics empower democratic oversight, allowing society to critically evaluate progress on human rights and social justice rather than accepting simplified political narratives at face value.
