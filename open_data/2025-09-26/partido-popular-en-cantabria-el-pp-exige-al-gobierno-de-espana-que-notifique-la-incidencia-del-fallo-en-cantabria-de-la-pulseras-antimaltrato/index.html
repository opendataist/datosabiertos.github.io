The article highlights a political conflict in Cantabria, where the People’s Party demands full transparency regarding the functionality of anti-domestic violence ankle monitors. This initiative underscores the critical importance of open data in public safety, arguing that citizens’ trust and effective protection depend on the government’s ability to provide verifiable, accessible information about system failures, rather than issuing vague assurances or concealing technical incidents. The text further critiques the broader administrative record, suggesting that a lack of accountability enables systemic inefficiencies and broken promises, such as delays in high-speed rail projects. This narrative illustrates how opaque governance and the refusal to share concrete data on public works exacerbate public discontent and hinder progress, reinforcing the need for rigorous data transparency to hold officials accountable for their commitments and operational outcomes. Ultimately, the piece links political accountability to the principle of open data, asserting that true security and civic satisfaction stem from certainty and transparency. By exposing what the author describes as a pattern of silence and mismanagement, the article reinforces the relevance of open data as a fundamental tool for democratic oversight, ensuring that public resources are managed effectively and that citizens are not left in a state of unjustified uncertainty regarding services vital to their safety and infrastructure.
