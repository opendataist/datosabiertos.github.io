Canada, Australia, Belgium, pledge to support NEITI on open data policy

Canada, Australia, Belgium, pledge to support NEITI on open data policy

Foreign diplomatic missions in Nigeria have formally pledged to support the Nigeria Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative’s new Open Data Policy by providing technical assistance, skills development, and training. This commitment follows an inspection of the newly established NEITI Data Center, which serves as a centralized repository for all extractive sector information. The collaboration underscores a shared international interest in strengthening transparency and governance within Nigeria’s oil, gas, and mineral industries through enhanced capacity building. The data center functions as a critical infrastructure for open data, aggregating and disaggregating industry information to ensure easy public access. It aims to empower civil society organizations, media, government agencies, and citizens with evidence-based insights. By aligning with global EITI standards, the initiative seeks to foster robust public knowledge and understanding, thereby supporting evidence-based decision-making and promoting accountability in resource management amidst the global transition to renewable energy. This development is highly relevant to open data as it demonstrates how structured data repositories can facilitate transparency and civic engagement in sensitive sectors. By making extractive industry data accessible and usable, the project exemplifies the practical application of open data principles to combat corruption and enhance governance. The involvement of international partners highlights the global importance of data openness as a tool for sustainable development and democratic oversight, setting a precedent for other resource-rich nations to follow.
