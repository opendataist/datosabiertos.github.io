Advierten que "los riesgos de hiperinflación están latentes" en Argentina - IMPULSO

Daniel Garro warns of latent hyperinflation risks driven by persistent fiscal deficits and excessive central bank money printing. He argues that short-term inflation solutions require painful, unpopular monetary tightening, which the current administration avoids due to electoral pressures. This political hesitation prevents the implementation of effective stabilization measures, leaving the economy vulnerable to severe monetary instability. The interview highlights how unclear long-term rules and forced currency exchange rates at artificial prices disincentivize exports. Producers face financial losses due to heavy taxation and lack of regulatory certainty, causing export volumes to decline. Without clear policy frameworks, Argentina fails to leverage its abundant resources to generate growth, further weakening the country’s economic foundation. This article is relevant to open_data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible economic indicators for public accountability. When citizens cannot verify real inflation or export data, they cannot hold policymakers accountable for cosmetic measures. Open data empowers society to detect hidden deficits and pressure governments toward sustainable, rather than political, economic strategies.
