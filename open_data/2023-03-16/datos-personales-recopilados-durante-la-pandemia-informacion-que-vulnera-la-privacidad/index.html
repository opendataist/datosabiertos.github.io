Datos personales recopilados durante la pandemia: ¿información que vulnera la privacidad?

The massive collection of sensitive data during the pandemic has raised concerns about its subsequent retention, requiring states to delete or anonymize this information once its purpose has been fulfilled. This stance is grounded in data protection principles that limit the duration of data retention and prevent arbitrary use, thereby fundamentally safeguarding the right to individual privacy. The relevance to open data lies in the tension between public transparency and the protection of privacy. The report reveals that although many countries reported on the purpose of data collection, they lack transparent mechanisms to verify the effective deletion of records. This highlights the critical need for accessible systems that allow citizens to audit the lifecycle of their personal data managed by the state. To address these shortcomings, it is recommended to strengthen accountability, proactively manage risks, and promote an ethical culture in the handling of information. Implementing simple public access mechanisms to verify data storage and deletion is essential. These measures aim to balance the utility of open statistics with the legal and moral obligation to protect human rights in the digital age.
