La inflación sin control en Argentina alcanza un 102,5% en 12 meses a febrero

Argentina’s consumer price index recently surged past 100 percent annually, marking a historic breach of triple-digit inflation for the first time since the early 1990s. This significant economic milestone reflects a chronic crisis that has persisted for nearly five decades, fundamentally altering the cost of living and eroding purchasing power across the nation. The discrepancy between official government forecasts and actual market performance highlights severe macroeconomic imbalances. While authorities anticipated much lower rates, prevailing expectations from financial entities and central bank surveys align closely with the triple-digit reality, suggesting that current stabilization policies, such as voluntary price agreements, may be insufficient to curb the accelerated rise in food and essential goods. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, high-quality statistical indicators in diagnosing complex economic crises. Reliable public data allows analysts, citizens, and international organizations like the IMF to verify official narratives, assess policy effectiveness, and understand the true impact of inflationary pressures on societal welfare.
