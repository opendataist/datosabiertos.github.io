The article emphasizes that digital financial technology is a crucial solution to Mexico’s persistent financial exclusion, where a significant portion of the adult population lacks access to formal credit. By leveraging digital wallets and investment platforms, fintech companies are bridging the gap created by shortages in traditional banking infrastructure and regulatory challenges, demonstrating that technology can effectively streamline risk management and improve service accessibility. Looking ahead, the sector is projected to achieve substantial user growth, significantly narrowing the financial inclusion gap among the population. This expansion is supported by specific regulatory frameworks and digital payment systems overseen by central authorities, which validate the integration of private innovation with public oversight. The surge in users across various segments indicates a robust shift toward digital assets and online investments, fostering an environment in which financial services become more inclusive and resilient. This case is highly relevant to open data, as the transparency and accessibility of financial metrics are essential for monitoring inclusion trends and regulating emerging technologies. Open data enables researchers and policymakers to analyze the real impact of fintech adoption, evaluate the effectiveness of regulatory measures, and identify remaining barriers to financial equity. Ultimately, leveraging open data ensures that the benefits of digital finance are measurable, accountable, and widely distributed across society.
