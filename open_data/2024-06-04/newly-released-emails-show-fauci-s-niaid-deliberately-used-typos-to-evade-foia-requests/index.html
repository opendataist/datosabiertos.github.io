Newly released emails show Fauci's NIAID deliberately used typos to evade FOIA requests

Recent revelations indicate that officials at the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases intentionally introduced typos into emails to evade Freedom of Information Act requests. This deliberate use of misspellings served as a strategic mechanism to obscure key terms from automated searches, allowing sensitive communications to remain hidden from public scrutiny. The practice suggests a systematic effort to control information flow and prevent accountability regarding government activities. The discovery undermines the fundamental premise of transparency in federal agencies, demonstrating how bureaucratic actors can manipulate administrative procedures to shield their operations from oversight. By treating the FOIA process as a hurdle to be circumvented rather than a mandate for openness, these actions erode public trust and highlight significant vulnerabilities in current record-keeping protocols. The implication is that standard legal tools for accessing government records may be easily defeated by coordinated efforts to alter documentation. This case is critically relevant to open data initiatives because it exposes how political interference can compromise the integrity of digital records. It illustrates that open government data is not guaranteed but must be actively protected against malicious manipulation by those in power. For open data advocates, this serves as a stark reminder that technical accessibility of information is insufficient without rigorous verification of the data’s authenticity and the institutional commitment to genuine transparency.
