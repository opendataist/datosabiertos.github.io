Jaén es la provincia andaluza con menos convenios colectivos

The report on collective bargaining in Andalusia reveals a complex landscape where negotiation activity varies significantly by province, with Jaén lagging behind while Cádiz and Málaga lead in signed agreements. Despite an overall increase in active conventions and covered workers, the region faces challenges in ensuring these labor agreements translate into tangible quality-of-life improvements for employees. The narrative highlights that while the number of enterprises and workers under convention has risen, real wage growth has not kept pace with inflation, resulting in a notable erosion of purchasing power for the working class. A critical concern emphasized by unions is the need for robust salary revision clauses to protect workers from economic instability. Currently, only a fraction of the conventions include mechanisms to adjust wages in response to inflation, leaving many employees vulnerable. Furthermore, while most conventions address health and safety, accident statistics remain alarmingly high, indicating a disconnect between policy frameworks and actual workplace conditions. The urgency to reduce working hours without salary cuts is presented as essential to restore balance between professional obligations and personal well-being. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the importance of transparent, publicly accessible labor statistics for identifying regional disparities and social risks. By making data on conventions, wages, and accidents available, stakeholders can move beyond aggregate numbers to understand specific local needs. The analysis shows how open data facilitates accountability, allowing civil society and unions to pinpoint where protections are lacking and advocate for targeted policy changes to improve workers' rights and economic security.
