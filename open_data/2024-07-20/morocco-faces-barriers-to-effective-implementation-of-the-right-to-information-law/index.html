Morocco Faces Barriers to Effective Implementation of the Right to Information Law

Morocco’s Right to Information Law establishes a foundational framework for citizen access to public data, yet its implementation is severely hindered by administrative bottlenecks. Significant delays in processing requests, coupled with ambiguous fee structures that burden users with potential costs, create substantial barriers to transparency. Furthermore, the lack of dedicated personnel to handle inquiries exacerbates these inefficiencies, preventing the law from fulfilling its promise of open government. The oversight mechanism is equally compromised by the inaction of the designated commission, which has failed to produce mandatory annual reports. This silence obstructs the evaluation of transparency efforts and leaves citizens uninformed about the state’s adherence to accountability principles. Consequently, the absence of regular, published assessments undermines public trust and allows misinformation to flourish, as the community remains unaware of actual institutional practices. This situation is critically relevant to open data because procedural opacity directly contradicts the core principles of accessibility and usability. Without timely, free, and clearly governed access to information, open data initiatives remain theoretical rather than practical. Strengthening this legal framework by enforcing strict deadlines, clarifying cost prohibitions, and ensuring robust oversight is essential to transform raw public data into a tool for genuine participatory democracy and social development.
