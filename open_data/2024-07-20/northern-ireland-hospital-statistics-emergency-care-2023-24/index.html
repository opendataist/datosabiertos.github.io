Northern Ireland Hospital Statistics: Emergency Care (2023/24)

Northern Ireland Hospital Statistics: Emergency Care (2023/24)

Emergency care statistics reveal a concerning decline in performance efficiency despite a slight rise in patient attendance. The proportion of individuals treated within four hours dropped significantly, while the number of patients waiting over twelve hours surged. This suggests systemic pressures are exacerbating delays, creating bottlenecks that compromise timely care delivery and highlighting the urgent need for resource optimization. Ambulance response targets further illustrate these operational challenges, with critical rapid response benchmarks consistently missed across all months. Although some intermediate targets were frequently met, the failure to achieve the fastest response categories indicates structural inefficiencies in initial triage and dispatch systems. These metrics underscore gaps in emergency readiness that require immediate policy attention. This data is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates how transparent, granular health statistics enable public scrutiny of service quality. By making such detailed performance records accessible, stakeholders can identify trends, hold authorities accountable, and drive evidence-based improvements in healthcare infrastructure and resource allocation.
