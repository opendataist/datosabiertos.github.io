Gobernador de Ucayali: "Si tuviéramos 30 o 40 millones de soles rápidamente podríamos bajar la anemia" | RPP Noticias

Gobernador de Ucayali: "Si tuviéramos 30 o 40 millones de soles rápidamente podríamos bajar la anemia" | RPP Noticias

Ucayali is facing a severe public health crisis, with half of its children under three years old suffering from anemia, making it the second most affected region in the country. Despite a slight recent decline, the regional governor considers current resources insufficient to achieve a real impact, given that the geographic complexity and isolation of indigenous communities drastically increase the cost of intervention logistics. The current administration advocates for significantly greater investment to reduce these rates and reach the national average. The relevance of this case for open data lies in its demonstration of how transparency in budget allocation and execution can reveal critical gaps between public policy and local realities. By making INEI data and progress in expenditure execution publicly available, it becomes evident that increased budgets do not automatically translate into effective solutions without proper distribution. This correlation between health and financial data enables citizens to demand more precise accountability regarding the efficiency of public fund usage. Finally, the article underscores the need to adapt prevention strategies to specific contexts, such as the Amazon, where limited access requires innovative approaches like food supplementation and family education. Open information about these regional disparities is essential for designing more equitable policies. Without robust and accessible data management, vulnerable regions continue to fall behind, perpetuating cycles of poverty and poor health that affect the cognitive and social development of future generations.
