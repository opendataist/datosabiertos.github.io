The widespread adoption of mobile technology in Spain has created a paradox in which high connectivity coexists with significant digital insecurity. Despite the ubiquity of internet access, a substantial portion of users feels unsafe online, leading to behavioral changes such as reducing their usage or physically blocking device sensors. This sentiment highlights a critical gap between technological integration and user confidence, suggesting that mere accessibility does not equate to perceived safety or security literacy among the general population. Deep-seated mistrust extends to artificial intelligence and personal data management, with many users fearing unauthorized surveillance and struggling to distinguish between automated systems and humans. Concerns persist regarding data privacy, including the permanence of digital identities after death and the handling of personal information. These anxieties reflect a broader societal struggle to understand data ownership and control, indicating that current technological interfaces often fail to provide transparent or intuitive mechanisms for users to manage their digital footprints effectively. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as the prevalence of digital myths and security fears can hinder public trust in data-driven services. Open data relies on the premise that accessible information benefits society, yet these entrenched misconceptions and privacy concerns may lead to resistance against sharing or utilizing open datasets. Addressing these issues requires not only robust cybersecurity measures but also targeted educational campaigns to demystify technology, thereby fostering an environment where open data can be embraced without the overshadowing anxiety of potential misuse or invisible threats.
