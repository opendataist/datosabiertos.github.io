Detector: Avianca no publicó trino sarcástico sobre cancelación de vuelos

The article debunks a viral false claim suggesting that Avianca intentionally exploited fuel shortages to generate panic and cancel flights. Fact-checkers confirmed that no such statement originated from the airline’s official channels, revealing the misinformation as a fabrication unrelated to the carrier’s actual crisis response. This distinction is crucial, as Avianca publicly maintained that its operations were disrupted by genuine supply chain failures rather than strategic malice. The context involves a real fuel shortage caused by a refinery failure in Cartagena and rising demand, which forced airlines to suspend flights temporarily. Government officials and industry leaders clarified that while availability was critically low, there was no structural scarcity, only a temporary logistical bottleneck that has since been resolved through emergency measures and imports. This story is relevant to open data because it highlights the necessity of transparent, accessible information from public entities and corporations during crises. When data regarding supply chains and operational decisions is not readily available or verified, rumors spread rapidly, eroding public trust. Ensuring that official communications and factual records are open and easy to verify helps combat disinformation and maintains confidence in institutional responses.
