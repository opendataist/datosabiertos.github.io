Eight early-career professors win NSF development awards | Cornell Chronicle

Eight early-career professors win NSF development awards | Cornell Chronicle

Eight Cornell assistant professors have received prestigious National Science Foundation awards to advance research in diverse fields, including artificial intelligence, climate science, and biomedical engineering. These projects aim to enhance AI training data efficiency, improve extreme weather predictions, and develop novel treatments for breast cancer-related swelling. The funding supports early-career faculty who demonstrate potential to lead academic advances while integrating significant educational components into their work. A central theme among the recipients is the creation of transparent, accessible, and efficient systems for public benefit. For instance, researchers are developing statistical methods to engineer open, transparent public interest systems that optimize resource allocation in sectors like health and government. By focusing on data-driven decision-making that accounts for missing information, this work directly supports the principles of open data by promoting accountability and efficiency in societal structures, ensuring that data usage benefits broader communities rather than remaining opaque. Furthermore, the emphasis on open knowledge extends to education and community engagement. Several projects prioritize training underrepresented groups and providing publicly available resources for municipal workers and hobbyists. This commitment to disseminating technical skills and research findings reinforces the open data movement’s goal of democratizing access to information. By combining rigorous research with broad educational outreach, these initiatives foster a more inclusive environment where data literacy and scientific understanding are shared widely, enhancing societal resilience and innovation.
