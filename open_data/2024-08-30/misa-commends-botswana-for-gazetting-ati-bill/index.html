MISA commends Botswana for gazetting ATI bill

Botswana’s gazetted Freedom of Information Bill represents a critical step toward institutionalizing transparency in a region lagging in legal frameworks for public access. The legislation introduces progressive mechanisms, such as mandatory proactive disclosure and a fee-free request system, which distinguish it favorably against regional peers. By establishing an independent oversight commission and fair complaint procedures, the bill aims to significantly enhance governmental accountability and improve the nation’s standing in international press freedom metrics. However, the bill’s democratic legitimacy and practical effectiveness hinge on addressing current gaps in its drafting and implementation. Stakeholders emphasize that limited public participation during the formulation stage risks undermining national ownership of the law. Furthermore, while the definition of "information" is forward-looking, it requires greater specificity regarding personal data contexts. Ensuring that future regulations operationalizing the law are developed through inclusive, proportional processes will be essential to meet citizens’ needs and prevent bureaucratic bottlenecks. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives because legislation mandates the proactive disclosure of information, shifting the default from secrecy to openness. It provides the legal foundation necessary for transforming raw government records into accessible, reusable public data. Without such statutory guarantees, open data efforts remain vulnerable to administrative discretion and inconsistent practices. Enacting this bill would not only protect the right to know but also create a structured, transparent environment where data can be reliably harvested and utilized for public benefit.
