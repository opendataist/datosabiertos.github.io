Un proyecto de inversión de agua y saneamiento en el Perú puede durar más de 10 años en implementarse, según SUNASS | RPP Noticias

Peru is grappling with a severe crisis in which millions of people lack access to safe drinking water and sewage services. This gap is exacerbated by sluggish bureaucratic processes and rapid urban growth. The implementation of infrastructure projects often takes more than a decade, rendering solutions obsolete as populations expand and demands increase. This inefficiency highlights a critical disconnect between political promises and the practical realities of public investment management, leaving vulnerable communities without basic services for years. The solution requires a strategic shift toward agile governance and region-specific planning, rather than relying solely on increased funding or tariffs. Current regulatory frameworks are burdened by unnecessary bureaucracy that delays execution and reduces the quality of engineering work. To effectively close these service gaps, authorities must streamline approval processes and prioritize long-term urban planning that anticipates demographic changes, ensuring that investments remain relevant and effective throughout their lifecycle. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and real-time data to drive policy decisions. Effective monitoring of service indicators, project timelines, and population trends allows for better accountability and helps identify regional disparities. By leveraging open data, stakeholders can move beyond vague promises to evidence-based strategies that address the root causes of inequality and improve the efficiency of public resource allocation in essential services.
