Cocineras y defensoras del maíz

The article highlights the disparity between the high presence of women in the hospitality workforce and their invisibility within male-dominated culinary hierarchies. This imbalance underscores a structural gap: although women constitute the majority of the sector, they rarely occupy leadership roles that receive media visibility. This reveals a critical disconnect between the demographic reality of labor and its social and professional recognition within the gastronomic industry. The cultural role of women as guardians of traditions and maize—a sacred and central element of Mexican identity—is emphasized. Their work goes beyond mere food preparation, serving as an act of love and historical preservation that connects families to their roots. This perspective humanizes cooking, positioning women not only as workers but also as active agents in cultural continuity and the preservation of ancestral practices tied to the land. The relevance to open data lies in the need to collect and publish disaggregated data that accurately reflect the true gender distribution at all levels of the gastronomic sector, including hidden power positions. Without access to transparent, structured information about who truly holds control in kitchens, it is impossible to design effective policies to address these inequalities. The visibility of open data is crucial for democratizing information about who genuinely contributes to culinary and labor heritage, enabling an objective assessment of inclusivity within the industry.
