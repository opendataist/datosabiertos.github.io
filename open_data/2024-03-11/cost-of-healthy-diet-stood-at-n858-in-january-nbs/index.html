Cost of healthy diet stood at N858 in January — NBS |

The National Bureau of Statistics has released data revealing the varying costs of maintaining a healthy diet across Nigeria, highlighting significant economic disparities in nutritional access. By establishing the Cost of a Healthy Diet as a lower bound for physical and economic accessibility, the report underscores how regional differences and rising food prices directly impact citizens' ability to meet global dietary standards. This metric serves as a critical tool for understanding the financial barriers that prevent individuals from achieving adequate nutrition. Animal-source foods, fruits, and vegetables constitute the most expensive components of a healthy basket relative to the calories they provide, whereas legumes and nuts remain the most affordable. The fact that these essential groups drive up costs disproportionately suggests that shifting dietary habits toward healthier choices may impose severe financial burdens on low-income households. This price structure reveals a fundamental tension between nutritional quality and affordability, particularly as the cost of healthy diets outpaces general inflation trends. This analysis is vital to the open data movement as it provides transparent, granular metrics for monitoring public health and economic policy effectiveness. By making detailed cost data publicly available, researchers and policymakers can identify vulnerable regions and specific food groups that require targeted intervention. Such transparency fosters accountability and enables data-driven advocacy for food security, ensuring that societal resources are allocated to bridge the gap between nutritional guidelines and economic reality.
