Total debt stands at $846.6million

Total debt stands at $846.6million

Samoa’s government debt decreased significantly in the final year, marking a positive fiscal shift despite large outstanding obligations to multilateral and bilateral lenders. This reduction in liabilities suggests improved debt management, even as total expenditures rose, driven largely by increased public sector employment and higher spending on core civil services. Revenue stability remains anchored in tax collections, which saw substantial growth, providing a stronger financial base to support these rising operational costs. The distribution of funds highlights a prioritization of health, education, and general public administration, indicating a commitment to maintaining essential social services and administrative capacity. While grants from overseas partners contribute to revenue, the domestic tax base has become the primary engine for funding government activities. This trend underscores the importance of robust internal fiscal collection mechanisms over external aid dependency for sustaining long-term government operations. This data is highly relevant to open_data initiatives because it demonstrates how transparently published government finance statistics enable stakeholders to track national fiscal health and policy implementation. By making detailed breakdowns of debt, revenue, and expenditure publicly available, governments foster accountability and allow researchers, journalists, and citizens to analyze economic trends. Such accessible information empowers communities to hold leaders accountable for fiscal decisions and ensures that public resources are aligned with stated national priorities.
