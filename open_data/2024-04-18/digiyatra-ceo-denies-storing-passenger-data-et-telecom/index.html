Digiyatra CEO denies storing passenger data - ET Telecom

The Digiyatra facial recognition app faces scrutiny due to its vendor’s criminal history, raising significant concerns about data security and corporate accountability in digital infrastructure. Despite the foundation’s claims that user biometric data remains only on personal devices and is purged quickly, the underlying controversy highlights the risks associated with opaque private partnerships in government-linked public services. This incident underscores the critical importance of vendor vetting and the potential for data exploitation when proprietary platforms handle sensitive citizen information without transparent oversight mechanisms. Relevance to open_data stems from the foundation’s refusal to disclose security audits or make information available under the Right to Information Act, despite its significant government ties. This lack of transparency prevents public scrutiny of how biometric data is managed, stored, and secured, creating a dangerous precedent where critical infrastructure operates in a black box. Open data advocates emphasize that such essential systems must be fully auditable and accessible to ensure trust and prevent misuse. The situation reveals a governance gap where privacy experts argue that the absence of published security practices renders the service unreliable. Without mandatory disclosure of data handling protocols, citizens cannot verify if their sensitive biometric data is truly protected from third-party breaches. This case serves as a vital warning about the necessity of enforcing open standards and accountability in all public-facing digital identities to safeguard individual privacy rights against corporate fraud and systemic negligence.
