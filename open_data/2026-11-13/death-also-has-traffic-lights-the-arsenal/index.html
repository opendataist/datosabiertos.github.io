Death also has traffic lights - The Arsenal

Death also has traffic lights - The Arsenal

Road safety in Mexico faces a structural crisis that treats accidents as a secondary issue, as evidenced by an overwhelming number of recorded crashes and a high number of fatalities. These figures show that prevention continues to be ignored by authorities, who tend to react only after tragedies occur rather than implementing proactive infrastructure and regulatory measures. It is crucial to understand that simply reducing fatalities does not justify stagnation, as the total volume of incidents continues to rise. This phenomenon reveals that current strategies are insufficient and that governments prioritize construction metrics over human life, perpetuating an environment where negligence is free to continue claiming unnecessary lives. This analysis is fundamental to the open data movement, as it underscores the need to transform raw statistics into tools for accountability. By making these data accessible and subjecting them to critical analysis, civil society can demand concrete interventions, compelling authorities to measure their success by lives saved rather than by obscured figures, thereby fostering governance based on transparency and the genuine protection of citizens.
