Canal Sur Turns Bullfighting Into Its Silver Bullet Against the Ratings Crisis

Canal Sur Turns Bullfighting Into Its Silver Bullet Against the Ratings Crisis

Bullfighting has ceased to be a secondary folkloric element on Canal Sur, becoming its primary strategy for audience capture. This paradigm shift has consolidated bullfighting broadcasts as the engine driving the regional channel’s share of the viewing audience, enabling RTVA to maintain its relevance in the face of market fragmentation and intense competition from national operators. The increased frequency and budget allocated to these broadcasts have yielded audience results significantly above the channel’s average. By reclaiming coverage of the April Fair and strengthening dedicated programs, the public broadcaster has succeeded in attracting millions of viewers, demonstrating that this type of programming serves as an effective strategic resource for ensuring television viability and leadership in its time slot. This approach is relevant to open data science because it exemplifies how public audience data can validate media cultural policies. Analyzing these metrics allows for an assessment of the efficiency of public fund usage in state television, providing an empirical basis for debates on cultural representation in public services and transparency in the management of audiovisual resources.
