Basques and Madrilenians led visits to Burgos in September

Basques and Madrilenians led visits to Burgos in September

The INE’s experimental statistics, which analyze the anonymous signals from mobile phones, represent a crucial innovation in the handling of open data. This methodology overcomes the time and detail limitations of traditional surveys, enabling precise and agile tracking of tourist flows. By using aggregated and anonymized data, it demonstrates the potential of big data technology to generate more reliable and timely public information, thereby enhancing the transparency and quality of official statistics accessible to citizens. This approach reveals detailed patterns about tourist mobility, such as the origins of visitors and their preferred destinations in specific regions. The results indicate a slight decrease in the number of domestic tourists compared to previous periods, highlighting clear preferences for neighboring autonomous communities. The ability to break down these movements by origin and length of stay provides a granular view that was previously difficult to obtain, facilitating a deeper understanding of tourist behavior. The relevance to open data lies in the possibility of democratizing access to high-resolution geospatial and demographic information. By converting phone signals into statistical indicators, it opens the door to innovative applications in urban planning, resource management, and economic analysis. This encourages the creation of ecosystems where third parties can use these open data to develop services that improve the tourist experience and the operational efficiency of destinations, fostering a smarter and more distributed use of public information.
