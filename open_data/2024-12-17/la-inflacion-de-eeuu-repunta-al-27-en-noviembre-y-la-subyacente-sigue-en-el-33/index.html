La inflación de EEUU repunta al 2,7% en noviembre y la subyacente sigue en el 3,3%

The recent US inflation data indicates a slight resurgence in consumer prices, with the general index rising to 2.7% year-on-year and core inflation holding steady at 3.3%. Although both figures met expectations, this marks two consecutive months of increases, signaling that the downward trend in prices has stalled. Housing costs remain the primary driver of this monthly inflation, highlighting persistent pressures within the broader economy despite the Federal Reserve’s 2% target. Expert analysis reveals a divergence in outlook regarding future monetary policy. While some strategists believe the Federal Reserve will likely proceed with a modest interest rate cut due to balanced economic risks, others argue that policymakers may hesitate. The uncertainty stems from the need to observe further labor market and inflation data before implementing additional easing, as the impact of previous cuts must fully materialize before considering further adjustments. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the importance of transparent, real-time economic indicators for public and private sector decision-making. Access to timely and granular data, such as the specific contribution of housing and food categories, allows for more accurate analysis of inflationary trends. By making these datasets openly available, stakeholders can better assess policy impacts, fostering informed discussions on economic stability and monetary regulation.
