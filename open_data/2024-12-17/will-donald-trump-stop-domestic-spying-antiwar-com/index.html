Will Donald Trump Stop Domestic Spying? - Antiwar.com

The article argues that the FBI and DEA are systematically violating Fourth Amendment privacy rights by utilizing foreign spyware to access devices without search warrants. By acquiring tools like Pegasus and Graphite, these agencies bypass judicial oversight, effectively treating digital data as unprotected regardless of whether the target is domestic or abroad. This practice represents a persistent erosion of constitutional safeguards, where executive branch actions consistently negate the legal protections citizens are entitled to against unreasonable searches and seizures. A critical concern highlighted is the government’s assertion that constitutional restrictions do not apply to operations conducted outside U.S. borders. Despite Supreme Court precedent establishing that the Constitution travels with the government, agencies like the DEA maintain that they operate beyond legal reach when engaging in overseas surveillance. This justification allows for warrantless hacking and data extraction, creating a dangerous precedent where the state can circumvent fundamental civil liberties simply by crossing a geographic boundary, thereby undermining the principle that law enforcement must adhere to the same rules everywhere. This behavior is highly relevant to open data advocacy because it demonstrates how opaque government technology procurement and secret surveillance methods exacerbate the lack of transparency in digital rights. When agencies rely on proprietary, foreign-made software acquired through secretive channels, public scrutiny and accountability are severely limited. The situation underscores the urgent need for greater transparency in government software usage and data handling, as closed systems facilitate unconstitutional surveillance and prevent citizens from understanding how their personal information is being harvested and stored by federal authorities.
