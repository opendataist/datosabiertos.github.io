Kenya Open data Should be Presented and Marketed as Open for all - KachTech Media

The article argues that Kenya’s open data initiative suffers from a critical disconnect between data availability and actual public utility. Although the data is technically open and accessible, its potential is undermined by a presentation strategy that exclusively targets developers and the concept of "innovation." This narrow focus confuses the general public, who mistakenly believe that using open data requires creating complex new applications rather than simply accessing and interpreting existing information. Consequently, the data remains underutilized domestically despite high traffic volumes, as most active users are foreign experts rather than local citizens. To rectify this, open data must be democratized to include non-technical stakeholders such as bloggers, teachers, farmers, and health workers. By shifting the narrative from technological innovation to broad societal access, the initiative can foster genuine community engagement and practical application. The current model risks alienating the majority of the population by imposing a barrier to entry that is not aligned with the everyday needs of Kenyans. True openness requires integrating diverse voices into the ecosystem, ensuring that data serves as a tool for everyone, not just a platform for app developers. This perspective is highly relevant to the open data movement because it highlights a common global pitfall: prioritizing technical infrastructure over human-centered design. Open data initiatives often fail if they do not actively cultivate demand and understanding among the general public. The article emphasizes that success depends on inclusive communication and partnerships with diverse community groups, such as bloggers, to bridge the gap between raw government data and actionable public knowledge. Without this broader engagement, open data remains an idle resource rather than a catalyst for social and economic improvement.
