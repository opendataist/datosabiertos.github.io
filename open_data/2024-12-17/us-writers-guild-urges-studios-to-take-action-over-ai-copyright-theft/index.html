US writers guild urges studios to take action over AI copyright “theft”

US writers guild urges studios to take action over AI copyright “theft”

The Writers Guild of America has issued a stern directive to major Hollywood studios and streaming platforms, demanding they initiate legal action against technology firms utilizing copyrighted scripts to train artificial intelligence models. This intervention marks a critical escalation in the ongoing conflict over intellectual property rights, shifting the responsibility from the creators directly to the studios who hold the copyrights in trust. Central to this dispute is the argument that tech companies have engaged in the unauthorized wholesale theft of creative labor to amass capital, only to subsequently sell AI services back to the entertainment industry. The Guild emphasizes that the studios’ collective bargaining agreement imposes a fiduciary duty to protect these rights, meaning inaction constitutes a breach of their obligation to defend writers against the plundering of their literary works without permission or compensation. This development is highly relevant to open data because it establishes a significant legal precedent for the unauthorized use of creative works in machine learning datasets. By forcing studios to assert copyright claims against AI trainers, the WGA is challenging the current norms of data scraping and fair use in the tech sector. This could restrict the availability of high-quality, licensed text data for AI development, forcing companies to pursue alternative, potentially less comprehensive data sourcing methods while raising complex questions about the ethical boundaries of data utilization in open and proprietary systems.
