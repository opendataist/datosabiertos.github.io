REVEALED: These are the 10 most dangerous streets in Llandudno

The analysis of open police data reveals that Llandudno’s crime landscape is heavily concentrated in specific zones, with Tudno accounting for over a third of incidents and violent offenses comprising the largest category. While overall crime rates remain relatively high compared to the national average, there is a notable decline in violent crime, alongside unusually low rates of vehicle theft and robbery. This suggests that public safety interventions might be more effective if they move beyond broad statistics to address the localized hotspots where specific types of antisocial and violent behavior cluster. At the street level, the data highlights distinct patterns tied to local geography and land use, such as shoplifting dominating near shopping areas and violent crime prevalent in residential zones with high deprivation or transient rental populations. By leveraging anonymized, street-level open data, communities can identify these nuanced trends, understanding that not all high-crime streets share the same underlying causes. This granularity allows for targeted community policing and urban planning strategies that address the unique drivers of crime in each neighborhood, rather than applying generic solutions across the entire town. This article is relevant to open_data because it demonstrates the value of granular, geospatial public records in transforming raw statistics into actionable local intelligence. It illustrates how accessible government datasets can empower residents and planners to visualize safety issues accurately, distinguishing between approximate crime concentrations and actual hotspots. Ultimately, it underscores the importance of open data transparency in fostering informed civic engagement and enabling evidence-based decision-making for community safety and urban development.
