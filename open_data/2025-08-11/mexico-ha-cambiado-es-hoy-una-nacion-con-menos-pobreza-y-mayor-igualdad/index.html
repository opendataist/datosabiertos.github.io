México ha cambiado: es hoy una nación con menos pobreza y mayor igualdad

México ha cambiado: es hoy una nación con menos pobreza y mayor igualdad

The article argues that the 2024 Household Income and Expenditure Survey reveals a significant rise in real household incomes and a substantial reduction in inequality during the 2018–2024 period. This data contradicts narratives based solely on GDP growth, demonstrating that average household earnings have increased meaningfully due to improved labor policies and targeted social subsidies, marking a clear recovery from previous economic downturns. From an open data perspective, this report highlights the critical value of publicly accessible, statistically rigorous datasets for validating public policy outcomes. The author emphasizes that the National Institute of Statistics and Geography provides strictly comparable and entirely available data, allowing independent verification of claims regarding poverty reduction. This transparency empowers researchers to analyze the direct impact of government programs on vulnerable populations, fostering accountability and evidence-based debate. The analysis concludes that the distribution of income has shifted toward greater equity, with the lowest income deciles experiencing significantly higher growth rates than the wealthiest. By showing that policy interventions effectively prioritized the poor, the study illustrates how robust open statistical infrastructure can confirm socio-economic progress. This case serves as a model for how open data initiatives can bridge the gap between political promises and measurable social reality, ensuring that economic improvements are transparently documented and publicly verifiable.
