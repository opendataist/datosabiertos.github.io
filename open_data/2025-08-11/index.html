Why AI Needs Open Data to Protect Kids

The Digital Public Square: Keeping Truth and Trust Safe for Our Kids

Mothers who raise the next generation know that a healthy society needs trust. We taught our children to check their sources and value honesty. Today, we must teach this same lesson for the digital world. Data shapes our laws, our economy, and our health. This data creates a living digital ecosystem. For this system to help everyone, it must be open, easy to access, and clear.

New technology and laws are changing how we use data. These changes bring both good and bad results. We need to understand them to leave a better world for our kids.

The Promise of “AI-Ready” Transparency

Algorithms now help decide policies for healthcare and money planning. For these systems to work fairly, data must be “AI-ready.” This means data must be easy to find, understand, and use. If we structure data this way, AI systems can understand the context correctly. Following FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) prevents mistakes. This shift lets more people access important insights. Global policies will then rely on real facts, not made-up errors. This approach protects the public good.

The Limits of Artificial Intelligence

We must be careful with the tools we use. Recent studies show that Large Language Models (LLMs) do not truly understand the world. These models rely on patterns, not real understanding. They cannot track changes or reason about new situations. LLMs cannot guarantee that data is correct. They are tools for finding patterns, not sources of truth. Humans must watch over them closely. We cannot assume an open-source model is safe. We must check it ourselves.

The Legal Backbone of Freedom

Laws help protect our freedom. The proposed People’s Freedom of Information Act in the Philippines aims to make the right to information official. This law changes openness from a choice to a requirement. It requires data to be easy for computers to read. It also punishes those who do not follow the rules. This makes open data a legal duty. It gives the public strong oversight. Transparency becomes a right, not just a favor.

In Spain, leaders are changing laws to balance transparency with privacy. They want to create an independent group to watch over this. This group will protect public accountability while keeping personal data safe. This legal structure builds trust. Data sharing stays secure and responsible.

When Privacy Blocks Transparency

Not all laws help. A proposed Personal Data Protection Law in Paraguay might block government transparency. This law creates many rules, such as requiring permission to view records of public officials. Private citizens need strict privacy. However, public officials should have less privacy to ensure they are accountable. Laws that let officials hide information about their work under the name of privacy hurt public oversight. We must push for simpler data formats. These formats promote openness instead of secrecy.

Open Data in Action: Housing, Health, and Economy

Open data helps us see the truth behind the news.

Housing and Accountability In Spain, short-term tourist rentals are making housing harder to find. New laws in 2025 allow homeowner groups to stop property conversions. Real-time data on housing is needed to enforce this law. Open data lets citizens and researchers watch for rule-breaking. It finds fake registrations. This process ensures that policies bring real stability to families.

Health and Equity Open data shows important health trends in Mexico. Early 2024 data shows a small rise in deaths. Heart disease and diabetes are the main causes. Chihuahua has the highest death rate. Analysts use rates adjusted for age. This method allows fair comparisons between regions. This transparency helps experts find health gaps. It helps check if medical treatments work. Resources can then go where they are needed most.

Economic Reality Open data corrects the story. The 2024 Household Income and Expenditure Survey in Mexico shows rising real incomes and less inequality. This fact contradicts stories that only look at GDP. This detailed data lets independent groups verify poverty reduction. It connects political promises to real social results. Data on Mexican households shows how money pressures change spending. Families spend less on education and fun to pay for health and food. Watching these trends helps us understand how economic challenges affect family life.

The Human Element of Data

Every dataset has a human story. The disappearance of a censista (census taker) in Mexico shows the link between worker safety and data quality. These workers are the foundation of accurate population stats. If they are missing, data flow stops. This hurts the accuracy of information. Keeping these workers safe is necessary for trustworthy, open, and accessible public information.

Practical Tools for Civic Engagement

People who want to use this data need to know the tools.

Understanding Statistics When you see a statistic, like the INEGI report showing that nearly half of Mexican youth are not working, you must understand what it means. High-quality, detailed public stats are essential for good policy. This transparency shows systemic inequalities. It lets researchers and citizens find gaps. They can then push for specific solutions. To ensure you read these numbers correctly, you can read our guide on how to read a statistic before you quote it.

Navigating Legal Requests When data is hidden, you have the right to ask for it. The People’s Freedom of Information Act in the Philippines and similar laws elsewhere allow you to demand access. Asking for data can be hard. If you face resistance or denial, knowing your rights is key. You can learn more about your options by reading our guide on how to file a FOIA request — and what to do when it is denied.

Technical Accessibility Open-source models like OpenAI’s gpt-oss:20b show how they think. However, using them is often limited by computer power. Just because data is open does not mean it is easy to use. When you get data from government sites, you must understand the technical side. Whether you use CKAN, DCAT, or OpenDataSoft, knowing how to use these systems helps you get the data you need quickly. You can find technical help in our guide on how to use an open-data portal’s API: CKAN, DCAT and OpenDataSoft.

The Value of Open Licenses

Open data is very useful for users who want to learn and check facts. It lets them verify claims on their own. Users can spot trends that official stories might hide. For example, Mexican household survey data let users see rising incomes despite political talk. This transparency builds trust. Users feel more confident in the information they use. They can make smart choices about health, housing, and money.
