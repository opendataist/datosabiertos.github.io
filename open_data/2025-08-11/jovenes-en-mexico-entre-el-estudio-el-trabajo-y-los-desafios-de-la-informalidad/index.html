Jóvenes en México: entre el estudio, el trabajo y los desafíos de la informalidad

The INEGI’s recent demographic report highlights a critical juncture for Mexican youth, revealing that nearly half of the young population remains economically inactive, primarily due to educational pursuits or domestic responsibilities. This data exposes significant gender disparities, particularly among women, who are largely excluded from the formal labor market compared to their male counterparts. The narrative underscores that this inactivity is not merely a lack of employment but a structural phenomenon influenced by age, education levels, and traditional gender roles, necessitating a deeper understanding of the diverse realities facing the 15-to-29 age bracket. Despite having a substantial share of the active workforce, young people face disproportionately high unemployment rates and informal employment levels compared to the national average. This vulnerability indicates that existing economic structures often fail to integrate youth into secure, rights-based positions, leaving them exposed to economic instability. The implications suggest that current labor policies are insufficient in addressing the unique barriers young adults encounter, reinforcing a cycle of precarious work that undermines long-term social and economic development. This article is vital to open data because it demonstrates how high-quality, disaggregated public statistics serve as an essential tool for evidence-based policymaking. By making such detailed demographic and labor metrics publicly available, the government enables researchers, civil society, and developers to identify systemic inequalities and advocate for targeted interventions. Transparency in this data fosters accountability and allows for the creation of innovative solutions that address the specific needs of marginalized groups, ensuring that open data directly contributes to social equity and informed public debate.
