El Gobierno lo aprueba: la comunidad de vecinos votará si el propietario puede alquilar o no su vivienda temporalmente

El Gobierno lo aprueba: la comunidad de vecinos votará si el propietario puede alquilar o no su vivienda temporalmente

The surge in short-term tourist rentals in Spain is significantly tightening the long-term housing market, driving up rents and accelerating gentrification. This shift prioritizes temporary vacation stays over stable residential needs, creating severe affordability crises for residents and fostering neighborhood conflicts. Data indicates a clear trend: as properties are converted into high-yield tourist accommodations, the availability of permanent housing diminishes, disproportionately affecting local communities in major urban centers. To address these challenges, new legislation has been introduced to empower homeowners’ associations with greater control over building usage. Starting in 2025, converting a property for tourist use will require a reinforced majority vote from neighbors, effectively giving communities the power to block or approve such conversions. This legal change aims to rebalance the interests of long-term residents against the growing commercialization of residential zones, ensuring that housing decisions respect the social fabric of neighborhoods rather than solely market demands. This development is highly relevant to open data, as it underscores the critical need for transparent, accessible, and real-time datasets on housing stock and rental regulations. Accurate public information on the number of tourist versus long-term rentals is essential for policymakers, researchers, and citizens to monitor compliance, identify fraudulent registrations, and evaluate the effectiveness of anti-gentrification measures. Open data facilitates the creation of digital platforms, such as the proposed single registration window, which enhance regulatory enforcement and promote accountability in the housing sector.
