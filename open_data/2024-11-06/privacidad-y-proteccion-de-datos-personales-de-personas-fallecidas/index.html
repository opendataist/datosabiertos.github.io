Privacidad y protección de datos personales de personas fallecidas

The article concludes that current data protection frameworks must evolve to address the ethical, legal, and social complexities surrounding the personal data of deceased individuals. It emphasizes that personal information retains significant value and relevance after death, necessitating a robust normative approach that respects human dignity and protects the privacy of the deceased while acknowledging the legitimate interests of their heirs. A central challenge lies in balancing the rights of the deceased with the needs of living relatives, particularly within digital platforms and social networks where data remains active post-mortem. The text highlights the importance of specific platform policies that allow for the memorialization of accounts, ensuring that information is managed with respect and preventing improper treatment while preserving the memory of the individual for friends and family. This topic is critically relevant to open data because it defines the boundaries of data accessibility and longevity. Open data principles must account for the temporal limits of personal information rights, ensuring that transparency and access do not infringe upon the ethical standards required to honor the dead. Consequently, regulators and data custodians must integrate cultural perspectives and privacy safeguards into open data governance to prevent the dehumanizing exposure of sensitive historical or biographical data.
