60 of SEND care plans not completed within legal time limit

60 of SEND care plans not completed within legal time limit

Suffolk County Council is struggling to meet legal deadlines for completing Education, Health and Care Plans, with a significant portion of requests exceeding the statutory twenty-week limit. Although recent data indicates an improvement in processing speed and a rise in overall completions compared to the previous year, the system remains severely backlogged. This delay leaves many children without the essential support they require, highlighting a critical gap between service provision and statutory obligations. The primary driver of this bottleneck is a national shortage of education psychologists, which hampers the council’s ability to provide timely advice and assessments. In response, local authorities are investing in recruitment and utilizing private sector resources to alleviate pressure. However, officials acknowledge that these measures are insufficient to overcome the broader systemic strain caused by funding that has not kept pace with growing demand, underscoring the fragility of current special needs infrastructure. This situation is highly relevant to open data because the disclosure of performance metrics via Freedom of Information requests reveals critical insights into public service efficiency and accountability. By making these figures public, stakeholders can scrutinize local government performance, advocate for necessary policy reforms, and highlight the real-world impact of systemic failures on vulnerable families. Transparency in such data is essential for driving the urgent structural changes needed to support children with special educational needs.
