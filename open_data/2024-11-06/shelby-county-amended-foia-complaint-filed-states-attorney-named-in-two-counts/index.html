Shelby County – Amended FOIA Complaint Filed – State’s Attorney Named In Two Counts

The article highlights a significant transparency failure within Shelby County’s emergency services, illustrating the critical need for robust open data enforcement. A Freedom of Information Act lawsuit revealed that public donations intended for a county-affiliated dive team were held by a nonprofit foundation rather than county-controlled accounts. This arrangement allowed the foundation to charge fees on public funds, a practice lacking clear legal authority and raising serious concerns about the proper stewardship of taxpayer and donor money. Further investigation uncovered severe deficiencies in record-keeping and accountability. Essential receipts and financial documents appear to have been removed or destroyed during periods of non-operation, creating an opaque trail that hinders public oversight. The State’s Attorney’s failure to promptly inform county officials about the litigation and inconsistent responses to information requests suggest a systemic disregard for public access rights. These omissions prevent citizens from verifying how public resources are managed and spent. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates because it exposes how vague interpretations of "public body" can be exploited to withhold financial records. It demonstrates that volunteer organizations performing public functions must be subject to the same transparency laws as government entities. Without strict adherence to open records statutes, public entities can obscure financial irregularities and lack of accountability, undermining democratic trust and preventing citizens from holding their government accountable for the use of public funds.
