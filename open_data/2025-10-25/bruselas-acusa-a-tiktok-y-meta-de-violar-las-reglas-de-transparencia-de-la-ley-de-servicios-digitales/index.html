Bruselas acusa a TikTok y Meta de violar las reglas de transparencia de la Ley de Servicios Digitales

Bruselas acusa a TikTok y Meta de violar las reglas de transparencia de la Ley de Servicios Digitales

The European Commission accuses TikTok and Meta of violating the Digital Services Act by hindering researchers’ access to public data. These platforms have implemented procedural barriers and deceptive interface mechanisms that obstruct the identification of illegal content, thereby compromising transparency and digital security. This situation is crucial for open data, as the regulation requires Big Tech companies to facilitate independent scrutiny of their algorithms and moderation practices. By imposing unnecessary obstacles for academics, these companies limit society’s ability to audit real-world social impacts, contradicting the fundamental principle of opening up systems and ensuring public accountability. The significance of the case lies in establishing a firm precedent: data transparency is not optional but an essential legal requirement. The potential imposition of severe fines reinforces the obligation of platforms to provide accessible and reliable data, ensuring that independent research can fulfill its vital role in safeguarding democracy and user safety.
