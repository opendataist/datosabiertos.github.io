California Expands Delete Act Disclosures with SB-361

California Expands Delete Act Disclosures with SB-361

California has significantly expanded regulatory oversight of data brokers through SB-361, mandating extensive disclosures in registration filings. This legislation forces these entities to reveal the specific categories of sensitive personal information they collect, including biometric, health, and demographic data, while also requiring transparency regarding data sharing with foreign governments, law enforcement, and AI developers. The expanded requirements aim to illuminate the opaque practices of the data brokerage industry by exposing who accesses consumer data and for what purposes. By compelling brokers to disclose their data handling practices, the law addresses growing public and regulatory concerns about privacy erosion and the unauthorized exploitation of personal information by third parties. This development is crucial for open data advocates as it establishes a precedent for mandatory transparency in data aggregation markets. Enhanced regulatory scrutiny and detailed public records of data broker activities can empower researchers and citizens to better understand data flows. Ultimately, these measures strengthen accountability mechanisms, ensuring that the collection and distribution of personal information are subject to greater public and governmental examination.
