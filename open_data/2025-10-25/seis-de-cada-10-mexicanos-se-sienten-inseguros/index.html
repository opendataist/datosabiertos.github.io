Seis de cada 10 mexicanos se sienten inseguros

Seis de cada 10 mexicanos se sienten inseguros

The ENSU reveals that a majority of Mexicans perceive their cities as unsafe, with significant variation across localities and gender. Women report higher insecurity rates than men, highlighting a gendered dimension of public safety concerns. This widespread fear drives behavioral changes, as many citizens alter their habits to avoid carrying valuables in public spaces. Trust in security institutions is unevenly distributed, with the military branches garnering the highest confidence for preventing crime. In contrast, municipal and state police receive significantly lower approval ratings. This disparity suggests a public preference for federal military involvement over local law enforcement for maintaining order and addressing criminal activities. This data is crucial for open data initiatives, as it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible statistics for analyzing social well-being. By making such survey results freely available, researchers and policymakers can better understand the root causes of insecurity and develop targeted, evidence-based strategies to improve urban safety and restore community trust in public institutions.
