Colombia perdió 5,68 millones de hectáreas de bosques en 40 años; zonas afectadas

Colombia perdió 5,68 millones de hectáreas de bosques en 40 años; zonas afectadas

MapBiomas Colombia has released Collection 3.0, providing a continuous four-decade satellite record of land cover changes from 1985 to 2024. This collaborative effort, led by Gaia Amazonas and supported by multiple academic and civil society institutions, documents significant environmental shifts, including the loss of millions of hectares of forest, particularly in the Amazon region. The data reveals that deforestation is highly concentrated in specific departments, highlighting critical zones for conservation and policy intervention. The platform stands out as a free, open-access resource designed to democratize environmental information. It allows users to conduct personalized analyses across various territorial scales and themes, from glaciers to mangroves. By offering these tools without barriers, the initiative supports evidence-based decision-making and strengthens territorial defense, ensuring that scientists, policymakers, and communities can access high-quality geospatial data to address environmental challenges effectively. This release is highly relevant to open data because it exemplifies how transparent, collaborative data ecosystems can drive social and ecological justice. By making decades of monitoring data publicly available, MapBiomas empowers indigenous groups and researchers to use spatial information for policy advocacy and land management. The accompanying award ceremony underscores the practical impact of open data, recognizing innovative applications in climate research, economic estimation of deforestation, and indigenous territorial planning, thereby proving that accessible data is a fundamental tool for protecting biodiversity and rights.
