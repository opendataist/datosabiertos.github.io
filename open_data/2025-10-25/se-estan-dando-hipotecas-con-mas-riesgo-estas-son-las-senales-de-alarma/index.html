¿Se están dando hipotecas con más riesgo?: estas son las señales de alarma

¿Se están dando hipotecas con más riesgo?: estas son las señales de alarma

The Spanish real estate sector is experiencing a notable increase in mortgage issuance, accompanied by a worrying trend toward loans with higher financial risk. The share of credits exceeding 80% of the property’s value has more than doubled in just over a year, reaching levels not seen since before the 2019 crisis. This situation is primarily explained by the surge in housing prices relative to buyers’ savings capacity, particularly among young people, combined with increased bank competition that has led to the relaxation of some financing limits due to falling interest rates. Despite the increased risk assumed by banks, experts maintain that there is no cause for alarm in the short term. The current market structure is considered stable because most mortgages are fixed-rate, which protects buyers against payment increases, and property valuations remain solid thanks to excess demand. Moreover, strict supervision by the European Central Bank acts as a safety filter, preventing financial institutions from irresponsibly relaxing their prudential criteria, unlike what occurred in previous cycles. This analysis is crucial for the field of open data and financial transparency, as it highlights the need to monitor indicators such as loan-to-value ratios and preliminary delinquency rates to detect systemic risks early. Access to public data on housing trends and credit behavior enables researchers, citizens, and regulators to assess market health beyond aggregate figures. Understanding these dynamics helps build more robust information systems capable of alerting to real estate imbalances before they escalate into widespread economic crises.
