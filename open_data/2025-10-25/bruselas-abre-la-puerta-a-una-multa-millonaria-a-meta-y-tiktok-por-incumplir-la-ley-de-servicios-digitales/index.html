Bruselas abre la puerta a una multa millonaria a Meta y TikTok por incumplir la ley de servicios digitales

Bruselas abre la puerta a una multa millonaria a Meta y TikTok por incumplir la ley de servicios digitales

The European Commission has initiated legal action against TikTok and Meta for alleged non-compliance with the Digital Services Act, claiming that they unjustifiably hinder researchers’ access to internal data and limit mechanisms for reporting illegal content. This accusation underscores the critical need to ensure algorithmic and operational transparency—a fundamental pillar in open data research—since reliable access to platform information is essential for auditing democratic health and digital security. Both companies deny the allegations, arguing that they comply with the regulations and highlighting the extent of their collaboration with the academic community. However, TikTok points to the tension between transparency obligations and data protection, while Meta emphasizes its technological improvements. This conflict highlights the practical challenges of implementing open data in complex corporate environments, where technical and legal barriers can impede the public scrutiny necessary for accountability. The relevance to open data lies in the fact that European legislation aims to empower society through accountability. By enabling successful appeals against content moderation decisions, it demonstrates how transparency is not merely a theoretical concept but an operational tool for defending users’ rights. The actual availability and usability of this data allow civil society and independent researchers to verify the legitimacy of platforms’ actions, ensuring that technology serves democracy rather than undermining it.
