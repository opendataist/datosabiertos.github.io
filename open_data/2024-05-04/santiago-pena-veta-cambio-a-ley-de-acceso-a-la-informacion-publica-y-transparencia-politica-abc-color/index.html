Santiago Peña veta cambio a Ley de acceso a la información pública y transparencia - Política - ABC Color

President Santiago Peña has partially vetoed a legislative proposal aimed at expanding public access to government information, specifically targeting the mandatory publication of statistical data collected by the National Institute of Statistics. The executive branch argues that forcing the public release of this information violates constitutional rights to privacy and intimacy. By exposing individual data points gathered for statistical purposes, the measure would undermine the legal concept of statistical secrecy, which is essential for protecting personal dignity and preventing the indiscriminate disclosure of sensitive citizen information. This decision highlights a critical tension between transparency initiatives and data protection frameworks, a core concern in open data governance. The administration contends that without anonymity and confidentiality guarantees, the trust required for citizens to provide accurate data to state entities would be eroded. Consequently, the proposed mandate could compromise the quality and feasibility of national statistical studies, demonstrating that unfettered data publication without proper safeguards may hinder rather than help institutional effectiveness and individual rights. The veto is particularly relevant to the open data community as it illustrates the delicate balance between the right to know and the obligation to protect personal privacy. It underscores the necessity of implementing robust anonymization techniques and legal frameworks that allow for the release of aggregated, non-sensitive data while preserving individual confidentiality. This case serves as a reminder that open data policies must be carefully designed to comply with constitutional standards, ensuring that transparency efforts do not inadvertently infringe upon fundamental human rights or disrupt essential statistical functions.
