Más de siete mil personas desocupadas en la región

The article highlights a notable increase in regional unemployment in early 2024, driven by workforce growth outpacing job creation. This shift reflects broader economic dynamics in which labor market participation is expanding, yet formal employment opportunities have not kept pace, resulting in higher joblessness. Additionally, the report notes a slight decline in informal employment, indicating a marginal improvement in labor formalization. Despite this positive trend, gender disparities persist, with women experiencing higher unemployment and informal employment rates than men, underscoring ongoing structural inequalities in the regional labor market. This data is relevant to open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how public statistics enable transparency in socio-economic monitoring. By making such detailed information accessible, stakeholders can analyze trends, identify vulnerable groups, and inform policy decisions aimed at fostering inclusive economic growth and improving labor conditions.
