The constitutional complaint against Education Minister Marco Antonio Ávila intensifies as political tensions rise ahead of the congressional vote. The debate centers on conflicting narratives regarding procurement irregularities within the National School Meals Program (JUNAEB). A former director’s allegations of exorbitant costs for school snacks have been vigorously denied by the agency, which attributes the cited figures to contracts established before the current administration took office. Significant friction also emerged regarding expert testimony, with opposition lawmakers alleging that invited legal advisors had lobbying ties to companies involved in these food contracts. Although these experts withdrew, the controversy highlights the difficulty of distinguishing objective technical analysis from partisan interests in legislative oversight. This dynamic underscores the challenges in establishing impartial fact-finding bodies when financial interests and political ideologies intersect. This case is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical importance of transparent, accessible, and immutable public information. The dispute over whether data was removed from transparency portals or merely edited in internal shared drives reveals how easily public records can be manipulated or obscured. It emphasizes the need for robust, verifiable digital infrastructure that prevents unauthorized alterations, ensuring that citizens and legislators can rely on accurate, unfiltered data for democratic accountability and informed decision-making.
