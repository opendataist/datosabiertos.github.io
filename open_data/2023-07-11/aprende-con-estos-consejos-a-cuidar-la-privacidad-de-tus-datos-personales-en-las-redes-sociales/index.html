Aprendé con estos consejos a cuidar la privacidad de tus datos personales en las redes sociales

Excessive sharing of personal information and geolocation data on social media exposes users to significant security risks, including fraud and identity theft. The study highlights that a culture of over-sharing, combined with the digital permanence of data, facilitates malicious manipulation and unintentional exposure, turning privacy into a vulnerable asset for millions of people in Latin America. The proposed solution involves actively managing privacy settings, using multi-factor authentication, and exercising caution with suspicious links. It emphasizes that protection is not static but requires constant review of permissions and avoiding the sharing of sensitive data such as exact locations or financial details. This critical stance suggests that a significant portion of security responsibility lies with the user, who must balance their desire for social connection with the need for cybersecurity safeguards. This article is crucial to the topic of open data because it illustrates the ethical and security tensions between transparency, voluntary information sharing, and individual protection. While the open data movement promotes the accessibility of public information to foster innovation and accountability, this report warns against the dangers of "inverse opacity," where private information becomes visible without consent. It serves as a vital reminder that, although open data aims to serve the common good, individual privacy must be preserved through clear legal frameworks and civic awareness, preventing the democratization of information from becoming a tool for exploitation.
