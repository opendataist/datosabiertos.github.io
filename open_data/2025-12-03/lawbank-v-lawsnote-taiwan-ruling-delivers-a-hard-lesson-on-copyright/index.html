Lawbank v Lawsnote: Taiwan ruling delivers a hard lesson on copyright

Lawbank v Lawsnote: Taiwan ruling delivers a hard lesson on copyright

This ruling establishes that comprehensive legal databases possess copyrightable value in their curated legislative histories, rejecting the notion that such factual compilations are immune to protection due to low creative thresholds. It underscores that the selection and arrangement of data reflect sufficient originality to warrant legal defense, challenging the assumption that raw legal information is entirely public domain. The decision highlights the legal risks associated with bulk data scraping, particularly when conducted for commercial gain. By automating the extraction of entire datasets, startups like Lawsnote failed the fair use test, as their actions displaced the original creator’s market and involved no transformative creative contribution. This confirms that simply avoiding the cost of compilation does not justify unauthorized reproduction, even if the end product offers a different search interface. Relevance to open data is critical here, as it delineates the boundary between accessible public records and protected database structures. While laws themselves are public, the specific expressive choices in their curation are not. This precedent warns open data initiatives that bulk scraping requires careful legal scrutiny, emphasizing that respecting intellectual property rights is essential for sustainable data innovation and market fairness.
