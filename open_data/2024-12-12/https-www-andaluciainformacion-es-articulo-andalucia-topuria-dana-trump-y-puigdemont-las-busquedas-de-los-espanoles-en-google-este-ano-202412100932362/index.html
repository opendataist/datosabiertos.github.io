The Google Trends data for 2024 reveals a distinct pattern in Spanish information consumption, driven primarily by major environmental disasters, global sporting events, and political upheavals. The severe impact of weather phenomena such as DANA in Valencia and international climate issues sparked widespread public inquiry into causes and solidarity efforts, highlighting how urgent crises mobilize digital attention toward understanding and responding to immediate environmental threats. Simultaneously, the Olympic Games and significant football tournaments dominated cultural interest, while entertainment and political elections fueled curiosity about key figures like Carles Puigdemont and Donald Trump. This surge in searches reflects a society deeply engaged with current affairs, using digital platforms not just for passive consumption but to seek explanations for complex social, athletic, and geopolitical developments that define the year. This data is relevant to open_data because it exemplifies how massive, freely available datasets can uncover real-time societal behaviors and collective priorities. By analyzing these anonymized search patterns, researchers and policymakers can identify emerging public concerns, track information needs during crises, and understand the cultural and political dynamics shaping public discourse without relying solely on traditional surveys.
