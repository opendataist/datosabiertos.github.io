SC Seeks Response Of J&K, Others On Plea For RTI portals - Kashmir Reader

SC Seeks Response Of J&K, Others On Plea For RTI portals - Kashmir Reader

The Supreme Court’s ongoing scrutiny of state compliance with mandatory RTI portal implementation highlights critical gaps in India’s digital transparency infrastructure. By identifying numerous states that have failed to establish these online platforms, the judicial process underscores the disconnect between legal mandates for accessibility and actual administrative execution. This delay obstructs citizens' ability to efficiently exercise their right to information, rendering the transparency law partially ineffective in many regions. Even where portals exist, they often lack necessary usability and accessibility standards, and many public authorities remain unregistered. These technical and administrative deficiencies suggest that mere existence of a website is insufficient for genuine openness. The court’s attention to these failures emphasizes that digital tools must be functional and inclusive to serve their purpose, rather than serving as superficial compliance markers. This issue is highly relevant to open data as it reveals how legal frameworks for information disclosure frequently fail to translate into usable, open digital ecosystems. When government data remains siloed or inaccessible through poorly maintained interfaces, the principles of open government are undermined. True open data requires not just legal permission to release information, but robust, user-friendly technical infrastructure that ensures equitable public access to these essential civic resources.
