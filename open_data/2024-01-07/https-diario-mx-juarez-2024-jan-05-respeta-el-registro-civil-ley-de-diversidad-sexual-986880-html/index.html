The article highlights a critical disconnect between Supreme Court jurisprudence and local legislative action regarding marriage equality in Chihuahua. Although the Civil Registry honors court rulings allowing same-sex marriages, the absence of specific statutory law creates a precarious legal framework. This legislative gap forces the administration to rely on individual judicial precedents rather than guaranteed rights, leaving citizens vulnerable to arbitrary interpretation and inconsistent application of the law during civil procedures. Conflict arises from systemic bureaucratic resistance, with reports indicating systematic denials and discriminatory practices against lesbian-mother families. While officials attribute rejections to administrative errors, advocates argue these are symptoms of a deeper lack of political will to formally codify these rights. This tension underscores the fragility of civil liberties when dependent solely on judicial mandates without corresponding legislative reinforcement, highlighting the urgent need for legal certainty to protect vulnerable populations from institutional prejudice. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how the lack of transparent, standardized public records exacerbates inequality. When data on marriage applications, denials, and demographics is fragmented or non-existent, it becomes impossible to audit government compliance with human rights standards. Open data advocacy can push for the publication of disaggregated administrative records, enabling civil society to monitor discrimination trends, verify official statistics, and hold institutions accountable through empirical evidence rather than anecdotal claims.
