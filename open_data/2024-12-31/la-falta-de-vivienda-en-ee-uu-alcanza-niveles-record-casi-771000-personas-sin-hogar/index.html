La falta de vivienda en EE.UU. alcanza niveles récord: casi 771,000 personas sin hogar

The article highlights a severe housing crisis in the United States, marked by a record number of people experiencing homelessness. This surge is driven by systemic issues including skyrocketing rental prices, stagnant wages, inflation, and the expiration of pandemic-era aid. The situation disproportionately affects families with children and marginalized communities, exacerbated by factors such as natural disasters and migration patterns. While certain demographic groups, such as veterans, have seen slight improvements, the overall trend indicates a deepening emergency. The political response remains contentious, with debates centering on whether immigration is a primary driver of housing costs or if the root causes lie in inadequate policy reforms and a lack of affordable housing stock. Legal decisions allowing cities to ban sleeping outdoors further complicate efforts to address the humanitarian aspect of this crisis. This data is crucial for open data initiatives, as it underscores the need for transparent, accessible, and comprehensive datasets to accurately track social indicators. Reliable open data enables researchers, policymakers, and advocates to analyze trends, identify disparities, and hold institutions accountable. By making such information publicly available, stakeholders can better understand the magnitude of the problem and develop evidence-based solutions to effectively mitigate the housing emergency.
