Kit Siang: Time for M’sia to transition from ‘official secrets country’ to ‘freedom of information nation’

The article advocates for Malaysia to transition from an "official secrets" state to a "freedom of information" nation, urging the government to declassify sensitive historical reports and books. This call for transparency aims to honor citizens' fundamental right to know and prevent government inefficiency or corruption. By releasing these documents, the state can demonstrate accountability and serve as a global role model for democratic openness in the modern information age. Historically, the push for transparency has faced significant resistance, often resulting in legal persecution for those who expose governmental malpractice. Past efforts to introduce freedom of information legislation were met with stricter laws that threatened civil liberties and judicial independence. These historical conflicts highlight the tension between state secrecy and public accountability, illustrating how draconian measures can undermine democratic principles and suppress the free press. This narrative is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical link between legal frameworks and the accessibility of public information. Open data initiatives cannot succeed without a political will to move beyond secrecy toward radical transparency. The argument emphasizes that true open government requires dismantling legal barriers that hide public records, ensuring that data is available to foster trust, enable civic oversight, and empower citizens with the information necessary for meaningful democratic participation.
