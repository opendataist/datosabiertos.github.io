Durango, a tres años de la pandemia por Covid-19

The article highlights that, although Durango has achieved economic recovery three years after the pandemic, this success obscures a broader national disconnect between macroeconomic growth and genuine citizen well-being. While the state ranks 16th in economic rebound, the analysis reveals that recovery metrics alone are insufficient indicators of societal health, as economic activity often coexists with persistent structural vulnerabilities and social deficits. Key findings indicate that economic gains do not automatically translate into improved living standards, particularly in regions where growth relies heavily on informal labor. Durango demonstrates relatively favorable social indicators compared to the national average, showing lower levels of informal employment and labor poverty. However, these figures underscore the urgent need to examine underlying social conditions, such as informal work and educational lag, rather than focusing solely on traditional economic benchmarks like GDP. This report is crucial for the open data community as it advocates for multi-dimensional data analysis. It emphasizes that transparent access to diverse datasets—spanning economics, labor informality, and social progress—is essential for policymakers to move beyond simplistic growth narratives. By integrating varied metrics, stakeholders can better understand the complex reality of post-pandemic recovery and design effective strategies that prioritize actual human welfare over abstract economic figures.
