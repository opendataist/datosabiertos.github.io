The government’s initiative to mobilize state-owned housing for social rent has a significant impact on regional disparities, particularly in Andalusia. By targeting idle assets from Sareb, this policy aims to alleviate housing shortages in areas with the highest concentration of such properties, shifting the focus away from traditional provincial capitals toward specific municipalities where demand is most acute. Data reveal a heterogeneous distribution, challenging the assumption that provincial capitals are the primary beneficiaries. In most regions, smaller towns hold the largest stock of these homes, indicating that effective housing policy must account for localized market dynamics rather than broad administrative boundaries. This spatial mismatch underscores the need for granular data to align supply with actual need. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the importance of transparent, granular datasets in public policy. Access to precise location and volume information enables governments to design equitable interventions. Moreover, publishing detailed asset inventories allows citizens and researchers to monitor resource allocation, ensuring accountability and optimizing social welfare outcomes through evidence-based decision-making.
