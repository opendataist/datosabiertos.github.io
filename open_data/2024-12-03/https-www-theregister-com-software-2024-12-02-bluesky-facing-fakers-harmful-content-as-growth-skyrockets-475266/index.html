Bluesky is experiencing explosive user growth, surpassing competitors like Threads in engagement metrics and attracting users fleeing other platforms due to political shifts. This rapid expansion highlights the platform’s appeal as an open alternative, particularly for publishers and open-source developers who find higher interaction rates despite smaller follower bases. The surge underscores a significant shift in the social media landscape, where decentralized networks are gaining traction by prioritizing engagement quality and developer-friendly infrastructure over sheer scale. However, this influx brings substantial challenges regarding trust and safety. The platform is grappling with widespread impersonation and harmful content, prompting a temporary shift toward aggressive moderation that led to erroneous suspensions. These issues reveal the difficulties of scaling community guidelines and enforcement mechanisms quickly, raising concerns about the balance between user safety and free expression. The need for more precise moderation tools and clearer policies becomes critical as the user base diversifies and grows. This case is vital for open_data discussions as it illustrates the real-world friction between open architecture and regulatory compliance. Bluesky’s struggles with AI scraping and EU reporting requirements demonstrate that open protocols must also address data privacy and legal obligations. As the platform expands, it faces the dual challenge of maintaining its open ethos while implementing necessary safeguards, setting a precedent for how open social networks handle scalability, data ethics, and external scrutiny.
