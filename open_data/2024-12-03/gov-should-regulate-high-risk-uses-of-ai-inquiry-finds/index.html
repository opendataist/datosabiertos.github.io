Gov should regulate 'high-risk' uses of AI, inquiry finds

Australia’s Senate inquiry recommends dedicated federal legislation to regulate high-risk artificial intelligence, requiring transparency in training data and proper licensing for copyrighted works. This push for accountability ensures developers disclose how their models utilize intellectual property, addressing critical ethical concerns in AI development. The report also mandates a unified legal framework for automated decision-making in government services, complementing recent privacy reforms that grant individuals rights to understand algorithmic choices. By harmonizing state and federal approaches, Australia aims to prevent regulatory fragmentation while protecting citizen rights against opaque digital governance. This legislation is vital for open data ecosystems, as it forces transparency in data sourcing and usage. Clear rules on copyright and algorithmic accountability ensure that data contributions do not violate intellectual property rights, fostering a trustworthy environment where public and private data can be shared and utilized responsibly within a regulated framework.
