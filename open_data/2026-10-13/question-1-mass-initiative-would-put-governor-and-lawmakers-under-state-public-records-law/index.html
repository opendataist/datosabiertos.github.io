Question 1: Mass. initiative would put governor and lawmakers under state public records law

Question 1: Mass. initiative would put governor and lawmakers under state public records law

Massachusetts is considering a ballot measure that would remove the legislative and executive branches from their current exemption under the state’s public records law. This initiative seeks to align the state with the vast majority of other jurisdictions by allowing citizens to formally request documents from lawmakers and the governor’s office, thereby addressing the state’s unique status as a national outlier in government transparency. Supporters argue that this change empowers taxpayers with direct access to financial and administrative data, such as contracts and settlements, without relying on legislative audits. While exemptions protect sensitive constituent communications and ongoing policy debates, proponents emphasize that the measure ensures greater accountability for public funds and reduces the secrecy that has long characterized the State House, fostering a more open democratic process. This development is highly relevant to open data because it expands the scope of mandatory digital disclosure beyond executive agencies to include the legislature. By mandating that legislative records be subject to public record laws, the measure highlights the critical intersection between legal frameworks and data accessibility, potentially reducing barriers for journalists and researchers seeking to analyze government operations and expenditures.
