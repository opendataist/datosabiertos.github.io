Sierra Leone’s Voter Registration Data discredit the Mid-term Census Data – implications for the coming Presidential election

The 2021 Sierra Leone mid-term census is widely regarded as unreliable due to significant statistical anomalies that contradict prior data and observable realities. The results suggested an unnatural population parity between major political regions and implausibly low figures for Freetown, the capital. These discrepancies were dismissed by experts and donors as likely manipulations intended to redraw electoral boundaries, rendering the official dataset unusable for legitimate development planning or demographic analysis. This untrustworthy data is particularly relevant to open data standards because it highlights the dangers of political interference in statistical processes. The author demonstrates that subsequent voter registration records completely invalidate the census findings, showing that the statistical agency ignored critical feedback and produced numbers that defy logical scrutiny. When official statistics are manipulated for political gain, it undermines public trust and compromises the integrity of all data-driven decision-making processes in the country. The implications extend directly to the 2023 presidential election, where accurate census data is crucial for determining constituency representation. Since the falsified census did not alter the underlying voter registration ratios, which still heavily favor the opposition’s traditional strongholds, the incumbent president faces a challenging electoral landscape. This situation underscores how flawed or manipulated open data can distort democratic outcomes and obscure the true demographic power dynamics within a nation.
