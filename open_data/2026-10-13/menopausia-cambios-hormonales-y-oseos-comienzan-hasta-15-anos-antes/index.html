Menopausia: cambios hormonales y óseos comienzan hasta 15 años antes

Menopausia: cambios hormonales y óseos comienzan hasta 15 años antes

The research reveals that menopause is not a gradual decline, but rather a “physiological precipice” that triggers abrupt changes across multiple body systems, even more than a decade before the final menstrual period. This finding, based on a large-scale analysis of laboratory data, indicates that the transition rapidly affects bone, cardiovascular, and metabolic health, challenging the previous belief that it was a slow and natural aging process. Hormone therapy is presented as a key intervention to mitigate these alterations, primarily protecting against osteoporosis and improving liver and kidney function. The results suggest that hormonal dysregulation has profound systemic consequences, implying that early detection and timely treatment can significantly reduce health risks associated with this life stage, beyond immediate symptoms such as insomnia or depression. This information is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how access to and integration of large volumes of retrospective data—even with limitations such as self-reporting—can reveal complex physiological patterns that were previously invisible. By validating innovative computational methodologies for analyzing massive cohorts, the study paves the way for future data-driven research using open data to optimize public health strategies and advance the understanding of gender-specific medicine.
