RTI flouted with impunity

Governments in Pakistan have enacted laws to guarantee the right to information, yet widespread non-compliance undermines this constitutional mandate. A recent assessment reveals that federal ministries and provincial divisions largely fail to proactively publish essential data, such as policies and audit reports. This systemic neglect prevents citizens from accessing basic governance details, effectively blocking their ability to monitor public administration and hold officials accountable for their actions. Direct inquiries further highlight this opacity, with numerous ministries ignoring requests or missing deadlines entirely. The silence from key departments suggests an intentional effort to conceal financial difficulties, controversial policies, and operational inefficiencies. By withholding information, these entities prioritize secrecy over transparency, directly contradicting the principle that citizens are the ultimate owners of information regarding public affairs and their own governance. This situation is critically relevant to open_data advocates, as it demonstrates how legal frameworks alone cannot ensure accountability without active implementation. The failure to release structured, accessible data erodes public trust and hinders efforts to combat corruption. Consequently, there is an urgent need to pressure authorities to embrace genuine openness, ensuring that information flows freely to empower citizens and foster a culture of transparency in public service delivery.
