Parliament House department shrouded in secrecy facing calls for greater transparency

Parliament House department shrouded in secrecy facing calls for greater transparency

Transparency advocates and legal experts are urging the Australian government to remove the Department of Parliamentary Services from Freedom of Information exemptions. This call follows serious allegations of a toxic workplace culture and systemic cover-ups within the agency tasked with protecting democracy. The current opacity allows internal issues to fester without adequate public scrutiny, undermining the integrity of Australia’s parliamentary institutions and violating principles of good governance. The department has remained exempt since 2013, initially framed as an interim measure to protect parliamentary research confidentiality. However, reviews have consistently recommended that administrative documents should be subject to FOI laws, similar to the courts and the Governor-General’s office. Removing this exemption would align the department with standard democratic accountability practices, ensuring that administrative decisions are transparent and that staff operate under necessary oversight rather than isolation. This situation is critical to open data because it highlights how legal exemptions can create "dark spots" in public administration, preventing the release of vital information even when transparency is officially claimed as a value. By resisting FOI requests and lacking public disclosure logs, the department impedes the flow of accountable data to citizens. Restoring these requirements would strengthen open data infrastructure, proving that democratic safeguards must extend to the operational mechanics of parliament itself.
