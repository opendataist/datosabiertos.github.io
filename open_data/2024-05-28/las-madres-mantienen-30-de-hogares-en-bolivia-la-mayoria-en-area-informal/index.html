Las madres mantienen 30% de hogares en Bolivia, la mayoría en área informal

In Bolivia, the vast majority of women, particularly single mothers, work in the informal economy. This structural reality excludes them from labor rights and social security, highlighting a critical gender gap where economic necessity forces women into precarious self-employment without legal protection. This lack of formalization directly threatens their well-being and limits their access to essential safety nets, perpetuating cycles of vulnerability. Despite these challenges, female entrepreneurship is visibly growing and contributing significantly to the national economy. A substantial increase in business registrations led by women demonstrates their vital role in economic development. This trend underscores a shift toward greater female leadership in the business sector, even if these ventures often remain within the informal or sole-proprietorship frameworks that lack robust regulatory support. This article is relevant to open data because it relies on statistical evidence from national institutes to expose systemic inequalities. By making this data public, policymakers and researchers can better understand the intersection of gender and informality. Transparent data is crucial for designing targeted interventions that address the root causes of labor exclusion and promote equitable economic participation for women.
