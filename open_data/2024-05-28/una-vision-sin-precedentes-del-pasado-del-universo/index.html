Una visión sin precedentes del pasado del Universo

The European Space Agency (ESA) has released the first set of scientific data from the Euclid telescope, offering an unprecedented view of the early universe. This release marks a milestone in cosmic exploration, as initial observations have already revealed new findings, ranging from previously unknown dwarf galaxies to the first structures formed after the origin of the cosmos. These results validate the instrument’s ability to unravel the mysteries of dark energy and dark matter, which are fundamental to understanding the evolution and current structure of the universe. The relevance to open data lies in the commitment to transparency and global accessibility that defines the mission. Data are shared through periodic, staggered releases, ensuring that a vast international community—including researchers from numerous countries—can analyze and contribute to scientific knowledge. This open-access policy not only accelerates discovery but also democratizes cutting-edge science, allowing technological advances to benefit society through open collaboration and shared information. By releasing critical information in advance, ESA fosters a collaborative research ecosystem in which cosmological data become a common resource. The imminent availability of extensive catalogs will ensure that evidence-based science thrives, setting a standard for future space missions. This approach reinforces the idea that open data are essential for addressing complex scientific challenges, bringing together diverse experts in a unified effort to map the dark universe and expand our understanding of space.
