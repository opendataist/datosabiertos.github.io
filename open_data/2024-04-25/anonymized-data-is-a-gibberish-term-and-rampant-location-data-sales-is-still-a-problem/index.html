'Anonymized Data' Is A Gibberish Term, And Rampant Location Data Sales Is Still A Problem

The widespread belief that "anonymized" data is safe is a dangerous misconception, as location data can easily be re-identified when cross-referenced with other information. This revelation exposes the fragility of current privacy protections, highlighting how easily personal movements can be mapped to specific individuals despite the removal of direct identifiers like social security numbers. This vulnerability is exacerbated by a lack of transparency and accountability in the data broker industry. Companies often operate in secrecy, selling vast amounts of user location data without clear disclosure or consent. The reliance on "anonymity" serves as a shield for these opaque practices, allowing businesses to profit from sensitive information while avoiding scrutiny from regulators and the public alike. This issue is critical to open data discourse because it demonstrates that releasing datasets does not guarantee privacy if the underlying context allows for re-identification. Without robust standards for true anonymization and legal frameworks that enforce transparency, open data initiatives risk enabling widespread surveillance and abuse. The article underscores the urgent need for stronger privacy laws and technical safeguards to ensure that data sharing does not come at the cost of individual rights.
