Caminos encontrados en el Día Mundial de la Propiedad Intelectual

The article argues that traditional intellectual property frameworks are failing to keep pace with the rapid evolution of the global economy, particularly due to the transformative impact of Artificial Intelligence. While the world is integrating AI to streamline patent and copyright processes, Mexico remains stagnant, treating these issues as routine bureaucratic exercises rather than recognizing the urgent need for systemic modernization. This disconnect highlights a critical failure to adapt legal mechanisms to new technological realities. Furthermore, the potential for intellectual property to drive sustainable development goals is currently underutilized. Key areas such as environmental protection, health, and culture lag behind because implementation strategies are either misunderstood or lack necessary resources. The continued academic debate over these measures is obsolete in a world facing immediate and complex challenges, necessitating a shift from theoretical discussions to practical, impactful applications that address pressing societal needs. This perspective is vital for open data as it underscores the tension between restrictive proprietary systems and the need for accessible, interoperable information. As AI and blockchain revolutionize data management, the reliance on rigid, siloed property rights hinders innovation. Open data advocates must push for frameworks that prioritize transparency and accessibility, ensuring that technological advancements serve the public interest rather than reinforcing outdated administrative bottlenecks that stifle global progress and equitable access to knowledge.
