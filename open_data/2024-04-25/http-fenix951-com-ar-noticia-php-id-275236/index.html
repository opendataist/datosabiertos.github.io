Inés Brizuela y Doria argues that the recent university marches defended public education rather than opposing audits, emphasizing that rigorous oversight does not justify defunding. She clarifies that while internal university controls exist, external audits by the General Audit Office depend on Congressional authorization. Consequently, she insists that the legislature must mandate these reviews to ensure legality without reducing necessary investment in the academic system. Regarding transparency, she criticizes the current budget increase as insufficient and assigns blame for previous budget freezes to prior political leadership. Her stance underscores the urgent need for proactive disclosure of financial flows. By urging authorities to publish detailed expenditure data independently of external audit triggers, she highlights the vital role of accessible public information in fostering accountability. This narrative is highly relevant to open data because it advocates for immediate, granular financial transparency from educational institutions. It promotes the idea that data accessibility should be a proactive governance standard, enabling citizens to monitor public spending effectively. This approach reinforces the principle that open data is essential for verifying integrity and ensuring that public resources are managed responsibly and visibly.
