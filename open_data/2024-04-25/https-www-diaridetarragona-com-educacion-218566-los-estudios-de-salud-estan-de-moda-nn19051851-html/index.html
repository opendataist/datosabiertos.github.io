Spain is undergoing a significant demographic shift driven by an aging population, which has fundamentally transformed labor market demands. This trend has triggered an exponential surge in interest in health-related educational pathways, as society increasingly values the medical and care sectors. The pandemic reinforced this perception, highlighting the critical importance of healthcare in daily life and solidifying the expectation that these professions will be essential for future economic and social stability. The core challenge lies in the severe imbalance between student interest and educational capacity. There is a dramatic disparity where applications for medicine and nursing far exceed the available university spots, creating high barriers to entry. This mismatch suggests that simply increasing academic enrollment is insufficient; the real bottleneck exists in hospital infrastructure and specialist training capacity, meaning that many graduates will face difficulties accessing the practical training or positions needed to fully integrate into the healthcare workforce. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the urgent need for transparent, real-time information systems to align education with demographic realities. By analyzing open datasets on enrollment trends, job market needs, and population aging, policymakers and institutions can better predict shortages and optimize resource allocation. Furthermore, open data can empower prospective students with accurate insights into program availability and career prospects, helping them make informed decisions that better match the actual demands of an evolving labor market.
