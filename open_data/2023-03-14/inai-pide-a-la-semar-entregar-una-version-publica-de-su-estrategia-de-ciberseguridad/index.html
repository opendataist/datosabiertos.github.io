INAI pide a la Semar entregar una versión pública de su estrategia de ciberseguridad

The INAI ruled that the Mexican Navy must release a public version of its joint cyberdefense strategy with the Army. This decision overturned the military’s initial refusal to share the document, which they had classified for five years citing national security risks following a major cyberattack. The oversight body determined that withholding the entire document was unjustified, as it contained general administrative and legal information not suitable for classification. The commission emphasized that while specific operational protocols require protection, the strategic framework’s introduction and legal basis pose no threat. By distinguishing between sensitive operational details and public policy foundations, the ruling ensures transparency without compromising actual security. This approach allows citizens to understand the government’s digital defense structure while safeguarding critical tactical data from potential exploitation by cybercriminals. This case is highly relevant to open data because it establishes a crucial precedent for balancing transparency with security in sensitive sectors. It demonstrates how oversight bodies can mandate the release of non-confidential parts of government strategies, reinforcing the principle that total secrecy is often an excuse to hide information. This encourages greater accountability in how state entities handle public information regarding national security policies, ensuring that open data principles apply even to highly regulated military and defense documents.
