The Justice Department Underscores the Need for FOIA ‘Presumption of Openness’

The Justice Department has issued new guidelines reinforcing a strong presumption of openness for federal agencies, marking a significant shift in how Freedom of Information Act requests are handled. Instead of focusing on reasons to withhold information, agencies are now instructed to prioritize disclosure, considering partial releases and consulting experts when determining foreseeable harm. This approach aims to reduce the growing backlog of pending requests and address the frustrations of journalists and transparency advocates who face delays and excessive redactions. These updates align with broader efforts to enhance the public’s user experience when accessing government records. The guidance encourages agencies to cooperate more effectively with requesters, providing prompt status updates and clear explanations for any denials. This procedural shift is designed to foster trust and ensure that the spirit of transparency prevails, particularly in cases where doubt exists about whether records should be released to the public. This development is crucial for open data advocacy because it challenges the traditional bureaucratic culture of secrecy and mandates greater accountability. With the decommissioning of legacy systems like FOIAOnline and the incomplete adoption of the centralized FOIA.gov portal, these guidelines serve as a critical counterbalance to technological fragmentation. By forcing agencies to articulate specific harms before withholding data, the policy strengthens the legal and practical foundations for citizens to hold the government accountable, ensuring that open data remains a viable right rather than an exception.
