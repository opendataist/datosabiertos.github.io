Pope bolsters women at Vatican but resistance remains

The article highlights a significant shift in the Vatican’s labor dynamics, demonstrating that Pope Francis has actively expanded female participation within church institutions, particularly in administrative and leadership roles. This trend challenges the historical norm of an exclusively male hierarchy, suggesting that while the priesthood remains restricted, professional access for women is improving. Despite these gains, women continue to encounter institutional resistance when seeking top-tier authority, revealing a gap between increased workforce presence and genuine power-sharing. The presence of women in high-ranking offices marks a historical first, yet the underlying cultural barriers within the "old guard" indicate that full equality in decision-making remains an ongoing struggle rather than a achieved reality. This development is relevant to open_data as it illustrates how transparency in workforce statistics can expose systemic disparities and track progress toward gender equity in traditionally closed organizations. By making employment data public, the Vatican allows external scrutiny, showing that measurable improvements do not automatically equate to structural change, a key insight for advocates pushing for accountability and inclusive practices through data-driven monitoring.
