Reforma del INDEC: los puntos centrales del proyecto de Sergio Massa para el organismo estadístico

The proposed reform of the National Institute of Statistics and Censuses (INDEC) aims to transform the agency into an entity with functional and economic autonomy, removing it from direct dependence on the Ministry of Economy. This structural change, aligned with international standards, seeks primarily to guarantee the political independence of national statistics, enabling greater coordination with other government data sources and strengthening the credibility of macroeconomic indicators before organizations such as the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD). The proposed institutional model introduces bicameral oversight mechanisms and changes in the appointment of leadership to reduce executive interference. By requiring Senate approval for the Executive Director and establishing fixed terms, the initiative aims to protect technical continuity against political cycles. Additionally, statistical confidentiality and methodological quality are reinforced, fundamental elements to ensure that official information is objective, reliable, and useful for both public and private decision-making. This reform is crucial for the open data movement because it redefines the guarantees of integrity and accessibility of public information. By institutionalizing transparency, impartiality, and technical quality, a more robust environment is created for the reuse of data by civil society and the academic community. INDEC’s autonomy not only improves statistical quality but also promotes a culture of open data based on trust and evidence, facilitating citizen scrutiny and innovation driven by reliable official information.
