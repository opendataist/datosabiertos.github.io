La deforestación en la Amazonía vuelve a subir a niveles récords

Deforestation in Brazil’s Amazon reached record highs in February, underscoring the severe challenges President Lula da Silva faces in reversing the environmental degradation fostered during the previous administration. Experts warn that dismantling the systemic support for illegal logging and restoring institutional capacity will take years, as regulatory gaps continue to enable destructive practices. This surge highlights the critical role of transparent, high-quality environmental data in holding governments accountable. Reliable satellite monitoring is essential for detecting illegal activities and verifying the effectiveness of policies, making open data a vital tool for civil society and researchers to advocate for stricter enforcement and ecological preservation. The relevance to open data lies in its ability to provide independent verification of official claims. By ensuring access to raw, unfiltered environmental metrics, stakeholders can challenge discrepancies in official narratives, demand timely interventions, and support evidence-based decision-making to protect vital ecosystems from short-term political pressures.
