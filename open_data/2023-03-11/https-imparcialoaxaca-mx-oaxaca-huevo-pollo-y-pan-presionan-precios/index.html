Oaxaca is experiencing a severe inflationary crisis, particularly in essential food items, despite a slight deceleration in annual price growth. The cost of basic goods such as eggs and bananas has surged dramatically, disproportionately affecting low-income households in the state. This economic pressure is significantly higher than the national average, indicating that poorer southern regions bear the brunt of rising costs, undermining consumer purchasing power and increasing the financial strain on local families. The data reveals a structural disparity where major urban centers in Oaxaca remain among the most expensive in the country. While inflation has slowed slightly, food prices continue to outpace national trends, with staples becoming increasingly unaffordable. This trend highlights a persistent economic inequality, as the cost of living in these areas does not reflect local income levels, creating a challenging environment for residents who must allocate a larger portion of their budget to survive. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how official statistical indices, such as the National Consumer Price Index, can uncover localized economic hardships that aggregate national figures might obscure. By making granular data on prices and geographic disparities publicly available, organizations like INEGI enable researchers and policymakers to identify vulnerable populations and evaluate the effectiveness of social programs, ensuring that transparency leads to informed public health and economic decisions.
