Santi y las ONG - Opinión - ABC Color

Santi y las ONG - Opinión - ABC Color

The article critiques the President’s ambiguous stance on new legislation affecting non-governmental organizations (NGOs), arguing that his focus on financial transparency overlooks the more profound threat these entities pose to democratic governance. By circumventing democratic participation through international agreements, these groups are accused of implementing a corporate governance model akin to the World Economic Forum’s proposals, thereby undermining national sovereignty and institutional integrity. The author emphasizes the constitutional necessity for NGOs to disclose their foreign funding sources, comparing this requirement to existing restrictions on political parties. The text warns that relying on European or foreign legal frameworks for these modifications is dangerous and suggests that the legislative errors stem from a lack of clarity regarding financial obligations, which are essential for maintaining democratic accountability and preventing external interference in domestic affairs. This narrative is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical intersection between institutional transparency and civic rights. It advocates for mandatory, publicly accessible information regarding NGO funding flows as a safeguard against opaque power structures. The piece underscores how open data principles serve as essential tools for citizens to verify compliance with constitutional norms and protect democratic processes from covert foreign influence.
