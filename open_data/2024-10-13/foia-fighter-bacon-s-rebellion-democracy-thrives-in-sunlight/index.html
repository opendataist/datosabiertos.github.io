FOIA Fighter | Bacon's Rebellion - Democracy Thrives in Sunlight

Walter Smith’s ongoing legal battle with the University of Virginia highlights the systemic challenges citizens face in obtaining public records. Through relentless Freedom of Information Act requests, Smith exposed how the university employs financial barriers, aggressive redactions, and broad exemptions to withhold information. His efforts revealed that the institution creates an illusion of transparency by publishing favorable data while keeping critical operational and policy-related documents confidential. This disparity underscores how deeply funded entities can effectively shield their decision-making processes from public scrutiny. The core conflict centers on the misuse of the “working papers” exemption, which UVA uses to classify committee deliberations as presidential private records. This legal strategy allows the university to hide the substantive discussions behind major policy decisions on issues like racial equity and free speech, revealing only final outcomes to the public. By treating foundational policy debates as privileged executive communications, the university restricts public access to the rationale behind its actions, setting a precedent that may extend to other state agencies across Virginia. This article is vital to open data discussions as it illustrates the gap between data availability and true transparency. It demonstrates that having data exist does not equate to public access when institutions control the narrative and access points. For open data advocates, this case serves as a cautionary tale about the need for robust legal frameworks that prevent the arbitrary deletion or withholding of digital records. It emphasizes that without stringent accountability measures, open data initiatives can be undermined by institutional opacity and resource asymmetries.
