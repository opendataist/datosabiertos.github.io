Procedimiento para cambiar los datos personales en los registros de la Seguridad Social

Updating personal data in Social Security records is an essential requirement to ensure the smooth management of fundamental rights, such as pensions, unemployment benefits, and healthcare assistance. Keeping this information up to date not only guarantees the correct receipt of notifications and the payment of economic benefits, but also prevents administrative and legal disruptions that could seriously affect the citizen's quality of life. The responsibility for this update primarily lies with the user, although there are authorized representation mechanisms in cases of incapacity or exceptional circumstances. This process can be carried out through in-person channels with prior appointment or, preferably, via the electronic headquarters. The digital channel stands out for its efficiency, allowing modifications to contact details, address, or bank information without the need for travel. Access usually requires digital identification, such as an electronic certificate or the Cl@ve system, although alternatives exist for those who do not have these means, through specific forms. The speed of these digital procedures reflects the institutional commitment to administrative modernization and the reduction of bureaucracy. The relevance of this article to the field of *open data* lies in the critical need for accuracy in the personal data that feed public information systems. Interoperability between government databases depends on the quality of the information entered by citizens. When records are outdated, errors arise in social analytics and in public decision-making. Therefore, promoting transparency and ease in the management of personal data is essential to improve the reliability of open data sets and the efficiency of the digital state.
