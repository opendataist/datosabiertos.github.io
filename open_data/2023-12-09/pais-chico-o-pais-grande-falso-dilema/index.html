¿País chico o país grande? Falso dilema

The recent census data reveals that Uruguay’s resident population falls short of public expectations, sparking familiar disappointment. This reaction reflects a historical misconception that equates national power and economic viability strictly with population size, a metric derived from the era of nation-states and capitalist markets. However, in an era of globalized capitalism, the traditional correlation between demographic magnitude and territorial influence has diminished, rendering this numerical anxiety largely obsolete regarding the country’s actual global integration. A critical limitation of these official figures is the exclusion of the vast “Uruguay abroad” community, estimated at hundreds of thousands who have emigrated but maintain cultural ties. By ignoring this dispersed population, the census presents an incomplete picture of the nation’s true social and political footprint. This omission highlights the limitations of static, territorial data in capturing the fluid realities of a globalized society, where citizenship and community extend beyond physical borders. This article is highly relevant to open data because it challenges the assumption that census statistics alone define reality. It advocates for integrating diverse data sources—such as migration records and diaspora engagement metrics—to create a more accurate, inclusive understanding of society. Open data initiatives must evolve to reflect these transnational connections, ensuring that digital public infrastructure supports a democratic social contract that includes non-resident citizens, rather than relying solely on traditional, exclusionary demographic boundaries.
