Australia’s freedom of information system ‘dysfunctional and broken’, inquiry finds

Australia’s freedom of information system is in crisis, characterized by chronic backlogs and a bureaucratic culture resistant to transparency. A Senate committee identified that years of under-resourcing and a pro-secrecy mindset within government have rendered the regime dysfunctional. The recent resignation of the Information Commissioner is viewed not merely as a personnel change, but as a symptom of a deeper institutional failure to enforce disclosure obligations effectively. The report recommends significant structural reforms, including transferring oversight to the Commonwealth Ombudsman to address accumulated delays and cultural biases. This move aims to clear the mounting backlog of unreviewed requests and restore integrity to the process. The findings highlight that current mechanisms are hindered by legislative roadblocks and an "absence of consequence" for non-compliance, necessitating independent investigation into workplace disputes and management failings within the oversight body. This situation is critical for open data advocates because it demonstrates that legal frameworks alone cannot ensure transparency without adequate resources and a supportive administrative culture. The proposal to retain documents when ministers leave office suggests a risk of data loss, further complicating public access to government records. Ultimately, the article underscores that successful open data initiatives require more than just policy; they demand sustained investment and a genuine political commitment to breaking down barriers to information.
