The Lithuanian Ministry of the Economy and Innovation allocates €6 million to boost digital innovation using open data, aiming for €1 billion economic upsurge - ArcticStartup

Lithuania’s Ministry of the Economy and Innovation has initiated a significant funding program to empower small and medium-sized enterprises to develop innovative digital services using open data. By allocating substantial financial resources to these projects, the government aims to transform open data from a mere informational resource into a strategic economic asset. This approach encourages businesses to create tangible value, thereby strengthening the nation's digital infrastructure and fostering a more competitive economic environment. The initiative highlights the profound societal and economic implications of accessible information, noting that open data can significantly expand market sizes and reduce public sector costs. By lowering financial barriers for entrepreneurs, the program seeks to drive digitalization across various sectors, resulting in increased social value and improved service levels. This strategy demonstrates how strategic data management can generate added value across multiple industries, promoting sustainable growth and efficiency in both the public and private sectors. This effort is directly relevant to open data because it provides a clear example of how policy can actively stimulate the creation and reuse of public information. It illustrates a successful model where government support bridges the gap between available data and commercial innovation. By facilitating the practical application of open datasets, this program enhances transparency and encourages a culture of data-driven development, proving that open access to information is a powerful tool for modern economic advancement.
