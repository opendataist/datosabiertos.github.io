Study: Pay and experience positively impact wildland firefighter retention; competing wages not so much

Research into federal wildland firefighter retention reveals that higher workloads and cumulative experience significantly boost the likelihood of Hotshots returning for subsequent seasons. Conversely, wages in alternative local jobs do not influence retention decisions, indicating that financial incentives within the specific context of firefighting duties are the primary drivers for staying in the role. This finding underscores the complex economic realities facing specialized wildfire crews, suggesting that compensation tied directly to seasonal intensity matters more than general market rates. The study confirms that while pay remains a critical factor for these skilled professionals, it operates differently than typical labor market dynamics, highlighting the unique value proposition of federal fire service careers. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the need for transparent, accessible workforce metrics to understand public sector employment trends. By publishing detailed research on retention factors, such studies provide empirical evidence that can inform policy and improve data collection methods for federal agencies. It demonstrates how open, rigorous analysis can guide resource allocation and workforce management strategies in critical government sectors.
