La inflación española continúa entre las más bajas de la zona euro

Spain’s inflation rose to 2.3% in July, marking a slight increase from June but remaining among the lowest in the Eurozone. This modest uptick is primarily driven by higher fuel and tourism costs, alongside a slower decline in clothing prices, while energy costs continue to decrease. Despite this temporary rise, underlying inflation excluding volatile items also increased, yet the overall trend suggests stabilization rather than a sustained spike in price pressures across the economy. Looking ahead, two significant threats loom over price stability: persistent food price spikes due to drought and unfavorable base effects that could push inflation toward 5% by year-end. Although experts predict a final average below previous forecasts, food inflation is expected to remain double-digit. Competitiveness for Spanish firms may improve as energy costs drop, helping to offset wage lags, though sectoral divergence will continue with services remaining expensive while industrial prices slow.
