12% of Luxembourg Residents Victim of Burglary Incidents

This report analyzes burglary trends in Luxembourg, revealing that incidents predominantly target residential homes rather than adjacent areas. A key finding is the correlation between higher household income and increased burglary prevalence, challenging the assumption that wealthier households are inherently safer. Additionally, local community issues such as drug activity and vandalism significantly elevate the risk of victimization, highlighting that neighborhood security dynamics play a crucial role in crime distribution. The study underscores the substantial personal and financial toll on victims, noting that a significant portion suffers uninsured losses and severe emotional distress, primarily characterized by a loss of security. Despite the high reporting rate for home invasions, police satisfaction levels remain divided, suggesting gaps in victim support or procedural efficiency. These insights into victim experience and systemic response are vital for understanding the full scope of the crime beyond mere statistics. For open data advocates, this article illustrates the importance of publishing granular, contextualized data that links crime metrics with socioeconomic factors. By making detailed statistics on location, income, and community issues publicly available, governments enable researchers and citizens to identify systemic vulnerabilities and advocate for targeted policy changes. This transparency fosters evidence-based discourse on public safety and resource allocation.
