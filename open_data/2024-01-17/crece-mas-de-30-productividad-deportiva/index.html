Crece más de 30% productividad deportiva

The article reveals a structural transformation in the Mexican sports sector, characterized by high value concentration and external dependence. Despite a significant reduction in the number of employees, productivity per worker experienced substantial growth, generating average revenues among the highest in the services sector. This phenomenon indicates that the business has become more sophisticated, prioritizing operational efficiency over labor mass, which contrasts with its pre-pandemic state when the sector was practically inactive. Simultaneously, a profound financial internationalization is highlighted, with the vast majority of companies relying on cross-border capital and contracts for their operations. This dynamic suggests that the local sports ecosystem is integrated into global value chains, implying that its economic stability is tied more to external factors than to purely national dynamics. The closing of gaps between statistical data and corporate news illustrates how sports function as a vehicle for strategic alliances, media sponsorships, and corporate social responsibility initiatives. This information is relevant to the field of open data because it demonstrates the need to integrate official statistics with corporate and contextual data to truly understand how markets operate. By combining productivity metrics with indicators of international financing and political activity, one can analyze how public policies and foreign investment impact the real economy. Transparency in these data allows for the evaluation not only of economic efficiency but also of the social and political implications of the sector, offering a multidimensional perspective essential for academic research and informed policy formulation.
