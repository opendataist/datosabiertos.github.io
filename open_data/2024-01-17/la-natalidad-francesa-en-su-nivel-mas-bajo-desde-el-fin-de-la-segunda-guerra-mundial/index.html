La natalidad francesa, en su nivel más bajo desde el fin de la Segunda Guerra Mundial

France has recorded its lowest birth rate since World War II, falling below 700,000 births per year. Although the natural population balance remains slightly positive, this demographic shift highlights a significant long-term decline in fertility rates, driven by a shrinking number of women of childbearing age and changing societal priorities among younger generations facing economic and geopolitical uncertainty. The article emphasizes how migration currently offsets this natural decline, sustaining overall population growth. This data reveals a complex interplay between natural demography and human mobility, illustrating how external factors heavily influence national population structures even when birth rates are at historically low levels. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how publicly accessible statistical records enable in-depth sociological analysis. By aggregating and analyzing government-published demographic metrics, researchers can identify underlying trends in social behavior and economic impact, underscoring the value of transparent data in understanding modern societal shifts beyond mere numerical counts.
