Los importadores anotaron deudas por US$ 26.000 millones y ya se pagaron US$ 2.900 millones en SIRAS atrasadas

The Argentine government registered and updated the Central Bank’s debt to importers, raising it to US$26 billion. Although this figure is lower than the initial estimates of US$60 billion, it reflects the magnitude of outstanding commitments stemming from the foreign exchange shortages and currency controls of the previous period. The implementation of the Commercial Debt Registry enables precise identification of who owes what, to whom, and in what amounts, creating a single, official record. This transparency mechanism is crucial because it transforms a chaotic situation of hidden obligations into structured, verifiable data. By requiring companies to register their liabilities, the government can design more effective financial relief policies, such as issuing bonds to cancel tax debts, and manage partial debt cancellations in an orderly manner. The visibility of these amounts facilitates negotiations between the private sector and financial institutions, reducing uncertainty. For the open data ecosystem, this case illustrates the importance of standardizing and publishing key administrative records. The existence of a public, up-to-date registry of external commercial debt represents progress toward greater accountability and facilitates independent economic analysis. By making information on the state’s obligations to the importing sector accessible, it empowers civil society and researchers to audit fiscal management and better understand the challenges facing the national economy.
