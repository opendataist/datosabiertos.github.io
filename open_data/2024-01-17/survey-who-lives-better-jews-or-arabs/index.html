Survey: Who lives better, Jews or Arabs?

The survey highlights persistent socioeconomic disparities between Israel’s Jewish and Arab populations, where Jews generally report higher standards of living, healthcare, housing, and education. Despite these structural advantages, the gap has narrowed significantly in recent years, particularly in employment rates and overall economic dissatisfaction, suggesting a convergence in material conditions. However, the data reveals a nuanced social dynamic where Arabs demonstrate greater optimism for the future and higher satisfaction with specific public services like healthcare institutions, contrasting with the declining expectations among Jews. While Jews maintain superior metrics in personal security and welfare outcomes, Arabs benefit from lower assault rates and certain educational parity, indicating that subjective well-being does not always align with objective socioeconomic indicators. This report is crucial for open_data initiatives as it underscores the necessity of disaggregated statistics to capture complex societal trends. By making such granular data accessible, policymakers can better address inequality and design targeted interventions. Transparency in this data fosters accountability and supports evidence-based strategies aimed at promoting equitable development across diverse community segments within the state.
