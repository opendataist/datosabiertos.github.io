Pide ONU-DH transparencia en la actualización del registro nacional de desaparecidos

The UN Human Rights Office emphasizes that sustainable solutions to forced disappearances require robust, well-resourced institutions capable of conducting searches and investigations independently. Strengthening these structures is essential to prevent the burden from falling disproportionately on victims’ families and to ensure that procedural advancements are not reversed. Without such institutional solidity, the search for missing persons remains inefficient and prone to failure. Transparency and methodological clarity in updating the National Registry are critical for maintaining public trust and data integrity. The registry must serve as a reliable tool for collaboration, ensuring that information is complete, protected, and disaggregated to prevent re-victimization. Effective implementation also depends on operationalizing key legal frameworks and forensic databases, which currently face significant gaps in their execution and integration. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the necessity of transparent, accessible, and high-quality public records for human rights accountability. It underscores that data systems must be open to scrutiny, methodologically sound, and inclusive of civil society participation. Without these open data principles, critical information remains fragmented or unreliable, hindering justice and the effective management of public resources in crisis situations.
