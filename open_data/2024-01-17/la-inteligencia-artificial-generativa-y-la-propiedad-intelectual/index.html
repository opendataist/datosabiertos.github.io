La Inteligencia Artificial generativa y la propiedad intelectual

The New York Times’ lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft exemplifies the critical conflict between original content creation and the training of generative AI models. This case underscores the urgent need to establish clear regulatory frameworks that define the legal boundaries for using protected data, ensuring that technology companies respect copyright and do not profit economically from creators’ work without their consent or adequate compensation. Beyond the legal dimensions, the debate exposes deep vulnerabilities in individual property rights, particularly concerning the unauthorized use of images and voices. Experts warn that AI does not “unlearn,” meaning that biases or infringements introduced into training data become permanently entrenched. This implies that initial accuracy and ethical standards are crucial, as the technology lacks the human capacity for self-correction, necessitating rigorous oversight from the earliest stages of development. This situation is highly relevant to open data, as it highlights the need to transition toward fairer and more democratic models in which end users can capitalize on the value of their own data. Transparency regarding the origin of information and clarity in terms of use are essential to protect both the integrity of creative works and individual rights. Fostering a sustainable digital ecosystem requires balancing technological innovation with the protection of intellectual property and privacy, ensuring that the evolution of AI does not come at the expense of ethics and economic justice.
