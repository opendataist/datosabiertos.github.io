Quadient: UK universities risk losing £2 million a year due to disputed invoices

Digitizing accounts payable processes in UK universities has proven insufficient for resolving deep-seated invoicing inefficiencies. Despite full adoption of digital tools, institutions continue to lose significant annual revenue due to late payments and disputed invoices. This indicates that mere technological integration cannot overcome systemic flaws, leaving finance teams vulnerable to substantial financial leaks and operational bottlenecks that threaten their economic stability. The root of the problem lies in complex, manual approval workflows rather than payment mechanics. With hundreds of non-specialist staff involved in processing tens of thousands of annual invoices, the cycle becomes exceptionally slow and prone to error. This fragmentation creates a lack of accountability and visibility, resulting in delayed payments and damaged supplier relationships, which ultimately distracts core staff from their primary academic and administrative duties. This narrative is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical need for transparent, standardized metrics in higher education finance. If universities are to address these inefficiencies, they must adopt open data practices to benchmark performance, share best practices, and enable external oversight. Open, comparable datasets would allow institutions to identify common failure points in their AP systems and collaborate on developing smarter, auditable solutions that restore fiscal health and operational efficiency.
