The article highlights a significant disconnect in the Costa Rican job market, where entry-level candidates face exclusion due to stringent experience requirements. This structural barrier forces many qualified graduates into underemployment, creating a cycle of frustration and wasted educational investment. The core issue lies in the prevalence of employers prioritizing immediate productivity over potential, seeking to minimize training costs by hiring seasoned professionals rather than investing in young talent. From an open data perspective, this report is relevant because it demonstrates how public statistical surveys, such as the Enadel, expose systemic inefficiencies in labor markets. By aggregating data on employer demands and skill gaps, official statistics provide the evidence base needed to advocate for policy reforms. These insights allow stakeholders to challenge unfair hiring practices and highlight the undervaluation of soft skills such as communication and attitude, which are crucial for a dynamic workforce. Ultimately, the findings suggest that bridging the experience gap requires a shift in perception regarding youth capabilities. When large-scale data reveals that thousands of qualified individuals are systematically marginalized, it strengthens arguments for mentorship programs and internships. This transparency encourages a more inclusive approach to hiring, ensuring that statistical evidence supports initiatives aimed at effectively integrating new graduates into the professional world.
