Alejandro Armenta presenta iniciativa para desaparecer el Inai y que la SFP realice sus tareas

Alejandro Armenta presenta iniciativa para desaparecer el Inai y que la SFP realice sus tareas

Mexico’s Senate has proposed abolishing the National Institute for Transparency and eliminating its autonomy by transferring its functions to the Ministry of Public Administration. This initiative argues that since both entities share goals of transparency and anti-corruption, consolidating them into a single body would streamline public information oversight and data protection duties. The plan aims to centralize the handling of public information requests and personal data safeguards within the executive branch. Proponents claim this merger enhances efficiency and aligns closely with national development objectives focused on ethical governance and regenerating public trust through unified institutional responsibility. This is critically relevant to open data because removing an independent watchdog threatens the separation necessary for unbiased information access. Autonomous institutions are vital for enforcing open data standards; their absorption by the government risks political interference, potentially undermining the integrity, accessibility, and trustworthiness of public sector data availability.
