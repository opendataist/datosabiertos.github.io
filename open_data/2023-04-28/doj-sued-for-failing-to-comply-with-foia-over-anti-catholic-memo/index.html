DOJ sued for failing to comply with FOIA over anti-Catholic memo

A lawsuit filed by CatholicVote and Judicial Watch alleges that the Justice Department and FBI have failed to comply with Freedom of Information Act requests regarding an internal memo labeling traditional Catholics as violent extremists. The plaintiffs argue that the agencies' silence and refusal to release documents indicate a pattern of prioritizing partisan ideology over the rule of law. This legal action demands transparency, seeking evidence of communications that suggest religious affiliation was improperly used to define potential threats, thereby challenging the government's commitment to open and accountable operations. The controversy centers on a leaked FBI field office memo that identified radical-traditionalist Catholics as racially or ethnically motivated violent extremists. Although FBI leadership publicly dismissed the memo as reflecting standards inconsistent with agency protocols, subpoenaed documents reveal efforts to cultivate sources within Catholic parishes and use undercover agents to monitor religious communities. These findings suggest a systemic issue where faith-based organizations were treated as intelligence-gathering targets, raising serious concerns about the boundaries between legitimate security operations and intrusive surveillance of protected religious practices. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical importance of FOIA in exposing government overreach and ensuring accountability. When agencies withhold records related to surveillance tactics and classification of religious groups, they obscure how public resources are used to monitor citizens. Ensuring access to such data allows civil society to verify that law enforcement actions respect constitutional rights, demonstrating that transparency is essential for maintaining trust between the government and the communities it serves.
