Labour Market Statistics

Northern Ireland’s labor market demonstrates a complex recovery where earnings and employment rates have improved compared to the previous year, yet remain slightly below pre-pandemic levels. While median monthly pay has seen significant growth and the unemployment rate has stabilized at historic lows, the overall workforce participation and total hours worked have not fully rebounded to their former peaks, indicating a lingering structural shift in the region’s economic activity. Conversely, there is a worrying trend regarding job security, characterized by a sharp doubling in proposed redundancies compared to the prior year. Although actual confirmed job losses remain relatively low, the surge in potential layoffs suggests growing employer caution or economic uncertainty. This divergence between positive wage data and rising redundancy proposals highlights a bifurcated labor environment where compensation improves for some while others face precarious employment conditions. This dataset is crucial for open data enthusiasts and policymakers because it illustrates the importance of triangulating multiple statistical sources to understand economic health. By combining HMRC payroll records, the Labour Force Survey, and redundancy notifications, analysts can uncover nuanced insights that single metrics might obscure. It underscores the value of accessible, granular public data in detecting early warning signs, such as the rise in proposed job cuts, allowing for more informed social and economic planning.
