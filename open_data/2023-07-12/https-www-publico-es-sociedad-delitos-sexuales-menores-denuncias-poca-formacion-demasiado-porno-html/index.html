The rise in sexual assaults committed by minors is primarily explained by a fundamental lack of sex and affective education, exacerbated by early access to pornography. This exposure turns explicit content into an incorrect guide for behavior, normalizing toxic gender roles and violence, and distorting the understanding of consent and healthy relationships during a crucial stage of personal and sexual development. Paradoxically, the increase in reported cases also reflects greater social awareness and the breaking of previous silences, driven by feminism and collective organization. This phenomenon shows that, although these crimes have existed structurally for a long time, they are now more visible and vocalized. Society has moved from minimizing these behaviors to detecting them and responding with firmness, creating an apparent statistical crisis that is actually a symptom of progress in victims' rights and safety. From a data perspective, this context highlights the critical importance of open data in criminological research and public policy. To design effective prevention strategies and understand the real dynamics behind these crimes, it is necessary to access disaggregated, verified, and accessible information. Data transparency allows for the identification of patterns, evaluation of the impact of educational interventions, and ensures that statistics are not used solely as a punitive tool, but as a basis for comprehensive, evidence-based solutions.
