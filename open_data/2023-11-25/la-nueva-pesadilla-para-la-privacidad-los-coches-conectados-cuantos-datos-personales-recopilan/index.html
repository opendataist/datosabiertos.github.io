La nueva pesadilla para la privacidad, los coches conectados: ¿cuántos datos personales recopilan?

A comprehensive report by the Mozilla Foundation reveals that current connected vehicles operate as powerful data collectors, gathering detailed information about drivers and passengers without any real privacy protections. The main conclusion is that most manufacturers share or even sell this data to third parties, while users’ ability to control, delete, or erase such information is practically nonexistent, rendering informed consent little more than an illusion. This situation raises serious implications for the protection of identity and individual autonomy. Because data are not adequately protected and security policies lack transparency, there is a significant risk that personal information—which can range from driving habits to intimate details—may be used by insurance companies, advertisers, or law enforcement agencies without proper legal safeguards. The absence of effective tools for managing data flows leaves users exposed to potential misuse or data breaches. This case is crucial for the open data movement because it illustrates the power asymmetry between technology corporations and citizens. The report underscores the urgent need to apply principles of transparency and accountability in the design of artificial intelligence systems and connected hardware. To safeguard basic digital rights, it is essential to promote open standards that enable users to regain control over their own information, preventing technological innovation from becoming a mechanism of opaque surveillance.
