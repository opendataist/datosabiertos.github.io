El desafío de proteger nuestros datos biométricos en tiempos de Worldcoin

El desafío de proteger nuestros datos biométricos en tiempos de Worldcoin

The article argues that biometric data poses unique, irreversible security risks because, unlike passwords, it cannot be changed if compromised. In developing nations such as the Dominican Republic, pressing socioeconomic issues often relegate data privacy to a lower priority, creating a vulnerable environment in which personal information is easily exploited. This lack of prioritization enables companies to collect sensitive data with minimal oversight, as citizens frequently underestimate the permanent value of their biological identifiers. Current regulatory frameworks fail to provide effective protection due to weak enforcement mechanisms and the absence of dedicated supervisory authorities. Although existing laws establish principles regarding consent and data processing, they lack substantial sanctions and independent oversight bodies to ensure compliance. As a result, private entities can operate with impunity, transferring sensitive data across borders without respecting local sovereignty or ensuring adequate security standards, thereby leaving citizens exposed to surveillance and identity theft. This situation is critical for open data initiatives, because the true transparency and utility of data depend on trust and security. Without robust legal frameworks and heightened citizen awareness, the collection of biometric data undermines public confidence in digital systems. Therefore, protecting data sovereignty and establishing clear accountability are prerequisites for any open data ecosystem, ensuring that digital innovation does not come at the expense of individual privacy and fundamental rights.
