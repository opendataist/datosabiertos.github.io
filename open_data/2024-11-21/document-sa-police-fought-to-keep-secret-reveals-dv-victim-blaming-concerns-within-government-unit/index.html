Document SA Police fought to keep secret reveals DV 'victim blaming' concerns within government unit

Document SA Police fought to keep secret reveals DV 'victim blaming' concerns within government unit

A confidential review of South Australia’s Multi-Agency Protection Service (MAPS) reveals significant concerns that the task force prioritizes victim behavior over perpetrator accountability. This imbalance has allegedly led to victim-blaming practices, where agencies focus excessively on whether survivors engage with services rather than holding abusers responsible. These findings challenge the effectiveness of the current multi-agency approach to domestic violence prevention, suggesting a systemic failure to address the root causes of abuse. The review also highlights a power imbalance within the collaborative framework, noting that police dominance in case selection marginalizes input from health and social service agencies. Because case triaging relies heavily on police reports, survivors who avoid law enforcement due to fear or past trauma are often excluded from critical support interventions. This structural bias limits the holistic understanding of risk, as vital information from non-police sources is rarely utilized, thereby reducing the overall safety and effectiveness of the protective measures. This incident is highly relevant to open data advocates because the review’s suppression via a lengthy Freedom of Information battle underscores the challenges of transparency in sensitive government operations. The struggle to access these findings illustrates how confidentiality clauses and methodological secrecy can hinder public scrutiny and accountability. Ensuring open access to performance and decision-making data in social services is crucial for identifying systemic flaws, enabling evidence-based policy improvements, and maintaining public trust in institutions tasked with protecting vulnerable populations.
