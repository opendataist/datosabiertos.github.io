¿Qué pasaría si reforma extingue Plataforma de Transparencia?; 15 mil millones de registros quedan vulnerables a hackeo | El Universal

¿Qué pasaría si reforma extingue Plataforma de Transparencia?; 15 mil millones de registros quedan vulnerables a hackeo | El Universal

Members of Mexico’s National Transparency System have issued a stark warning about the proposed constitutional reform that would abolish the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) and, inadvertently, the National Transparency Platform (PNT). They argue that the reform fails to ensure the continuity of the PNT, leaving more than 14 million public records vulnerable. Without the platform’s centralized, high-security infrastructure, these vast amounts of data risk becoming exposed to cyberattacks, as individual entities may lack the resources or technical capacity to maintain equivalent security standards. Furthermore, the dissolution of the PNT would fragment the current unified access system into thousands of disparate portals operated by various federal and state agencies. This dispersion would hinder citizens’ ability to easily exercise their right to information, replacing a single, efficient interface with inconsistent systems that vary widely in format, accessibility, and reliability. The current structure ensures coordinated, standardized access to public data, whereas the reform threatens to return transparency efforts to isolated, uneven local implementations. This situation is critically relevant to open data because it highlights the importance of centralized, secure, and standardized platforms for managing public information. The PNT serves as a vital example of how robust infrastructure can protect data integrity while ensuring equitable public access. Losing this unified system would not only compromise data security but also undermine the principles of open governance by making public information harder to find, use, and trust across Mexico.
