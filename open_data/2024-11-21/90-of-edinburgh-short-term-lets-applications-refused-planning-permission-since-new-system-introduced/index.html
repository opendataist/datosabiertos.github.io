90% of Edinburgh short-term lets applications ‘refused planning permission’ since new system introduced

The stringent planning regime for short-term lets in Edinburgh, introduced in 2022, has resulted in a near-total rejection of applications, revealing a severe disconnect between licensing and planning authorities. While the licensing process itself may be administratively straightforward, the mandatory requirement for planning permission acts as a prohibitive barrier, effectively preventing most operators from legally offering their properties. This structural mismatch suggests the system is designed to fail rather than regulate, as operators are often granted licenses only to subsequently face enforcement notices for lacking planning consent, creating legal and financial instability for individuals rather than large corporations. This regulatory complexity disproportionately impacts homeowners and small-scale hosts who rent out properties occasionally while living elsewhere, rather than professional landlords. By classifying these arrangements as a material change of use, the council imposes costs and delays that many cannot sustain, leading to significant stress and financial loss for residents. The situation highlights a critical governance failure where inconsistent application of rules and a lack of inter-departmental coordination punish ordinary citizens attempting to comply with the law, undermining the fairness and clarity expected in public administration. The relevance to open data lies in the transparency of these administrative outcomes, which expose the efficacy of public policy. By analyzing freedom of information data on application refusals, stakeholders can demonstrate that rigid regulatory frameworks often produce unintended negative consequences, such as reduced housing availability and increased costs for visitors. This case study illustrates how accessible, disaggregated government data can validate concerns about regulatory overreach, urging policymakers to adopt more inclusive and coherent approaches that balance economic benefits with housing sustainability.
