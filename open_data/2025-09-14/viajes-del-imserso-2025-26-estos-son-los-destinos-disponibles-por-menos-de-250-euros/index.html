The article highlights a strategic partnership between the Spanish public administration and the hospitality sector that significantly reduces tourism costs for vulnerable populations. By securing bulk agreements to maintain hotel occupancy during low seasons, the state offers packages at prices substantially below market rates. This model demonstrates how public policy can leverage collective purchasing power to create equitable access to leisure activities, ensuring that economic barriers do not exclude specific demographics from participating in society. This approach is particularly relevant to open data initiatives because it illustrates the tangible impact of transparent, data-driven governance on social welfare. The program’s modernized, electronic application process relies on accurate administrative data to manage demand and allocate resources efficiently. Furthermore, the pricing structures and destination availability serve as valuable datasets for analyzing regional tourism trends and the effectiveness of budget allocation, promoting accountability and informed decision-making in public service delivery. Ultimately, the initiative underscores the importance of integrating technology and data accessibility into social programs to enhance user experience. By offering both digital and traditional application channels, the administration ensures inclusivity while streamlining operations. This case study emphasizes that open data principles, when applied to public services, not only improve operational efficiency but also foster greater trust and engagement among citizens, particularly those from lower-income backgrounds, by making complex bureaucratic processes more transparent and user-friendly.
