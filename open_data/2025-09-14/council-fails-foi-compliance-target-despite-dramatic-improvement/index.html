Council fails FOI compliance target despite 'dramatic' improvement

Council fails FOI compliance target despite 'dramatic' improvement

The council significantly improved its handling of public information requests, rising from an unacceptable 52% response rate to nearly 80%. This dramatic reversal, driven by enhanced training and operational efficiency rather than increased staffing, successfully addressed previous failures. The improvement demonstrates that targeted internal reforms can effectively resolve systemic backlog issues in public administration. This progress is highly relevant to open data because it validates the importance of institutional capacity for transparency. By meeting statutory deadlines more consistently, the organization ensures that citizens can actually access the information they seek, turning legal rights into practical reality. It highlights that open data initiatives depend on reliable delivery mechanisms to maintain public trust and utility. Consequently, the Information Commissioner’s Office recognized these efforts as good practice for other local authorities. The council’s ability to embed these improvements suggests that sustainable open data governance is achievable through process optimization. This serves as a compelling case study for other bodies struggling with transparency metrics, offering a roadmap for achieving compliance without merely increasing headcount.
