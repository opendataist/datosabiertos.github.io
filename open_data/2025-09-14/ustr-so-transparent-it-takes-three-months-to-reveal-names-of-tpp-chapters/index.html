USTR So Transparent It Takes Three Months To Reveal Names Of TPP Chapters

The article highlights a stark contradiction in the Trans Pacific Partnership negotiations, where USTR officials claim to prioritize transparency while conducting talks behind closed doors. Despite appointing a "chief transparency officer," the agency’s communications remain vague and propagandistic, ignoring the significant delays and public concern surrounding the deal. This discrepancy suggests that the administration is prioritizing secrecy over the democratic accountability it ostensibly advocates for. Relevance to open data is evident in the agency’s resistance to even basic information disclosure. When subjected to a Freedom of Information Act request for merely the chapter titles of the agreement, the USTR took months to respond. This deliberate delay and obstruction demonstrate a systemic preference for obfuscation, reinforcing the idea that the organization lacks the institutional will to operate with the openness required in a modern democracy. Ultimately, the situation illustrates how powerful trade agreements can become vehicles for reduced public scrutiny rather than improved governance. The failure to provide simple structural details of the TPP underscores the urgent need for stronger open data mandates in international negotiations. Without mandatory transparency, such agreements risk undermining democratic processes by keeping critical policy decisions hidden from public view and scrutiny.
