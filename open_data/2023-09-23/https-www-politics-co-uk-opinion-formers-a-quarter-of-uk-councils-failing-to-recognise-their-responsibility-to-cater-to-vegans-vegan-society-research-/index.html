This article highlights a significant gap in UK local government compliance with vegan inclusion and sustainability mandates. By analyzing Freedom of Information responses, the report reveals that many councils fail to recognize veganism as a protected characteristic, despite legal obligations to accommodate it. This oversight persists even though public demand for plant-based options is rising and the market for vegan food is expanding rapidly. The implications extend beyond individual dietary needs to broader environmental and health goals. Transitioning to plant-based diets is crucial for reducing carbon emissions, as they produce significantly fewer greenhouse gases than meat-heavy alternatives. Consequently, the failure of public sector catering to offer consistent vegan options undermines national climate reduction strategies and public health initiatives, leaving a substantial portion of the population without adequate ethical food choices in public services. This data is highly relevant to open_data because it demonstrates the power of FOI requests in exposing institutional non-compliance and driving accountability. The resulting heatmap serves as a transparent tool for citizens to monitor their local authorities, illustrating how accessible public data can empower communities to demand better service delivery. By making performance metrics visible, open data facilitates civic engagement and encourages councils to adopt more sustainable and inclusive practices, ultimately pushing for systemic change in public food systems.
