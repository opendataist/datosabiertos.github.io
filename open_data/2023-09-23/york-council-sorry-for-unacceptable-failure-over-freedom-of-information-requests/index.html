York council sorry for 'unacceptable failure' over Freedom of Information requests

City of York Council has admitted to a severe failure in transparency, allowing over 261 Freedom of Information requests to go unanswered for more than two years. This significant backlog, acknowledged by the Information Commissioner’s Office as a legal breach, highlights systemic issues within local government data management. The council has issued an unreserved apology, citing understaffing and procedural flaws as primary drivers for this unacceptable lapse in statutory duty. The delay specifically impacted public access to critical information regarding legal costs associated with a contentious planning inquiry near Askham Bog. Such prolonged withholding of data undermines the fundamental purpose of freedom of information laws, which exist to ensure accountability and public scrutiny of local authority decisions. The council recognizes that restoring trust requires not just clearing the accumulated delays, but implementing robust improvements to prevent recurrence. This situation is highly relevant to the open data community as it demonstrates the real-world consequences of poor data governance and delayed disclosure. It serves as a cautionary tale for organizations managing public information, emphasizing that timely access to data is essential for civic engagement. The council’s commitment to an improvement plan and cooperation with regulators offers a pathway toward better practices, reinforcing the need for transparent, accessible, and efficiently managed public records.
