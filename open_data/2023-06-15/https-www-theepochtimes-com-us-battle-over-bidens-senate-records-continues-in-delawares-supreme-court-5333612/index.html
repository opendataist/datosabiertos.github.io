The Delaware Supreme Court is currently deliberating on a critical open data dispute regarding the University of Delaware’s handling of Senator Joe Biden’s archival records. At the heart of the matter is whether the university complied with the state’s Freedom of Information Act by denying public access requests without conducting a thorough search. The court must determine if relying on general assertions about funding sources, rather than a specific examination of the documents, satisfies the legal burden of proof for denying public record requests. The core tension lies between the university’s claim that no search was necessary because state funds did not maintain the records, and the plaintiffs’ argument that such an approach lacks transparency and evidentiary weight. Legal representatives for the public interest groups contend that the university’s affidavit is ambiguous and based on hearsay, failing to demonstrate due diligence. The Supreme Court’s previous ruling emphasized that factual assertions must be detailed and sworn to, highlighting that open data laws require more than categorical denials to protect the public’s right to information. This case is highly relevant to open data principles as it establishes precedents for how institutions manage politically sensitive archives. It clarifies the limits of "public record" definitions when private donations are involved and reinforces the necessity of transparent, verifiable processes in FOIA compliance. The outcome will impact how universities and public entities balance legal obligations with the availability of historical data, ensuring that access to public information is not hindered by administrative loopholes or insufficient justification.
