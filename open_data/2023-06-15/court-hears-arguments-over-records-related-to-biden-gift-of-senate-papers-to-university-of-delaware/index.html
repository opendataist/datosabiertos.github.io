Court hears arguments over records related to Biden gift of Senate papers to University of Delaware

The Delaware Supreme Court’s review of this legal dispute highlights a critical tension in open data governance: the obligation of public institutions to conduct transparent, verifiable searches when handling records with public interest. The core issue involves the University of Delaware’s refusal to release records regarding former President Biden’s papers, which are subject to public freedom of information laws despite the university’s private governance structure and agreements restricting immediate public access. This case underscores that relying on unsubstantiated claims or internal conversations is insufficient to justify withholding information. The court’s previous rulings emphasized that public bodies must provide sworn evidence of actual document searches to meet their burden of proof. By attempting to bypass thorough searches based on prior, informal inquiries, the institution failed to demonstrate compliance with statutory requirements, raising significant concerns about the accountability of entities receiving public funds. The outcome of this case is highly relevant to the open_data community as it tests the boundaries of what constitutes a "public record" for mixed-status institutions. It establishes a precedent for how strictly courts enforce the requirement for demonstrable transparency in data handling. A ruling favoring public access could strengthen mandates for rigorous, documented search processes, ensuring that private agreements do not automatically override the public’s right to scrutinize information generated or held by organizations with substantial public financial ties.
