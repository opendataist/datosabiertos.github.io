Los casos de covid aumentaron un 80% en todo el mundo, aunque la mortalidad bajó

The article highlights a significant increase in global COVID-19 cases, driven by the circulation of new variants such as EG.5, despite a decline in mortality. However, the World Health Organization (WHO) warns that these figures do not reflect the true epidemiological situation due to a sharp drop in diagnostic testing and pandemic monitoring in many regions. This lack of accurate data creates an informational fog that hinders effective and timely public health responses. This article is critically relevant to open data, as it illustrates the consequences of interrupting transparency and the systematic collection of public health data. When health authorities fail to maintain robust and accessible surveillance systems, the ability to monitor viral evolution and assess the effectiveness of interventions is lost. Data opacity prevents the early identification of trends, such as the spread of more transmissible variants, and complicates evidence-based decision-making. To mitigate these risks, experts advocate for restoring reliable surveillance mechanisms, such as wastewater analysis, and ensuring that data are public and accessible. Additionally, the importance of vaccination, particularly for vulnerable groups, and the development of updated vaccines are emphasized. The key lesson is that continuity in the generation and publication of open health data is essential for addressing complex health crises and proactively safeguarding global health.
