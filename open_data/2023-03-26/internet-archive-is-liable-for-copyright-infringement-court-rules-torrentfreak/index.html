Internet Archive is Liable for Copyright Infringement, Court Rules * TorrentFreak

The recent court ruling against the Internet Archive establishes a significant precedent regarding digital lending and copyright law, directly impacting the open data community’s understanding of intellectual property boundaries. The court rejected the Internet Archive’s fair use defense, determining that scanning physical books to create digital copies for lending does not constitute a transformative use. This decision clarifies that non-profit status and controlled access limits do not automatically shield organizations from liability, reinforcing that unauthorized replication remains infringement even without direct profit motive. This judgment highlights the tension between traditional library rights and the commercial ebook licensing market. By siding with major publishers, the court emphasized that the Internet Archive’s model threatens established revenue streams, effectively substituting licensed platforms with an unauthorized, cheaper alternative. This implication is crucial for open data initiatives, as it signals that widespread, unlicensed digital distribution of copyrighted material faces substantial legal hurdles, regardless of its educational or cultural value. Relevance to open data lies in the potential chilling effect this ruling has on digital preservation and free access to knowledge. If institutions cannot freely digitize and lend owned physical materials, the ability to create comprehensive, accessible digital archives is severely constrained. This case underscores the fragility of open access models when they conflict with entrenched commercial interests, urging advocates to prioritize legally compliant methods for data and content dissemination to ensure sustainable open sharing practices.
