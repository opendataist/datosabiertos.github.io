Percepción de inseguridad sube en Chetumal, baja en Cancún, según la encuesta del INEGI - Noticaribe

Percepción de inseguridad sube en Chetumal, baja en Cancún, según la encuesta del INEGI - Noticaribe

The latest survey conducted by the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) reveals a complex picture of security perceptions in Quintana Roo, where Cancún and Chetumal experienced significant declines in fear of crime during late 2024. Despite these recent improvements, year-over-year data indicates rising anxiety in Chetumal, highlighting the volatility of public sentiment and the need for nuanced, longitudinal open-data analysis to accurately track safety trends. Public trust in municipal police forces remains critically low compared to national military and federal entities, reflecting a deep-seated perception of ineffectiveness at the local level. This stark contrast in institutional confidence underscores the importance of transparently publishing performance metrics and citizen satisfaction scores. Such open-data initiatives are essential for holding local authorities accountable and fostering evidence-based policy reforms to bridge the gap between public expectations and service delivery. Furthermore, the report emphasizes that physical spaces such as ATMs and public transportation continue to generate substantial unease among citizens, regardless of broader statistical trends. Understanding these specific friction points requires granular, accessible datasets that go beyond aggregate figures. By making such detailed findings widely available, researchers and policymakers can better address the lived realities of urban insecurity, ensuring that safety strategies are responsive to the actual concerns of the population.
