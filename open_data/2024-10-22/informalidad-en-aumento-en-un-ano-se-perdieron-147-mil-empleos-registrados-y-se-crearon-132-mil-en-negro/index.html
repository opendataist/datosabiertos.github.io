Informalidad en aumento: En un año se perdieron 147 mil empleos registrados y se crearon 132 mil en negro

The article highlights a worrying increase in Argentina’s informal labor market, reflecting deeper economic vulnerabilities. This shift is primarily driven by high inflation, recessionary pressures, and bureaucratic obstacles that make it difficult for businesses to formalize employment. As a result, the growth of unregistered work undermines workers’ access to social security and benefits, exacerbating social inequality while simultaneously threatening the financial sustainability of the national pension system due to reduced contributions. Specific sectors such as domestic service, construction, and agriculture exhibit critical levels of informality, with more than half of their workforce operating without proper registration. In construction, this surge is partly attributed to reduced public works initiatives, which pushed previously formal companies toward informal arrangements. These sector-specific trends illustrate how macroeconomic conditions and policy decisions directly impact labor stability, leaving significant portions of the workforce exposed to precarious conditions and lacking legal protections. This data is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the importance of accessible, transparent, and detailed statistical publications for public accountability. By making granular employment data available through institutions like INDEC, citizens and researchers can analyze the true state of the economy, scrutinize government policies, and advocate for informed reforms. Open access to such metrics is essential for fostering public debate and ensuring that decision-makers are held accountable for addressing structural labor issues.
