¿Por qué los jubilados de Baleares son de los que menos cobran de España?

¿Por qué los jubilados de Baleares son de los que menos cobran de España?

Pensioners in the Balearic Islands receive significantly lower benefits than the Spanish national average, a disparity rooted in the region’s specific labor market structure. The primary driver is the economy’s heavy reliance on tourism and service sectors, which predominantly offer low-skilled, non-specialized jobs with low salaries. This structural characteristic limits the accumulation of contributions necessary for higher retirement income, creating a systemic gap in pension amounts. Furthermore, high rates of temporary and part-time contracts, alongside a prevalence of “forced” self-employed workers who contribute minimally, further depress future pension entitlements. The scarcity of high-level executive positions and large corporations headquartered in the islands exacerbates this issue, as these roles typically offer the higher salaries that translate into better social security contributions. Consequently, the local workforce often fails to meet the thresholds required for substantial retirement payouts. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights how granular socioeconomic statistics can reveal regional inequalities invisible in national aggregates. By analyzing detailed employment and pension data, researchers and policymakers can identify the specific drivers of economic disparity, such as sectoral dependence and contract types. This enables targeted interventions, such as fiscal incentives to attract diverse industries, fostering a more equitable economic landscape through evidence-based policy making.
