Los jubilados de Baleares cobran 105 euros mensuales menos que la media nacional y 414 euros menos que los vascos

The article highlights a severe disparity between the high cost of living in the Balearic Islands and stagnant pension benefits, forcing many retirees into economic hardship. Despite having one of the country's highest living standards, retirees often cannot afford basic needs like housing and food, as their limited income fails to cover rising expenses for essential goods. This situation reveals a critical gap between regional economic wealth and the adequacy of social protection. The findings underscore how inflation and housing costs disproportionately affect vulnerable populations, regardless of a region's overall prosperity, illustrating that national averages often mask deep local inequalities in elderly welfare. This content is relevant to open data as it demonstrates the importance of granular, publicly accessible datasets for social analysis. By exposing regional disparities in pensions and living costs, open data enables policymakers and citizens to identify systemic inequalities, advocate for targeted reforms, and ensure transparent monitoring of social equity across different geographic areas.
