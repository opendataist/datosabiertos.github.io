Freedom of Information Commissioner and Privacy Commissioner appointments

The Australian government has reinstated the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, appointing distinct Privacy and Freedom of Information Commissioners for the first time since 2015. This strategic restoration signals a renewed political commitment to robust data protection and transparency, contrasting sharply with previous efforts to dismantle the independent oversight structure. By elevating these roles to standalone positions, the administration prioritizes the privacy rights of citizens and the integrity of information access as fundamental democratic pillars. This structural change is highly relevant to open data because it ensures dedicated, expert leadership for FOI requests, which are critical for accessing government-held datasets. Strengthening the FOI Commissioner’s office facilitates greater public accountability and reduces barriers for researchers and journalists seeking transparency. Consequently, the availability and quality of open government data are likely to improve through more rigorous and consistent enforcement of information disclosure obligations. Simultaneously, the appointment of a specialized Privacy Commissioner enhances governance frameworks surrounding personal information within data ecosystems. With expertise in AI and technology law, this new role helps balance open data initiatives with necessary safeguards for individual privacy. This dual focus ensures that while data transparency increases, the ethical handling of sensitive information remains protected, fostering public trust in digital governance and supporting sustainable open data practices.
