Capitalinos: percepción de inseguridad es real

The article underscores the deep sense of insecurity in Tuxtla Gutiérrez, fueled by rising violent crime and high-profile cases such as feminicides. This climate has compelled residents to modify their daily routines, particularly women and young people, who feel pressured to avoid traveling at night to ensure their safety. Although some individuals report feeling secure due to personal luck or specific local circumstances, the prevailing narrative reflects a collective anxiety that undermines personal freedom and social cohesion. This tension between official statistics and lived experience is critical for open data initiatives. It illustrates that numerical safety indicators do not always capture the nuanced realities of citizens’ everyday lives. The gap between aggregate data and individual testimonies reveals shortcomings in how security is measured and understood, urging data creators to integrate qualitative, community-driven insights alongside quantitative metrics to achieve a more comprehensive perspective. The relevance to open data lies in the need for transparent, accessible, and multi-dimensional information. By making diverse sources of safety perceptions available, policymakers and researchers can identify vulnerabilities that standard statistics might overlook. This approach fosters trust and enables more effective, empathetic public policies that address the actual needs and fears of the population, rather than relying solely on abstract numerical trends.
