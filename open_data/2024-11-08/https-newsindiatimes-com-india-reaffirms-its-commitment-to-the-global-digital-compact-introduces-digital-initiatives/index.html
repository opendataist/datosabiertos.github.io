India’s presentation at the UN General Debate highlights the critical intersection between national policy and global digital governance. By leveraging the Right to Information Act and parliamentary oversight, India demonstrates how transparent, non-discriminatory legal frameworks are essential for ensuring safe and reliable digital access. This approach serves as a practical model for implementing the Global Digital Compact, proving that domestic accountability mechanisms are vital for maintaining public trust and combating digital fraud on a global scale. The article underscores the importance of open data and infrastructure in bridging the digital divide and fostering inclusive economic participation. India’s initiatives, which expand broadband connectivity and digital literacy, illustrate how open access to technology empowers citizens and strengthens democratic governance. This relevance to open data lies in the recognition that equitable access to information and digital tools is a prerequisite for social harmony, informed decision-making, and the sustainable development goals emphasized in international agreements. Furthermore, the discussion on multilingual communication strategies for UN peacekeeping missions highlights the need for inclusive information dissemination across cultural barriers. By advocating for localized content and language services, India emphasizes that open communication channels must be accessible to all communities, not just those speaking official languages. This reinforces the open data principle that true inclusivity requires removing linguistic and structural barriers, thereby enhancing global engagement and ensuring that peacekeeping efforts are supported by trustworthy, culturally relevant information.
