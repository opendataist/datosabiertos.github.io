El Ayuntamiento de Madrid, primera administración pública en adherirse al Manifiesto por los Datos Abiertos Reutilizables

The Madrid City Council has become the first public administration in Spain to formally join the Manifesto for Reusable Open Data. This pivotal move signals a strengthened institutional commitment to maximizing the reusability of public information, transforming data from a passive record into an active asset. By adopting this manifesto, the administration aims to set a national precedent for how public entities can leverage open data to drive transparency and civic engagement. The agreement entails specific operational improvements, including the maintenance of a continuously updated data catalog, ensuring content is available under open licenses, and standardizing publication processes. Crucially, it mandates training for municipal staff and the development of intuitive tools to facilitate access. These measures are designed to enhance data quality and usability, ensuring that information is not just published but is genuinely accessible and ready for societal benefit. This development is highly relevant to the open data community as it demonstrates the practical integration of policy frameworks with technical infrastructure. It highlights how local governments can operationalize open data principles through standardized procedures and active collaboration between the public sector and academic or industry partners. Ultimately, it underscores the transition toward a culture where data reusability is central to governance, fostering innovation and accountability through transparent, high-quality information ecosystems.
