The article clarifies that registering in a municipal census different from one’s habitual residence is illegal under Spanish law. This violation can lead to significant fines and, in severe cases, imprisonment, highlighting the strict enforcement of administrative accuracy. Beyond legal penalties, inaccuracies disrupt access to essential services like healthcare and voting. These practical impediments demonstrate how precise civil registration is vital for ensuring citizens can effectively utilize public resources and participate in democracy without bureaucratic obstacles. This topic is relevant to open data because municipal census records serve as foundational datasets for urban planning and resource allocation. Ensuring the integrity and real-time accuracy of such open government data is crucial for creating reliable, transparent, and efficient public services that truly reflect the demographic reality of communities.
