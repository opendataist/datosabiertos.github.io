The article details the evolution of Japan’s foreign trade, highlighting a significant increase in the trade deficit driven by rising import costs for energy due to geopolitical instability. Although exports grew notably, particularly in fuels and electronics, they did not offset the surge in the crude oil bill, thereby affecting the overall trade balance. This dynamic reveals Japan’s vulnerabilities stemming from its dependence on external energy sources and its asymmetric position in its key bilateral relationships, with notable deficits vis-à-vis China and the European Union, but substantial surpluses with the United States and Mexico. The analysis demonstrates how international events directly impact the country’s key economic indicators.
