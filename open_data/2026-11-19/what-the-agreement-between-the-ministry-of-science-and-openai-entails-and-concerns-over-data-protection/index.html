What the agreement between the Ministry of Science and OpenAI entails, and concerns over data protection

What the agreement between the Ministry of Science and OpenAI entails, and concerns over data protection

The Chilean government has signed an agreement with OpenAI to integrate artificial intelligence into public administration, sparking intense debate over data protection and national technological autonomy. Although the Ministry of Science insists that the arrangement is a non-commercial collaboration and that the use of open or synthetic data will be prioritized, the lack of transparency regarding the exact content of the memorandum has fueled distrust. The refusal to publish the signed document contrasts with assurances that no sensitive information will be transferred, yet these guarantees have proven insufficient to allay political concerns. The opposition has strongly questioned the direct selection of the U.S. company and the inherent risks of this strategic alliance, demanding a full breakdown of the agreement and its legal review. They are particularly concerned about OpenAI’s potential influence on the state’s technology priorities and about the agreement’s compatibility with existing legislative initiatives on privacy and AI regulation. These concerns underscore the urgency of establishing clear mechanisms for human oversight and risk assessment before implementing any project, ensuring that innovation does not compromise citizens’ rights or digital sovereignty. This case is especially relevant to the open data ecosystem, as it sets a precedent for how the public sector manages privacy in the face of third-party technologies. It illustrates the critical tension between accelerated technological innovation and the need for robust regulatory frameworks that protect citizens’ information. To advance toward genuine data governance, it is essential to ensure transparency in international agreements and to guarantee that the training and development of tools strictly adhere to data protection principles, avoiding the opacity that undermines public trust in state institutions.
