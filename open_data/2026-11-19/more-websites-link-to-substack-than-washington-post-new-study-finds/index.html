More websites link to Substack than Washington Post, new study finds

More websites link to Substack than Washington Post, new study finds

A recent analysis of Common Crawl’s open web link maps reveals a significant decline in web connectivity for traditional news outlets between 2024 and 2026. While general websites experienced a moderate drop in inbound links, news sites suffered a steeper decline, with the majority of tracked outlets losing referring domains. This trend was most acute for mid-sized and upper-middle-sized news organizations, suggesting that established journalism brands are struggling to maintain their digital footprint relative to the broader web. Simultaneously, the study highlights a dramatic shift in influence toward non-traditional platforms. Substack, an independent newsletter hosting service, more than doubled its linking domains, surpassing major national newspapers. Additionally, AI platforms like ChatGPT and OpenAI saw exponential growth in web citations, outpacing many legacy news outlets. This data suggests that the web’s reference structure is increasingly favoring creator-led and artificial intelligence-driven content over institutional journalism, reshaping how information is distributed and cited across the internet. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the utility of large-scale, openly available web crawls for tracking structural changes in the information ecosystem. By leveraging Common Crawl data, researchers can quantify the erosion of traditional news authority and the rise of new media forms without relying on proprietary metrics. These findings provide critical evidence for understanding digital equity, media fragmentation, and the changing architecture of public knowledge, informing policy and research into how open data infrastructure shapes online visibility.
