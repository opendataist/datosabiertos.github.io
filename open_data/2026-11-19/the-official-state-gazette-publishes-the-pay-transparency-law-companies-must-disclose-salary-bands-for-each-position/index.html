The Official State Gazette publishes the pay transparency law: companies must disclose salary bands for each position

The Official State Gazette publishes the pay transparency law: companies must disclose salary bands for each position

The new legislation transposes European directives to eliminate pay secrecy, requiring companies to disclose salary bands before interviews and to maintain verifiable records. This structural change ensures that pay differences between jobs of equal value are based solely on objective criteria, transforming pay equity from a declarative promise into an auditable and enforceable reality, backed by a robust sanctioning regime. The law strengthens workers’ rights by guaranteeing clear information from the first day of employment and protecting employees against retaliation when they assert their rights. By prohibiting discriminatory orders and ensuring indemnity, it empowers the workforce to negotiate on more equitable terms, reducing the information asymmetries that have historically perpetuated discrimination, particularly gender-based discrimination, in recruitment and career progression processes. This advancement is crucial for the principles of open data, as it requires that salary information cease to be private and opaque and instead become a verifiable public asset. Mandatory transparency fosters accountability and enables citizens, researchers, and public authorities to analyze patterns of inequality, demonstrating that access to structured and auditable data is essential to ensuring social justice and labor market efficiency.
