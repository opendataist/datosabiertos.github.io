Once European countries call for a law to safeguard copyright in the face of artificial intelligence

Once European countries call for a law to safeguard copyright in the face of artificial intelligence

A group of eleven European Union member states has requested that the European Commission draft specific legislation to protect copyrighted works from being used in the training of artificial intelligence models. This initiative aims to establish stronger protection for the cultural and creative sectors, driven by the perception that the large-scale use of data without consent poses a threat to creators’ livelihoods. There is significant tension between the urgency of safeguarding cultural heritage and the desire to avoid losing technological competitiveness against global powers. While creative industries view data extraction as theft, the technology sector argues that such restrictions could put Europe at a disadvantage. The document’s dual objective is to protect human labor while maintaining fair conditions for AI innovation, acknowledging the existing divide between national ministries of culture and those of economy. The relevance to open data lies in the challenge of balancing transparency and access to the data needed for scientific and technological development with individual and collective rights over intellectual property. This debate underscores the need to define clear frameworks specifying which data should be considered open and which should remain restricted, ensuring that technological progress does not come at the expense of creators. The proposed legislation, expected in 2027, will mark a milestone in the ethical governance of data in the digital age.
