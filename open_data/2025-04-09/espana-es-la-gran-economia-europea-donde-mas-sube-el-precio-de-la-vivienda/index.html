España es la gran economía europea donde más sube el precio de la vivienda

Spain’s housing market has undergone a dramatic surge in prices, significantly outpacing the average growth observed across the European Union. This sharp inflation places the country among the top economies for residential cost increases, creating a severe affordability crisis in which the effort required to purchase a home has reached unsustainable levels for many households. The disparity with other major European economies underscores a unique structural imbalance within the Spanish real estate sector. The primary driver of this inflation is a critical shortage of housing supply, caused by restrictive policies and excessive bureaucratic delays in granting construction permits. Experts highlight a substantial gap between the formation of new households and the actual number of homes being built, a situation exacerbated by an investment climate that discourages new developments. The slow administrative processing of permits prevents the market from responding to demand, locking out potential buyers and keeping the housing stock artificially low despite clear demographic needs. This situation is highly relevant to open data, as transparent, real-time access to housing metrics—such as permit processing times, construction starts, and inventory levels—is essential for holding authorities accountable. By making granular data on land use and bureaucratic bottlenecks publicly available, citizens and researchers can identify inefficiencies and advocate for necessary regulatory reforms. Ultimately, open data serves as a vital tool for diagnosing the root causes of the housing crisis and driving the policy changes needed to increase supply and stabilize prices.
