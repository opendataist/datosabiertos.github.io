PSC urged to be transparent and accountable

PSC urged to be transparent and accountable

The article highlights a critical contradiction within the Public Service Commission, which plays a key role in drafting Freedom of Information legislation while allegedly failing to adhere to its own transparency standards. By remaining silent on serious complaints involving staff misconduct, the Commission undermines its stated commitment to openness and accountability. This behavior suggests a disconnect between the institution’s public policies and its internal practices, raising doubts about its integrity and ethical leadership. The core implication is that true governance requires leading by example. If the body responsible for promoting good governance and ethical behavior in the civil service does not practice what it preaches, it weakens the foundation of public trust. The failure to respond to public queries contradicts the principles of the Public Service Act, demonstrating that accountability must start at the top to be credible. Without this internal consistency, the promise of responsible administration remains hollow. This scenario is vital to the open data movement because it illustrates the gap between legal frameworks and actual implementation. Freedom of Information laws are designed to empower citizens and combat corruption by ensuring access to government records, but these tools are ineffective if agencies resist disclosure or evade scrutiny. The article serves as a reminder that open data initiatives rely on genuine political will and cultural change within government bodies, not just statutory requirements.
