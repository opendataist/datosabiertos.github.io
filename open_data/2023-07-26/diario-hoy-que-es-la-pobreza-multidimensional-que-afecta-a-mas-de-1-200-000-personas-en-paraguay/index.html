Diario HOY | Qué es la "pobreza multidimensional" que afecta a más de 1.200.000 personas en Paraguay

The report concludes that sexual exploitation is the predominant form of human trafficking, disproportionately affecting women and young people. This data highlights a critical vulnerable demographic, revealing that the majority of victims are female and under thirty, with a significant portion being minors. These statistics underscore the urgent need for targeted interventions that prioritize the protection of vulnerable groups, as the current landscape shows trafficking systems actively exploiting youth and women for sexual services and forced labor. The context of these crimes is diverse, ranging from international borders to private residences and various commercial establishments, indicating that trafficking is not confined to specific geographic zones but permeates multiple sectors of society. The profound psychological and social consequences for victims, including trauma and isolation, necessitate comprehensive support systems. Effective response mechanisms must go beyond legal prosecution to include robust psychological assistance, emphasizing that recovery requires holistic care that addresses both immediate trauma and long-term social reintegration needs. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how statistical transparency in government observatories can reveal hidden social crises. By making crime data accessible, society can analyze patterns of exploitation, identify at-risk populations, and advocate for policy changes based on empirical evidence rather than speculation. Open data serves as a crucial tool for accountability, enabling researchers and activists to expose the scale and nature of human rights violations, thereby driving informed decision-making and resource allocation to combat trafficking effectively.
