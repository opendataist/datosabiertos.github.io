El precio del alquiler sube en junio un 4,5% interanual y marca máximos en 27 provincias

Spain’s rental market is experiencing persistent price inflation, driven by a critical shortage of available housing. Demand remains high while supply shrinks because properties are being sold, converted to tourist rentals, or left vacant. This imbalance creates intense upward pressure on rents, particularly in regions with limited stock, indicating a structural deficit rather than temporary market fluctuations. Legal uncertainty regarding housing regulations is exacerbating the crisis by discouraging owners from offering long-term rentals. Many landlords are migrating to alternative markets or holding properties empty to avoid regulatory risks. Consequently, even as national inflation shows slight signs of easing, rental costs continue to soar, especially in coastal and major urban areas, reaching historical highs that outpace general economic trends. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it highlights the urgent need for transparent, accessible, and standardized housing statistics. Public authorities and citizens require reliable, real-time information to understand market dynamics, advocate for effective policy interventions, and hold stakeholders accountable. Open data serves as a vital tool for democratizing knowledge about housing affordability and fostering informed public debate on residential rights.
