The article highlights Paraguay’s strategic shift from exporting raw hydroelectric energy to creating a domestic market for high-value technological services through the Iguazú AI City project. This transition aims to capture significantly higher returns by utilizing local energy resources for computationally intensive industries, thereby fostering economic diversification and reducing reliance on traditional energy sales to neighboring countries. The initiative represents a fundamental change in how the nation leverages its natural resources, moving towards a knowledge-based economy that generates greater internal wealth and industrial linkages. By establishing local infrastructure for artificial intelligence training and inference, Paraguay intends to attract researchers, developers, and companies to utilize domestic computing power. This approach not only mitigates concerns about high energy consumption by emphasizing efficient cooling systems and a transition to solar power but also addresses connectivity issues by ensuring low latency for global operations. The project promises to integrate local talent into the global AI landscape, offering accessible cloud services and positioning the country as a competitive hub for data processing and model development without significant geographic disadvantages. This development is critically relevant to open data as it democratizes access to high-performance computing resources within the region. By providing local infrastructure, it enables the creation and processing of data-driven applications that can improve transparency, governance, and public service efficiency. Furthermore, it supports the growth of a digital ecosystem where open data initiatives can be scaled effectively, fostering innovation and allowing civil society and businesses to leverage advanced analytics for societal benefit, ultimately strengthening the nation’s capacity to manage and utilize information as a public asset.
