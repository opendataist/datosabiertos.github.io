Proteger sin espiar

Proteger sin espiar

The article examines the delicate balance between national security and individual privacy, highlighting how advanced surveillance technologies can inadvertently enable arbitrary intrusions or serve criminal interests without strict oversight. This tension reveals that while security and privacy are not inherently incompatible, achieving equilibrium requires robust institutional safeguards to prevent democratic erosion and protect civil liberties. Regarding open data, the piece underscores the critical importance of international standards and transparency protocols when managing personal information. It argues that systems lacking independent oversight or proper cybersecurity are dangerously flawed, emphasizing that data infrastructure must adhere to democratic principles. The absence of these controls turns data aggregation from a security tool into a potential instrument for repression. The author specifically criticizes Mexico’s recent creation of interconnected biometric and intelligence platforms for bypassing these essential safeguards. By prioritizing surveillance capabilities over independent supervision, the state risks establishing an oppressive regime. This case study serves as a vital warning for open data advocates, demonstrating that data collection initiatives must prioritize privacy rights and democratic accountability to remain legitimate and safe.
