Denver Post Doxxes Citizens For Sharing Pictures And Public Records About Crime

Denver Post Doxxes Citizens For Sharing Pictures And Public Records About Crime

The Denver Post’s decision to publicly identify three citizens for sharing legally obtained public records with a social media account represents a severe erosion of transparency norms. By utilizing their own access to public records to expose the identities of those exercising their right to information, the publication undermined the foundational principle that public data should remain accessible and anonymous for legitimate civic engagement. This action signals a chilling effect on citizens who rely on open government laws to hold institutions accountable, suggesting that media outlets may now actively work to discourage the very transparency they claim to uphold. The newspaper’s defense, which framed these individuals as citizen journalists subject to public scrutiny, ignores the legal distinction between professional reporting and ordinary citizens exercising statutory rights. The fact that only a small fraction of the social media account’s content derived from these records highlights the disproportionate nature of the backlash. This incident demonstrates how media entities can weaponize their privileged access to information to intimidate individuals, transforming public records from tools of accountability into liabilities for those who seek to use them responsibly. This case is critical to the open data community because it illustrates a dangerous precedent where accessing public information leads to privacy violations rather than public benefit. It raises urgent concerns about the security of open data systems when media organizations and government bodies collude, intentionally or otherwise, to punish information seekers. If citizens fear that requesting public records will result in personal doxxing, the efficacy of open data initiatives collapses, rendering transparency laws ineffective and eroding public trust in democratic institutions.
