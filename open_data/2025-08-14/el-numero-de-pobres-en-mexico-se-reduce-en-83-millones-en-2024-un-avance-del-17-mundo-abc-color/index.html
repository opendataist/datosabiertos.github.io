El número de pobres en México se reduce en 8,3 millones en 2024, un avance del 17 % - Mundo - ABC Color

El número de pobres en México se reduce en 8,3 millones en 2024, un avance del 17 % - Mundo - ABC Color

Mexico has achieved a significant reduction in poverty rates, marking a notable shift in the nation’s socioeconomic landscape following a recent institutional change in how data is officially measured. The transition from Coneval to INEGI for these measurements represents a pivotal moment for transparency and accountability in national statistics. This institutional update is crucial for the open data community, as it establishes new standards for public accessibility and methodological rigor in poverty metrics, ensuring that citizens and researchers can scrutinize the government’s approach to social welfare with greater clarity and trust in the underlying data infrastructure. The core finding reveals a substantial decline in both multidimensional poverty and income-based poverty lines, indicating that millions of citizens have improved their living standards and access to social services. This improvement suggests that economic growth has translated into tangible benefits for a significant portion of the population, reducing the number of people living in extreme poverty. For open data advocates, these results highlight the importance of multidimensional metrics that go beyond simple income figures to capture a holistic view of well-being, encouraging the development of more comprehensive data indicators that reflect real human conditions rather than just economic transactions. Despite overall progress, persistent regional inequalities reveal a stark divide between northern and southern states, with southern regions continuing to suffer from high poverty and educational gaps. This disparity underscores the need for targeted, data-driven policy interventions that address local nuances rather than applying uniform solutions. The relevance to open data lies in the necessity of granular, geolocated datasets that allow for precise monitoring of these inequalities. By making this detailed spatial data open, stakeholders can better advocate for equitable resource distribution and hold local governments accountable for closing the developmental gap between regions.
