Las mujeres mexicanas están menos satisfechas con su vida que los hombres: Inegi

The study reveals a persistent gender gap in subjective well-being in Mexico, with women consistently reporting lower emotional balance and life satisfaction than men. This disparity is particularly pronounced among young adults aged 18 to 29, suggesting that structural or social factors disproportionately affect the mental and emotional states of younger women compared to their male counterparts. Beyond overall life satisfaction, the data highlights significant variations across specific domains, with personal relationships receiving the highest ratings while safety and neighborhood conditions score notably low. The widening gender gap in areas like personal security and perceived national well-being indicates that women feel less secure and satisfied with their immediate environment than men, pointing to systemic issues in social infrastructure and public safety. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparently collected statistical indicators can expose deep-seated social inequalities. By making metrics on subjective well-being publicly available, institutions like INEGI enable researchers and policymakers to move beyond economic indicators and address the qualitative aspects of human development, fostering more informed and equitable public health and social policies.
