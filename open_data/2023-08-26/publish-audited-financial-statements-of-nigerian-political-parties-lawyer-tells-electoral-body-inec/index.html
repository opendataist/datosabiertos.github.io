Publish Audited Financial Statements Of Nigerian Political Parties, Lawyer Tells Electoral Body, INEC

The article highlights a critical gap in Nigeria’s democratic transparency, where the Independent National Electoral Commission has failed to publish audited financial statements for political parties. A legal practitioner argues that this omission violates constitutional mandates and electoral laws designed to ensure accountability. This lack of disclosure prevents citizens from understanding how parties are funded, fostering an environment where corruption and opaque patronage networks can thrive unchecked. This situation underscores the vital role of open data in combating political corruption. When financial records of political entities remain hidden, it becomes nearly impossible to track "godfatherism" or illicit funding sources. By withholding these accounts, the electoral body obscures the true cost of democracy, making it difficult for researchers and the public to analyze the influence of money in politics. Transparency in this sector is not merely a bureaucratic formality but a fundamental requirement for healthy political competition. The relevance to open data lies in the necessity of accessible, machine-readable financial records for public scrutiny. Without published data, independent researchers cannot build comprehensive databases to study political financing trends or hold actors accountable. The call for INEC to release these documents serves as a reminder that open government requires proactive data publication. Ensuring that political party finances are open and auditable empowers civil society to enforce accountability and deepen democratic inclusivity.
