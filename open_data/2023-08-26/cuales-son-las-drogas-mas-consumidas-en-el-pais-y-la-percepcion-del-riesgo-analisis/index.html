Cuáles son las drogas más consumidas en el país y la percepción del riesgo | Análisis

The official joint survey conducted by INDEC and SEDRONAR reveals that the use of psychoactive substances in Argentina is a cross-cutting social phenomenon, not confined to specific age groups or socioeconomic levels. Alcohol has emerged as the most commonly used substance, characterized by its exclusively collective use and low perceived risk, which calls for redesigning prevention strategies toward community-based rather than individual approaches. This perspective is crucial for open data initiatives, as it dispels stigmas and provides a solid empirical foundation for universal public policies. Furthermore, the study highlights distinct patterns by substance: tobacco shows early initiation but low current usage frequency, while cannabis predominates among young people for recreational purposes. This contrasts with tranquilizers, whose use is largely medicalized, and cocaine, which remains at marginal levels. This segmentation allows for identifying differentiated care needs and understanding that legality does not guarantee safety; rather, excessive use of any substance entails shared physical and social risks. The relevance for open data lies in methodological transparency and access to detailed information on health behaviors. By expanding the age range up to 75 years and including variables on self-care practices, a more inclusive and complex dataset is generated. This facilitates long-term comparative analysis and promotes accountability, enabling civil society and public officials to monitor the effectiveness of health interventions using verifiable and publicly available information.
