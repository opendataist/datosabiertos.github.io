Buscan quitar a SAT 'préstamo' de datos

The Supreme Court is reviewing a proposal to annul a 2020 reform that permitted the tax authority to use biometric data for third-party identity verification. This action challenges the government’s capacity to monetize or share private citizens’ information without prior consent, raising significant concerns about privacy rights. The court is examining whether such practices comply with legal standards for data protection and legitimate purposes. This situation underscores the tension between administrative efficiency and individual privacy within open data ecosystems. When public institutions hold sensitive personal information, clear boundaries must be established to prevent misuse. The argument that simple yes-or-no checks justify access to deep personal records is being questioned, emphasizing the need for strict oversight. The relevance to open data lies in establishing precedents for how government-held data can be shared. If this reform is struck down, it reinforces the principle that personal data cannot be freely repurposed for commercial or other governmental uses without explicit authorization. This safeguards citizens from potential exploitation of their biometric and private information by third parties.
