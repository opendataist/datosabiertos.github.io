Gender-disaggregated data serves as a vital tool for understanding and overcoming systemic barriers that prevent women from fully participating in the global economy. By highlighting persistent issues such as widespread gender-based violence, stagnant labor force participation gaps, and the economic toll of child marriage, these insights reveal the root causes of inequality. This evidence is essential for policymakers to design targeted interventions that address specific needs, ensuring that development strategies are both effective and equitable. The article emphasizes that without accurate, sex-disaggregated data, it is impossible to measure progress or allocate resources efficiently. The stagnation of key metrics over decades underscores the urgency of improving data collection efforts. Investing in robust statistical infrastructure allows governments and organizations to track outcomes accurately, moving beyond vague assumptions to evidence-based decision-making. This precision enables the creation of policies that genuinely reflect the realities women face in accessing education, income, and safety. This content is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the transformative power of transparent, accessible datasets in driving social change. When gender statistics are open and widely available, they empower advocates, researchers, and citizens to hold institutions accountable. It demonstrates how open data initiatives can bridge the gap between knowledge and action, fostering a more inclusive society where women’s contributions are recognized and supported through informed, data-driven governance.
