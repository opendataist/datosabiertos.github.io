ChatGPT will no longer comply if you ask it to repeat a word 'forever'— after a recent prompt revealed training data and personal info

OpenAI has implemented a new safety measure in ChatGPT, preventing the AI from repeating specific words indefinitely, even when explicitly requested by users. This restriction addresses technical limitations, ensures practical utility by avoiding spammy interactions, and maintains a positive user experience. The change reflects a proactive approach to managing model behavior beyond existing usage policies, signaling a shift toward stricter content filtering to enhance interaction quality. This update follows significant security concerns raised by researchers who discovered that such requests could expose sensitive training data, including private contact information. By stopping this behavior, OpenAI aims to close a vulnerability that allowed users to inadvertently extract verbatim memorized examples from the model’s internal dataset. This incident highlights the critical need for robust safeguards in generative AI systems to prevent data leakage and protect user privacy. The relevance to open data lies in the ongoing debate surrounding transparency versus security in AI development. While open data principles advocate for accessibility and understanding of how information is processed, this move underscores the complexities of balancing openness with safety. It demonstrates that AI providers must actively guard against unintended data exposure, ensuring that systems remain both informative and secure, which is essential for maintaining public trust in emerging technologies.
