Toledo cierra noviembre con 171 desempleados, que acumula 50.209 personas paradas

In November, registered unemployment in Castilla-La Mancha rose slightly, marking the fifth consecutive month of increases. Despite this short-term upward trend, the total number of unemployed individuals represents the lowest figure for this specific month since 2007. Over the past year, the region has still managed to reduce its overall unemployment rate, indicating a broader, positive structural improvement in the labor market despite recent monthly fluctuations. The increase was not uniform across all sectors or demographics. While unemployment declined in agriculture and industry, it rose significantly in the services sector, which remains the largest employer and the primary driver of these changes. The data reveals a notable divergence between age groups; unemployment increased among younger workers under twenty-five, whereas it decreased for those aged twenty-five and older. This suggests that entry-level job opportunities may be tightening, even as stability improves for more experienced workers. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the value of granular, public statistics in understanding nuanced economic realities. By accessing detailed datasets that break down unemployment by sector, gender, and province, analysts can move beyond aggregate headlines to identify specific labor market vulnerabilities. Transparent access to such diverse indicators allows researchers and policymakers to evaluate the effectiveness of employment policies and address structural imbalances more effectively than by relying on simplified national summaries alone.
