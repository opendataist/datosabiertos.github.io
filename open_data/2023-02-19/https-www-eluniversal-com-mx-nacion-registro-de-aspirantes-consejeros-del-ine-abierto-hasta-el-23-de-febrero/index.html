The Mexican Chamber of Officials has opened the registration period for four new members of the National Electoral Institute (INE) Council, requiring applicants to submit their digital documentation by the end of February. A newly established Technical Committee will objectively assess these candidates to ensure that the process complies with the principles of legality, impartiality, and maximum transparency. This selection mechanism ensures that future council members are chosen through a transparent and rigorous evaluation process, which includes written exams and interviews. By prioritizing merit and open criteria, the system aims to strengthen the integrity of electoral governance and enhance public trust in the institution. This case is highly relevant to open data, as it illustrates how digital platforms can facilitate transparent, accessible, and verifiable public recruitment processes. The reliance on digital submission and publicly stated evaluation principles demonstrates a commitment to data-driven governance, enabling citizens to monitor and verify that institutional appointments adhere to established standards of fairness and openness.
