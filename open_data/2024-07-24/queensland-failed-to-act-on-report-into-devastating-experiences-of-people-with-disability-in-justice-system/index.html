Queensland failed to act on report into ‘devastating’ experiences of people with disability in justice system

A commissioned report reveals that the Queensland justice system systematically fails people with disabilities, subjecting them to brutality, mistreatment, and coercive interactions with police. Despite containing devastating firsthand accounts and actionable recommendations, the government withheld the full document from public view, releasing only a sanitized summary that omitted critical reforms. This suppression highlights a structural indifference to the lived experiences of marginalized groups, suggesting that policy decisions are prioritized over transparency and accountability. The study underscores how current practices, such as a lack of independent support during interviews and inappropriate communication by law enforcement, exacerbate distress rather than de-escalate situations. These failures indicate that individuals with disabilities are often trapped in abusive relationships with the state, leading to increased criminalization rather than support. The evidence points to a justice framework that is inherently unresponsive to neurodiversity and cognitive impairments, resulting in severe psychological and physical harm to vulnerable citizens. This case is highly relevant to open data advocacy as it demonstrates the tangible risks of withholding government-commissioned research. When critical evidence regarding human rights abuses remains classified or partially redacted, policymakers cannot be held accountable for systemic inefficiencies or cruelty. True openness requires not just the release of data, but the transparent publication of full findings, including uncomfortable truths, to ensure that institutional failures are visible and addressable by the public.
