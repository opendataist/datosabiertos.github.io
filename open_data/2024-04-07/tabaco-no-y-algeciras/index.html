Tabaco, no. ¿Y Algeciras?

Tabaco, no. ¿Y Algeciras?

The article argues that the Spanish government’s focus on anti-smoking campaigns overlooks the far more critical crisis of illegal drug trafficking in Algeciras. It highlights that this region operates as a failed state, where organized crime outmatches law enforcement and fosters widespread corruption, ultimately posing a greater threat to public safety and institutional integrity than tobacco use. The author critiques the political prioritization of superficial public health measures over urgent security failures. By neglecting the dominance of drug traffickers and their ability to corrupt local authorities, the state fails to protect citizens and maintain order. This selective response suggests a lack of political will to address the root causes of territorial instability and criminal empowerment in affected areas. This text is relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible statistics to expose government blind spots. Reliable, open datasets on crime, corruption, and public health outcomes are essential for holding authorities accountable and ensuring that policy decisions reflect the true scale of societal challenges rather than political convenience.
