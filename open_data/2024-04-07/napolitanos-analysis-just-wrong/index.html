Napolitano’s Analysis Just Wrong

This letter refutes Andrew Napolitano’s misinterpretations of St. Thomas Aquinas and Pope Francis, clarifying that natural law requires active learning to actualize human potential rather than representing innate, instinctive knowledge. By emphasizing that rational creatures must voluntarily seek their purpose through difficult effort, the author highlights how Aquinas recognized the essential, positive role of government in fostering the common good, contradicting claims that state structures inherently negate liberty. The argument further defends Pope Francis’s integrity against accusations of diluting Church teaching, positioning his leadership as a faithful continuation of Gospel values that prioritize care for the marginalized and the environment. This correction is vital for open data discussions because it underscores the critical importance of accurate contextualization and expert verification when interpreting complex philosophical or theological frameworks within public discourse. Such rigorous correction of misinformation ensures that public understanding of moral and social structures remains grounded in verified scholarly consensus rather than distorted narratives. In an era where data shapes public opinion, relying on authoritative sources prevents the spread of conceptual errors that could mislead policy decisions or social engagement, thereby reinforcing the need for transparency and accuracy in how foundational ideas are communicated to the wider community.
