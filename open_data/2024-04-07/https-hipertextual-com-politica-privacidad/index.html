This article serves as the privacy policy for Hipertextual, detailing the collection and management of user data, including cookies and usage statistics. It outlines the legal bases for processing, such as contractual necessity and legitimate interest, while specifying that data is shared with third-party analytics providers like Google to monitor web traffic and deliver targeted advertising. The policy emphasizes user rights, including access, correction, deletion, and the ability to opt out of commercial processing, ensuring transparency regarding data storage periods and security measures. The relevance to open data lies in the critical intersection between private data governance and public transparency. By explicitly listing third-party services and international data transfers, the document highlights how digital platforms operate within a complex ecosystem of data flows. This transparency is essential for the open data movement, as it allows civil society and researchers to audit corporate practices, understand the infrastructure behind digital services, and advocate for stronger data protection standards. Ultimately, this policy underscores the necessity of accountability in the digital economy. It demonstrates that even private entities must adhere to strict regulatory frameworks when handling personal information, reinforcing the principle that data subjects retain control over their digital footprint. For the open data community, this serves as a model for how organizations can publicly disclose their data practices, fostering trust and enabling broader discussions on privacy, security, and the ethical implications of data collection in online environments.
