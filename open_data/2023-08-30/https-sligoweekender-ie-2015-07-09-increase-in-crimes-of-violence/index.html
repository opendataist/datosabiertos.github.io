Crime statistics for the Sligo-Leitrim division reveal a complex shift in criminal activity over the past year. While violent offenses, including sexual assaults, robberies, and burglaries, have risen significantly, certain property crimes and fraud cases have declined. This data highlights the nuanced reality of local crime trends, showing that overall safety improvements in one area do not necessarily negate deterioration in others. The Central Statistics Office resumed publishing these figures after a suspension triggered by concerns over data integrity. A prior investigation uncovered significant irregularities in how gardai recorded crimes on the PULSE system, with a notable portion of reported crimes either missing or misclassified. The recent decision to restart publication indicates that a comprehensive review of data quality has been completed, aiming to restore confidence in the administrative records. This development is crucial for open data advocates because it underscores the persistent challenges in ensuring accurate, accessible public records. The incident illustrates how technical and procedural errors can compromise data transparency, making it difficult for citizens and researchers to rely on official statistics. It emphasizes the need for robust validation processes in public sector data systems to maintain trust and ensure that open information remains a reliable tool for accountability and analysis.
