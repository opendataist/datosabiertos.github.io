Can A Convict Access Case Diary Under RTI? Telangana High Court Reserves Judgment

The Telangana High Court is deliberating whether a convicted individual has the right to access Case Diary Part-I under the Right to Information Act, despite the investigation being concluded. The central tension lies between the petitioner’s claim of transparency regarding their own case and the state’s assertion that the diary contains confidential investigative details that remain protected to ensure physical safety and procedural integrity. This case highlights the ambiguity in exception provisions, forcing a legal interpretation of whether a conviction ends the "prosecution" status that typically restricts such access. This dispute is highly relevant to open data principles as it questions the boundaries of public accountability versus state secrecy in law enforcement records. If the court rules in favor of the petitioner, it would establish that completed investigations do not warrant permanent secrecy, thereby promoting greater transparency in criminal justice data. Conversely, upholding the rejection reinforces the notion that certain state records are inherently non-public, limiting the scope of open data initiatives within the legal sector and potentially obscuring how police conduct investigations even after charges are filed. Ultimately, the judgment will clarify the interplay between the Right to Information Act and the Code of Criminal Procedure regarding evidentiary documents. The outcome determines whether citizens can actively request detailed investigative logs post-conviction or if such information remains exclusively within the control of judicial bodies during appeals. A ruling favoring disclosure could set a significant precedent for open data in governance, encouraging citizens to hold public authorities accountable for their investigative processes, while a restrictive ruling may perpetuate information asymmetry between the state and the accused.
