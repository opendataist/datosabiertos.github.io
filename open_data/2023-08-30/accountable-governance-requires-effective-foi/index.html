Accountable governance requires effective FOI

The Law Council of Australia warns that Australia’s Freedom of Information regime is severely undermined by chronic underfunding and the excessive, often incorrect, use of exemption clauses. These systemic failures create bureaucratic delays and extensive redaction, effectively shielding government information from public scrutiny. This lack of transparency restricts informed public discourse and weakens the rule of law, posing a direct threat to democratic integrity. The appeals process is particularly constrained, with reviews taking an excessive amount of time due to insufficient resources at the Information Commissioner’s Office. This delay discourages accountability and prevents timely resolution of access disputes. Consequently, the system fails to support a culture of disclosure, allowing agencies to routinely deny information rather than proactively sharing it with the community. This situation is critical for open data advocates because it highlights the gap between legislative intent and practical reality. While laws mandate transparency, administrative bottlenecks and resource shortages stifle the availability of public information. Strengthening oversight and funding is essential to ensure that open government initiatives truly function as intended, fostering a more accountable and transparent society.
