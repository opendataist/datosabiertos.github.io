Corte Suprema ratifica que es aplicable la ley de transparencia a corporación cultural de las Condes

The Supreme Court of Chile has confirmed that municipal corporations are subject to the Transparency Law, reaffirming that public funds cannot evade scrutiny through the creation of independent entities. This legal ruling definitively closes the debate on the obligation of these organizations to be accountable, establishing that administrative independence does not constitute a mechanism to circumvent transparency controls or those of the Comptroller General. The decision has profound implications for administrative integrity, as it ensures that all municipal corporations fall under the supervision of the Council for Transparency. This represents a significant advance in the fight against corruption, by preventing specific legal structures from being used to conceal essential financial information. The legal certainty generated by this ruling allows control measures to take effect immediately, without the need to wait for additional legislative reforms. This case is fundamental to the field of open data, as it validates public access to the financial information of entities that perform administrative functions, even if they operate with separate legal personality. By guaranteeing that data such as balance sheets and budgets are public, it promotes fiscal transparency and enables citizens and civil society to monitor the use of state resources. This strengthens the open data ecosystem in Chile, ensuring that relevant information on public management is accessible, verifiable, and used for accountability purposes.
