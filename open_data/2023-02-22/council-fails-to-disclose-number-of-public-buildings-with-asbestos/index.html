Council fails to disclose number of public buildings with asbestos

Thousands of public buildings across Britain remain contaminated with cancer-causing asbestos, posing a significant health risk to students, staff, and community members. A Freedom of Information request by a leading law firm revealed that the majority of surveyed local authorities have not fully accounted for the extent of this hazard. With schools comprising nearly a quarter of affected sites, the presence of this carcinogen in densely populated areas like classrooms represents a critical failure in public safety management. Despite government rejection of a proposed forty-year removal deadline, experts argue that the current pace of remediation is dangerously insufficient. Only a small fraction of identified buildings have seen asbestos removed in recent years, while maintenance budgets are stretched thin and older structures are deteriorating. The legal perspective emphasizes that while some materials may currently be stable, any disturbance or disrepair can quickly release deadly fibers, making proactive removal essential rather than merely managing existing risk. This situation is highly relevant to open_data because it illustrates the limitations of transparency when authorities fail to respond to information requests. Bristol’s failure to engage highlights how data gaps hinder public accountability and policy oversight. Reliable, accessible data on public infrastructure hazards is crucial for identifying systemic risks, enforcing safety standards, and ensuring that citizens and policymakers have the necessary information to demand effective government action.
