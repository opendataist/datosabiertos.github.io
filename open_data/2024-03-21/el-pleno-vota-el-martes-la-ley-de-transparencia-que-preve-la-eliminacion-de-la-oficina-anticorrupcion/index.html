El pleno vota el martes la ley de transparencia que prevé la eliminación de la Oficina Anticorrupción

The Balearic Parliament is set to approve a transparency law that abolishes a specialized anti-corruption office in order to cut costs, a move criticized by opposition parties as harmful to democratic health. This decision has sparked concerns about the handling of ongoing cases and the loss of specialized expertise within the regional administration. The debate underscores a clash between fiscal efficiency and institutional integrity, with critics arguing that centralizing these functions undermines the region’s capacity to effectively combat corruption. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the political tension between transparency mandates and the dismantling of dedicated oversight bodies. It raises critical questions about how data on public officials and corruption cases will be managed, accessed, and preserved when specialized institutions are dissolved, potentially affecting public accountability and data availability.
