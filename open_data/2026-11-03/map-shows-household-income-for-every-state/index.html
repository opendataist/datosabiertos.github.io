Map shows household income for every state

Map shows household income for every state

The 2025 American Community Survey data reveals that while the U.S. national median household income saw a modest inflation-adjusted increase, this improvement was neither uniform nor significant across the majority of the country. The report highlights a stark geographic disparity, with significant gaps between high-income areas like the District of Columbia and low-income regions such as Mississippi. This uneven distribution underscores that economic gains are concentrated in specific locales, leaving many households behind despite national headline increases. Further analysis demonstrates that income inequality has slightly widened, as indicated by a rise in the Gini index. The data also exposes deep disparities based on race and age, with Asian-headed households earning significantly more than Black-headed households, and middle-aged earners outpacing younger and older demographics. These demographic breakdowns suggest that structural inequalities persist, affecting economic stability for specific racial groups and age cohorts, thereby complicating efforts to achieve broad-based financial well-being. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it exemplifies the critical value of granular, publicly accessible statistical datasets. By breaking down national trends into state, metropolitan, and demographic levels, the Census Bureau enables researchers, policymakers, and citizens to identify specific areas of economic distress and success. Such transparency fosters evidence-based decision-making, allowing for targeted interventions to address the widening income gaps and regional disparities identified in the open data release.
