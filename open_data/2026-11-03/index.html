Laws decide how open things can be. Courts in Scotland recently used a new idea called “jigsaw identification” to limit openness. These courts said it is a crime to share complaints about former officials if putting different pieces of data together reveals who they are. This rule shows that we must balance being open with protecting privacy. We must protect vulnerable people from having too much data revealed. But we also demand that leaders be held accountable.

There is a big tension between open access and copyright in the United States. A U.S. court recently ruled that the Internet Archive broke copyright laws. The Archive lent out digitized books. The court rejected its claim that this was fair use. This decision shows that copyright rules stop the digital saving and sharing of public knowledge. We must see that open access faces big legal hurdles. It does not guarantee freedom from legal trouble.

We must check official stats against independent public records. This helps detect methodological flaws and political manipulation. We cannot just accept government statistics as true. We must verify them against other sources. This ensures they reflect reality.
