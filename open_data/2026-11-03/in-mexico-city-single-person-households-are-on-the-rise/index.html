In Mexico City, single-person households are on the rise

In Mexico City, single-person households are on the rise

The transformation of the family structure in Mexico City reflects a profound shift in which singlehood and single-person households are gaining ground at the expense of traditional family units. This phenomenon is not isolated but rather part of a broader social reconfiguration driven by declining fertility rates and population aging, signaling a destabilization of the classic nuclear family model in favor of more individualized and diverse living arrangements. The relevance of these data for open data initiatives lies in the need to update public policies and regulatory frameworks to reflect new demographic realities. By capturing subtle yet consistent changes in household composition, open data enable the identification of structural trends that require tailored responses, particularly in areas such as housing, taxation, and urban planning, thereby preventing official statistics from becoming outdated in the face of current social dynamics. Cultural and economic factors act as drivers of this transition, with the pursuit of personal and professional stability prioritizing independence over family formation. Evidence suggests that social expectations regarding marriage and reproduction are changing, and that limited economic resources encourage individual autonomy. Understanding these motivations is crucial for correctly interpreting sociodemographic indicators and designing more precise and effective interventions.
