Precisión y transparencia en las estadísticas oficiales

The article argues that statistical agencies such as DANE must periodically update their surveys to reflect societal changes, rather than relying on static instruments. As Colombia evolves, questions that were once relevant may become obsolete or inefficient. By optimizing the National Quality of Life Survey, DANE aims to reduce the burden on respondents and improve data accuracy, ensuring that measurement tools remain aligned with the actual needs and realities of the population. Contrary to claims that health-related data is being concealed, the agency clarifies that key indicators, including those defined by the OECD, have been retained or reintroduced with improved design. These adjustments are driven by technical necessities, such as eliminating redundant questions or addressing low response rates, rather than political agendas. The process prioritizes precision and transparency, making methodologies and microdata publicly available for independent verification. This transparency is vital for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates a commitment to international statistical standards and allows for external scrutiny. By publishing methods and inviting constructive critique, DANE fosters trust in official statistics. The article highlights that open access to methodologies empowers users to understand data limitations, reinforcing the role of statistical offices as reliable pillars in an era of widespread misinformation.
