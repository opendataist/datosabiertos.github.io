FOIA Friday: $223 for one email, Portsmouth needs redo on pay records

Virginia’s government culture often contradicts its Freedom of Information Act by treating records as private by default rather than public. This systemic opacity is evident in extreme fee structures, where officials charge substantial upfront costs for minimal retrieval tasks, effectively pricing out citizens. Such practices create unnecessary financial and procedural barriers that suppress public scrutiny and hinder the fundamental right to access government information. Transparency is further compromised by inaccurate or incomplete disclosures that undermine trust in public institutions. When municipalities fail to provide comprehensive data, requiring additional requests to correct omissions, it suggests inadequate internal tracking and a lack of accountability. This inefficiency not only burdens the public with repeated efforts to obtain basic facts but also obscures how public funds are utilized, revealing significant gaps in administrative oversight. These incidents are critical to open data advocacy as they highlight the gap between legal mandates and actual implementation. They demonstrate that without strict enforcement and cultural shifts toward proactive disclosure, transparency remains theoretical rather than practical. Understanding these barriers is essential for reformers who seek to ensure that open data principles translate into accessible, accurate, and timely information for all citizens.
