Coastal Carolina University faces scrutiny for delaying the release of police reports involving high-profile individuals, highlighting systemic inefficiencies in public information transparency. Unlike local law enforcement agencies that typically provide records within days, the university has taken over a month, raising questions about consistency and accountability in handling freedom of information requests. This delay underscores the critical importance of timely open data access for public accountability. When educational institutions obstruct or prolong the disclosure of incident records, it impedes the public’s right to know and hinders independent journalism’s ability to report on campus safety and conduct. The discrepancy between standard legal timeframes and actual delivery times suggests administrative barriers that undermine trust. The article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how institutional opacity can conflict with legal mandates for transparency. It serves as a cautionary example of why robust, standardized processes for releasing public records are essential. Ensuring that data is accessible without unnecessary delays or financial hurdles is fundamental to maintaining an informed citizenry and ensuring that government and educational entities remain answerable to the public.
