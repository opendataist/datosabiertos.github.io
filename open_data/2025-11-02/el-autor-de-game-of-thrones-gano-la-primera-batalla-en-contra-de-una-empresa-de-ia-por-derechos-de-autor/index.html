El autor de ‘Game of Thrones’ ganó la primera batalla en contra de una empresa de IA por derechos de autor

El autor de ‘Game of Thrones’ ganó la primera batalla en contra de una empresa de IA por derechos de autor

A federal judge has allowed a class-action lawsuit against OpenAI to proceed, ruling that training ChatGPT on copyrighted works could constitute copyright infringement. This decision acknowledges that artificial intelligence models can generate texts with substantial similarities to original works, challenging the notion that the technology merely learns abstract ideas without replicating the specific creative expression of the authors who filed the lawsuit. The precedent establishes that the AI’s ability to imitate styles and generate detailed narratives based on texts used without permission justifies judicial review. Although the “fair use” defense remains pending, the ruling underscores the vulnerability of creators whose works are used as raw material to train algorithms without authorization, presenting a complex legal scenario for technological innovation. This case is pivotal for the open data movement, as it questions the transparency and legality of collecting content under open licenses or protected by copyright. By revealing how texts from well-known authors are processed without consent, the ruling highlights the need for clear ethical frameworks that protect intellectual property in the age of AI, promoting a culture of respect for human-generated data.
