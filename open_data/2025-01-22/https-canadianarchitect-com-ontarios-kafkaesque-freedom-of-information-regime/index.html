The article details how the Ontario government allegedly concealed the premeditated decision to close the Ontario Science Centre’s original location by using infrastructure issues as a pretext. Authors argue that internal documents, buried in lengthy appendices, reveal that alternative options were considered long before the closure announcement. This suggests the official justification was manufactured post-hoc to mask a political choice, raising serious concerns about transparency in public infrastructure planning. Despite filing Freedom of Information requests for specific digital files, the authors faced prolonged delays and unhelpful bureaucratic responses, contrasting sharply with the prompt delivery of other simple documents. This inconsistency implies intentional obstruction rather than administrative backlog. By leveraging delay as a tactic to evade accountability, the government avoids the immediate scrutiny that comes with refusal or heavy redaction, effectively shielding decision-making processes from public view. This case is critically relevant to open_data because it highlights the fragility of transparency mechanisms when political interests conflict with public accountability. It demonstrates that simply publishing data or responding to requests is insufficient if the underlying data is withheld or buried. True open data requires not just the availability of information, but the political will to release it without obstruction, ensuring that citizens can access the raw evidence needed to evaluate government actions.
