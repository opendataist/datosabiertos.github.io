Cámaras de seguridad en casa: ¿Existe multa? ¿Qué dice la Ley de Protección de Datos Personales?

The viral claim that installing home security cameras is illegal is incorrect. According to Mexico’s Federal Law on Protection of Personal Data Held by Private Parties, individuals may legally use surveillance devices. The key takeaway is that legality hinges on strict compliance with data protection regulations, not on a blanket prohibition of the technology itself. The primary risk lies in how the recorded footage is handled. Fines can be imposed if recordings are shared without consent or if cameras capture areas beyond the property’s boundaries. The law seeks to protect the privacy rights of individuals in public spaces who may be inadvertently recorded, emphasizing that unauthorized sharing of images—even in cases of theft—constitutes a serious violation. This issue is crucial to open data discourse because it highlights the intersection between individual security tools and public privacy rights. Understanding these boundaries ensures that data collection practices do not infringe upon personal liberties. It underscores the need for transparent measures, such as visible signage and properly angled cameras, to maintain trust and legal compliance in an increasingly monitored society.
