CPLT ordena investigación sumaria en 3 subsecretarías por grave incumplimiento a Ley de Transparencia

The Council for Transparency has launched investigations into three government subsecretariats due to serious failures in fulfilling their proactive transparency obligations. By not meeting mandatory online disclosure requirements, these entities have directly hindered citizens’ access to essential public information. This enforcement action underscores that transparency is a non-negotiable legal duty crucial for democratic accountability, rather than an optional administrative task. The core implications involve systemic gaps in the publication of budgets, contracts, and organizational structures within the Tourism, Human Rights, and Culture sectors. These deficiencies prevent effective public scrutiny of how state resources are managed and allocated. As a result, the lack of timely and complete data undermines trust in government institutions and obstructs citizens’ ability to evaluate public performance and hold officials accountable for their decisions. This situation is highly relevant to open data, as it highlights the critical need for proactive, standardized data publication by public entities. True open data requires more than mere digitization; it demands strict compliance with accessibility standards and timely updates. Without such adherence, the potential benefits of transparency—such as civic engagement and anti-corruption measures—cannot be realized, reinforcing the need for robust oversight mechanisms in public information management.
