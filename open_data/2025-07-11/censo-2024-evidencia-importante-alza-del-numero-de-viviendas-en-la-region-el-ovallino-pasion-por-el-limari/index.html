Censo 2024 evidencia importante alza del número de viviendas en la región - El Ovallino - Pasion por el Limarí

Censo 2024 evidencia importante alza del número de viviendas en la región - El Ovallino - Pasion por el Limarí

The 2024 Census reveals a significant demographic shift in the Coquimbo region, characterized by a steadily aging population and smaller household sizes. As the ratio of older adults to youth rises sharply, the region mirrors national trends toward an older society. This structural change demands a reevaluation of social services, particularly healthcare and elder care, as families become smaller and fewer in number, altering the traditional support structures for the elderly. Concurrently, the data highlights profound changes in migration patterns and social diversity. International migration has grown substantially, with a younger, active workforce arriving primarily from Latin American countries. At the same time, internal migration flows show a net influx of residents, contributing to regional growth. These movements enrich the cultural landscape and necessitate inclusive policies that address the specific needs of a more diverse and transient population, ensuring equitable access to housing, education, and employment opportunities. This article is crucial for open data because it demonstrates how granular, accessible census information drives evidence-based public policy. By publishing detailed statistics on gender identity, indigenous affiliation, and migration for the first time, the National Institute of Statistics (INE) makes previously invisible realities visible. This transparency allows researchers, policymakers, and civil society to analyze complex social transformations, fostering the design of more inclusive and effective public strategies that respond to the actual needs of a diverse and evolving population.
