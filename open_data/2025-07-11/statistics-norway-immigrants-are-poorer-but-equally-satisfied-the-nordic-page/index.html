Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

The article reveals a complex paradox regarding immigrants in Norway, highlighting that despite facing significantly poorer living conditions in employment, housing, and health compared to the native population, they report equal levels of life satisfaction. This discrepancy suggests that material disadvantages do not necessarily translate into lower subjective well-being, challenging common assumptions about the correlation between objective socioeconomic status and happiness in host societies. Beyond basic living standards, the data indicates higher rates of mental health struggles and loneliness among immigrants, though these experiences vary notably by country of origin. However, these challenges coexist with unusually strong institutional confidence, as immigrants often express greater trust in Norway’s political system, judiciary, and police than native citizens. This high level of civic engagement and trust signifies a deep social integration and affiliation with the state, despite personal hardships. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how comprehensive, transparent statistical surveys can uncover nuanced social dynamics that might otherwise remain invisible. By making detailed findings on income disparities, mental health, and social trust publicly accessible, open data allows researchers and policymakers to move beyond superficial metrics. It enables a deeper understanding of how marginalized groups integrate into society, fostering evidence-based discussions on inclusion and well-being rather than relying on stereotypes or incomplete information.
