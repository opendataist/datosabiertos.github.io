Martensen IP Offers Critical Guidance on AI Intellectual Property Risks, Examples of Copyright Issues, and FAQs

Martensen IP Offers Critical Guidance on AI Intellectual Property Risks, Examples of Copyright Issues, and FAQs

The rapid adoption of generative AI has created significant legal uncertainty regarding copyright infringement. While AI models learn patterns rather than storing literal copies, they can inadvertently reproduce protected content, exposing users to liability for "regurgitated" outputs. This risk is amplified because copyright infringement does not require intent, meaning companies can be held responsible for accidental violations even if they believe the generated material is original. Current U.S. law has not definitively resolved whether training AI on copyrighted data constitutes fair use, leaving businesses in a legal gray area. Concurrently, purely machine-generated works without substantial human authorship are generally ineligible for copyright protection. This creates a dangerous paradox where users may neither own the rights to the AI-generated output nor be shielded from lawsuits if that output infringes upon third-party intellectual property rights. This article is critically relevant to open data advocates because it highlights the tension between accessible data and intellectual property rights. As AI systems increasingly rely on vast datasets, often including publicly available information, the lack of clear legal frameworks threatens the sustainability of open data ecosystems. Understanding these liabilities is essential for ensuring that the benefits of open data do not come at the cost of legal instability for both creators and technology developers.
