El padrón móvil pondría en riesgo los datos de 26.4 millones

El padrón móvil pondría en riesgo los datos de 26.4 millones

The implementation of the new Mobile User Registry in Mexico, which links the CURP (Unique Population Registry Code) to mobile lines, poses a significant risk to personal data security due to unclear regulatory guidelines. This ambiguity threatens millions of users, particularly those relying on Virtual Mobile Operators that utilize shared infrastructure. Without precise legal definitions determining who is responsible for safeguarding this sensitive information, a critical vacuum in data protection emerges, leaving individuals vulnerable to potential exploitation. Altán Redes, the provider of the shared network, asserts that it lacks the legal authority to store or process end-user identity data, functioning only to activate or deactivate services. However, experts argue that its role as the technical platform transporting voice and data traffic makes it an indirect participant in handling sensitive information. Consequently, establishing clear boundaries of responsibility is essential to define how data flows through the chain of custody, ensuring that all actors, including infrastructure providers, adhere to strict security commitments despite not directly managing user databases. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical intersection between public policy, infrastructure transparency, and individual privacy rights. It underscores that open access to regulatory frameworks and clear data governance protocols are not merely technical issues but fundamental requirements for maintaining public trust. As data becomes more biometric and interconnected, the lack of definitive guidelines in open regulatory discussions can lead to systemic vulnerabilities, emphasizing the need for inclusive, transparent, and robust legal standards in the digital ecosystem.
