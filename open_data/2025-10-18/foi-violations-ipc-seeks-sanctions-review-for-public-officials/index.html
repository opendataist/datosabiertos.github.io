FOI violations: IPC seeks sanctions’ review for public officials

FOI violations: IPC seeks sanctions’ review for public officials

The article advocates for a comprehensive review of Nigeria’s Freedom of Information Act to address persistent non-disclosure by public officials. It argues that current sanctions are insufficient against deliberate information withholding, which undermines democratic transparency. Strengthening penalties and compliance mechanisms is essential to ensure government accountability and protect the public’s right to know, which serves as a fundamental pillar of democratic governance. Reform efforts also target the overhaul of reporting standards to include all government agencies in annual reviews. Currently, many ministries escape scrutiny because their data is omitted from submissions to the National Assembly. Additionally, the proposal includes provisions to guarantee adequate budgeting for capacity building within these entities, ensuring they possess the necessary resources to meet their legal obligations and maintain effective information management systems. This discussion is highly relevant to open data advocates because it highlights the critical need for structural and legal improvements to support data transparency. By pushing for universal application of FOI laws across all government levels and eliminating bureaucratic loopholes, the article supports a more accessible and reliable open data ecosystem. This alignment ensures that data published by public bodies is not only available but also legally protected and consistently maintained, fostering greater trust and utility in public information.
