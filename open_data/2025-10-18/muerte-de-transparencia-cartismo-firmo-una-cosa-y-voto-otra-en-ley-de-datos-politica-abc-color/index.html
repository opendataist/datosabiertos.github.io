Muerte de transparencia: cartismo firmó una cosa y votó otra en ley de datos - Política - ABC Color

Muerte de transparencia: cartismo firmó una cosa y votó otra en ley de datos - Política - ABC Color

The proposed Personal Data Protection Law in Paraguay faces severe criticism for undermining public transparency and access to information. The legislation introduces a problematic mechanism requiring official permission to release public data, a move that fundamentally conflicts with the rights established in existing transparency frameworks. This shift threatens to obscure essential government information, including sworn declarations, effectively prioritizing bureaucratic control over citizen access. Political irregularities have intensified concerns, as numerous legislators from the Honor Colorado party reversed their previously supportive stances to ratify a version of the bill deemed more damaging. Despite signing favorable reports, these lawmakers voted for the most restrictive provisions, raising allegations of bad faith and inconsistency. This sudden reversal suggests coordinated efforts to weaken the law’s safeguards, despite prior consensus on less harmful alternatives that balanced privacy with openness. This situation is critical for the open data community, as it illustrates how well-intentioned privacy laws can be manipulated to restrict the flow of public information. The potential implementation of Article 24 sets a dangerous precedent, where public servants hold unilateral power to deny data disclosure. If enacted, this could dismantle years of progress in digital transparency, forcing civil society to demand executive intervention or legislative veto to preserve fundamental rights to information.
