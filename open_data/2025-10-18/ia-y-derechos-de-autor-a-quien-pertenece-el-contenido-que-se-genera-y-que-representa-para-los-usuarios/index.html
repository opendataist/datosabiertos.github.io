IA y derechos de autor: ¿A quién pertenece el contenido que se genera y qué representa para los usuarios?

IA y derechos de autor: ¿A quién pertenece el contenido que se genera y qué representa para los usuarios?

In Guatemala, the creation of works using artificial intelligence presents a significant legal gap, as current legislation only recognizes natural persons as copyright holders. This legal ambiguity leaves creators without protection, contrasting with other countries where nascent regulatory frameworks are beginning to emerge. The lack of clear rules generates uncertainty regarding authorship and ownership of algorithmically generated content, posing an urgent challenge to adapt the legal system to today’s technological reality. The training of these AI models relies on the massive use of existing information, extracted without authorization, which raises ethical and legal conflicts concerning intellectual property. This practice devalues human labor and has triggered global lawsuits, highlighting the need to establish mechanisms that protect original creativity. Transparency is essential, as the use of third-party protected data without consent violates fundamental copyright principles and undermines trust in digital platforms. This issue is crucial for open data, as it exposes the tensions between reusing information to train technological systems and the rights of original creators. The lack of regulation governing access to and use of open or protected data for commercial purposes puts the sustainability of digital culture at risk. To move forward, a balance must be struck that ensures technological innovation while respecting attribution and consent norms, promoting an ethical digital economy where transparency and human validation are essential pillars.
