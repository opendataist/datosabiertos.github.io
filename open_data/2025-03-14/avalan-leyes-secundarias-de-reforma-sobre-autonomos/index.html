Avalan leyes secundarias de reforma sobre autónomos

Avalan leyes secundarias de reforma sobre autónomos

The reform centralizes transparency and anti-corruption functions under the federal government, merging the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) into the Secretariat of Anti-Corruption. Critics argue that this undermines judicial independence by allowing the state to control its own information requests, effectively acting as both party and judge. This structural change threatens the core principle of open data by weakening independent oversight mechanisms. When the government controls access to its own data, accountability diminishes, making it more difficult for citizens to verify public actions and effectively combat corruption. The approval also favors state-owned energy companies, reflecting a broader consolidation of power. For open data advocates, these moves signal a regression in democratic transparency, emphasizing state control over citizens' rights to information and oversight.
