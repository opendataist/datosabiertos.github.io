Our view: Time for sunshine to disinfect secrecy in Cogswell administration - Charleston City Paper

Our view: Time for sunshine to disinfect secrecy in Cogswell administration - Charleston City Paper

Charleston’s current administration exhibits a troubling trend of withholding information from both the press and the public, prioritizing controlled messaging over genuine transparency. The mayor has repeatedly declined direct interviews with major local newspapers and skipped traditional annual status reports, opting instead for pre-packaged social media content. This avoidance of direct accountability raises significant concerns about open governance, as it prevents citizens and journalists from engaging with critical issues such as public safety and infrastructure through independent scrutiny. This lack of openness is particularly relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates how opacity undermines the fundamental democratic function of public records. When government officials restrict access to information, they hinder the community’s ability to monitor performance and challenge poor policy decisions. Transparency is not merely a bureaucratic preference but a necessary mechanism for ensuring that elected leaders remain answerable to the people who fund their operations and hold them responsible for their actions. Ultimately, the article highlights that sunlight is the most effective tool for preventing governmental missteps. By keeping residents informed, the community can identify and reject detrimental proposals before they are implemented. The narrative serves as a reminder that access to government information is essential for civic health, reinforcing the principle that open data and unrestricted media access are vital for maintaining trust and preventing the success of flawed initiatives.
