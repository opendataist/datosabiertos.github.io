Report reveals who owns Scotland’s land

Report reveals who owns Scotland’s land

The Registers of Scotland’s 2024 report reveals that international ownership, while small in total land area, is concentrated in key urban centers and driven largely by individual buyers from the USA and Hong Kong. This data highlights how global capital flows into Scotland’s real estate market, offering a transparent view of foreign investment patterns that are often opaque in traditional property records. Companies based in Crown dependencies, particularly Jersey, dominate the corporate leasing sector, whereas individuals predominantly hold ownership titles. This distinction underscores the complex legal structures used by international investors, emphasizing the need for clear, accessible registries to trace beneficial ownership and understand the true dynamics of land control. This transparency is vital for open data initiatives, as it enables researchers and policymakers to track cross-border asset flows and assess potential impacts on local housing markets. By making origin data publicly available, the Register supports informed debate on land reform and economic equity, demonstrating how open government data can illuminate hidden ownership structures for public benefit.
