El 37% de los hogares con dependientes no tiene cubiertos los cuidados a domicilio

El 37% de los hogares con dependientes no tiene cubiertos los cuidados a domicilio

The article reveals a persistent gap in access to long-term care, highlighting that a significant portion of households with dependents still lack the necessary home support. This deficit disproportionately affects lower-income families, exposing a stark inequality in how essential care services are distributed across different economic segments. Conversely, child care coverage has expanded, with more children attending centers and many families benefiting from free access. However, this positive trend remains stratified, as children from wealthier households utilize these services at significantly higher rates than those from poorer backgrounds, indicating that socioeconomic status continues to dictate access quality. These disparities underscore the critical relevance of open data for identifying systemic social inequalities. By making statistics on care access, transportation burdens, and discrimination in housing and public spaces transparent, open data empowers researchers and policymakers to design targeted interventions that address the specific needs of vulnerable populations and promote greater social equity.
