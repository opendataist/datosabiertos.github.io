La Iglesia, en contra de bajar la edad de imputabilidad: "Más oportunidades que penas"

La Iglesia, en contra de bajar la edad de imputabilidad: "Más oportunidades que penas"

The Catholic Church’s Episcopal Commission for Social Pastoral has firmly rejected proposals to lower the age of criminal responsibility for minors, arguing that such a punitive measure is an inadequate idealization that fails to address the root causes of youth violence. By emphasizing that minors are not the primary perpetrators of crime statistically, the document challenges the narrative focus on young offenders and urges society to look beyond simple penal adjustments. This stance highlights the importance of prioritizing comprehensive social policies over reactionary legal changes that may appear electorally attractive but lack substantive efficacy. Instead of incarceration, the Church advocates for "social containment and education" as the essential tools for rehabilitation and prevention. The argument posits that access to drugs and the lack of genuine opportunities for youth are key drivers of criminal behavior, necessitating a humane, integral juvenile justice system focused on reinsertion. This perspective underscores the need for experts, such as psychologists and educators, to guide policy discussions, ensuring that solutions are grounded in professional expertise rather than media-driven panic or short-term political gains. This article is relevant to open data because it implicitly critiques policies based on incomplete or misinterpreted information. It calls for decisions driven by serious debate and long-term planning, which relies heavily on the transparent availability and rigorous analysis of statistical data regarding crime demographics and social indicators. By highlighting the discrepancy between public perception and official statistics, the text advocates for evidence-based governance, where open access to reliable data empowers stakeholders to advocate for effective, human-centric public policies rather than ineffective punitive measures.
