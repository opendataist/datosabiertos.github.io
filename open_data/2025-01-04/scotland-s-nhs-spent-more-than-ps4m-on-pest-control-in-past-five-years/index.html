Scotland's NHS spent more than £4m on pest control in past five years

Recent data reveals significant NHS expenditure on pest control, highlighting severe infrastructure decay in Scottish healthcare facilities. This financial burden underscores the urgent need for capital investment to replace aging buildings, ensuring sanitary environments for both patients and staff. Addressing these structural failures is critical to preventing health risks and maintaining public trust in the system. Specific incidents, including rodent infestations and insect outbreaks, demonstrate how crumbling estates directly compromise patient safety and hygiene standards. These problems are not merely cosmetic but represent serious hazards that demand immediate attention. The recurring nature of these issues points to long-term neglect that can no longer be sustained within current operational budgets. This situation is highly relevant to open data as it illustrates the power of transparency in exposing systemic neglect. By making spending and incident reports publicly accessible, citizens can hold officials accountable and advocate for necessary reforms. Open data transforms isolated complaints into a compelling narrative for policy change, driving the political pressure required to secure funding for essential infrastructure upgrades.
