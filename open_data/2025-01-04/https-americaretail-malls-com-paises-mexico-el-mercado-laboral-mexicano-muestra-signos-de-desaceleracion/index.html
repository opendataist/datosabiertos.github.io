The Mexican labor market exhibits a complex duality in which low unemployment masks underlying precarity. High levels of underemployment and persistent informality indicate that many workers lack adequate hours or social security, revealing a disconnect between mere job availability and decent working conditions. Open data enthusiasts must recognize that this statistical landscape demands nuanced interpretation. The absence of comparable data from the previous year due to natural disasters highlights the fragility of longitudinal analysis. This underscores the critical need for robust, transparent, and consistently updated datasets to support accurate economic modeling and policy evaluation. Ultimately, this report emphasizes the importance of accessible, granular data in driving meaningful public policy. By tracking informal sectors and underemployment alongside traditional metrics, stakeholders can better advocate for economic formalization. Reliable open data is essential for exposing these structural challenges and fostering informed debates on improving labor quality and social protection systems.
