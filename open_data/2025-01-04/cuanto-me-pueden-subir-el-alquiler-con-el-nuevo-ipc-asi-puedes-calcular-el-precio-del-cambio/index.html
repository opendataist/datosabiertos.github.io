¿Cuánto me pueden subir el alquiler con el nuevo IPC? Así puedes calcular el precio del cambio

The Spanish government has introduced a new monthly index, published by the National Statistics Institute, to cap annual rent increases for housing contracts signed after May 2023. This measure aims to prevent disproportionate rent hikes by establishing a strict maximum limit that is lower than the general Consumer Price Index. The primary goal is to provide greater stability and affordability for tenants, ensuring that rent adjustments remain controlled and predictable rather than fluctuating wildly with broader inflation trends. This policy creates a clear distinction based on when a lease was signed. Contracts established under the new Housing Law are subject to this stricter reference index, while older agreements generally continue to follow the standard inflation metric as originally agreed. By introducing a digital tool to calculate maximum allowable increases, the administration ensures transparency and helps landlords and tenants easily determine their legal obligations under the current regulatory framework, reducing disputes and confusion regarding annual updates. This development is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how public statistical institutions can provide timely, granular datasets that directly drive policy enforcement and consumer protection. The monthly publication of specific indices allows for real-time application of legal standards, showcasing the power of accessible, machine-readable data in modern governance. It highlights how open statistical data serves not just as a record, but as a functional mechanism for equity, enabling precise implementation of social housing laws and empowering citizens with the information needed to understand their rights.
