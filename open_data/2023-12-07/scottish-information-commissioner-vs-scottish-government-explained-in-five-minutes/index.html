Scottish Information Commissioner vs Scottish Government explained in five minutes

The Scottish Government is appealing a ruling by the Information Commissioner regarding the release of evidence collected during an independent investigation into former First Minister Alex Salmond. The Commissioner determined that the government actually "held" this information because it had established the procedural framework for the inquiry, despite arguments that the evidence belonged to an independent figure. This legal clarification forces the government to re-evaluate its stance on whether it possesses the requested documents, challenging its previous claim that the data was outside its direct control. The controversy stems from a Freedom of Information request by Mr. Benjamin Harrop, who sought all written evidence from the investigation into Nicola Sturgeon’s potential breach of the Ministerial Code. Sturgeon had referred herself to the code following allegations concerning her interactions with Salmond’s former chief of staff and the subsequent investigation's conduct. Although Sturgeon was found not to have breached the code, the transparency of the evidence gathering process remains under scrutiny. The government initially denied holding the records, citing that the independent investigator held them exclusively, a position the Commissioner rejected as technically insufficient. This case is highly relevant to open_data principles as it tests the boundaries of institutional memory and data ownership in public administration. It highlights how technical definitions of "holding" information can be used to obscure transparency, even when an agency designed the data collection process. The outcome will influence how public bodies define their responsibilities under freedom of information laws, ensuring that procedural control implies a duty to disclose. Ultimately, this appeal underscores the critical need for clear, standardized definitions of data retention and access to prevent bureaucratic maneuvering that undermines public accountability and open governance.
