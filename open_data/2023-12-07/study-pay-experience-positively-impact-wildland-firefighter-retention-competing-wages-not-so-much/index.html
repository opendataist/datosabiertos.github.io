Study: Pay, experience positively impact wildland firefighter retention; competing wages not so much

This research highlights a critical trend in federal wildland firefighting workforce retention, offering valuable open data insights into human resource dynamics within high-risk public service sectors. By analyzing detailed workforce metrics, it demonstrates how specific variables directly influence career longevity, providing a model for other industries to study employee stability using similar transparent methodologies. The study reveals that increased workload and cumulative experience significantly boost retention among elite Hotshot crews, while alternative market wages show no impact. This suggests that for these skilled professionals, internal factors like earning potential through higher demand and professional growth outweigh external salary comparisons, contradicting common assumptions about labor market mobility. These findings are relevant to open data because they validate the use of large-scale, longitudinal datasets to identify nuanced behavioral patterns in public sector employment. Policymakers and researchers can leverage such transparent, empirical evidence to design better retention strategies, ensuring that data-driven decisions effectively support workforce sustainability in challenging environmental sectors.
