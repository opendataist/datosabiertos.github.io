Ignorance of FOI Act fueling ridiculous responses in Plateau

The Freedom of Information Act was designed to ensure public accessibility to government records while protecting officials who comply with disclosure requests. However, despite its existence for over a decade, significant gaps remain in its implementation due to widespread ignorance among public officers. Many officials remain unaware of their legal obligations, leading to arbitrary denials of information and bureaucratic hurdles that contradict the Act’s intent to promote transparency and accountability. Recent inquiries into incomplete data from public portals revealed that state institutions frequently ignore FOI requests without valid justification. Responses ranged from citing procedural technicalities, such as missing letterheads, to claiming missing documents or refusing to engage with unknown applicants. These delays and refusals demonstrate a systemic failure to uphold citizens' right to know, driven largely by a lack of understanding rather than malicious intent or legal prohibition. This situation is critically relevant to open data because effective data access requires not just published information, but a culture of responsiveness and compliance. Without proper orientation for public servants, even publicly available portals may contain gaps that remain unaddressed. Sensitizing officials is essential to bridge the gap between policy and practice, ensuring that the legal framework for open government translates into actual, timely access for the public and media alike.
