España crea 209.100 empleos en tercer trimestre 2023 y marca récord con 21,26 millones de ocupados

The Spanish labor market has reached a historic peak in total employment, driven primarily by the services sector and an influx of foreign workers. While quarterly unemployment figures saw a slight increase due to a surge in the active population seeking work, the long-term annual trend demonstrates a substantial net gain in jobs and a reduction in joblessness. This structural improvement highlights the resilience of the economy, characterized by a shift toward stable, indefinite contracts and full-time positions, which effectively reduced temporary employment rates. These dynamics are crucial for open data initiatives as they illustrate the complex interplay between macroeconomic indicators and demographic shifts. Understanding how specific subgroups, such as gender and nationality, experience divergent labor market outcomes allows for more granular and accurate data analysis. The disparity in unemployment rates between men and women, as well as between nationals and foreigners, underscores the necessity of disaggregated datasets to identify systemic inequalities and policy gaps. Furthermore, the concentration of job creation in private services versus public administration reveals evolving economic priorities that require transparent tracking. By monitoring these detailed sectoral and demographic trends, researchers and policymakers can better evaluate the effectiveness of labor interventions. Consequently, this dataset serves as a vital resource for assessing social equity and economic health, proving that accessible, high-quality statistical data is essential for informed decision-making and societal transparency.
