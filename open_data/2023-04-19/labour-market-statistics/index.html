Labour Market Statistics

Northern Ireland’s labor market exhibits a complex recovery trajectory, characterized by significant improvements in employment rates and earnings that have largely surpassed pre-pandemic levels. However, broader metrics such as total hours worked and the proportion of people economically inactive have not yet fully returned to their previous baselines. This divergence suggests that while more individuals are employed and earning more, the overall depth and breadth of labor market participation remain constrained compared to historical norms, indicating a partial rather than complete restoration of pre-crisis economic conditions. Despite the positive trends in general employment, recent data points to emerging volatility, specifically highlighted by a rise in the claimant count and an uptick in confirmed and proposed redundancies. The increase in unemployment claims marks the first growth since late 2022, while redundancy notifications have reached their highest levels since mid-2022. Although these figures remain below long-term historical trends, the simultaneous decrease in economic inactivity and rise in joblessness signals potential instability, suggesting that the labor market is facing new pressures despite the overarching narrative of improvement. This report is relevant to open data initiatives as it exemplifies the critical necessity of integrating multiple statistical sources to form a coherent narrative. Relying solely on the Labour Force Survey might present an overly optimistic view, while focusing exclusively on payroll data could mask emerging risks reflected in claimant counts and redundancy notifications. By making these diverse datasets accessible and interoperable, stakeholders can perform more robust cross-validation, ensuring that policy decisions are based on a holistic understanding of labor market dynamics rather than isolated metrics.
