Se unen expresidentes del Inai para exigir al Senado nombramientos

Ex-commissioners of Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) are pressuring the Senate to appoint the remaining commissioners, whose presence is crucial for restoring the quorum and operational capacity of the agency. This situation jeopardizes the Mexican state’s ability to guarantee access to public information and protect personal data. The current paralysis prevents the institution from fulfilling its constitutional mandate, directly undermining government transparency. It is vital for political parties to reach an agreement promptly to avoid legal gaps that could weaken citizens’ right to request information. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates how institutional instability can block the flow of public data. Without a fully constituted commission, oversight and promotion of data openness are compromised, negatively impacting accountability and citizens’ access to information.
