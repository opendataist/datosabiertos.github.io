No le alcanza para comprar vivienda a 45% de trabajadores saltillenses

The housing market in Coahuila exhibits a severe imbalance where demand vastly outstrips supply, leaving the majority of workers unable to access affordable homes. Data reveals that nearly half of Saltillo’s workforce earns below the minimum income required to qualify for the cheapest social housing units. This discrepancy highlights a critical gap between official valuation tables and actual market realities, rendering formal credit inaccessible for a significant portion of the population despite their employment status. Consequently, the affordability crisis is structural, as even middle-class households struggle to purchase entry-level properties due to rising costs and stagnant wages. The absence of new construction since 2020 has exacerbated the shortage, forcing prices upward and eliminating the price distinction between new and existing homes. This situation demonstrates how policy frameworks and market dynamics can effectively exclude large segments of society from homeownership, creating a rigid barrier to social mobility and housing stability. This issue is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and granular datasets for effective public policy analysis. By aggregating information from census data, employment surveys, and financial records, researchers and citizens can critically evaluate the alignment between official housing metrics and lived economic conditions. Open data enables the identification of such systemic disparities, fostering accountability and driving evidence-based solutions to address the housing deficit and improve social equity.
