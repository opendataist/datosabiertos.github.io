Congreso aprueba reformas para usar coordenadas UTM en delimitación de Eldorado y Juan José Ríos

The Sinaloa Congress approved reforms to define the precise territorial boundaries of the new municipalities of Eldorado and Juan José Ríos using UTM coordinates. This legislative action aims to eliminate legal uncertainty regarding their borders, ensuring that public services and infrastructure reach all communities effectively. By establishing clear demarcations, the state seeks to facilitate the proper operation of these newly municipalized entities ahead of the 2024 elections. To ensure administrative accuracy, the decrees were immediately transmitted to key national institutions, including the electoral institute and the statistical agency. These bodies are tasked with updating their records and cartographic data to reflect the new boundaries. This synchronization allows for the issuance of voter identification cards and the generation of official geographic keys for residents, guaranteeing that citizens are legally recognized within their correct jurisdictions. This case highlights the critical importance of open data in governance, as it demonstrates how standardized geographic information systems underpin democratic processes. Accurate, shared geospatial data enables seamless integration between legislative decisions, electoral administration, and statistical tracking. Ultimately, it ensures that public resources are distributed fairly and that citizen identification aligns with verified territorial realities, showcasing the necessity of interoperable public data standards.
