Longest Running Study Into Open Research Practices Shows Strong Researcher Adoption, Yet Recognition Gaps and Regional Variations Remain | Newswise

The State of Open Data report confirms that open data has evolved from a niche aspiration into an entrenched component of modern research practice. With widespread recognition of FAIR principles and high researcher endorsement for openness, the field has achieved significant maturity. However, the report highlights that technical compliance alone is insufficient; sustainable progress now depends on integrating openness into daily workflows to reduce researcher burden and ensure data quality. Despite this cultural shift, structural barriers persist, particularly regarding professional recognition. Although awareness of data standards has grown substantially, many researchers feel they receive inadequate credit for sharing their work. This discrepancy underscores the critical need for reform in research assessment systems. Aligning institutional rewards with open practices is essential to incentivize data sharing and ensure that the effort required to make data reusable is valued equally with traditional publication outputs. The integration of artificial intelligence represents a pivotal opportunity to streamline these processes. As AI tools become more prevalent in data processing and metadata creation, they offer the potential for seamless, automated, and trusted openness. This technological advancement allows for greater interoperability and efficiency. For the open data community, these findings emphasize that future success relies on coupling technological innovation with supportive policies and infrastructure, ensuring that openness remains practical, equitable, and beneficial for all stakeholders.
