Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions, including lower income and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness, yet remain equally satisfied with life in the country. This paradox highlights a complex reality where material disadvantages do not diminish subjective well-being or national affiliation, challenging assumptions that socioeconomic status solely dictates happiness. The data reveals that despite these challenges, immigrants maintain strong social ties and a distinct sense of belonging to their new home. Furthermore, the report underscores a unique dynamic in social trust. While immigrants exhibit lower interpersonal trust compared to the general population, they display significantly higher confidence in key public institutions such as the political system, judiciary, and police. This elevated institutional trust among a marginalized group offers valuable insights into how civic engagement and social cohesion are maintained across diverse demographic lines in Nordic societies. This article is crucial for open_data initiatives because it demonstrates the power of detailed, disaggregated statistical datasets to reveal nuanced social patterns that aggregate data might obscure. By making such comprehensive survey results accessible, policymakers and researchers can identify specific vulnerabilities and strengths within immigrant communities. This transparency supports evidence-based decision-making and fosters a deeper understanding of social integration, ensuring that open data serves as a tool for equity and improved public services.
