Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions than the native population, struggling with lower income, employment disparities, and substandard housing. Despite these structural inequalities, they report comparable levels of life satisfaction and demonstrate surprisingly high confidence in state institutions like the judiciary and police. This paradox highlights that material disadvantage does not necessarily equate to social dissatisfaction, suggesting that institutional trust plays a vital role in immigrant well-being. However, the data reveals a severe mental health crisis within this demographic, with rates of mental problems and loneliness being double those of the general population. While some groups report higher isolation, others show resilience, indicating that the immigrant experience is not monolithic. The stark contrast between objective hardship and subjective contentment raises critical questions about how well current social support systems address the specific psychological needs of these communities, particularly given the varying impacts across different national origins. This article is highly relevant to open_data because it underscores the necessity of granular, transparent statistical monitoring to uncover hidden inequalities. By making comprehensive survey results publicly available, policymakers can move beyond aggregate averages to identify specific vulnerabilities and cultural nuances. Open data empowers researchers and civil society to advocate for targeted interventions, ensuring that social integration efforts are evidence-based and effectively address the complex intersection of economic status, mental health, and civic trust.
