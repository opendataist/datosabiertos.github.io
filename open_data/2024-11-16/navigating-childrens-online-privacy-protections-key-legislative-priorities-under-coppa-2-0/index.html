Navigating Children’s Online Privacy Protections: Key Legislative Priorities Under COPPA 2.0

The proposed Children and Teens’ Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA 2.0) represents a pivotal shift in digital privacy standards, significantly expanding legal protections for minors. By extending coverage from children under thirteen to include teenagers under seventeen, the legislation forces a reevaluation of how online platforms classify and safeguard user data. This broader scope challenges businesses to adjust their internal assessments, ensuring that compliance efforts no longer rely on the narrow historical definition of childhood but rather encompass the vulnerable developmental stage of adolescence. Central to this regulatory evolution is a stringent mandate for data minimization, requiring companies to collect only information essential for service functionality. This principle aligns with global privacy trends but imposes a unified national standard, compelling organizations to map and restrict data flows across entire enterprises. Additionally, the act restricts targeted advertising without explicit parental consent and grants users the right to erasure, fundamentally altering the economic and operational models of social media and digital platforms. These measures prioritize user autonomy and security over data-driven revenue streams. This development is critical for the open data community as it establishes a new baseline for anonymization and data retention practices. As platforms adapt to these stricter minimization and deletion requirements, the methods used to aggregate, analyze, and share public datasets must evolve to respect these heightened privacy boundaries. Understanding COPPA 2.0 is essential for researchers and advocates working with open data, as it highlights the tension between transparency and individual rights, ensuring that future data practices remain ethically robust and legally compliant in a rapidly changing digital landscape.
