Defiende INAI su trabajo en favor de la transparencia

The INAI commissioner emphasizes that combating corruption requires strengthening cultures of transparency and accountability, urging unity among institutions rather than division. He highlights a critical lack of quorum at INAI due to legislative delays, which has left thousands of cases unresolved and contributes to Mexico's regression in regional anti-corruption indices. This situation underscores the fragility of democratic safeguards when political will fails to support oversight bodies. The commissioner asserts that transparency systems belong to society, not the government, making their proper functioning essential for protecting fundamental rights against institutional inaction. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how opaque governance and administrative paralysis directly hinder data-driven accountability. Without functional, transparent institutions, the public cannot access reliable information, weakening society's capacity to monitor corruption and demand better governance through data.
