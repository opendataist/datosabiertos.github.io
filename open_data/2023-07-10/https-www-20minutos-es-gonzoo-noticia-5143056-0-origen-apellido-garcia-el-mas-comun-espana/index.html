The article highlights that García is the most common surname in Spain, a fact derived from statistical data held by the National Institute of Statistics. This exemplifies the critical role of open government data in democratizing access to demographic information. By making detailed population statistics publicly available, institutions empower citizens to explore their cultural heritage and understand societal patterns without barriers. The text discusses various etymological theories regarding the name’s origins, ranging from Basque roots meaning “bear” to Gothic interpretations. Rather than focusing on definitive answers, it illustrates how public historical records and linguistic research contribute to a shared cultural narrative. This transparency allows individuals to trace their lineages and understand the evolution of naming conventions across different regions and historical periods. Furthermore, the surname’s spread through migration to the Americas demonstrates how historical data reflects broader demographic shifts. The relevance to open data lies in the accessibility of these statistics, which facilitate genealogical research and the exploration of cultural identity. When governments publish raw demographic data, they enable a deeper public understanding of national history and personal ancestry, fostering a more informed and connected society.
