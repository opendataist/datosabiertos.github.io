Comments on AI taking away jobs non-sense: MoS IT Rajeev Chandrasekhar - ET Telecom

Indian Minister of State for Information Technology Rajeev Chandrasekhar dismissed fears that artificial intelligence will eliminate jobs, labeling such concerns as baseless. He argued that current AI systems are primarily task-focused efficiency tools rather than human replacements. This perspective challenges the narrative of widespread technological unemployment, emphasizing that AI augments human capabilities rather than rendering them obsolete. The minister compared these anxieties to past hype cycles, suggesting that worst-case scenarios often distract from the practical benefits of innovation. The government is actively supporting the AI ecosystem through a curated data sets programme, making anonymized government data available to researchers and startups. This initiative aims to foster innovation by providing high-quality resources for developing domestic AI solutions. By facilitating access to critical information, the state seeks to accelerate the growth of homegrown technologies and ensure that Indian developers can compete effectively in the global market. This data-driven approach is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how public sector information can be leveraged for private sector innovation. The minister also highlighted rapid progress in India’s semiconductor industry, noting significant private investment and startup growth in just eighteen months. This trajectory suggests India could establish a competitive semiconductor ecosystem within a few years, potentially outpacing slower international efforts. The broader implication is that strategic data availability and supportive policies are key drivers for transforming industrial capabilities and securing future economic competitiveness.
