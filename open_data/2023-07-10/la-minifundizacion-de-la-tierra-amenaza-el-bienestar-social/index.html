La minifundización de la tierra amenaza el bienestar social

The 2022 Agricultural Census confirms the alarming trend of land concentration in Paraguay, revealing a significant decline in small family farms while minifundia increase to unsustainable levels. This structural shift implies that small-scale agriculture is becoming increasingly marginalized, exacerbating social inequality and weakening the rural economy. The data highlights a critical disconnect between land ownership patterns and the actual capacity for local food production, signaling a deepening crisis for communities dependent on these resources. This consolidation of land ownership has severe implications for food security and national stability, as the reduction in viable family farms leads to greater reliance on food imports and contraband. The lack of effective state policies, including inadequate technical assistance and poor market regulation, further hinders the competitiveness of small producers. Consequently, the deterioration of nutritional health and the failure to support climate adaptation strategies threaten both public well-being and the potential for industrial development, creating a vicious cycle that endangers political stability. This article is highly relevant to open_data because it demonstrates how official statistical records can expose systemic structural failures that are often obscured by political narratives. By making census data accessible and interpretable, citizens and researchers can hold governments accountable and advocate for evidence-based policy reforms. It underscores the necessity of transparent data in identifying disparities in land distribution, thereby empowering civil society to demand equitable resource management and sustainable agricultural practices.
