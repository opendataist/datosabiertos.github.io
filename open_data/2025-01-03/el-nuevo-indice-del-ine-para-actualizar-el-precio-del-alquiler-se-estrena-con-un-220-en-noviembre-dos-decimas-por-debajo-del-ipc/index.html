El nuevo índice del INE para actualizar el precio del alquiler se estrena con un 2,20% en noviembre, dos décimas por debajo del IPC

The Spanish National Statistics Institute (INE) has officially published the first reference index for annual rent updates, establishing a new mechanism to limit rental increases for contracts signed after the implementation of the Housing Law in May 2023. This index, calculated by combining consumer price inflation, underlying inflation, and long-term growth expectations, is capped at the lowest of these three values. By adopting this method, the government aims to prevent disproportionate rent hikes that could exacerbate housing affordability crises, ensuring that adjustments remain modest and predictable for tenants. This regulatory shift marks a significant departure from previous emergency measures that relied on fixed caps or specific competitive guarantees. While earlier contracts may still follow inflation-linked or fixed percentage adjustments depending on their terms, the new framework creates a standardized, transparent ceiling for modern leases. This approach balances economic realities with social protection, acknowledging that unbridled inflationary pressure should not automatically translate into higher housing costs for residents protected by recent legislation. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the critical role of public statistics in enforcing social policy. The reliance on the INE’s monthly publications demonstrates how open, accessible, and timely data becomes a legal benchmark for economic transactions. It underscores the necessity of maintaining high-quality, publicly available datasets that can be reliably integrated into regulatory frameworks, thereby enhancing transparency and trust in both statistical institutions and the housing market.
