Ni Palma ni Calvià: este es el municipio de Mallorca con más extranjeros que nativos

The article analyzes the demographic distribution of foreign-born residents across Mallorca’s municipalities, highlighting a significant disparity driven by economic demand and climate appeal. Areas such as Deià, Calvià, Alcúdia, and Andratx exhibit a majority of international residents, largely because the island’s labor market, particularly in services and construction, struggles to find a local workforce. This influx is further fueled by the region’s desirable weather, attracting immigrants primarily from Central and Northern Europe who settle in these high-demand areas. Conversely, municipalities such as Marratxí and Consell show minimal foreign populations, with the majority of inhabitants either born locally or originating from other parts of the island. This contrast underscores how migration patterns are not uniform but are tightly linked to specific economic opportunities and lifestyle preferences. The data reveals that while some towns are becoming increasingly international hubs, others maintain a predominantly native or internally mobile demographic structure. This report is relevant to open data as it demonstrates the utility of detailed, granular demographic datasets for understanding complex social trends. By providing interactive maps and comprehensive municipal tables, such data allows researchers and policymakers to visualize migration flows, assess labor market impacts, and formulate targeted urban planning strategies. It exemplifies how transparent, accessible statistics can inform decisions regarding housing, infrastructure, and social integration in diverse communities.
