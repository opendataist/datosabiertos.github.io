Crime statistics reveal conflicting trends in the Sligo-Leitrim division, with violent offenses such as sex crimes and assaults rising sharply alongside a dramatic surge in drink driving. Conversely, property-related crimes like shoplifting and fraud have decreased, suggesting a complex shift in local criminal activity rather than a uniform trend toward increased insecurity. The relevance of these figures to open data lies in the Central Statistics Office’s recent decision to resume publishing after suspending releases due to significant data quality issues. The suspension followed an inspectorate report highlighting irregularities in how gardai recorded crimes on their digital systems, indicating that raw administrative data often requires rigorous validation to be trustworthy. This context underscores the critical importance of data integrity in public sector open data initiatives. It demonstrates that releasing granular statistics without addressing underlying recording errors can lead to misleading narratives. For open data advocates, this highlights the necessity of transparency regarding data provenance and quality assurance processes to ensure that published statistics accurately reflect reality and support informed public discourse.
