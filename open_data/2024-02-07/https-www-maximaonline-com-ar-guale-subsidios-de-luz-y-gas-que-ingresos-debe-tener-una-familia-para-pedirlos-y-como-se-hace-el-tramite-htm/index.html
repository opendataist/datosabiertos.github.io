The rapid inflationary evolution has drastically reduced the real value of the income threshold required to access public service subsidies, forcing families previously classified as high-income to now require state support. This adjustment reflects a reality where salaries and incomes have not grown at the same pace as the cost of the basic basket, creating an economic gap that the state seeks to mitigate by recalibrating subsidy categories and applying significant tariff increases only to segments with greater economic capacity. The relevance of this article for open data lies in the transparency needed to understand social segmentation and the allocation of public resources. Clear rules for defining income, asset, and consumption levels are not merely administrative norms, but structured data that determine equity in access to basic services. By making the enrollment process and eligibility criteria public, accountability is facilitated, allowing civil society to audit whether resources are effectively reaching those who need them most, thereby avoiding the exclusion of vulnerable sectors due to bureaucratic barriers or lack of information. To ensure that benefits reach their intended recipients, the government maintains open digital registries, requiring specific documentation to validate each household’s status. This mechanism implies that citizens must interact with government databases to certify their economic and asset situations. The availability of these procedures online and the detailed publication of requirements underscore the importance of accessibility to public information, enabling citizens to verify their inclusion in social protection schemes amid the country’s economic fluctuations.
