FOIA'd Emails Reveal MI AG Nessel Personally Visited Detroit USPS Distribution Center Day Before 2020 Election After Whistleblower Claimed "Thousands of ballots are sitting" Inside Post Office Warehouse

The article presents a narrative constructed from Freedom of Information Act records, alleging that Michigan’s Attorney General coordinated with state election officials to suppress whistleblower claims regarding uncounted ballots in Detroit just before the 2020 presidential election. It suggests that high-ranking state officials intentionally delayed public disclosure of an inspection into the USPS distribution center to avoid influencing the upcoming vote, implying a coordinated effort to conceal potential irregularities during a critical period. Furthermore, the text questions the integrity of the investigation itself, highlighting internal communications where officials expressed skepticism about whether the whistleblower’s claims warranted action or if the inspection would uncover significant issues. The article interprets these emails as evidence of a strategic decision to minimize the visibility of the inquiry, raising concerns about transparency and the handling of electoral complaints by those tasked with ensuring election security and fairness. This content is relevant to open_data discussions as it underscores the critical role of public records in scrutinizing government actions during elections. By analyzing the timing and content of these disclosed emails, the article demonstrates how accessible data can reveal potential discrepancies in official narratives, fostering public trust or skepticism depending on one's interpretation. It serves as a case study for how data transparency mechanisms function—or fail—to hold election administrators accountable to the public.
