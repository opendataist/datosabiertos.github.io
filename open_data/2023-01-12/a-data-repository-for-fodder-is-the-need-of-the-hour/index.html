A data repository for fodder is the need of the hour

Reliable data quality is foundational for valid research and effective policy formulation. While public data has significantly advanced research across disciplines like demography and economics, gaps in specific sectors remain critical. For instance, although agricultural statistics are abundant, fodder data is scarce and fragmented, revealing how data availability directly influences the feasibility and direction of scientific modeling. The scarcity and inconsistency of fodder datasets create significant challenges in understanding livestock support systems. Without disaggregated information on specific forage types and continuous regional tracking, it is impossible to accurately assess green fodder deficits. This lack of granularity leads to widely varying estimates of shortages, rendering current projections unreliable for long-term strategic planning in the agricultural sector. This discrepancy highlights the urgent need for comprehensive, standardized data collection to bridge the gap between policy requirements and available information. Expanding national surveys to include detailed fodder metrics, supported by technical expertise from research institutes, is essential for generating reliable estimates. For open data, this case underscores that meaningful impact depends not just on data existence, but on its granularity, continuity, and accessibility to support precise policy decisions.
