Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical character recognition transforms static image files into searchable, editable digital text, addressing the critical challenge of managing information from traditional print media. This capability is essential for modern businesses seeking to automate workflows and enhance productivity by eliminating manual data entry. By converting physical documents into structured data, organizations can seamlessly integrate scanned content into broader analytical and operational systems. The technology relies on sophisticated software algorithms that analyze visual patterns to identify characters. Through methods like pattern matching and feature extraction, the system distinguishes relevant text from background noise, ensuring accurate conversion even when dealing with varied fonts or document conditions. This technical precision allows for the reliable digitization of complex identity documents, including those with embedded biometric data. This process is highly relevant to open data because it unlocks vast amounts of unstructured information stored in public and private archives. By converting scanned records into machine-readable formats, OCR facilitates greater transparency, accessibility, and reuse of government and corporate datasets. Consequently, it empowers researchers and developers to apply advanced analytics to historical and administrative records, fostering innovation and informed decision-making within the open data ecosystem.
