Stack Overflow survey finds devs hooked on AI, but not totally sold on its judgment

Stack Overflow survey finds devs hooked on AI, but not totally sold on its judgment

Developers integrate AI extensively into their workflows, yet maintain deep skepticism regarding its reliability. Trust is conditional, relying heavily on transparency, source attribution, and the ability to verify outputs before considering them accurate. This cautious stance serves as a vital safeguard against autonomous agents, emphasizing that credibility in technical contexts must be earned rather than assumed. The tension between utility and ethical concerns is evident in the backlash against corporate partnerships, where developers refused to let their contributions train proprietary models. This resistance highlights a growing demand for respect of intellectual property and user rights within the AI ecosystem, forcing platforms to reconsider how they engage with open communities and data sources. This narrative is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of provenance and verifiability in algorithmic systems. As AI becomes more autonomous, ensuring that underlying data sources are transparent and attributable becomes essential for maintaining developer trust and preserving the integrity of open knowledge bases.
