Elecciones 29N: Hasta 444.325 votos en el aire por la ley de nietos

Elecciones 29N: Hasta 444.325 votos en el aire por la ley de nietos

The recent advance of the general elections has highlighted a significant growth in the register of Spanish citizens residing abroad, notably driven by the implementation of the so-called “Grandchildren Law.” This increase not only reflects greater participation from newly naturalized citizens but also underscores the demographic impact of recent reforms that extend voting rights to those who obtain Spanish nationality through this pathway, sparking debate over electoral inclusion and transparency. However, the Supreme Court has provisionally suspended the electoral registration of individuals who acquired nationality through the Ministry of Justice’s procedures, pending the resolution of appeals filed by political groups and associations. This decision aims to ensure the objectivity of the process, while maintaining an exception for descendants of political and sexual exiles, whose voting rights remain unaffected on a provisional basis. The measure separates cases to distinguish between new applications and already-completed naturalizations, thereby avoiding mass cancellations while regulating their immediate impact at the polls. This case is crucial for the open data movement, as it illustrates the critical need for access to clear, auditable public electoral information. Transparency in registration criteria and the availability of census data enable civil society to verify democratic integrity and detect potential irregularities. By facilitating public scrutiny of these records, institutional trust is strengthened, and governance based on accountability and the openness of government data is promoted—elements essential for a modern, vigilant democracy.
