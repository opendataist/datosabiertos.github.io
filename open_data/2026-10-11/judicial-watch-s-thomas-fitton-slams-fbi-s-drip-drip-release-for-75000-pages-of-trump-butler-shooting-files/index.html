Judicial Watch's Thomas Fitton slams FBI's 'drip-drip' release for 75,000 pages of Trump-Butler shooting files

Judicial Watch's Thomas Fitton slams FBI's 'drip-drip' release for 75,000 pages of Trump-Butler shooting files

Judicial Watch has criticized the FBI for an excessively slow release of documents related to the attempted assassination of Donald Trump, warning that current processing rates could delay transparency for over a decade. This protracted timeline prevents public scrutiny and challenges to withheld information, raising concerns that the government is obscuring details rather than fostering open inquiry into a major national security event. The controversy centers on the redaction of communications between the shooter and an unidentified individual, which critics argue creates an appearance of a cover-up. Despite claims of privacy protection, watchdog groups assert that the public’s right to understand how a former president was nearly killed outweighs the privacy interests of those who may have interacted with the perpetrator. This tension highlights the ongoing struggle to balance individual privacy with the imperative for investigative transparency in high-profile cases. This situation is highly relevant to the open data movement, as it exemplifies how bureaucratic inefficiencies and rigid interpretations of privacy can obstruct access to public records. It underscores the critical need for accelerated FOIA processing and clear standards that prioritize the public interest in understanding government actions, particularly when life-threatening events involve federal agencies. Ensuring timely access to such data is essential for maintaining accountability and trust in democratic institutions.
