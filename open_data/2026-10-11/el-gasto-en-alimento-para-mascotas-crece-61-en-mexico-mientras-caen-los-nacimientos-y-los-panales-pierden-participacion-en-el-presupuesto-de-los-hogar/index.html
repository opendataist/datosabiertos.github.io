El gasto en alimento para mascotas crece 61% en México mientras caen los nacimientos y los pañales pierden participación en el presupuesto de los hogares, según datos del INEGI

The analysis reveals a significant transformation in the structure of household spending in Mexico, characterized by a substantial increase in investment in pet food alongside a decline in expenditures linked to traditional child-rearing. This budgetary reallocation reflects profound changes in consumption habits, where products such as diapers are losing relevance while pet care gains priority in the household. These economic shifts are intrinsically linked to the country’s demographic evolution, marked by a notable decline in birth rates and a progressive delay in the average age at marriage. The trend indicates a consolidation of new family composition models, in which the decision to have children is postponed or partially replaced by companionship with pets, influenced by socioeconomic factors such as the cost of living and education. This information is relevant to the open data field, as it demonstrates how public statistics can reveal complex correlations between social indicators and consumption behaviors. By integrating data from income surveys and civil registry records, it becomes easier to understand emerging social dynamics, enabling researchers, policymakers, and markets to explore demographic patterns with greater precision and analytical depth.
