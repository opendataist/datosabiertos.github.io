Harassment by FOIA | ScienceBlogs

The article argues that FOIA requests targeting scientists often stem from bad faith rather than genuine transparency needs. It concludes that Vani Hari’s demand for Kevin Folta’s emails is a strategic distraction designed to harass critics and protect her commercial interests, rather than to uncover hidden conspiracies. This highlights how public records mechanisms can be weaponized by influencers to silence scientific dissent without addressing the underlying factual disputes. This dynamic is critical to open data advocates because it demonstrates how transparency tools can be misused for intimidation instead of accountability. The author illustrates that such requests typically yield trivial results, revealing more about the requester’s vanity than any substantive misconduct. This warns open data communities against assuming all FOIA activity serves the public good, as some may merely aim to disrupt legitimate scientific communication. Ultimately, the piece underscores the importance of distinguishing between legitimate oversight and frivolous legal harassment. It suggests that open data initiatives must remain vigilant against efforts that exploit transparency laws to shield pseudoscience from scrutiny. By exposing these tactics, the article reinforces the need for robust protections for researchers and clarifies how open data should be ethically applied to serve truth rather than corporate profit or personal vendettas.
