NI Business Demography Statistics 2022

Northern Ireland’s business demography statistics reveal a significant slowdown in entrepreneurial activity, characterized by a sharp decline in new business registrations and a concurrent rise in closures. The business birth rate fell to its lowest level since 2014, while the death rate increased, narrowing the gap between starts and exits. This trend mirrors broader patterns across the United Kingdom, indicating that regional economic dynamics are heavily influenced by wider national shifts and lingering post-pandemic adjustments rather than isolated local factors. The data highlights substantial variation across industries and geographic areas, with transport and storage seeing the most dramatic drops in new ventures, while construction experienced the highest volume of business deaths. These fluctuations underscore the volatility inherent in specific sectors, particularly those most sensitive to external shocks like supply chain disruptions or changing consumer behaviors. The disparity in performance among different district councils further illustrates how local economic conditions can diverge significantly even within a single jurisdiction, complicating uniform policy interventions. This article is highly relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates the value of transparent, granular statistical reporting for evidence-based policymaking. By making detailed records from the Inter-Departmental Business Register publicly available, statistical agencies enable researchers, journalists, and the public to scrutinize economic health independently. This transparency fosters accountability and allows stakeholders to identify emerging trends, such as sector-specific fragilities or regional inequalities, ensuring that government responses are grounded in accurate, accessible information rather than opaque estimates.
