Domenicali, sobre Márquez: “Que gane el mejor”

The article highlights how Marc Márquez’s move to the Gresini Ducati team reshapes MotoGP dynamics through unprecedented data sharing. Ducati’s CEO confirms that all rider telemetry is openly accessible to the factory, allowing Márquez to study Bagnaia’s techniques and vice versa. This transparency challenges traditional racing hierarchies, prioritizing collective technological improvement over isolated team secrets. This open-data philosophy enables Márquez to adapt quickly to a championship-winning machine, despite acknowledging he cannot instantly match the reigning champion’s level. By leveraging insights from official riders, the eight-time world champion aims to recover his competitive edge. Ducati benefits significantly by analyzing Márquez’s unique riding style to refine their motorcycle’s performance across their entire portfolio. This development is highly relevant to open data, as it demonstrates how shared information ecosystems can drive innovation and fairness in competitive sports. Breaking down data silos between affiliated teams fosters a collaborative environment where transparency enhances both individual performance and product evolution. It illustrates the practical value of accessible data in optimizing complex mechanical systems and leveling the playing field, proving that openness can be a strategic advantage rather than a vulnerability.
