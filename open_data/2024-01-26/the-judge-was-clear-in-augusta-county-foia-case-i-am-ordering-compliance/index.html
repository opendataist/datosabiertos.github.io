The judge was clear in Augusta County FOIA case: ‘I am ordering compliance’

The article highlights a critical conflict between judicial authority and local government transparency, centering on Augusta County’s refusal to comply with a clear court order releasing recordings of an illegal closed meeting. Supervisor Gerald Garber’s claim that the directive was merely an opinion rather than a binding order illustrates a dangerous precedent where elected officials selectively interpret legal mandates to maintain secrecy. This disregard undermines the rule of law and erodes public trust in local governance. The core legal dispute underscores that records transferred to a government entity become public property, regardless of their original intent or creator. By denying Freedom of Information Act requests for these recordings, the county argued they were private, but the circuit court correctly ruled that possession by the county triggers public record status. This distinction is vital for ensuring that officials cannot circumvent transparency laws by framing private actions as personal matters, thereby safeguarding the integrity of public decision-making processes. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how legal frameworks must be strictly enforced to prevent administrative obstruction. When local entities ignore court orders regarding information release, it creates significant barriers to accessing essential civic data. Ensuring compliance with such rulings is fundamental to maintaining an open society, where citizens have unimpeded access to the records of government actions and where transparency is not subject to the whims of individual officials.
