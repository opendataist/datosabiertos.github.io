La importancia del gobierno abierto (I)

La importancia del gobierno abierto (I)

The article examines the transition from traditional state governance to contemporary “open government” frameworks. It adopts a broad definition of government, highlighting decentralized entities such as municipal corporations in Guatemala that operate on the principles of solidarity and citizen participation. This shift underscores that guaranteeing transparency and the right to information are essential for democracy, marking a move away from bureaucratic opacity toward models that encourage public scrutiny and accountability. Guatemala’s implementation of the Law on Access to Public Information, in effect since 2008, serves as a key example of this transformation. This legislation establishes a legal framework that guarantees citizens’ right to request public data without discrimination. The text argues that such laws are fundamental to fostering a new culture of openness, thereby replacing outdated paradigms in which public affairs were alien to the general population. This legal foundation supports accountability mechanisms and helps combat corruption by requiring public services to adopt more responsive practices. The relevance to open data lies in the integration of these legal mandates with technological solutions, particularly e-government. By leveraging digital infrastructure to deliver public services, governments can effectively implement open data principles. The article emphasizes that technology is not merely a tool for efficiency but a means to operationalize transparency and participation. Ultimately, combining legislative rights to information with digital access empowers citizens, creating a more inclusive and transparent democratic environment in which open data serves as the bridge between state institutions and society.
