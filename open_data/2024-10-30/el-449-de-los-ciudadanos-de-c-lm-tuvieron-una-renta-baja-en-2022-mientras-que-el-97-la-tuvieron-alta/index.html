El 44,9% de los ciudadanos de C-LM tuvieron una renta baja en 2022 mientras que el 9,7% la tuvieron alta

The article analyzes income distribution across Spanish municipalities, revealing significant geographical disparities that highlight the urgent need for granular, accessible public data. It demonstrates that wealth is not evenly distributed but is instead concentrated in specific regions and population centers, with the Basque Country and Madrid showing high averages, while Andalusia and Extremadura lag behind. This stark contrast underscores how open data can serve as a critical tool for identifying structural inequalities and informing equitable regional policies. A key finding is the unexpected correlation between population size and wealth: numerous small municipalities with fewer than 2,000 inhabitants rank among the richest in the country, while some larger towns remain in the lowest income brackets. This challenges simplistic assumptions about urban economic density and suggests that local economic drivers vary widely regardless of settlement size. Such nuanced insights are vital for policymakers and researchers who require detailed datasets to understand the true drivers of local prosperity beyond aggregate national figures. Ultimately, this data is highly relevant to the open data community because it exemplifies the value of transparent, location-based statistics. By providing detailed breakdowns of income by municipality and census section, official statistics enable citizens and civil society to hold governments accountable and advocate for targeted interventions. The ability to visualize and interrogate this data fosters greater civic engagement and ensures that resource allocation addresses the specific needs of both affluent and disadvantaged areas effectively.
