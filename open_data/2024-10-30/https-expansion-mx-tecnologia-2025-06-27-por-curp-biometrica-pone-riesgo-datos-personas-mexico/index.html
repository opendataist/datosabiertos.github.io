The proposed legislative reforms aim to implement a mandatory biometric CURP and expand the National Registry, effectively establishing a massive surveillance system involving the military. This shift reverses previous Supreme Court precedents by centralizing sensitive personal data, including fingerprints and facial images, under government control without adequate transparency or accountability mechanisms. Critics highlight severe implications for digital rights and public security. Centralizing biometric data creates a single point of failure susceptible to cyberattacks, while the non-renewable nature of biometric identifiers exacerbates these risks. Furthermore, the mandatory nature of the system threatens social exclusion, potentially denying essential services to vulnerable populations who opt out or face registration barriers, echoing negative precedents observed in similar international identification frameworks. This issue is critical for the open data community as it demonstrates how poor data governance can undermine fundamental human rights. It underscores the necessity of transparent, secure, and consent-based data handling practices. The debate serves as a stark warning against the unregulated expansion of state surveillance powers, emphasizing the need for robust legal safeguards that prioritize privacy and prevent the weaponization of citizen data for mass monitoring.
