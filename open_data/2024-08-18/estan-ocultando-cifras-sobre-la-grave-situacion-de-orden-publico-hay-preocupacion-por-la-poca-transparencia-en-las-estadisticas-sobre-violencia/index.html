¿Están ocultando cifras sobre la grave situación de orden público? Hay preocupación por la poca transparencia en las estadísticas sobre violencia

¿Están ocultando cifras sobre la grave situación de orden público? Hay preocupación por la poca transparencia en las estadísticas sobre violencia

Cerac suspended the publication of its reports on the ceasefire with the ELN after facing intimidation and public harassment from the armed group. This decision illustrates how direct pressure on independent researchers can silence the objective verification of peace agreements, leaving society without reliable mechanisms to assess compliance with humanitarian norms and the conduct of armed actors during negotiations. Additionally, there is a systemic deterioration in access to official security and crime data in Colombia. State institutions have restricted access to geolocated information and reporting methodologies, which hinders in-depth analysis of criminal trends and makes it difficult for local authorities to design effective public policies. The lack of institutional transparency erodes public trust in official statistics and limits the capacity to respond to violence. This article is crucial for open data because it exposes how opacity and censorship, whether induced by violence or administrative decisions, destroy the data infrastructure necessary for accountability. Without open, verified, and accessible data, civil society and international organizations cannot effectively monitor peace and security, violating the fundamental right to information and weakening democratic processes.
