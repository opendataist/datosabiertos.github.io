Gobierno promete cobertura total del programa “Hambre cero” en 2025 - Nacionales - ABC Color

Gobierno promete cobertura total del programa “Hambre cero” en 2025 - Nacionales - ABC Color

The article outlines the phased implementation of Paraguay’s “Hambre Cero” school feeding program, prioritizing the most impoverished districts based on national statistical data. By initially targeting the neediest areas, the government aims to address extreme hunger where it is most critical, with plans to expand coverage to 100% of public schools next year. This strategy highlights the practical challenge of resource allocation in social policies, demonstrating how open data on poverty levels directly informs equitable distribution frameworks and ensures that aid reaches the most vulnerable populations first. Despite these efforts, the program faces significant operational challenges, including widespread complaints about food quality, insufficient quantities, and logistical gaps such as missing kitchens. The government acknowledges these issues as historical problems that require time and systemic improvement rather than immediate fixes. This situation underscores the necessity of transparent reporting mechanisms and accessible complaint channels, allowing citizens to monitor service delivery and hold authorities accountable for maintaining standards in public welfare initiatives. Furthermore, administrative bottlenecks, such as delayed parental authorization, have resulted in food shortages for some students, revealing gaps in data collection and communication between ministries and families. The relevance to open data lies in the need for integrated, real-time information systems that synchronize enrollment, consent, and supply logistics. Improving data interoperability is essential to prevent exclusions, ensure accurate resource planning, and ultimately guarantee that no child is left without adequate nutrition during the school year.
