Banco Central promete publicar actas de Junta Directiva en línea tras cuestionamientos

Banco Central promete publicar actas de Junta Directiva en línea tras cuestionamientos

The Central Bank of Costa Rica is undertaking a comprehensive redesign of its digital platform to improve data accessibility and transparency, responding to audit criticisms regarding the non-disclosure of internal findings. By shifting toward open data formats and committing to publish full board minutes, the institution aims to align its operations with constitutional mandates that guarantee citizens’ free access to public information. This structural change seeks to resolve significant gaps in current transparency practices and facilitate easier public scrutiny of the bank’s activities. The audit revealed serious deficiencies, including the unjustified classification of information as confidential and substantial delays in finalizing official records. These lapses undermined accountability and hindered the public’s ability to monitor administrative decisions effectively. Consequently, the bank has prioritized correcting these procedural flaws to ensure that all documentation, including governance structures and financial reports, is readily available without unnecessary barriers or legal ambiguities. This development is crucial for open data advocacy, as it demonstrates how institutional pressure can drive tangible improvements in government transparency. It highlights the importance of standardizing data releases into accessible, machine-readable formats that empower citizens and researchers. By addressing specific audit findings and committing to broader digital reforms, the Central Bank sets a precedent for how financial institutions can enhance civic engagement through responsible data management and accountability.
