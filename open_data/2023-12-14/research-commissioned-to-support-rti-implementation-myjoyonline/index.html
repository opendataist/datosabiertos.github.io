Research commissioned to support RTI implementation - MyJoyOnline

Ghana’s Ministry of Information has commissioned a comprehensive study to strengthen the implementation of its Right to Information Act. This research highlights persistent hurdles such as resource shortages, institutional cultural barriers, and legislative delays. These insights reveal the complex reality behind the law's passage, offering a critical diagnostic tool for policymakers seeking to overcome systemic obstacles. The initiative underscores the vital role of collaboration among government entities, civil society, and citizens in achieving genuine transparency. Stakeholders emphasize that committed leadership and structured approaches are essential for sustaining the framework established in 2019. This collective effort aims to translate legal provisions into tangible public benefits, ensuring that access to information becomes a practical reality rather than just a statutory right. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates as it demonstrates how empirical research can drive effective governance. By identifying specific implementation challenges, the study provides a roadmap for creating more robust, accessible, and accountable information systems. It reinforces the principle that successful open data ecosystems require not just legal frameworks, but continuous institutional adaptation and cross-sector cooperation.
