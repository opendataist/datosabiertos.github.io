The U.S. death row population continues to decline significantly, marking the lowest numbers seen in decades. This trend reflects a sustained reduction in the number of individuals held under capital sentences across state and federal jurisdictions since the peak at the turn of the millennium. Concurrently, the average time prisoners spend on death row before their cases conclude is increasing. This prolonged incarceration period highlights systemic delays within the criminal justice process, contributing to an aging demographic among those awaiting final resolution or execution. This data is crucial for open_data initiatives aimed at scrutinizing criminal justice outcomes. Transparent access to such statistical trends enables researchers and advocates to analyze the effectiveness of legal reforms, monitor equity in sentencing, and advocate for policy changes based on empirical evidence rather than speculation.
