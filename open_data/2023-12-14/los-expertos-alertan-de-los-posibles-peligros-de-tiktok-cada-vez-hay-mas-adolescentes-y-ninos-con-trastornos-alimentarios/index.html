Los expertos alertan de los posibles peligros de TikTok: cada vez hay más adolescentes y niños con trastornos alimentarios

The early introduction of smartphones to children is causing severe, early-onset psychological and behavioral disorders, particularly among girls. Experts warn that this premature access leads to critical issues such as eating disorders appearing years before previous norms, alongside premature hypersexualization and exposure to harmful content. This shift highlights a societal failure to protect childhood development, replacing healthy socialization with digital dependencies that adults often enable without understanding the long-term consequences. The nature of short-form video platforms creates a continuous stream of visual stimuli that distorts perceptions of beauty and sexuality, fostering unrealistic standards and hypersexualization at an alarming age. Simultaneously, constant interruptions from devices impair cognitive functions, specifically the ability to concentrate and engage with text-based learning. This results in significant educational challenges, as students accustomed to rapid visual feeds struggle with the sustained attention required for academic success, contributing directly to increased school failure rates. This article is relevant to open data because the alarming trends in pediatric mental health and educational outcomes rely on systematic observation and data collection to be recognized and addressed. Open, accessible health and educational statistics can empower policymakers and communities to identify these correlations early. By making such data transparent, society can better understand the impact of digital technology on development, fostering informed public debate and encouraging responsible parenting practices and regulatory measures to mitigate these harms.
