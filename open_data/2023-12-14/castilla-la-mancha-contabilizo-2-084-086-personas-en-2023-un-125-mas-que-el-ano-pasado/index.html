Castilla-La Mancha contabilizó 2.084.086 personas en 2023, un 1,25% más que el año pasado

The article highlights Spain’s first annual census based exclusively on administrative registers, marking a pivotal shift from traditional decennial surveys to more frequent, precise data collection. This new methodology eliminates the need for household surveys by relying entirely on existing public records, ensuring that future population statistics are updated annually with granular territorial detail. This transition addresses previous limitations in data latency and accuracy, offering a robust framework for real-time demographic analysis. The demographic landscape reveals a significant national population increase driven primarily by foreign residents, while the domestic population structure shows a growing proportion of elderly citizens and a decline in youth. This data underscores deep societal shifts, including aging and migration patterns, which are critical for policy-making. The regional analysis further indicates uneven growth, with certain coastal cities expanding rapidly while others face stagnation, reflecting broader economic and social trends across the country. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it establishes a precedent for utilizing administrative data to create public, high-frequency statistics. By making detailed, yearly censuses freely available without intrusive surveys, the National Statistics Institute sets a benchmark for transparency and efficiency in public information. It empowers researchers, developers, and policymakers to access timely, reliable datasets that can inform decisions on urban planning, social services, and economic strategies, thereby enhancing the utility and responsiveness of public data ecosystems.
