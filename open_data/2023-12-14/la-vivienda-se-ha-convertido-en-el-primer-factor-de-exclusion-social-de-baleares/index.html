La vivienda se ha convertido en el primer factor de exclusión social de Baleares

The report by the Observatory of Social Balears highlights a critical contradiction in housing policy: treating property as a market commodity rather than a fundamental human right. This perspective has driven a shift toward rental markets and speculative real estate practices, significantly increasing prices and excluding vulnerable populations. The study argues that this market-driven approach exacerbates social vulnerability, as seen in the disproportionate growth of real estate businesses and the prioritization of profit over social needs. Baleares exhibits extreme trends, including high rental costs, a surge in small luxury units for foreign tourists, and a leading share of evictions and substandard housing. These indicators demonstrate that the current model fails to provide adequate shelter for residents. The concentration of foreign investment and the dominance of short-term rentals further distort local housing availability, creating a dual system that benefits investors while marginalizing local communities and increasing residential exclusion. This analysis is vital to open data discussions because it illustrates how transparent, standardized data can expose systemic inequalities and challenge prevailing economic narratives. By making housing metrics visible, open data empowers civil society to advocate for policy changes. The findings underscore the necessity of non-profit, social-oriented housing strategies, showing that data-driven evidence is essential for shifting governance from market regulation to the protection of fundamental rights.
