Workplace fatality rates have declined significantly, signaling improved safety conditions in the labor market. This trend suggests that current regulations and preventive measures are effectively reducing severe incidents. However, accidents occurring during commutes have increased, indicating a growing risk during travel that requires targeted attention and separate analysis from workplace safety protocols. The data reveals distinct patterns between employees and self-employed workers, with a notable decrease in deaths among both groups. This differentiation is crucial for understanding how different employment structures face varying levels of risk. By separating these categories, policymakers can better tailor interventions to the specific vulnerabilities of each group rather than applying a one-size-fits-all approach. This information is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how accessible government statistics allow for detailed socioeconomic analysis. Transparent data enables researchers and the public to verify safety trends, hold institutions accountable, and drive evidence-based policy improvements. Open access to such granular metrics fosters greater trust in public administration and empowers stakeholders to contribute to safer working environments through informed debate.
