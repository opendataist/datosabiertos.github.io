Balears pierde más de 2.300 viviendas turísticas en dos años

The article reveals a national trend in which the total number of tourist accommodations increased over a two-year period, while specific autonomous communities such as Madrid and Catalonia maintained stable figures. This highlights a divergent landscape in Spain’s tourism housing market, where growth is not uniform across regions. Understanding these regional disparities is crucial for open data initiatives aiming to analyze the nuanced dynamics between urban tourism and residential needs, enabling researchers to pinpoint where regulatory pressures are most acute. The Balearic Islands dominate the landscape, holding the highest proportion of tourist dwellings relative to total census housing, significantly above the national average. This concentration implies intense pressure on local housing stocks and underscores the importance of transparent, granular data in assessing the impact of short-term rentals on community stability. Such detailed geographic breakdowns enable better policy evaluation and help stakeholders monitor saturation levels in specific municipalities versus broader regional trends. At the municipal level, Pollença emerges as the leader in tourist housing volume, illustrating how specific locales can become focal points for rental activity. This granularity supports open data principles by making localized impacts visible to the public and policymakers. The availability of such detailed datasets fosters accountability, allowing communities to scrutinize how tourism infrastructure evolves and to advocate for balanced urban planning that considers both economic benefits and social sustainability.
