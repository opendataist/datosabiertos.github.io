Cuántas armas circulan en Entre Ríos: datos públicos, aunque no tanto | Análisis

The article highlights a significant transparency failure by Argentina’s National Agency for Controlled Materials (ANMAC) regarding firearm registration data in Entre Ríos. Despite formal requests for information on the number of legal firearms and annual permit trends, the agency withheld these details. This lack of official disclosure contradicts public commitments to transparency and obstructs evidence-based policy-making, illustrating how essential government-held datasets are for validating claims and planning effective public safety strategies. While provincial authorities provided partial insights into weapon preferences and recent application volumes, the absence of comprehensive, centralized figures from the national level leaves crucial gaps. Activists emphasize that without accurate, publicly available statistics on circulating firearms, discussions on security remain speculative. This underscores the critical importance of open data in public administration: reliable metrics are necessary to evaluate the efficacy of disarmament programs and to understand the true scope of the problem, rather than relying on anecdotal evidence or incomplete local reports. This situation is highly relevant to the open data community as it demonstrates the real-world consequences of data opacity. When essential public records are withheld, it hampers accountability, research, and civic engagement. The case serves as a compelling argument for the necessity of robust access to information frameworks, showing that the availability of transparent, granular data is not merely a technical preference but a fundamental requirement for democratic oversight and effective governance in sensitive areas like national security.
