The Supreme Court’s declaration of the unconstitutionality of the electoral reform marks a significant victory for the protection of personal data and access to information. This decision validates the concerns raised by the National Institute of Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI), which identified that specific provisions of the reform threatened fundamental rights established in the Mexican Constitution. The invalidated decree contained mechanisms that dangerously exposed sensitive information. By allowing unrestricted information flow among public bodies and political parties, including classified data, and by enabling foreign residents to register on the electoral roll without strict identity verification, the law compromised data security and privacy. These changes undermined legal certainty and the principles of purpose limitation and lawfulness, which are essential for the responsible handling of citizens’ data. This outcome is crucial for open data because it reinforces the authority of the INAI as the sole body capable of enforcing data protection standards. It ensures that the management of political data remains subject to rigorous oversight, preventing unauthorized use and maintaining the integrity of public records. Ultimately, the ruling upholds the balance necessary for transparent governance while safeguarding individual privacy against potential abuses by political entities.
