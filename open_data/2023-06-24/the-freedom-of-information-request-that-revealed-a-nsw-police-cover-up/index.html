The freedom of information request that revealed a NSW Police cover-up

The article highlights a critical failure in transparency by NSW Police, who deliberately obscured the fact that a Taser was used on a dementia-stricken elderly woman. Initial internal communications revealed that executives actively edited a media release to remove this key detail, contradicting their stated commitment to honesty. This incident underscores the urgent need for government agencies to provide accurate, unvarnished information to the public, especially during critical incidents involving vulnerable citizens. Journalists played an indispensable role in exposing this discrepancy by utilizing freedom of information requests to access internal emails. Their investigation revealed that the original draft included the Taser usage, but it was subsequently removed to downplay the severity of the event. This demonstrates how investigative journalism serves as a vital check on power, ensuring that attempts to mislead or sanitize official narratives are brought to light. This case is highly relevant to open data principles because it illustrates the consequences of withholding information. Open data advocates for the proactive release of complete and accurate information rather than selective or manipulated records. When authorities obscure facts, they erode public trust and hinder accountability. True transparency requires not just releasing data, but ensuring that the data reflects the full reality of events, enabling citizens to understand how their government operates.
