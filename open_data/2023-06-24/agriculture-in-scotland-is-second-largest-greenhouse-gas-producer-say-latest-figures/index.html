Agriculture in Scotland is second largest greenhouse gas producer say latest figures

Scotland has missed its latest emissions reduction target, marking a fourth failure out of the last five years. Although emissions are significantly lower than 1990 levels, the recent rise highlights the challenges in maintaining consistent progress toward net-zero goals. This trajectory underscores the urgent need for more decisive government action and substantial investment to accelerate decarbonization efforts across all sectors. The increase in emissions is primarily driven by a post-lockdown rebound in domestic transport and residential sectors, while agricultural emissions show modest rises due to updated calculation methodologies. NFU Scotland welcomes these methodological changes, arguing that accurate baselines are essential for measuring genuine progress. The sector emphasizes that farmers are already implementing sustainable practices, such as increasing carbon sequestration and biodiversity, but require greater resource access and policy support to scale these solutions effectively. This situation is highly relevant to open_data because it demonstrates the critical role of transparent, accurate, and accessible emissions data in holding governments accountable. Reliable metrics allow stakeholders to assess policy effectiveness, identify specific sectoral trends, and demand evidence-based interventions. Without open, rigorous data transparency, it becomes impossible to verify claims, track real-world impact, or ensure that public resources are directed toward effective climate mitigation strategies.
