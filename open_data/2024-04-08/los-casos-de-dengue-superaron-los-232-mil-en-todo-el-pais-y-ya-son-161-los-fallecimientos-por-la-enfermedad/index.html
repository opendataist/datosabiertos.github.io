Los casos de dengue superaron los 232 mil en todo el país y ya son 161 los fallecimientos por la enfermedad

Argentina has experienced a historic dengue outbreak during the 2023–2024 season, far surpassing the record figures of previous years. The disease has spread locally across most of the country, characterized by an unusually early seasonal onset and persistent circulation throughout the entire analyzed period. This epidemiological scenario demands a coordinated and urgent response to the viral spread across multiple jurisdictions. The report highlights the magnitude of the health impact, with thousands of confirmed cases and a significant number of severe hospitalizations and deaths. The territorial distribution shows that the northeastern, northwestern, and central regions are the most affected, while other areas exhibit lower incidence rates. This geographic variability underscores the need to tailor containment strategies according to local conditions and the intensity of viral circulation. This article is relevant to the open data movement because it illustrates the critical importance of transparency in public health. The regular publication of detailed epidemiological bulletins enables citizens, researchers, and civil society to access vital information for understanding the evolution of the epidemic. Such data foster government accountability, facilitate independent analysis by third parties, and empower the population to make informed decisions—key elements for evidence-based democratic governance.
