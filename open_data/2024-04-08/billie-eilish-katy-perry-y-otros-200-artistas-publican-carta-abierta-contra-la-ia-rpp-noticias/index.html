Billie Eilish, Katy Perry y otros 200 artistas publican carta abierta contra la IA | RPP Noticias

Billie Eilish, Katy Perry y otros 200 artistas publican carta abierta contra la IA | RPP Noticias

Artists and major music publishers are demanding that AI developers stop using copyrighted works without permission, arguing that such practices devalue human creativity and infringe upon intellectual property rights. They warn against the unethical cloning of voices, which threatens to replace human artistry with synthetic alternatives, thereby undermining the integrity and compensation structures of the musical ecosystem. This conflict highlights critical transparency issues regarding the sources of training data in generative AI. The controversy is relevant to open data because it underscores the urgent need for clear, accessible records of dataset origins. Without standardized open data protocols for copyright clearance, the AI industry risks legal challenges that could destabilize data-sharing practices and harm creative industries. The collective action demonstrates how stakeholders are resisting opaque data mining. It emphasizes that true openness must include respect for creators’ rights and fair attribution. This sets a precedent for how open data initiatives must evolve to balance innovation with ethical standards, ensuring that public data resources do not come at the expense of individual artistic ownership and economic sustainability.
