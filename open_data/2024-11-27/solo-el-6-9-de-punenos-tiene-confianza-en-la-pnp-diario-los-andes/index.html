Solo el 6.9 % de puneños tiene confianza en la PNP - Diario los Andes

Solo el 6.9 % de puneños tiene confianza en la PNP - Diario los Andes

A widespread crisis of confidence in Peru’s National Police underscores the urgent need for transparent, accessible data to restore institutional trust. The article highlights that significant portions of the population, particularly in regions like Puno, view corruption and crime as primary threats. This systemic distrust stems from a tangible disconnect between citizens and security forces, driven by escalating insecurity and a perceived inability of the state to guarantee safety. The narrative reveals that victimization rates have risen sharply over recent years, with specific urban centers showing alarming trends. Citizens feel increasingly unsafe, a sentiment exacerbated by high-profile failures in police responsiveness and procedural delays. These inefficiencies not only fail to prevent crime but also deepen the public’s skepticism regarding the institution’s capacity to protect and serve effectively, creating a feedback loop of disengagement and fear. This case is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how statistical evidence can expose gaps in public service performance and accountability. By making such metrics transparent, governments can facilitate informed public discourse and drive policy reforms based on empirical reality rather than perception. Open data serves as a critical tool for monitoring institutional credibility, allowing society to demand concrete improvements in security governance and restore faith in democratic institutions through verifiable facts.
