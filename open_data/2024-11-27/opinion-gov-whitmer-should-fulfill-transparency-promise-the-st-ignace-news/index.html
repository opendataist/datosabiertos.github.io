Opinion: Gov. Whitmer Should Fulfill Transparency Promise - The St. Ignace News

Opinion: Gov. Whitmer Should Fulfill Transparency Promise - The St. Ignace News

The article highlights a persistent failure in Michigan to expand Freedom of Information Act protections to the Governor’s office and the legislature, despite a 2016 pledge by Governor Gretchen Whitmer to eliminate this transparency gap. Eight years later, the state remains at the bottom of national rankings for government accountability, illustrating a significant lag between political promises and actual policy implementation. This delay underscores the difficulty of translating executive commitments into legislative action, particularly when facing entrenched institutional exemptions. Recurring bills designed to remove these exemptions have repeatedly passed one chamber but stalled in the other, surviving multiple legislative sessions without becoming law. Even with recent changes in partisan control and broader support from leadership, the measures remain trapped in committee. This legislative inertia persists despite several high-profile scandals that have heightened public scrutiny and underscored the urgent need for greater openness in state government operations. This situation is critically relevant to open data advocacy because it demonstrates that robust legal frameworks are insufficient without proactive enforcement and cultural commitment to transparency. The exclusion of the executive and legislative branches from FOIA requests creates a significant blind spot in public records, preventing citizens from accessing vital information about their government’s activities. Ultimately, improving trust in government requires closing these loopholes to ensure that all elected officials are subject to the same disclosure standards as other state entities.
