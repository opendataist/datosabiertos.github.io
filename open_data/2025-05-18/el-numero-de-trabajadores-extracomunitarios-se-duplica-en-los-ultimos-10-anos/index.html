El número de trabajadores extracomunitarios se duplica en los últimos 10 años

El número de trabajadores extracomunitarios se duplica en los últimos 10 años

The article highlights the impressive growth of non-EU labor in Spain, a phenomenon particularly pronounced in regions affected by rural depopulation. The data reveal that provinces in Castile and León, Galicia, and Aragon have experienced extreme percentage increases in the registration of workers born outside the EU. This trend suggests that revitalization initiatives and the structural advantages of these areas, such as affordable housing and lower population density, are actively attracting migrants seeking employment opportunities and a better quality of life. This population shift contrasts with stagnation or moderate growth in major metropolitan areas, where the highest absolute numbers of foreign workers are concentrated. Evidence indicates that the "empty Spain" is not only halting its demographic decline but is also emerging as an attractive destination for international migration, diversifying its labor base and revitalizing local economies through the integration of new residents. The relevance of this text to open data lies in the transparency of public statistics that enable the visualization of these structural changes in the geographic and demographic distribution of the population. By analyzing open data on labor registration and migration, citizens and analysts can better understand how territorial development policies impact migrant integration and efforts to combat depopulation, underscoring the importance of access to public information for informed decision-making regarding social and territorial cohesion.
