La inflación de la eurozona se mantiene en el 2,4% en abril, mínimos desde noviembre

Eurostat reports that inflation in the Eurozone remained stable in April, hovering at an annual rate of 2.4%. While headline inflation has reached its lowest level since November, underlying pressures persist, as core inflation—excluding volatile energy and food prices—remains slightly above the European Central Bank’s target. This data suggests that price levels are proving more sticky than anticipated, complicating the path toward the desired 2% goal. The stability of inflation is driven by a complex mix of sectoral trends. Energy prices have declined, yet services and industrial goods continue to push prices upward. Consequently, economic growth is recovering with strong domestic demand, which threatens to keep inflation persistent. This dynamic creates a challenging environment for policymakers, as the usual arguments for rapid rate cuts diminish when growth remains robust and unemployment stays at historic lows. For open data initiatives, this article highlights the critical importance of granular, real-time statistical transparency in monetary policy. Accurate data dissemination allows markets and citizens to understand the nuanced relationship between economic recovery and price stability. Reliable, detailed open data empowers stakeholders to make informed decisions and fosters accountability, ensuring that public discourse is grounded in verified evidence rather than speculation during periods of economic uncertainty.
