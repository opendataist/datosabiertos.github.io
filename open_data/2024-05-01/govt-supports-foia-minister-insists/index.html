Govt 'supports' FOIA, minister insists

Government officials in the Bahamas are redefining their stance on transparency, with the Minister of Foreign Affairs clarifying that his previous statements opposing the Freedom of Information Act were ironic rather than literal. This clarification follows political pressure regarding the administration's perceived lack of openness, highlighting a disconnect between campaign promises to implement the Act and current governance priorities focused on economic stability. The core implication for open data is the government’s reliance on ad hoc, discretionary information sharing rather than systematic, proactive disclosure. By stating that journalists should direct specific inquiries to spokespeople instead of utilizing formal transparency mechanisms, the administration effectively prioritizes control over accessibility. This approach undermines the principles of open data, which require predictable, standardized, and readily available public information. This situation is critically relevant to open data advocates as it illustrates how political rhetoric can hinder institutional accountability. When leaders treat transparency requests as burdensome inconveniences rather than statutory rights, it signals a systemic resistance to the cultural shift necessary for true data openness. Such attitudes prevent the development of robust data ecosystems, keeping valuable public information locked behind bureaucratic hurdles instead of being freely available for public scrutiny and innovation.
