The study reveals that socioeconomic origin remains a decisive factor in shaping an individual’s future, demonstrating that intergenerational poverty persists as a significant barrier. Individuals raised in economically disadvantaged households face drastically reduced opportunities to achieve high incomes or pursue higher education compared to their wealthier peers. This data highlights a rigid social structure in which early-life financial constraints severely limit long-term mobility and access to quality education. This persistence of inequality underscores the critical role of transparency in public policy analysis. Without detailed, accessible statistical evidence, the structural nature of disadvantage remains obscured. Open data is essential here because it enables researchers, journalists, and citizens to verify claims about social mobility, understand the true scale of inequality, and hold institutions accountable for addressing these disparities. Consequently, the availability and quality of open government data directly affect our ability to comprehend and remediate social exclusion. By making such demographic and economic surveys publicly accessible, authorities enable a deeper societal understanding of how poverty is transmitted. This transparency fosters informed debate and supports the development of targeted interventions aimed at breaking the cycle of poverty, demonstrating that open data is not merely a technical asset but a fundamental tool for social justice.
