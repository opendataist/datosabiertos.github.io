Although official statistics indicate that Oaxaca exceeds the national average in school attendance for children aged five to eleven, a visible contradiction exists in its capital. Dozens of minors are observed working in informal commerce, carrying heavy loads of textiles or candies instead of school supplies. This stark disparity between aggregated data and lived reality highlights the urgent need for open data initiatives that capture granular, local-level socioeconomic conditions rather than relying solely on broad national indicators. Activists argue that this situation reflects a failure by authorities to guarantee children’s rights and coordinate effective social work interventions. There are concerns that these minors are also used for surveillance by criminal groups, yet existing policies lack the nuanced, detailed information required to address root causes like family violence or poverty. Without specific, accessible data, policymakers cannot design targeted state policies that effectively protect vulnerable populations in specific municipalities. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the limitations of high-level statistics in revealing hidden social crises. True transparency requires disaggregated, real-time information that exposes the gap between official narratives and ground-level realities. By improving data quality and accessibility, society can move beyond criminalizing impoverished children and instead develop evidence-based strategies that truly safeguard their education and safety.
