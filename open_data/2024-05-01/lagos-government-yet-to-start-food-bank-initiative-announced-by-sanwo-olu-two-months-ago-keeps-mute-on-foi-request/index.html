Lagos Government Yet To Start 'Food Bank' Initiative Announced By Sanwo-Olu Two Months Ago, Keeps Mute On FOI Request

The delayed launch of Lagos State’s “Food Kitchen” initiative and the government’s refusal to disclose funding details highlight significant gaps in public accountability. By ignoring a Freedom of Information request for crucial expenditure and vendor selection data, the administration undermines trust in its palliative efforts. This opacity prevents citizens from verifying how resources are allocated, raising serious concerns about the management of state affairs during economic hardship. This situation is highly relevant to open data principles because it demonstrates the critical need for proactive transparency in public service delivery. When governments withhold financial and operational metrics, they violate the core tenet that public information should be accessible by default. Such secrecy hinders public oversight and makes it impossible to assess the efficacy or fairness of social programs. Ultimately, the case underscores that without mandatory data disclosure, citizens cannot hold leaders accountable. Open data serves as a mechanism to ensure that government interventions are both efficient and equitable. Ensuring immediate access to details regarding beneficiaries and budgets is essential for restoring faith in institutional processes and guaranteeing that aid reaches those who need it most.
