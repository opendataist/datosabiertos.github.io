Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

This article demonstrates the critical role of open data in fostering transparency within higher education institutions. By leveraging Freedom of Information requests, independent platforms can access and disseminate institutional performance metrics that are not always publicly promoted. This case highlights how FOI mechanisms empower third-party entities to curate and publish comprehensive academic datasets, thereby democratizing access to information that was previously siloed within university administrative systems. The analysis reveals significant disparities in student achievement across different disciplines and academic levels. Physics and English modules exhibited the highest attainment rates, while foundation years and specific language courses lagged significantly. These variations suggest that factors such as module design, assessment methods, and student preparation heavily influence outcomes. The data provides a granular view of academic success, identifying which courses yield the highest grades and which struggle to meet standard benchmarks, offering a clear picture of institutional strengths and weaknesses. Understanding these patterns is vital for stakeholders seeking to improve educational equity and quality. Open data allows students to make informed decisions about their coursework and enables educators to identify areas requiring pedagogical intervention. By making attainment statistics accessible, universities and external observers can drive evidence-based reforms. This transparency supports accountability, encouraging institutions to address systemic biases in grading and ensuring that all students have a fair opportunity to succeed, regardless of their chosen field of study.
