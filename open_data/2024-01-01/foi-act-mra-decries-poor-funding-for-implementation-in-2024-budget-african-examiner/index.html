FoI Act: MRA Decries Poor Funding for Implementation In 2024 Budget | African Examiner

The Media Rights Agenda highlights a critical failure in the Nigerian government’s 2024 budget, noting that the vast majority of federal institutions fail to allocate specific funds for implementing the Freedom of Information Act. This severe underfunding signals a lack of political commitment to transparency, suggesting that the law will continue to perform poorly. With only a handful of institutions setting aside resources, the report implies that the FOI Act remains largely symbolic rather than a practical tool for accountability, jeopardizing the integrity of public spending and development projects. Beyond mere budgetary neglect, the article emphasizes that adequate financial provisions are essential for the Act’s effectiveness, enabling government efficiency and public trust. The absence of funding in key oversight bodies, particularly the Federal Ministry of Justice, is identified as a major structural flaw. Without deliberate investment, the government cannot ensure accurate information flow or foster a knowledge society, leaving citizens without reliable data on how they are governed and undermining the democratic process. This issue is vital to the open data community because it exposes the resource constraints that prevent public institutions from generating and releasing high-quality, accessible information. True open data initiatives require not just legal frameworks but also the operational capacity to collect, manage, and share data. By ignoring these funding needs, the government hinders the creation of a transparent, accountable, and participatory governance environment, ultimately stifling the potential benefits of open government data for civil society and the public.
