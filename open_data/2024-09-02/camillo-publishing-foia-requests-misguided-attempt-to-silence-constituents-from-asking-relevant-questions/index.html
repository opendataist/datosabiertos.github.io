Camillo Publishing FOIA Requests: Misguided attempt to silence constituents from asking relevant questions

Camillo Publishing FOIA Requests: Misguided attempt to silence constituents from asking relevant questions

First Selectman Fred Camillo’s plan to publish all FOIA requests, ostensibly to expose abuse, actually risks chilling legitimate public inquiry into significant local decisions. While framed as transparency, this move may deter citizens from asking pertinent questions about major expenditures and administrative processes. This strategy is particularly concerning given ongoing controversies, such as the redevelopment of the Havemeyer Building and associated administrative moves. Publishing these requests without context shifts focus away from substantive issues, potentially obscuring the very details citizens have a right to scrutinize. For open data advocates, this highlights the critical distinction between raw transparency and meaningful accessibility. Simply releasing data does not guarantee insight; without careful curation and context, such publications can hinder rather than help public accountability and informed civic engagement.
