Conade convoca a una marcha para el lunes en rechazo a los datos del censo y la crisis económica

Conade convoca a una marcha para el lunes en rechazo a los datos del censo y la crisis económica

The article highlights widespread public unrest in Bolivia, fueled by deep dissatisfaction with the government’s economic management and contested census data. The Conade has organized nationwide protests demanding structural solutions to the financial crisis, fuel shortages, and rising food prices, accusing the administration of failing its citizens. At the same time, municipal leaders and civil society groups are mobilizing to challenge the official population figures released by the INE, arguing that they contradict local projections and calling for greater transparency from statistical authorities. This convergence of economic grievances and demographic skepticism underscores the fragility of institutional trust in public data and policy implementation. Citizens and local governments are actively rejecting official narratives they perceive as detached from reality, creating pressure for immediate accountability and alternative strategies to ensure basic welfare. The relevance to open data lies in the critical role of accurate, transparent statistics in maintaining governance legitimacy. When citizens distrust official census results, it signals a breakdown in the social contract regarding data integrity. Ensuring that statistical information is accessible, verifiable, and aligned with on-the-ground realities is essential not only for administrative planning but also for preserving democratic stability and public confidence in state institutions.
