Sobre 2,8 millones de nacimientos en última década, INE pide tomar en cuenta defunciones

The INE clarifies that discrepancies between demographic projections and census data stem primarily from the impacts of the pandemic, which reduced births and increased deaths, rather than from statistical errors. This context explains why population growth lagged behind birth rates, a trend that contrasts with the global pattern in which many developed nations face stagnant or negative growth, highlighting Bolivia’s relative resilience with a higher intercensal rate than the Latin American average. Regarding urban dynamics, the article reveals a shift in metropolitan structures, notably in La Paz, where high living costs have driven residents to adjacent municipalities. This “metropolization” process demonstrates how administrative boundaries often obscure actual population distribution, as surrounding areas absorb the growth while core cities stagnate. This is relevant to open data as it underscores the necessity of transparent communication regarding methodological adjustments and contextual factors. It illustrates how raw data must be interpreted with awareness of external crises and complex urban shifts to avoid misleading conclusions, emphasizing the importance of providing comprehensive metadata alongside published statistics for accurate public understanding.
