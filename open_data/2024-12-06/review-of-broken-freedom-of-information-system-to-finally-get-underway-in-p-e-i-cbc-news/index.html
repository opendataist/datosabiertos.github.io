Review of 'broken' freedom-of-information system to finally get underway in P.E.I. | CBC News

Review of 'broken' freedom-of-information system to finally get underway in P.E.I. | CBC News

This article highlights a systemic failure in Prince Edward Island’s Freedom of Information process, where extensive redactions and bureaucratic delays prevent citizens from accessing critical government information. The case of a mother seeking details on asbestos breaches at a local high school exemplifies how the current system obstructs transparency, leaving families and the public in the dark despite legal rights to information. This erosion of accountability underscores a broader democratic deficit where the government effectively withholds data through procedural bottlenecks and resource-starved oversight mechanisms. The structural flaws are exacerbated by a lack of political will and adequate resources within the Privacy Commissioner’s office. With an overwhelming caseload and under-staffing, appeals take years to resolve, allowing agencies to avoid scrutiny without significant consequence. Lawmakers’ inability to establish a dedicated special committee further dilutes the urgency of reform, suggesting that the deterioration of public access to information is being tolerated rather than addressed. This imbalance empowers officials to use bureaucratic hurdles as a shield against accountability. For open data advocates, this narrative serves as a stark warning about the dangers of opaque governance and the necessity of robust, well-resourced transparency frameworks. It illustrates how technical legal mechanisms, when poorly maintained, can render freedom of information laws ineffective. The article emphasizes that true open data requires not just the release of information, but timely, accessible, and unredacted data supported by strong institutional oversight. Without such safeguards, public trust erodes, and the fundamental principle of government accountability is compromised, urging immediate legislative and structural reforms.
