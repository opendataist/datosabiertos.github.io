Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions regarding employment, housing, and health compared to the general population, yet paradoxically report equal or even higher satisfaction with life. This resilience is evident in their strong affiliation with Norway and notably higher confidence in key institutions like the political system and judiciary, contrasting sharply with the general populace. Despite this institutional trust and life satisfaction, immigrants experience disproportionately high levels of mental distress and loneliness. Data indicates that mental health problems are twice as prevalent among immigrants, with substantial variance across different origin groups. This discrepancy highlights a complex reality where structural disadvantages and psychological burdens coexist with a positive subjective evaluation of their place in society. This finding is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how comprehensive, disaggregated statistics can reveal nuanced social dynamics that simple averages might obscure. By making such detailed datasets accessible, researchers and policymakers can better understand the multifaceted nature of integration challenges. It underscores the importance of transparent data in addressing specific vulnerabilities within immigrant communities while acknowledging their unique social strengths.
