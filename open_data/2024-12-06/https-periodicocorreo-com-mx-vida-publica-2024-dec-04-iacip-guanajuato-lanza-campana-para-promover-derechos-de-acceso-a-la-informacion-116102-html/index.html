The Institute for Access to Public Information in Guanajuato is launching an urgent promotional campaign during its final six months before it ceases to exist. The commissioner acknowledges that this effort comes too late, as society remained indifferent during legislative debates due to a general lack of understanding of the institution’s vital role in guaranteeing transparency and protecting personal data. This apathy highlights a significant disconnect between regulatory bodies and citizens, underscoring the need for better public education on civic rights. The strategy focuses on engaging diverse demographics, particularly teenagers and the elderly, through social media, university partnerships, and community events. The goal is to raise awareness of rights related to access to information and data protection, areas often overlooked by the public. By fostering an open academic network, the institution aims to generate knowledge about transparency, hoping to ensure that the transfer of these obligations does not result in a loss of accountability. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the fragility of institutional frameworks that ensure data accessibility. The potential dissolution of an independent oversight body threatens the independence necessary for impartial arbitration of government information. It emphasizes that open data initiatives require robust, independent institutions and continuous public engagement to survive political changes and remain effective safeguards for citizen rights.
