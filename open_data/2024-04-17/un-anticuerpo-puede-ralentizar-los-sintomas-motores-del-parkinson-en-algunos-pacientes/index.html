Un anticuerpo puede ralentizar los síntomas motores del Parkinson en algunos pacientes

A recent data analysis of the Pasadena trial reveals that the experimental monoclonal antibody prasinezumab can significantly slow motor deterioration in patients with rapidly progressing Parkinson’s disease after one year of treatment. By targeting alpha-synuclein, a key protein in the disease’s pathology, the study suggests that therapeutic efficacy is specific to subgroups with aggressive symptom progression, without showing similar results in cases of slow progression. The relevance of these findings for the field of open data lies in the critical importance of precise stratification of clinical data. This case illustrates how detailed and accessible analysis of heterogeneous datasets enables the identification of differentiated therapeutic responses, avoiding erroneous generalizations and facilitating personalized medicine. However, experts urge caution due to the modesty of the observed improvement and the need for long-term trials. Transparency in the publication of ongoing data and identified limitations is essential to assess the true balance between benefit, cost, and adverse effects, promoting rigorous and reproducible scientific evaluation in the development of future treatments.
