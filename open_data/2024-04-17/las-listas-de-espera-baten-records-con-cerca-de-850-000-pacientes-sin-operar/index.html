Las listas de espera baten récords con cerca de 850.000 pacientes sin operar

The report highlights a significant deterioration in access to non-urgent public healthcare in Spain, characterized by rising wait times and increasing volumes of patients on waiting lists compared to pre-pandemic levels. Despite a higher volume of surgeries being performed, the system’s capacity has not kept pace with demand, resulting in prolonged delays that affect patient well-being and strain healthcare resources across various medical specialties. Geographically, there is a stark disparity in care efficiency, with regions like Extremadura and Andalusia experiencing some of the longest delays and highest per-capita waiting lists, while Madrid and the Basque Country manage significantly better outcomes. This uneven distribution underscores systemic inequalities in service provision, indicating that regional management strategies and resource allocation play a critical role in determining wait times for specialized consultations and surgical procedures. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how structured, transparent, and longitudinal statistical data can reveal critical inefficiencies within public health systems. By making such granular metrics accessible, researchers and policymakers can move beyond anecdotal evidence to identify specific bottlenecks and regional discrepancies. This transparency fosters accountability and enables data-driven decision-making to improve operational efficiency and equity in healthcare delivery.
