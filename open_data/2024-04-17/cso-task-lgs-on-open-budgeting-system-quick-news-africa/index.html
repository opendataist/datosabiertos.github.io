CSO task LGs on open budgeting system - QUICK NEWS AFRICA

The DEAN Initiative urges local governments to join the Open Government Partnership, countering misconceptions that the framework enables financial investigations. Instead, OGP is designed to enhance citizen participation in governance through transparent, participatory budgeting. This shift moves away from assumptions about public needs, requiring active collaboration between leaders and communities to ensure resources reflect actual priorities. Participation is framed as essential for cooperative governance, fostering trust and reducing reliance on traditional campaign tactics. By engaging citizens early, authorities demonstrate that people are central to administration. This approach aligns with global standards adopted by nations like Nigeria, aiming to create systems where citizens can input key priorities during budget preparations, thereby strengthening the legitimacy of public spending. For open data initiatives, this highlights the critical link between data transparency and civic engagement. When budget data is open and accessible, it empowers citizens to hold governments accountable and contribute meaningfully to planning. Successful implementation can attract further funding for development projects, proving that open data is not just a technical requirement but a vital tool for effective, inclusive public service delivery and sustainable local development.
