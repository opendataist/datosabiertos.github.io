Sierra Leone’s Freedom of Information Act needs activating

Sierra Leone’s recent enactment of the Freedom of Information Law faces significant implementation hurdles, as the government has failed to establish the necessary administrative frameworks and enforcement mechanisms. Critics argue that without a concrete delivery plan and a functioning commission, the legislation remains symbolic, undermining public trust and preventing citizens from effectively accessing government records. This delay highlights a critical gap between legislative intent and practical governance. The article suggests resolving these bottlenecks through a collaborative task force involving government officials and civil society groups to oversee the commission’s establishment and funding. A major concern is the potential redundancy of maintaining both the Open Government Initiative and the new FOI Commission, which perform overlapping functions. Consolidating these efforts would prevent resource wastage and ensure that the enforcement body possesses the necessary independence and authority to compel compliance from public authorities. This situation is vital to the open data community as it illustrates how legal frameworks alone are insufficient without robust institutional support and clear implementation strategies. For open data to drive accountability, governments must proactively build the infrastructure that enables transparency, rather than merely passing laws. The Sierra Leone case serves as a cautionary tale about the importance of operationalizing transparency rights to ensure they are accessible, enforceable, and sustainable for the public.
