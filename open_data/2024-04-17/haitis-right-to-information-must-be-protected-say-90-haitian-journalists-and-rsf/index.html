Haiti’s right to information must be protected, say 90 Haitian journalists and RSF

Haiti is currently trapped in a severe multidimensional crisis characterized by extreme political instability and rampant gang violence following the assassination of President Jovenel Moïse. While a Transitional Presidential Council has been announced to restore order and organize elections, the immediate environment remains hostile to basic safety and rule of law. This instability has created a vacuum where armed groups control vast areas, making entire neighborhoods inaccessible and plunging the country into a state of lawlessness that hinders any effective governance or recovery efforts. The most critical aspect of this crisis is the endangerment of independent journalism, which is essential for keeping the international community informed about the unfolding reality on the ground. Journalists face constant threats of abduction, violence, and murder with complete impunity, leading to a significant decline in the profession’s viability. Many media professionals are forced to self-censor, flee the country, or abandon their work due to financial fragility and lack of training. The suppression of these voices threatens the right to information, as the lack of reliable reporting obscures the true extent of the humanitarian and political disaster from global awareness. This situation is highly relevant to open data because free and diverse journalism serves as a primary mechanism for generating, verifying, and disseminating transparent information to the public. When media ecosystems collapse, the flow of open, verifiable data about security, governance, and human rights violations is severely disrupted. Restoring a safe environment for journalists is not merely a press freedom issue but a foundational requirement for maintaining the integrity of open information systems. Without independent media to collect and share factual data, accountability mechanisms fail, and the global community loses the ability to monitor and respond effectively to crises, undermining the principles of transparency and informed citizenship.
