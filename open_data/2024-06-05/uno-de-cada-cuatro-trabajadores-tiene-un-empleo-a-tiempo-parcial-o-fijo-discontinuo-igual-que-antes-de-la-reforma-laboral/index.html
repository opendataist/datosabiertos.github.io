Uno de cada cuatro trabajadores tiene un empleo a tiempo parcial o fijo discontinuo, igual que antes de la reforma laboral

The article reveals that despite a significant rise in permanent full-time employment following labor reforms, the proportion of workers on part-time or fixed-discontinuous contracts remains unchanged since pre-pandemic levels. This stagnation at the 25% figure highlights a persistent structural challenge in the Spanish labor market, where job quality and stability do not fully align with recent regulatory improvements. While the share of stable indefinite contracts has grown considerably, it is largely offset by a sharp decline in temporary full-time roles, indicating a fundamental shift in employment composition rather than a comprehensive solution to precariousness. The prevalence of these specific contract types suggests that certain sectors, such as hospitality and domestic services, rely heavily on fragmented work models. Employers frequently utilize indefinite part-time or fixed-discontinuous arrangements to manage operational intermittency or reduce costs, such as avoiding holiday payments during non-working periods. Consequently, many workers in these fields desire more hours but face limited opportunities for expansion, underscoring a mismatch between worker expectations and employer strategies driven by seasonal or irregular business needs. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it illustrates the necessity of transparent, granular labor statistics to monitor policy effectiveness accurately. Open datasets enable researchers and policymakers to track the long-term implications of legislative changes, such as the distinction between genuine stability and merely shifted precariousness. By making this detailed breakdown publicly accessible, stakeholders can better analyze the true impact of labor reforms on job quality, ensuring that statistical indicators reflect not just the quantity of employment, but the evolving nature of work security and worker well-being.
