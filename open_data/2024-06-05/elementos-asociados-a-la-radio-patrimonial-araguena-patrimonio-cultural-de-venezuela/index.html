Elementos asociados a la Radio patrimonial aragüeña: Patrimonio Cultural de Venezuela

The article documents the rich and diverse radio heritage in Venezuela’s Aragua state, categorizing assets into movable, immovable, and intangible patrimony. It highlights significant collections of historical objects in private homes, architectural landmarks such as stadiums and ruins, and crucial intangible cultural expressions, including musical groups and social institutions. This comprehensive inventory serves as a testament to the region's material and cultural history, preserving testimonies of the past through various physical and artistic forms. A central focus is the historical significance of pioneering radio stations, particularly Radio Maracay, which played a vital role in broadcasting major global events since the 1930s. The text also identifies numerous other active stations and highlights the extensive contributions of over thirty years of practitioners, including musicians, poets, radio hosts, and cultural promoters. By recognizing these individuals as "Patrimonial Bearers," the article underscores the human element essential to maintaining and transmitting cultural memory through sound and media practices. This inventory is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how structured digital registries can systematically preserve and make accessible cultural heritage information. By categorizing diverse assets—from audio archives to architectural sites and biographical data of key figures—into a unified system, it facilitates public access to historical records. Such open cataloging encourages the safeguarding of intangible heritage and supports research, education, and cultural policy by providing transparent, searchable data about a region's identity and media history.
