MRA Accuses Federal Government of Under Funding FOI Implementation | African Examiner

The Media Rights Agenda has highlighted a critical disconnect in Nigeria’s governance, revealing that the Federal Government’s 2025 budget allocates a negligible fraction of resources to implementing the Freedom of Information Act. With only a tiny percentage of federal institutions including specific provisions for FOI compliance, the law remains largely symbolic. This severe underfunding undermines the Act’s potential to serve as a functional tool for accountability, suggesting that the state lacks the political will to enforce transparency despite the law’s existence for over a decade. The core implication of this neglect is that without adequate investment in information accessibility, public infrastructure and development projects are at heightened risk of misappropriation. Transparency acts as a safeguard for all government expenditures; when the FOI framework is starved of funds, it creates a culture of secrecy that facilitates corruption. Consequently, the lack of financial support for this legislation not only weakens democratic processes but also erodes public trust and hinders the emergence of a knowledge-based society where citizens can effectively participate in governance. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates that data accessibility is not merely a technical issue but a fiscal and political one. Effective open data ecosystems require robust funding for institutional capacity, monitoring, and compliance mechanisms. The report urges stronger oversight and resource allocation to ensure that data is not just released, but is actively used and regulated. It underscores that without dedicated budgetary support, open data initiatives fail to achieve their intended impact, leaving citizens without reliable information about how they are governed.
