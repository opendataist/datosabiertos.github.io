El índice de referencia del INE para la actualización de alquileres sube en diciembre al 2,28%

Spain has introduced a new statistical index to limit annual rent increases for leases signed after May 2023. This measure aims to prevent disproportionate hikes in housing costs by establishing a reference cap that combines standard inflation, underlying inflation, and long-term growth expectations. By applying a moderate coefficient, the government seeks to balance market realities with tenant protection, ensuring that rental adjustments remain stable and predictable rather than reacting sharply to short-term economic volatility. For tenancies established before the new housing law, the traditional update mechanisms remain in place, allowing landlords to adjust rents based on the Consumer Price Index or the Competitiveness Guarantee Index, as agreed in the original contracts. This distinction creates a dual system in which the timing of the lease signing determines its financial trajectory, highlighting the legislative effort to stabilize the rental sector during periods of high inflation while respecting existing contractual freedoms. This development is particularly significant for open data enthusiasts, as it requires the National Statistics Institute to regularly publish a complex, multi-variable statistical index. The creation and dissemination of this dataset enhance transparency in public administration, enabling citizens and researchers to examine the methodology behind price controls. Moreover, it illustrates how open statistical tools can be directly integrated into regulatory frameworks to address socio-economic challenges, offering a clear example of the societal value generated by accessible, high-quality government data.
