Indonesia Tightens Social Media Guidelines to Protect Children | Technology

Indonesia proposes temporary guidelines protecting children online while drafting a minimum age law, balancing safety with access. This interim measure aims to shield minors from harm without fully restricting digital engagement, reflecting growing concerns over young users' exposure to threats. The initiative highlights the tension between safeguarding welfare and preserving information rights. With nearly half of Indonesian children under twelve already online, policymakers must navigate complex ethical landscapes to ensure secure yet open digital environments for youth. This development is crucial for open data advocates, as it underscores the need for transparent regulatory frameworks. Clear standards on data usage and age verification are essential to build trust, ensuring that child protection measures do not inadvertently obscure critical public insights or hinder legitimate research.
