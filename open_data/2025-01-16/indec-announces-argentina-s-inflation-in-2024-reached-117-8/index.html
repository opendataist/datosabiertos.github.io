Indec announces Argentina's inflation in 2024 reached 117.8%

Indec announces Argentina's inflation in 2024 reached 117.8%

Argentina’s Consumer Price Index rose by 2.7% in December 2024, marking the lowest monthly inflation rate since the early pandemic. This figure aligns with the Libertarian government’s expectations and reinforces their narrative of a successful disinflation process. Housing and communication costs saw the sharpest increases, while food prices remained relatively stable in many regions, indicating a nuanced economic recovery despite high annual cumulative inflation. The Central Bank responded to this cooling inflation by reducing the monthly peso devaluation rate from 2% to 1% starting in February. This policy shift aims to anchor inflation expectations further, leveraging the improved economic trajectory to stabilize the currency. The decision reflects confidence in the downward trend of price rises, aiming to consolidate these gains as economic activity recovers and seasonal price pressures emerge. This development is highly relevant to open data enthusiasts, as it highlights the critical role of transparent, high-frequency statistical releases in shaping monetary policy. The debate over accurate price indices underscores the necessity for accessible, verifiable economic data. Open data platforms can facilitate public scrutiny of these metrics, ensuring that government claims of disinflation are supported by rigorous, independent analysis of consumer price trends.
