Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical character recognition transforms static images into machine-readable data, addressing the critical challenge of managing information from traditional print media in an increasingly digital world. This capability allows businesses to efficiently store and manipulate text that was previously inaccessible to standard editors, facilitating the digitization of complex documents like identity papers while maintaining data integrity. The technology significantly enhances operational efficiency by eliminating manual data entry and enabling seamless integration with other business software. By converting visual text into analyzable data, organizations can automate processes, conduct advanced analytics, and streamline workflows. This automation not only reduces human error but also accelerates productivity, allowing companies to focus on strategic decision-making rather than repetitive administrative tasks. This innovation is vital to open data initiatives as it unlocks vast amounts of unstructured information for public analysis and transparency. When identity documents and other restricted data are converted into open, standardized formats, it fosters better interoperability and trust in digital identity systems. Ultimately, OCR serves as a bridge between physical records and open data ecosystems, empowering researchers and citizens to access, verify, and utilize information more effectively across various sectors.
