Maine shooter signaled to police during check in that fellow reservists were afraid of him

The footage reveals that Robert Card explicitly warned his Army reservist peers he was likely to commit violence, indicating clear red flags were present long before the tragic event in Maine. This underscores a critical failure in the systems responsible for monitoring mental health and weapon access, as colleagues and family members expressed serious concerns that went unaddressed by authorities. This case highlights the urgent need for better interoperability between military personnel screening processes and law enforcement data sharing. When warning signs are documented but not acted upon, vulnerable populations remain at risk, emphasizing that institutional barriers often prevent effective intervention despite known threats. For open data advocates, this incident illustrates the life-saving potential of transparent, accessible government records. Making psychiatric evaluations and behavioral warning indicators more visible to relevant oversight bodies could prevent future tragedies. Improved data transparency ensures that critical safety information is not siloed, allowing systems to react proactively rather than retrospectively.
