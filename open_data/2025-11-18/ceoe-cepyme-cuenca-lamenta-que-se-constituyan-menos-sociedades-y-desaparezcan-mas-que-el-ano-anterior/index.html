CEOE CEPYME Cuenca lamenta que se constituyan menos sociedades y desaparezcan más que el año anterior

CEOE CEPYME Cuenca lamenta que se constituyan menos sociedades y desaparezcan más que el año anterior

The article highlights a concerning decline in the vitality of Cuenca’s productive fabric, as evidenced by a negative balance between the creation and dissolution of commercial societies. The data reveals that fewer new businesses are being established while, simultaneously, more companies are closing down compared to the previous year. This dual trend underscores the fragility of small enterprises in the region, which are increasingly struggling to survive amidst rising operational pressures. Several structural and economic factors contribute to this downturn. Small businesses, which rely heavily on local economic activity, are disproportionately affected by rising inflation and increased fiscal burdens. These costs directly threaten their viability, leading to closures. Furthermore, a prevailing climate of distrust among small entrepreneurs is suppressing the willingness to start new ventures, creating a cycle where the entry of new businesses lags while exits accelerate, weakening the overall market structure. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it illustrates the critical importance of accessible, transparent economic statistics for real-time policy intervention. By monitoring open datasets on company registrations and dissolutions, stakeholders can identify emerging trends and vulnerable sectors before they become crises. Such transparency empowers economic actors and policymakers to design targeted support measures, ensuring that raw data translates into actionable insights that can help stabilize and revive local economies.
