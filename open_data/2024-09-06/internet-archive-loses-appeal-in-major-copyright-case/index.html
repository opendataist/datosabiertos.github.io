Internet Archive loses appeal in major copyright case

Internet Archive loses appeal in major copyright case

The court upheld the ruling against the Internet Archive, rejecting its appeal that digital book lending constituted fair use. This decision confirms that unauthorized digital distribution of copyrighted materials, even during library closures, violates existing laws. The outcome reinforces publishers' rights over content accessibility, signaling a strict legal boundary for digital preservation efforts. For the open data community, this highlights the tension between public access initiatives and copyright enforcement. It demonstrates that digital repositories cannot bypass legal protections simply by adapting services for emergency contexts. Organizations aiming to provide broad access to cultural heritage must navigate complex legal frameworks to ensure sustainability. Ultimately, this case serves as a cautionary tale for projects involving digitized cultural assets. It emphasizes the critical importance of adhering to intellectual property laws when building open access platforms. Without strict compliance, even well-intentioned efforts to democratize knowledge face significant operational and legal risks.
