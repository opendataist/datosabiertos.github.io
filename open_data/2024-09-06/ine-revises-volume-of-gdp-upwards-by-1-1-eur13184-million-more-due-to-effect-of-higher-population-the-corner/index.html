INE revises volume of GDP upwards by 1.1%, €13,184 million more, due to effect of higher population | The Corner

The National Statistics Institute has announced a revision of Spain’s National Accounts, revealing that the 2021 nominal GDP figure has risen significantly. This adjustment primarily stems from updated data regarding the population and housing census, which increased the estimated number of inhabitants. Consequently, economic output calculations have been scaled up to reflect a larger demographic base, directly impacting the overall size of the economy for that year. While the absolute volume of GDP has changed, the actual growth rates of the Spanish economy remain largely unaffected, fluctuating only marginally. The revision also integrates new estimation methods and public finance updates mandated by European authorities. This ensures that the statistical framework aligns with international standards for Gross National Income and government deficit reporting, maintaining consistency and accuracy across national economic indicators. This update is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how statistical foundations evolve with better information sources. Researchers and developers relying on historical economic data must account for these baseline shifts to ensure accurate long-term analysis. Transparency regarding methodological changes and source substitutions allows users to interpret trends correctly, highlighting the dynamic nature of official statistics in reflecting societal changes.
