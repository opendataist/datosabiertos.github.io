The article argues that the U.S. government continues to fund gain-of-function research in China despite the pandemic’s origin, while deceiving Congress about these activities. It highlights a stark contradiction where officials denied data sharing with the Chinese Academy of Sciences, yet documentation proves a collaborative tri-country project involving significant U.S. taxpayer money. This suggests ongoing institutional collusion that poses severe public health risks by continuing the exact practices that triggered previous global crises. Relevance to open data lies in the transparency issues surrounding public funding and scientific collaboration. The case demonstrates how opaque reporting and false statements can hide critical safety information from the public and lawmakers. By uncovering these hidden agreements, the report underscores the necessity of open data policies to ensure accountability in government-funded research. Without accessible and truthful data, society cannot effectively assess or mitigate risks associated with dangerous biological experiments conducted under the guise of non-collaboration. Ultimately, the narrative exposes a failure of oversight where lies prevent necessary regulatory action. It implies that open data is essential for detecting such malfeasance and protecting public safety. The continued funding of high-risk research without genuine transparency illustrates the dangers of concealing scientific partnerships. Addressing these issues requires strict adherence to open data principles to prevent future outbreaks caused by undisclosed biological tinkering.
