Labour should take lead on lobbying and transparency register, says former MEP

Labour should take lead on lobbying and transparency register, says former MEP

Malta’s leadership is urged to establish a comprehensive lobby and transparency register, moving beyond political posturing. The article highlights that previous commitments to such measures were either unimplemented or voluntarily self-imposed by individual politicians. This context emphasizes that genuine open_data initiatives require institutional adoption rather than relying on voluntary, isolated efforts by specific actors who choose to disclose information publicly while others remain opaque. Criticism is directed at European Parliament President Roberta Metsola for excluding her office from transparency protocols, particularly regarding her husband’s lobbying role. The narrative suggests that true accountability demands consistent application of ethical standards without exceptions. By exempting herself from scrutiny, the President undermines the integrity of transparency reforms, illustrating how power can manipulate disclosure rules to avoid oversight, which directly impacts public trust in open governance structures. This discussion is vital to open_data because it exposes the gap between published policies and actual data availability. It demonstrates how transparency is often selectively applied, with key decision-makers avoiding the publication of critical interaction data. For open_data advocates, this serves as a reminder that data must be universally accessible and consistently recorded to ensure true accountability, rather than being optional or restricted to those who voluntarily participate in disclosure.
