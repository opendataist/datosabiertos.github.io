Judges grill Trump allies on bid for voting plans from EPA, other agencies

Federal judges questioned a conservative group’s request for Biden administration voting access plans, viewing them as protected internal deliberations. The ruling emphasizes that executive orders do not waive Freedom of Information Act exemptions for sensitive agency discussions. This case matters to open data because it clarifies the limits of transparency. It highlights how governments may withhold strategic documents even when initiated by public directives, protecting inter-agency deliberative processes from disclosure. The decision suggests that interested parties can still request outcome-focused information. It reinforces the distinction between raw planning data and finalized administrative actions in public records law.
