Inflación en México aceleró en marzo por segundo mes consecutivo; se ubicó en 3.80% anual

Inflation in Mexico experienced a slight acceleration in March, although it remained within the target range set by the Bank of Mexico. This behavior indicates that, despite recent upward pressures, monetary policy has succeeded in stabilizing prices at a manageable level, generating expectations that the central bank may continue its interest rate reduction cycles. The distinction between non-core inflation, which showed greater volatility, and core inflation, which decelerated, is key to understanding the current dynamics of consumption and production in the country. The relevance of this article to the field of open data lies in highlighting the critical importance of transparency and public access to official statistical data generated by institutions such as the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI). By providing detailed indicators like the Consumer Price Index (INPC) and the Producer Price Index (INPP), government organizations enable analysts, researchers, and the general public to conduct independent verification and understand the real economic implications. This open flow of information not only facilitates accountability but also empowers civil society to monitor compliance with economic stability objectives. Furthermore, the report illustrates how disaggregating data into specific categories, from food to industrial services, transforms abstract figures into actionable insights. The ability to track monthly and annual variations in specific economic subsectors demonstrates the value of granular datasets for identifying emerging trends and sectoral inequalities. Therefore, the availability of these open data is fundamental to fostering an informed, evidence-based public debate, which is essential for democratic governance and strategic planning in both the private and public sectors.
