Imágenes con IA al estilo Ghibli desatan debate por derechos de autor y su impacto ambiental

The viral trend of applying the Ghibli artistic style through AI image generators highlights a complex intersection of creativity, technology, and sustainability. While the ease of use and aesthetic appeal have democratized artistic expression, they have also sparked intense debates regarding copyright. Legal experts clarify that artistic styles themselves are not protected by copyright, but critics argue that reducing complex human art to a mere visual commodity devalues the emotional and philosophical intent behind original creative works. A critical implication of this phenomenon is the substantial environmental cost of AI infrastructure. The sudden, massive surge in demand for image generation has overwhelmed server capacities, leading to excessive electricity consumption and significant water usage for cooling data centers. This highlights the urgent need for more efficient computing models and sustainable cooling technologies to mitigate the ecological footprint of rapid AI adoption, as current operational practices struggle to keep pace with global user demand. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the hidden externalities of data processing and the ethical dimensions of training datasets. It challenges the open data community to consider not just the accessibility of algorithms, but also their societal impact on intellectual property and environmental resources. It suggests that transparency and ethical guidelines are as crucial as technical openness, urging developers to optimize data usage and address the sustainability issues inherent in large-scale computational systems.
