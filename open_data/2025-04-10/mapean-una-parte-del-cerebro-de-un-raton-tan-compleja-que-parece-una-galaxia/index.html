Mapean una parte del cerebro de un ratón tan compleja que parece una galaxia

Mapean una parte del cerebro de un ratón tan compleja que parece una galaxia

The creation of the most detailed functional map to date of a mouse brain represents a crucial milestone in understanding neural connections. By correlating the anatomy of 84,000 neurons with their visual activity, researchers have unraveled how the brain circuits underlying information processing are structured and operate, offering an unprecedented window into the complexity of the nervous system. This advance is fundamental to progressing in the treatment of neurological disorders, as it establishes a direct parallel with the Human Genome Project. By providing the first comprehensive connectivity resource, it enables the scientific community to formulate and test hypotheses about anomalous patterns in neural networks that may be associated with diseases such as Alzheimer’s or autism, transforming theoretical neuroscience into a discipline grounded in precise empirical data. For the open data field, this project is extraordinarily relevant because the terabytes of data generated have been openly published under permissive licenses, democratizing access to high-complexity research resources. By enabling scientists worldwide to use these 3D reconstructions without access barriers, it accelerates collaborative innovation and establishes a new standard for transparency and big data sharing in biomedical science.
