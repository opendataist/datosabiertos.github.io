¿Si usa Temu o Shein tendría problemas con su visa para entrar a Estados Unidos?

The U.S. administration has eliminated the duty-free exemption for small packages originating from China, a policy primarily aimed at platforms such as Shein and Temu. This decision responds to concerns about the security of personal data and the desire to protect domestic industries from unfair competition driven by artificially low prices. Although rumors circulated regarding the impact on visa applications, these threats lack current legal basis and remain purely commercial and national security measures. The removal of this regulation means that low-value shipments will now be subject to significant tariffs and more rigorous inspections. The stated objective is to curb the massive volume of imports that have overwhelmed the logistics system and to affect the profitability of these apps. This policy seeks to rebalance the trade balance by requiring imported products to comply with safety standards and contribute to tax revenues, thereby altering the dynamics of cross-border e-commerce. This article is relevant to the field of open data because it highlights the tension between government transparency and privacy protection. The official justification for these measures is based on cybersecurity risks and unauthorized access to personal information, underscoring the urgency of auditing how digital platforms collect, store, and share user data. Furthermore, it illustrates how public policies can affect the availability and flow of commercial information, impacting researchers’ ability to analyze consumption trends and competition in a global digital environment.
