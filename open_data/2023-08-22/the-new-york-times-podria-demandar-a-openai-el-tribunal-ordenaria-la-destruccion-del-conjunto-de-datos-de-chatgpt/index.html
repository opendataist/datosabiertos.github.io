The New York Times podría demandar a OpenAI: el tribunal ordenaría la 'destrucción' del conjunto de datos de ChatGPT

The recent update to The New York Times’ terms of service, which prohibits the unauthorized use of its content to train AI models, marks a critical turning point in the protection of digital intellectual property. The potential lawsuit against OpenAI seeks to establish legal precedents on whether the extraction of texts to generate automated responses constitutes infringement, potentially forcing technology companies to rebuild their databases using only authorized material. This conflict highlights the growing tension between generative artificial intelligence innovation and traditional copyright rights. As chatbots act as direct competitors by replicating original writings, media outlets are seeking to protect their commercial value. A favorable ruling could impose severe penalties and the destruction of datasets, compelling tech companies to negotiate licenses rather than using content arbitrarily. This dispute is fundamental to the open data movement, as it establishes clear limits on what information can be freely used by algorithms. If unauthorized access to copyrighted content is prohibited, it defines that “open data” does not apply to protected intellectual property without authorization. This directly impacts the ethics and legality of datasets available for AI research and development, prioritizing explicit consent over indiscriminate access.
