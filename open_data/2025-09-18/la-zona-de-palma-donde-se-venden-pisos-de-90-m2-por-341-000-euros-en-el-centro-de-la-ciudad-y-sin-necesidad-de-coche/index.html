La zona de Palma donde se venden pisos de 90 m2 por 341.000 euros: en el centro de la ciudad y sin necesidad de coche

La zona de Palma donde se venden pisos de 90 m2 por 341.000 euros: en el centro de la ciudad y sin necesidad de coche

The article highlights a severe housing accessibility crisis in the Balearic Islands, where young people face disproportionately high barriers to homeownership due to soaring property prices that far exceed the national average. This situation creates a generational exclusion from the property market, as the extreme cost of living in an island with limited space and high tourist pressure makes it nearly impossible for residents to save for a mortgage within a reasonable timeframe. For the specific area of Avenidas in Palma, the combination of strategic location, excellent public transport, and urban amenities drives intense demand. However, this desirability exacerbates price disparities between neighborhoods, effectively segregating the market along socioeconomic lines. While the zone offers significant quality-of-life benefits by reducing car dependency, the escalating costs push local residents toward an unsustainable cycle where even renting hinders the ability to eventually buy a home. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparent, granular pricing datasets (such as those from Idealista or property registries) can reveal systemic inequality and inform policy decisions. By making detailed market metrics visible, open data empowers citizens and governments to understand the root causes of housing unaffordability, moving beyond aggregate statistics to identify specific geographic and demographic vulnerabilities that require targeted intervention.
