Plantea Pliego participación ciudadana para elaborar nueva Ley de Transparencia

Plantea Pliego participación ciudadana para elaborar nueva Ley de Transparencia

The State of Mexico is overhauling its transparency framework through a new law designed to ensure robust public access to information. This legislative shift addresses structural changes following the dissolution of the previous institute, clarifying that internal control bodies and the Legislative Comptroller will now assume the critical responsibility of responding to information requests. This transition aims to prevent conflicts of interest and maintain continuity in governance, ensuring that accountability mechanisms remain effective despite institutional reorganization. Crucially, the development process prioritizes inclusive civic engagement through an open parliament and social media channels. By inviting experts, associations, and citizens to contribute to the final text, the initiative seeks to create a consensus-driven document that reflects societal needs rather than just political agendas. This participatory approach underscores the principle that transparency laws are most effective when they are co-created with the populace they serve, fostering trust and legitimacy in the new regulatory structure. This article is highly relevant to open data as it highlights the essential link between legal frameworks and practical data accessibility. It demonstrates how open government requires not just technical compliance, but active societal participation to define who holds data and how it is shared. Understanding these legislative nuances is vital for data advocates, as the clarity of responsibilities directly impacts the ability of journalists, researchers, and citizens to obtain and utilize public information for democratic oversight and informed decision-making.
