La deuda de las administraciones públicas se modera en julio al 102,3% del PIB, hasta 1,68 billones de euros

La deuda de las administraciones públicas se modera en julio al 102,3% del PIB, hasta 1,68 billones de euros

Public administration debt in Spain moderated its growth relative to the economy in mid-2025, reflecting a slight decoupling from the rapid expansion seen previously. Although the absolute amount of debt remained substantial, the debt-to-GDP ratio decreased year-over-year. The government anticipates a continued, gradual reduction in this ratio over the coming decades, aiming to align with long-term fiscal stability goals, while the central bank’s forecasts suggest only marginal improvements in the near term. The dynamics driving this trend reveal divergent behaviors among different administrative levels. While the central government, autonomous communities, and social security all experienced year-on-year increases in borrowing—largely due to state subsidies for social security deficits—municipalities were the only sector to successfully reduce their debt burden. This contrast highlights that overall stabilization is not uniform, with local governments showing resilience while other public entities continue to accumulate liabilities, complicating efforts to achieve a broad-based decrease in public indebtedness. This information is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for granular, accessible, and timely public financial statistics. Transparent, detailed data allows stakeholders to monitor the specific contributions of each administrative tier to national debt levels, fostering accountability and informed policy-making. By providing clear insights into where debt is accumulating or receding, open data initiatives empower citizens and analysts to assess fiscal health beyond aggregate figures, ensuring that governmental projections and actual outcomes can be rigorously compared and scrutinized.
