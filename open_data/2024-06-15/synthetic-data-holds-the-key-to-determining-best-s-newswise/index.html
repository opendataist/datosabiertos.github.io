Synthetic data holds the key to determining best s | Newswise

The study demonstrates that synthetic population data offers a powerful alternative to traditional surveys, which often underrepresent marginalized communities. By using an artificial dataset representing millions of residents, researchers developed an "equity-aware" decision support tool that allows policymakers to model transportation investments more accurately. This approach reveals how funding distribution impacts equity, providing a reliable method to assess the effects of new service designs on diverse populations without the biases inherent in conventional data collection methods. The analysis highlights a critical trade-off between efficiency and equity in transportation planning. While prioritizing high-cost ride-hailing services in metropolitan areas maximizes revenue and overall consumer surplus, investing in on-demand microtransit significantly reduces disparities in benefits among different groups. The findings suggest that a balanced budget strategy, combining both service types across urban and rural regions, is necessary to ensure baseline benefits reach less profitable areas. This underscores the importance of targeted subsidies for microtransit to support disadvantaged communities effectively. This research is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it validates the utility of synthetic datasets for public good and policy analysis. It promotes transparency by making model parameters accessible to any agency, fostering inclusive urban development. By proving that synthetic data can drive equitable outcomes while protecting individual privacy, the study encourages the broader adoption of open, privacy-preserving data solutions in transportation planning and urban science.
