The Nagaland Finance Department confirmed via RTI that no legal framework authorizes deducting funds from contracts for ministers or political parties. This revelation exposes the absence of statutory justification for such widespread financial exactions, highlighting significant gaps in regulatory compliance. This finding underscores critical accountability failures in public procurement, suggesting that extracted monies lack transparent governance mechanisms. Consequently, the legitimacy of these financial practices remains legally questionable, undermining public trust in state fiscal management. For open data, this case illustrates how transparent information requests can reveal hidden or non-existent legal provisions. It emphasizes the vital role of public access to government records in ensuring financial integrity and exposing arbitrary administrative actions.
