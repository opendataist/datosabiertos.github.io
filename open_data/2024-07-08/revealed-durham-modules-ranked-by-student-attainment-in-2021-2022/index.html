Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

This article highlights how Freedom of Information requests can unlock granular institutional data, demonstrating the potential for open data to enhance transparency in higher education. By analyzing university module performance, it reveals significant variations in academic attainment across disciplines and student years. This approach validates the utility of public records in scrutinizing educational outcomes and holds implications for accountability and institutional self-assessment within the open data ecosystem. The data shows substantial disparities in grading standards, with physics and English leading in high-grade attainment while foundation years and certain language modules lag significantly. Such findings underscore the importance of contextualizing metrics, as factors like class size and assessment types influence results. These insights suggest that open datasets can expose systemic inequalities or structural differences in teaching effectiveness, prompting stakeholders to question the fairness and consistency of grading practices across different departments. Relevance to open data lies in its demonstration of how raw administrative records can be transformed into actionable public knowledge. When institutions release detailed performance metrics, it empowers students, policymakers, and researchers to make informed decisions about course selection and resource allocation. This case illustrates that open data is not merely about accessibility but also about fostering a culture of evidence-based dialogue, ultimately encouraging universities to justify their academic standards through transparent, publicly available evidence.
