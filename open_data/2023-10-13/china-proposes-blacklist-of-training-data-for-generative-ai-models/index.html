China proposes blacklist of training data for generative AI models

China has proposed stringent security requirements for generative AI providers, mandating the blacklisting of training data containing illegal or harmful content. This approach prioritizes national stability and ideological control, ensuring that models are not trained on information censored within China or those threatening social order. The regulations also emphasize the protection of individual rights, requiring explicit consent for using personal and biometric data, while providing guidelines to prevent intellectual property violations. These measures reflect a broader strategy to establish rigorous guardrails for AI development, balancing technological advancement with strict regulatory oversight. This framework is crucial for understanding open data, as it illustrates how governments can restrict data accessibility and usage based on political and security criteria. It highlights the tension between the open exchange of information necessary for AI innovation and the state’s desire to control narrative and data sources, potentially limiting the transparency and inclusivity of global AI training datasets.
