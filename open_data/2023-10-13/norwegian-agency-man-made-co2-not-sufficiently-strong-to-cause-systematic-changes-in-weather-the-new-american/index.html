Norwegian Agency: Man-Made CO2 "Not Sufficiently Strong" to Cause "Systematic Changes" in Weather - The New American

This article presents a study by Norwegian statisticians arguing that human CO2 emissions are insufficient to cause the observed global warming trends. The researchers contend that standard climate models fail to accurately track historical temperature data and suggest that recent warming is likely a natural continuation of post-Ice Age recovery. By analyzing isotopic records from Greenland, they highlight that warmer periods have occurred naturally in the past, often predating significant industrial activity, thereby questioning the causal link between anthropogenic emissions and current temperature rises. The relevance of this article to open data lies in its critical examination of data transparency and scientific consensus. The authors argue that the prevailing view on climate change is sustained not by clear evidence, but by a lack of accessible information for non-experts. They accuse the scientific community of obscuring data and making it difficult for the public to understand the true statistical basis of climate models. This narrative emphasizes the importance of open access to raw data and independent statistical verification, suggesting that without full transparency, consensus may mask uncertainty or bias rather than reflect objective reality. Ultimately, the study concludes that temperature variations may be random and stationary, indicating that CO2 has not statistically driven systematic changes in global temperatures over the last two centuries. The authors position their work as a corrective to what they perceive as a media-driven narrative, advocating for a re-evaluation of climate science based strictly on statistical evidence available to the public. This perspective underscores the tension between established scientific consensus and alternative interpretations derived from open statistical analysis, highlighting how data accessibility can influence public understanding of complex global issues.
