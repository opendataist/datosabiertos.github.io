Transparency group unveils proposal to enshrine Arkansas FOIA in state Constitution

Transparency group unveils proposal to enshrine Arkansas FOIA in state Constitution

A coalition in Arkansas is proposing a constitutional amendment to enshrine government transparency, ensuring that any future reductions to public openness require direct approval by voters. This initiative responds to recent legislative efforts to expand exemptions to the state’s Freedom of Information Act, aiming to protect the right to information as a fundamental constitutional right rather than a statutory privilege granted by the legislature. The proposal seeks to clarify longstanding ambiguities regarding what constitutes a public meeting, closing loopholes that allow officials to conduct government business through informal communications. By establishing strict definitions and substituting overly broad security exemptions with balanced ones, the measure intends to prevent officials from hiding deliberations or sensitive operational details under the guise of security or efficiency, thereby strengthening the public’s ability to hold government accountable. Additionally, the amendment introduces stricter enforcement mechanisms, including personal financial liability for officials who knowingly violate transparency laws and guaranteed attorney’s fees for successful plaintiffs. This effort is highly relevant to open data advocates as it demonstrates a structural approach to protecting data accessibility; by raising the barrier for restricting information, it ensures that the principles of open government remain resilient against political shifts and administrative secrecy.
