RTI Day celebrated - OrissaPOST

The article highlights the critical tension between the empowering spirit of India’s Right to Information Act and the systematic erosion of its effectiveness by state institutions. Despite legislative dilutions and administrative opacity, continued citizen engagement suggests that the law remains a vital tool for accountability. However, the deliberate neglect of mandatory proactive disclosures by public authorities significantly undermines the act’s intended transparency and efficiency. The discussion underscores the urgent need for an information revolution that bridges communication barriers and empowers the populace through accessible data. Regressive amendments have weakened the autonomy of information commissions, while recent data protection laws have been leveraged to exempt personal information from disclosure. This legal maneuvering effectively restricts public access to information that should remain visible, thereby compromising the democratic function of transparency. This content is relevant to open data because it illustrates the real-world obstacles preventing the free flow of public sector information. The deliberate withholding of mandatory data and the legal framing of privacy to hide government actions demonstrate how policy can actively suppress open data initiatives. Understanding these political and legal barriers is essential for advocates aiming to establish robust, unencumbered open data ecosystems that truly serve the public interest.
