Baja California en la Métrica de Gobierno Abierto

The Open Government Metric reveals that, although Baja California showed progress in institutional openness up to 2021, this trend reversed in 2023 with a significant drop in its average score. This decline underscores the urgency of addressing the gaps identified in public management, particularly in critical areas that directly affect the relationship between the State and citizens. The analysis clearly distinguishes between a relatively strong performance in transparency and a deficient one in participation. While certain aspects of transparency exceed the national average, the participation dimension records extremely low scores, indicating the absence of effective mechanisms that allow society to influence public policies. This disparity highlights that merely publishing information does not guarantee truly open governance if it is not accompanied by real channels for public engagement. This report is crucial for the open data movement because it exposes that the availability of digital files does not equate to genuine openness if they lack standardized structure, free licenses, or feedback capabilities. By identifying open data and proactivity as areas for improvement, the study encourages institutions to transform information into accessible and useful resources, which are essential for building trust and enabling effective, evidence-based citizen oversight.
