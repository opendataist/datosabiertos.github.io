Right To Information (RTI) Act: All India Annual Digest 2024

Right To Information (RTI) Act: All India Annual Digest 2024

The judiciary has recently reinforced the operational efficacy and transparency mandates of the Right to Information Act. A pivotal Supreme Court ruling clarified that the Central Information Commission possesses inherent authority to frame regulations and constitute benches, ensuring efficient case disposal without needing explicit statutory provisions for such structural mechanisms. This decision underscores the necessity of robust administrative frameworks to uphold the core objective of timely information access, which is fundamental to the open data ecosystem’s credibility. Concurrently, high courts have defined the boundaries of this transparency by balancing public interest against privacy concerns. Rulings indicate that while disclosure of merit-based data in public recruitment serves the public interest, personal details such as employee service records, travel history, and educational institutions are protected under privacy exemptions. These decisions highlight the critical importance of precise legal definitions in open data initiatives, ensuring that released datasets enhance accountability without compromising individual rights. Furthermore, the judiciary has clarified the scope of "public authorities" and information duties, affirming that agencies like the CBI remain subject to disclosure requests regarding corruption, provided sensitive investigative details are withheld. By rejecting administrative delays as valid grounds for denial and limiting the Commission’s jurisdiction over specific fund utilization matters, these rulings establish clear procedural standards. This legal clarity is vital for open data advocates, as it delineates exactly which information must be proactively disclosed to foster genuine governmental transparency.
