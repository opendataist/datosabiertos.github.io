18.5 mdp de algo deben servir… / Armando Vásquez A. - Kioscomayor

18.5 mdp de algo deben servir… / Armando Vásquez A. - Kioscomayor

The article reports that various state government agencies have accumulated significant arrears in water service payments, revealing that while information on public debts is accessible, it lacks structured analysis. Although the data are public, their fragmented presentation in numerical lists hinders a comprehensive understanding of the fiscal situation, highlighting a gap between the availability of raw data and its usefulness for accountability. The main implication is that these outstanding payments directly affect access to water resources, including public infrastructure such as schools. This underscores how opacity in the management of government financial obligations has tangible social consequences, demonstrating the need for open data publication that links financial transparency with the impact on essential citizen services. This case is relevant to the open data community because it illustrates the difference between publishing information and generating real transparency. For open data to be effective, access alone is not enough; normalization and contextualization are required so that citizens can understand the relationships between public spending, state debts, and the quality of essential services, thereby fostering more informed and effective civic oversight.
