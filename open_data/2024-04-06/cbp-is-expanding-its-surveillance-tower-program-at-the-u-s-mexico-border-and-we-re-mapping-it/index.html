CBP Is Expanding Its Surveillance Tower Program at the U.S.-Mexico Border–And We're Mapping It

The Electronic Frontier Foundation has released a comprehensive, publicly accessible dataset mapping the extensive surveillance infrastructure along the U.S.-Mexico border, significantly expanding beyond previous records. This initiative documents hundreds of surveillance towers, including autonomous systems powered by artificial intelligence, as well as automated license plate readers and facial recognition technology at border crossings. By compiling this information through public records, satellite imagery, and virtual exploration, EFF provides a transparent "living snapshot" of what they term the "virtual wall," ensuring that researchers and the public have independent access to critical location data. This resource is vital for analyzing the real-world implications of border security policies, challenging the narrative that such surveillance is confined to remote areas. The data reveals that many towers are situated in densely populated urban zones, directly impacting local communities and migrants. Researchers can utilize these details to evaluate the effectiveness of these technologies in preventing smuggling, assess environmental impacts, and investigate how surveillance infrastructure influences migrant safety and migration routes. The openness of this dataset allows for rigorous, independent scrutiny of government claims regarding border security efficacy and human rights impacts. For the open data community, this release exemplifies the power of civic tech and transparency advocacy in holding powerful institutions accountable. It demonstrates how combining open procurement data with innovative visualization techniques can expose hidden governmental operations. The dataset, released under a Creative Commons license, invites remixing and further analysis, fostering a collaborative environment for journalism and academic research. By democratizing access to sensitive infrastructure data, EFF enables a deeper public understanding of privacy, surveillance, and civil liberties in the digital age.
