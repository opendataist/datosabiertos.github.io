Expertos cuestionan enfoque del Gobierno al presentar datos de muertes prevenibles

The article reveals a significant dispute regarding the use of health data for political purposes. While the government cites hundreds of thousands of "avoidable deaths" to justify aggressive reforms against health insurers, experts argue this narrative is biased. They contend that the administration distorts the report to advance an agenda of dismantling existing structures, ignoring that the health system has successfully reduced mortality rates more effectively than many regional peers. A central conclusion is that avoidable deaths are driven primarily by socioeconomic determinants rather than healthcare access alone. Factors such as poverty, malnutrition, and lack of basic sanitation are major contributors, meaning these issues fall under the responsibility of other government sectors. Therefore, attributing these fatalities solely to health service failures is misleading and overlooks the complex interplay of social conditions that truly impact public health outcomes. This case is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency can be manipulated to serve specific policy interests. It highlights the critical need for accurate data interpretation and context when public information is released. To foster genuine improvement, stakeholders must understand the full scope of the data, ensuring that open data initiatives promote holistic solutions involving multiple sectors rather than being weaponized for targeted political attacks.
