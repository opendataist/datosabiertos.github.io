EPA Might Approve Pesticide That May Harm Children’s Health

The article reveals that the US EPA faced significant political and industry pressure to approve aldicarb, a pesticide classified as extremely hazardous and banned in over 100 countries. Despite internal scientific consensus that the chemical poses unacceptable risks to public health, particularly for children, agency records suggest that regulatory decisions were influenced by lobbyists seeking to expand market access rather than strict adherence to safety standards. This conflict highlights a systemic challenge where scientific integrity is often undermined by external interference. Internal communications show that scientists attempted to mitigate identified risks through flawed modeling or usage caps to satisfy political demands, while simultaneously raising alarms about potential contamination of drinking water and food supplies. These actions reflect broader concerns about the EPA’s commitment to objective risk assessment when confronting powerful agricultural interests. This case is highly relevant to the open data movement because it demonstrates the critical importance of transparency in government decision-making. The revelations were only possible through FOIA lawsuits, underscoring how public access to internal agency records serves as a vital check on regulatory capture. By making these communications public, citizens can hold institutions accountable and ensure that policy decisions are based on robust scientific evidence rather than hidden political or commercial pressures.
