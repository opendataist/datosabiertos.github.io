The U.S. death row population has declined significantly, reaching its lowest level since the early 1990s. This long-term downward trend indicates a shift away from capital punishment, reflecting broader societal and legal changes that are reducing the number of individuals sentenced to death. Despite fewer people entering death row, incarceration durations are increasing, with prisoners serving longer periods before resolution. The aging inmate population further complicates the system, as a growing proportion of those sentenced are elderly. These factors suggest that while the scope of capital punishment is shrinking, its administrative and human costs remain high for those remaining. This data is relevant to open_data as it demonstrates how transparent, accessible government statistics can reveal long-term justice trends. By making such records public, researchers and citizens can monitor systemic changes, ensuring accountability in criminal justice policies and supporting informed public discourse on penal reform.
