Secretaría de Salud debe informar sobre casos de violencia obstétrica en hospitales: INAI

The National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) ordered the Ministry of Health to publish the awards issued by the National Commission of Medical Arbitration that confirm cases of obstetric violence, as well as statistics on rulings against doctors and hospitals. This resolution aims to bring visibility to a form of violence that affects a significant proportion of women in Mexico, which has been socially normalized. By demanding the dissemination of this data, the transparency authority establishes that transparency is a fundamental tool for updating the women’s rights agenda and combating patriarchal practices in healthcare. The case illustrates the relevance of open data for accountability and the protection of human rights. By requiring a health institution to make its arbitration processes public, it demonstrates how access to public information enables auditing of the healthcare system and ensures that institutions take responsibility for their actions. The disclosure of these awards not only documents the scale of the problem but also serves as evidence for civil society and international organizations to monitor compliance with standards of dignified care. Furthermore, the decision reinforces the principle that information that is not generated or recorded cannot be used as an excuse to deny rights. Although it was determined that the Ministry of Health does not maintain an official statistical registry of such cases, it did possess information derived from the arbitral awards. Therefore, the obligation to publish these documents guarantees that victims have access to the truth and establishes a clear precedent for institutional transparency in health matters, promoting a fairer society that respects human dignity.
