The Endutih survey reveals that, despite the growing use of the internet in Mexico, a significant digital divide persists, affecting millions of citizens. This exclusion is driven primarily by a lack of digital skills and economic constraints, rather than merely by the availability of infrastructure. Understanding these specific barriers is crucial for open data advocates, as it highlights that simply providing connectivity is insufficient without addressing the human capital and socioeconomic factors that prevent equitable access. The data exposes stark regional disparities, with connection rates varying drastically from one state to another. This geographic inequality mirrors broader social issues, such as poverty and employment gaps. For open data initiatives, this underscores the need for disaggregated datasets that enable policymakers to identify and address localized bottlenecks, ensuring that digital inclusion strategies are tailored to the specific needs of underserved communities rather than relying on a uniform national approach. Furthermore, the shift toward mobile connectivity and the declining interest in traditional paid television indicate changing consumption habits. These trends suggest that future digital policies must prioritize mobile-friendly open data platforms and digital literacy programs. Recognizing how people actually access technology allows for more effective public interventions that leverage existing habits, such as smartphone use, to bridge the gap between those who are connected and the millions still excluded from the digital economy.
