The 2021 Salary Structure Survey reveals persistent economic and gender inequalities in Spain’s labor market. A significant portion of the workforce earns wages at or near the minimum threshold, with women disproportionately affected, as they represent a much larger share of low-income earners compared to men. This disparity extends to hourly and annual earnings, where the gender pay gap remains pronounced despite women’s salaries growing at a faster rate than men’s. The data highlight a structural skew in wage distribution, where a small number of high salaries inflate the average, masking the reality that the majority of workers earn considerably less. Consequently, the median salary is substantially lower than the mean, indicating that most employees are clustered in the lower to middle income brackets rather than benefiting from the upward trend in average wages. This article is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates the necessity of granular, high-quality statistical data for identifying systemic social inequities. By making these detailed salary distributions public, institutions enable researchers and policymakers to analyze labor market dynamics in depth. Such transparency supports evidence-based interventions aimed at reducing gender gaps and improving overall economic fairness through informed legislative and corporate decisions.
