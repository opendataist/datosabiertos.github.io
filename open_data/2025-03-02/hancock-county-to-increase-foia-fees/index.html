Hancock County to increase FOIA fees

Hancock County to increase FOIA fees

Hancock County has significantly updated its Freedom of Information Act fee structure, increasing charges for document production to better reflect actual operational costs. After holding rates steady for decades, the county is now aligning FOIA fees with general office reproduction costs, acknowledging that the previous minimal charge failed to cover the wear on equipment and technical resources required for document processing. This adjustment ensures that the financial burden of public records requests is more equitably distributed, moving away from a subsidized model to one that accurately captures the labor and technological expenses involved in retrieving information. By setting fees that mirror the true cost of production, the county aims to maintain sustainable service levels for residents and entities seeking official records, rather than absorbing these expenses into the general budget. This case is relevant to open data because it highlights the tension between the ideal of free public access and the practical realities of maintaining data infrastructure. As government agencies increasingly digitize records, understanding how fees are structured helps clarify whether data remains truly accessible or becomes restricted by cost. It serves as a reminder that while open access is a principle, sustainable implementation requires adequate funding to support the technical systems that make data retrievable for the public.
