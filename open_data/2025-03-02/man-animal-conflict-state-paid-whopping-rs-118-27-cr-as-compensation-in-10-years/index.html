Man-animal Conflict State paid whopping Rs 118.27 cr as compensation in 10 years

Man-animal Conflict State paid whopping Rs 118.27 cr as compensation in 10 years

This article highlights the escalating severity of human-wildlife conflict in Maharashtra, revealing a sharp rise in tiger attacks and associated financial burdens over the past decade. The data demonstrates that the majority of fatalities and compensation payouts occurred recently, indicating a deteriorating situation that has triggered public protests and forced government intervention, including plans to relocate excess tigers to other states. The relevance to open data lies in the critical role played by transparency and citizen-led information access in driving accountability. The findings were uncovered through a Right to Information (RTI) request, showcasing how accessible public records can expose hidden costs and trends in wildlife management. This case illustrates that open data empowers civil society to monitor government performance, hold authorities responsible for safety and spending, and advocate for more effective biodiversity conservation strategies amidst sustainable development challenges.
