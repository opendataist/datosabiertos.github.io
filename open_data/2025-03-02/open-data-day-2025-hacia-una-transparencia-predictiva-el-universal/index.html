Open Data Day 2025: Hacia una Transparencia Predictiva | El Universal

Open Data Day 2025: Hacia una Transparencia Predictiva | El Universal

The article highlights Open Data Day as a critical international platform for promoting citizen participation and governmental accountability through open data. It introduces “predictive transparency” as a transformative evolution from traditional reactive models. This approach uses historical data analysis to anticipate citizens’ information needs, enabling governments to proactively publish relevant datasets before they are requested. By reducing access barriers, this strategy aims to strengthen public trust and enhance the efficiency of governmental reporting. Achieving predictive transparency requires overcoming significant technical and political challenges. Success depends on integrating and standardizing data related to information access requests, as well as developing robust analytical capabilities within public institutions. Crucially, it demands political will to view transparency not merely as a compliance obligation, but as a strategic tool for improving decision-making. The INAI is currently collaborating to define the methodologies needed to operationalize this concept, leveraging machine learning to optimize government responses and foster a more efficient accountability system. This shift is highly relevant to open data because it redefines the purpose of data publication from passive availability to proactive utility. It demonstrates how open data can drive smarter, more responsive governance by anticipating user behavior rather than waiting for specific inquiries. Ultimately, this approach advocates for a democratic society where information is not just accessible, but intelligently anticipated, ensuring that open data serves as a dynamic engine for civic engagement and institutional confidence.
