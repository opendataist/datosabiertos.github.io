La trampa del copyright en la era de la inteligencia artificial: no es innovación, es retroceso

The article argues that traditional copyright laws are obsolete mechanisms that primarily benefit corporate intermediaries rather than creators themselves. By highlighting the hypocrisy of artists protesting AI as pawns for record labels, it concludes that the current intellectual property regime stifles innovation. Rigid ownership structures prevent genuine artistic and technological progress, serving as a barrier that shields established powers while offering negligible benefits to individual creators. A central theme is the redefinition of generative AI, which synthesizes and recombines information in a manner analogous to human creativity. Treating this process as theft is presented as a misleading narrative designed by large technology firms to consolidate power. The text asserts that AI’s future must be open-source to ensure equitable development, contrasting this with a licensing model that would restrict innovation to only the wealthiest corporations capable of securing universal agreements. This perspective is crucial for the open_data community because it directly challenges proprietary data practices that hinder AI advancement. The article supports the idea that open knowledge and data are essential for cultural evolution, aligning with open_data principles that advocate for unrestricted access and collaboration. It calls for legal and structural reforms that prioritize the public good and shared learning over exclusive control, ensuring that technological tools remain accessible and beneficial to society as a whole.
