Producción del sector Pesca creció 23,51% durante enero de 2025 por anchoveta para harina y aceite de pescado

Producción del sector Pesca creció 23,51% durante enero de 2025 por anchoveta para harina y aceite de pescado

The primary conclusion of this economic report is that Peru’s productive sectors, particularly fishing and mining, exhibited significant divergent performance in early 2025, driven by shifting extraction priorities and energy dynamics. The fishing industry experienced a surge due to substantial increases in marine extraction for indirect consumption, contrasting with declines in direct-consumption segments. Similarly, while metal mining grew due to key minerals, hydrocarbon production contracted, although renewable energy generation accelerated, highlighting a structural transition in the energy mix. Regarding infrastructure and financial indicators, the data reveals increased public investment and credit activity, yet underlying consumption metrics such as cement usage remained sluggish. This suggests that while government spending and financial liquidity are rising, they have not yet fully stimulated private sector construction or broad-based economic dynamism. The sharp rise in imports further indicates strong demand for capital goods and raw materials, aligning with the investment-led growth pattern observed in public works. This article is relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of high-quality, timely, and disaggregated statistical information for effective policy analysis. It demonstrates how diverse datasets—from ecological extraction volumes to financial credit flows—must be integrated and accessible to stakeholders. By making such granular economic indicators openly available, researchers and citizens can better monitor sectoral trends, identify structural changes, and hold institutions accountable for economic management and resource allocation.
