Perú registró una inflación de 0,12% en febrero de 2025

Perú registró una inflación de 0,12% en febrero de 2025

The February 2025 consumer price index data reveals a nuanced economic landscape where specific sectors drive inflation while others experience deflation. Key increases were observed in recreation, education, and hospitality, largely due to rising costs in services and educational enrollment, particularly in non-state institutions. Conversely, prices for essential food items like fish and poultry declined, indicating a bifurcated trend where service-oriented and educational expenses outpaced the cost of certain basic goods. This divergence highlights how different household budgets are impacted unevenly, with discretionary spending areas facing upward pressure while staple protein costs stabilized or dropped. These findings are highly relevant to open data initiatives because they underscore the critical need for granular, transparent, and accessible price metrics. By providing detailed breakdowns across numerous product categories and geographic regions, institutions like the INEI enable researchers, policymakers, and the public to move beyond aggregate averages. This level of detail allows for the identification of specific inflationary drivers, such as the sharp rise in vegetable prices versus the drop in meat costs, facilitating more accurate economic modeling and resource allocation. Open data on these micro-trends ensures that stakeholders can scrutinize the real-world impact of inflation on diverse population segments. Ultimately, the release of such comprehensive statistical data fosters accountability and informed decision-making within the public sphere. It allows civil society to verify government claims regarding economic performance and ensures that monetary policies address the actual pain points experienced by consumers. The availability of this structured information supports the broader goal of data sovereignty, empowering citizens to understand the factors affecting their purchasing power. Consequently, open access to these indicators not only improves economic literacy but also strengthens the democratic process by making economic realities visible and analyzable for all.
