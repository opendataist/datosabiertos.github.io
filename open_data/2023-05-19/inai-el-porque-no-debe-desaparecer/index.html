INAI, el porqué no debe desaparecer

The article argues that the political attack on Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency and Access to Information (INAI) threatens democratic transparency and anti-corruption efforts. By suspending the institute’s operations and proposing its replacement by government bodies, the administration undermines the institutional independence required to check executive power. This maneuver represents a significant regression in the separation of powers, shifting control away from autonomous entities toward the executive branch. The INAI functions as a crucial component of the National Anti-Corruption System, specifically handling the right to information, transparency, and personal data protection. Removing this specific pillar would cripple the broader anti-corruption mechanism, as visibility is essential for preventing and sanctioning corrupt acts. Transferring these duties to existing government agencies would consolidate power and eliminate the necessary oversight that ensures accountability without political interference. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the vulnerability of information infrastructure when political will opposes transparency. The potential dismantling of an autonomous body responsible for data access signals a systemic risk to public accountability. It highlights how legislative and executive pressures can obstruct the principles of open government, demonstrating that sustainable open data ecosystems require robust legal protections against political interference.
