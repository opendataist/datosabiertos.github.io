The article highlights the tension between the moral criticisms leveled by some Paraguayan lawmakers against European Union financial cooperation and the reality of a crisis-ridden education system. Although certain political sectors unfoundedly argue that this support threatens traditional values, the truth is that millions of children live in poverty and many are out of school. Cutting off these non-repayable funds, which are earmarked for food, school kits, and infrastructure, exacerbates a social emergency that the very same lawmakers claim to defend, revealing a complete disconnect between their ideological stances and the public good. The political narrative reveals a deep contradiction, as several opponents of the agreement have been legally compelled to assume basic parental responsibilities, calling into question their moral legitimacy to dictate policies on family and education. This hypocrisy underscores how the budget debate has been instrumentalized to discredit the current government and gain political capital during electoral cycles, rather than addressing the real strategies needed to re-engage children in school. The result is a stagnation in resolving structural problems, where partisan interests prevail over the urgency of guaranteeing basic rights to children. This situation is highly relevant to the open data community because it illustrates how political opacity and misinformation can hinder access to essential public resources. By debating external funding in an atmosphere of false narratives, the traceability and effectiveness of public spending are obscured, making accountability more difficult. Open data are crucial here to debunk misinformation campaigns, empirically demonstrating the positive impact of international cooperation and compelling political actors to take real responsibility for the use of resources that improve critical social indicators.
