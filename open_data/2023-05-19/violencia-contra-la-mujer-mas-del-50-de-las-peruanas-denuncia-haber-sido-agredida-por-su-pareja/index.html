Violencia contra la mujer: más del 50% de las peruanas denuncia haber sido agredida por su pareja.

The article highlights that a majority of Peruvian women have experienced domestic violence, with psychological and verbal aggression being the most common forms. This persistent issue reveals critical gaps in social support systems, as many victims refrain from seeking help due to stigma, lack of knowledge, or the belief that intervention is unnecessary. These trends underscore the urgent need for improved reporting mechanisms and accessible resources to effectively protect vulnerable individuals. In the realm of public health, the data shows stable fertility rates alongside diverse contraceptive usage patterns. However, significant disparities persist regarding child nutrition, with malnutrition rates rising slightly and remaining disproportionately high in rural areas compared to urban centers. This highlights a persistent inequality in health outcomes based on geographic location, where rural communities face more severe nutritional challenges than their urban counterparts. These findings are relevant to open data because they demonstrate how standardized, publicly available statistics can uncover systemic social inequalities. By making this information accessible, policymakers and civil society can better analyze the root causes of violence and health disparities. Open data serves as a vital tool for driving evidence-based decisions, ensuring that interventions target the specific needs of marginalized populations and promote social justice through transparency.
