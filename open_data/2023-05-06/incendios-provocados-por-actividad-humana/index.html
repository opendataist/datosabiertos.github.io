Incendios provocados por actividad humana

Incendios provocados por actividad humana

Most forest fires in Mexico are human-caused, with agricultural burning being a primary driver. This practice not only degrades ecosystems but also imposes severe social costs, contributing significantly to disaster-related mortality and economic losses. Recognizing this, sustainable conservation agriculture offers a vital alternative by using crop residues to improve soil health rather than burning them. This shift helps mitigate climate change risks and drastically reduces fire hazards. By protecting the soil and optimizing resource use, farmers can enhance their resilience against the projected increase in devastating wildfires over the coming decades. Such simple, consistent actions on small and medium-sized farms directly protect lives and the environment. This article highlights the critical role of open data and transparency in environmental management. Accurate statistics from institutions such as INEGI and Semarnat inform evidence-based policies. Furthermore, sharing knowledge about sustainable practices empowers communities with accessible solutions, demonstrating how data-driven awareness and open collaboration are essential for effective disaster prevention and climate adaptation strategies.
