No olvides desactivar el WiFi de tu celular al salir de casa: estos son los peligros de no hacerlo

No olvides desactivar el WiFi de tu celular al salir de casa: estos son los peligros de no hacerlo

Disabling Wi-Fi on mobile devices when in public spaces is a critical cybersecurity measure that protects the integrity of personal data. Keeping this feature enabled exposes users to insecure networks, facilitating attacks such as "man-in-the-middle" attacks, where criminals intercept sensitive information like passwords and financial data, and even install malware. This basic practice not only prevents identity theft but also location tracking, since the constant search for networks emits signals that can be exploited for commercial or malicious purposes. In addition to security risks, the impact on device performance is significant. The continuous activity of the connection chip consumes internal resources that could be allocated to other functions, affecting the browsing experience and causing interference with services such as GPS. Moreover, the constant effort to identify available networks drains the battery unnecessarily, which is crucial in contexts where access to charging is limited, thereby optimizing both the hardware's lifespan and the device's autonomy. This article is relevant to the open_data movement because it highlights user sovereignty and security over their own information. By revealing how devices surrender location and traffic data without explicit consent, it illustrates the need for transparency in connectivity technologies. Raising awareness about these vulnerabilities empowers individuals to take control of their privacy, aligning with the principles of open and responsible data protection, where personal information should not be a non-negotiable byproduct of everyday connectivity.
