Coahuila: Censo y vigilancia sanitaria ‘recortan’ de 450 a 250 los anexos en el Estado

Coahuila: Censo y vigilancia sanitaria ‘recortan’ de 450 a 250 los anexos en el Estado

A recent census in Coahuila revealed a significant reduction in the number of unregulated addiction residential centers, commonly known as "anexos." By cross-referencing the initial registry with municipal and state agencies, authorities determined that nearly half of the reported facilities were no longer operational, had relocated, or never existed. This rigorous verification process refined the official registry from approximately 450 entries to around 250 active centers, highlighting the prevalence of informal operations that had previously escaped strict regulatory oversight. The article emphasizes that this regulatory tightening is a direct response to historical issues of abuse and lack of compliance within these institutions. Authorities have shifted their focus toward enforcing mandatory health and human rights standards, ensuring that remaining centers meet specific operational requirements. The government warns that non-compliance will result in immediate closure, aiming to eliminate the impunity that previously allowed these centers to operate with minimal supervision and frequent reports of misconduct against interned individuals. This case is highly relevant to open data as it demonstrates the critical importance of accurate, verifiable public registries for effective governance and public safety. It illustrates how raw, unverified data can obscure the true scale of a social issue, whereas systematic data validation enables targeted policy interventions. By exposing the gap between declared and actual facilities, the article underscores that open data initiatives must prioritize verification mechanisms to ensure transparency, accountability, and the protection of vulnerable populations in specialized care sectors.
