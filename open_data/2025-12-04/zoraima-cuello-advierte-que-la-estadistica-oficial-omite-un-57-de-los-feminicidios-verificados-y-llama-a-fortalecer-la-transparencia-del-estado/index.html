Zoraima Cuello advierte que la estadística oficial omite un 57% de los feminicidios verificados y llama a fortalecer la transparencia del Estado

Zoraima Cuello advierte que la estadística oficial omite un 57% de los feminicidios verificados y llama a fortalecer la transparencia del Estado

The article highlights a critical statistical gap in the Dominican Republic, particularly regarding femicide data, where official records significantly underreport actual cases compared to verified media sources. This discrepancy reveals a structural issue beyond mere errors, indicating that nearly forty percent of these tragedies are omitted from national statistics. Consequently, the state’s ability to protect lives and design effective public policies is severely compromised by the lack of accurate, up-to-date information. Furthermore, this data vacuum extends to other essential sectors, such as water quality and public safety, which have lacked reliable reporting for over two years. The absence of comprehensive data means that national decisions are being made without the necessary evidence, undermining transparency and weakening public trust. This situation illustrates how poor data management directly impacts governance, resource allocation, and the capacity to respond to urgent social problems. This report is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the vital link between transparent, accessible, and accurate data and effective governance. It argues that modernizing public information systems to meet international standards is essential for creating a responsive state. By advocating for data that is verified and consistent, the article emphasizes that open data is not just a technical requirement but a fundamental tool for justice, efficiency, and democratic accountability.
