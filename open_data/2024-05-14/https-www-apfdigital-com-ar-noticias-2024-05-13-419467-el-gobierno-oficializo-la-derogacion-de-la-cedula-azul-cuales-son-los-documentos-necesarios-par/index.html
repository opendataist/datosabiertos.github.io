The primary conclusion is that original or duplicate vehicle identification cards in Buenos Aires no longer expire, remaining valid indefinitely unless ownership changes. This regulatory shift eliminates the need for periodic renewals for existing documents, streamlining administrative processes for vehicle owners. Consequently, the previously mandatory blue card for third-party drivers has been abolished, as its function is now unnecessary given the permanent validity of the green card. This change reduces bureaucratic hurdles and simplifies legal driving requirements for both owners and authorized non-owners. Furthermore, the issuance of these documents is now accessible through both electronic and physical channels, enhancing convenience. Digital versions are automatically available via the Mi Argentina app, while physical copies remain part of the registry and can be collected if desired. This dual availability ensures flexibility for citizens, allowing them to choose the method that best suits their needs without compromising the document’s legal standing. Existing green and blue cards retain their validity until owners voluntarily revoke them, ensuring a smooth transition for all stakeholders. This article is relevant to open data as it highlights the digital transformation of government services. By making vehicle identification data instantly accessible through digital profiles, the state promotes transparency and interoperability. Such moves encourage the integration of public services into unified digital platforms, fostering a culture where citizens can easily access, verify, and manage official records. This efficiency reduces reliance on physical paperwork and supports broader open data initiatives that prioritize user-friendly, real-time information access.
