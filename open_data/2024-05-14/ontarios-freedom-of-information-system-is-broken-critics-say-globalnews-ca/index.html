Ontario’s freedom of information system is ‘broken,’ critics say | Globalnews.ca

Ontario’s freedom of information system is under intense scrutiny after two government ministries provided contradictory responses to identical requests regarding critical healthcare staffing data. While one ministry withheld specific numbers, citing potential economic harm, another ministry released the same information to a different media outlet. This inconsistency, occurring despite shared administrative teams, highlights significant flaws in how transparency rules are applied and suggests the system may be used to obscure essential public knowledge rather than facilitate it. The conflicting decisions underscore the tension between government secrecy and public accountability in managing critical services. The withheld data revealed severe and growing shortages of nurses and personal support workers across the province, figures that the adjudicator acknowledged were of compelling public interest. The fact that these stark realities were protected from one outlet while being revealed to another raises serious questions about the integrity of the review process and whether privacy claims are being leveraged to hide the true state of the healthcare crisis from citizens. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates how opaque bureaucratic practices can prevent the public from accessing vital, high-level strategic information. When governments withhold aggregated, non-sensitive data by claiming vague economic harms, they undermine the core principle that public records should be accessible. For open data to function effectively, there must be consistent, transparent criteria for release, ensuring that citizens can hold their government accountable with the same information available to journalists.
