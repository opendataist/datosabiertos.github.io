Bolivia 2024: PIB nominal de $us. 46.967 millones, crecimiento económico de 0,73%, el 3ro más bajo en los últimos 44 años

Bolivia 2024: PIB nominal de $us. 46.967 millones, crecimiento económico de 0,73%, el 3ro más bajo en los últimos 44 años

Bolivia’s economy suffered its worst performance in decades, with 2024 GDP growth significantly falling short of both government targets and international forecasts. This stagnation underscores a critical failure in economic management, where persistent fiscal deficits and monetary expansion have driven high inflation rather than promoting genuine development. The data reveal that the state’s reliance on public credit to finance current expenditures has created a fragile financial structure, pushing the country toward a dangerous pattern of stagflation. The discrepancy between official statistics and actual economic reality highlights the urgent need for transparent, accessible open data. When government projections consistently diverge sharply from observed outcomes—as evidenced by large gaps in growth, inflation, and deficit figures—public trust erodes and accountability becomes unattainable. Reliable, open data is essential not only for verifying these macroeconomic indicators but also for enabling independent analysis that can identify structural flaws in the current rentier model before a full-blown recession takes hold. Addressing these challenges requires moving beyond superficial adjustments toward deep structural reforms. However, such changes cannot be effectively designed without precise, verifiable information. Open data initiatives play a pivotal role in this process by allowing economists and citizens to scrutinize the true impact of policies, such as the relationship between monetary emission and inflation. Consequently, ensuring the availability and integrity of economic datasets is crucial for diagnosing the root causes of Bolivia’s financial dependency and guiding the multidimensional policies needed to stabilize the economy.
