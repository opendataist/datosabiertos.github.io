Mapa de las basura de verano: Baleares es el territorio que más residuos por persona genera en España

The article highlights the critical lack of transparency in local waste management data across Mallorca’s municipalities, making it difficult to obtain a unified view of the island’s waste generation. However, because all non-recycled waste eventually ends up in the Son Reus incinerator, this facility serves as a central hub for measuring environmental impact. This centralization allows for a more accurate assessment of waste trends, particularly in this major tourist destination, revealing significant disparities in how different towns handle their seasonal waste spikes. The core finding is that door-to-door waste collection is the most effective strategy for reducing non-recyclable waste and boosting recycling rates. Municipalities like Felanitx and Sa Pobla achieved dramatic reductions in incinerated waste by implementing this model, proving that the improvement is not merely seasonal behavior but a result of successful policy. Conversely, larger cities like Palma continue to lead in waste incineration due to the limited scope of these initiatives, failing to meet upcoming European recycling targets. This report is vital for open data because it exposes the fragmentation and opacity of municipal records while demonstrating how centralized, accessible datasets from a single facility can drive public accountability. By making incineration metrics transparent, stakeholders can identify best practices and pressure underperforming administrations to adopt effective waste reduction strategies, ensuring that open data translates into tangible environmental progress.
