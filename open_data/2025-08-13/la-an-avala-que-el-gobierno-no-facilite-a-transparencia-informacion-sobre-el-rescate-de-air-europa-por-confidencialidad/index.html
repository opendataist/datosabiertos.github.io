La AN avala que el Gobierno no facilite a Transparencia información sobre el rescate de Air Europa por confidencialidad

La AN avala que el Gobierno no facilite a Transparencia información sobre el rescate de Air Europa por confidencialidad

The Spanish National Court has ruled that specific financial regulations take precedence over general transparency laws when disclosing details about public bailouts, such as those provided to Air Europa. By rejecting the Council for Transparency’s appeal, the court affirmed that the government’s obligation to maintain confidentiality regarding reserved strategic funds overrides citizens’ right to access this information. This decision highlights a legal hierarchy in which specialized sectoral rules can effectively limit the scope of broader transparency obligations. The ruling establishes that when a specific law explicitly defines information as confidential and outlines strict exceptions, there is no room for balancing public interest against state secrecy. Consequently, the general framework for government transparency does not apply if the specialized legislation already contains a complete regulatory regime governing access limits. This creates a significant precedent whereby specific statutory confidentiality clauses can permanently shield sensitive financial transactions from public scrutiny, regardless of the perceived importance of the underlying data. This case is crucial for open data advocacy because it illustrates how legislative specificity can be used to restrict data accessibility in the name of economic protection. It underscores the tension between transparent governance and the need for confidential financial interventions. For those working in the open data sector, this ruling serves as a critical reminder that legal exceptions can override transparency principles, potentially limiting the availability of high-impact public financial data for research and civic oversight.
