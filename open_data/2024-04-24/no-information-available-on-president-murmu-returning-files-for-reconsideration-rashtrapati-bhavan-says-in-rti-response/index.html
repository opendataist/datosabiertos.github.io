No information available on President Murmu returning files for reconsideration, Rashtrapati Bhavan says in RTI response

The Rashtrapati Bhavan recently confirmed that it holds no records of President Droupadi Murmu returning decisions by the Prime Minister or Council of Ministers for reconsideration. This response, provided under the Right to Information Act, highlights a significant gap in transparency regarding the executive powers of the President. The lack of accessible data raises questions about the operational history and constitutional checks exercised by the highest office in India. Critics argue that this absence of information is incomplete and misleading, potentially obscuring the mechanisms of accountability and transparency essential for democratic governance. By stating that no such data exists, the President’s Secretariat fails to provide clarity on how often the president has utilized their statutory powers to seek reconsideration. This opacity prevents citizens and scholars from fully understanding the interplay between the executive and the presidency. This incident is relevant to open data advocates as it demonstrates institutional resistance to sharing vital governmental records. When public authorities claim non-existence of data without providing justification or alternative access routes, it undermines the principles of open governance. Ensuring that records of constitutional actions are available and accessible is crucial for maintaining public trust and enabling meaningful scrutiny of state power.
