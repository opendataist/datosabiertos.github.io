Adobe unveils AI features for Photoshop — but not everyone is happy about it

Adobe’s recent update to Photoshop introduces powerful generative AI tools that lower barriers for novice users by enabling image creation and editing through simple text prompts. While these features democratize content creation, they have sparked significant backlash from creative professionals who view them as a threat to their livelihoods. The controversy centers on whether these tools respect intellectual property rights or effectively replace human artistic effort with automated replication. The core controversy involves Adobe’s claim that its Firefly model is trained exclusively on licensed and public domain data, ensuring it is safe for commercial use. However, reports indicate the training set includes AI-generated images derived from other platforms, raising questions about the ethical sourcing of data. This contradiction highlights the broader industry struggle to define ownership and compensation in an era where generative AI models are built upon vast, often opaque, datasets that may include copyrighted or derivative works. This situation is critical for open data discourse because it exposes the hidden complexities of AI training data transparency. It demonstrates how claims of ethical data sourcing can obscure the reality of using derivative or user-contributed content without clear attribution or compensation mechanisms. For the open data community, this case serves as a cautionary tale about the need for rigorous standards in data provenance, ensuring that innovation does not come at the expense of creator rights and data integrity.
