Fomentar la lectura: Las bibliotecas como centros de aprendizaje

The article reveals a critical gap in Peru’s reading culture, where less than half of the population reads regularly and access to physical or digital libraries is marginal. This reality underscores that promoting reading is not merely a matter of material availability, but also of fostering reading habits and ensuring equitable access, particularly affecting children and young people in low-income areas. Low attendance at cultural institutions indicates that current state initiatives have failed to embed reading as a deeply rooted social habit. It is argued that libraries should be viewed as dynamic, accessible learning centers for all, rather than simple book repositories. Inadequate management and the lack of libraries in many public and private schools hinder the development of reading habits from childhood. Therefore, strong political will—potentially formalized through legislation—is needed to ensure that every school has adequate spaces, guaranteeing that access to knowledge does not depend on students’ socioeconomic status. Finally, the text questions the effectiveness of reducing book prices as the primary solution, asserting that the State’s role should be to guarantee physical and cultural access rather than intervene in costs. For the open data community, this report is relevant because it highlights the need for accurate data on library infrastructure and reading habits to design evidence-based public policies. Transparency in information about which schools have functional libraries and who accesses them is crucial to closing the identified digital and social gaps.
