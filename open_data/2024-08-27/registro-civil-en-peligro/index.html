¿Registro civil en peligro?

The article alleges widespread falsification of Dominican nationality by Haitian mothers, claiming that most births are fraudulently registered as Dominican to circumvent legal requirements for foreign registration. This suggests systemic complicity within maternity facilities and civil registry institutions, undermining the integrity of the national identity system and potentially violating constitutional mandates for tracking foreign nationals. The narrative implies that these actions facilitate the permanent settlement of undocumented populations and strain public services, particularly education, by prioritizing the enrollment of children who may lack proper documentation. This phenomenon is framed as a coordinated effort, allegedly supported by international organizations, aimed at demographic transformation rather than temporary migration, thereby threatening the country’s social and cultural fabric. This report is relevant to open data because it highlights critical failures in public record-keeping and birth registration processes. It underscores the urgent need for transparent, accurate, and verifiable civil databases to ensure legal compliance and national security. The allegations point to potential corruption and data manipulation, emphasizing that open and auditable government data systems are essential for detecting irregularities and maintaining public trust in institutional governance.
