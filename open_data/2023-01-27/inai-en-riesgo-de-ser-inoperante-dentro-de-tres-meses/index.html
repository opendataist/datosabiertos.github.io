‘Inai, en riesgo de ser inoperante dentro de tres meses’

The National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) faces imminent legal paralysis due to a critical shortage of commissioners, which threatens the fundamental mechanisms of accountability and personal data protection in Mexico. With the upcoming expiration of a term, only four members will remain, below the legally required minimum, causing the institute to lose its ability to deliberate or issue binding resolutions. This potential inoperability underscores how institutional integrity directly depends on the proper staffing of oversight bodies to function effectively. The implications extend beyond administrative delays, as the INAI’s inactivity would halt its role in exposing corruption and ensuring transparency in significant public scandals. Without the capacity to review information requests or impose sanctions for non-compliance, the checks and balances necessary to combat impunity and protect citizens’ rights would be severely weakened. This highlights the vital function of independent agencies in maintaining public trust and enforcing legal standards regarding access to information. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates that technical frameworks for transparency are ineffective without robust, legally empowered institutions to enforce them. The crisis illustrates that sustainable open data ecosystems require not just policy frameworks, but active, fully constituted regulatory bodies capable of safeguarding data rights and holding power accountable. Preventing such institutional failures is essential for maintaining a transparent and trustworthy information environment.
