INAI pide a AMLO hablar de sus prestaciones antes de señalar a otros

Francisco Javier Acuña Llamas, commissioner of the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), refuted the president’s claims regarding the institute’s salaries, clarifying that the actual compensation is significantly lower than the alleged 300,000 pesos mentioned. This disagreement underscores the tension between the executive branch and autonomous guarantee bodies, challenging the official narrative on equitable remuneration in public service. The controversy extends to supplementary benefits, which the commissioner argued are part of the total cost of employing officials. When these benefits are included, the actual compensation goes beyond the debate over base salary, highlighting the complexity of comparing incomes across different branches of government. This discussion reflects a broader debate on financial transparency and the valuation of work in institutions that oversee access to information. This article is relevant to open data because it exemplifies the importance of verifiable data in public scrutiny. Transparency not only entails access to information but also clarity regarding the true costs of administration. By demonstrating that official figures can be misinterpreted or politically manipulated, it reinforces the need for open, accurate, and contextual data to ensure accountability and combat misinformation in government management.
