The article highlights a significant surge in rural property transactions, driven by a shift in investment strategies in which institutional capital and high-net-worth individuals view farmland as a stable refuge against inflation and financial instability. This trend marks a departure from traditional urban real estate, as investors seek tangible assets that offer both security and consistent, albeit slow, revaluation. The demand is fueled by the concept of "agribusiness," where professional investment in agriculture and livestock meets the need for food security and diversification of national wealth. Concurrently, individual buyers are increasingly motivated by lifestyle changes, such as telework and a desire for self-sufficiency through personal gardens. This demographic shift transforms rural land into a dual-purpose asset, serving both as a recreational retreat and a productive agricultural resource. The changing preferences reflect a broader societal value placed on nature and health, reinforcing the idea that land ownership is becoming essential for maintaining a connection to sustainable living and local food production. This data is relevant to open_data because it underscores the critical role of public statistical frameworks, such as those provided by the INE, in tracking economic shifts in tangible asset markets. Transparent, high-quality datasets enable experts and policymakers to monitor investment flows, understand market volatility, and analyze the socioeconomic impact of land ownership. By making these transaction figures publicly accessible, authorities facilitate informed decision-making for investors and help define the evolving role of agricultural land in the modern economy, linking economic data directly to broader themes of sustainability and resource management.
