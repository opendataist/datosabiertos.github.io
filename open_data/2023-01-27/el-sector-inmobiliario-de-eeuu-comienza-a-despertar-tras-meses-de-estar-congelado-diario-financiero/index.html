El sector inmobiliario de EEUU comienza a despertar tras meses de estar congelado | Diario Financiero

U.S. mortgage rates have declined for three consecutive weeks, showing early signs of thawing the housing market stagnation caused by previous high borrowing costs. Although rates remain elevated compared to last year, this downward trend is encouraging increased buyer activity, particularly among first-time purchasers who are responsive to these cost reductions. Consequently, new home sales have risen for the third straight month, surpassing economic expectations and signaling a potential recovery in consumer confidence. Builders are also reporting improved sentiment and optimistic outlooks for future demand, suggesting that the sector is beginning to overcome the significant headwinds that characterized the previous year’s disappointing performance. This development is relevant to open data because housing market health relies on the transparent, timely publication of mortgage rates and sales statistics. Such accessible data allows policymakers and analysts to track monetary policy impacts accurately, ensuring that economic indicators reflect real-time shifts in affordability and market dynamics for public scrutiny.
