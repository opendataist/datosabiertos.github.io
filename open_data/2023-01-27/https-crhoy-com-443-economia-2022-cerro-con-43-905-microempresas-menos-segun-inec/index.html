The article reveals that Costa Rica’s microenterprise sector faces significant operational pressures from external economic factors, particularly fluctuating fuel and raw material prices, alongside a decline in customer traffic. This vulnerability is compounded by the fact that nearly half of these businesses were born out of necessity rather than strategic opportunity. This context highlights the fragility of the informal economy, which constitutes a substantial portion of national employment yet lacks the resilience and formal support structures often required to withstand market shocks. Structurally, these enterprises are predominantly small, single-person operations led by individuals with limited formal education, predominantly men aged between 35 and 64. The vast majority operate without formal accounting records or legal registration, indicating a high degree of informality across all sectors. This lack of formalization suggests that while these businesses are crucial for job creation, they exist largely outside the regulatory and financial safety nets, making them invisible to standard economic monitoring and policy interventions. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the critical need for improved statistical transparency in the informal sector. Open data platforms can play a pivotal role in aggregating and visualizing these hidden economic activities, fostering better-informed public policy. By making this information accessible, stakeholders can better understand the true scale of the microenterprise ecosystem and design more effective support systems for entrepreneurs who are essential to the national economy but currently marginalized in official metrics.
