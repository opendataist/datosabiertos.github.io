“No sabemos donde vamos a llegar con esto”: quienes trabajan con anchoas o rabas se las ingenian para capear la crisis de precios del aceite - El Faradio | Periodismo que cuenta

The article highlights a drastic surge in olive oil prices, which has become economically prohibitive for both households and businesses in Cantabria. This inflation poses a significant threat to local industries that rely heavily on olive oil, such as hospitality and traditional food preservation. For restaurants and manufacturers, the cost spike disrupts operational budgets and forces a reevaluation of how essential ingredients are managed during production. In response to these financial pressures, establishments are adopting stricter conservation practices to maximize the lifespan and utility of their supplies. Hospitality workers report adjusting serving styles and maintenance routines to prevent waste, treating oil as a scarce resource that requires careful handling. These adaptive measures reflect a broader shift towards sustainability and cost-efficiency, as businesses attempt to maintain quality standards despite rising input costs and market uncertainty. This situation is highly relevant to open data because transparent, accessible price indices from institutions like the INE are crucial for monitoring economic trends and supply chain vulnerabilities. Open data enables stakeholders to analyze the impact of inflation on specific sectors, such as regional gastronomy, and informs policy decisions regarding consumer protection and industrial stability. Without such data, understanding the real-world implications of market fluctuations remains difficult.
