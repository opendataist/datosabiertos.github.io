A New Method to Evaluate the Performance of Models Trained with Synthetic Data When They are Applied to Real-World Data

This research addresses the critical tension in financial modeling between the need for robust credit scoring data and the imperative to protect borrower privacy. By leveraging generative models to create synthetic datasets, institutions can conduct necessary risk assessments without exposing sensitive personal information. This approach offers a viable alternative to traditional data sharing, enabling continuous innovation in financial technology while adhering to strict privacy regulations. The study evaluates the practical efficacy of applying creditworthiness models trained on this synthetic data to real-world scenarios. It highlights that while modern generative techniques like TVAE can effectively mimic real-world distribution and feature relationships, they introduce a measurable performance cost. Specifically, there is a noticeable reduction in predictive power when models rely on synthetic inputs rather than authentic historical records, indicating a trade-off between data accessibility and model precision. This finding is highly relevant to the open data community as it establishes a realistic baseline for privacy-preserving analytics. It demonstrates that while synthetic data facilitates open research and collaboration by removing barriers to sensitive datasets, it is not a perfect substitute for raw data. Understanding this accuracy deficit is essential for developers and researchers building open-source financial tools, as it necessitates careful consideration of the limitations inherent in privacy-preserving data generation strategies.
