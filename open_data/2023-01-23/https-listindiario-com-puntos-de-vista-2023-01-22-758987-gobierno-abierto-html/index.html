The Dominican Republic has launched its Fifth Action Plan under the Open Government Partnership, committing to ten specific obligations. This move marks a significant paradigm shift in public management, emphasizing transparency, civic participation, collaboration, accountability, integrity, and the use of technology. By joining this multilateral initiative, the country aims to enhance governmental performance and responsiveness, fostering sustainable public policies that improve citizens' quality of life and rebuild trust in state institutions. The plan promotes a collaborative and participatory governance model in which citizens and private entities work alongside the public administration to achieve common goals. It ensures that public information is accessible for monitoring and encourages active citizen involvement in shaping and implementing public policies. These elements are crucial for leveraging collective knowledge and experience, thereby strengthening the democratic process and ensuring that public resources are managed in the general interest. This development is highly relevant to open data, as the success of such government open initiatives fundamentally relies on the availability, accessibility, and usability of public information. Without robust open data practices, the promises of transparency, accountability, and citizen collaboration cannot be fully realized or effectively monitored by society.
