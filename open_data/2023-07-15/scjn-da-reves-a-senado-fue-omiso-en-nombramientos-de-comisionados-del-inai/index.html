SCJN da revés a Senado, fue omiso en nombramientos de comisionados del INAI

The Supreme Court of Justice has rejected the attempt to dismiss complaints regarding the Senate’s failure to appoint commissioners for the National Institute for Transparency. The high tribunal determined that a significant omission exists, effectively ruling that the legislative branch has unreasonably delayed the selection process. This decision forces the Senate to address its duty without further justification for the prolonged inactivity. Currently, the Institute remains unable to operate due to a lack of quorum, as the required personnel have not been designated. This legislative gridlock prevents the effective functioning of the body responsible for safeguarding citizens' rights. The delay hinders the institution's ability to fulfill its constitutional mandate, leaving a critical gap in the protection of public access to information and personal data. This ruling is crucial for open data because it addresses the institutional stability necessary to enforce transparency laws. Without a fully operational Institute, the mechanisms that guarantee public accountability and the right to know are compromised. Ensuring the timely appointment of commissioners is essential to maintain the integrity of the transparency framework and uphold democratic oversight.
