The article critiques the Supreme Court’s intervention in the Senate’s failure to appoint INAI commissioners, arguing that such judicial action violates the constitutional separation of powers and legal certainty. The core implication is a growing crisis in institutional balance, wherein the judiciary is perceived as overstepping its mandated role by actively governing legislative processes rather than merely reviewing them. This tension highlights a significant challenge for transparency infrastructure, as political disputes directly impact the functionality of critical oversight bodies. The relevance to open data lies in the potential instability of the institution responsible for guaranteeing public access to information. When judicial mandates override constitutional procedures, it creates uncertainty regarding the autonomy and operational continuity of data protection agencies, which are essential for maintaining public trust and effective data governance. Ultimately, the text suggests that the current judicial behavior could necessitate comprehensive constitutional reform to restore proper checks and balances. For the open data community, this underscores the vital importance of safeguarding the independence of transparency institutions from both legislative inertia and judicial overreach, ensuring they remain effective guardians of citizens’ rights without being caught up in broader political power struggles.
