La compraventa de viviendas modera su caída en mayo al 6,4% pero ya suma cuatro meses de descensos

Housing transactions in Spain fell year-on-year in May, extending a four-month streak of contraction. Although the overall market retreated, the decline was more moderate than in previous months, suggesting a potential stabilization rather than a sharp downturn. This development underscores the sector’s sensitivity to broader economic fluctuations and indicates that recent volatility may be easing. Regionally, performance varied considerably: Andalusia and Valencia led in activity, while areas such as Madrid and the Canary Islands experienced sharper declines. Urban property sales dropped more noticeably than rural ones, and the composition of transfers shifted, with donations decreasing while inheritances and swaps increased. This geographic and typological divergence highlights the crucial role of local market dynamics in shaping current real estate trends. This data is essential for open-data initiatives, as it provides transparent, granular insights into the national economy. By analyzing these detailed transaction patterns, researchers and policymakers can better assess market resilience and regional disparities. Access to such precise statistical information supports evidence-based decision-making, fostering greater accountability and a deeper understanding of real estate dynamics in public discourse.
