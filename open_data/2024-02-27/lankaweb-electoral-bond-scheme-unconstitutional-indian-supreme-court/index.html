LankaWeb – Electoral Bond Scheme 'unconstitutional'. Indian Supreme Court

The Supreme Court’s recent decision to strike down the Electoral Bond Scheme marks a pivotal moment for electoral transparency, declaring the mechanism unconstitutional due its violation of citizens' right to information. By ruling that anonymous political funding undermines participatory democracy and creates opportunities for quid pro quo arrangements, the judgment reinforces the principle that voters deserve to know who funds political narratives. This ruling prioritizes public accountability over donor privacy, establishing that the potential for corruption outweighs the state’s interest in shielding financial contributors. The implications extend beyond mere procedural correction, as the court has directed immediate disclosure of all past donations by the State Bank of India. This mandate ensures that the historical record of political financing becomes accessible to the public, allowing for scrutiny of potential undue influence from corporate entities. The decision dismantles the legal framework that previously allowed anonymous, large-scale contributions, thereby closing a significant loophole that facilitated opaque economic support for political agendas. This verdict is highly relevant to open data because it transforms previously restricted financial flows into public information, enhancing the integrity of democratic data ecosystems. It sets a precedent that transparency in political finance is a fundamental right, encouraging the creation of open databases for electoral funding. Consequently, this promotes greater civic engagement and informed voting, as citizens can now access critical data regarding the financial backing of political parties, strengthening democratic oversight.
