El 9,3% de los hogares de Castilla-La Mancha admite que llegó "con mucha dificultad" a fin de mes durante 2023

The 2023 survey data highlights a growing economic fragility across Spain, characterized by rising inflation and high interest rates that have significantly eroded household purchasing power. A central finding is the record increase in the number of families unable to maintain adequate home temperatures, signaling a severe energy poverty crisis. This issue is compounded by a decline in the ability to cover unexpected expenses and meet basic nutritional needs, indicating that financial stress is no longer limited to specific vulnerable groups but is becoming widespread. Regional disparities further illustrate the depth of this crisis, with Andalusia, Extremadura, and Castile-La Mancha reporting some of the highest difficulties in meeting monthly expenses and affording essential goods. While average income levels have technically reached historic highs, this improvement is offset by the soaring cost of living and reduced disposable income. The data reveals that despite higher nominal wages, a significant portion of the population faces material deprivation and social exclusion, creating a stark contrast between statistical income growth and actual living standards. This analysis is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and granular socioeconomic statistics for effective policy-making. Openly available datasets allow researchers, journalists, and civil society to verify official narratives, identify regional inequalities, and hold institutions accountable for addressing poverty. By democratizing access to such critical information, open data facilitates a deeper public understanding of economic trends and promotes more informed debates on social welfare and resource distribution.
