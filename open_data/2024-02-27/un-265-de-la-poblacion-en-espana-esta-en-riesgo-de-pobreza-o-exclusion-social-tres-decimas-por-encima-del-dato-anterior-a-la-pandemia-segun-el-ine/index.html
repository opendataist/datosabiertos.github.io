Un 26,5% de la población en España está en riesgo de pobreza o exclusión social, tres décimas por encima del dato anterior a la pandemia, según el INE

The recent publication of the Living Conditions Survey by Spain’s National Statistics Institute reveals a nuanced picture of social welfare, indicating that while overall poverty risks have decreased, severe material deprivation has worsened. This shift highlights a complex economic reality where rising average incomes have not uniformly translated into reduced hardship for the most vulnerable segments of the population, particularly children. The divergence between general income statistics and specific exclusion metrics suggests that economic recovery is unevenly distributed across different social groups. A critical area of concern is the significant rise in social exclusion among minors, marking a troubling trend for future generations. While older demographics experienced a slight improvement in their circumstances, the increasing precarity faced by younger citizens underscores a growing intergenerational disparity. This pattern implies that current policy measures may be failing to adequately protect youth from the adverse effects of inflation and cost-of-living pressures, necessitating targeted interventions to prevent long-term societal fragmentation. This data is particularly relevant to the open data community as it demonstrates the value of standardized, comparable European indicators like AROPE in tracking multidimensional poverty. By making granular, regional, and demographic statistics publicly accessible, open data initiatives enable rigorous analysis of policy effectiveness and highlight local disparities. Such transparency empowers researchers and advocates to identify specific geographic areas and populations in crisis, fostering data-driven decisions that address not just income levels, but the full spectrum of social exclusion.
