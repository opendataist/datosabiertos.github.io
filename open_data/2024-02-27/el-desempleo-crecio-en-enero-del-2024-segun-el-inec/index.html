El desempleo creció en enero del 2024 según el INEC

The article reveals that while Ecuador’s unemployment rate showed a negligible increase, significant structural disparities persist within the labor market. Gender and geography remain critical determinants of economic stability, as women and rural residents face disproportionately higher unemployment and lower access to adequate employment compared to their male and urban counterparts. This highlights systemic inequalities that statistical aggregates often obscure. Furthermore, the predominance of informal employment underscores widespread job insecurity, with the majority of workers lacking formal protections. The data indicates that only a minority of the workforce enjoys adequate employment conditions, revealing a gap between nominal job availability and quality of life. These nuances are vital for understanding the true health of the economy beyond headline numbers. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how disaggregated, transparent statistics can expose hidden social inequities that are masked in broad averages. By making detailed indicators on gender, region, and employment quality publicly available, policymakers and civil society can identify specific vulnerable groups. This transparency fosters evidence-based advocacy for targeted labor reforms, ensuring that data serves not just as a metric, but as a tool for social justice and inclusive development.
