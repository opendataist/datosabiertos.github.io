Alerta banca por campañas ‘agresivas’ para defraudar

Mexican financial institutions are facing an escalating wave of sophisticated digital fraud targeting users of internet banking. Criminals are employing increasingly complex social engineering tactics to extract sensitive credentials, including passwords, security tokens, and personal contact information, often by disguising these requests as necessary security updates or authentication steps. This surge in aggression highlights the vulnerabilities created by the widespread adoption of mobile and digital payment systems, which have inadvertently expanded the attack surface for cybercriminals. The scale of this threat is underscored by significant statistical increases in both consumer complaints and cyberattacks across the region. Financial fraud currently accounts for a substantial portion of banking-related disputes, with Mexico emerging as one of the most affected countries in Latin America. The trend points toward a maturation of criminal operations that leverage automation and potentially artificial intelligence to generate convincing fake communications and applications, making it increasingly difficult for the average user to distinguish between legitimate institutional channels and fraudulent impersonations. This situation is critically relevant to open data discourse, as it illustrates the high stakes of data security within the financial ecosystem. The proliferation of digital banking services relies on the secure exchange and protection of personal information, yet current vulnerabilities expose the fragility of these open channels when subjected to malicious intent. Addressing this challenge requires not only robust technological defenses from financial institutions but also widespread data literacy among consumers, emphasizing that safeguarding open data flows is essential for maintaining trust in digital financial infrastructure.
