According to INEGI, a Mexican lives an average of 3,961 weeks, of which 1,320 are spent sleeping, 557 working, and the leading causes of death are heart disease, diabetes mellitus, and malignant tumors.

The article transforms Mexico’s projected life expectancy of 75.9 years into a weekly metric to visualize the temporal distribution of human existence. This approach reveals that, under hypothetical assumptions about rest and work activity, time is divided among sleeping, working, and dedicating space to other essential activities such as personal care and social interaction. The quantitative perspective highlights that a significant portion of life is not spent on economic production, inviting reflection on the quality of the remaining time. Marked demographic differences are evident, particularly between genders, with women gaining more than six years of average life expectancy compared to men. Furthermore, the analysis identifies chronic diseases and health-related conditions as the leading causes of mortality, followed by external factors such as accidents. These data allow us to assess how sleep habits and working conditions directly impact the life experience and public health in the long term. This information is relevant to open data initiatives by demonstrating the potential to transform raw demographic indicators into comprehensible narratives about time use. By making INEGI’s projections accessible and relatable, it facilitates transparency and public understanding of the structure of life, promoting a deeper interpretation of statistical data beyond mere figures, with a focus on their social and well-being implications.
