Records show what books parents want removed from school libraries - Valley News

Records show what books parents want removed from school libraries - Valley News

Recent records reveal a surge in parental attempts to remove books from New Hampshire school libraries, particularly those featuring LGBTQ+ themes and explicit content. Critics argue that materials like George M. Johnson’s “All Boys Aren’t Blue” and “This Book Is Gay” are inappropriate for minors, citing sexual explicitness and mature subjects. However, educators and librarians maintain that these works are essential for providing diverse perspectives and affirming identities for queer students, resisting censorship despite intense local and national pressure. This trend reflects a broader conflict between parental oversight and academic freedom, underscored by failed legislative efforts to streamline book removal processes. Although governors vetoed bills that would have made it easier for parents to challenge materials, the volume of complaints remains high across various districts. The persistence of these challenges highlights the tension between protecting children from potentially disturbing content and preserving the integrity of educational resources that explore complex social and personal realities. These disputes are highly relevant to open data initiatives because the publication of such records demonstrates the critical role of public transparency in accountability. By leveraging public records requests, journalists have exposed the specific objections and institutional responses that often remain hidden from public view. This access to information allows communities to scrutinize local decision-making, ensuring that library policies remain robust and that the struggle for intellectual freedom is visible, measurable, and subject to democratic oversight.
