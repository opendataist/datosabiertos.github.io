478 Public Institutions Have Information Units And Designated RTI Officers - Info. Minister | - Ghanamma.com

The implementation of Ghana’s Right to Information law has successfully institutionalized transparency mechanisms across the public sector. By mandating the creation of dedicated information units and assigned officers within numerous state entities, the government has established a structural framework designed to facilitate citizen access to official records. This move signals a strong administrative commitment to embedding accountability into the daily operations of public institutions, ensuring that the legal framework is not merely symbolic but functionally integrated into governance. This structural shift is highly relevant to open data principles, as it creates the necessary bureaucratic infrastructure for proactive information disclosure. When public bodies are required to maintain dedicated channels for information requests, it naturally encourages the digitization and organization of records. Consequently, these institutional changes lay the groundwork for more systematic open data practices, transforming passive compliance into active transparency and making government data more accessible and usable for public scrutiny. Ultimately, empowering citizens to demand information fosters a culture of good governance and trust. The law serves as a critical tool for civic engagement, enabling citizens to hold institutions accountable through informed participation. This alignment between legal rights and administrative action demonstrates how policy can drive the open data movement, ensuring that transparency is not just a goal but an operational reality that supports democratic values and societal development.
