Mexico’s Senate has postponed voting on the remaining commissioners of the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), putting the transparency agency at risk of being unable to legally convene. This delay, fueled by intense political conflict between the ruling party and the opposition, threatens the body’s operational capacity and independence, creating a significant governance vacuum during a critical period for public accountability. The stalemate underscores the fragility of democratic institutions when executive vetoes clash with legislative processes. By failing to fill these vacancies, the government undermines the structural integrity of the agency responsible for enforcing transparency laws, signaling a potential retreat from institutional checks and balances. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocates, as the INAI serves as the primary guardian of citizens’ right to access government information. Without a fully functional commission, the enforcement of open data mandates weakens, directly impairing public scrutiny of state actions and reducing the transparency necessary for a healthy digital democracy.
