PM Modi’s degree order set aside, Kejriwal fined

The Gujarat High Court has nullified a 2016 directive by the Central Information Commission that compelled Gujarat University to disclose Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s academic credentials. This ruling establishes a significant precedent regarding the limits of the Right to Information Act, affirming that personal educational details held in a fiduciary capacity are exempt from disclosure unless a compelling public interest is demonstrated. The court emphasized that a leader’s academic qualifications do not inherently impact their public duties, thereby prioritizing privacy and statutory exemptions over general curiosity or political pressure. From the perspective of open data, this decision highlights the tension between transparency mandates and the protection of personal information. It suggests that the Right to Information Act is not intended as a tool for settling political scores or accessing private data merely because an individual holds public office. The ruling reinforces the principle that open data initiatives must respect legal boundaries concerning privacy, particularly when the requested information does not relate to official conduct or decision-making processes. Consequently, public access to such records is restricted, limiting the scope of freely available data regarding the personal backgrounds of high-ranking officials. Ultimately, this case underscores the critical importance of defining clear parameters for what constitutes public interest in government transparency. It serves as a reminder that while accountability is vital, the indiscriminate release of personal data can undermine legal protections and distort the original intent of open data legislation. For activists and policymakers, the implication is that future efforts to access official records must carefully balance the need for transparency with the legal safeguards designed to protect individual privacy rights, ensuring that open data remains a tool for genuine public service rather than political maneuvering.
