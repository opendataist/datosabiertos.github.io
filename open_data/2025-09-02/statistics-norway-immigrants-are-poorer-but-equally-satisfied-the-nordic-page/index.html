Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions than the general population, encompassing lower income, employment challenges, and housing standards. Furthermore, they report higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Despite these objective disadvantages, immigrants maintain a level of life satisfaction comparable to native Norwegians, alongside notably high trust in state institutions like the judiciary and police. This paradox highlights a complex social integration where material hardship coexists with strong institutional confidence. This data is crucial for open_data initiatives because it demonstrates how disaggregated statistics can reveal nuanced societal dynamics that aggregate data might obscure. By providing transparent, accessible information on specific demographic groups, open data enables researchers and policymakers to identify hidden disparities in health and social connection. Such transparency fosters evidence-based discussions on social equity and integration, ensuring that the experiences of marginalized groups are visible and acknowledged in public discourse. Ultimately, this report underscores the importance of comprehensive, publicly available datasets in understanding the multifaceted nature of immigration. It illustrates that well-being is not solely defined by economic metrics but also by psychological and social factors. Open data platforms play a vital role in democratizing this knowledge, allowing for a more inclusive and accurate representation of societal diversity. This empowers communities to engage with the realities of immigrant life, promoting informed dialogue and targeted support systems that address both material and emotional needs effectively.
