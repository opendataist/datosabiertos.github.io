Vibes won’t make your software successful

Vibes won’t make your software successful

The article argues that open source success depends on practical utility rather than ideological purity, rejecting the notion that it is morally superior. Developers prioritize tools that effectively solve specific problems, regardless of their licensing model or openness. This focus on tangible value over normative values shapes how technology is adopted and valued in the industry. Financial trends reveal that market momentum favors hyperscale clouds over open source initiatives, emphasizing customer convenience and immediate results. While open source platforms advocate for their models as the ideal standard, real investment flows toward proprietary solutions that deliver measurable performance benefits. This shift highlights a growing disconnect between open source advocacy and actual commercial success in the competitive tech landscape. This perspective is relevant to open data because it challenges the assumption that openness alone guarantees adoption or sustainability. Just with software, open data initiatives must demonstrate clear, differentiated value to users and stakeholders to secure funding and widespread usage. Success relies on practical impact and user needs, not just the principle of accessibility or transparency.
