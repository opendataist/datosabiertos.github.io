La Pampa entre las provincias con más localidades con cobertura de telefonía celular

La Pampa entre las provincias con más localidades con cobertura de telefonía celular

The article reveals a significant digital divide in Argentina’s mobile connectivity, where nearly all population centers have access to 4G services. However, a stark disparity exists between large urban areas and small localities, with nearly four percent of the total population remaining uncovered. This gap highlights the systemic lack of investment in rural zones and underscores how market-driven coverage strategies fail to serve communities with fewer than five hundred inhabitants, leaving them marginalized in the digital economy. The primary driver of this exclusion is regulatory, as telecom operators are legally obligated to cover only towns exceeding five hundred residents. Consequently, smaller communities fall outside mandatory service mandates, and the universal service fund designed to bridge this gap has been abolished. This regulatory vacuum, combined with recent market consolidations among major providers, threatens to further reduce competitive pressure and geographic reach, exacerbating the isolation of rural populations who lack viable alternatives for connectivity. This issue is critically relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates how opaque regulatory frameworks and the disappearance of public funding mechanisms create invisible inequalities. Transparent, open data on coverage gaps and operator obligations is essential to hold entities accountable. Without accessible information on these disparities, policymakers cannot effectively design interventions, and civil society lacks the evidence needed to demand equitable digital rights and infrastructure development for underserved areas.
