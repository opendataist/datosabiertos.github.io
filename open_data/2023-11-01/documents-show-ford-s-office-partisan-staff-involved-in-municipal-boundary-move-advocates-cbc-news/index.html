Documents show Ford's office, partisan staff involved in municipal boundary move: advocates | CBC News

Documents obtained by environmental groups suggest the Premier’s office played a direct role in forcing municipal boundary expansions against local wishes, potentially boosting private land values. Opposition parties argue these records link high-level government decisions to specific developers, casting doubt on the integrity of the planning process and implying that political influence outweighed public interest in land use policy. The controversy stems from the Ministry’s actions to alter official plans and extend city borders into protected green spaces, which critics claim favored sprawl developers over sustainable urban growth. Although the government has since reversed these specific boundary changes due to excessive involvement from previous ministers, the underlying issue remains the lack of transparency and the perceived erosion of public trust in how zoning and development approvals are managed by provincial authorities. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical role of Freedom of Information requests in exposing government opacity. The release of thousands of pages of internal correspondence demonstrates how accessible public records can reveal hidden political interference and hold officials accountable, reinforcing the necessity of transparency in democratic governance and land-use planning.
