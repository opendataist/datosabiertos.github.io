More than £30 million of Wiltshire’s public pensions fund fossil fuels

The Wiltshire Pension Fund’s significant investments in fossil fuels highlight a critical gap between local climate pledges and actual financial behavior. This discrepancy reveals that public sector savings are actively financing industries responsible for the climate crisis, undermining the council’s declared carbon neutrality goals and eroding public trust in their environmental commitments. This case exemplifies a broader national failure where local government pensions collectively fuel climate destruction with billions in fossil fuel investments. These allocations far exceed renewable energy markets, demonstrating that institutional capital is disproportionately supporting high-emission sectors. Such misalignment poses a systemic risk to global climate targets and exposes the vulnerability of public funds to environmentally harmful ventures. The findings are vital to open data discussions as they illustrate how transparent, accessible financial data can empower civil society to hold institutions accountable. By making investment portfolios visible, activists can directly challenge political narratives and demand policy changes. Open data serves as an essential tool for revealing hidden contradictions in public sector actions, driving pressure for ethical realignment and supporting community-led campaigns for sustainable finance.
