Emergency care waiting time statistics (July - September 2023)

The Department of Health’s latest bulletin reveals a modest increase in Emergency Department attendances across Northern Ireland, yet highlights the transformative impact of introducing PhoneFirst and Urgent Care Centre services. By routing patients through these initial assessment platforms, the system successfully diverted a significant majority of callers away from physical Emergency Departments, demonstrating how triage innovation can reduce unnecessary hospital visits while ensuring appropriate care levels are maintained. Performance metrics against waiting time targets show mixed results, with disparities remaining evident between different types of facilities. While smaller and medium-sized centers largely met the four-hour target, larger Type 1 centers struggled, where a substantial portion of patients exceeded this threshold. This variance suggests that resource allocation and capacity planning are critical bottlenecks, particularly in high-volume facilities, where median wait times for treatment initiation have remained stable year-over-year despite rising demand. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it illustrates the importance of granular, standardized statistical reporting in healthcare transparency. By making detailed metrics on wait times, discharge rates, and service diversion publicly available, stakeholders can analyze systemic inefficiencies and monitor the efficacy of new care models. Such accessible data enables researchers and policymakers to drive evidence-based improvements, ultimately enhancing patient outcomes and operational accountability within the public health sector.
