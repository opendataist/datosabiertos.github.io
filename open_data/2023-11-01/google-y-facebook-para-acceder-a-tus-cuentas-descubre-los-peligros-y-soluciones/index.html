Google y Facebook para acceder a tus cuentas? Descubre los peligros y soluciones

The use of single sign-on via technology platform credentials offers significant convenience by simplifying registration and reducing the need to remember multiple passwords. However, this convenience carries considerable risks, as a compromise of the primary account can simultaneously expose all linked services. Additionally, there is a risk of sharing more personal data than intended, complicating the management of digital privacy. The primary recommendation is to use this authentication method only if two-factor authentication is enabled on the base account and secure payment methods are employed to isolate financial information. For sites that store sensitive data or are used infrequently, it is advisable to opt for independent accounts protected with complex passphrases. This strategy limits the impact of a potential security breach and provides greater control over what information is shared with third parties. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the fundamental tension between interoperability facilitated by the APIs of major providers and user data sovereignty. It promotes practices that minimize dependence on centralized silos, suggesting alternatives such as local password managers and decentralized digital identities. This fosters an ecosystem where individuals retain control over their digital footprint, a key principle for transparency and privacy in open data initiatives.
