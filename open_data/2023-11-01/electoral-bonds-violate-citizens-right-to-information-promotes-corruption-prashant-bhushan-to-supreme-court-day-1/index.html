Electoral Bonds Violate Citizens' Right To Information, Promotes Corruption: Prashant Bhushan To Supreme Court [Day 1]

The Supreme Court proceedings reveal that the Electoral Bond Scheme fundamentally compromised democratic transparency by legally exempting political donations from disclosure across multiple key legislations. This structural opacity allowed the ruling party to dominate funding channels while opposition voices were effectively marginalized, creating an uneven electoral landscape. By shielding donor identities, the scheme undermined the public’s right to information, a cornerstone of informed democratic participation. The removal of caps on corporate contributions and the classification of foreign subsidiaries as domestic sources opened the door for potential misuse, including donations from entities restricted in other sectors. This arbitrariness suggested that financial influence could be leveraged for favorable policies without accountability, as the anonymity of bearer bonds facilitated covert quid pro quo arrangements. Consequently, the mechanism prioritized secrecy over the integrity of political funding, allowing significant capital to flow into politics without public scrutiny. This case is critically relevant to open data because it highlights the dangers of obscuring financial flows that are essential for public accountability. The arguments demonstrate how legal exemptions can create systemic black boxes, preventing citizens from accessing data necessary to evaluate potential conflicts of interest. True openness requires that political financing be visible and traceable, ensuring that democratic processes remain transparent and free from undisclosed corporate or foreign influence.
