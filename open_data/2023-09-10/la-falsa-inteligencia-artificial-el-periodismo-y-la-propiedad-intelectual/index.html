La falsa Inteligencia Artificial, el periodismo y la propiedad intelectual

La falsa Inteligencia Artificial, el periodismo y la propiedad intelectual

The article asserts that while Artificial Intelligence can simulate or imitate journalistic work, it cannot replace the human element essential for quality communication. Machines lack intrinsic human values, ethics, and moral judgment, meaning they cannot replicate the deep intellectual and creative engagement required to produce trustworthy news. Consequently, the role of the journalist must remain distinctly human, serving as a guardian of truth against the superficiality of algorithmic output. A central concern highlighted is the exploitation of human labor by generative AI systems, which utilize existing journalistic and artistic content without recognition or remuneration. This unauthorized use threatens the sustainability of the media sector by undermining intellectual property rights. The text emphasizes that the complex, collaborative effort behind verified reporting constitutes valuable property, and failing to protect it represents an ethical failure that endangers the public’s right to accurate information. This issue is highly relevant to open data, as it underscores the critical need for transparency regarding data provenance and usage rights within AI development. It advocates for frameworks that respect creators' rights and ensure ethical data sourcing, aligning with open principles that prioritize accountability and sustainability. By demanding proper acknowledgment of intellectual contributions, the article supports a data ecosystem where innovation does not come at the expense of creators' livelihoods or journalistic integrity.
