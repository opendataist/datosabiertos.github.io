Expertos advierten que 2 extensiones de Chrome están robando los datos de los usuarios, ojo si las tiene instaladas

This article highlights a significant breach of user trust in digital platforms, revealing how ostensibly harmless Chrome extensions silently harvested private data for years. The study underscores the critical vulnerability of browser add-ons, which operate with extensive permissions to intercept sensitive information like passwords and login credentials without user knowledge. This incident serves as a stark reminder that third-party tools can compromise personal security even when distributed through official channels. The relevance to open_data lies in the transparency and accountability of the ecosystems managing digital infrastructure. While the article focuses on privacy risks, it reflects broader concerns regarding the open nature of plugin markets where verification processes may lag behind malicious innovations. Understanding these vulnerabilities encourages users and developers to demand greater openness in how extensions handle data, ensuring that accessibility does not come at the cost of security. Ultimately, the case illustrates the necessity for rigorous oversight and user education in open technology environments. It emphasizes that trust in official stores cannot replace due diligence in evaluating software permissions. This narrative reinforces the importance of advocating for stricter data governance standards, ensuring that open platforms remain safe spaces for innovation while protecting individual privacy from silent exploitation.
