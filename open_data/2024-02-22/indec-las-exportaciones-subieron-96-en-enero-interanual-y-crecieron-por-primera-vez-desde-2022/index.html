The case of Osvaldo Rofrano, a gas industry businessman found dead at his home with his limbs bound, illustrates the complexity of investigating deaths that present contradictions between physical evidence and public narrative. Although the restraint marks initially suggested a crime, the integration of surveillance footage and testimonies led to the prevailing hypothesis of suicide. This contrast highlights the need for rigorous forensic analysis to unravel the truth behind external signs of violence versus the victim’s declared intent. The relevance of this case to open data lies in the critical role played by the transparency of public and private records in the factual reconstruction of events. Security cameras and digital communication logs serve as verifiable information sources that complement or contradict initial statements. Access to these structured data allows independent authorities to validate hypotheses, demonstrating how open digital information and security metadata can be essential for correcting biases and ensuring accuracy in official reports. Furthermore, the case underscores the importance of data integrity when interacting with social media activity. The victim’s public statements, available on open platforms, create an immediate public context that can influence social perception of the incident. The correlation between these digital footprints and physical findings shows that open data analysis serves not only for police accountability but also for understanding the intersection of privacy, public safety, and freedom of expression in the digital age.
