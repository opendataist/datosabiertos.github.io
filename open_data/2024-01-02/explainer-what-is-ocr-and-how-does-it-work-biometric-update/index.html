Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical character recognition transforms uneditable image files into machine-readable text, addressing the critical need to digitize physical media in an increasingly digital world. This capability allows businesses to store, search, and manage information that was previously difficult to handle, effectively bridging the gap between analog records and modern data systems. By converting scanned documents into structured data, organizations can automate workflows, streamline operations, and enhance productivity without manual intervention. This technological shift enables the seamless integration of text from diverse sources into business software, facilitating advanced analytics and reducing operational friction while supporting broader digital transformation goals. This process is particularly vital for open data initiatives, as it democratizes access to information trapped in legacy formats. By unlocking text within identity documents and other records, OCR facilitates the creation of standardized, machine-actionable datasets. This promotes transparency, enables better integration with biometric systems, and supports the ethical reuse of public and private records for societal benefit.
