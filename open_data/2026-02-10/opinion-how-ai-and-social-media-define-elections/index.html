[OPINION] How AI and social media define elections

This article highlights the dual impact of artificial intelligence on recent and upcoming elections in Asia, where technologies are increasingly used for voter engagement through entertainment and personal branding. While these tools offer new ways for candidates to connect with the public, they simultaneously facilitate the spread of misinformation, hate speech, and polarization. The rapid adoption of AI outpaces regulatory frameworks, creating a landscape where digital influence can manipulate electoral outcomes and exacerbate information asymmetry among voters. The discussion emphasizes the difficulty policymakers face in regulating these technologies without infringing on civil liberties. Experts warn that strict top-down controls might lead to government overreach, while insufficient regulation allows bad actors to exploit platform vulnerabilities. Consequently, there is a strong call for self-regulation, voluntary labeling of AI-generated content, and robust independent fact-checking. The consensus suggests that effective governance requires balancing technological innovation with the protection of democratic integrity and media freedom. This content is highly relevant to open_data because it underscores the critical need for transparency in digital political campaigning. By advocating for open data regimes and freedom of information laws, the forum promotes accountability in the use of algorithms and AI by political entities. Accessible data on campaign spending, algorithmic practices, and content origins empowers citizens and independent watchdogs to verify claims, detect disinformation, and ensure that electoral processes remain fair and transparent in an increasingly digital world.
