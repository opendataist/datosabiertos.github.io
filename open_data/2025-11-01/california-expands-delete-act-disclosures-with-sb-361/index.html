California Expands Delete Act Disclosures with SB-361

California Expands Delete Act Disclosures with SB-361

California’s SB-361 significantly expands data broker registration requirements, demanding disclosure of extensive personal data categories and specific sharing practices with sensitive entities like foreign governments and AI developers. This legislative shift targets the opacity of the data brokerage industry, ensuring regulators can track how consumer information flows to potentially harmful actors. The amendment forces transparency by requiring brokers to reveal their most common data types if they do not handle high-risk identifiers, while mandating detailed reports on sales to state, federal, or law enforcement agencies. These measures aim to close loopholes that previously allowed limited oversight, placing greater compliance burdens on companies operating within the California privacy landscape. This development is crucial for open data advocates, as it establishes a more comprehensive public record of data collection and dissemination. By exposing previously hidden data flows to foreign entities and AI systems, the law enhances accountability and supports the broader goal of informed public discourse regarding data privacy and corporate responsibility.
