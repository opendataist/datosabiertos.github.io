Jefferies favours China's AI strategy over US, citing energy advantage and open-source model

Jefferies favours China's AI strategy over US, citing energy advantage and open-source model

This article highlights China’s structural advantage in the AI sector, driven by its extensive open-source model and abundant, cheap energy capacity. These factors are expected to fuel an "inference boom," where entrepreneurs leverage accessible tools to create cost-effective applications, contrasting sharply with the US approach. This preference for open-source innovation suggests a more democratized development landscape, which is highly relevant to open_data as it emphasizes transparency and community-driven advancement over proprietary restrictions. Financially, the report predicts a period of massive over-investment in data centers, fueled by corporate fears of disruption rather than immediate market demand. While this "capex mania" creates short-term inefficiencies, it parallels the Dotcom era’s excess capacity. The implication is that this eventual correction will drastically reduce the cost of running AI models, potentially triggering a surge in demand as infrastructure becomes significantly cheaper and more accessible to a broader range of users and developers. Ultimately, the article underscores the uncertainty of who will dominate the global race, noting the absence of a definitive "killer app" for the masses. It warns that AI technology obsolesces much faster than previous tech infrastructures, meaning the current boom may lead to rapid shifts in market leadership. For open_data, this highlights the critical importance of adaptable, open standards and infrastructure that can survive rapid technological churn, ensuring that data ecosystems remain resilient and inclusive despite the volatile investment cycles.
