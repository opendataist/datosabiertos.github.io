Juanma Muñoz, triatleta: “Si empiezas a correr solo, tienes en mente comparaciones con la gente que hace maratones y te ves ahogándote al kilómetro uno, vas a dejar el hábito”

The article highlights the persistent challenge of physical inactivity in Spain, despite modest improvements in outdoor exercise rates. It identifies social media as a significant barrier, where unrealistic athletic standards foster negative self-comparison and discourage beginners. This digital pressure creates a perception that moderate activity is unattainable, leading many to abandon their efforts when initial struggles seem excessive compared to curated online images. The core solution proposed involves building a supportive, positive social environment rather than relying on solitary motivation. Running or exercising in groups helps normalize physical fatigue and early-stage limitations, shifting the focus from competition to community. This communal aspect provides a realistic perspective that counters the isolating and often demoralizing influence of digital platforms, making sustained participation more accessible for the average citizen. Finally, the text emphasizes mental resilience and intentional exposure to discomfort as essential components of habit formation. By preparing the mind to endure difficult workouts, individuals can overcome the urge to quit when motivation wanes. This approach is relevant to open data because it underscores the need for datasets that capture qualitative social contexts and psychological barriers, not just quantitative activity metrics, to effectively design interventions that address the root causes of sedentarism.
