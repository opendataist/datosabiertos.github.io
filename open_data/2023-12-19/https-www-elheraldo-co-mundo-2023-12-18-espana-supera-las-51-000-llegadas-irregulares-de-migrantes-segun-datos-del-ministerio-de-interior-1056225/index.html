Spain recorded more than 51,000 irregular migrant arrivals in 2023, marking the highest figure since 2018. This surge highlights intensifying migration pressures, particularly affecting the Canary Islands and Mediterranean routes. The data underscores the urgent need for comprehensive policy responses to manage these complex humanitarian challenges. The relevance to open data lies in the transparency of these official statistics. By making such migration data publicly available, authorities enable independent analysis and accountability. Citizens and organizations can utilize this information to scrutinize government actions and advocate for evidence-based immigration reforms, fostering informed public discourse. Understanding these trends helps identify emerging patterns and resource needs. Open access to raw datasets allows researchers to model future flows and assess the impact of existing policies. This transparency is crucial for building trust in institutions and ensuring that migration management strategies are responsive to real-time humanitarian realities across Europe.
