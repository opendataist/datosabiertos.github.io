The Chief Information Commissioner recently rejected the Islamabad High Court’s assertion that the Right to Information Act does not apply to it. The court registrar argued that a recent Supreme Court ruling exempted the judiciary from transparency laws, claiming the Information Commission lacked jurisdiction over constitutional bodies. By dismissing this stance, the CIC clarified that the Supreme Court’s decision only applied to its own institution, not the High Court, thereby reinforcing the principle that all judicial bodies must adhere to the same statutory obligations for openness. This ruling is significant because it establishes a clear legal precedent that higher courts cannot arbitrarily claim immunity from information requests. It underscores the expectation that judicial institutions, as pillars of the state, must lead by example in upholding the rule of law. When courts refuse to comply with transparency mandates, they undermine public trust and create a dangerous double standard where the enforcers of justice are themselves above the law. The decision ensures that the Islamabad High Court remains subject to the same accountability mechanisms as other state entities. For the open data community, this development is crucial as it validates the push for comprehensive institutional transparency. It demonstrates that legislative frameworks for information access are enforceable even within powerful state organs, preventing the judiciary from operating in secrecy. This strengthens the legal basis for citizens and activists to request data from court records, promoting greater accountability and democratic participation. Ultimately, it affirms that transparency is not optional for any branch of government, ensuring that open data initiatives can effectively target all sectors of the state.
