Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical character recognition transforms static image data into machine-readable text, enabling organizations to efficiently digitize and manage information from print media. By eliminating manual intervention, this process allows businesses to store, search, and analyze documents that were traditionally difficult to handle. The technology bridges the gap between physical records and digital analytics, turning unstructured visual inputs into actionable data streams. Technically, OCR systems analyze scanned images by distinguishing characters from background noise, using algorithms like pattern matching or feature extraction to identify specific glyphs. This precise conversion ensures that text data is accurately captured and structured, facilitating seamless integration with other business software. The resulting digital files support automated processes, significantly enhancing operational productivity and reducing human error in data entry tasks. This capability is particularly relevant to open data initiatives, as it unlocks valuable information hidden within historical or physical documents. By converting diverse formats into standardized, accessible text, OCR promotes transparency and broader data sharing across sectors. Furthermore, its application in verifying digital identities through biometric binding underscores its role in creating reliable, interoperable data ecosystems that support secure and efficient public and private services.
