Lawmakers must address FOIA ruling

A recent Michigan court ruling limits Freedom of Information Act access to public bodies, excluding individual employees. This decision allows government workers to withhold records created during their duties, potentially shielding administrative actions from public view. Such a gap undermines the core principle that transparency requires visibility into how policies are implemented daily. This distinction threatens meaningful open data initiatives by creating obscure pockets of unexamined information. When employee-generated data remains private, the public cannot fully verify government performance or accountability. Consequently, the integrity of civic oversight is weakened, as critical operational details stay hidden within individual staff members rather than accessible public repositories. Legislators must amend the law to explicitly include public employees in disclosure requirements. Ensuring that all taxpayer-funded documents are accessible strengthens democratic trust and facilitates robust public scrutiny. True transparency demands that records created by those implementing policy remain subject to public access, aligning legal frameworks with the original intent of open governance and enabling comprehensive civic engagement.
