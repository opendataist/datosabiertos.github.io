Technological advancement has accelerated the sophistication of virtual frauds, with messaging platforms like WhatsApp becoming prime targets for criminals seeking financial gain and personal data. The central conclusion is that users must remain vigilant against evolving deceptive tactics, as these scams exploit trust and technical vulnerabilities to compromise digital security and privacy. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical intersection between user-generated content and data protection in digital ecosystems. Understanding these threats emphasizes the need for robust privacy frameworks and transparency in how personal information is handled by service providers, reinforcing the importance of securing open networks against malicious exploitation. Key risks include identity theft through verification code interception, unauthorized access via shared screens during video calls, and phishing schemes disguised as job offers or technical support. These methods demonstrate how easily sensitive information can be extracted, urging a broader cultural shift towards digital literacy and proactive security measures to safeguard individual rights in an increasingly connected world.
