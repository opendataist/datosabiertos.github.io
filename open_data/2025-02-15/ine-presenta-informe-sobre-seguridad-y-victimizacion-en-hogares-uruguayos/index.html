INE presenta informe sobre seguridad y victimización en hogares uruguayos

INE presenta informe sobre seguridad y victimización en hogares uruguayos

The National Statistics Institute (INE) has integrated a security module into its Continuous Household Survey, complementing police reports with academic data. This initiative aims to optimize public policies through empirical evidence, ensuring that security decisions are not subject to shifting political cycles. The results reveal that, although most people feel safe, there is a significant gap, particularly among women. The inclusion of this historic study marks a turning point toward a state policy grounded in continuous research rather than relying solely on traditional crime statistics. This advancement is crucial for open data, as it democratizes access to sensitive information on citizens’ perceptions. By making these data public, it fosters transparency and enables civil society, researchers, and the media to analyze security dynamics, promoting more informed and rigorous scrutiny of public management.
