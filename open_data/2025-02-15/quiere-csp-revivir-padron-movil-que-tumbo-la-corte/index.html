Quiere CSP revivir padrón móvil que tumbó la Corte

The Mexican federal executive is proposing the creation of a mandatory registry for mobile phone users, administered by the Digital Transformation and Telecommunications Agency. This initiative aims to strengthen public security investigations by requiring providers to verify users through official identification, such as voter credentials or CURP. The proposal allows security institutions direct access to this data, aiming to ensure every active line is legally associated with an individual within a two-year window, suspending non-compliant numbers. This legislative push directly contradicts the 2022 ruling by the Supreme Court, which declared a similar system unconstitutional for violating fundamental rights to privacy, intimacy, and personal data protection. By reviving a mechanism previously rejected as arbitrary state interference, the current administration disregards judicial precedent on civil liberties. The move signals a prioritization of investigative efficiency over established constitutional safeguards regarding data privacy. This development is critical for the open data community as it highlights the tension between state surveillance capabilities and individual rights. It underscores the urgent need for transparent, accessible, and legally sound data governance frameworks. The controversy exemplifies how government initiatives can impact digital privacy standards, making it a focal point for advocates who monitor how public data systems balance security needs with the protection of personal information against unchecked state power.
