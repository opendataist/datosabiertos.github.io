Gonzalo Miró y Toni Cantó se enfrentan por los últimos datos de opinión sobre inmigración: "No compremos el discurso fascista de Trump"

The article challenges the perception that anti-immigrant sentiment is rising uniformly, revealing a complex reality where fear often stems from distorted political narratives rather than factual evidence. It highlights how ultra-right discourses erroneously link immigration with crime and job loss, fostering ignorance and intolerance. This narrative is crucial for open data advocates, as it underscores the danger of interpreting public opinion surveys without rigorous contextualization. When data lacks proper framing, it can reinforce xenophobic myths rather than illuminate social truths, making transparency and methodology in survey design essential to prevent the manipulation of public perception. Furthermore, the text emphasizes the vital economic contribution of immigrants, particularly those who have regularized their status through hard work, thereby supporting the national pension system and labor market. Contrary to the narrative of job displacement, these individuals are presented as essential solutions to demographic and economic challenges. This perspective is relevant to open data because it advocates for metrics that accurately reflect economic participation and integration success. By promoting datasets that highlight positive contributions and legal regularization, we can counteract negative stereotypes and provide evidence-based insights that support inclusive policy-making. Ultimately, the discussion calls for linking delinquency to poverty rather than immigration, urging a shift in how social issues are analyzed and reported. This aligns with open data principles by encouraging the publication of granular, context-rich data that exposes the root causes of social friction. When data sources clearly distinguish between correlation and causation, they empower society to address inequality effectively. Therefore, this article reinforces the need for ethical data storytelling that prioritizes accuracy over sensationalism, ensuring that public discourse is driven by comprehensive understanding rather than fear-mongering.
