County takes second look at Delta airport FOIA requests

County takes second look at Delta airport FOIA requests

The Delta County Board has agreed to reverse a previous Freedom of Information Act denial regarding investigation notes and recordings held by the county’s attorney. By acknowledging that these documents exist and fall under public record standards established by Michigan case law, the board recognizes its obligation to provide them to former Airport Manager Andrea Nummilien. This decision highlights the critical importance of accurate record-keeping and adherence to legal precedents that prioritize transparency over claims of non-existence. In a parallel development, the board is seeking a second legal opinion on emails previously withheld under attorney-client privilege. Concerns about a potential conflict of interest within the prosecutor’s office, which also serves as the county’s primary counsel, prompted commissioners to request an independent review. This step underscores the complexity of determining exemptions and the necessity of impartial oversight when balancing legal confidentiality with the public’s right to access information. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocates because it illustrates the real-world challenges of enforcing FOIA compliance. It demonstrates how initial denials can stem from administrative errors or misplaced assumptions, requiring active intervention to correct. Furthermore, the debate over conflict-of-interest checks emphasizes that true transparency requires robust, independent verification processes to ensure that withheld data is not arbitrarily protected from public scrutiny.
