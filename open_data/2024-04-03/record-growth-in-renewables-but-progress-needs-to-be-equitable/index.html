Record growth in renewables, but progress needs to be equitable

The 2024 report reveals a critical dichotomy in the global energy transition: while renewable capacity additions hit historic highs, the distribution of these gains is dangerously uneven. Rapid expansion is heavily concentrated in Asia, particularly China, while vast regions like Africa lag significantly behind. This geographic disparity threatens to widen the development gap, excluding many emerging economies from the economic and environmental benefits of decarbonization despite their urgent needs. Simultaneously, the transition faces a technological imbalance where solar energy dominates growth, overshadowing other renewable sources like wind and hydropower. Although renewables now constitute the majority of new power capacity, current trajectories are insufficient to meet the 2030 tripling target. The lack of diversified deployment and the reliance on specific technologies in a few nations highlight structural weaknesses that hinder a resilient, global energy system capable of achieving climate goals. This article is vital to open data discussions as it underscores the necessity for transparent, accessible datasets to identify and address global inequities. By highlighting where resources and data are concentrated versus absent, open data initiatives can drive the policy interventions and international collaboration needed to bridge the divide. Accurate, shared information is essential to mobilize financing and support developing nations, ensuring the energy transition is inclusive and aligned with global sustainability objectives.
