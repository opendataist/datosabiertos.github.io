£2.6bn lost to investment scams between 2020 and 2023, Pensions Management Institute finds

Research reveals that investment scams have systematically defrauded nearly 100,000 people of billions since 2020, with boiler room fraud and Ponzi schemes emerging as the primary vehicles for theft. The data indicates that criminals consistently exploit victims through high-pressure tactics and false promises of excessive returns, resulting in significant average financial losses per individual. This widespread exploitation highlights the urgent need for heightened public awareness and skepticism regarding unsolicited financial offers. The findings underscore the growing sophistication of these criminal operations, which increasingly target individuals through various channels, including recruitment and pension liberation schemes. As the volume and complexity of these frauds escalate, they pose a severe threat to personal financial security, demonstrating that traditional detection methods are often insufficient against modern, multi-layered con artistry. The persistence of these crimes over several years suggests a deep-seated vulnerability in the financial ecosystem that extends beyond simple oversight. This information is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it demonstrates how Freedom of Information requests can transform raw police statistics into actionable public insight. By making granular fraud data transparent, citizens and policymakers can better understand emerging trends, identify red flags, and advocate for improved financial education and regulatory frameworks. Open data serves as a critical tool for empowering the public to defend themselves against increasingly complex financial exploitation.
