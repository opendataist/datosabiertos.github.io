How Open Data Combats Misinformation and Scams

Open data gives people quick access to public records. This helps users check facts and question official stories. It stops false information from spreading. For example, a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request showed that a driver in a deadly crash was not drunk. This proved earlier news reports wrong.

Open data also protects people from scams. Users can study police reports to find fraud trends. Since 2020, this method has saved nearly 100,000 people from losing billions of dollars.

Healthcare users also benefit. In Scotland, FOIA requests revealed that patients waited four years for neurodevelopmental diagnoses. In Australia, documents showed that hospitals did not consistently report when dementia patients escaped. This led to calls for better care and accountability.

However, getting open data is not easy. Many people are just starting to learn about it. They face big challenges. Governments often make it hard to get records. For example, Victoria, Australia, lacks resources and keeps too many secrets. This delays information and stops proper oversight.

Users also face pushback from officials. In Wesley Township, U.S., leaders punished residents for asking for records. They used rules to make it difficult.

Technical problems are another hurdle. There are no standard ways to organize location data. This makes it hard to clean and combine information. Errors often happen when moving data between different areas.

Finally, users must balance transparency with privacy. They need to protect individual identities. Tools like TrialAssure’s AI help with this. They keep data useful while hiding personal details.

These obstacles take time and money to overcome. Users must work hard to use data effectively.
