Número récord de incendios forestales golpean a Venezuela en medio de la sequía amazónica: Reuters

Venezuela is facing a historic wildfire season, recorded as the most severe since 1999, due to the convergence of drought exacerbated by climate change and anthropogenic deforestation. This phenomenon not only destroys critical ecosystems such as the Amazon and Henri Pittier National Park but also highlights the lack of effective prevention plans by authorities, putting biodiversity and local communities at risk. The situation creates a vicious cycle in which wildfires release greenhouse gases that intensify global warming, which in turn exacerbates extreme weather conditions. Experts warn that these events are not isolated incidents but rather signs of impending catastrophic megafires in the region, with far-reaching impacts that go beyond vegetation loss, severely affecting the hydrology of the Amazon River and the resource supply for entire cities. This crisis is relevant to the field of open data because it highlights the gap between the availability of satellite information and the implementation of evidence-based public policies. Although there are agencies that monitor and publish data on heat sources, the inefficacy of the government's response reveals that access to information is not enough without robust governance and transparency mechanisms. This underscores the need for open data to be integrated into early warning systems accessible to civil society, enabling independent monitoring and effective accountability in the face of disastrous environmental management.
