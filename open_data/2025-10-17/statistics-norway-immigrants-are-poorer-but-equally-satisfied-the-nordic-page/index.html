Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Despite facing significantly poorer living conditions regarding employment, housing, and income, immigrants in Norway report satisfaction levels comparable to the native population. This paradox highlights a complex resilience where material disadvantages do not necessarily translate into lower subjective well-being, suggesting that social integration and personal fulfillment may operate on different tracks than economic prosperity. The data reveals stark disparities in mental health, with immigrants experiencing higher rates of anxiety, depression, and loneliness than the general populace. These findings underscore the hidden costs of migration, indicating that while outward satisfaction exists, underlying psychological burdens are disproportionately borne by immigrant communities, particularly those from specific regions, necessitating targeted mental health support and social interventions. Interestingly, immigrants exhibit higher trust in political institutions, the judiciary, and police compared to the rest of the population, reflecting strong institutional affiliation. This insight is crucial for open data initiatives as it demonstrates how granular demographic datasets can reveal nuanced social cohesion metrics, challenging simplistic narratives about immigrant integration and informing policies that leverage this institutional trust for broader societal stability.
