Muerte de la transparencia: alertan que ley de datos personales hasta podría ocultar DD.JJ. - Política - ABC Color

Muerte de la transparencia: alertan que ley de datos personales hasta podría ocultar DD.JJ. - Política - ABC Color

A proposed Personal Data Protection law in Paraguay is facing widespread criticism from opposition legislators, who argue that it poses a severe threat to existing public transparency frameworks. The primary concern is that this new legislation could effectively nullify previous laws mandating the disclosure of public information, including those concerning the use of public funds. Critics argue that by prioritizing data privacy over access, the government risks obscuring how taxpayers' money is managed, thereby undermining the fundamental right of citizens to hold their authorities accountable. The legislation specifically jeopardizes regulations that require the publication of sworn asset and interest declarations by public officials. Since the current draft implies that access to such information requires the individual’s explicit authorization, it creates a barrier that could lead to widespread denials of information requests. This mechanism, referred to as bypassing transparency laws, suggests that the state could shield public figures from scrutiny, preventing the necessary oversight that these disclosure laws were designed to enforce. This development is critically relevant to the open data movement as it highlights the tension between data protection and the public’s right to know. Open data advocates emphasize that transparency is essential for democratic integrity and anti-corruption efforts. If personal data laws are structured to override freedom of information statutes, they could dismantle the infrastructure of accountability, making it difficult for civil society to access vital information about public administration and financial flows.
