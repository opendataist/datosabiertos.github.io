Pilar García de la Granja, experta económica: "Con los nuevos precios del supermercado te puedes hacer unas patatas fritas, que bajan un 4,5%, pero no una tortilla porque los huevos suben un 18"

Spain currently faces the highest inflation rate among major European economies, creating a pervasive sense of financial strain for consumers. Prices have risen across nearly all categories, from essential groceries to public services and utilities. This widespread increase means that everyday expenses, which were previously manageable, have become significantly more burdensome for households, disrupting standard purchasing habits and reducing disposable income. The underlying causes extend beyond international instability, heavily rooted in domestic factors such as high energy costs, labor expenses, and increased taxes or bureaucratic burdens. Even services excluded from basic food and energy calculations are rising, indicating a broad-based economic pressure. The burden ultimately falls on the consumer, who must absorb these escalating costs, thereby limiting the ability of families and small businesses to maintain their previous levels of consumption and investment. This article is relevant to open_data because it highlights the real-world impact of publicly available economic statistics, such as those from the INE. Understanding how official data translates into tangible price changes for goods and services is crucial for advocating transparency. Open access to detailed inflation metrics allows citizens and policymakers to hold institutions accountable, verify the accuracy of reported trends, and develop more effective strategies to mitigate the economic hardship experienced by the general population.
