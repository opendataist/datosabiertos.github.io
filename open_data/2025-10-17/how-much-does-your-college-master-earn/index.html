How much does your college master earn?

How much does your college master earn?

The investigation reveals significant disparities in the remuneration of Cambridge College Heads of House, highlighting a stark divide between lower and higher earnings. While some masters earn just above £50,000, others command packages exceeding £130,000, with top salaries rising sharply since 2018. This wage gap underscores the financial stratification within the university’s collegiate system, reflecting broader economic pressures and inflationary trends affecting academic leadership compensation. Transparency remains inconsistent, as data sources vary between precise figures and broad pay bands, and several colleges declined to disclose information entirely. The complexity is further compounded by differences in how benefits, such as pension contributions and accommodation, are categorized. Consequently, direct comparisons are difficult, but the available data suggests that compensation structures are not standardized, leading to potential inequities in how institutional wealth is distributed among senior administrators. This article is relevant to open data principles because it demonstrates the challenges of accessing and interpreting public sector compensation information. It illustrates how freedom of information requests can expose gaps in transparency, particularly when institutions provide incomplete data or use ambiguous reporting methods. The case serves as a critical example for advocates of open data, emphasizing the need for standardized, granular disclosure practices to ensure accountability and facilitate meaningful public scrutiny of institutional spending.
