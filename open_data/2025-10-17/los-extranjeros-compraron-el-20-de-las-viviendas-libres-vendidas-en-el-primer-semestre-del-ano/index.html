Los extranjeros compraron el 20% de las viviendas libres vendidas en el primer semestre del año

Los extranjeros compraron el 20% de las viviendas libres vendidas en el primer semestre del año

Foreign buyers significantly shape Spain’s real estate market, accounting for nearly one-fifth of residential transactions in the first half of the year. While total foreign acquisitions saw a slight decline, the proportion of purchases by residents remains dominant, reflecting a shift in investment patterns. This trend highlights the evolving nature of property ownership, where non-residents are increasingly navigating regulatory changes, such as the end of golden visas, while residents maintain a strong presence in key economic hubs. Geographic distribution reveals distinct preferences, with the Valencian Community and Andalusia attracting the highest volume of sales. Price disparities further illustrate market segmentation, as non-residents consistently pay premium rates compared to both residents and nationals. Countries like the United States and Switzerland lead in expenditure per square meter, whereas buyers from North Africa and South America opt for more affordable segments. This stratification impacts local housing markets, influencing availability and affordability for domestic populations. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible statistics on property transactions. Detailed, disaggregated datasets enable researchers and policymakers to analyze migration patterns, economic impacts, and market dynamics accurately. By providing granular insights into buyer origins and pricing trends, open data supports evidence-based decision-making and fosters a deeper understanding of how global capital influences local real estate ecosystems.
