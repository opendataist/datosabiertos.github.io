Harmonising and improving statistics in Ghana: The role of statistical players - MyJoyOnline

Harmonising and improving statistics in Ghana: The role of statistical players - MyJoyOnline

Effective national development and policy-making rely on credible, harmonized statistics that ensure consistency and comparability across all sectors. In Ghana, this requires moving beyond isolated data collection to a unified system where different government bodies, academic institutions, and civil society organizations align their methodologies. Without such coordination, conflicting data undermines public trust and hinders accurate progress monitoring, making harmonization a foundational necessity for economic stability and social equity. The Ghana Statistical Service acts as the central coordinator, establishing standards and fostering collaboration among diverse stakeholders. While ministries and local assemblies produce essential sector-specific data, they must integrate with national frameworks to prevent duplication and ensure interoperability. Furthermore, academic research and citizen-generated data fill critical gaps regarding marginalized communities, provided they adhere to established quality benchmarks. This collective effort transforms fragmented inputs into a coherent, reliable statistical ecosystem. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the technical and institutional prerequisites for making data usable and trustworthy. Open data initiatives cannot succeed without the underlying harmonization that ensures datasets are compatible, standardized, and interoperable. By emphasizing coordination among all data producers, the text underscores that transparency and accessibility must be built on a foundation of consistent, high-quality information that supports evidence-based decision-making at both local and global levels.
