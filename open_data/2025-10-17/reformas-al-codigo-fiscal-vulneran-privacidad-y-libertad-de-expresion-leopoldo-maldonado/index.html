Reformas al Código Fiscal vulneran privacidad y libertad de expresión: Leopoldo Maldonado

Reformas al Código Fiscal vulneran privacidad y libertad de expresión: Leopoldo Maldonado

Proposed tax reforms in Mexico threaten user privacy and internet freedom by mandating real-time government access to personal data on digital platforms. This measure raises serious concerns about authoritarian oversight and the potential for blocking dissenting voices under ambiguous legal frameworks. The risk extends beyond commercial entities to include media and academic channels, effectively granting tax authorities unchecked power to define access rules. Coupled with existing surveillance laws, this shift creates a dangerous precedent for mass data monitoring and the erosion of fundamental civil liberties. This issue is crucial for open data advocates because it illustrates how administrative mandates can undermine data sovereignty and transparency. It highlights the urgent need to protect digital rights against state overreach, ensuring that data sharing mechanisms respect individual privacy and freedom of expression rather than serving as tools for unchecked surveillance.
