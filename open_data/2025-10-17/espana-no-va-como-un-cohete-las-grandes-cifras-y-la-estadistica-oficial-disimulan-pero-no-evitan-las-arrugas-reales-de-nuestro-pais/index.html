"España no va como un cohete, las grandes cifras y la estadística oficial disimulan, pero no evitan las arrugas reales de nuestro país"

"España no va como un cohete, las grandes cifras y la estadística oficial disimulan, pero no evitan las arrugas reales de nuestro país"

The article reveals that Spain’s positive macroeconomic indicators mask deep structural weaknesses, particularly low productivity and an overreliance on tourism. These underlying issues indicate that the country has not genuinely transformed its productive model, despite the recovery from the pandemic. The apparent progress is largely superficial, hiding significant gaps in economic resilience and development. A key mechanism behind this statistical illusion is the "fixed-discontinuous" worker status, which improves job security without providing true stability. This arrangement excludes workers from unemployment statistics during inactive periods, creating a paradox where more people receive unemployment benefits than are officially counted as jobless. Consequently, official data fails to reflect the reality of widespread temporary and seasonal employment, especially in agriculture and services. This discrepancy is highly relevant to open data, as it demonstrates how specific definitions and reporting methods can distort public understanding of socioeconomic conditions. The divergence between aggregated figures and lived experiences, such as high poverty rates and inequality, highlights the importance of transparent, granular data. Open data initiatives must prioritize detailed, accessible metrics to expose these statistical "makeup" and ensure policy decisions are based on accurate, comprehensive evidence rather than misleading aggregates.
