Trump firma ley para forzar la publicación de los archivos Epstein

Trump firma ley para forzar la publicación de los archivos Epstein

The President of the United States signed the Epstein Files Transparency Act, requiring the Department of Justice to release documents related to the case. This move responds to years of demands from victims’ families to learn the identities of possible accomplices and hold them criminally accountable, ensuring that full disclosure is completed within a set timeframe. The legislation received nearly unanimous support in Congress, reflecting a bipartisan demand for greater transparency. Although the administration attempted to divert attention toward political victories, public pressure and prior revelations about the complicity of influential figures forced this legal measure, highlighting the tension between state interests and the right to truth. This event is crucial for open data because it demonstrates how institutional transparency can prevail over political opacity, even in highly polarized contexts. By making sensitive data publicly accessible, it sets a precedent for citizens’ access to relevant government information, strengthening accountability and trust in democratic institutions in the face of high-profile scandals.
