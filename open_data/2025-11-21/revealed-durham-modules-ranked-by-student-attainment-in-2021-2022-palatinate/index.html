Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022 - Palatinate

Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022 - Palatinate

The article highlights how Freedom of Information requests enable the public to access granular academic performance data, demonstrating the practical utility of open data initiatives like whatdotheyknow.com. By revealing specific module attainment levels, this transparency allows stakeholders to scrutinize institutional standards and identify patterns in student success across different disciplines. This example underscores how FOI mechanisms can democratize information, turning opaque university metrics into accessible resources for public analysis and debate. Analysis of the data reveals significant disparities in academic outcomes, with Physics and English leading in attainment while Foundation years and Modern Foreign Languages showed the lowest results. Such disparities raise critical questions about grading consistency, assessment design, and support structures within higher education. Understanding these variations is essential for evaluating the fairness and effectiveness of different academic programs, providing insights that were previously unavailable to students, researchers, and policy-makers. This transparency is crucial for fostering accountability in educational institutions. When universities are compelled to disclose detailed grade distributions, it encourages rigorous self-reflection and potential improvements in teaching methodologies and assessment strategies. Furthermore, it empowers prospective students and current learners to make informed decisions based on empirical evidence rather than anecdotal reputation. Ultimately, this case illustrates how open data can drive meaningful conversations about educational quality and equity, ensuring that institutions remain responsive to public scrutiny and committed to continuous improvement.
