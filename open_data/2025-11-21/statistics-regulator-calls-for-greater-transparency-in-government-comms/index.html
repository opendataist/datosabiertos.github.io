Statistics regulator calls for greater transparency in government comms

Statistics regulator calls for greater transparency in government comms

The Office for Statistics Regulation has highlighted a critical gap in how UK government departments communicate data, emphasizing that transparency must become the standard rather than an exception. Although there is noticeable progress in the statistical community’s understanding of best practices, public-facing communications often fail to cite sources or provide direct links to underlying data. This lack of traceability undermines the credibility of government figures and suggests that intelligent transparency is not yet fully embedded in routine operations. Specific structural and cultural barriers within the most central parts of government, particularly regarding the planning and approval of announcements, exacerbate these issues. Stakeholders report that low awareness among special advisers and communication teams, combined with logistical difficulties in securing publication slots, creates significant hurdles for timely and open data sharing. The absence of dedicated statistical leadership in key strategic offices further isolates statisticians, making it difficult to resolve disputes or ensure compliance with established codes of practice. This review is vital to the open data movement because it exposes the disconnect between internal statistical rigor and external public communication. By identifying specific institutional flaws and calling for mandatory source citation and improved leadership structures, the report provides a roadmap for ensuring that public data is accessible, verifiable, and trustworthy. It serves as a reminder that true open data requires proactive, clear, and accessible approaches from all levels of government, not just specialized statistical bodies.
