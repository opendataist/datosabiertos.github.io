Posting Dead. By 2070 Dead Facebook Users Could Overtake the Living

Posting Dead. By 2070 Dead Facebook Users Could Overtake the Living

A recent study by the Oxford Internet Institute projects that the number of deceased Facebook accounts will soon surpass active ones, transforming the platform into a massive repository of digital remains. This demographic shift implies that social media companies will eventually manage more data belonging to the dead than the living, raising urgent questions about digital legacy management and the long-term storage of personal information. The article is highly relevant to open data as it highlights the tension between individual privacy rights and the potential value of deceased users' data as part of global digital heritage. It argues that this vast accumulation of profiles could serve as a crucial historical resource for future researchers, suggesting that archiving and curating such data should involve historians and ethicists. This challenges the current open data paradigm by introducing complex ethical dimensions regarding access, preservation, and the public interest in data that is traditionally considered private. Facebook’s current solutions, such as legacy contacts, offer a mechanism for controlled memorialization but do not fully address the societal implications of storing billions of profiles. The research emphasizes that managing digital remains is not just a technical issue for tech companies but a broader societal responsibility requiring sustainable, long-term policies. As these digital footprints become permanent fixtures of our history, society must develop frameworks that balance familial interests with the potential open access needed for historical and sociological study.
