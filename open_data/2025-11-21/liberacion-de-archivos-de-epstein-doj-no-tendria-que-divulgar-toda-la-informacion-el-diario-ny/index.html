Liberación de archivos de Epstein: DOJ no tendría que divulgar toda la información - El Diario NY

Liberación de archivos de Epstein: DOJ no tendría que divulgar toda la información - El Diario NY

The recent approval of the Epstein Archives Transparency Act marks a significant milestone in the fight for government accountability and access to public information. This legislation mandates the Department of Justice to systematically disclose records related to Jeffrey Epstein and his network, establishing a crucial precedent for transparency in high-profile cases where public interest often clashes with state discretion. The legislative mandate covers a broad range of documents, from internal communications and immunity agreements to travel records and evidence concerning Epstein’s death. However, the law permits certain limited withholdings, specifically to protect the identities of victims and ongoing investigations. This balance aims to prevent re-victimization and ensure the integrity of current criminal proceedings, while requiring written justifications and the publication of the rationale for any classified or redacted information. This development is highly relevant to the open data movement, as it transforms traditionally opaque government archives into accessible and downloadable resources. By mandating public formats and excluding censorship for political or reputational reasons, the law reinforces the principle that public information must be usable by citizens. This not only empowers civil society to conduct its own investigations but also increases trust in institutions by demonstrating that state secrecy has clear and justified limits.
