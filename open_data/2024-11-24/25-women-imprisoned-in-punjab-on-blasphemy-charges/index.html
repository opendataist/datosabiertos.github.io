25 women imprisoned in Punjab on blasphemy charges

Data obtained through transparency laws reveals that women are disproportionately incarcerated under blasphemy accusations in Punjab, highlighting a systemic issue where legal frameworks often fail to protect vulnerable populations. This underscores the critical role of open data in exposing human rights violations that might otherwise remain hidden from public scrutiny. The information demonstrates how accessing official records can illuminate the true scale of legal persecution, moving beyond anecdotal evidence to provide concrete insights into judicial processes. Such transparency enables advocates and researchers to identify patterns and push for necessary legal reforms. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates how citizen-led information requests can force governmental accountability. By making prison statistics visible, open data initiatives empower civil society to demand justice and ensure that marginalized groups are not silently suffering within the legal system.
