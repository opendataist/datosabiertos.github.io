Derriford issues statement as mental health absences rise

The article highlights a significant rise in mental health-related staff absences within the NHS, particularly at University Hospitals Plymouth NHS Trust. This trend underscores a systemic crisis where prolonged understaffing, inadequate pay, and unsustainable working conditions have pushed healthcare workers to the brink of burnout. Despite the introduction of dedicated wellbeing teams, the data indicates that support measures alone are insufficient to counteract the intensifying pressures on the workforce. Crucially, the withdrawal of government funding for national mental health hubs has exacerbated the situation, removing vital lifelines for frontline workers. The correlation between the loss of these external support structures and the subsequent increase in absences suggests that institutional backing is as critical as internal trust initiatives. Without comprehensive systemic interventions, the cycle of exhaustion and turnover is likely to continue, threatening the stability of healthcare services across the country. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the value of transparency in identifying societal trends. By making FOI data public, advocates and policymakers can track the real-time impact of funding cuts and staffing decisions on employee wellbeing. Open data allows the public to hold institutions accountable, transforming isolated reports of burnout into a measurable evidence base for demanding policy changes and resource allocation.
