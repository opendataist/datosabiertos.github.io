Unicef: Tres de cada cinco niños no tienen acceso a seguridad social en Panamá

Unicef: Tres de cada cinco niños no tienen acceso a seguridad social en Panamá

Unicef’s report reveals that over half of Panamanian children lack social security coverage, highlighting a severe inequity in access to public health services. The data shows that indigenous populations are disproportionately affected, with nearly all children in specific comarques completely excluded from these essential protections. This stark disparity underscores systemic failures in reaching vulnerable groups, particularly in marginalized regions where structural barriers prevent their inclusion in national welfare systems. The analysis emphasizes that without targeted, equity-focused policies, children from indigenous communities and adolescent mothers remain at high risk of exclusion. These gaps directly hinder the achievement of Sustainable Development Goal 1, which aims to establish effective safety nets for the poorest and most vulnerable. The situation calls for urgent policy reforms that prioritize inclusive approaches to ensure all children can reach their full potential, regardless of their ethnic or geographic background. This article is highly relevant to open data, as it illustrates the critical role of transparent, accessible census and statistical information in exposing social inequalities. By leveraging publicly available data from institutions such as INEC, civil society and international organizations can hold governments accountable and advocate for evidence-based policymaking. Open data thus serves as a vital tool for identifying gaps in social protection and driving reforms that address the needs of the most disadvantaged populations.
