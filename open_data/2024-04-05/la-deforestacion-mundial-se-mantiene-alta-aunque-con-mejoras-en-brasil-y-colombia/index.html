La deforestación mundial se mantiene alta, aunque con mejoras en Brasil y Colombia

The global loss of primary tropical forest in 2023 remains critically high, underscoring the urgent need for open, transparent data to monitor environmental progress. Although significant reductions occurred in Brazil and Colombia due to political leadership, these gains were offset by increased deforestation in Bolivia, Laos, and Nicaragua, as well as severe wildfire damage in Canada. This discrepancy highlights that without accessible, real-time geospatial monitoring, it is impossible to accurately assess which policy interventions are effective and where immediate attention is required to prevent biodiversity loss and excessive carbon emissions. The article demonstrates that satellite data and collaborative global platforms can reveal nuanced trends, such as the success of specific national policies versus failures in other regions. By making this data openly available, researchers and policymakers can identify the drivers of deforestation, such as agricultural expansion or mining, and hold governments accountable for their climate commitments. Transparency in data ensures that improvements are not just local anecdotes but part of a verifiable global effort, preventing false narratives that might otherwise obscure the overall lack of progress toward halting forest loss by 2030. Ultimately, this report serves as a stark reminder that current efforts are insufficient to meet international climate goals. The relevance of open data lies in its capacity to democratize information, allowing civil society and international bodies to independently scrutinize environmental reports. Ensuring that data on land use and deforestation is free, accurate, and widely disseminated is essential for driving the systemic changes needed to protect the Earth’s most critical ecosystems and effectively mitigate the impacts of climate change.
