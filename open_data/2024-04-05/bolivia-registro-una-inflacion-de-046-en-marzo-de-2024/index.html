Bolivia registró una inflación de 0,46% en marzo de 2024

The National Institute of Statistics of Bolivia reported a general increase in consumer prices during March 2024, driven primarily by rises in non-alcoholic food items and other goods. This trend reflects inflationary pressure that affects regions unevenly, with capital cities and concentrated urban areas experiencing more pronounced price increases compared to other areas, underscoring regional variability in the cost of living and the need for economic policies tailored to specific local contexts. The structure of consumption and prices shows that basic products such as onions, tomatoes, and eggs were key factors in the rise of the index. This behavior not only impacts household purchasing power but also demonstrates how fluctuations in the supply of basic food items can significantly distort the overall economic perception. The concentration of these increases in certain essential goods highlights families' vulnerability to price shocks in the basic food basket, a critical issue for social and economic planning. This report is fundamental to the open data movement, as it illustrates the importance of having detailed and disaggregated official statistics. By providing city-specific and category-specific information, it facilitates independent analysis by researchers, media outlets, and citizens. This transparency enables a better understanding of economic realities, promotes government accountability, and empowers civil society to use these data in advocating for more equitable, evidence-based public policies.
