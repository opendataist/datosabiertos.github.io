Assessment study on Bennettsville police reveals internal challenges with leadership

A confidential assessment of the Bennettsville Police Department reveals a deep rift between leadership and staff, highlighting systemic issues in trust and transparency. The report contrasts former officers’ claims of broken promises and improper conduct by Chief Kevin Miller against his official performance evaluations, which consistently praise his exceptional leadership. This disparity suggests that internal cultural failures and interpersonal conflicts may be driving turnover more than the financial factors cited by city officials. The investigation highlights significant concerns regarding professional boundaries and ethical management within the department. Former officers described uncomfortable interactions with the Chief, including meetings at private residences and allegations of mishandled harassment complaints. These accounts, juxtaposed with the Chief’s clean administrative record, underscore the critical need for objective oversight mechanisms that protect both employees and administrators from subjective disputes that can erode institutional credibility. This case is vital to the open_data movement as it demonstrates how accessing public records can expose hidden organizational realities that official narratives attempt to obscure. By securing the assessment study through freedom of information requests, the community gained visibility into the gap between published performance metrics and lived employee experiences. It serves as a powerful example of how transparency acts as a necessary check on power, ensuring that public institutions remain accountable to the truth rather than just their own internal assessments.
