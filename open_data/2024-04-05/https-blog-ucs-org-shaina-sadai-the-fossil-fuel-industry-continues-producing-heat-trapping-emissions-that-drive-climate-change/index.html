Open Data
ES EN

This article highlights the significance of a new dataset from InfluenceMap that traces heat-trapping emissions to major fossil fuel producers. The central conclusion is that despite decades of scientific consensus and international climate agreements, cumulative emissions from these entities have continued to rise dramatically. This persistent growth demonstrates a fundamental disconnect between corporate production strategies and global efforts to mitigate climate change, underscoring the urgent need for accountability. The author utilizes this data to support litigation-relevant research, aiming to hold producers responsible for their contribution to global warming and sea-level rise. By analyzing company-specific emissions, the research provides empirical evidence that can be used in climate lawsuits and policy advocacy. This approach bridges the gap between scientific inquiry and legal action, offering a robust framework for demonstrating how specific corporations have exacerbated environmental crises while prioritizing profits over planetary health. This dataset is highly relevant to the open data community as it exemplifies how transparent, granular information can drive social and legal change. By making emission data accessible and actionable, it empowers researchers, activists, and litigators to challenge corporate opacity. The article illustrates that open data is not merely an academic exercise but a critical tool for enforcing accountability, fostering climate justice, and advocating for the necessary phase-out of fossil fuels in alignment with scientific reality.
