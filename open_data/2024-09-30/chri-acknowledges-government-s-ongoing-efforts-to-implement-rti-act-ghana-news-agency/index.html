CHRI acknowledges government's ongoing efforts to implement RTI Act | Ghana News Agency

CHRI acknowledges government's ongoing efforts to implement RTI Act | Ghana News Agency

The Commonwealth Human Rights Initiative highlights a critical gap in Ghana’s implementation of its Right to Information Act, specifically the failure to proactively disclose essential governance data. Despite establishing a commission and hosting a significant global conference, public agencies lack standardized efforts to publish basic information without citizen requests. This reactive approach undermines transparency, as monitored institutions consistently failed to release key documents like annual reports and audit results, leaving citizens unable to effectively hold the government accountable. This discrepancy between legal framework and practical application reveals a systemic weakness in democratic engagement across the continent. While Ghana serves as a regional leader in hosting international discussions on information access, its domestic execution lags behind best practices. The absence of routine publication mechanisms suggests that the current system prioritizes bureaucratic processing over genuine openness, thereby limiting the public’s ability to participate meaningfully in governance and eroding trust in public institutions. The article is vital to open data advocates because it underscores that legal recognition of information rights is insufficient without active, standardized disclosure practices. It calls for urgent regulatory updates to mandate routine transparency, illustrating that true open government requires proactive data availability rather than just response to specific queries. This serves as a cautionary example for other nations: without enforcing proactive sharing, access to information laws remain ineffective tools for accountability and democratic participation.
