Tres millones de teletrabajadores en España

The adoption of telework in Spain has increased significantly in the post-pandemic period, reaching nearly 14.4% of the workforce, primarily driven by hybrid work models. Although this growth remains below the European average, the trend reflects a structural shift in how work is organized. The current landscape is characterized by a gradual normalization of remote work, contrasting with the emergency measures of previous years and highlighting evolving professional habits across different demographics and regions. Spanish legislation requires formal agreements for substantial use of remote work, which inadvertently encourages companies to limit telework to marginal levels to avoid regulatory burdens. This regulatory framework creates a discrepancy between reported remote work and formalized arrangements, suggesting that compliance issues or inaccuracies in surveys may skew the data. As a result, implementation remains largely superficial, with many firms opting for minimal remote work schedules to efficiently navigate legal requirements while maintaining traditional office-centric operations. The disparity between autonomous communities and the gendered implications of this shift are critical concerns. While Madrid leads in adoption due to its public sector and corporate density, regional inequalities persist. Furthermore, the potential for telework to reinforce traditional care roles, disproportionately affecting women, underscores the need for balanced policies. This report is vital for open data because it reveals how aggregated statistics can mask structural inequities and regulatory gaps, emphasizing the importance of granular, transparent datasets to inform effective labor and digital transformation policies.
