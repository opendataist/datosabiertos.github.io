The National Institute for Transparency (INAI) has formally challenged the President’s recent decree, which reclassifies major infrastructure projects as matters of national security. By invoking secrecy provisions, the administration risks restricting public access to critical information regarding these developments. This action directly contradicts a previous Supreme Court suspension that explicitly protected the public’s right to know from such limitations, establishing a clear legal conflict between executive power and transparency rights. This dispute is crucial for open data because it highlights the fragility of information access when governments prioritize security narratives over public oversight. The INAI argues that the new decree effectively bypasses existing judicial orders, potentially hiding procurement and execution details from citizens. If upheld, this practice could set a dangerous precedent, allowing future administrations to shield significant public expenditures and project data from scrutiny under vague security justifications, thereby undermining democratic accountability. Furthermore, the controversy is compounded by reported budget cuts that may impair the INAI’s operational capacity. Legislative inquiries into these financial restrictions suggest a broader institutional weakening, which could hinder the institute’s ability to enforce transparency laws. Ultimately, this situation underscores the urgent need for robust legal frameworks that prevent the arbitrary classification of public works as secret, ensuring that open data principles remain protected against political maneuvering.
