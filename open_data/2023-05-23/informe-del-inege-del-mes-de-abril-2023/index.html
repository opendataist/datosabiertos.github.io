Informe del INEGE del mes de abril 2023

The INEGE report reveals that although inflation in Equatorial Guinea moderated in April 2023 compared to the previous month, it remains significantly higher than the previous year’s level, driven primarily by price increases in food, transport, and tobacco. This inflationary trend affects different cities unevenly, with Mongomo recording the highest rate and Malabo the lowest, underscoring the importance of disaggregating statistical data to understand local economic realities and design appropriate policies. The relevance of this article to the field of open data lies in its detailed and disaggregated structure. By presenting information not only at the national level but also by product categories and specific cities, INEGE facilitates transparency and access to high-granularity public data. This enables researchers, analysts, and the general public to conduct more precise impact studies, cross-reference information across regions, and examine the evolution of the cost of living in greater depth, thereby promoting the critical use of official statistics. Finally, the publication of these data in an accessible format with clear sources exemplifies good practices in open data governance. By providing both macroeconomic aggregates and microeconomic deviations by locality, the report not only informs but also empowers users to audit and understand the causes behind inflation. This openness fosters accountability and enables more informed social participation in response to the country’s economic dynamics.
