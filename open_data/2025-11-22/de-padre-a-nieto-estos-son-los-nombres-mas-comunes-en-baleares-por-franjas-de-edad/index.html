De padre a nieto: estos son los nombres más comunes en Baleares por franjas de edad

The evolution of personal names in the Balearic Islands illustrates how onomastic choices serve as a dynamic mirror of cultural identity, shifting from rigid religious and traditional conventions to increasingly diverse, globalized trends. This analysis reveals that naming practices are not merely fashion statements but significant indicators of societal transitions, reflecting broader impacts of international currents and changing familial values within the region. A major shift occurred in the mid-to-late 20th century, where dominant traditional names were gradually replaced by more modern choices, signaling a departure from historical precedents. The recent decades show a clear trend toward globalization and diversification, with a simultaneous recovery of classic names, suggesting a complex interplay between preserving heritage and embracing contemporary influences in a more open society. This data is highly relevant to open_data as it demonstrates the value of publicly available demographic records in tracing long-term sociocultural patterns. By analyzing structured public data over time, researchers can uncover subtle narratives about community identity and cultural adaptation that remain invisible in short-term snapshots, highlighting how open datasets facilitate deeper understanding of societal evolution without compromising individual privacy.
