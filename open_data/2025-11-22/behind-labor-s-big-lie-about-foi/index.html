Behind Labor's Big Lie about FOI

Behind Labor's Big Lie about FOI

The Albanese government’s proposal to weaken freedom of information laws lacks the evidentiary support necessary for such a significant policy shift. Officials argue that current transparency requirements discourage public servants from offering candid advice, a claim they link to the failures seen during the robodebt scandal. However, this narrative collapses under scrutiny, as no evidence exists to demonstrate that freedom of information obligations have led to breaches of professional conduct or withheld critical warnings. In fact, the relevant regulatory body confirmed that not a single investigation has been initiated regarding such failures, exposing the government’s rationale as baseless. Furthermore, the central example used to justify reduced transparency—specifically the withholding of legal advice during the robodebt crisis—irrelevantly relies on mechanisms that are already exempt from public disclosure. Since legal advice is categorically shielded from freedom of information requests and other oversight inquiries, its suppression has nothing to do with the proposed legislative changes. This disconnect reveals that the rollback is not a corrective measure for transparency issues but rather an expansion of secrecy, particularly regarding cabinet documents, contrary to recommendations from the royal commission that called for greater accountability. This article is highly relevant to open data advocates because it highlights the political dangers of conflating transparency with operational inefficiency. It demonstrates how governments may exploit high-profile scandals to justify rolling back access-to-information rights, even when the alleged problems are unrelated to public record requests. For open data supporters, this serves as a critical warning to defend existing transparency frameworks against erosion, emphasizing the need to distinguish between genuine bureaucratic concerns and unfounded justifications for increased secrecy in government operations.
