La Agencia Española de Protección de Datos advierte sobre la baliza V16 obligatoria: "No tienes que dar sus datos personales a ninguna administración al adquirirlo"

La Agencia Española de Protección de Datos advierte sobre la baliza V16 obligatoria: "No tienes que dar sus datos personales a ninguna administración al adquirirlo"

The Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) clarifies that the V16 emergency beacon does not constitute a tool for mass surveillance. When purchasing the device, users do not provide personal data, so the Directorate General of Traffic (DGT) remains unaware of their identity. Only coordinates and a technical identifier are transmitted when the beacon is activated in an emergency, thereby ensuring the driver’s anonymity and preventing the tracking of movements or direct linkage to the vehicle’s registered owner. This information is crucial for the open data movement, as it dispels concerns about the creation of continuous location profiles. The system’s design prioritizes immediate road safety over the collection of historical data, ensuring that the technology serves solely to alert others to breakdowns or accidents without generating travel histories. This establishes an important precedent for how telematics can coexist with privacy, limiting data extraction to what is strictly necessary for incident management. Beyond privacy concerns, the high cost of these devices has generated public discontent, leading some users to seek cheaper alternatives through international platforms. However, there is a significant risk in purchasing unofficial products or those lacking proper certification, as they may not meet safety or data protection requirements. It is essential that citizens purchase only certified devices to ensure both their own safety and the integrity of the DGT’s data system.
