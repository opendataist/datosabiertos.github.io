Information Weaponization at NASA – Part 1: FOIA

Information Weaponization at NASA – Part 1: FOIA

The article argues that NASA has systematically weaponized its records management program to evade transparency and accountability under the Freedom of Information Act. By failing to comply with federal electronic records regulations for nearly two decades, the agency creates a system where it can selectively alter, hide, or destroy incriminating documents. This structural non-compliance means that FOIA responses are inherently unreliable, as the agency lacks the certified infrastructure required to preserve the authenticity and integrity of its digital records. Key findings highlight that NASA does not use compliant electronic repositories, rendering its official records management claims false. The agency has failed to implement necessary functionality in its information systems and ignores guidelines for managing emails and social media records. Consequently, even when records are produced, there is no verifiable proof that they have not been tampered with or that non-responsive documents were not destroyed improperly. This deliberate neglect turns the transparency law into a tool for concealing agency activities rather than revealing them. This issue is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how poor records management directly corrupts public access to government information. When agencies treat transparency mechanisms as optional or manipulate their data lifecycle to avoid scrutiny, the integrity of open data initiatives is undermined. The case illustrates that technical compliance with records standards is a prerequisite for genuine openness; without it, data released to the public may be incomplete or inaccurate, preventing meaningful oversight and eroding trust in democratic institutions.
