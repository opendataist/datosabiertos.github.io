Inai reitera petición de diálogo con Sheinbaum | Periodico Hidrocalido Digital

The National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI) has called for a constructive dialogue with President-elect Claudia Sheinbaum concerning the proposed constitutional reform aimed at eliminating autonomous bodies. The institution argues that abolishing these entities would adversely affect Mexico’s international standing, specifically jeopardizing critical trade agreements such as the USMCA and compliance with global data protection standards. Strong, independent oversight is presented as essential for maintaining diplomatic and commercial relationships, as its absence could render the country unattractive for international business and data flows. Contrary to claims that dismantling these bodies would yield substantial fiscal savings, INAI emphasizes that the actual financial benefit would be negligible when weighed against the administrative costs and the loss of institutional expertise. The institute asserts that its functions do not duplicate those of other government departments, warning that the perceived economic advantage is misleading. Instead of elimination, the leadership advocates for internal modernization, proposing greater austerity and rationality in resource use to enhance effectiveness without compromising the core mission of protecting citizens’ rights. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical role of independent authorities in safeguarding personal information privacy and ensuring the reliability of cross-border data flows. It underscores that transparency and data protection are not merely domestic concerns but foundational elements for international integration and trust. The debate reflects broader concerns about how political decisions regarding institutional independence can directly impact the legal frameworks and security guarantees necessary for a robust, open, and globally connected data ecosystem.
