El crecimiento de las ciudades cambia: Antes era hacia afuera y ahora es hacia arriba

The study reveals a global transformation in urban structure, with cities decisively shifting toward vertical development in recent decades. This change reflects an adjustment to regional economic development, with rapidly expanding areas prioritizing height over horizontal sprawl. The trend indicates that densification is the predominant response to contemporary urbanization. Evidence shows that upward growth has increased dramatically, while lateral expansion has significantly declined worldwide. This dynamic varies by region, with areas such as Asia and Africa accelerating their transition toward taller buildings during different periods. Advanced economies have not experienced this acceleration, underscoring differences in urbanization patterns between the developing and developed worlds. This analysis is relevant to the field of open data, as it demonstrates the critical value of public satellite datasets for understanding global urban trends. The availability of transparent and accessible information enables researchers and urban planners to validate growth models. Moreover, it fosters informed decision-making on sustainability and resource management, based on verifiable and shared evidence.
