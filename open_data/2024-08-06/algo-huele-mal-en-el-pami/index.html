Algo huele mal en el PAMI

Algo huele mal en el PAMI

The article critiques the current state of public management in Argentina, arguing that widespread knowledge of institutional failures exposes a crisis in subadministration. It highlights how vacant official positions and reliance on interim leadership have created a disconnect between the government’s mandate and actual service delivery. This opacity is no longer viable in an era of social networks and an interconnected society, which ensure that public issues quickly become known to the broader community. A primary implication is the severe degradation of essential services, particularly in healthcare through institutions like PAMI. The text illustrates how systemic underfunding and poor management result in the provision of substandard goods and neglect of citizens’ rights, causing tangible harm to individuals who have contributed to the system for decades. These failures demonstrate that poor governance is not an isolated incident but a societal problem affecting everyone, eroding trust and exacerbating inequality. This narrative is relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparency in verifying the efficiency of public management and the allocation of resources. When information is accessible, citizens can hold officials accountable for service quality and financial responsibility. The article advocates for ethical governance and effective administration, suggesting that without robust open data and scrutiny, political cycles will continue to repeat failures rather than delivering the improvements voters expect.
