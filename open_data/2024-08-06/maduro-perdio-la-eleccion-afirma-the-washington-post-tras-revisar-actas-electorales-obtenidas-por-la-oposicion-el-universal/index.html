Maduro perdió la elección, afirma The Washington Post, tras revisar actas electorales obtenidas por la oposición | El Universal

Maduro perdió la elección, afirma The Washington Post, tras revisar actas electorales obtenidas por la oposición | El Universal

Independent analyses by major news organizations suggest that the opposition candidate in Venezuela’s recent elections likely received significantly more votes than the incumbent president. By examining thousands of physical tally sheets released by the opposition, journalists corroborated digital data indicating a decisive victory for the opposition, showing results far from the official announcements. This discrepancy highlights a substantial gap between the government's declared outcomes and the evidence preserved by civil society actors. The reliability of this alternative data stems from rigorous verification processes, including the physical inspection of hundreds of original documents stored securely by opposition networks to evade government repression. Although independent confirmation remains elusive because the national electoral council has not released its own records, the data indicates these documents originate from legitimate polling stations. This effort demonstrates how opposition groups are systematically collecting and digitizing electoral evidence despite political pressure and lack of institutional transparency. This case is highly relevant to open data as it illustrates how citizen-led transparency initiatives can challenge official narratives when state institutions withhold public information. The widespread digitization and analysis of physical records underscore the importance of accessible, verifiable data in ensuring electoral integrity. Furthermore, it showcases the critical role of third-party verification and open-source intelligence in holding power accountable when official channels fail to provide a complete or truthful picture of public mandates.
