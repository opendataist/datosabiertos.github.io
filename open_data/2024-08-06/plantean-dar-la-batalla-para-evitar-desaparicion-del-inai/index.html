Plantean dar la batalla para evitar desaparición del INAI

Plantean dar la batalla para evitar desaparición del INAI

Leaders from Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency and State Transparency Bodies have proposed a strategic action plan to defend their existence against proposed reforms that would dismantle them. They emphasize the necessity of engaging in technical debates involving civil society, academia, and international organizations to highlight the constitutional right to information. By fostering open dialogue and seeking convergence with incoming authorities, they aim to counter narratives that frame these institutions as obsolete or overly costly, arguing instead that their removal constitutes a significant regression in human rights protection. The articles underscore that transferring powers from independent bodies to government entities undermines the separation of powers and returns the system to outdated, non-transparent models. Experts warn that eliminating these oversight mechanisms not only compromises the public’s right to access information but also jeopardizes Mexico’s international reputation. Specifically, the loss of an independent data protection authority threatens diplomatic and commercial relations, as reliable data governance is a global standard required for maintaining trust in business and diplomatic engagements. This situation is critically relevant to open data because the integrity and independence of these institutions directly impact the reliability of public information and personal data protection. Without autonomous oversight, the quality and accessibility of open data are at risk, potentially discouraging investment and eroding public trust. The debate highlights how structural independence is essential for maintaining high standards in data governance, ensuring that open data initiatives remain credible, secure, and aligned with international human rights and business standards.
