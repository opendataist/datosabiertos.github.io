Tras unas elecciones muy igualadas, Turquía se encamina a una segunda vuelta

The article examines the intense Turkish presidential election, in which Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and opposition candidate Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu advance to a decisive runoff. The vote count was marked by deep polarization and mutual distrust, with the opposition questioning the transparency of official figures in light of data favorable to the ruling administration. Despite high voter turnout, the tense atmosphere reflects the democratic challenges that have emerged after more than a decade of authoritarian drift. This scenario is crucial for the open data movement, as it underscores the urgency of having independent and transparent verification mechanisms. When trust in institutions declines, the availability and accessibility of real-time electoral data become essential tools for combating misinformation. The lack of clarity in the vote-counting process fosters competing narratives that can undermine the legitimacy of the electoral process, demonstrating that technical transparency is fundamental to maintaining public credibility. Moreover, the legislative election results directly influence the dynamics of the runoff, given that the current electoral structure favors the ruling party. The opposition’s ability to secure a majority in parliament could determine whether constitutional reform is achieved or whether the existing presidential system is consolidated. For the open data community, this case illustrates how politics and citizenship depend on reliable, auditable data to exercise democratic sovereignty in contexts of high complexity.
