País borrado, país desaparecido

The article denounces that Venezuela has become a state without viable official statistics, where the websites of key agencies such as the National Institute of Statistics remain outdated, contain obsolete data, or simply do not function. This systemic opacity is not the result of technical chaos, but rather a deliberate policy to conceal the country’s demographic, economic, and social reality, keeping the population in a state of misinformation. The consequences of this lack of data go beyond mere unavailability of information; they prevent any rigorous state planning and effective public management. Without reliable figures on production, crime, health, or education, it is impossible to measure the effectiveness of implemented policies or to establish realistic foundations for decision-making. The regime prioritizes propaganda over transparency, eliminating the tools necessary to evaluate the impact of its governance. This situation is fundamental to the debate on open data, as it illustrates how the absence of public information serves as a tool for political control and censorship. The Venezuelan case demonstrates that transparency is not merely a technical issue, but an indispensable requirement for accountability and democracy. Without accessible and up-to-date data, civil society loses the capacity to monitor power and demand answers, consolidating a model of government based on deception and social fragmentation.
