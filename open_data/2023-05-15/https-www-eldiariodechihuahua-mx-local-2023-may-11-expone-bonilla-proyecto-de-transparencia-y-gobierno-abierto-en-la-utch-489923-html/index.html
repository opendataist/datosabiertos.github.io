The article highlights a crucial shift in governance, moving from passive state disclosure to active citizen engagement. The mayor emphasizes that while governments must provide open data and financial reports, citizens are equally responsible for demanding and utilizing this information. This reciprocity is presented as the most effective strategy to rebuild trust, counter the prevailing perception of corruption, and foster a culture of accountability in which public officials are answerable to an informed electorate. The relevance to open data lies in the recognition that data alone is insufficient without mechanisms for public scrutiny. The municipal administration’s adoption of international transparency standards and the publication of quarterly financial and asset reports demonstrate a technical commitment to openness. However, the narrative suggests that true transparency requires civic literacy, encouraging citizens to use these data points to monitor resource allocation and participate in processes such as public bidding through social witnessing. Ultimately, the article argues that improving a country’s global corruption perception index depends on this dual effort: institutional rigor in data release and societal pressure for its use. By highlighting local successes alongside the ongoing need for improvement, it illustrates how open data serves as a foundational tool for democratic oversight, transforming abstract statistics into tangible evidence of integrity and enabling the public to hold power to account.
