Freedom of Information requests in Northern Ireland reveal that recorded pandemic mortality significantly overstated direct Covid-19 fatalities, as most deaths involved co-existing conditions rather than the virus alone. Concurrently, influenza-related deaths vanished during the peak pandemic years before re-emerging, suggesting systemic changes in death certification and reporting practices. This discrepancy challenges official narratives regarding the specific lethality of the virus and highlights potential distortions in public health data during the crisis. Beyond mortality metrics, the data indicates that pandemic interventions caused severe collateral damage to the broader healthcare system. The prioritization of Covid patients led to extended waiting lists and reduced treatment rates for critical chronic conditions like cancer. This shift resulted in a noticeable rise in excess deaths from non-Covid causes, pointing to the long-term health consequences of diverting medical resources and suspending routine care for other diseases. This article is highly relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates the power of accessing raw government records to uncover hidden trends. By using FOI laws to extract granular statistics, researchers were able to challenge official narratives and expose the true impact of policy decisions. This case underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and detailed public data to ensure accurate public health assessment and accountability.
