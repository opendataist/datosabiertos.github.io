Journalists urged to use RTI for investigative reporting

The article highlights a strategic effort to empower journalists in Pakistan through the Punjab Transparency and Right to Information Act. By enhancing media professionals’ ability to utilize legal frameworks for investigative reporting, this initiative directly supports the core open data principle of public access to government-held information. This capacity building transforms passive transparency laws into active tools for accountability and truth-seeking. Focusing on the implications for civic engagement, the seminar emphasized how structured access to data helps combat misinformation and strengthens journalistic credibility. When citizens and media can effectively request and verify official records, it fosters a more informed public sphere. This process demonstrates that open data is not merely about releasing datasets, but about enabling society to use that information to challenge narratives and ensure institutional transparency. Ultimately, this initiative underscores the vital role of legal mechanisms in operationalizing open data rights. It illustrates how legislative frameworks, backed by independent oversight bodies, create the necessary infrastructure for democratic participation. By training journalists to navigate these systems, the program reinforces the connection between legal transparency rights and the practical dissemination of reliable information, a fundamental aspect of a healthy open data ecosystem.
