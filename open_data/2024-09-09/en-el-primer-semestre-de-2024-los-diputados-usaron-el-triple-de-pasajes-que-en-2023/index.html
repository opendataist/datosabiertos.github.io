En el primer semestre de 2024, los diputados usaron el triple de pasajes que en 2023

The article reveals a significant increase in the use of tickets allocated to Argentine lawmakers, linking this rise to greater legislative activity as well as to a debate over the discretion involved in their distribution. Regulations allow legislators to assign non-nominated ticket segments to third parties, a practice that defenders argue is necessary for political work and social assistance in the provinces, while critics point to the lack of transparency and the risk of partisan use. The relevance to open data lies in the demand for public access to this information. Updated data show that, although the Chamber publishes records, there is a critical need for open databases that detail the specific destination of each ticket. Without such granular transparency, citizens cannot assess whether resources are legitimately allocated to parliamentary work or to the cultivation of political loyalties, thereby hindering effective accountability. The underlying discussion centers on the need to modernize public resource management through data openness. By enabling public scrutiny of representatives’ decisions, open data not only reduces discretion but also promotes a more transparent political culture. This case illustrates how the availability of structured and accessible data is essential to transform public perception of legislative spending and strengthen institutional trust.
