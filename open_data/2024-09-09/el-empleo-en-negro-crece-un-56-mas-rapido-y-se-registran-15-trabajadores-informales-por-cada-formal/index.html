El empleo en negro crece un 56% más rápido y se registran 1,5 trabajadores informales por cada formal

El empleo en negro crece un 56% más rápido y se registran 1,5 trabajadores informales por cada formal

The article reveals a critical shift in Argentina’s labor market, where informal employment is expanding at a significantly faster rate than formal jobs. This trend highlights a deepening precarization of work, driven by economic recession and declining purchasing power, which forces many into unregulated sectors lacking social security. Consequently, vulnerable groups such as women, youth, and domestic workers face heightened exclusion from essential rights like pensions and health coverage, exacerbating existing social inequalities. This narrative is vital for open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible statistical records to monitor labor dynamics accurately. High-quality public data enables researchers and policymakers to identify structural vulnerabilities, track the correlation between economic downturns and job quality, and advocate for evidence-based interventions. Without reliable open datasets, the scale of informal labor remains obscured, hindering efforts to protect workers' rights and design effective social policies. Ultimately, the article emphasizes that informal work is not merely a national issue but a widespread challenge across Latin America. By making such data openly available, stakeholders can foster greater accountability and facilitate comparative analyses across borders. This transparency empowers civil society to demand better working conditions and pushes governments to address the root causes of labor informality, ensuring that economic recovery includes formal, dignified employment for all citizens.
