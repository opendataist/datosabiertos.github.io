‘Con propuesta de desaparecer, nos metieron a un juego electoral que no pedimos’: INAI | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The article highlights a significant internal conflict within Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI) concerning the executive branch’s proposal to abolish autonomous bodies. Commissioner Julieta del Río argues that the institute has been forcibly drawn into a political and electoral game, contradicting the president’s claim that the body remains neutral. This divergence reveals the immense pressure exerted on transparency institutions when their existence is tied to partisan interests, challenging the ideal of technical impartiality. President Adrián Alcalá advocates for an open parliamentary process to evaluate the reform, warning that eliminating the INAI would violate international trade obligations under the USMCA, potentially triggering international legal challenges. The debate centers on preserving democratic mechanisms that ensure public access to government information. This tension underscores the fragility of institutional independence when legislative reforms threaten the structural integrity of oversight bodies essential for democratic accountability. This situation is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how political agendas can jeopardize the legal frameworks protecting public information. The potential abolition of the INAI would not only weaken data privacy rights but also diminish the enforceability of transparency laws. It illustrates that open data initiatives depend on robust, independent institutions; without them, access to government data becomes vulnerable to political manipulation, eroding public trust and international compliance with data protection standards.
