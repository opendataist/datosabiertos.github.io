Detector: portada de Pastrana sobre la “lista Epstein” es un montaje

The article debunks a viral fake image claiming that former Colombian President Andrés Pastrana was featured on a pedophilia-related cover of *Semana* magazine, linked to Jeffrey Epstein’s network. Verification confirms the image is a digital fabrication, utilizing incorrect typography and design elements not used by the publication, and no such edition exists. This misrepresentation exploits public curiosity regarding high-profile names associated with the Epstein case to generate false accusations and spread disinformation. Clarifying the context, the circulation of these rumors stems from a misunderstanding of recently declassified judicial documents in the case against Ghislaine Maxwell. Contrary to claims of a definitive “client list,” these files contain various testimonies and emails where names are mentioned merely as associated individuals, without implying guilt or illegal activity. Crucially, Pastrana’s name does not appear in these specific judicial records, although he had previously acknowledged being a passenger on Epstein’s private jet for travel to Cuba, denying any involvement with the island’s illegal activities. This story is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical need for verifying the provenance and integrity of digital information against official public records. It highlights how easily manipulated visuals and selective interpretations of legal archives can be weaponized to distort truth. Understanding the difference between unverified social media narratives and accessible, authentic government documents is essential for maintaining data literacy and preventing the spread of fabricated political attacks in the digital age.
