C-LM se sitúa por debajo de la media nacional en la tasa de litigiosidad, con 134,89 casos por cada mil habitantes

The 2024 judicial data reveal a persistent gap between the number of incoming cases and the capacity to resolve them across Spain, indicating systemic strain within the legal infrastructure. Despite a significant increase in resolved cases, the backlog continues to grow, suggesting that the current judicial framework struggles to keep pace with the rising volume of litigation. This imbalance highlights urgent challenges in ensuring timely justice for citizens and maintaining institutional efficiency. Regional disparities further complicate this landscape, with certain autonomous communities experiencing disproportionately high litigation rates while others remain comparatively stable. These variations underscore the need for tailored regional strategies to address local legal pressures. Understanding these geographic trends is crucial for policymakers aiming to distribute judicial resources more equitably and improve access to justice across different territories. This report is vital for open data initiatives, as it exemplifies how transparent, accessible public statistics can drive evidence-based policy reforms. By analyzing such granular datasets, stakeholders can identify inefficiencies, advocate for necessary structural improvements, and ensure that judicial management aligns with societal needs. Open access to this information empowers researchers and citizens to monitor government performance and foster greater accountability within the public sector.
