Trump desclasificó miles de documentos sin editar sobre el asesinat...

The Trump administration’s decision to release unedited JFK assassination records marks a significant shift toward maximum transparency in historical intelligence. By publishing thousands of previously restricted documents, the government aims to eliminate lingering secrecy surrounding the event, challenging previous narratives with unprecedented openness. This move allows researchers and citizens to access raw data without administrative filters, potentially revealing new evidence regarding the Warren Commission’s lone gunman theory. The immediate implication is a democratization of historical truth, enabling independent verification rather than relying solely on official conclusions. For open data, this case highlights the critical importance of machine-readable, uncensored archives. It demonstrates how removing classification barriers can transform opaque government records into usable resources, fostering accountability and deeper public understanding of complex historical events through direct access to primary sources.
