Bases de la CIA fueron reveladas en documentos secretos del asesinato de Kennedy, ¿Colombia cuenta con alguna?

The Trump administration’s release of thousands of unedited documents related to the JFK assassination finally reveals the extensive, covert operational network of the CIA across Latin America and the globe. This disclosure goes beyond the original investigation into the assassination, exposing decades of secret intelligence activities, including undisclosed bases in major cities such as Bogotá and Caracas, which were used during the Cold War to monitor regional stability and advance U.S. interests. The relevance to open data lies in the unprecedented transparency surrounding historical government secrecy. By declassifying these files, authorities dismantle the long-standing narrative of complete intelligence opacity, enabling researchers and the public to access primary sources that detail the true scope of agency operations. This shift from restricted archives to accessible digital records strengthens civic accountability and historical analysis. Ultimately, these documents challenge previous assumptions about the limits of covert action, highlighting the CIA’s deep embeddedness in foreign nations under the guise of national security. The availability of this information is crucial for understanding how intelligence gathering intersects with political history, demonstrating that open access to state records is essential for verifying official narratives and exposing hidden geopolitical strategies.
