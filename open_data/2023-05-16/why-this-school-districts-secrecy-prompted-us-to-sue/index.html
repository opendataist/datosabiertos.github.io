Why this school district’s secrecy prompted us to sue

The article argues that a 2017 Iowa law designed to mandate public disclosure of employee termination reasons is being undermined by school districts prioritizing secrecy over accountability. By classifying forced departures as voluntary resignations, officials evade the requirement to explain the rationale behind dismissing staff, effectively hiding misconduct from the public. This opacity prevents taxpayers from understanding how their districts handle allegations of serious misconduct and shields problematic employees from public scrutiny. Such lack of transparency creates dangerous gaps in public safety, as illustrated by cases where accused educators resigned abruptly without background checks or licensing board notifications. Without mandatory disclosure of the specific reasons for leaving, these individuals can secure new teaching positions elsewhere, potentially exposing future students to similar risks. The failure to document and share these details allows misconduct to recur across different districts, betraying the trust of parents and communities who rely on institutional integrity. This narrative is critically relevant to open data because it highlights the urgent need for structured, accessible information regarding public sector employment and performance. When data on resignations and disciplinary actions is withheld or obscured, the public loses essential insight into the quality and safety of public services. True open data requires not just the existence of records, but their meaningful availability, ensuring that citizens can hold institutions accountable and make informed decisions based on comprehensive, unredacted facts.
