Crucial that RTE's new Register of Interests is made public – Cannon

The article argues that the public must have access to RTE’s Register of Interests for on-air staff engaged in paid external promotions. This transparency is essential for viewers to assess potential biases and verify the impartiality of news reporting. Without clear visibility into corporate sponsorships affecting journalists, maintaining trust in the broadcaster’s integrity becomes significantly more challenging. Implementing a robust disclosure system modeled after the BBC’s framework is proposed as the optimal solution. This approach requires regular, public updates of all external earnings and engagements, ensuring accountability for both talent and senior leaders. Such a structured, monthly publication schedule would provide consistent oversight, distinguishing RTE’s professional standards from those deemed acceptable by journalist unions for internal use only. This initiative is vital for open data advocacy as it highlights how accessible public records foster democratic accountability in media. By demanding transparency in financial relationships, society can better evaluate the influence of private interests on public information. Open access to these registers empowers citizens to make informed judgments about the credibility of news sources, reinforcing the link between data availability and institutional trust.
