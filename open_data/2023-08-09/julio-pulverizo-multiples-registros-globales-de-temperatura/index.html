Julio pulverizó múltiples registros globales de temperatura

July 2023 shattered historical temperature records, marking the hottest month since data collection began in 1940. This unprecedented heatwave was driven by a combination of anthropogenic emissions and unusually high sea surface temperatures, which have remained at historic highs since April. The event signifies a critical breach of the 1.5°C threshold above pre-industrial levels, illustrating the urgent reality of climate change and serving as a stark preview of future global conditions. This phenomenon highlights the accelerating impact of human-induced greenhouse gas emissions on planetary stability. The correlation between rising oceanic temperatures and atmospheric heat underscores the interconnected nature of Earth’s climate systems. Consequently, extreme weather events are becoming more frequent and intense, affecting millions worldwide and demanding immediate, coordinated action to mitigate further warming trends. The article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the power of transparent, publicly accessible meteorological records in tracking global environmental shifts. By making detailed climate datasets available, organizations enable researchers and policymakers to verify critical climate milestones accurately. This transparency fosters public awareness and supports evidence-based decision-making, ensuring that societal responses to the climate crisis are grounded in reliable, verifiable scientific observation rather than speculation.
