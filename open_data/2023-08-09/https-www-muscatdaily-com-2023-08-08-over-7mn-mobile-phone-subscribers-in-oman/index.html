Oman’s telecommunications sector has experienced significant expansion, driven by a substantial surge in mobile subscriptions and a strategic shift toward postpaid plans. The data reveals that users are increasingly migrating from prepaid to postpaid services, reflecting higher demand for data usage and greater willingness to commit to long-term contracts. This trend indicates a maturing market where consumers value reliability and enhanced connectivity over the flexibility of prepayment models. Telecom operators have responded to this shift by reporting robust financial growth, primarily anchored in increased revenue from postpaid mobile and broadband services. Major providers like Omantel and Ooredoo have seen their customer bases expand, with the majority of new acquisitions coming from the postpaid segment. This transition not only boosts individual subscriber value but also stabilizes revenue streams for companies, highlighting the economic viability of modernizing service offerings to meet evolving consumer needs. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical role of accurate, publicly available statistics from national centers and regulatory authorities in tracking technological adoption and economic trends. By making such granular data on subscriptions and revenue accessible, policymakers and analysts can better understand digital infrastructure development. Transparent reporting enables stakeholders to assess market health, guide future investments, and ensure that telecommunications growth aligns with broader national development goals, demonstrating how open data facilitates informed decision-making in the digital economy.
