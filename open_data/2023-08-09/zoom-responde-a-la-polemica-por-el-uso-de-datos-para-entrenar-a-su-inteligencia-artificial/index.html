Zoom responde a la polémica por el uso de datos para entrenar a su inteligencia artificial

Zoom has reversed its decision to use content from video calls and chats to train its artificial intelligence models, following significant public controversy. The company confirmed that, in order to maintain trust and regulatory compliance, it will not use audio, video, or text data for AI development without users’ explicit consent. This change aims to give account administrators and owners full control over these sensitive features. It is particularly crucial to highlight the protection of educational data and health records. Zoom commits to respecting applicable laws and separate terms, ensuring that sensitive information belonging to students and patients is not used to improve its algorithms. This distinction underscores the importance of handling highly sensitive personal information with higher privacy standards, differentiating it from general usage data used to enhance the user experience. This incident is pivotal for the fields of open data and technology governance, as it establishes a clear precedent regarding the need for algorithmic transparency and informed consent. It illustrates the inherent tension between data-driven innovation and the protection of individual privacy. By prioritizing user control over automatic AI training, Zoom reinforces the principle that the technical availability of data should not override individuals’ fundamental rights, thereby driving more ethical standards in the handling of digital information.
