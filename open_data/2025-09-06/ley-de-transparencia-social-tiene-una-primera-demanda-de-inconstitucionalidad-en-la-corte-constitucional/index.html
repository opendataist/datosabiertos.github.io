Ley de Transparencia Social tiene una primera demanda de inconstitucionalidad en la Corte Constitucional

Ley de Transparencia Social tiene una primera demanda de inconstitucionalidad en la Corte Constitucional

The Social Transparency Law is facing a constitutional challenge that alleges serious procedural violations. Critics argue that the legislation was improperly rushed through the legislature, bypassing essential deliberation and violating the principle of legal certainty. By using the law as an emergency vehicle for unrelated economic reforms, the process undermined democratic rigor and set a dangerous precedent for legislative integrity. The core of the dispute centers on the lack of thematic unity, with provisions on taxation and mining abruptly added to a statute primarily dedicated to regulating non-profit organizations. This inclusion suggests that the law served as a covert channel to pass controversial reforms without the required specialized debate. Such a practice erodes the structural coherence of legislation and denies citizens the transparency and scrutiny that these distinct policy areas demand. This case is crucial for open data because it highlights the critical need for clear, lawful data governance structures. If the legal framework governing transparency is fundamentally flawed, the resulting data systems may lack legitimacy and reliability. Ensuring that laws adhere to strict procedural standards protects the integrity of public information, fostering genuine accountability rather than opaque, procedurally compromised regulatory regimes.
