El debate sobre los fijos discontinuos

The article argues that Spain’s official unemployment statistics are artificially deflated due to the reclassification of temporary contracts into "fixed-discontinuous" agreements. While the government highlights a drop in temporary hiring and lower overall unemployment rates, this shift masks a significant portion of the workforce. Many workers in these new contracts remain inactive for large periods, effectively unemployed, but are excluded from official jobless counts because they receive specific benefits rather than traditional unemployment aid. Consequently, the "real" unemployment rate is substantially higher than reported figures suggest. Independent analyses indicate that when inactive fixed-discontinuous workers are accounted for, actual unemployment in Spain exceeds official numbers by nearly 30%, returning to levels seen before the pandemic. This discrepancy reveals that despite legislative efforts to stabilize the labor market, the underlying precariousness and high joblessness persist, challenging the narrative of a successful labor reform. This situation is critical for open data advocates because it highlights how statistical methodology changes can obscure social realities and hinder transparency. The lack of publicly available, disaggregated data on these specific contract types prevents accurate public scrutiny and policy evaluation. True open data requires not just the publication of numbers, but the clarity and accessibility needed to understand the full scope of socioeconomic conditions, ensuring that citizens and analysts can assess government performance against a comprehensive and truthful baseline.
