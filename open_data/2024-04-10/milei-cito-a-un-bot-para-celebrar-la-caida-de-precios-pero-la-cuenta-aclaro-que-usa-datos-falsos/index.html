Milei citó a un bot para celebrar la caída de precios, pero la cuenta aclaró que usa datos falsos

This article highlights a significant instance of misinformation involving the monitoring of economic indicators, illustrating the critical importance of data transparency and verification. The president and the economy minister cited a Twitter account, "Jumbo Bot," as evidence of deflation, only for it to be revealed as a satirical experiment that never actually tracked real prices. This event underscores how non-verified, non-open data sources can be mistaken for authoritative evidence, potentially leading to flawed public policy decisions or misleading narratives about economic reality. The relevance to open data lies in the necessity for trustworthy, accessible, and auditable information systems. When official discourse relies on opaque or fictitious metrics, it erodes public trust and obscures the true state of affairs, such as the ongoing recession and inflation. Open data principles mandate that metrics used for public accountability must be derived from clear, verifiable, and standardized sources. Without such rigor, society cannot accurately assess government performance or understand the actual impact of economic measures on citizens' lives. Ultimately, this incident serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of ignoring established data governance. It emphasizes that for open data to fulfill its democratic function, officials must commit to using transparent methodologies rather than anecdotal or fabricated proxies. Ensuring that economic data is open, accurate, and independently verifiable is essential for building informed public debate and maintaining credibility in democratic institutions.
