Google's alleged YouTube scraping could be the biggest AI controversy yet

Major AI developers like OpenAI and Google have trained models on millions of YouTube videos, directly contradicting platform policies against unauthorized scraping. Despite internal awareness, these companies failed to intervene, prioritizing their own AI development goals over creator rights and copyright compliance. This lack of transparency raises serious ethical concerns about mass copyright infringement and the exploitation of creative content without permission. The situation highlights a systemic disregard for intellectual property, fueling fears that AI growth is built on the unauthorized use of human-generated work. This controversy is highly relevant to open data, as it demonstrates how opaque training datasets can undermine trust and legal standards. It underscores the critical need for clear, auditable data sourcing practices in AI development to ensure accountability and respect for creators’ rights.
