Computer hackers broke into City of London IT systems five times in last 4 years

The recent surge in ransomware attacks against major UK public institutions highlights the critical vulnerability of essential governance structures to cyber threats. These incidents demonstrate that traditional physical security measures are no longer sufficient, as organized digital criminals increasingly target high-value data with sophisticated, corporate-like operations. This shift underscores an urgent need for robust digital defenses and specialized skills within the public sector to mitigate national security risks. For the open data community, this situation emphasizes the delicate balance between transparency and security. While open data initiatives aim to increase government accountability, they also expand the potential attack surface for malicious actors. Consequently, protecting the integrity of publicly available datasets is paramount, as breaches can compromise personal information and erode public trust in institutional data management practices. Ultimately, this article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the consequences of inadequate cybersecurity infrastructure. It serves as a stark reminder that open government policies must be paired with rigorous security protocols to ensure that data remains both accessible and safe. Without such protections, the benefits of open data are overshadowed by the significant risks of data exposure and the potential for widespread digital crime.
