Thousands of Derriford patients waited over 24 hours in A&E

The article reveals a dramatic surge in patient waiting times in UK hospitals, particularly for elderly individuals, following years of pandemic-related pressures. Data obtained via Freedom of Information requests indicates that thousands of patients spent over 24 hours in Emergency Departments before admission, a stark contrast to pre-2020 levels. This trend highlights a systemic strain on healthcare infrastructure, with older adults disproportionately affected, comprising the majority of those experiencing these severe delays. In response to these challenges, hospital trusts and government officials have pointed to increased demand, seasonal illnesses, and workforce issues as primary drivers. NHS leadership emphasizes ongoing efforts to alleviate pressure through staffing increases, expanded community-based care models, and new facility developments. Conversely, political opponents attribute the crisis to government neglect, arguing that chronic underfunding and social care failures are the root causes of the deteriorating emergency care standards and prolonged patient suffering. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency mechanisms, such as Freedom of Information requests, enable civil society and the media to hold public institutions accountable. By making internal hospital statistics public, open data facilitates informed public debate and political scrutiny. It allows citizens to visualize the scale of service failures, transforming abstract administrative challenges into tangible evidence of the need for policy reform and resource allocation in the public health sector.
