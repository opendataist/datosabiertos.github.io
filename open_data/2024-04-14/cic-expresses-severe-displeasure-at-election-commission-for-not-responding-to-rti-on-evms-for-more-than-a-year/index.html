CIC Expresses 'Severe Displeasure' At Election Commission For Not Responding To RTI On EVMs For More Than A Year

The Central Information Commission’s recent order highlights a critical failure in public accountability, specifically criticizing the Election Commission of India for ignoring a Right to Information request concerning voting machine credibility for over a year. This ruling underscores the fragility of transparency mechanisms within major electoral bodies, demonstrating how institutional silence can undermine public trust in democratic processes. The core issue revolves around the demand for answers regarding the electronic voting infrastructure, where officials dismissed the absence of records as proof of non-receipt. By mandating a written explanation for this gross violation, the Commission reinforces that statutory timelines are not merely suggestions but enforceable legal obligations. This emphasizes that authorities must maintain rigorous documentation practices to satisfy citizens’ demands for information, preventing bureaucratic evasion through neglect. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the necessity of responsive data governance in high-stakes sectors like elections. It serves as a warning that without strict compliance with information disclosure laws, the potential for corruption and misinformation increases. Ultimately, the order validates the RTI Act as a vital tool for enforcing transparency, ensuring that public institutions remain answerable to the citizens they serve by prioritizing accessible, verifiable data over secrecy.
