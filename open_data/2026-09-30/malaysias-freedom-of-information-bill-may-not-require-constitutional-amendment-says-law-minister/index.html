Malaysia’s Freedom of Information Bill may not require constitutional amendment, says law minister

Malaysia’s Freedom of Information Bill may not require constitutional amendment, says law minister

The proposed Freedom of Information Bill 2026 in Malaysia may pass without constitutional amendment, relying on a simple majority, though legal experts remain divided on this procedural necessity. This uncertainty highlights the complex legislative hurdles involved in establishing robust transparency frameworks, a critical consideration for open data advocates navigating varying political and legal landscapes globally. Legislators emphasize that the law must genuinely enable public access to government documents rather than imposing restrictive barriers that effectively nullify its purpose. Ensuring the legislation protects media freedom is paramount, as open data initiatives depend on a free press to interpret and disseminate information effectively to the public. The review process actively seeks public feedback to identify weaknesses and balance transparency with legitimate confidentiality concerns. This participatory approach underscores the importance of stakeholder engagement in crafting effective open data policies, ensuring they meet international standards and truly empower citizens with accessible, usable government information.
