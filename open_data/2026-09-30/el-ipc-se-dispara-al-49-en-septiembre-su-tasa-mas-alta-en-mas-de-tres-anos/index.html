El IPC se dispara al 4,9% en septiembre, su tasa más alta en más de tres años

El IPC se dispara al 4,9% en septiembre, su tasa más alta en más de tres años

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) reached its highest level since early 2023, driven by the persistent impact of the war in the Middle East. This inflationary increase is not solely attributable to internal market dynamics; rather, it reflects the transmission of global geopolitical crises to the local economy, highlighting the vulnerability of energy prices to external shocks. To mitigate these effects, the Government has begun preparing new protective measures. These initiatives aim not only to alleviate households’ purchasing power through direct compensation but also to structurally transform the economy. The long-term approach prioritizes reducing dependence on external energy sources and accelerating the transition to electrification, seeking greater resilience against future volatility in international markets. This situation is relevant to open data, as it underscores the critical need for timely and detailed access to official statistics. Analyzing the components of the CPI allows for the identification of specific sources of inflationary pressure, facilitating the assessment of the effectiveness of public policies. Transparency in these data empowers civil society and researchers to understand how external variables impact daily life and economic planning.
