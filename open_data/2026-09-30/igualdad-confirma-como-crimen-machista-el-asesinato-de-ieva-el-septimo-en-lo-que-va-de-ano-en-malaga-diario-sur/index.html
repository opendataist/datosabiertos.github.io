The article highlights a significant discrepancy in official femicide statistics, revealing that multiple victims, such as transgender women and daughters of previous victims, are excluded due to bureaucratic barriers or restrictive legal interpretations. This systematic omission distorts the true perception of gender-based violence, demonstrating that current records do not accurately reflect the full scope of lethal aggressions—a critical failure in the management and visibility of public gender-related data. The exclusion of specific cases underscores the urgency of revising registration criteria to ensure that public information is inclusive and representative. By ignoring these deaths, institutions perpetuate an incomplete narrative that hinders the design of effective policies and the appropriate allocation of resources, compromising the transparency and accountability necessary to eradicate this violence. This narrative is fundamental to the field of open data because it illustrates how biases in data collection and definition create informational gaps with real social consequences. It advocates for the need for accurate, disaggregated, and inclusive open data that enable robust analysis and informed policy action, ensuring that no victim remains invisible in official statistics and thereby promoting fairer and more effective governance.
