Rise in medicinal cannabis prescriptions and admissions

Rise in medicinal cannabis prescriptions and admissions

The article highlights a significant rise in medicinal cannabis prescriptions and hospital admissions linked to cannabis use, raising questions about public health impacts. While prescription numbers have climbed steadily year over year, emergency department data shows that cannabis-linked admissions increased from sixteen percent to eighteen percent in a single year. This trend suggests that as availability grows, so does the prevalence of adverse health outcomes requiring medical intervention, particularly for severe conditions like psychosis and cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome. Despite these statistics, industry leaders and law enforcement dispute the severity of the crisis, arguing that regulated medicinal use is safe and distinct from recreational abuse. The medicinal cannabis industry CEO notes a lack of serious psychosis reports from general practitioners and emphasizes that diversion is a minimal issue handled by police. This perspective contrasts with hospital data, illustrating a disconnect between clinical observations and industry claims regarding the safety profile of legally prescribed cannabis products. This content is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the value of Freedom of Information requests in revealing fragmented health and policing metrics. The article illustrates how disparate datasets—prescription records, emergency attendances, and arrest statistics—can be analyzed together to challenge narratives and inform policy. It underscores the importance of transparent, accessible data in understanding the real-world effects of regulated substances, enabling stakeholders to make evidence-based decisions rather than relying on anecdotal evidence or conflicting claims.
