You can now try OpenAI's gpt-oss:120b for free and privately without using Ollama or LMStudio

DuckDuckGo has integrated the massive gpt-oss:120b open-source language model into its private chat service, allowing users to access powerful AI capabilities without local hardware constraints. This development is significant because the model’s sheer size typically requires expensive, specialized consumer GPUs to run effectively. By hosting it on their servers, DuckDuckGo democratizes access to high-performance open-source technology, removing the financial and technical barriers that usually prevent average users from utilizing such sophisticated tools. The platform offers a streamlined, privacy-focused experience by requiring no account registration and anonymizing all interactions, which aligns with the transparency and user sovereignty principles often championed within the open data community. While the service currently hides the model’s internal reasoning processes and restricts file uploads, these limitations may serve as deliberate measures to protect user privacy and data integrity. This approach highlights a growing trend where open-source models are leveraged by privacy-centric platforms to provide robust services that respect user anonymity, balancing computational accessibility with strict data protection standards. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates a practical application of open-source AI that prioritizes user privacy and accessibility. It illustrates how leveraging open models can reduce reliance on proprietary, data-hungry ecosystems, offering an alternative that keeps data interactions transparent and secure. By making powerful open-source technology available without compromising personal information, this initiative supports the broader open data ethos of creating equitable, trustworthy, and user-controlled digital tools.
