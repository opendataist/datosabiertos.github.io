Global potato statistics: Latest FAO data published

Global potato production reaches massive scales, led by dominant contributors like China and India, with total output comprising hundreds of millions of tonnes. This significant agricultural volume highlights the crop’s critical role in global food security and supply chains, demonstrating how a single commodity drives economic and nutritional stability across diverse regions. Understanding these production levels is essential for analyzing market dynamics and resource allocation in the agricultural sector. The distribution of cultivation spans numerous countries, revealing widespread global engagement rather than concentration in a few nations. Data indicates substantial harvested areas with varying efficiency metrics, showing that while some regions lead in volume, others contribute through extensive land use. This broad participation underscores the universal importance of potato farming, reflecting adaptability to different climates and agricultural practices worldwide. This information is relevant to open data initiatives as it highlights the value of accessible, standardized datasets for tracking global trends. The FAO’s FAOSTAT database exemplifies how transparent, publicly available data enables researchers and policymakers to conduct independent analyses without relying on fragmented national sources. Such openness facilitates cross-border comparisons, improves transparency in agricultural reporting, and empowers users to verify statistics, thereby fostering more informed decision-making in food system management and sustainable development efforts.
