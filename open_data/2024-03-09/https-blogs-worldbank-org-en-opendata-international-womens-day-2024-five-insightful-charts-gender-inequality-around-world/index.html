The article highlights the critical need for data-driven interventions to achieve gender parity, emphasizing that combating gender-based violence is fundamental to human rights and economic stability. It underscores how prevalent violence disproportionately affects young women, causing severe physical and mental health issues while limiting access to education and employment. This pervasive inequality creates a cycle that hinders broader developmental goals, making comprehensive statistical tracking essential for identifying and addressing these systemic barriers effectively. Economic disparities are further exacerbated by women’s disproportionate burden of unpaid care work, which restricts their labor force participation and confines many to low-paying informal jobs. Although human capital has improved for both genders, women still face significant economic hurdles due to time poverty and lack of opportunity. Valuing this unpaid labor is crucial for improving labor market outcomes. By recognizing these constraints, policymakers can design more inclusive economic strategies that allow women to fully participate in and contribute to the global economy. Finally, the text reveals a persistent gap in leadership representation, with women remaining underrepresented in national governance and corporate management despite modest gains in parliament seats. Closing these gaps is vital for enhancing economic competitiveness and resilience, particularly against challenges like climate change and technological shifts. This article is highly relevant to open data because accurate, accessible statistics on violence, economic participation, and leadership are indispensable for monitoring progress, holding institutions accountable, and crafting evidence-based policies that promote genuine gender equality worldwide.
