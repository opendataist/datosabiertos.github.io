The National Institute of Statistics (INE) has celebrated surpassing the national volunteer targets for the census, while highlighting critical recruitment gaps in Santa Cruz and Cochabamba, urging final local efforts. This situation underscores the logistical challenges inherent in executing large-scale demographic data collection, which relies heavily on accessible, community-driven participation. The data reveals that despite high national mobilization, regional disparities persist, with specific urban zones still understaffed. These shortages illustrate the practical difficulties in achieving comprehensive geographic coverage, a key metric for evaluating the completeness of public census operations. This case is relevant to open data as it demonstrates the intersection of official statistics with grassroots civic engagement. It highlights how transparent reporting on recruitment progress helps identify systemic bottlenecks, ensuring that future datasets are more inclusive and accurately reflect the entire population without significant demographic blind spots.
