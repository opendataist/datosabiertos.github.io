Estudio analiza tiempo de trabajo de las mujeres en Costa Rica - Noticias Prensa Latina

Estudio analiza tiempo de trabajo de las mujeres en Costa Rica - Noticias Prensa Latina

The research reveals a profound gender disparity in unpaid care work, with women dedicating significantly more hours than men to domestic duties and caregiving. This unequal distribution restricts their participation in the formal labor market, limiting income, pensions, and career progression. Consequently, households led by women face higher poverty rates, exacerbating economic vulnerability across different income levels. This imbalance represents a critical socio-economic challenge, particularly as demographic shifts reduce the available workforce. Integrating these women into the labor force is essential for national development, yet care responsibilities remain the primary obstacle. The article highlights that while women often work longer total hours, their unpaid contributions are unrecognized, leading to higher unemployment and access to lower-quality jobs. Relevant to open data, these findings underscore the necessity of comprehensive, gender-disaggregated statistics to visualize and measure unpaid labor. Current data gaps obscure the true economic value of care work, hindering effective policy design. Improving data transparency and inclusion is vital for recognizing this invisible economy and creating strategies that promote gender equity and support sustainable economic growth.
