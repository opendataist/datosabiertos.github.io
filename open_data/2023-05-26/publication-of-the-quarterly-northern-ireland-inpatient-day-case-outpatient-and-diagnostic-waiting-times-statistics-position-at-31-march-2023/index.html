Publication of the quarterly Northern Ireland inpatient, day case, outpatient and diagnostic waiting times statistics - position at 31 March 2023

The Department of Health’s latest report reveals significant challenges in meeting Northern Ireland’s healthcare targets, particularly regarding waiting times for outpatient and diagnostic services. Although the total number of patients waiting for inpatient or day-case treatment saw a marginal decline, the median waiting times remain drastically longer than the recommended nine-week standard for outpatients and diagnostic tests. This data highlights a systemic delay where the majority of patients endure waits far exceeding policy guidelines, indicating persistent capacity constraints within the healthcare system. To address statistical anomalies caused by extreme outliers, the government has adopted median and 95th percentile metrics for reporting. This methodological shift provides a more accurate representation of the typical patient experience by mitigating the distortion caused by very long waits, while the 95th percentile specifically identifies the threshold beyond which a small fraction of patients face exceptional delays. This approach ensures that policymakers and the public can better understand the core distribution of waiting times rather than focusing solely on averages that may mask underlying inequalities. This publication is vital for open data advocates as it demonstrates the evolution of health sector transparency and metric standardization. By making detailed, granular statistics on waiting lists and diagnostic turnarounds publicly available, the department facilitates independent analysis and accountability. The release of these comprehensive datasets allows researchers, journalists, and citizens to scrutinize healthcare performance, assess the impact of policy decisions, and hold health providers responsible for adhering to service level agreements.
