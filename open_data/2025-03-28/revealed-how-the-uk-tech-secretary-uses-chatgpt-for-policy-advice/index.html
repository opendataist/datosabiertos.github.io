Revealed: How the UK tech secretary uses ChatGPT for policy advice

Revealed: How the UK tech secretary uses ChatGPT for policy advice

The revelation that UK Technology Secretary Peter Kyle used ChatGPT for official inquiries marks a significant precedent in the intersection of artificial intelligence and public transparency. By obtaining these interactions under Freedom of Information laws, New Scientist highlighted a world-first scenario where AI dialogues are treated as public records. This development challenges existing legal frameworks, raising fundamental questions about whether generative AI conversations constitute private digital footprints or new, state-created information subject to public scrutiny. A critical implication for open data is the emerging ambiguity regarding data classification. Legal experts debate whether AI interactions resemble traditional communication logs, like emails, or search queries, which are often exempt from disclosure. Unlike simple search results that retrieve existing data, ChatGPT generates novel responses, complicating the definition of public records. This uncertainty suggests that current transparency laws may be ill-equipped to handle the unique data production mechanisms of large language models, potentially requiring new legislative definitions to clarify what constitutes open government information. Ultimately, this case underscores the urgent need for clear governance regarding the use of proprietary commercial AI in government operations. The exposure of these chats warns that reliance on private sector tools can inadvertently create vast, searchable archives of ministerial reasoning and strategy. To safeguard both data sovereignty and public accountability, policymakers are advised to prioritize open-source or government-developed AI solutions. Ensuring that the tools used to process public information are themselves transparent is essential for maintaining trust in the integrity of open data ecosystems.
