Roban datos personales en redes falsas de WiFi

Roban datos personales en redes falsas de WiFi

The Cyber Police warn that public WiFi networks, even those offered by government agencies or businesses, pose significant security risks. Criminals use fake access points as bait to intercept data traffic, monitoring activity in real time and stealing sensitive information. This facilitates crimes such as fraud, extortion, and identity theft, since anyone with technical knowledge can exploit these security vulnerabilities. Attackers employ sophisticated techniques, such as creating networks with names similar to legitimate ones, to trick users into connecting inadvertently. By joining these networks, devices can become infected with malware that allows remote access to personal information, even after disconnecting. The interception of passwords and banking data is common, posing a serious threat to users’ financial integrity and digital identity, regardless of the service provider’s reputation. This article is relevant to the field of open data because it illustrates how accessible network infrastructure, often associated with transparency and public access, can become a vector of vulnerability if not properly managed. The exposure of personal data in open connectivity environments underscores the critical need to implement robust privacy, encryption, and authentication standards in any system handling sensitive information. Understanding these threats is essential for designing data policies that protect citizens without restricting their access to public digital services.
