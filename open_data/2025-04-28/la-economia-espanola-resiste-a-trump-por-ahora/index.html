La economía española resiste a Trump, por ahora

La economía española resiste a Trump, por ahora

Spanish macroeconomic data from early 2025 suggest the consolidation of an expansionary cycle characterized by robust growth, sustained job creation, and inflation controlled below the European Central Bank’s target. This triple advantage indicates that the economy maintains a strong capacity for resilience and demonstrates a virtuous circle between productive activity and household purchasing power, even amid a complex international environment. The relevance of this information lies in its ability to assess the initial impact of new trade tensions, such as the announced tariffs. Although current indicators do not show negative effects, the openness of the Spanish economy makes it vulnerable to changes in global policies. Monitoring these variables allows anticipation of how external shocks could alter current trends, providing a crucial snapshot of the true health of the labor market and price stability. This article is fundamental to open data because it illustrates how the timely and transparent availability of public statistics (such as GDP, the Labor Force Survey, and the Consumer Price Index) is essential for rigorous economic policy analysis. By making these data accessible, transparency is enhanced, citizens and experts can verify hypotheses about the impact of trade policies, and accountability is fostered, demonstrating that open information is a key tool for informed decision-making in times of uncertainty.
