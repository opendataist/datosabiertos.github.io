Rotten wood, insect damage discovered after Fort Gibraltar walkway collapse, documents reveal | CBC News

Rotten wood, insect damage discovered after Fort Gibraltar walkway collapse, documents reveal | CBC News

The collapse of a wooden walkway at Winnipeg’s Fort Gibraltar, which hospitalized numerous schoolchildren, was caused by severe structural degradation including rotten support beams and insect damage. Freedom of information documents reveal that these hazards were evident in post-accident inspections, highlighting a critical failure in infrastructure maintenance that directly endangered public safety during a routine educational visit. This incident exposes long-standing neglect of historical site upkeep, with former staff noting that such deterioration required constant, costly interventions to prevent exactly this type of failure. The gap between recorded minor repairs years prior and the catastrophic collapse suggests systemic issues in how public heritage sites are monitored and maintained, raising serious questions about accountability for both the city and the managing non-profit organization. For the open data community, this case illustrates the vital role of transparency in public safety. By obtaining and publishing hidden municipal records, journalists and citizens can hold authorities accountable for infrastructure risks that might otherwise remain obscured. It underscores how accessible, detailed public records are essential for identifying negligence and advocating for the rigorous maintenance standards required to protect vulnerable populations in public spaces.
