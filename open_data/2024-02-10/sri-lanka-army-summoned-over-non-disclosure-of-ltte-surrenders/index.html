Sri Lanka Army summoned over non-disclosure of LTTE surrenders -

This case highlights the critical intersection of transparency laws and historical accountability, demonstrating how the Right to Information Act can serve as a vital tool for accessing state-held records regarding human rights violations. The Sri Lankan Appeal Court’s decision to summon the military underscores the legal necessity for institutions to justify non-disclosure, challenging the traditional culture of secrecy surrounding sensitive wartime data. The core implication is that public authorities cannot easily evade information requests by redefining institutional responsibilities or claiming data unavailability without robust justification. By presenting evidence that the military maintained detailed registries of surrenders, the applicant established that the information was neither missing nor exempt. This creates a precedent where administrative silence is scrutinized against existing documentary evidence, forcing greater operational transparency from defense bodies. For the open data community, this is relevant as it validates the efficacy of structured legal frameworks in exposing information asymmetry. It shows that systematic data collection by governments, even for controversial purposes, creates an audit trail that citizens can leverage. Consequently, the case reinforces the importance of protecting RTI mechanisms to ensure that historical records remain accessible, facilitating justice and preserving an accurate historical archive for future research and reconciliation efforts.
