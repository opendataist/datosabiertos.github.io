Augusta County hides texts on Steven Morelli resignation in FOIA response

This article highlights a significant concern for open government by illustrating how public agencies can exploit legal loopholes to withhold information from citizens. Augusta County is using exemptions previously ruled improper by a judge to block access to text messages between board members discussing a colleague’s resignation. This strategy creates a double barrier to transparency, as the same legal justifications used to illegally hold a closed meeting are now cited to keep related private communications secret. The situation underscores the vulnerability of public records when local governments prioritize secrecy over statutory compliance. The core implication is that the lack of enforceable penalties for improper redactions allows officials to obscure critical decision-making processes. By citing expired or invalidated exemptions, the county effectively silences the public discourse surrounding the resignation, which was linked to internal harassment allegations. This practice erodes trust in local governance and suggests that the Freedom of Information Act is insufficient for protecting the public’s right to know when agencies are determined to conceal administrative controversies. The legal limbo delays accountability, leaving citizens in the dark about the true reasons behind high-profile departures. This case is vital to the open data and transparency movement because it demonstrates the necessity of robust, enforceable open data standards. It shows that mere statutory existence is not enough; mechanisms must exist to prevent agencies from gaming exemptions or delaying disclosures indefinitely. For open data advocates, this serves as a cautionary tale about the importance of strict auditing and the potential for political power to undermine informational rights. Ultimately, it reinforces the need for stronger legal frameworks that prioritize immediate access to government communications, ensuring that transparency is a default rather than a privilege granted by officials.
