Improvements badly needed to Michigan sunshine law

Michigan’s legislative transparency is undergoing a pivotal shift as bills move to remove Freedom of FOIA exemptions for the governor and legislature. This change directly addresses the state’s historically poor ranking in openness, signaling a necessary move toward greater government accountability. Although the legislation contains imperfect exemptions regarding security and executive privilege, it remains a significant positive step. By limiting these loopholes, the bills aim to close long-standing gaps in public access to information that have previously hindered effective oversight. This development is crucial for open data advocates because it targets the highest levels of state power. Expanding FOIA coverage ensures that citizen journalists and the public can scrutinize executive and legislative decisions, thereby strengthening democratic engagement and information equity.
