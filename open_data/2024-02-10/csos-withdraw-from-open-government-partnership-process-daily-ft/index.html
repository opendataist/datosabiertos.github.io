CSOs withdraw from Open Government Partnership process | Daily FT

Civil society organizations in Sri Lanka are withdrawing from the Open Government Partnership to protest government laws that restrict civic freedoms. This decisive action highlights the critical tension between authoritarian legislative measures and the core democratic principles of open governance. The withdrawal signifies a breakdown in trust, demonstrating that open government commitments cannot survive when states suppress dissent and limit public participation. It serves as a stark warning that procedural cooperation is insufficient without genuine respect for fundamental rights and civic space. This incident is highly relevant to open data because transparency and accountability are interdependent. Without a free and active civic sphere, the potential of open data to empower citizens and hold authorities accountable is severely undermined, rendering data initiatives ineffective in the absence of broader democratic protections.
