Sensación de inseguridad en Aguascalientes, mucho más baja que en Zacatecas y Fresnillo | Periodico Hidrocalido Digital

The recent national survey on urban public security reveals a significant deterioration in citizens’ perception of safety across major Mexican cities. The data highlight a marked increase in feelings of insecurity, particularly noting sharp rises in specific municipalities while contrasting them with areas that maintain higher levels of perceived safety. This shift indicates that the current security landscape is becoming more polarized, with certain regions facing critical challenges that far exceed national averages. Beyond general sentiment, the report identifies specific pain points driving this anxiety, such as fear in public transportation, at ATMs, and on streets, alongside frequent conflicts arising from alcohol consumption and property crime. Furthermore, the findings demonstrate a clear disparity in public trust among security forces; citizens express substantially higher confidence in the military and navy compared to local police institutions. This divergence suggests that the effectiveness of security strategies varies drastically depending on the branch of authority and the local context. This article is vital to open data initiatives because it underscores the importance of accessible, granular public statistics for evidence-based policymaking. By making detailed insights on crime perception and institutional trust available, governments can better allocate resources and design targeted interventions. The transparency provided by these metrics allows civil society and researchers to hold authorities accountable and drive systemic improvements in urban safety through data-driven solutions.
