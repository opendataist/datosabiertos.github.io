Your Discord Data Is Being Sold to Law Enforcement and AI Companies

The exposure of services like Spy Pet, which scrape and sell public Discord activity, highlights a critical vulnerability in open data ecosystems: the ease with which fragmented public information can be aggregated to create invasive user profiles. This practice demonstrates how openly available data, intended for community interaction, is repurposed for surveillance and commercial gain, often sold for minimal cost to law enforcement or AI developers without user consent. This scenario underscores the inherent risks of platforms that lack end-to-end encryption and default privacy protections. While direct messages remain secure, the aggregation of public server chats reveals how seemingly harmless posts can be compiled into comprehensive behavioral datasets. This challenges the assumption that "public" data is safe from exploitation, illustrating how technical loopholes allow third parties to bypass intended social boundaries and monetize individual digital footprints. For the open data community, this serves as a stark warning about data ethics and privacy by design. It emphasizes the need for stricter governance on how publicly accessible data is collected, processed, and sold, particularly when used for surveillance or training artificial intelligence. Users and developers must recognize that openness does not equate to safety, advocating for transparent consent mechanisms and robust tools that allow individuals to control how their contributions are aggregated and utilized by external entities.
