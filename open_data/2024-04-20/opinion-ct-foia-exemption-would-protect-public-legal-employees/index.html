Opinion: CT FOIA exemption would protect public legal employees

The article highlights the urgent need to extend Freedom of Information Act protections to Connecticut Assistant Attorneys General, ensuring their residential addresses are exempt from public disclosure. This legislative effort addresses a critical safety gap, as these civil lawyers face significant risks of harassment, stalking, and threats from individuals they represent or oppose. While other legal and public safety professionals already enjoy these safeguards, AAGs remain exposed, creating an inconsistent and dangerous disparity in workplace security. Several documented incidents illustrate the severity of this issue, including death threats and intimidation directed at AAGs and their families. These cases demonstrate that the absence of address protection exposes employees and their loved ones to tangible physical dangers. Opponents argue for transparency, but the article contends that professional contact information is already publicly available, rendering personal home details unnecessary for accountability while unnecessarily escalating safety risks. This narrative is relevant to open data discussions as it examines the balance between the public’s right to information and the fundamental right to personal safety. It underscores how data accessibility policies must evolve to account for potential harms, suggesting that indiscriminate disclosure can endanger individuals. The argument reinforces the principle that open data frameworks should prioritize protecting vulnerable workers over absolute transparency when public safety is at stake.
