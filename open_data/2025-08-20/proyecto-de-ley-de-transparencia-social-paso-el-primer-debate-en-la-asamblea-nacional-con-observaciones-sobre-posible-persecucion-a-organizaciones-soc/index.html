Proyecto de Ley de Transparencia Social pasó el primer debate en la Asamblea Nacional con observaciones sobre posible persecución a organizaciones sociales

Proyecto de Ley de Transparencia Social pasó el primer debate en la Asamblea Nacional con observaciones sobre posible persecución a organizaciones sociales

The proposed Law of Social Transparency in Ecuador aims to regulate civil society organizations by mandating transparency in resource management and profit distribution. The government’s primary conclusion is that this framework is essential to identify and disrupt illegal capital flows linked to criminal economies, arguing that distinguishing organizations by risk level allows for targeted audits without penalizing lawful entities. The legislation seeks to close loopholes used for money laundering while ensuring that civil society groups contributing to social development remain protected from arbitrary intervention. Opposition legislators contend that the bill violates constitutional principles by mixing unrelated economic and tax reforms under the guise of transparency, creating legal uncertainty and potential tools for political persecution. Critics emphasize that regulating civil society without prior precedents of intervention raises concerns about restricted freedom of association, suggesting that the measure could serve as a mechanism for social control rather than genuine anti-corruption efforts. There are specific objections regarding the inclusion of tax measures on productive sectors and the potential for abuse against indigenous leaders. This discussion is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the tension between regulatory oversight and data privacy in the civil sector. It underscores the necessity of transparent, standardized data on funding and profits to prevent illicit activities, while simultaneously raising critical questions about how data collection powers can be misused. The debate illustrates the challenge of designing open data frameworks that protect public integrity without infringing on organizational autonomy or enabling political surveillance.
