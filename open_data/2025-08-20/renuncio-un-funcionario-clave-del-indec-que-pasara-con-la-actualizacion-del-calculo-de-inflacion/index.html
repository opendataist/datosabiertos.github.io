Renunció un funcionario clave del INDEC: qué pasará con la actualización del cálculo de inflación

Renunció un funcionario clave del INDEC: qué pasará con la actualización del cálculo de inflación

The resignation of Guillermo Manzano, head of living conditions statistics at INDEC, highlights significant internal tensions regarding data management. Official statements attribute this departure to managerial disagreements, yet it coincides with delays in updating the Consumer Price Index. This situation suggests deeper institutional divergences, potentially impacting the reliability and timeliness of critical social indicators such as poverty and unemployment rates. The recent release of inflation data further underscores the sensitivity of these statistics, as price variations directly influence poverty measurement methodologies. In Argentina, social metrics are closely linked to income levels adjusted for inflation. Consequently, any delay or alteration in the CPI update has immediate implications for how economic hardship is quantified, affecting public perception and policy assessment during a period of high economic volatility. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how political and administrative pressures can compromise the independence and accessibility of official statistics. When the production of critical data is stalled or opaque, it hinders transparency and accountability. Reliable, timely public data is essential for informed civic engagement and accurate economic analysis, making the integrity of statistical agencies a fundamental concern for open data advocates seeking truth in public information.
