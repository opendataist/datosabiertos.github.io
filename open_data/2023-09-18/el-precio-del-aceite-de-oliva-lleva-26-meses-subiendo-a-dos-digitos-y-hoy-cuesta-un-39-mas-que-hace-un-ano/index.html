El precio del aceite de oliva lleva 26 meses subiendo a dos dígitos y hoy cuesta un 39% más que hace un año

The dramatic surge in olive oil prices underscores how climate-induced supply shocks severely disrupt essential commodity markets. Prolonged droughts have drastically reduced harvest yields, creating a supply-demand imbalance that drives costs beyond sustainable levels for consumers. This highlights the critical vulnerability of agricultural systems to environmental changes, a key concern for data transparency in food security monitoring. Household expenditure on this staple has reached historic highs, reflecting a significant reduction in purchasing power. As average spending climbs while per-capita consumption stagnates, the financial burden on families intensifies. This trend illustrates how inflationary pressures in basic goods can erode living standards, emphasizing the need for accessible economic data to track household welfare and market accessibility over time. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, real-time statistics in diagnosing economic trends. By making production volumes, price indices, and consumption patterns publicly available, stakeholders can better understand the impact of environmental factors on the economy. Open data facilitates informed policy responses and helps society monitor the interplay between climate events, market volatility, and consumer wellbeing.
