The Current State of the Theory that GPL Propagates to AI Models Trained on GPL Code

The Current State of the Theory that GPL Propagates to AI Models Trained on GPL Code

The debate over whether copyleft licenses like the GPL propagate to AI models trained on open-source code remains legally indeterminate. Although earlier theories suggesting the model itself becomes a derivative work have lost mainstream traction in favor of AI utility, this perspective has not been legally refuted. The persistence of active litigation, particularly regarding GitHub Copilot and OpenAI, indicates that courts have not yet dismissed the core argument that training on licensed code imposes licensing obligations on the resulting model. Current legal proceedings highlight the tension between technical innovation and open-source compliance. In high-profile cases, plaintiffs argue that failing to provide attribution or release model derivatives under compatible licenses constitutes a breach of contract and copyright violation. Courts have allowed these claims to proceed, acknowledging that ignoring open-source conditions during the training and distribution process may be illegal, even if they have not yet mandated the full source release of AI systems under strict copyleft terms. This issue is critical for open data and open source ecosystems because it defines the future of software freedom in the AI age. If courts rule that AI models containing proprietary or copyleft code are subject to those same licenses, it could fundamentally alter how data is used for training and how AI developers operate. Establishing clear legal precedents is essential to ensure that the benefits of AI do not come at the expense of the ethical and legal principles underpinning collaborative software development.
