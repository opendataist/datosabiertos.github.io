Aragón, entre las comunidades donde más han subido la compra de vivienda, los precios y las hipotecas

Aragón, entre las comunidades donde más han subido la compra de vivienda, los precios y las hipotecas

Spain’s housing market demonstrated a notable recovery in September, with transaction volumes increasing by five percent and reversing a two-month downward trend. Despite this positive development, the sector continues to grapple with a supply deficit that fails to keep pace with rising demand. This imbalance is the primary driver behind substantial increases in both property prices and mortgage lending, signaling intensifying affordability pressures across the country. Regional disparities underscore the uneven nature of this recovery. While autonomous communities such as Aragón and Murcia recorded double-digit growth in sales and borrowing, major markets like Madrid and the Canary Islands experienced declines. Moreover, price increases varied significantly by location, with some regions witnessing sharp escalations while others remained stable. This fragmentation indicates that local housing policies and demographic factors play a critical role in shaping market dynamics, rather than a uniform national trend. This data is vital for open data initiatives, as it highlights the need for transparent, granular real estate statistics for policymakers and researchers. By making such detailed information accessible, stakeholders can better analyze market inefficiencies, identify regional inequalities, and design effective interventions to address housing shortages. Open data promotes accountability and supports evidence-based decision-making, ensuring that the digital infrastructure underpinning housing markets accurately reflects on-the-ground economic realities and facilitates more equitable resource allocation.
