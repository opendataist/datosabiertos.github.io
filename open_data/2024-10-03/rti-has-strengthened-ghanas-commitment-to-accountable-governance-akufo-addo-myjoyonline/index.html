RTI has strengthened Ghana’s commitment to accountable governance - Akufo-Addo - MyJoyOnline

RTI has strengthened Ghana’s commitment to accountable governance - Akufo-Addo - MyJoyOnline

Ghana’s implementation of the Right to Information Act marks a significant step toward open governance, empowering citizens to scrutinize public institutions and hold officeholders accountable. By mandating information units within government bodies, the legislation fosters greater civic engagement and transparency, ensuring that citizens can actively participate in the democratic process. This legal framework reinforces the administration’s commitment to an inclusive information age where no individual is excluded from accessing critical public data. The relevance to open data lies in the structural shift toward treating information as a global public good. International praise, notably from UNESCO, highlights how robust access laws underpin freedom of expression and sustainable development. By integrating digital infrastructure investments with legal mandates, Ghana aims to create an ecosystem where citizens can effectively monitor resource utilization and development projects, thereby transforming raw data into actionable accountability. Ultimately, mainstreaming access to information in the public sector is crucial for building just and peaceful societies. The narrative emphasizes that when individuals can seamlessly seek, receive, and relay information, they are better equipped to engage in democratic processes. This approach not only enhances government accountability but also ensures that open data principles serve as foundational tools for transparency, encouraging a culture where information flow strengthens the bond between the state and its people.
