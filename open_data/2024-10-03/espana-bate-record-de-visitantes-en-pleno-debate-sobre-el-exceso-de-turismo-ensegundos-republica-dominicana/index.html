España bate récord de visitantes en pleno debate sobre el exceso de turismo Ensegundos República Dominicana

España bate récord de visitantes en pleno debate sobre el exceso de turismo Ensegundos República Dominicana

Spain has recorded unprecedented tourism figures, welcoming more visitors than ever before and generating record-breaking economic revenue. This surge underscores the sector’s critical role in the national economy, highlighting its capacity to drive substantial financial growth and social cohesion. For open data initiatives, this massive influx illustrates the sheer volume of mobility and consumption patterns that require robust, large-scale data collection systems to monitor effectively. However, this growth coexists with rising social friction as local populations increasingly protest against overtourism. Residents criticize the strain on infrastructure, environmental degradation, and the housing crisis driven by the shift from long-term rentals to short-term tourist accommodations. The tension reveals a complex dynamic where economic success creates negative externalities, emphasizing the need for transparent, publicly accessible data to understand the spatial and temporal distribution of these impacts on communities. The relevance to open data lies in the urgent requirement for granular, real-time information to balance tourism management with resident rights. By making mobility trends, housing market shifts, and economic contributions open, policymakers and citizens can engage in evidence-based debates. This transparency is essential to support sustainable urban planning and mitigate the social costs associated with mass tourism, ensuring that data serves not just economic metrics but also community well-being and equitable resource distribution.
