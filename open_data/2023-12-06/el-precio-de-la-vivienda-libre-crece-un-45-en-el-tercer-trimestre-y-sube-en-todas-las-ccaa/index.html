El precio de la vivienda libre crece un 4,5% en el tercer trimestre y sube en todas las CCAA

El precio de la vivienda libre crece un 4,5% en el tercer trimestre y sube en todas las CCAA

Spain’s housing market shows remarkable resilience, with free-market prices rising in all autonomous communities for thirty-eight consecutive quarters. The cost of new homes has surged to its highest annual rate in sixteen years, while the secondary market has continued its steady climb since 2014. This sustained growth contradicts earlier expert predictions of a downturn, indicating robust underlying demand capable of absorbing current market pressures. The main driver of this trend is a critical imbalance between supply and demand. Despite financing becoming more expensive and transaction volumes declining, the available housing stock remains insufficiently replenished. As a result, solvent buyers continue to push prices upward, suggesting that price increases will persist, albeit at a more moderated pace as the year draws to a close. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it highlights the need for transparent, timely, and granular housing statistics. Accurate data from institutions such as the INE enables stakeholders to monitor market health, identify regional disparities, and understand structural imbalances. Such transparency is essential for policymakers and researchers to develop effective strategies to address housing affordability and supply constraints in real time.
