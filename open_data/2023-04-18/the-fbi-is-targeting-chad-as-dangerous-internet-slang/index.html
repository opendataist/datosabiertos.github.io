The FBI is targeting 'Chad' as dangerous internet slang

The FBI’s secret monitoring of internet terminology raises significant concerns about free speech and the overreach of government surveillance. By flagging common slang like "Chad" and "based" as indicators of extremism, authorities risk criminalizing normal online discourse and chilling open expression. This surveillance strategy highlights the dangers of using vague, subjective metrics to identify radicalization. Instead of focusing on concrete threats, the list reflects a broader cultural anxiety that conflates subcultural language with actual harmful ideology, potentially targeting marginalized communities rather than genuine security risks. For open data advocates, this incident underscores the critical need for transparency in how government agencies collect and utilize digital information. Without clear guidelines and public oversight, secret watchlists can erode trust in institutions and infringe upon individual privacy rights, making public access to such data essential for democratic accountability.
