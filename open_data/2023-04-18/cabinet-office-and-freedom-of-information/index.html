Cabinet Office and Freedom of Information

Cabinet Office and Freedom of Information

The Cabinet Office acts as the central authority for Freedom of Information policy, ensuring consistent compliance across government departments while balancing transparency with the protection of sensitive national interests. Rather than making final disclosure decisions, it serves an advisory role, guiding departments through complex requests involving collective responsibility, security concerns, or personal data. This structure emphasizes that individual departments remain accountable for their specific information holdings, while the Cabinet Office provides the framework for uniform handling of overlapping requests. A significant structural shift occurred with the closure of the former “Clearing House” function in late 2022, which was redesignated to operate strictly as an advisory body following an independent review. This change reflects a strategic move toward establishing higher standards and promoting best practices in FOI handling rather than managing individual cases. By focusing on cross-cutting themes and technical guidance, such as requirements for spreadsheet formats, the Office aims to streamline processes and ensure alignment with current data protection regulations. This article is highly relevant to open data because it outlines the evolving governance mechanisms that dictate how public information is managed and released in the UK. It highlights the critical intersection between statutory transparency laws and data privacy safeguards, illustrating how centralized policy bodies influence the accessibility and format of public records. For practitioners and researchers, understanding this advisory model clarifies the procedural landscape for accessing government data and underscores the importance of standardized, secure, and consistent data release practices.
