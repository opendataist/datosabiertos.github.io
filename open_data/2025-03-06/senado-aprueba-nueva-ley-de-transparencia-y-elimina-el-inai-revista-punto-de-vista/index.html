Senado aprueba nueva ley de transparencia y elimina el INAI | Revista Punto de Vista

Senado aprueba nueva ley de transparencia y elimina el INAI | Revista Punto de Vista

Mexico’s Senate approved the “Law of Transparency for the People,” effectively dismantling the autonomous National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) and transferring its authority to the anti-corruption secretariat. This legislative shift centralizes oversight of public information access and data protection under the executive branch, fundamentally altering the institutional framework that previously ensured independent scrutiny of government transparency efforts. The reform introduces significant procedural changes, restricting appeals to judicial review and exempting key security agencies from transparency committees. By placing the regulator and the regulated within the same administration, critics argue this creates an inherent conflict of interest that undermines procedural legitimacy and reduces the ability of civil society to hold power accountable through independent administrative mechanisms. This development is highly relevant to open data because it directly threatens the structural independence essential for robust data governance. When oversight bodies lose autonomy, the integrity and accessibility of public data are compromised, potentially hindering citizens’ rights to information and weakening the democratic checks necessary for effective open data ecosystems.
