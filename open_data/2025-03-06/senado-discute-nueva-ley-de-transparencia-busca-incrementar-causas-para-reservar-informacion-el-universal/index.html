Senado discute nueva Ley de Transparencia; busca incrementar causas para reservar información | El Universal

Senado discute nueva Ley de Transparencia; busca incrementar causas para reservar información | El Universal

Mexican senators approved a major legal overhaul that centralizes transparency and data protection authorities under the Secretariat of Anti-Corruption and Good Government. By transferring the powers formerly held by the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI) to a new entity called “Transparency for the People,” the reform aims to streamline the management of public information. This structural change significantly affects the institutional independence previously guaranteed to INAI, aligning oversight directly with the executive branch’s anti-corruption agenda. The legislation also expands the grounds for withholding information, increasing the number of reserve categories and broadly defining confidentiality in matters of national security and public order. However, it explicitly prohibits classifying information as secret when it involves proven acts of corruption, aiming to enhance accountability. At the same time, the framework relaxes consent requirements for publishing data found in public records or mandated by law, potentially facilitating easier access to certain personal data under specific legal justifications. This development is critical for the open data community, as it redefines the balance between transparency and privacy in Mexico. The shift in regulatory authority raises concerns about potential political influence over access to public records, while the expanded reserve clauses may obscure information that was previously accessible. Consequently, advocates must closely monitor how these new regulations affect the actual availability of government data and the protection of citizens’ digital rights in practice.
