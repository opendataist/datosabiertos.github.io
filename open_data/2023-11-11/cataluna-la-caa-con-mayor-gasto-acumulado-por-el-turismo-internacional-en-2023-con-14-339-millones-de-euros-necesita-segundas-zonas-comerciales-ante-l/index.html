Cataluña: la CAA con mayor gasto acumulado por el Turismo internacional en 2023, con 14.339 millones de euros (necesita segundas zonas comerciales ante la gran demanda de locales)

Barcelona has seen a significant increase in commercial spending, fueled by an influx of new brands and tourism levels that have surpassed pre-pandemic figures. This rapid growth has led to saturation in prime areas, creating a supply-demand imbalance that forces retailers to seek expansion into secondary zones. The city is responding with regulatory plans to diversify commercial activity, although experts warn that rigid legislation may hinder economic vitality. A collaborative, adaptive approach between public authorities and the private sector is essential to balance tourism capacity with local business needs. This dynamic is highly relevant to open data because effective urban management and commercial planning rely on transparent, real-time accessibility to information regarding store locations, foot traffic, and spending patterns. When municipal data on commercial density and tourist flows is openly available, stakeholders can better identify emerging trends, such as the shift toward secondary retail axes. Open data fosters accountability, allowing organizations to verify if government interventions successfully alleviate saturation or if they inadvertently restrict market competition. Ultimately, the case highlights the necessity of integrating open datasets into policy-making to ensure sustainable urban development. By making data on commercial usage and tourism impact publicly accessible, researchers and civil society can monitor the effectiveness of diversity initiatives. This transparency supports evidence-based decisions that prevent oversaturation while promoting equitable economic growth across different districts, ensuring that data-driven insights directly inform strategies for maintaining a healthy and competitive commercial ecosystem.
