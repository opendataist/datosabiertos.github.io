Open Data
ES EN

The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence faces a critical bottleneck as the industry approaches the limit of high-quality training data available on the open web. While AI systems require vast amounts of accurate information to function effectively, the growth of usable online content is slowing significantly. This scarcity threatens to stall the development of large language models and other advanced algorithms, potentially altering the future trajectory of AI innovation and economic contribution. To mitigate these data shortages, the industry is exploring several strategic alternatives, including developing more efficient algorithms that require less data and leveraging synthetic data generated by AI itself. Additionally, there is a growing movement to utilize offline repositories and negotiate licensing deals with content creators, moving away from the current practice of free web scraping. These solutions aim not only to sustain model performance but also to reduce computational costs and address ethical concerns regarding copyright and bias in training sets. This situation is particularly relevant to open data advocates because it highlights the tension between unrestricted data access and the sustainability of AI ecosystems. As AI companies increasingly seek to monetize data sources and respect creator rights, the open data community must engage with these emerging norms. The shift from free extraction to licensed or synthetic data generation challenges traditional open access principles while emphasizing the need for high-quality, ethical, and legally compliant data infrastructures to support future technological growth.
