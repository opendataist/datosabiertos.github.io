El Solar Orbiter muestra imágenes de los polos solares por primera vez en la historia

The European Space Agency’s (ESA) Solar Orbiter mission has successfully captured the first historic images of the Sun’s south pole, offering a unique perspective that breaks with traditional observations made from the equatorial plane. This advance enables the study of previously unexplored polar regions from a new orbital inclination, revealing fundamental details about the Sun’s magnetic field, its activity cycle, and the generation of the solar wind. The initial discovery of a chaotic magnetic field at the south pole during solar maximum underscores the need to understand the complex dynamics of the star in order to predict its future behavior. These new views are crucial for understanding how the Sun influences space weather and affects modern energy systems. By mapping surface magnetism and analyzing the chemical composition of the solar atmosphere, the mission aims to decipher the mechanisms that drive the ejection of particles into space. Understanding these processes not only answers basic scientific questions about our closest star but is also vital for protecting terrestrial and space-based technological infrastructure from potential disruptions caused by disruptive solar activity. The relevance of this article to open data lies in ESA’s commitment to democratizing access to this cutting-edge science. By making the data collected by Solar Orbiter’s instruments publicly available, the global community is empowered to conduct independent analyses, validate findings, and develop new applications. This cross-cutting approach ensures that the generated knowledge does not remain isolated but instead serves as a shared foundation for researchers and developers worldwide, maximizing the social and scientific impact of investment in space exploration.
