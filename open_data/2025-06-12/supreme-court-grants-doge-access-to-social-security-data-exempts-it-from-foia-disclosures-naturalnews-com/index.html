Supreme Court grants DOGE access to Social Security data, exempts it from FOIA disclosures – NaturalNews.com

Supreme Court grants DOGE access to Social Security data, exempts it from FOIA disclosures – NaturalNews.com

The Supreme Court’s recent decision to allow the Department of Government Efficiency access to sensitive Social Security data without mandatory public disclosure raises critical concerns about transparency in government operations. By lifting an injunction that cited serious privacy risks, the Court has prioritized administrative efficiency over the public’s right to scrutinize how taxpayer money is managed. This shift significantly impacts open data advocates, who argue that unrestricted executive access to personal records undermines the fundamental principle that government information should be accessible and accountable to citizens. The ruling exempts the department from Freedom of Information Act requests, effectively shielding internal communications and targeting criteria from independent review. This lack of visibility prevents civil society organizations from verifying the legitimacy of fraud detection initiatives or assessing whether data requests are justified. For the open data community, this represents a dangerous precedent where executive power can bypass established mechanisms for public oversight, creating a black box of data usage that cannot be externally audited or challenged through standard legal channels. Ultimately, this case highlights the tension between modernizing government systems and protecting civil liberties. It demonstrates how legal frameworks can be leveraged to prioritize speed and internal efficiency over democratic transparency. The decision serves as a stark warning to open data proponents that without robust legal safeguards, access to public records can be restricted by executive orders, thereby eroding the trust and scrutiny necessary for a healthy, accountable democracy.
