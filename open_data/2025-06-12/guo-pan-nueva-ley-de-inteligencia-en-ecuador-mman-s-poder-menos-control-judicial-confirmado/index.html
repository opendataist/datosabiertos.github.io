馃搫 Nueva Ley de Inteligencia en Ecuador: m谩s poder, menos control judicial - Confirmado

The new Ecuadorian Intelligence Law reorganizes the National Intelligence System to strengthen state security, raising significant concerns about potential overreach into civil liberties. By eliminating the requirement for prior judicial authorization for surveillance, the legislation grants intelligence agencies broad powers to intercept communications and access personal data without prior oversight, shifting the balance heavily toward state control rather than individual protection. Furthermore, the law mandates that any public or private entity must provide information to the National Intelligence Service (SNI) directly and free of charge. This compels widespread data sharing across sectors, enabling access to real-time telecommunications and mobile location data. The existence of a special, tax-exempt funding source, with audit reports destroyed for security purposes, further exacerbates fears of secrecy and accountability deficits within the intelligence apparatus. This development is crucial for open data advocates, as it undermines transparency principles by restricting public access to information on how intelligence data is collected and used. It highlights the tension between national security needs and the right to know, emphasizing the urgent need for robust legal frameworks that ensure surveillance powers are balanced with independent oversight and respect for fundamental human rights.
