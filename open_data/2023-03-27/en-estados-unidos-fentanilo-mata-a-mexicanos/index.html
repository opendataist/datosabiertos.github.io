En Estados Unidos, fentanilo “mata a mexicanos”

The article highlights that fentanyl-related deaths in the United States significantly affect the Mexican community, despite the absence of precise official statistics. By extrapolating data on opioid-related fatalities, experts suggest that a substantial number of Mexican and Mexican-American individuals are succumbing to this drug, which largely originates in Mexico. This underscores the severe human cost of transnational drug flows on specific demographic groups. Understanding these hidden or underreported mortality figures is crucial for open data initiatives, as it reveals gaps in how government agencies track ethnic-specific health outcomes. The reliance on indirect mathematical estimations rather than direct, disaggregated records indicates systemic transparency issues. This case study demonstrates how open data principles can expose disparities in public health reporting and challenge the availability of precise, identity-based statistics in cross-border health crises. Ultimately, the narrative argues for greater visibility into how drug policies affect minority populations living abroad. It emphasizes that open data efforts must prioritize granular, inclusive datasets to accurately reflect the true impact of public health emergencies. Without such transparency, communities remain vulnerable to invisible crises, hindering effective policy responses and international cooperation in addressing the societal toll of the fentanyl epidemic.
