Internet Archive's digital book lending violates copyrights, US judge rules - ET Telecom

A US judge ruled that the Internet Archive infringed copyrights by digitally scanning and lending books from major publishers. The court rejected the nonprofit’s fair use defense, determining that creating digital copies merely replaced authorized physical copies without adding transformative value. This decision establishes that owning a physical book does not grant the right to scan and lend it electronically to the public, setting a strict precedent for digital dissemination of copyrighted works. This ruling significantly impacts the concept of open access to digital information. By prioritizing traditional publisher controls over the Internet Archive’s lending model, the decision limits the ability of digital libraries to provide widespread, free access to copyrighted content. The outcome suggests that open data initiatives relying on digital reproduction and distribution face substantial legal hurdles when dealing with protected intellectual property, potentially restricting the breadth of freely available digital resources. The case is highly relevant to open data discussions as it highlights the tension between information accessibility and copyright enforcement. It demonstrates how legal frameworks can restrict the aggregation and sharing of digital content, even when driven by public interest. For those advocating for open data, this ruling underscores the importance of distinguishing between public domain materials and copyrighted works, emphasizing the need for careful legal navigation to ensure sustainable and lawful open access ecosystems.
