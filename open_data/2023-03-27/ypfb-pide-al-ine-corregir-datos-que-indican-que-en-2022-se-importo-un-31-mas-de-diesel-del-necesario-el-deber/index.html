YPFB pide al INE “corregir” datos que indican que en 2022 se importó un 31% más de diésel del necesario | El Deber

The controversy arose because the preliminary report on Bolivia’s diesel imports in 2022 showed a volume exceeding domestic demand, prompting the state-owned oil company to request a correction from the National Institute of Statistics (INE). YPFB argued that the discrepancy occurred because the preliminary data did not reflect the final adjustments to import declarations, which were caused by logistical and administrative factors. This highlights a misalignment between initial customs records and the official statistical data published, raising questions about the accuracy and timeliness of public information. The relevance of this case for the open data movement lies in the need to ensure that published datasets are accurate, up-to-date, and clearly documented. The lack of synchronization between preliminary and consolidated reports demonstrates how data collection and publication methodologies can generate misinformation if error margins or validation processes are not clearly specified. For citizens and researchers to trust state statistics, it is crucial to establish clear protocols that distinguish between provisional and final data, ensuring transparency in original sources such as customs agencies and statistical bodies. Finally, the article highlights strategic actions implemented to reduce costs and dependence on imports, including the use of more economical routes and the reactivation of pipelines. Although the primary focus is on statistical correction, the context shows that data quality directly impacts perceptions of operational efficiency and public resource management. Transparency in presenting figures is not only a technical requirement but also a fundamental tool for accountability and informed decision-making in national energy policy.
