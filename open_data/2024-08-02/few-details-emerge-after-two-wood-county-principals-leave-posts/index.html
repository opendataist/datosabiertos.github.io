Few details emerge after two Wood County principals leave posts

A Freedom of Information Act request revealed that Emerson Elementary conducted a bomb threat drill while students were on holiday break. This incident highlights significant discrepancies between official crisis response documentation and actual school operations. The school’s principal was responsible for submitting these drill records, raising questions about the oversight and verification processes used by the district to ensure compliance with state safety mandates. The district settled with former principals, but the public records regarding their departures were heavily redacted. While officials claimed some employee-specific details were protected, the lack of transparency in these settlement agreements complicates public accountability. This opacity prevents the community from fully understanding the circumstances surrounding staff changes and potential policy violations. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical need for transparent, accurate public records in education. When safety drills and personnel decisions are obscured by redactions or inconsistent reporting, public trust erodes. Open data initiatives must ensure that information related to student safety and administrative accountability is accessible, verifiable, and free from unnecessary withholding to maintain civic integrity.
