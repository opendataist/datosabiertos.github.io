One fifth of UK under 40s to be dragged into paying higher or additional rate tax by 2028 - Quilter - IFA Magazine

One fifth of UK under 40s to be dragged into paying higher or additional rate tax by 2028 - Quilter - IFA Magazine

The article highlights a significant fiscal shift where frozen income tax thresholds are effectively increasing tax liabilities for millions of UK residents under 40. By keeping tax bands static while wages rise to match inflation, the government is implementing "fiscal drag," pulling a substantial portion of the younger workforce into higher and additional tax brackets. This policy change serves as a stealth revenue generator for the state, impacting public sector finances without formally raising tax rates. For open data enthusiasts, this scenario illustrates how publicly available tax records and demographic statistics can reveal unintended societal consequences of legislative decisions. Analyzing Freedom of Information data allows researchers to quantify the specific impact of policy choices on different age groups, demonstrating the power of data to expose the real-world effects of abstract fiscal strategies. It shows that transparency in government data is crucial for understanding how economic policies disproportionately affect specific demographics. The narrative emphasizes that as tax burdens increase, individuals must strategically utilize existing allowances, such as pension contributions and Individual Savings Accounts, to mitigate their liabilities. This underscores the importance of accessible, accurate information regarding tax laws and financial planning tools. When citizens understand their rights and available deductions through clear data sources, they can make informed decisions that protect their financial well-being amidst changing government policies.
