C-LM contabilizó 161.773 pernoctaciones de 86.908 viajeros en alojamientos turísticos extrahoteleros en el mes de junio

The article reveals that Spain’s non-hotel tourism sector experienced moderate growth in overnight stays during June, driven primarily by international visitors and rising prices across all accommodation types. This trend highlights a broader recovery in tourist demand, with non-resident travelers increasingly contributing to the expansion, outpacing domestic growth. The simultaneous increase in occupancy rates and pricing suggests a robust market capable of absorbing higher costs while maintaining visitor volume, signaling economic vitality in the hospitality industry. Regional preferences vary significantly by accommodation type, with the Canary Islands dominating the apartment market and Catalonia leading in camping usage. Rural tourism saw substantial growth in foreign arrivals, particularly favoring the Balearic Islands. These distinct patterns indicate that travelers are diversifying their choices beyond traditional hotels, seeking specific experiences in different geographic locations. The data underscores how specific regions have carved out niche strengths, attracting distinct demographic groups based on their lodging preferences and travel styles. This report is relevant to open data as it demonstrates the value of standardized, granular statistical outputs from national institutions like the INE. By making detailed metrics on occupancy, overnight stays, and prices publicly available, the government enables researchers, businesses, and policymakers to analyze tourism trends with precision. Access to such high-quality, structured datasets fosters transparency and supports evidence-based decision-making for regional planning, economic forecasting, and competitive analysis within the tourism sector.
