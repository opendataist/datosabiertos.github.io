John Grisham, George R.R. Martin y otros escritores demandan a OpenAI por usar sus obras para entrenar el modelo de lenguaje GPT sin permiso - Tecnología - ABC Color

The Authors Guild has filed a class-action lawsuit against OpenAI, accusing the company of using copyrighted works without licenses to train its artificial intelligence models. This practice poses an existential threat to writers, as it relies on capturing texts from pirated sources and generates content that imitates or exploits the styles and narrative universes of the original authors. The impact extends beyond basic training, as users can request that the chatbot write new works imitating specific creators, facilitating the unauthorized distribution of derivative content. This constitutes a clear infringement of intellectual property rights, allowing third parties to commercialize messages or narratives based on protected characters and stories without the creators' consent or appropriate compensation. This conflict is crucial for the open data and shared knowledge sectors, as it marks a turning point in the tension between accelerated technological advancement and the moral and economic rights of creators. Establishing legal precedents in this area will determine whether data generated by users and authors can be freely used to develop AI or whether strict protective barriers must be respected, affecting transparency and ethics in the development of emerging technologies.
