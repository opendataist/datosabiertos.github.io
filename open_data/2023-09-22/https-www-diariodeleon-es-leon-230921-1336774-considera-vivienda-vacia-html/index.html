Tinsa reports significant vacant housing in Spain, defined by INE standards as permanently unoccupied properties. This issue is critical for open data policy, as transparent metrics reveal how empty homes contrast with growing urban demand and unaffordable rents. Supply fails to meet need due to land shortages and bureaucratic delays, causing social strain. The study highlights that while new construction increases, it remains insufficient. These findings underscore the need for accessible data to address housing accessibility crises effectively. Understanding these gaps helps policymakers identify inefficiencies in the market. By tracking vacancy rates alongside demand trends, authorities can better regulate the sector. Open data thus serves as a vital tool for diagnosing and resolving systemic housing challenges.
