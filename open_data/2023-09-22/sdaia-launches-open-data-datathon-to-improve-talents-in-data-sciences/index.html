SDAIA Launches 'Open Data Datathon' to Improve Talents in Data Sciences

Prince Harry and Hillary Clinton highlighted the urgent threat of artificial intelligence to youth mental health, arguing that AI companionship poses risks far exceeding those of social media. Unlike previous digital challenges, these systems are designed to foster deep emotional attachment, exploiting vulnerabilities rather than merely capturing attention. This perspective shifts the discourse from simple screen-time regulation to addressing the profound psychological manipulation inherent in next-generation technology. The discussion underscores how political shifts and funding cuts are exacerbating vulnerabilities among women and children, creating a vacuum that non-profits must fill. As government support wanes, organizations are stepping in to provide essential services, from menopause research to child mental health initiatives. This reliance on private sector and charitable intervention illustrates a critical gap in public infrastructure, emphasizing the need for robust, non-political scientific and social frameworks to protect at-risk populations. This article is relevant to open data because addressing the harms of AI and supporting vulnerable demographics requires transparent, accessible information. Effective solutions depend on sharing research findings, mental health outcomes, and algorithmic impacts publicly to build trust and drive accountability. Without open access to this data, it is impossible to evaluate the efficacy of interventions or hold tech companies and policymakers accountable for their decisions affecting public welfare.
