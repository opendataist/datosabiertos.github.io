Estos son los argumentos legales del Banco Central para pedir la identidad de los deudores y los de Sugef para negar esos datos

The conflict between Costa Rica’s Superintendence of Banks, Insurance, and AFPs and the Central Bank of Costa Rica illustrates the critical tension between statistical transparency and privacy protection. The core dispute centers on whether the monetary authority can demand credit data containing identification numbers without anonymization, a stance that has led to mutual legal actions and precautionary measures that have paralyzed the exchange of information. From a data protection perspective, current regulations require explicit consent to process information classified as sensitive, prioritizing constitutional rights to privacy and informational self-determination. Proponents of this position argue that banking confidentiality and personal data protection statutes prohibit the sharing of unique identifiers, ensuring that citizens’ financial resources are not exposed to unnecessary risks. Conversely, the Central Bank maintains that aggregating databases with identifiers is essential for analyzing financial risks and designing effective monetary policies, justifying its requests based on legal statistical obligations and binding rulings. This debate is fundamental to the open data movement, as it delineates the ethical and legal boundary between sharing public information for the general interest and preserving individual privacy against the state’s large-scale use of personal records.
