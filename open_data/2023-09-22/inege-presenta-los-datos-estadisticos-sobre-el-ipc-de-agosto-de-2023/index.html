INEGE presenta los datos estadísticos sobre el IPC de agosto de 2023

This report details the factors driving inflation in Equatorial Guinea, highlighting that rising costs are primarily concentrated in essential sectors such as food, transport, and healthcare. Rather than presenting a static economic snapshot, the analysis underscores the structural pressures on household budgets, particularly through significant price hikes in imported goods, fuel-linked services, and medical care. This granular breakdown reveals how external supply chain dynamics and local market conditions intersect to impact daily living expenses across different demographics. The narrative extends to a geographical comparison, illustrating that inflationary pressures are not uniform across the country. Cities like Malabo and Mongomo experience higher rates driven by specific local drivers, such as transport or health services, while Bata remains relatively more stable. This geographic disparity suggests that localized economic conditions and the availability of goods play a crucial role in price stability, offering a nuanced view of regional economic health that goes beyond national averages. This data is highly relevant to the open data community as it exemplifies the importance of accessible, detailed, and geographically disaggregated statistical information. By making specific consumer price indices and sectoral breakdowns available through the National Institute of Statistics, government bodies enable researchers, journalists, and developers to create tools that monitor economic well-being more effectively. Transparent, open data fosters accountability and allows for deeper analysis of how macroeconomic trends affect citizens in their daily lives.
