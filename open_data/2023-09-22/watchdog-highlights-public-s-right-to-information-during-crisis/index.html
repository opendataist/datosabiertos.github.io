Watchdog highlights public's right to information during crisis

The article highlights a local ombudsman’s investigation into government response to severe coastal flooding, emphasizing the critical importance of transparency during disaster recovery. By visiting affected communities and listening to residents’ concerns, officials aim to better understand failures in infrastructure and emergency services while addressing public frustration over delayed or withheld information. This engagement directly impacts open data by reinforcing the legal obligation of local authorities to promptly disclose official records regarding disaster preparedness and response. The ombudsman’s strategy relies on hearing firsthand experiences to guide future investigations, ensuring that decision-making processes are visible and accountable to citizens who need clarity amidst uncertainty. Ultimately, the piece underscores that public trust depends on accessible information. It serves as a reminder that open data is not just a technical requirement but a vital tool for accountability, enabling citizens to scrutinize government actions and understand the rationale behind policies that affect their safety and recovery efforts after extreme weather events.
