Un drama aún por solucionar: hasta 37.000 personas viven en la calle en España a día de hoy

The article highlights the urgency of addressing homelessness in Spain, where thousands of people face long-term social exclusion. The central conclusion is that ensuring access to stable housing is the fundamental strategy for eradicating homelessness, serving as an indispensable foundation for labor recovery, mental health, and social reintegration of affected individuals. It is emphasized that housing is not merely a shelter, but a basic right that enables the construction of a dignified future. The testimonies collected reveal that overcoming street homelessness requires continuous professional support and the restoration of personal autonomy, demonstrating that housing is the primary catalyst for breaking the cycle of poverty and structural inequality. This information is relevant to open data because it underscores the need for evidence-based public policies to achieve the goal of eliminating homelessness by 2030. Transparency in housing and employment data allows for measuring the real impact of social interventions, facilitating efficient resource allocation and fostering accountability in the management of fundamental human rights.
