The article highlights that, despite policy interventions aimed at reducing the cost of staple foods, rice prices have continued to rise, contradicting government claims that consumers are benefiting. This persistent inflation underscores a failure in economic management strategies, as liberalization measures have failed to translate into affordable goods for the public, thereby eroding trust in official inflation metrics and policy effectiveness. Concurrently, the shift toward a free-market model for rice has devastated domestic agriculture, causing a sharp decline in local cultivation and the number of producers. This structural damage poses a long-term threat to national food security and rural employment, illustrating the broader implications of deregulation implemented without adequate transitional support or economic safeguards for local industries. This case is highly relevant to open data, as it demonstrates how transparent, publicly available statistics from the National Institute of Statistics and Census can serve as a critical tool for independent verification. By accessing and analyzing certified price and production data, civil society can objectively assess the real-world impact of government policies, challenge official narratives, and ensure accountability in public administration.
