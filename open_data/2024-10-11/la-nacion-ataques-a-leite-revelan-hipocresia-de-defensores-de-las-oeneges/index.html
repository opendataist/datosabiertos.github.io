La Nación / Ataques a Leite revelan hipocresía de defensores de las oenegés

The article exposes a coordinated smear campaign against Senator Gustavo Leite, initiated by media outlets and non-governmental organizations (NGOs) at the time he was pushing forward a transparency law for NGO funds. The narrative seeks to discredit the legislative initiative by attacking the lawmaker’s track record, leveraging alleged irregularities and concealing the hypocrisy of those who define the legitimacy of these organizations based on political alignment rather than universal ethical principles. It highlights a fundamental contradiction: the very entities demanding rigorous transparency from the Bicameral Investigation Commission refuse to disclose their own financial origins, keeping in the shadows the source of millions in foreign currency received from foreign governments and international foundations. This double standard undermines the purported defense of human rights or justice, revealing that the real interest is to protect power structures that operate without public accountability. This situation is crucial for the open data movement, as it illustrates how institutional opacity is used as a tool for political control. The lack of access to verifiable financial information by citizens not only prevents social auditing but also undermines trust in democratic institutions. Therefore, mandatory transparency is not merely an administrative measure, but an indispensable requirement to ensure the integrity of the social sector and prevent the use of foreign funds as a mechanism for improperly opaque influence.
