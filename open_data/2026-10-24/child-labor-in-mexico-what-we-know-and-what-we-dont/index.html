Child Labor in Mexico: What We Know and What We Don’t

Child Labor in Mexico: What We Know and What We Don’t

The National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) confirmed a reduction in child labor in Mexico by comparing 2025 data with those from 2022, with declines in both the absolute number and the overall rate. However, the main takeaway is not that the problem has been resolved, but rather that millions of minors remain in working conditions. Transparency in these data is crucial for understanding the true scale of the phenomenon, clearly distinguishing between prohibited forms of work and other categories, thereby avoiding misinterpretations about the impact of specific public policies without a deeper causal analysis. The relevance of this publication lies in the National Survey on Child and Adolescent Work (ENTI)’s ability to break down information by federal entity, demographic characteristics, and types of activity. This enables identification of where the problem is most concentrated and adaptation of local strategies, as illustrated by the differences observed between Yucatán and Quintana Roo. Open data is vital here because it transforms abstract figures into actionable inputs, helping authorities and civil society prioritize resources in areas where children’s conditions are most vulnerable or where school attendance is most compromised. Finally, this exercise underscores the importance of maintaining a critical approach toward statistical indicators, reminding us that a lower national rate does not guarantee individual well-being nor explain the structural causes of worsening conditions in some regions. The availability of open, disaggregated data promotes accountability and evidence-based policy design. The challenge remains to use this information to safeguard children’s rights, ensuring that statistical reductions translate into real protection, security, and educational development for all affected children and adolescents.
