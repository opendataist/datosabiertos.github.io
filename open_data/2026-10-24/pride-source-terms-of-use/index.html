Pride Source | Terms of Use

This article is relevant to open data as it explicitly establishes legal boundaries regarding the extraction and reuse of digital information. By strictly prohibiting automated scraping, crawling, and data mining, the provider defines what constitutes unauthorized access to their proprietary database. This highlights a growing tension in the open data movement between the ideal of accessible information and the commercial interests of entities that wish to control their datasets. A critical implication for developers and researchers is the explicit ban on using site content to train or improve artificial intelligence and machine learning models. This restriction directly challenges the practices of many current AI initiatives that rely on large-scale web scraping for dataset creation. Consequently, organizations seeking to build open-source AI tools must navigate these legal barriers, often resulting in a more closed ecosystem where valuable data remains siloed rather than being freely shared for public benefit. Furthermore, the terms outline strict licensing conditions for user-generated content and directory listings, emphasizing that mere public availability does not equate to open usage rights. The requirement for written permission for substantial reproduction or redistribution underscores the importance of clear licensing frameworks in open data initiatives. Without such clarity, the potential for misuse increases, making it difficult to determine which data can genuinely be considered open and freely reusable by the community.
