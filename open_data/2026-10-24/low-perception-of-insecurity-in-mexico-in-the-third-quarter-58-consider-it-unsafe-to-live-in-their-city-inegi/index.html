Low perception of insecurity in Mexico in the third quarter; 58% consider it unsafe to live in their city: INEGI

Low perception of insecurity in Mexico in the third quarter; 58% consider it unsafe to live in their city: INEGI

The perception of urban insecurity in Mexico shows a statistically significant decline during the third quarter, although the majority of the population continues to feel vulnerable, with a particular emphasis on women’s experiences. This change indicates a relative improvement in the sense of safety, but it highlights the gender gap and the need to address the root causes of citizens’ fear in urban spaces. At the same time, distrust toward local authorities persists, contrasting with the high level of trust placed in federal security institutions such as the Navy and the Army. Infrastructure problems, such as potholes and failures in public services, have emerged as central concerns that impact quality of life, while citizens are modifying their daily routines and consumption habits as a defensive mechanism against crime and public disorder. This report is crucial for the open data field because it demonstrates the value of open indicators for measuring the effectiveness of public policies and citizen trust. By making these data accessible, disaggregated by gender, type of crime, and location, it facilitates accountability, enables civil society to analyze real trends, and promotes evidence-based decision-making to strengthen urban security and governance.

Source: eluniversal.com.mx
