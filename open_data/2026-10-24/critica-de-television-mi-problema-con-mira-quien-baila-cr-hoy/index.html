Crítica de televisión: Mi problema con ‘Mira quién baila’ | CR Hoy

The article highlights a significant shift in the composition of the Costa Rican reality show "Mira quién baila," where participants are increasingly recruited for their established fame on digital platforms rather than traditional media. This transition reflects a broader transformation in how public recognition is generated and consumed, moving away from shared experiences like radio or sports toward fragmented, influencer-driven ecosystems that may not resonate with older audiences. This evolution has important implications for the concept of open data and media transparency. While digital fame creates new, distinct celebrity classes, it also underscores the necessity for media outlets to provide context that bridges the gap between online popularity and mainstream visibility. The narrative demonstrates that data regarding an individual’s background and achievements—often siloed within specific social media communities—needs better integration into public discourse to ensure broader accessibility and understanding across different demographic groups. Ultimately, the piece illustrates that television retains a unique power to introduce audiences to unfamiliar figures, prompting a necessary adjustment in viewers' perceptions. The author acknowledges that their initial disconnect stemmed from personal media consumption habits rather than a lack of merit on the part of the dancers. This dynamic highlights the evolving relationship between open digital presence and traditional broadcast, showing how media literacy requires actively seeking out and validating diverse forms of public contribution beyond familiar cultural touchstones.
