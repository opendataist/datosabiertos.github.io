Navarra, for another year, the community with the highest level of quality of life

Quality of life in Spain experienced a slight uptick, with Navarre maintaining its position as the national leader. This indicator, compiled by the National Statistics Institute (INE), reflects an overall improvement in material conditions, work, and health, although a slight deterioration is observed in leisure and personal safety. The gender gap persists, with men recording better overall results, although women stand out in education and subjective well-being. This territorial and demographic distribution reveals structural disparities that warrant attention. The relevance of this article to the field of open data lies in its demonstration of how multidimensional indicators can be disaggregated to expose hidden inequalities. By publishing detailed differences between autonomous communities and demographic groups, it facilitates transparency and critical analysis of public policies. The data not only show national trends but also allow for pinpointing where advantages or disadvantages are concentrated, offering a powerful tool for accountability. Including these metrics in public repositories empowers researchers and citizens to evaluate the effectiveness of social interventions. When well-being information becomes accessible and granular, it fosters evidence-based discussion. This is essential for advancing toward a fairer and more efficient governance model, ensuring that improvements in quality of life are not anecdotal but translate into tangible changes for citizens.
