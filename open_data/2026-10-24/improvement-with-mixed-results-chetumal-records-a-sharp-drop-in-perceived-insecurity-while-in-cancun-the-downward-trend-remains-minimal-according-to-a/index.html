IMPROVEMENT WITH MIXED RESULTS: Chetumal records a sharp drop in perceived insecurity, while in Cancun the downward trend remains minimal, according to an INEGI survey - Noticaribe

IMPROVEMENT WITH MIXED RESULTS: Chetumal records a sharp drop in perceived insecurity, while in Cancun the downward trend remains minimal, according to an INEGI survey - Noticaribe

The article reveals a significant divergence in the perception of insecurity between Chetumal and Cancún during the third quarter of 2026. While the state capital experienced a substantial and statistically significant improvement in citizens’ sense of safety, the main tourist destination maintained high levels of fear with only marginal variations, highlighting that most of its residents still consider it unsafe. This distinction underscores the complexity of local security dynamics within the same federal entity. At the national level, there is a general downward trend in the sense of insecurity, with the country falling below the average of previous years. However, the survey clarifies that subjective perception does not necessarily correspond to actual crime incidence, as factors such as trust in authorities and the surrounding environment directly influence the results. This implies that improvements in perception do not guarantee a proportional reduction in criminality, requiring a more nuanced analysis of public policies. This report is crucial for the field of open data, as it illustrates how official statistics can be misinterpreted if the distinction between perception and objective reality is not clearly made. The availability of disaggregated data by municipality enables civil society and academics to challenge simplified narratives about security, fostering more precise, evidence-based accountability grounded in contextualized analysis.
