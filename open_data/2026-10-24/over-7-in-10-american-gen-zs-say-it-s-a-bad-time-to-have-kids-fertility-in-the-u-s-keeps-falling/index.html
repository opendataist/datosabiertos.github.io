Over 7 in 10 American Gen Zs Say It's A Bad Time To Have Kids. Fertility in the U.S. Keeps Falling.

Over 7 in 10 American Gen Zs Say It's A Bad Time To Have Kids. Fertility in the U.S. Keeps Falling.

Recent data reveals a deepening economic pessimism among younger generations regarding parenthood, with a significant majority of Gen Z and Millennials viewing the current environment as unfavorable for raising children. This sentiment coincides with a historic decline in national birth rates and a shifting demographic landscape where childbearing is increasingly delayed or forgone entirely. The convergence of high living costs and economic uncertainty serves as a primary driver, reflecting broader anxieties about financial stability and the affordability of family life in the United States. The long-term implications of this trend are profound, potentially leading to a population decline as deaths eventually surpass births. As the proportion of older adults grows relative to children and working-age individuals, societal structures such as retirement systems and healthcare will face intensified pressure. International migration is projected to become the main source of population growth, highlighting a structural transition in how the nation sustains its demographic future amid changing family formation patterns. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how interconnected, large-scale datasets from government agencies like the CDC and Census Bureau can validate and contextualize public opinion surveys. By combining official statistical records with survey metrics, researchers can clearly illustrate the causal links between economic indicators and social behaviors. It underscores the importance of integrating diverse data sources to understand complex societal shifts, ensuring that policy decisions are grounded in comprehensive, empirical evidence rather than isolated trends.
