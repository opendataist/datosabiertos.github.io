This article highlights a contentious debate regarding government transparency and the practical challenges of fulfilling public-records requests. Mayoral challenger Tom Goldenberg argues that the Elicker administration is failing to provide timely access to information, citing a nearly three-month delay in responding to a specific inquiry about methadone clinics. He compares New Haven’s high volume of unresolved complaints unfavorably to Bridgeport, asserting that the current level of opacity undermines public trust and hinders accountability. In defense, Mayor Justin Elicker emphasizes the massive scale of the city’s workload, noting that it processes approximately ten thousand Freedom of Information Act requests annually. He contends that the delay is not due to negligence but rather a necessary legal review process to protect confidential data and privileged information. The mayor points to recent infrastructure improvements, such as new digital portals and dedicated staff, as evidence of systemic efforts to balance thoroughness with efficiency. This conflict is relevant to open data because it exposes the friction between the public’s demand for immediate transparency and the administrative realities of data management, privacy, and legal compliance. It raises critical questions about whether current statutory timeframes are realistic for modern municipalities and whether improved technological tools can bridge the gap between rapid public access and responsible information handling. The dispute illustrates the ongoing struggle to define what constitutes reasonable responsiveness in an era of high information demand.
