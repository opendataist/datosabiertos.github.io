Va Senado por periodo extra para comisionados del INAI

Mexico’s Senate is convening an extraordinary session to appoint new commissioners to the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), in compliance with a judicial mandate. This step is crucial for restoring the institution’s operational capacity and ensuring robust transparency governance, as vacancies have hindered its ability to effectively enforce access-to-information laws. The process involves selecting nominees for two vacant seats while deferring consideration of another until legal disputes are resolved. This strategic delay aims to preserve the legal standing of a previously appointed commissioner, highlighting the complex interplay between administrative appointments and constitutional rights. This development is highly relevant to open-data advocates, as a fully staffed INAI is essential for safeguarding citizens’ rights to public information. Strengthening this regulatory body ensures greater accountability and promotes a culture of transparency in the public sector, which are foundational pillars for an open-data ecosystem.
