Secrecy gives the government control

Secrecy gives the government control

The article argues that excessive government secrecy, exacerbated by resource constraints and political efforts to restrict transparency, undermines public participation in self-government. While the Freedom of Information Act remains the primary mechanism for citizens to access federal records, its effectiveness is severely compromised by growing backlogs, inadequate funding, and active administrative resistance. This erosion of transparency threatens the public’s ability to hold officials accountable in critical areas ranging from domestic policy to foreign affairs. Despite these systemic challenges, FOIA has proven to be a powerful tool for uncovering truth and ensuring public safety. Historical and recent examples demonstrate that successful requests can expose war crimes, facilitate justice in high-profile legal cases, and reveal misleading food labeling practices. These outcomes highlight the Act's essential role in enabling investigative journalism and empowering citizens to make informed decisions, proving that when functioning correctly, FOIA provides indispensable checks on governmental power. This content is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of proactive transparency and accessible public records. The article illustrates that without robust legal frameworks to combat secrecy and ensure data availability, the principles of open government fail. It calls for legislative reforms that not only fix procedural bottlenecks but also mandate broader data disclosure, emphasizing that sustained public pressure and congressional action are vital for maintaining the integrity of open information systems.
