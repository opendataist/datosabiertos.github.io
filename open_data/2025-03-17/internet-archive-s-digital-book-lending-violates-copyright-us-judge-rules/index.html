Internet Archive's digital book lending violates copyright, US judge rules

Internet Archive's digital book lending violates copyright, US judge rules

A U.S. court ruled that the Internet Archive infringed copyright by scanning and digitally lending books from major publishers, rejecting its claim of fair use. The judge determined that creating digital copies to replace licensed library lending practices lacked the necessary transformation required for fair use protection. This decision clarifies that while physical books can be lent, bulk scanning for digital distribution requires authorization. The ruling establishes a significant legal precedent regarding the boundaries of copyright law in the digital era, prioritizing publishers’ rights over unrestricted digital access. For the open data community, this case highlights the critical tension between preserving informational access and respecting intellectual property rights. It underscores the importance of understanding legal constraints when developing open repositories, ensuring that digitization efforts comply with copyright frameworks to remain sustainable and legitimate.
