Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway’s comprehensive report highlights a striking paradox in immigrant integration: while individuals with immigrant backgrounds face significantly poorer living conditions across employment, housing, health, and income compared to the general population, they report equal or even higher satisfaction with life in Norway. This data reveals a complex reality where objective socioeconomic disadvantages do not necessarily translate into subjective discontent, challenging assumptions about the direct correlation between material conditions and well-being. A closer examination of social and mental health metrics shows that immigrants experience higher rates of mental health problems and loneliness than the native population. Despite these personal struggles, there is a notably strong affiliation with the country and elevated trust in key institutions, including the political system, judiciary, and police. This indicates that immigrants may rely more heavily on state structures for stability and support, maintaining a distinct form of civic engagement that coexists with social isolation and economic precarity. This research is vital for open data advocates because it demonstrates the power of transparent, publicly available statistics to uncover nuanced societal truths. By making detailed survey data accessible, researchers and policymakers can move beyond simple averages to understand the lived experiences of marginalized groups. Such transparency enables the development of targeted interventions that address specific gaps in health and social services while recognizing the resilience and institutional trust that characterize immigrant communities, fostering more inclusive and effective public policy.
