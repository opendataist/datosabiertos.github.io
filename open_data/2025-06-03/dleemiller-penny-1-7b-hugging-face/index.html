dleemiller/Penny-1.7B · Hugging Face

Penny‑1.7B demonstrates how Group Relative Policy Optimization can effectively transfer historical writing styles into modern language models. By fine-tuning a base model to emulate the specific prose of the Irish Penny Journal, it achieves a distinct Victorian-era voice while maintaining core reasoning capabilities. This approach proves that reinforcement learning can successfully bridge the gap between contemporary AI functionality and niche aesthetic requirements. The process relies on a specialized reward classifier that distinguishes between original historical texts and modern translations. This mechanism guides the model toward authentic diction without sacrificing its underlying utility. Such techniques are significant for researchers exploring how reinforcement learning can be applied to creative and stylistic tasks beyond traditional performance metrics. This work is particularly relevant to the open data community as it showcases how publicly available historical archives can be leveraged to create specialized open-weight models. By using open corpora and transparent training methods, it encourages the preservation and creative reuse of cultural heritage. This fosters a richer ecosystem where open data directly fuels innovative, culturally contextual AI applications rather than just generic information retrieval.
