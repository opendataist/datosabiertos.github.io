Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

University performance data, obtained through a Freedom of Information request, reveals significant disparities in student attainment across various academic disciplines. The findings highlight that science and humanities departments generally outperform others, while foundational and specific language modules tend to lag behind. This transparency demonstrates how public pressure can force institutions to release granular academic metrics, empowering stakeholders to scrutinize educational outcomes and institutional accountability. The analysis identifies physics and English as top-performing subjects, whereas foundation year modules show notably lower achievement rates. Such variations suggest that structural factors, including student preparation levels and assessment methods, heavily influence results. By exposing these gaps, the data provides valuable insights for educators and administrators aiming to improve support systems and align expectations across different stages of the curriculum. Furthermore, final-year projects indicate that modern foreign languages and computer science students achieve significantly higher distinctions compared to peers in sports science. This disparity underscores the varying rigor or support structures associated with different disciplines. For open data advocates, this case exemplifies the critical role of accessible, raw data in fostering informed debate about quality assurance, equity, and the effective use of public resources in higher education.
