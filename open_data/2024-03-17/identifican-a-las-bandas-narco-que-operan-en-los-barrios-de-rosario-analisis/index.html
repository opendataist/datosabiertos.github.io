Identifican a las bandas narco que operan en los barrios de Rosario | Análisis

The Finint report reveals that armed violence in Rosario is intrinsically linked to the organizational structure of drug trafficking, demonstrating that the imprisonment of leaders does not dissolve criminal gangs but rather transforms their operations. When top bosses are arrested, a “franchise” model consolidates, enabling territorial control from prisons through the collection of fees, thereby ensuring the uninterrupted continuity and expansion of the illegal drug market. This dynamic generates fierce competition for territory, where franchisees must eliminate rivals and ensure the absence of police in their areas of influence. The result is a sustained increase in homicides in specific areas, severely affecting innocent civilians caught in these power struggles. The fragmentation of control among multiple groups, each with interests in different neighborhoods, creates a violent mosaic that is concentrated in small percentages of the urban territory but accounts for the majority of the social tragedy. This case is crucial for the field of open data because it illustrates how the structured publication of open data—court records, official registries, and conflict mapping—can expose complex patterns of organized crime that remain invisible to superficial analysis. By visualizing the connections between arrests, hierarchical structures, and conflict zones, open data facilitates a systemic understanding of public security, offering society and policymakers a fundamental tool for designing evidence-based public policies rather than relying solely on isolated perceptions.
