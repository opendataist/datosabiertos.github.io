El 74,9% de los extranjeros residentes en CLM tienen entre 26 y 64 años

The article analyzes demographic shifts among foreign-born residents in Spain, highlighting that the majority fall within the working-age bracket of 26 to 64 years. This concentration indicates that international migration is primarily driven by labor mobility, reinforcing the role of immigrants in sustaining the active workforce. However, regional disparities reveal distinct patterns: while some autonomous communities rely heavily on this demographic for economic activity, others exhibit different profiles, such as a higher proportion of retirees in the Canary Islands or a significant presence of young students and children in the Valencian Community and the Basque Country. These variations underscore the localized impact of migration policies and economic opportunities across the country. The data suggests that migration is not a uniform phenomenon but rather a targeted response to specific regional needs, whether for immediate employment, family reunification, or education. Understanding these nuances is crucial for policymakers aiming to integrate migrant populations effectively, as the social and economic pressures differ significantly depending on whether a region attracts young workers, families, or the elderly. This information is relevant to open data because it exemplifies how detailed, granular statistical releases from national institutes like the INE enable evidence-based governance. By providing accessible breakdowns of population metrics by age, gender, and region, open data facilitates rigorous analysis of societal trends. Researchers, journalists, and public administrators can leverage these structured datasets to monitor integration progress, allocate resources efficiently, and foster transparency in how demographic changes shape the social fabric of the nation.
