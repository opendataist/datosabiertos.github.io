Coahuila, entre los estados que más reciben quejas contra policías, pero uno de los que menos sanciona

The article highlights a significant rise in complaints against police in Coahuila, revealing a systemic gap between high public denunciations and minimal disciplinary actions. This data underscores the critical necessity for transparent, accessible official statistics to hold institutions accountable, as current reporting methods often obscure the true extent of misconduct and lack of consequence. Open data initiatives are essential to expose these discrepancies, allowing civil society to verify whether investigative bodies are fulfilling their mandates. Without granular, published datasets detailing complaint types and sanction outcomes, it remains difficult to assess the effectiveness of internal controls or the genuine commitment to human rights protection within law enforcement agencies. Ultimately, this case illustrates how structured open data can drive institutional reform by making accountability metrics visible. By demanding comprehensive publication of such censuses, society can pressure governments to improve oversight mechanisms, ensuring that reported abuses translate into meaningful disciplinary measures rather than remaining as unaddressed grievances.
