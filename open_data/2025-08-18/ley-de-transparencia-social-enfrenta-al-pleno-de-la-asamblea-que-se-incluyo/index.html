Ley de Transparencia Social enfrenta al Pleno de la Asamblea: ¿Qué se incluyó?

Ley de Transparencia Social enfrenta al Pleno de la Asamblea: ¿Qué se incluyó?

Ecuador’s government is advancing urgent legislation to regulate non-governmental organizations, citing significant risks of financial irregularities and criminal infiltration. By mandating that the Superintendence of the Popular and Solidarity Economy oversee these entities, the executive branch aims to monitor fund flows and prevent money laundering. This regulatory shift emphasizes that organizations managing substantial resources, particularly those with international funding or high risk profiles, must undergo strict government supervision. The proposed law establishes a mandatory unified information system under the Ministry of Government, requiring all social organizations to register before operating. While officials argue this is a necessary security measure to combat organized crime’s influence on civil society, the framework introduces rigorous criteria based on resource volume and beneficiary numbers to determine oversight levels. The legislation intends to create a transparent registry of all non-profit activities within the national territory. This development is critical for open data advocates because it centralizes sensitive information about social organizations under state control, potentially impacting transparency and civil liberties. Although the bill includes safeguards against political persecution, the consolidation of oversight mechanisms raises concerns about arbitrary restrictions on freedom of association. Open data principles emphasize that such registries must remain accessible and verifiable to ensure accountability, rather than serving solely as tools for administrative surveillance or political suppression.
