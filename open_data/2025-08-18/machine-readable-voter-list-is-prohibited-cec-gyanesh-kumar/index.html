"Machine-readable voter list is prohibited": CEC Gyanesh Kumar

"Machine-readable voter list is prohibited": CEC Gyanesh Kumar

The Election Commission of India has reaffirmed that machine-readable voter lists are prohibited, a stance grounded in a 2019 decision following a Supreme Court judgment. This prohibition exists because providing data in a machine-readable format was found to potentially violate voter privacy. The Commission clearly distinguishes this from searchable lists, which remain available online for public verification by individual EPIC numbers, thereby balancing transparency with individual data protection. In response to political criticism regarding the Special Intensive Revision in Bihar, the Chief Election Commissioner emphasized a commitment to transparency and collaboration with all stakeholders. By highlighting the extensive involvement of Booth Level Agents and the active participation of voters in the verification process, the Commission seeks to counter narratives of opacity. The focus remains on demonstrating that the revision process is credible, inclusive, and conducted with the consent and oversight of diverse political parties and the electorate. This clarification is highly relevant to open_data advocacy, as it illustrates the delicate balance between data accessibility and privacy rights in sensitive civic databases. While open data initiatives often push for granular, machine-readable access to public records, this case demonstrates why certain governmental data must remain structured to prevent misuse. It underscores the necessity of implementing privacy-preserving frameworks, such as searchable but non-downloadable formats, when dealing with personal citizen information in democratic processes.
