The study reveals a labor paradox characterized by a disconnect between the supply and demand for skills. Although the unemployment rate remains high, many service-sector establishments report significant vacancies, particularly in technology and transportation. This situation indicates that the core problem is not merely a lack of jobs, but rather a mismatch between the competencies of job seekers and the requirements demanded by employers, such as experience, language proficiency, and specific training. The relevance to open data lies in the need for transparency and granular analysis to address this friction. The data demonstrate that generic employment policies are insufficient; what is required is microeconomic, sector-specific precision grounded in real evidence. By opening and analyzing data on vacancies and missing skills, governments can design targeted training programs, bridging the gap between available education and market needs. Finally, the report underscores the urgency of coordinating efforts between educational institutions and the productive sector. Only through the rigorous use of public data on skills gaps can effective educational reforms be implemented. This transforms open data into a vital tool for labor inclusion, ensuring that the workforce is prepared to fill the positions the economy truly needs, thereby avoiding the waste of talent and resources.
