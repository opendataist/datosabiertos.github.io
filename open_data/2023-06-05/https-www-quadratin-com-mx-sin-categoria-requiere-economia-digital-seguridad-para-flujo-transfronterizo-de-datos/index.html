Open Data
ES EN

The digital economy relies heavily on personal data as its primary currency, necessitating robust security mechanisms to ensure its safe cross-border flow. A key conclusion from recent international dialogues is the urgent need for collaborative efforts between the private and public sectors to adopt innovative cooperation instruments. This joint action aims to protect users while facilitating global data movement, emphasizing that trust is the foundation of a thriving digital marketplace. Harmonized regulatory frameworks and strict international rules are essential to guarantee effective protection and transparency when personal information crosses borders. Experts argue that consistent standards and effective oversight by authorities prevent a lack of confidence and governance. When consumers trust that their data is handled professionally and legally, they are more willing to engage with e-commerce platforms, thereby strengthening the digital economy. This discussion is highly relevant to open data, as it highlights the critical balance between data utility and privacy rights. For data to be truly open and accessible, it must first be secure and governed by clear, interoperable regulations. Establishing trust through standardized protection mechanisms allows for the responsible sharing of information, ensuring that open data initiatives do not compromise individual privacy but instead enhance societal and economic value through safe, transboundary data exchange.
