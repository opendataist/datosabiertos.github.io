Ireland saw a 10.1% decrease in births from 2021 to 2022

Ireland’s 2022 vital statistics reveal a significant demographic shift, marked by a sharp decline in births and a simultaneous rise in deaths. This divergence highlights deepening changes in population dynamics, with the birth rate dropping considerably from previous years while mortality increases. These contrasting trends suggest a period of profound adjustment in the country’s demographic structure, moving away from the growth patterns observed in the early 2010s. The data further illustrates evolving social norms and health patterns, including an older average age for mothers and a substantial increase in births outside of marriage. The fertility rate has fallen well below the replacement level, indicating long-term challenges for population sustainability. Meanwhile, the geographic distribution of births and the stability of home births offer insights into regional healthcare access and societal preferences, reflecting a complex interplay between cultural change and statistical reality. This report is crucial for open data enthusiasts because it demonstrates how granular, disaggregated datasets can uncover nuanced societal shifts beyond headline figures. By providing detailed breakdowns by geography, age, nationality, and occupation, the Central Statistics Office enables complex analysis and visualization. Such transparency allows researchers and the public to explore interconnected themes like migration, healthcare, and family structures, fostering a deeper understanding of how raw data informs public policy and social discourse.
