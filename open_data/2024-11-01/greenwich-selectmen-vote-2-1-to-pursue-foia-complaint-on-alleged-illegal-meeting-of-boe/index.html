Greenwich Selectmen Vote 2-1 to Pursue FOIA Complaint on Alleged “Illegal Meeting” of BOE

The Greenwich Board of Selectmen voted to file a Freedom of Information Commission complaint against the Board of Education, alleging that recent emergency meetings held to appoint a new board member were conducted illegally. The core contention involves the lack of proper notice for these sessions, which the Selectmen argue violated transparency statutes. By challenging the validity of these meetings, the Selectmen aim to nullify the appointment decisions made during them, thereby forcing the town to restart the selection process in strict compliance with public access laws. This administrative conflict highlights the critical intersection of government procedures and the public’s right to access information. It underscores how strict adherence to transparency protocols prevents political maneuvering and ensures that elected bodies operate within legal boundaries. The dispute serves as a cautionary example of what happens when notice requirements are bypassed, potentially rendering official actions void and destabilizing municipal governance. The relevance to open data lies in its demonstration of how information accessibility laws enforce accountability in local democracy. When data regarding meeting schedules and participant eligibility is withheld or mishandled, it undermines public trust and invites legal challenges. This case illustrates that open data is not merely about publishing records, but about ensuring that the processes governing their creation are transparent, legally sound, and accessible to all citizens, preventing unilateral power grabs by any single faction.
