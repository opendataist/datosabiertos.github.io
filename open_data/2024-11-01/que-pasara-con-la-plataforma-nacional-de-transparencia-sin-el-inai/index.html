¿Qué pasará con la Plataforma Nacional de Transparencia sin el INAI?

¿Qué pasará con la Plataforma Nacional de Transparencia sin el INAI?

The article examines the historical evolution of transparency mechanisms in Mexico, highlighting how the National Transparency Platform consolidated two decades of effort by centralizing access to information from thousands of entities. This unified tool drastically simplified accountability, enabling citizens to consult data, submit requests, and file complaints through a single, efficient digital channel, thereby eliminating the administrative fragmentation that characterized previous systems. The Executive Branch’s reform proposal to absorb the National Institute of Transparency into the Executive Power is presented as a direct threat to this infrastructure. By eliminating autonomous bodies, the platform would lose its independent oversight and appeal functions, being reduced to a mere informational repository of the government. This would fragment access to information, forcing citizens to interact directly with each authority, which creates conflicts of interest and hinders effective supervision of transparency obligations. This analysis is crucial for the open data movement because it illustrates how the technical governance of data depends on institutional independence. Without autonomous mechanisms that guarantee access and correction, the quality and availability of public data are compromised, undermining accountability. The potential disappearance of the platform represents a systemic setback, demonstrating that effective transparency requires not only accessible data but also institutions capable of ensuring its integrity and democratic use.
