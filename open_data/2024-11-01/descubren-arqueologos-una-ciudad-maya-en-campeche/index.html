Descubren arqueólogos una ciudad maya en Campeche

Descubren arqueólogos una ciudad maya en Campeche

Archaeologists uncovered thousands of hidden Maya structures in Campeche by repurposing LiDAR data originally collected for environmental carbon monitoring. This discovery underscores the immense value of open scientific data, demonstrating how publicly available datasets can yield unexpected archaeological breakthroughs when analyzed through different disciplinary perspectives. The finding reveals a densely populated ancient urban landscape, including a major city near modern settlements, fundamentally reshaping our understanding of the region’s historical significance. It shows that open data transcends its original purpose, serving as a critical resource for diverse research communities and fostering interdisciplinary collaboration. This case is crucial for the open data movement, as it illustrates the potential for secondary use of published information. By making environmental data accessible, scientists enabled this cultural heritage discovery, reinforcing the argument that open access accelerates knowledge creation and maximizes the societal impact of publicly funded research initiatives.
