Blockchain+ Bi-Weekly; Highlights of the Last Two Weeks in Web3 Law: October 31, 2024

Blockchain+ Bi-Weekly; Highlights of the Last Two Weeks in Web3 Law: October 31, 2024

The article highlights a critical juncture for Web3 regulation, where legal strategies are shifting toward demanding transparency and defining jurisdictional boundaries. A primary focus is Coinbase’s litigation against the SEC to obtain withheld documents via Freedom of Information Act requests. This move signals a broader trend where industry participants may leverage transparency laws to challenge opaque regulatory practices, setting a precedent for how digital asset companies interact with federal agencies during ongoing enforcement actions. Simultaneously, the sector is grappling with the extent of U.S. legal reach in a decentralized global internet. Through amicus briefs in Supreme Court appeals, industry advocates are questioning whether server location alone should dictate jurisdiction over crypto transactions. This challenge addresses fears that expansive interpretations of U.S. law could unfairly target global users and infrastructure, emphasizing the need for clear rules that accommodate the borderless nature of modern digital networks. Regulatory bodies are also adapting their frameworks to encompass emerging technologies like the metaverse and prediction markets. These developments, combined with examination priorities from the SEC, illustrate a regulatory environment in flux, influenced by upcoming political changes. The article underscores that the balance between fostering innovation and enforcing compliance is being actively negotiated, directly impacting the operational viability and legal clarity of open data and digital asset ecosystems.
