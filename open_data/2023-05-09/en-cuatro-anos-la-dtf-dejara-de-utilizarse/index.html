En cuatro años la DTF dejará de utilizarse

En cuatro años la DTF dejará de utilizarse

The incorporation of open data and open banking principles into Colombia’s new National Development Plan marks a significant shift toward financial transparency and user empowerment. This policy framework mandates that financial data sharing requires explicit user consent and is strictly limited to improving access to financial services, thereby prioritizing consumer protection and privacy. By establishing clear boundaries for data usage, the government aims to foster trust in the digital financial ecosystem while encouraging secure interoperability among institutions. This approach is crucial for open data initiatives as it demonstrates how structured data sharing can drive social inclusion without compromising individual rights. The plan intends to enhance financial accessibility, particularly for vulnerable populations, by enabling better customer profiling through aggregated data. This mechanism promotes competition among banks, potentially lowering costs and reducing reliance on informal lending practices. Consequently, it validates the potential of open data to create more efficient and equitable financial markets by connecting diverse information sources to serve the public interest. Ultimately, the integration of these technologies supports broader economic goals, including agrarian reform and support for the popular economy. The mandatory affiliation with specific insurance entities and new credit mechanisms further illustrate a holistic strategy to formalize financial relationships. By embedding open banking within the national roadmap, Colombia sets a precedent for using open data as a tool for socio-economic development. This highlights the critical role of open data policies in modern governance, proving that transparency and user-centric data models are essential for building inclusive and resilient financial systems.
