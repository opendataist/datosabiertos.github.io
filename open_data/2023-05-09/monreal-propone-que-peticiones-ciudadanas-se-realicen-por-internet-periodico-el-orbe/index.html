Monreal Propone que Peticiones Ciudadanas se Realicen por Internet - Periódico El Orbe.

Senator Ricardo Monreal advocates for a constitutional reform to recognize electronic petitions, aiming to establish a truly open government in Mexico. By updating the right of petition to include digital channels, the proposal seeks to ensure that all citizens can effectively interact with state authorities through modern means. This legislative change is designed to bridge the gap between traditional bureaucratic processes and contemporary digital habits, fostering a more inclusive and responsive democratic dialogue. The reform addresses a critical gap where informal digital interactions often lack legal recognition, leading to neglected public requests and subsequent judicial disputes. Monreal argues that the Constitution must evolve to reflect technological realities, ensuring that online complaints and petitions are treated with the same weight as formal written submissions. This update would eliminate the need for citizens to seek legal protection through amparo trials to obtain basic governmental responses, thereby streamlining civic engagement and reducing administrative burdens on the judiciary. This initiative is highly relevant to open data because it establishes the legal foundation for digital transparency and accessibility. Recognizing electronic petitions ensures that the flow of information between the state and citizens is recorded, processed, and made available through digital platforms. This alignment of legal frameworks with digital tools promotes accountability and enables the use of open data technologies to monitor government responsiveness, ultimately empowering citizens with greater access to information and more effective channels for participation.
