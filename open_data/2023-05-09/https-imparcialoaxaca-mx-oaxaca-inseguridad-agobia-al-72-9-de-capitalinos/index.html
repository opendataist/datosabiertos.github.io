The article highlights a rise in perceived insecurity in Oaxaca de Juárez, revealing widespread fear during daily activities and negative expectations for the future. Residents significantly altered their behavior, restricting mobility and limiting children’s independence due to crime concerns, while identifying specific high-risk locations such as ATMs and public transport. Crucially, there is a stark contrast in public trust, with citizens favoring the military and navy over local police forces. This data underscores the gap between institutional effectiveness and citizen satisfaction, illustrating how perceived safety directly impacts community well-being and freedom of movement in urban environments. This resource is vital for open data initiatives because it demonstrates how standardized, transparent survey metrics can quantify subjective societal experiences. By making perception data accessible, researchers and policymakers can better analyze the correlation between security policies and public sentiment, enabling more evidence-based interventions to rebuild trust and address the root causes of urban insecurity.
