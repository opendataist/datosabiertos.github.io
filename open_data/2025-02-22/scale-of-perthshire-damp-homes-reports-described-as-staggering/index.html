Scale of Perthshire damp homes reports described as "staggering"

Scale of Perthshire damp homes reports described as "staggering"

Recent data reveals a dramatic surge in complaints regarding mould and damp within social housing in Perth and Kinross, highlighting a significant decline in living conditions over the last two years. This escalation underscores the growing pressure on local authorities to maintain habitable environments, raising serious concerns about tenant health and the adequacy of current maintenance resources. While political figures blame budget cuts for these deteriorating standards, the council has responded by implementing innovative technological solutions. By installing environmental sensors to monitor temperature and humidity in real-time, the authority aims to proactively identify risks before they escalate. This shift toward data-driven management allows for more precise intervention and the establishment of dedicated teams to address complex structural issues effectively. This case is particularly relevant to open data discussions as it demonstrates the utility of Freedom of Information requests in holding public bodies accountable. The transparency of complaint statistics not only exposes systemic failures but also validates the impact of new monitoring technologies. Such publicly available data empowers citizens and researchers to assess housing quality objectively, fostering informed debates on policy effectiveness and resource allocation in the public sector.
