México confirma crecimiento del PIB de 1,5 % en 2024, pero desacelera el ritmo

Mexico’s 2024 economic growth fell short of government projections, registering a modest 1.5% annual increase followed by a technical recession in the final quarter. This downturn, driven primarily by sharp declines in agriculture and industry, signals weakening domestic momentum. The data highlights the vulnerability of Mexico’s economy to external shocks, particularly as it navigates the beginning of President Claudia Sheinbaum’s term amidst significant global uncertainty. The situation is further complicated by US political changes, specifically the anticipation of new tariffs under Donald Trump’s administration. Financial institutions have drastically revised their growth forecasts downward, warning of potential recessions or significant contractions. This shift reflects deep concerns that the loss of preferential trade conditions with the United States, which absorbs the majority of Mexican exports, could trigger a severe economic downturn, overshadowing domestic policy efforts.
