Estos son los pasos a seguir para desaparecer el INAI: ¿Ahora quién realizará sus funciones?

Estos son los pasos a seguir para desaparecer el INAI: ¿Ahora quién realizará sus funciones?

The initiative dismantles the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), transferring its powers and resources to the Anti-Corruption Secretariat. This centralization shifts transparency oversight from an independent body to the executive branch, fundamentally altering Mexico's governance structure. It creates a new national system managed by the government, consolidating control over access to public information and the transparency platform within the same institution it is meant to regulate. This legislative change impacts the open data ecosystem by weakening institutional checks and balances essential for information freedom. By removing the INAI, the state risks reducing the independence and efficiency of data requests, potentially compromising the quality and availability of public information. The restructuring prioritizes administrative control over impartial oversight, raising concerns about the integrity of data management. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates as it signals a regression in transparency standards. The removal of an autonomous regulatory body may hinder accountability and limit civil society’s ability to demand information freely. It underscores the critical importance of maintaining independent institutions to protect data rights and ensure that open government initiatives remain truly transparent and accessible to all citizens, rather than subject to political discretion.
