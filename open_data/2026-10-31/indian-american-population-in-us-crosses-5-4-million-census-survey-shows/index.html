Indian American population in US crosses 5.4 million, Census survey shows

Indian American population in US crosses 5.4 million, Census survey shows

The latest American Community Survey highlights significant demographic shifts, particularly noting that Asian Indians have become the largest single Asian group in the United States. This growth underscores the increasing diversity of the immigrant population and reflects changing migration patterns, with India emerging as the second-largest source country for US immigrants. These detailed population metrics are crucial for understanding community composition and planning resources for diverse cultural groups. Beyond demographics, the survey reveals notable economic and technological trends, such as rising median household incomes and a surge in electric vehicle adoption. Western states, especially California, lead in EV ownership, indicating regional variations in infrastructure development and consumer behavior. The inclusion of new data points, like sewer system access, demonstrates the Census Bureau’s commitment to expanding the scope of available statistical information to better serve local and national planning needs. This article is relevant to open data because it emphasizes the role of publicly accessible, granular datasets in informing policy and business decisions. The American Community Survey provides essential, high-quality data that supports transparency and enables researchers to analyze social, economic, and environmental trends. By making such detailed estimates available, government agencies foster an open data ecosystem that empowers stakeholders to make evidence-based decisions, ensuring that diverse population needs and emerging technologies are adequately addressed in public discourse and resource allocation.
