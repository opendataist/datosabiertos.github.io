2024 Census: Venezuela hosts the largest immigrant population, while Chile surpasses 1.6 million foreigners

2024 Census: Venezuela hosts the largest immigrant population, while Chile surpasses 1.6 million foreigners

The recent census reveals that Chile’s immigrant population has doubled over the past decade, with the majority originating from Venezuela, Peru, and Colombia. This demographic expansion highlights the country’s growing diversity and the consolidation of new communities that are reshaping its social fabric. The presence of these groups reflects significant migratory dynamics that require ongoing monitoring. Immigrants exhibit distinct profiles, being notably younger and more highly educated than native-born Chileans. They also show substantially higher labor force participation rates, underscoring their fundamental economic contribution. These demographic and labor market differences indicate that the foreign-born population not only fills demographic gaps but also serves as a key driver of productivity and dynamism in the national labor market. This report is relevant to the open data community by exemplifying how official statistics can be disaggregated to analyze social and economic impacts. Transparency in these data enables the investigation of inequalities, the design of evidence-based public policies, and a deeper understanding of cultural integration. Finally, it facilitates government accountability, providing crucial tools for citizens and academic research on migration and diversity.
