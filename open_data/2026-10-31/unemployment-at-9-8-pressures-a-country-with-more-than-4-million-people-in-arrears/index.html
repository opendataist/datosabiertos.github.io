Unemployment at 9.8% pressures a country with more than 4 million people in arrears

The current labor and financial situation reveals a systemic crisis in which rising unemployment directly drives an increase in payment delinquency. This correlation demonstrates that failure to make payments is not the result of individual irresponsibility, but rather an inevitable response to income loss, disproportionately affecting households with family responsibilities and limited economic resources. It is essential to understand that unpaid debt acts as a vicious cycle that worsens over time, generating psychological stress and additional difficulties beyond the purely monetary aspect. The tendency to avoid debt collectors only exacerbates the problem, as interest charges and penalties make the situation increasingly difficult to resolve, negatively impacting family stability and the overall well-being of those affected. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the importance of integrating and making transparent a variety of socioeconomic indicators. By cross-referencing employment data with information on public and private debt, the urgency of evidence-based policies becomes evident. Transparency in these datasets enables the identification of vulnerable populations, facilitating the design of effective solutions and the development of tools that help people manage their finances proactively and with less stigma.
