Eugene Becomes 5th Oregon City to Adopt Home Energy Score Requirement - CleanTechnica

Eugene, Oregon, has officially adopted a Residential Energy Performance Disclosure Requirement, joining other major Oregon cities in mandating home energy scores during property sales. This ordinance requires sellers to provide an assessment evaluating a home’s energy efficiency, emissions impact, and potential upgrades. By integrating this requirement into the real estate transaction process, the city ensures that prospective buyers receive critical data regarding energy costs and climate impacts before finalizing their purchase. The policy serves as a vital tool for empowering consumers to make informed financial and environmental decisions. Home energy scores not only rank a property’s efficiency but also highlight available local, state, and federal incentives for improvements. This transparency aims to address the widespread underutilization of existing subsidies, guiding new homeowners toward energy-saving measures like heat pumps and weatherization. Consequently, residents are better positioned to reduce their utility bills while contributing to broader decarbonization efforts. This development is highly relevant to the open data movement because it institutionalizes the public release of granular housing efficiency metrics. By mandating that performance data be disclosed during sales, the ordinance transforms private energy characteristics into accessible public information. This practice supports transparency, enables community analysis of building emissions, and fosters accountability in climate recovery goals. Such standardized data disclosure models demonstrate how local policy can leverage open information to drive energy efficiency and support evidence-based environmental planning.
