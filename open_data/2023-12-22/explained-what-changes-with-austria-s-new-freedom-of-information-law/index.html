EXPLAINED: What changes with Austria's new Freedom of Information law?

EXPLAINED: What changes with Austria's new Freedom of Information law?

Austria’s new Right to Information Act fundamentally transforms government transparency by establishing a constitutional entitlement for citizens to access public data. This landmark shift dismantles historical official secrecy, compelling authorities to proactively justify any refusal to disclose information while ensuring that all inquiries remain free of charge. By mandating short processing deadlines and introducing judicial oversight for denials, the legislation empowers individuals to hold public institutions accountable. The law also introduces a proactive duty for most public bodies to automatically publish information of general interest, significantly reducing the administrative burden on citizens seeking public records. This extension covers state-owned enterprises and integrates data protection considerations into the disclosure process, ensuring a balance between openness and privacy. Small municipalities are exempted, but larger entities must streamline their operations to meet these enhanced transparency standards. This development is highly relevant to open data because it legally enforces the availability of public sector information, moving beyond voluntary disclosure to mandatory transparency. It establishes a robust framework for data accessibility, encouraging the proactive release of datasets and fostering a culture where government information is treated as a public asset. Consequently, this legislation serves as a critical foundation for advancing open data initiatives globally.
