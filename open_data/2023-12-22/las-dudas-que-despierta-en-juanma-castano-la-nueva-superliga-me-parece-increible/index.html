Las dudas que despierta en Juanma Castaño la nueva Superliga: "Me parece increíble"

The European Court of Justice’s ruling marks a pivotal shift by declaring that UEFA and FIFA cannot prevent clubs from joining alternative competitions, categorizing their previous interference as an abuse of dominant power. This decision legally dismantles the monopoly held by established governing bodies, affirming that football clubs have the right to self-governance and organize their own tournaments without facing sanctions. Consequently, the viability of new structures like the Superliga is fundamentally secured, transitioning the project from a prohibited entity to a legitimate organizational possibility. The newly detailed competition structure aims to decentralize control and revenue distribution away from traditional federations toward the clubs themselves. By establishing a tiered system with promotion and relegation, the initiative seeks to create a more open market where teams dictate their financial future. Although currently supported primarily by major Spanish clubs, the legal framework allows for broader participation, challenging the status quo and empowering individual entities to negotiate their own economic terms within the European football landscape. This ruling is highly relevant to open data as it underscores the critical need for transparent, accessible, and standardized information in sports governance. For such a multi-club, multi-nation competition to function fairly and be monitored effectively, stakeholders require reliable data regarding club eligibility, financial flows, and performance metrics. Without open data standards, the promised transparency regarding revenue distribution and tournament structure could remain obscured, potentially leading to new forms of opacity despite the legal victory.
