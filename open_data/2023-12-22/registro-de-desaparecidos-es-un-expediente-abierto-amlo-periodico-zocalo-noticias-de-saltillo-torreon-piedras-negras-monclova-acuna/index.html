Registro de desaparecidos es un expediente abierto: AMLO | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

President López Obrador defends his government’s methodology for updating the register of missing persons, dismissing criticisms regarding the validation of over 12,000 cases as politically motivated attacks by civil society groups aligned with past neoliberal regimes. He argues that recent inquiries show higher disappearance rates not due to systemic failure, but because new commissions encourage more people to report cases, thereby triggering a necessary government response to locate them. The administration emphasizes that the review is an ongoing process rather than a closed case, aiming to clarify the status of tens of thousands of individuals by categorizing them as found, located, or lacking sufficient data. By ordering further reports, the presidency seeks to demonstrate that their actions have successfully resolved numerous missing-person cases, contrasting their approach with that of previous administrations and accusing former officials of manipulating data to undermine current efforts. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical role of transparency in official statistics, particularly concerning human rights. It underscores the tension between governmental narratives and independent verification, demonstrating how disputed datasets can spark public controversy and necessitate clearer, reproducible methodologies to build trust and ensure accurate accountability in public records.
