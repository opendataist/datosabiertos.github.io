Inflación de Panamá fue de 0,2% en mayo de 2023

The analysis of Panama’s Urban National Consumer Price Index reveals a nuanced economic landscape in which short-term stability masks significant long-term pressures. Although monthly fluctuations were minimal, indicating a pause in immediate price spikes, year-over-year data expose a distinct divergence in cost structures across essential sectors. This contrast highlights how certain basic needs are becoming increasingly burdensome for households, while other discretionary or durable goods are experiencing price relief. Food, energy, and housing costs have driven substantial year-over-year inflation, significantly impacting residents’ cost of living. In contrast, transportation and healthcare expenses have actually decreased over the same period, likely due to stabilization in global fuel markets and specific sectoral dynamics. This bifurcation underscores that inflation is not uniform but is instead concentrated in items that constitute the core basket of household consumption, directly affecting daily welfare and purchasing power. This data is particularly relevant to the open data community, as it exemplifies the importance of granular, sector-specific analysis over aggregate metrics. By providing detailed breakdowns of price changes across diverse categories, such datasets enable researchers and policymakers to move beyond simple headline inflation figures. Open access to this level of detail facilitates a better understanding of economic resilience, supports the design of targeted social policies, and allows for more accurate modeling of consumer behavior in response to specific price shocks.
