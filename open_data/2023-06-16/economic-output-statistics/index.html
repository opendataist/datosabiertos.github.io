Economic Output Statistics

Northern Ireland’s economic landscape shows a distinct divergence between its robust services sector and its struggling production and retail industries. While services have reached series highs and significantly outperformed pre-pandemic levels, production has faced consecutive declines, and retail sales remain well below their previous peaks. This split highlights a recovery that is uneven across different economic activities, with consumer-facing services rebounding strongly while manufacturing and utility outputs contract. The resilience of the services sector is particularly notable, as it continues to grow both quarterly and annually, driven largely by business services and finance. In contrast, the production sector’s recent downturn suggests ongoing challenges in industrial output, despite remaining slightly above pre-pandemic benchmarks. Meanwhile, retail has failed to recover fully, lagging behind its pre-pandemic status. This creates a complex economic narrative where growth is concentrated in specific high-value service areas while traditional trade and manufacturing lag behind. This data is relevant to open_data because it illustrates the importance of granular, sector-specific economic indicators for transparent governance and analysis. By making detailed statistics on output, subsectors, and temporal trends publicly available, agencies like NISRA enable researchers, policymakers, and businesses to identify structural shifts accurately. Open access to such nuanced data fosters accountability and allows stakeholders to make informed decisions based on a complete picture of economic health, rather than relying on aggregated or opaque figures.
