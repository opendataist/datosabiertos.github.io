Arkansas AG Tim Griffin forms group to consider possible changes to state's Freedom of Information Act

Arkansas Attorney General Tim Griffin has established a working group to review the state’s Freedom of Information Act, aiming to modernize regulations that have remained largely unchanged since the late 1960s. This initiative arises after recent legislative attempts to weaken public records access were defeated due to concerns over transparency. The new body seeks to address the complexities introduced by the exponential growth of digital records and communication technologies, which have made fulfilling FOIA requests significantly more challenging for public entities. The group will deliberate on potential updates, considering the technological landscape that has evolved far beyond the era when the original law was enacted. Although the working group’s internal meetings are closed to the public, any recommendations proposed for the upcoming legislative session will be made available. This approach allows for strategic planning and discussion among legal experts, lawmakers, and press representatives before formal bills are introduced, ensuring that proposed changes are carefully considered. This development is highly relevant to the open data community, as it highlights the ongoing tension between administrative efficiency and the public’s right to access government information. The review process underscores the urgent need for laws to adapt to digital advancements, ensuring that open data principles are preserved rather than eroded by expanding exemptions or opaque meeting practices. Maintaining strong FOIA frameworks is essential for fostering transparency and accountability in an increasingly complex digital governance environment.
