Caídas en Asia: los PMI servicios de China y Japón aún crecen, pero se ralentizan

Financial markets across the Asia-Pacific region experienced a broad-based decline due to investor disappointment with the latest services sector activity data from China and Japan. Although both countries still show expansionary indicators, the pace of growth has slowed notably compared to previous months. This deceleration has fueled uncertainty about the robustness of recent economic recoveries, negatively affecting market confidence and triggering losses in the region’s major stock indices. In China, surveys reflect weaker-than-expected demand, with new business and order volumes expanding at a slower pace, suggesting that initial post-pandemic optimism is cooling. Similarly, Japan’s services sector appears to have likely peaked, even though it remains above its historical average. The convergence of these deceleration signals indicates that growth momentum may be waning, posing a challenge to medium-term economic sustainability in these key economies. This article is relevant to *open_data* because it highlights how public and immediate access to high-quality economic indicators, such as Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) data, is essential for market transparency and investment decision-making. The ability of analysts and citizens to quickly interpret these open data sets enables a better understanding of global macroeconomic dynamics and helps anticipate volatility, underscoring the importance of making official statistics available to foster an informed and responsible economy.
