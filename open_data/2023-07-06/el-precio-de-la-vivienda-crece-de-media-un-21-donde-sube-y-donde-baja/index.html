El precio de la vivienda crece de media un 2,1%: ¿Dónde sube y dónde baja?

The Spanish real estate market is experiencing a historic increase in second-hand housing prices, reaching levels similar to those before the 2006 crisis, although they remain below their all-time high. This price acceleration is primarily driven by a shortage of supply and a rebound in demand, which are side effects of the European Central Bank’s interest rate hikes. However, it is anticipated that the growing burden of mortgage costs will moderate this trend in the short term, restoring stability to the market. Price dynamics vary significantly by region, highlighting a divergence between high-demand areas and those with lower activity. The most expensive autonomous communities, such as the Balearic Islands and Madrid, exceed €3,000 per square meter, while several central and southern provinces offer more affordable prices. In capital cities, the disparity is even more pronounced, with San Sebastián leading in costs, far surpassing other major cities. This reflects how geographic location remains the primary determinant of real estate asset value. This article is relevant to the field of open data by illustrating the importance of transparency and accessibility of statistical information. Indices such as Fotocasa’s demonstrate how the availability of detailed data at the provincial and municipal levels enables citizens, investors, and policymakers to understand market trends and territorial inequalities. The dissemination of these indicators fosters accountability and informed decision-making, which are essential for assessing the impact of monetary policies and urban planning on social well-being.
