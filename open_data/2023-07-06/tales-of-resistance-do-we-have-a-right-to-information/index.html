Tales Of Resistance: Do We Have A Right To Information?

The article argues that the Venezuelan state operates with a patronizing secrecy, treating citizens like partners who should intuitively understand systemic failures without explanation. This lack of transparency creates a toxic dynamic where the public is forced to guess about essential services like water, electricity, and fuel. Instead of receiving proactive updates, people must uncover basic information through grassroots efforts, leading to unnecessary anxiety and an inability to plan for daily survival. This selective disclosure contradicts official rhetoric that frames information as a fundamental right rather than a gift. While authorities occasionally demand better reporting, they simultaneously suppress inconvenient data, such as the abrupt halt of economic statistics or the reasons behind infrastructure breakdowns. The government’s narrative is curated to show only success, hiding operational failures until forced into the open by public reality, thereby undermining any genuine claim to respect for citizen知情权. For open data, this piece highlights the critical distinction between mere publication and genuine transparency. True open data requires accessibility and context, not just the presence of figures that can be easily withheld. When states treat data as a controlled narrative tool rather than a public resource, they erode trust and prevent accountability. The article serves as a stark warning that without systematic, unbiased disclosure, open data initiatives remain superficial, failing to empower citizens or ensure governmental responsibility.
