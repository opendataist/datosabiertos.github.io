The US economy is resilient Investing.com

The article highlights the unexpected resilience of the US economy, characterized by a robust labor market, a rebounding housing sector, and steady GDP growth. This stability stands in stark contrast to the anticipated recession, positioning the US as an economic oasis amid global slowdowns. Simultaneously, aggressive monetary tightening by the Federal Reserve has successfully driven inflation down significantly, demonstrating that central bankers are winning their primary battle against rising prices. However, this positive outlook masks a critical underlying vulnerability: the massive expansion of government fiscal stimulus. While central banks raise rates to cool the economy, governments continue to borrow heavily to maintain stability, effectively undoing monetary tightening efforts. This dynamic creates a paradox where inflation falls due to rate hikes, yet asset prices remain high and economic activity persists due to continued fiscal support, raising concerns about the long-term sustainability of this dual-policy approach. This dynamic is highly relevant to open data because understanding these complex, conflicting economic forces requires transparent, accessible, and real-time statistical information. To accurately assess whether the US economy is truly resilient or merely propped up by unsustainable debt, researchers and the public need open access to precise employment, housing, and fiscal data. Without this transparency, it becomes impossible to monitor the emerging "doomloop" of rising interest costs and debt, hindering the ability to predict future policy shifts or economic stability.
