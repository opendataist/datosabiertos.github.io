El precio de la vivienda en la UE encadena dos trimestres a la baja por primera vez desde 2013

The European housing market is undergoing a prolonged downturn, characterized by consecutive quarterly price declines across numerous member states. This trend reflects the significant impact of the European Central Bank’s aggressive interest rate hikes, which have tightened credit conditions and increased borrowing costs for prospective buyers. Although some countries still report annual growth, the overall momentum has shifted toward stabilization or decline in key economies. The data highlight a divergence between countries, with certain regions maintaining growth while others face sharp corrections, illustrating the uneven recovery and regional disparities within the broader economic landscape. This report is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how publicly accessible, harmonized statistics from Eurostat enable transparent monitoring of critical economic sectors. Such open datasets empower researchers and policymakers to analyze market trends, assess policy effectiveness, and foster evidence-based decision-making regarding housing affordability and financial stability across the European Union.
