Mobile subscriptions in Nigeria decline to 220.9 million in May – NCC

Mobile service subscriptions in Nigeria have entered a sustained downward trend, marked by consecutive monthly declines that significantly impacted the industry's total database. This reduction is primarily driven by regulatory enforcement mechanisms, specifically the mandatory linking of SIM cards with national identification numbers. Consequently, major operators have seen substantial losses as users abandon non-compliant lines, reflecting a broader shift toward stricter identity verification standards within the telecommunications sector. The decline in active subscriptions has directly reduced the country’s teledensity, highlighting the tangible effects of policy implementation on market penetration. While one major operator suffered significant user attrition, other networks managed to post minor gains, suggesting varied capacity to adapt to these stringent compliance requirements. This divergence underscores the challenges smaller and larger players face differently when navigating regulatory mandates that prioritize security and identification over sheer connectivity volume. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how regulatory policies directly influence publicly available industry metrics. The NCC’s release of granular subscription data allows for transparent analysis of how compliance rules, such as those regarding inactive lines and SIM registration, reshape market dynamics. It demonstrates the importance of open datasets in tracking the real-world impact of government interventions on digital infrastructure and user behavior.
