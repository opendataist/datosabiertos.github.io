In the era of AI, focus shifts to management of data collected from users

The rapid integration of artificial intelligence into digital services has triggered a critical debate regarding user data privacy. As major technology firms update their policies to collect publicly available information for AI model training, it raises fundamental questions about the boundaries of data utilization. This shift necessitates a reevaluation of current safeguards, particularly concerning the removal of identifiers and the secure handling of data throughout the training lifecycle, highlighting the tension between technological advancement and individual privacy rights. To mitigate these risks, industry experts advocate for robust structural and technological solutions. Strategies such as data compartmentalization limit internal access to minimize breach impacts, while blockchain technology offers decentralized, immutable storage to prevent forgery and unauthorized alterations. These approaches emphasize that secure data handling is no longer optional but a core component of modern IT infrastructure, ensuring that privacy remains pronounced in an era where data breaches can have severe consequences. Relevance to open data lies in the urgent need to define ethical frameworks for using public information to train public or semi-public AI tools. While transparency in data sourcing is a principle of open data, the current trend of aggregating online information for proprietary models challenges the spirit of openness. Establishing clear guidelines on consent, anonymization, and access rights ensures that the benefits of AI do not come at the expense of user trust, making it essential for the open data community to influence how corporate data practices intersect with public interest and privacy regulations.
