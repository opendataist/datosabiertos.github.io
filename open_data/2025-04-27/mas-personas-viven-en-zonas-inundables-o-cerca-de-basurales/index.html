Más personas viven en zonas inundables o cerca de basurales

Más personas viven en zonas inundables o cerca de basurales

The article reveals that, despite a reported decline in poverty rates, living conditions in Argentina have significantly deteriorated. A substantial increase in the number of residents living near landfills or in flood-prone areas indicates a worsening of housing security and environmental risks. This suggests that aggregate economic indicators may mask the declining quality of life for vulnerable populations, highlighting a disconnect between statistical improvements and material reality. Furthermore, critical sanitation standards and property ownership have regressed, with more people lacking adequate bathroom facilities or stable housing tenure. The data underscores a rise in multidimensional poverty, driven not just by income but by the erosion of basic habitability. This regression points to systemic failures in infrastructure and social protection, showing that the reduction in monetary poverty has not been matched by gains in essential services or residential stability. This case is crucial for open data initiatives because it illustrates the necessity of disaggregated, multidimensional metrics. Relying solely on broad poverty lines obscures vital trends in housing quality and sanitation. Open data must prioritize accessible, granular indicators that capture these qualitative degradations, ensuring that policy decisions are based on a comprehensive understanding of social well-being rather than simplified economic aggregates.
