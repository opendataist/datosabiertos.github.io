‘Transparency key to stop misuse of RTI Act’

‘Transparency key to stop misuse of RTI Act’

Rahul Pande, Maharashtra’s Chief Information Commissioner, asserts that transparency under the Right to Information Act is a vital shield for government officials, preventing blackmail and controversy. By proactively disclosing information, authorities reduce opportunities for misuse, fostering an environment where honesty protects employees from intimidation. This approach emphasizes that secrecy, rather than openness, emboldens those with ill intent to target officials who have nothing to hide. Pande firmly rejects claims that the RTI Act is frequently weaponized for harassment, noting a lack of statistical evidence to support such allegations. He argues that genuine misuse is rare and accessible through legal channels, while suggesting that critics of the act often avoid using it themselves. His perspective highlights that accountability mechanisms function correctly when citizens utilize them to monitor government actions, spending, and works effectively. This article is relevant to open_data as it advocates for proactive data disclosure and robust public access to information. Pande’s stance reinforces the principle that open data serves as a crucial tool for transparency and accountability, encouraging broader citizen engagement. By promoting the systematic release of government information, including past RTI responses, the initiative aims to strengthen democratic oversight and ensure that data openness reduces corruption rather than enabling it.
