Open Data: Securing Mexican Trade Under USMCA

Global trade needs transparency. This protects honest businesses. It also keeps things fair. Tensions on the northern border show why this matters. The American Society in Mexico warns about a big problem. US rules check Chinese goods under the USMCA. This hurts Mexican exporters. Honest businesses face penalties. They struggle to prove where their products come from. Data helps us tell good partners from bad ones.

Open data is a key asset here. Mexico can check the true origin of goods. It does this by sharing data openly. This changes the story. It moves from suspicion to advantage. Governments and companies must choose how they use data. They must decide who owns it. Users need to know the rules for public records. This stops them from breaking the law. Our guide on open data licences explains the law. It helps users understand these rules.

Getting data is easier with the right format. Static PDFs are not enough. We need dynamic, machine-readable files. Supply chains need traceability. Auditors, partners, and the public must see raw data. Organizations use standards like CKAN, DCAT, and OpenDataSoft. These tools put public records into bigger systems. This makes open data truly “free.” Users with the right tools can pull stats. They use these to check for compliance. Our guide on how to use an open-data portal’s API shows the technical steps. It details how to get this data.

Users in the consideration stage must weigh the pros and cons. The main benefit is trust. It reduces the risk of penalties. Open data creates a fixed record of truth. This shows legal supply chains. It also spots illegal tricks. This transparency protects exporters. It shields them from geopolitical tensions. However, there are downsides. Keeping high-quality data is hard. Organizations must spend money on tech. They need to support APIs and new formats. Users also face a challenge. They must understand complex legal rules. This ensures they use data correctly. Despite these issues, open data builds a fair economy. It makes information a public good. Integrity is verified, not just claimed.
