A proposed bill in Wisconsin seeks to restore the ability for public records requesters to recover attorney’s fees when lawsuits prompt government compliance. This legislation directly challenges a 2022 Supreme Court ruling that denied fee recovery if records were voluntarily released after litigation began but before a judicial order was issued. By shifting the standard to allow fee awards when a lawsuit is a substantial factor in the release, the measure aims to reinforce the accountability mechanisms inherent in transparency laws. The initiative addresses concerns that the previous court decision weakened the spirit of the state’s public records law, potentially emboldening government bodies to delay disclosures until the last possible moment. Transparency advocates and legal experts argue that restoring this precedent is essential for ensuring that taxpayer-funded entities respond efficiently and in a timely manner. The bill has garnered bipartisan support, reflecting a shared concern that the prior judicial interpretation undermined the intended function of open government statutes. This development is highly relevant to the open data community as it highlights the critical role of legal incentives in maintaining data accessibility. Without the threat of financial penalties for delayed compliance, agencies may lack sufficient motivation to prioritize prompt data release, effectively hindering public access to information. Strengthening these legal remedies ensures that open data frameworks remain robust and enforceable, preventing bureaucratic inertia from obstructing the public’s right to know.
