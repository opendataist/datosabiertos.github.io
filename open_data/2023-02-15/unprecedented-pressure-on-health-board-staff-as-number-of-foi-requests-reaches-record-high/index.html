‘Unprecedented pressure’ on health board staff as number of FOI requests reaches record high

NHS Shetland faces an unprecedented surge in Freedom of Information requests, largely driven by political parties rather than typical public interest. This disproportionate burden places enormous strain on staff, forcing them to divert time from critical patient care to meet strict legal response deadlines. The health board lacks the necessary resources to handle this volume effectively, highlighting a systemic mismatch between legislative intent and operational capacity. This situation underscores the critical challenges in open data governance when transparency mechanisms are exploited for partisan purposes. It reveals how open access laws, while designed for public accountability, can overwhelm small public organizations if not properly managed. Consequently, the balance between openness and operational sustainability is threatened, affecting service quality. For open data enthusiasts, this case illustrates the real-world costs of unrestricted information access without adequate infrastructure support. It suggests that future open data frameworks must consider resource implications and prevent misuse. Understanding these tensions is vital for designing systems that uphold transparency while preserving the core functions of public institutions under pressure.
