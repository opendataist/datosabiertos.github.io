Ramadass calls for affirmative action to ensure more govt. school students clear NEET

Data obtained through Right to Information requests reveals a stark disparity in medical college admissions, where students from underprivileged government schools are grossly underrepresented compared to their private school peers. This systemic exclusion highlights how educational inequalities rooted in socioeconomic factors prevent deserving candidates from accessing higher education, despite their potential. The author argues that merit-based exams like NEET currently disadvantage these students due to broader cognitive and resource gaps created by caste, wealth, and parental education. To achieve genuine equity, the government must implement affirmative action, such as reserving seats specifically for those who completed their schooling in government institutions. Such preferential treatment is necessary to correct historical imbalances and ensure equal opportunities for all segments of society. This issue is highly relevant to open_data because it demonstrates how transparent access to government records can expose critical social inequities. By making admission statistics public, citizens and policymakers can identify systemic failures and advocate for targeted reforms. Ultimately, this case underscores the power of data transparency in driving policy changes that promote social justice and inclusive educational opportunities.
