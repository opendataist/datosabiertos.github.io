The article presents a political critique of Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection, arguing that its understaffing and politicization undermine its constitutional role. By portraying the institution as inactive and instrumentalized for political bargaining, the text underscores the critical importance of robust, independent data protection mechanisms. This relevance to open data is paramount, as effective oversight bodies are essential for ensuring transparency, protecting citizens’ privacy, and maintaining public trust in information governance. Without fully functioning autonomous organs, the integrity of open data ecosystems remains vulnerable to political manipulation and opacity, thereby compromising democratic accountability and the free flow of public information. Additionally, the narrative highlights the government’s defense against allegations of intentional institutional paralysis, while also denying rumors concerning the President’s health. This reflects a broader pattern of managing public perception through controlled information channels. For the open data community, such efforts to control narratives and dismiss external scrutiny underscore the tension between state transparency objectives and political interests. The incident serves as a cautionary reminder that open data initiatives require not only legal frameworks but also genuine political commitment to impartiality and institutional stability.
