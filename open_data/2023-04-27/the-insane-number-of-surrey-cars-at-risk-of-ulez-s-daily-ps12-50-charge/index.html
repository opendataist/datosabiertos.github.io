The insane number of Surrey cars at risk of ULEZ's daily £12.50 charge

The expansion of London’s Ultra Low Emission Zone into Surrey threatens to impact hundreds of thousands of vehicles currently on the road. A significant portion of these cars fails to meet the new emission standards, imposing substantial daily charges on residents and creating financial uncertainty for commuters who regularly travel between the county and the capital. The data highlights a critical gap between vehicle registration ages and compliance requirements, revealing that many petrol and diesel vehicles registered prior to specific cutoff dates are now non-compliant. This disparity affects both licensed and off-road cars, underscoring the scale of the transition required for local drivers and the potential economic burden on households that must either upgrade their vehicles or pay ongoing fees. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how public sector transparency drives civic engagement and legal accountability. By leveraging Freedom of Information requests to access DVLA records, citizens and local councils can scrutinize government policies, quantify impacts, and build evidence-based arguments for necessary adjustments or exemptions, ensuring that digital public information serves democratic oversight.
