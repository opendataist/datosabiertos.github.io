The National Agricultural Survey reveals the significant scale of Panama’s agricultural production, highlighting staple grains and permanent crops as primary economic drivers. By detailing vast cultivation areas and high yields for rice, maize, and sugarcane, the data underscores the sector’s reliance on these specific commodities for national food security and export revenue. Permanent crops, particularly sugarcane, bananas, and oil palm, dominate the landscape, accounting for the vast majority of production volume. While coffee occupies the largest planted area, its relatively lower output compared to other crops suggests distinct agronomic or economic priorities. This disparity offers valuable insights into land use efficiency and the varying productivity rates across different agricultural profiles. This information is crucial for open data initiatives as it provides granular, official statistics necessary for evidence-based policy-making and resource allocation. Transparent access to such comprehensive agricultural metrics enables researchers, investors, and citizens to monitor food supply chains, assess economic trends, and demand greater accountability in public administration and environmental stewardship.
