Catorce menores condenador por delitos sexuales en 2022 en CLM

The article highlights a significant national increase in juvenile sexual offenses, which stands in sharp contrast to regional declines in Castilla-La Mancha. This discrepancy underscores how aggregated national statistics can mask local trends, emphasizing the need for granular, region-specific data transparency. For open-data advocates, this illustrates the critical importance of accessible, localized datasets to understand nuanced societal shifts, rather than relying solely on broad summaries. Moreover, the data reveal stark disparities in conviction rates by nationality and gender among juveniles. Foreign minors have significantly higher per capita conviction rates than their Spanish counterparts, whereas adult offenses display a different demographic profile. These insights show how open data can inform evidence-based policy discussions on social integration and legal equity, moving beyond simplistic metrics to address the underlying structural causes of crime. Ultimately, the report’s value lies in its detailed breakdown of offense types and geographical distribution, providing a comprehensive view of judicial outcomes. By making such granular information publicly available, stakeholders can better assess the effectiveness of justice systems and identify areas requiring intervention. This transparency fosters accountability and informed debate—core principles of the open-data movement—ensuring that statistical evidence accurately reflects complex social realities and supports targeted public improvements.
