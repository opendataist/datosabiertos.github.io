Haiti’s right to information must be protected, say 90 Haitian journalists and RSF

The article highlights the severe deterioration of press freedom in Haiti, driven by extreme political instability and pervasive gang violence following the assassination of President Jovenel Moïse. Journalists face constant threats, including kidnapping, murder, and physical attacks, often with impunity due to the collapse of the rule of law. Consequently, many media professionals are forced to flee, censor themselves, or abandon their work entirely to survive, leading to a critical decline in the diversity and reliability of information available to the public. This crisis creates a significant barrier to transparency and accountability, which are foundational principles of open data and civic engagement. When independent journalism collapses, the flow of verified, accessible information about government actions and security realities is severed. The resulting information vacuum hinders public oversight and prevents citizens from making informed decisions, undermining the democratic processes that the new Transitional Presidential Council aims to restore. The piece is relevant to open data because it underscores that data alone is insufficient without the journalistic infrastructure to interpret and disseminate it. It calls for international support to protect the media ecosystem, arguing that safe, independent journalism is essential for revealing the truth about the crisis. For open data initiatives to be effective, they must be coupled with efforts to secure the freedom of information, ensuring that data can be effectively communicated to society without fear of retribution.
