Here's how many young people in Scotland have been prescribed puberty blockers

Health authorities in Scotland have paused prescribing puberty blockers to minors, aligning with recent recommendations from England’s Cass Review and guidance from the Chief Medical Officer. This strategic shift prioritizes patient safety and evidence-based practice, reflecting a broader trend across the UK to reassess current clinical pathways. The decision underscores the importance of rigorous scientific evaluation in shaping sensitive medical policies for vulnerable youth populations. Freedom of Information requests reveal that only a small fraction of referred young people actually received these medications, with prescriptions dropping significantly in recent years. This data highlights the limited scale of medical intervention within the broader scope of gender care services. The findings support the view that psychological support remains the primary focus, while medical treatments are subject to stricter scrutiny and lower utilization rates than previously assumed. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how transparency through public records can inform public health decisions. By making referral and prescription statistics accessible, stakeholders can better understand service impacts and advocate for evidence-based policy changes. Such openness fosters trust and ensures that healthcare strategies evolve based on concrete, publicly available evidence rather than speculation or limited internal reviews.
