NEW: NYT Sues Over AI Scraping; Meta Cuts AI Deals With Fox, WashEx

The article argues that current AI tools are fundamentally limited to natural-language search, offering no genuine intelligence beyond their source material. This distinction drives a critical legal and ethical conflict: while media outlets view their content as protected property, AI platforms often scrape this data without licenses to generate competing outputs. The resulting lawsuits, such as those filed by the New York Times and Dow Jones against Perplexity, highlight the tension between unlicensed data scraping and established copyright laws, emphasizing that commercial reuse requires permission and compensation. Relevance to open data lies in the urgent need to redefine data provenance and access rights in the age of generative AI. The case illustrates how opaque data aggregation practices can undermine the value of authoritative information sources. Unlike legitimate open data initiatives that promote transparency and attribution, current AI models often strip away context and ownership, creating a "black box" that misattributes information or hallucinates facts. This undermines trust in digital information ecosystems and challenges the principles of fair use and proper attribution that underpin robust open data practices. Conversely, Meta’s decision to license content from a broad spectrum of publishers demonstrates a viable alternative. By paying for access, Meta acknowledges the value of curated, verified data and attempts to mitigate issues like hallucinations through official partnerships. This shift suggests a future where AI development relies on structured, licensed datasets rather than uncontrolled scraping. For the open data community, this underscores the importance of sustainable data governance models that respect intellectual property while enabling technological innovation.
