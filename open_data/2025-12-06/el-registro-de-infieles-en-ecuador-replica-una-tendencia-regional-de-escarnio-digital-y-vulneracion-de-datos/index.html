El “registro de infieles” en Ecuador replica una tendencia regional de escarnio digital y vulneración de datos

El “registro de infieles” en Ecuador replica una tendencia regional de escarnio digital y vulneración de datos

The Superintendency of Personal Data Protection in Ecuador has issued a public warning against the viral spread of the "National Registry of Unfaithful," an anonymous and collaborative online list that exposes individuals’ private information without their consent. This initiative violates fundamental data protection laws, as it allows users to add unverified personal details—such as names, photos, and workplaces—through easily accessible public links. The authority emphasizes that the viral or recreational nature of the content does not exempt creators and participants from legal responsibility, highlighting the severe risks to individual privacy and security. The implications extend beyond social embarrassment, as these platforms often serve as vectors for cybercriminal activities. The unsecured nature of these databases, frequently hosted on sites with compromised security certificates, exposes users to phishing, extortion, and identity theft. The phenomenon reflects a growing global trend in which platforms are misused for non-consensual data exposure. Similar cases have emerged in neighboring countries such as Peru, Chile, and Mexico, demonstrating a regional pattern of digital shaming that leverages anonymity to bypass ethical and legal boundaries regarding personal information. This article is highly relevant to open data discussions because it illustrates the critical distinction between transparent public information and the misuse of open collaborative tools for illegal data processing. It underscores the necessity of robust data governance and user education, showing how the openness of platforms like Google Docs can be exploited to violate privacy rights. The incident serves as a cautionary example for the open data community, emphasizing that accessibility must always be balanced with strict adherence to legal frameworks protecting individual consent and personal security.
