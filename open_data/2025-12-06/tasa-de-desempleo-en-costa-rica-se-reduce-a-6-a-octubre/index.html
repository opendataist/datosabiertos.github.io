Tasa de desempleo en Costa Rica se reduce a 6 % a octubre

Tasa de desempleo en Costa Rica se reduce a 6 % a octubre

The latest labor data for Costa Rica highlights a notable improvement in employment quality, specifically through significant reductions in both unemployment and underemployment. These positive trends indicate a strengthening of the labor market, where fewer individuals are without work or in inadequate working conditions. This shift suggests growing stability and potential for better economic integration for workers, reflecting a broader improvement in labor dynamics despite the overall workforce size remaining relatively constant. A crucial development is the marked decrease in labor informality, particularly among women. This reduction implies that more jobs are becoming formalized, offering greater legal protection and social security. As the proportion of workers in the informal sector shrinks, the economy moves toward greater transparency and fairness. This trend is especially relevant for gender equity, as the data shows a disproportionately larger decline in informal employment for women, helping to narrow long-standing gaps in labor rights and benefits between genders. This article is highly relevant to open data because it exemplifies how high-quality, publicly accessible statistical indicators can drive informed policy and public discourse. By making granular data on employment, gender disparities, and formalization available, institutions like the INEC enable researchers, journalists, and citizens to hold governments accountable and monitor social progress. Open access to such detailed metrics is essential for verifying claims of economic improvement and ensuring that development goals are being met transparently.
