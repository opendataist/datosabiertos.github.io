The New York Times and Chicago Tribune sue Perplexity over alleged copyright infringement

The New York Times and Chicago Tribune sue Perplexity over alleged copyright infringement

Major newspapers are suing Perplexity for unauthorized use of their copyrighted material to train AI models and generate verbatim outputs. These legal actions highlight the critical tension between AI developers and content creators, emphasizing the urgent need for transparent data sourcing and licensing in open data ecosystems. The lawsuits underscore the ethical and legal risks of scraping protected content without permission. By highlighting issues like hallucinations and unauthorized replication, these cases stress that open data initiatives must respect intellectual property rights to remain sustainable and legally compliant. This ongoing conflict demonstrates the evolving landscape of data usage. While some companies secure licensing deals, others face litigation, showing that sustainable AI development requires clear agreements. This relevance to open data lies in defining how publicly accessible information can be used ethically without infringing on creator rights.
