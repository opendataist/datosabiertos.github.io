Urgent Steps Needed to Improve Implementation of Right to Information in India | Countercurrents

Urgent Steps Needed to Improve Implementation of Right to Information in India | Countercurrents

The article highlights a critical decline in the implementation of India’s Right to Information (RTI) legislation, primarily due to severe delays and massive backlogs in information commissions. This systemic failure undermines the law’s core purpose of ensuring government accountability, as citizens face wait times lasting years or even decades for appeal decisions. The erosion of timely access to information threatens the foundational democratic principle that an open society requires public awareness of state functions. This decline is particularly significant given that India’s RTI framework was pioneered by grassroots movements empowering the poor to combat corruption and secure entitlements. Unlike many other nations, the Indian movement uniquely linked transparency with social justice, utilizing tools like public hearings to expose malpractice in rural and urban development schemes. These historical successes demonstrate how robust information access serves as a vital mechanism for marginalized communities to claim their rights and hold local authorities accountable. The situation is relevant to open data because it illustrates the gap between legislative intent and practical accessibility. Even with laws promoting transparency, open data principles fail if the mechanisms for retrieval are inefficient or inaccessible. The article argues that restoring the RTI’s effectiveness is essential for democratic health, suggesting that efficient data release processes are not merely administrative tasks but crucial civil rights necessary for active citizenship and systemic integrity.
