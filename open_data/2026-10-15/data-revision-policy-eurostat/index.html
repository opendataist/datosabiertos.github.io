Data revision policy - Eurostat

Data revision policy - Eurostat

Data revision is a fundamental component of producing high-quality statistics, ensuring that published information accurately reflects the latest and most reliable sources. Eurostat’s comprehensive revision policy establishes this process as a standard, transparent, and harmonized practice across all statistical domains. By adhering to the European Statistics Code of Practice, the agency guarantees that revisions are not only routine but also clearly documented, allowing users to understand the reasons behind data updates and their scheduling. The framework distinguishes between three types of revisions: routine, major, and unscheduled. Routine updates occur regularly as new source data arrives or seasonal adjustments are applied, while major revisions address significant structural changes such as new classifications, base year updates, or improved methodologies. Unplanned corrections handle detected errors, ensuring that any inaccuracies are swiftly rectified. This multi-tiered approach balances the need for timely data with the necessity of maintaining longitudinal consistency and methodological integrity over time. This policy is highly relevant to open data because it prioritizes transparency and accessibility in data management. By mandating clear metadata and public documentation of revision practices, Eurostat enables users to trace data lineage and assess quality with confidence. This openness fosters trust in statistical outputs, ensuring that developers and researchers relying on open datasets can make informed decisions based on a clear understanding of how data evolves and improves.
