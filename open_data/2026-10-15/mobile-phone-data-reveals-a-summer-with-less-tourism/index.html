Mobile phone data reveals a summer with less tourism

Mobile phone data reveals a summer with less tourism

The article uses experimental statistics from the National Statistics Institute (INE), based on mobile phone location data, to reveal a more holistic perspective on tourism in Ávila during August. Unlike traditional hotel occupancy indices, this approach captures total visitor movement, including those staying in second homes, alternative accommodations, and individuals visiting family. This methodology highlights the relevance of open data by democratizing tourism information, enabling more comprehensive and accurate analyses that go beyond formal infrastructure and offer a realistic view of the economic and social impact of local tourism. The main conclusion points to a notable decline in visitor arrivals during the summer compared to previous years, a drop that coincides temporally with severe forest fires in the region. Although the figures indicate that Ávila remains an active destination, the observed retreat suggests that external factors, such as natural disasters, can significantly influence tourist mobility. This underscores the importance of monitoring real-time indicators to understand the sector’s resilience and the factors affecting travel decisions. Most tourists come from Madrid, reinforcing the dynamics of regional connectivity and the use of second homes. The relevance for the open data community lies in demonstrating how large-scale mobility data can complement and correct official statistics, providing valuable insights into travel patterns not traditionally recorded. This case exemplifies the need to integrate multiple open data sources to inform tourism policies and enable agile responses to crises, improving transparency and public resource management in tourism-dependent destinations.
