Prices surge by 5.5% in September in Castilla y León, which, along with Galicia, records the largest increase

Prices surge by 5.5% in September in Castilla y León, which, along with Galicia, records the largest increase

The article examines the recent inflationary trends in Spain and the Castilla-La Mancha region, highlighting that prices have posted consecutive increases, primarily driven by rising fuel costs and package tour prices. This dynamic is attributed to the persistent energy shock stemming from the war in the Middle East, although government protection measures have mitigated approximately half of this impact on final prices. The most significant variations are observed in housing, transport, and food, while other sectors show more contained developments. The relevance of this document for the open data field lies in its practical demonstration of how macroeconomic statistical data inform and validate public policies in real time. By breaking down the Consumer Price Index (CPI) by autonomous communities, expenditure categories, and underlying indices, it underscores the need for transparent access to these time series. This enables citizens, researchers, and the media to verify the effectiveness of crisis-response measures, such as fuel tax discounts, thereby fostering evidence-based accountability. Furthermore, the detailed publication of year-on-year and monthly rates by region illustrates the importance of territorial disaggregation in open data analysis. It allows for the identification of local disparities and the assessment of the differentiated impact of energy crises across various parts of the country. This level of detail is crucial for democratizing economic information, facilitating a better understanding by civil society of the dynamics affecting their cost of living, and enabling informed participation in debates on economic stability and distributive justice.
