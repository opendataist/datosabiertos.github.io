NGO Protectora representative stated on Aconcagua Radio how many fewer users will access the Cold Zone subsidy

NGO Protectora representative stated on Aconcagua Radio how many fewer users will access the Cold Zone subsidy

The article highlights modifications to the energy subsidy that, while maintaining discounts in specific zones, reduce their real impact by being applied directly to the price of gas rather than to the tariff schedule. This technical alteration results in lower savings for users and higher bills, requiring strict income and asset thresholds to avoid exclusion from the benefit. Criticism is directed at the lack of technical criteria in defining the geographic scope of the subsidy, which prioritizes administrative boundaries over actual climatic data, creating inequality between neighboring localities. Additionally, significant bureaucratic barriers are pointed out, such as the reliance on digital wallets and the complexity involved in accessing payments, which hinder the inclusion of older adults and vulnerable sectors, revealing shortcomings in the transparency and accessibility of social programs. This news is relevant to open data research, as it illustrates how opacity in allocation criteria and access requirements can unjustly exclude portions of the population. The visibility of these details, often scattered or inaccessible, underscores the need for transparency mechanisms that enable auditing of equity in the distribution of public resources, ensuring that benefits effectively reach those who need them most.
