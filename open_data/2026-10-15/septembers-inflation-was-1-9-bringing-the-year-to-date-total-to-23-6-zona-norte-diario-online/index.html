September’s inflation was 1.9%, bringing the year-to-date total to 23.6% - Zona Norte Diario Online

September’s inflation was 1.9%, bringing the year-to-date total to 23.6% - Zona Norte Diario Online

INDEC reported an acceleration in inflation in September, highlighting increases in key services such as housing and education. This scenario reflects persistent pressure on the cost of living and regional disparities in the rising cost of goods, underscoring the need for transparent and timely official data. Access to this statistical information is fundamental to accountability and government transparency, which are essential pillars of the open data movement. The discrepancy between the political objective of slowing inflation and the year-to-date reality demonstrates how the availability of public data allows official narratives to be contrasted with citizens’ everyday experiences. Free access to economic indicators fosters social auditing and independent research, empowering civil society to analyze structural trends beyond immediate political statements. The relevance of this article lies in its practical demonstration of why open data is a critical public good. By ensuring the complete and understandable dissemination of statistical information, institutional trust is strengthened and informed debate on economic policies is enabled. Transparency in macroeconomic indicators not only informs the present but also builds an evidence base necessary for future decision-making and effective democratic oversight.
