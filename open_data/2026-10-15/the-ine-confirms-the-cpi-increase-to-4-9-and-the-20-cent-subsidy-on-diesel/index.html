The INE confirms the CPI increase to 4.9% and the 20-cent subsidy on diesel

The INE confirms the CPI increase to 4.9% and the 20-cent subsidy on diesel

Inflation in Spain has accelerated notably, driven primarily by the impact of the war in the Middle East on energy prices and tourism. This increase justifies the continuation of government support measures, such as fuel discounts, which have significantly mitigated the inflationary pressure stemming from the conflict. However, the persistence of these external factors maintains uncertainty regarding the future evolution of basic costs for households and businesses. The price structure reveals that transport and recreational activities are the most affected sectors, reflecting global instability. Although there are price increases in certain food products, accumulated inflation in this area shows slight signs of mitigation, while underlying inflation remains below headline inflation, indicating that energy volatility is the main driver of the rise. This dynamic compels policymakers to maintain strict control over energy tariffs to protect purchasing power without altering other taxes. This article is relevant to the open data sector because it illustrates how public statistics, such as those published by the National Statistics Institute (INE), are fundamental for accountability and evidence-based decision-making. Transparency in macroeconomic data enables civil society and analysts to verify the effectiveness of public policies in the face of geopolitical crises, ensuring that the state’s responses align with observable reality and safeguard citizens’ economic rights.
