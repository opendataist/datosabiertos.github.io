El paro baja en 10.900 personas en el tercer trimestre en Baleares y se crean 31.300 empleos

The Balearic Islands have experienced a significant improvement in their labor market, marked by two consecutive quarters of job creation and a substantial increase in overall employment. This positive trend is particularly notable because it occurred despite a slight year-on-year rise in unemployment, driven by stronger growth in the active population. The data reveal that the private sector was the primary engine of this expansion, generating the vast majority of new jobs, while the public sector contributed minimally. Employment quality has also improved, characterized by a sharp rise in permanent contracts and full-time positions compared to temporary and part-time jobs. Unemployment rates dropped notably across most sectors, with the service industry leading the recovery. Furthermore, gender gaps narrowed significantly, as female unemployment decreased at a faster rate than male unemployment. This shift suggests a stabilization in the labor market structure, moving away from precarious working conditions toward more stable professional engagements. This data is crucial for open data initiatives, as it highlights the importance of accessing granular, disaggregated statistics to understand local economic resilience. Open datasets enable researchers and policymakers to track these nuanced trends, such as the contrast between quarterly growth and annual stagnation, and to benchmark regional performance against national averages. By making such detailed employment indicators accessible, stakeholders can better evaluate the effectiveness of labor policies and identify structural shifts in specific industries or demographic groups.
