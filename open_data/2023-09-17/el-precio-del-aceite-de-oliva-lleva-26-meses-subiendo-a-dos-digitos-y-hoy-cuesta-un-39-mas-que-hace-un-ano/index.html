El precio del aceite de oliva lleva 26 meses subiendo a dos dígitos y hoy cuesta un 39% más que hace un año

The sharp surge in olive oil prices, driven primarily by persistent drought conditions, highlights a critical vulnerability in agricultural supply chains where climate change directly disrupts market stability. This scenario underscores the urgent need for transparent, real-time data sharing regarding crop yields and weather patterns, allowing stakeholders to anticipate shortages and mitigate economic shocks more effectively. As consumer spending hits historic highs, the lack of accessible information about production bottlenecks exacerbates market volatility and harms household budgets. Open data initiatives can bridge this gap by providing standardized metrics on production and distribution, fostering accountability and enabling better-informed decisions for both policymakers and everyday shoppers navigating essential food costs. Ultimately, this case illustrates why open data is vital for building resilient food systems. By making agricultural and economic indicators publicly available, society can improve resource allocation, support sustainable farming practices, and ensure that critical dietary staples remain affordable and accessible despite environmental challenges.
