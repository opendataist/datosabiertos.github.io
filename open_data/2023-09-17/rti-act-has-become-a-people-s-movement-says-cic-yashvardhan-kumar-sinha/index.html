RTI Act has become a people's movement, says CIC Yashvardhan Kumar Sinha

The Right to Information Act has evolved into a significant grassroots movement, establishing India as a global leader in transparency due to the unprecedented volume of applications and responses it generates. This widespread participation demonstrates that the law effectively empowers citizens to seek accountability, surpassing the capabilities of other nations with large populations but lacking similar legislative frameworks. While the implementation process is still refining its approach to handle inherent shortcomings, the active engagement of RTI activists fosters crucial dialogue between civil society and the government. This interaction is viewed as a positive force for systemic improvement, provided it avoids misuse or ulterior motives, highlighting the act’s role in strengthening democratic participation and civic responsibility. This development is vital to the open data movement because it institutionalizes the public’s right to access government information, creating a culture of mandatory disclosure. By treating frequent applicants as legitimate citizens rather than nuisance filers, the framework supports a robust ecosystem where data availability drives governance efficiency and public trust, reinforcing the fundamental principle that transparency is a cornerstone of modern democratic oversight.
