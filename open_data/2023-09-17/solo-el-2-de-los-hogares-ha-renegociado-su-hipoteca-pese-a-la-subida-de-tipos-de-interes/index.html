Solo el 2% de los hogares ha renegociado su hipoteca pese a la subida de tipos de interés

The article highlights a significant stagnation in mortgage renegotiations among Spanish households, driven by rising interest rates and economic uncertainty. Despite the substantial number of families holding variable-rate mortgages, the proportion actively seeking to modify their loan conditions remains critically low. This data suggests that consumers are either unable or unwilling to navigate the complexities of renegotiation, indicating a disconnect between policy intent and market behavior during a period of financial tightening. The relevance to open data lies in the discrepancy between expected and actual engagement with government initiatives. Although the administration projected widespread participation in a code of good practices designed to help vulnerable families, actual adherence was minimal. This gap underscores the importance of transparent, accessible data in evaluating the real-world impact of financial policies. Without precise, publicly available metrics, it becomes difficult to assess whether legislative measures effectively protect citizens or merely exist on paper, highlighting the need for rigorous data monitoring to ensure accountability. Ultimately, the decline in novations and substitutions signals a hesitant housing market that contrasts sharply with previous crisis-driven periods. The fact that transfers to other banks increased slightly, while direct renegotiations dropped, reveals a fragmented response to economic pressure. For open data advocates, this case demonstrates how statistical transparency can expose the limitations of governmental interventions and inform future decisions. It reinforces the necessity of open datasets to track economic indicators accurately, allowing for better analysis of how monetary policies affect everyday financial stability.
