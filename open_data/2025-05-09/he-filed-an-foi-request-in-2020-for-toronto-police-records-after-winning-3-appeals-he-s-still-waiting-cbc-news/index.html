He filed an FOI request in 2020 for Toronto police records. After winning 3 appeals, he's still waiting | CBC News

This article illustrates the critical weaknesses in Ontario’s freedom of information legislation, specifically regarding the lack of enforcement mechanisms for regulatory orders. When public institutions like the Toronto Police Service delay or ignore disclosure requirements, requesters are forced into a burdensome cycle of repeated appeals with little guaranteed recourse. This highlights a systemic failure where the burden of enforcement falls on individuals rather than the oversight body, undermining the rule of law and institutional accountability. The relevance to open data lies in the precedent set for how law enforcement handles sensitive technological records, such as those concerning facial recognition. Effective open data initiatives require transparent, accessible, and timely release of information to ensure public trust and accurate research. Without strict compliance and meaningful penalties for non-compliance, public institutions can indefinitely withhold data, rendering open data principles ineffective in practice and allowing powerful entities to operate with impunity. Ultimately, this case underscores the urgent need for legislative modernization to include enforceable mechanisms similar to those in other jurisdictions. Strengthening these laws is essential to prevent agencies from exploiting procedural delays or ignoring oversight orders. For the open data community, this serves as a cautionary tale: without robust legal backing, the promise of transparency remains vulnerable to administrative obstruction, necessitating stronger advocacy for legislative reform.
