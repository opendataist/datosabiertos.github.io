The article highlights significant operational inefficiencies in municipal administrative services, particularly concerning resident registration documentation. Long queues and excessive wait times have emerged following the elimination of the previous appointment system, demonstrating how removing structured scheduling mechanisms negatively impacts citizens’ access to essential public services. This situation underscores the critical role of open data in evaluating and improving public administration. Transparent information about appointment availability and service performance metrics enables the identification of bottlenecks and holds authorities accountable. Without accessible data on processing times and capacity, citizens are unable to advocate for effective solutions, resulting in persistent inefficiencies in basic civic interactions. Ultimately, the text illustrates that open data is essential for ensuring transparency and efficiency in government operations. By making service delays and resource allocation visible, stakeholders can demand better planning. This case serves as a reminder that accessible information is not merely about freedom, but also about citizens’ practical ability to navigate and improve public service delivery.
