Labour’s cuts to disability benefits hit 700,000 families – time to kick out Starmer government with a general strike - Workers Revolutionary Party

Labour’s cuts to disability benefits hit 700,000 families – time to kick out Starmer government with a general strike - Workers Revolutionary Party

The article reveals that the UK Labour government’s internal assessment confirms planned disability benefit cuts will deepen poverty for hundreds of thousands of families, contradicting Chancellor Rachel Reeves’ assertions that these measures would encourage employment. This disclosure highlights a significant discrepancy between official projections and the actual impact, suggesting the government is intentionally understating the severity of the financial hardship imposed on disabled individuals and their caregivers. This situation is particularly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of transparency in government decision-making. The fact that these damaging internal assessments remained hidden until forced into the public domain via Freedom of Information requests illustrates how restricted access to official information can obscure the true costs of policy changes. Without robust mechanisms for data disclosure, citizens are left unaware of the specific vulnerabilities affected by austerity measures, preventing informed public scrutiny and accountability. Furthermore, the article argues that this lack of transparency facilitates a broader narrative where vulnerable groups are forced to bear the cost of economic crises. By keeping such detrimental impacts hidden until late in the process, authorities can pursue aggressive spending reductions while minimizing immediate political backlash, as seen in recent local election results. Consequently, the article advocates for greater openness as a tool for the working class to understand and resist policies that prioritize capitalist stability over social welfare.
