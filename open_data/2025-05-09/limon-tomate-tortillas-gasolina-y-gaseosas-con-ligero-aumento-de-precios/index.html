Limón, tomate, tortillas, gasolina y gaseosas con “ligero” aumento de precios

Limón, tomate, tortillas, gasolina y gaseosas con “ligero” aumento de precios

The April 2025 Consumer Price Index reveals a slight uptick in inflation, although the monthly rate remains significantly lower than historical peaks and falls below the monetary board’s target range. This deceleration indicates that while certain essential goods, such as gasoline and tomatoes, experienced price increases, the overall economic pressure on consumers is moderating compared to previous years. The data highlights specific sectoral shifts, with food and transportation seeing modest rises, while items like chicken and onions saw slight decreases. These granular price movements, reflected in the cost of the basic food basket, demonstrate the nuanced dynamics of the local economy. Persistent low inflation suggests that external global factors and internal supply conditions are currently stabilizing price levels, offering a reprieve from the higher inflation seen in 2024. This report is crucial for open data initiatives, as it exemplifies the importance of transparent, high-frequency statistical tracking. By making detailed breakdowns of the Consumer Price Index publicly available, institutions empower researchers and citizens to analyze economic trends accurately. Such accessibility fosters accountability and allows for better-informed public discourse on monetary policy and its tangible effects on household budgets and market stability.
