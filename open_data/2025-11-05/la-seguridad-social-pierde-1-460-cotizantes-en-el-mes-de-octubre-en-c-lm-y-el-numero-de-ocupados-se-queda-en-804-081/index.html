La Seguridad Social pierde 1.460 cotizantes en el mes de octubre en C-LM y el número de ocupados se queda en 804.081

The article highlights a significant strengthening of the labor market, marked by historical peaks in social security affiliations and a notable reduction in temporary contracts. This structural shift, driven largely by labor reforms, indicates a transition toward more stable employment, particularly benefiting young workers and sectors with high value-added productivity. The data reveals that the labor market is not only growing in size but also improving in quality, moving away from precarious conditions toward sustained professional integration. A key narrative thread is the substantial increase in female employment, which outpaced male growth and brought the gender gap closer to parity. This trend, alongside strong gains in education and specialized technical services, suggests that recent policy interventions are effectively promoting inclusivity and economic diversification. The rise in jobs among older workers further demonstrates the reform’s broad impact across different demographic groups, enhancing overall labor force participation and stability. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it provides transparent, high-quality metrics for analyzing labor market dynamics. Access to such granular information allows researchers and policymakers to evaluate the real-world impact of legislative changes on employment structure, gender equity, and sectoral productivity. By making these trends visible, open data fosters evidence-based decision-making and supports the creation of more equitable and resilient economic policies.
