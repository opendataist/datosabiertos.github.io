CCOO valora el crecimiento del empleo "de calidad" pero insiste en superar las brechas en el mismo

The apparent rise in unemployment in Castilla-La Mancha is largely driven by the end of the seasonal grape harvest, rather than genuine economic decline. By contextualizing these figures, it becomes clear that the drop in agricultural registrations is offset by significant job creation in general regimes, particularly in education, public administration, and transport. This nuanced view prevents demagogic interpretations and highlights that underlying sectors are actually expanding despite seasonal fluctuations. A key development is the historic growth in stable, indefinite employment, a trend attributed to recent labor reforms. These changes have successfully improved job quality by reducing precariousness, leading to record numbers of permanent full-time contracts. This structural shift indicates that the labor market is maturing, prioritizing security and stability for workers, which stands in contrast to previous periods marked by higher temporary employment rates. This article is relevant to open_data because it demonstrates the necessity of high-quality, sector-disaggregated data to accurately assess labor market health. Raw aggregated statistics can be misleading without context; therefore, open datasets must provide granular details on contract types, gender breaks, and seasonal adjustments. Such transparency allows stakeholders to distinguish between temporary seasonal effects and genuine structural improvements, ensuring that policy decisions and public discourse are based on precise, actionable insights rather than superficial trends.
