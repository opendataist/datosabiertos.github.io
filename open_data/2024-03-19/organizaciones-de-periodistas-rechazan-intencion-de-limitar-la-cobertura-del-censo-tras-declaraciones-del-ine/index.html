Organizaciones de periodistas rechazan “intención de limitar la cobertura del Censo” tras declaraciones del INE

The National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INE) restricted media access during the census, citing statistical secrecy to prevent the recording of interviews with citizens or officials. However, the National Association of Journalists argued that current laws only prohibit revealing individual data, not reporting on the process itself. This conflict highlights the tension between institutional control and the public’s right to transparent information, a core concern in open data governance. Journalists demanded the right to cover logistics, budget usage, and citizen perception without undue interference. They emphasized that transparency in public administration is essential for accountability. The dispute underscores how legal frameworks must clearly distinguish between protecting individual privacy and ensuring the openness of governmental operations, preventing arbitrary restrictions on the flow of information. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the real-world challenges of implementing data transparency principles. It shows how unclear interpretations of privacy laws can hinder public scrutiny of large-scale administrative efforts. Ensuring that open data policies do not become tools for secrecy is crucial for maintaining trust in statistical institutions and promoting citizen engagement in national processes.
