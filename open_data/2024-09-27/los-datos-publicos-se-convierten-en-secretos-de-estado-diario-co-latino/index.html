Los datos públicos se convierten en secretos de Estado - Diario Co Latino

Los datos públicos se convierten en secretos de Estado - Diario Co Latino

The article argues that the current administration systematically withholds critical public data, creating a significant transparency deficit. By concealing health statistics, pandemic expenditures, and information regarding victims of violence, the government obstructs academic analysis and hinders the ability of professionals and families to seek accountability. This pattern of secrecy prioritizes political narrative over factual reporting, undermining public trust and limiting the evidence base necessary for effective policy-making and scientific inquiry. Furthermore, the handling of sensitive personal information reveals severe vulnerabilities in data protection and governance. The misuse of digital platforms for financial schemes and the exposure of millions of citizens' private data through leaks and hacking incidents demonstrate a disregard for digital rights. The state’s failure to secure information systems, coupled with the concealment of contractual and financial details, exposes the population to exploitation and highlights a lack of robust regulatory frameworks for managing public data responsibly. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates because it exemplifies how state opacity can erode democratic oversight and citizen rights. It underscores the urgent need for legal frameworks that enforce transparency, protect personal data, and guarantee access to truthful, complete public records. The situation serves as a critical warning about the consequences of withholding information, particularly when it involves public health, financial integrity, and human rights, reinforcing the principle that open data is essential for a functioning, accountable society.
