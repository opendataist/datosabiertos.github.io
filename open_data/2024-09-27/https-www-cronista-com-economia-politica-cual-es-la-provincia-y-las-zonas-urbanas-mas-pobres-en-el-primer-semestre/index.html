The INDEC report reveals a widespread social crisis in Argentina, where poverty and extreme poverty experienced sharp increases across all regions during the first half of 2024. This trend indicates that the economic deterioration is not isolated but systematically affects the majority of the population, with the situation in the country’s northeast being particularly critical. The geographic distribution shows profound inequality, with northern provinces such as Chaco and Formosa recording alarming rates that far exceed the national average. In contrast, larger urban areas like Buenos Aires maintain relatively lower levels, although they too have worsened. This territorial disparity highlights how the impacts of economic policies are unevenly distributed, exacerbating vulnerability in already precarious regions. This analysis is crucial for the open data community, as it demonstrates the value of official statistical indicators for monitoring social well-being in real time. By making these data accessible, researchers and citizens can audit the effectiveness of public policies, visualize structural inequalities, and demand transparency. Disseminating this information facilitates the development of visualization and analysis tools that empower accountability and informed decision-making regarding the national reality.
