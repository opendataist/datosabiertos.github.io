VPD documentation ‘ranges from very poor to fair’ says retired officer’s report - BC | Globalnews.ca

VPD documentation ‘ranges from very poor to fair’ says retired officer’s report - BC | Globalnews.ca

A retired Vancouver Police Department sergeant’s report reveals a critical disconnect between high-quality police service and poor documentation standards. The investigation highlights that inadequate reporting significantly hinders successful prosecutions, as incomplete files often fail to reach Crown counsel or lack the necessary evidence to secure charges. This systemic issue creates a "veneer of arrests" without substantive legal outcomes, undermining the justice system's ability to hold offenders accountable. The relevance to open data is profound, as this case illustrates how administrative opacity directly impacts public accountability and trust. When government institutions withhold internal assessments of their operational failures under privacy exemptions, it obscures the reality of their performance from public scrutiny. The redaction of the report prevents citizens from accessing vital information about how their public servants are performing, thereby limiting the community's ability to demand transparency and effective governance. Ultimately, the situation underscores the necessity of treating internal process documentation as a matter of public interest rather than purely privileged advice. By masking institutional inefficiencies, authorities risk eroding confidence in law enforcement capabilities. Open data initiatives must advocate for the disclosure of performance metrics and procedural critiques to ensure that public resources are effectively utilized and that the justice system operates with full transparency.
