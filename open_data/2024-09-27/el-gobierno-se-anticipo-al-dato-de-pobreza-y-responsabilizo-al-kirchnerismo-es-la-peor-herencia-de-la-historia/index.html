El gobierno se anticipó al dato de pobreza y responsabilizó al kirchnerismo: “Es la peor herencia de la historia"

The article highlights the Argentine government’s political strategy to preempt negative poverty statistics released by INDEC, attributing the crisis entirely to the previous administration. The central conclusion is that the current administration faces a disastrous economic inheritance, characterized by hyperinflation and widespread destitution, rather than being responsible for creating these conditions. By framing the situation as the worst in democratic history, officials aim to justify their harsh austerity measures and deflect blame for the current social hardship onto the Kirchnerist legacy. This narrative emphasizes that avoiding a total economic collapse required immediate, orthodox shocks to stabilize inflation, contrasting sharply with the populist spending of the past. The implication is that while the transition causes short-term pain, it is necessary to reconstruct the economy from a state of near-total ruin. Officials argue that previous governments destroyed the economic foundation, and rebuilding it is a slow process that requires accepting brutal realities rather than denying them or relying on temporary subsidies. This content is relevant to open data because it illustrates how statistical information, such as poverty and inflation rates, is often politicized before official publication. It demonstrates the tension between raw data and political narratives, where governments may attempt to pre-emptively interpret or contextualize upcoming figures to manage public perception. For open data advocates, this serves as a case study on the importance of data transparency and the need for independent verification to counteract narratives that seek to obscure the immediate impact of policy decisions on social indicators.
