Saltillo y Monterrey, ciudades con más trabajadores ganando más de 25 mil al mes

Monterrey and Saltillo lead Mexican metropolitan areas in high-wage employment, significantly outperforming major cities such as Mexico City and Guadalajara. This disparity highlights deep regional inequalities in labor markets, demonstrating how specific local dynamics drive wealth concentration and affect the distribution of economic opportunities across different states. The data reveals that while both cities boast high proportions of well-compensated workers, they simultaneously exhibit the lowest percentages of employees earning just a minimum wage. This dual characteristic underscores a polarized labor structure where high-income sectors thrive, yet the baseline for basic economic security remains precarious for a substantial portion of the workforce, reflecting broader structural challenges in industrial zones. This information is crucial for open data initiatives, as it showcases how public statistical agencies provide the transparency necessary to analyze urban competitiveness and investment trends, such as nearshoring. By making these detailed labor metrics accessible, open data enables stakeholders to evaluate the real social impact of economic policies and infrastructure developments, fostering informed public debate on regional development and equity.
