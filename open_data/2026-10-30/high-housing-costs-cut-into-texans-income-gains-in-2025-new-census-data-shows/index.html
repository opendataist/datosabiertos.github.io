High housing costs cut into Texans’ income gains in 2025, new Census data shows

High housing costs cut into Texans’ income gains in 2025, new Census data shows

Texas experienced mixed economic trends, with median household income rising slightly but remaining below the national average. Significant disparities emerged across regions and demographics, as Hispanic households saw substantial gains while racial income gaps persisted and widened compared to national figures. Despite a slight decline in the overall poverty rate, inequality remains high, highlighting uneven economic progress across different communities. Housing affordability continues to be a critical challenge, with more than half of renters and a significant portion of homeowners with mortgages facing cost burdens. This financial strain limits resources for essential needs like healthcare and savings, despite relatively flat rental prices. The situation is compounded by Texas’s historically high property taxes, although recent legislative efforts have provided minor relief for certain homeowners, contrasting with rising national property tax trends. This data is vital for open data advocates as it underscores the importance of accessible, granular demographic and economic statistics. By revealing hidden inequalities and regional disparities, such datasets empower communities and policymakers to identify specific areas requiring intervention. Transparent access to this information facilitates more equitable resource allocation and fosters informed public discourse on living costs and social welfare in the state.
