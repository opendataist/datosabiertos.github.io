Investment in Mexico breaks streak of declines, rising 5.9% in April

Investment in Mexico breaks streak of declines, rising 5.9% in April

Gross fixed investment in Mexico showed a recovery after nearly a year and a half of decline, driven primarily by the construction sector and machinery. This rebound suggests an incipient stabilization, although the economy faces a general slowdown, as evidenced by the contraction of quarterly GDP despite marginal annual growth. The relevance for open data lies in the fact that these official figures allow for an analysis of the viability of nearshoring and economic resilience. Seasonally adjusted and disaggregated data facilitate the transparency needed to evaluate public policies and attract foreign investment, linking macroeconomic indicators with specific sectoral trends. Thus, the flow of public information on long-term productive assets helps interpret the impact of global supply chains. Understanding this dynamic is crucial for researchers and citizens seeking to comprehend how private and public investment influence the recovery of the labor market and national productivity in the face of external shocks.
