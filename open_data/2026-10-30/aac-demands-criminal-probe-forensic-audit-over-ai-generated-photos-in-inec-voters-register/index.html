AAC Demands Criminal Probe, Forensic Audit Over AI-Generated Photos In INEC Voters' Register

AAC Demands Criminal Probe, Forensic Audit Over AI-Generated Photos In INEC Voters' Register

The African Action Congress has demanded a criminal investigation and an independent forensic audit of Nigeria’s preliminary voters’ register after AI-generated images were discovered within the dataset. The party rejects the electoral commission’s explanation that these were flagged errors, arguing that the presence of synthetic faces indicates severe security flaws in the registration infrastructure. This discovery has triggered urgent calls for the prosecution of responsible officials and technology vendors, with warnings that such vulnerabilities threaten the integrity of the upcoming general elections. Central to this controversy is the perceived failure of the Automated Biometric Identification System, which was designed to prevent duplicate or fraudulent entries. The fact that social media users detected these anomalies before the official verification process did highlights a critical gap in institutional oversight. The AAC contends that if synthetic identities can bypass these systems, the entire biometric framework is compromised, raising fears that malicious actors could manipulate the electorate on a massive scale. This situation is highly relevant to open data principles, as it underscores the necessity of transparency and third-party verification in sensitive public datasets. The party insists on publishing a detailed audit trail and a final report that quantifies invalid records, arguing that closed-door clean-ups erode public trust. Ultimately, the incident illustrates that without robust, transparent open data practices and independent forensic scrutiny, the credibility of digital civic infrastructure remains fundamentally vulnerable to manipulation.
