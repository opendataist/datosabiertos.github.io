Trump administration asks Supreme Court to block watchdog access to DOGE documents

Trump administration asks Supreme Court to block watchdog access to DOGE documents

This case determines whether the Department of Government Efficiency qualifies as a federal agency subject to the Freedom of Information Act. The administration argues it is merely an exempt presidential advisory body, while lower courts assert its substantial power to cut contracts and employees suggests it requires transparency and accountability. For open data advocates, this ruling defines the boundaries of public access to government operations. If classified as a non-agency, DOGE’s extensive data on federal restructuring remains hidden, undermining democratic oversight. Conversely, recognizing it as an agency establishes crucial precedents for disclosing information from entities wielding significant executive power. Ultimately, the Supreme Court’s decision will shape how transparent modern governance must be. It clarifies whether newly empowered administrative units are accessible to watchdogs and citizens, ensuring that significant shifts in government structure do not occur behind closed doors without public scrutiny or data availability.
