Mantiene suspenso último informe sobre IA y derechos de autor

Mantiene suspenso último informe sobre IA y derechos de autor

The article highlights the disruptive challenge that artificial intelligence poses to traditional copyright regimes, threatening to erode the commercial value of authored works. Unlike previous technological shifts, AI creates a scenario in which the need to reward authorship may be fundamentally undermined, necessitating a reevaluation of how intellectual property laws adapt to these new realities. Central to this discussion is the concept of market dilution, which assesses whether AI-generated content infringes upon the potential market for protected works. Recent guidance from the U.S. Copyright Office suggests that training uses are not uniformly illegal; rather, they are evaluated based on their transformative nature and market impact. Non-commercial research uses that do not reproduce original content are likely permissible, whereas copying expressive works to compete directly in the market remains problematic. This analysis is highly relevant to open data, as it establishes a legal framework for utilizing existing information without infringing on rights. By clarifying that certain data processing methods constitute fair use, it supports the development of open datasets for AI training. This balance allows innovators to access and analyze vast amounts of data openly, ensuring that technological progress does not stall due to ambiguous copyright restrictions, while still protecting creators’ interests.
