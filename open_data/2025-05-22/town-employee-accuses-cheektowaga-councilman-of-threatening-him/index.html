Town employee accuses Cheektowaga councilman of threatening him

Town employee accuses Cheektowaga councilman of threatening him

A Cheektowaga town employee alleges he was threatened with violence by Councilman Michael Jasinski following a dispute over a social media post. Surveillance footage captures Jasinski appearing aggressive and pointing at the employee outside a school, prompting a police report. Jasinski denies the allegations, claiming he was defending his family from stalking and that the interaction was a calm request for the employee to stay away from his home. This conflict highlights the fragility of professional boundaries when public officials and employees engage in personal disputes in public spaces. The incident underscores how quickly digital activism can escalate into physical confrontations, creating volatile environments for local government operations. Such behavior erodes trust and disrupts the normal functioning of municipal services, as personal animosity spills over into public interactions. The story is relevant to open data because it involves a FOIL request for public surveillance video, a core transparency mechanism. However, it also raises complex questions about the public interest in releasing footage of private citizens or low-level employees versus the right to privacy. It demonstrates that while FOIL requests can uncover misconduct, they do not always resolve underlying interpersonal conflicts or establish clear guidelines for what constitutes public record in heated local political disputes.
