FOIA request reveals more information about hill-slide slip that’s closed Logan County school

FOIA request reveals more information about hill-slide slip that’s closed Logan County school

A geotechnical investigation into a hillslide at a West Virginia elementary school revealed the instability originated from the upper shale formation near the building's cut slope. Despite the closure, experts determined the structure itself suffered no obvious damage, allowing essential staff to return while students remained displaced until mitigation efforts were completed. This highlights the critical role of transparent geological data in managing public safety risks during infrastructure emergencies. The agency provided specific recommendations to stabilize the slope, including clearing vegetation to remove destabilizing lean and ensuring drainage systems remain functional to prevent debris from impacting the structure. These findings, obtained through a Freedom of Information Act request, demonstrate how accessing raw technical reports and internal decision-making processes fosters accountability. It allows the community to verify that safety protocols were based on professional engineering assessments rather than vague assurances. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how public records requests can uncover precise, actionable information about environmental hazards. By making geotechnical reports and administrative decisions accessible, authorities build trust and enable independent verification of safety claims. Transparent sharing of such specialized data empowers citizens to understand the true extent of risks and the rationale behind emergency responses, ensuring that public resources are managed responsibly and effectively.
