The article highlights that used car prices have risen modestly, outpacing inflation in specific segments while showing slight monthly declines. This trend underscores the economic accessibility challenges for consumers, particularly regarding older vehicles, which constitute a significant portion of the market. The data reveals regional disparities, with some areas experiencing higher costs than others, reflecting broader market dynamics rather than uniform national behavior. The relevance to open data lies in the necessity of transparent, granular market statistics to evaluate policy efficacy. By making detailed pricing and sales data publicly accessible, stakeholders can assess whether current initiatives, such as electric vehicle subsidies, truly democratize access. Without such transparency, it remains difficult to verify whether aid reaches those with limited purchasing power or if structural barriers persist in the secondary market. Ultimately, the industry argues that current measures are insufficient because they exclude used vehicles, which are crucial for affordable electrification. Open data enables the precise identification of these gaps, allowing policymakers to refine their strategies. Accessible information is vital for creating equitable mobility solutions that balance environmental goals with socioeconomic realities, ensuring that no demographic is left behind in the transition to electric transport.
