¿Padeces crisis de ansiedad? Aquí hay un para manejarlas…

¿Padeces crisis de ansiedad? Aquí hay un para manejarlas…

The prevalence of anxiety in Mexico represents a public health crisis affecting a significant proportion of the population, with women and certain regions of the country being the most impacted. This disorder, which goes beyond the natural emotion of stress to become a disabling condition, impairs social, academic, and occupational functioning and can lead to serious consequences such as depression or suicidal ideation. Normalizing and recognizing these symptoms early on is essential to avoid stigma and facilitate access to comprehensive treatments. To address this issue, institutions such as the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) have implemented evidence-based strategies, such as the "Anxiety Management Kit." These tools, which include diaphragmatic breathing techniques, muscle relaxation, and mindfulness, enable patients to actively manage their symptoms and reduce the physiological activation associated with stress. The effectiveness of these methods lies in empowering individuals to develop self-regulation skills, complementing professional care and promoting prevention in everyday contexts. The relevance of this report for *open_data* lies in its demonstration of how the dissemination of robust epidemiological data, derived from national surveys, is the critical first step in designing effective public policies. By making demographic and geographic disparities in mental health visible, open data facilitate the precise allocation of resources and the evaluation of the impact of clinical interventions. This transparency fosters a more informed society regarding mental well-being and strengthens accountability within health systems, ensuring that solutions respond to the actual needs of the population.
