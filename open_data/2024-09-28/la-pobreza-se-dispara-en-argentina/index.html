La pobreza se dispara en Argentina

La pobreza se dispara en Argentina

Argentina has experienced a sharp increase in poverty and extreme poverty during the first half of the year, reaching levels not seen since 2003. This social crisis is the direct result of the severe economic adjustment policies implemented by the government, which, although aimed at controlling inflation and balancing public finances, have caused a significant erosion of wages, a decline in economic activity, and a substantial rise in the cost of living. The government justifies this deteriorated social situation as a necessary and temporary side effect of an inherited adjustment, arguing that the fight against inflation is the only sustainable path to eradicating poverty in the long term. However, experts warn that public pressure will increase if favorable economic results are not visible soon, especially considering that the most vulnerable sectors, who typically form the base of political opposition, have less patience for immediate economic sacrifices. This case is highly relevant to the field of open data, as it demonstrates the critical importance of transparency and statistical accessibility in crisis contexts. When official figures are public, clear, and comparable, they enable citizens, academics, and the media to contrast the government’s narrative with objective reality. Moreover, it facilitates independent analysis of the correlations between macroeconomic policies and their social impact, which is essential for maintaining democratic accountability and guiding public debates based on factual evidence.
