La pobreza en Argentina sube al 52.9% en medio de la crisis económica bajo el gobierno de Milei

Recent data from Argentina’s national statistics institute reveal a severe economic crisis, with poverty rates exceeding half of the population in early 2024. This surge is largely attributed to strict adjustment policies implemented by the current administration, including the reduction of subsidies for essential services. These measures, combined with a significant contraction in GDP and hyperinflation, have drastically eroded purchasing power, pushing many into destitution and highlighting the tangible social costs of rapid fiscal tightening. This situation underscores the critical importance of open data in monitoring societal well-being during periods of political and economic transition. Transparent, timely, and accurate statistical information enables citizens and analysts to distinguish between political rhetoric and lived reality. By making these indicators publicly accessible, stakeholders can hold governments accountable, verify the actual impact of austerity measures, and advocate for evidence-based policy adjustments that protect vulnerable populations, rather than relying solely on official narratives. Ultimately, the article demonstrates that data is not merely a record of past performance but a vital tool for future governance. Without reliable open data, it becomes impossible to design effective social safety nets or achieve the necessary balance between fiscal responsibility and human rights. Therefore, maintaining robust, accessible statistical systems is essential for democratic oversight, enabling society to navigate crises with clarity and ensuring that economic strategies do not exacerbate inequality unchecked.
