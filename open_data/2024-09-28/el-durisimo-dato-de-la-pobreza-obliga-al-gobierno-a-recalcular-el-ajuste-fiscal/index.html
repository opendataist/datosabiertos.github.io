El durísimo dato de la pobreza obliga al Gobierno a recalcular el ajuste fiscal

The official report revealing a dramatic surge in poverty and destitution has fundamentally challenged the administration’s commitment to its stringent fiscal adjustment. This social reality acts as a critical constraint, forcing the government to reconsider the sustainability of its zero-deficit policy in the face of rising political pressure and declining public approval. Although targeted social assistance was increased to cushion the impact, it proved insufficient to halt the rise in extreme poverty, particularly among children. This outcome highlights the limited efficacy of isolated measures against a dual shock of recession and high inflation, suggesting that current policies are not adequately protecting the most vulnerable sectors despite their expansion. This situation is vital for open data discourse as it demonstrates how transparent, official statistics serve as a crucial accountability mechanism in a democracy. The data exposes the gap between political narratives and lived realities, illustrating that while fiscal metrics may show a surplus, social indicators reveal deep structural failures. Consequently, the government has already begun implementing temporary relief measures, proving that accurate, accessible data can directly influence policy shifts and force a pragmatic recalibration of economic strategies.
