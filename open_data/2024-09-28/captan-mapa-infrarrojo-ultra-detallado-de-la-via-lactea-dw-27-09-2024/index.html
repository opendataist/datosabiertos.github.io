Captan mapa infrarrojo ultra detallado de la Vía Láctea – DW – 27/09/2024

Astronomers have unveiled the most detailed infrared map of the Milky Way to date, fundamentally altering our understanding of the galaxy’s structure. By penetrating dense cosmic dust that previously obscured the central regions, this comprehensive survey reveals over 1.5 billion objects, including newly formed stars and ancient globular clusters. This shift in perspective allows for a precise three-dimensional view of areas previously hidden, providing deeper insights into galactic formation and dynamics. The dataset, accumulated over more than a decade, tracks stellar movements and brightness changes, serving as crucial cosmic rulers for distance measurement. It also identifies hypervelocity stars ejected from the galactic center, offering clues about the supermassive black hole’s influence. This extensive temporal coverage ensures that dynamic changes within the galaxy are captured accurately, moving beyond static snapshots to reveal living, evolving celestial mechanics. This release is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it exemplifies the power of massive, publicly accessible scientific datasets. The sheer volume of terabytes involved demonstrates how open access to such complex astronomical records empowers global researchers. It underscores the importance of transparent data sharing in accelerating discoveries and validating findings across international scientific communities, fostering collaboration in astrophysics.
