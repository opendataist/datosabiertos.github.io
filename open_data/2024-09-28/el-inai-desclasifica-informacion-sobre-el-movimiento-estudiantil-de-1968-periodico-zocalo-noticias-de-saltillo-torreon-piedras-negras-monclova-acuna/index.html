El INAI desclasifica información sobre el Movimiento Estudiantil de 1968 | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The INAI has declassified critical historical documents related to the 1968 student movement, emphasizing that public access to this information is essential for establishing the truth and preventing future human rights violations. By integrating these records into public collections, the initiative prioritizes historical memory over state secrecy, ensuring that society can reconstruct events without political barriers. A significant precedent was set by mandating maximum publicity for investigations into crimes against humanity, such as the charges against former President Luis Echeverría. This ruling reinforces the principle that allegations of genocide cannot remain hidden, compelling government agencies to release official archives despite resistance, thereby upholding the principle that transparency is a fundamental requirement in addressing state-sponsored repression. Furthermore, the INAI successfully challenged attempts to withhold visual evidence of torture sites, even when judicial processes were ongoing. This outcome highlights the robustness of open data frameworks in protecting the public interest against claims of ongoing investigations, ensuring that evidence of historical atrocities remains accessible for educational and judicial purposes rather than being suppressed by institutional inertia.
