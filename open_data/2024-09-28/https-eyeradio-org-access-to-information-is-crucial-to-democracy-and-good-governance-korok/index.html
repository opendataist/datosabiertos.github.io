Open Data
ES EN

South Sudan’s government highlights that constitutional access to information is fundamental to democracy and good governance, rather than a mere privilege. This right empowers citizens to scrutinize government actions, ensuring public resources are used efficiently and fostering an environment where decision-makers act ethically under public scrutiny. By enabling journalists and civil society to investigate and advocate for the public interest, the state strengthens its institutions and promotes a just, transparent society. To operationalize these rights, officials announced plans to establish a national news agency and empower information officers to engage directly with the public. These initiatives aim to sensitize citizens about their entitlements while leveraging new technologies to facilitate easier information retrieval. The strategy involves training focal points who can assist individuals in accessing data, thereby increasing literacy and ensuring the public is enlightened regarding their rights within the digital age. Relevance to open data lies in the institutional commitment to transparency and data availability. By opening parliamentary proceedings for live transmission and designating officers to handle public inquiries, the government is creating structural pathways for information flow. This approach demonstrates how political will translates into practical mechanisms for open data implementation, ensuring that critical government information is not only released but is actively accessible and understandable to all citizens, thereby enhancing civic participation and accountability.
