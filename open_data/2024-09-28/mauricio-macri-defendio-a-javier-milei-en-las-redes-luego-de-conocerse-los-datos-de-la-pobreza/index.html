The article criticizes political narratives surrounding poverty rates, highlighting how macroeconomic data is often framed to assign blame rather than address root causes. This politicization undermines public trust in official statistics, a core concern in open data initiatives that prioritize transparency and neutrality. Open data requires a clear separation between raw statistics and political interpretation to maintain credibility. When institutions like INDEC release findings, they must remain independent of current governmental discourse to ensure accurate public understanding. The relevance to open data lies in defending statistical integrity against ideological manipulation. Ensuring that poverty metrics are accessible, unfiltered, and contextually clear helps citizens make informed decisions, free from partisan spin or historical revisionism disguised as economic analysis.
