Baja pobreza multidimensional, según el INE: ¿Qué factores mide esta encuesta?

The article highlights a significant decline in multidimensional poverty in Paraguay, indicating that a substantial number of individuals have improved their living conditions beyond mere income levels. This shift is attributed to a comprehensive measurement tool that evaluates deprivation across health, education, living standards, and employment. Consequently, the data reveals a nuanced reality where over half of those classified as multidimensionally poor are not monetarily impoverished, suggesting that non-financial factors like access to services and job security remain critical barriers to well-being despite sufficient earnings. The relevance of this study to open data lies in the methodological rigor and transparency of the Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI). By adopting frameworks established by the University of Oxford and grounded in Amartya Sen’s capabilities approach, the National Institute of Statistics (INE) provides a publicly accessible, standardized metric for assessing human development. This approach allows researchers and policymakers to move beyond traditional GDP-centric views, offering a richer, more granular understanding of social welfare that can be openly compared across regions and time periods. Ultimately, the distinction between monetary and multidimensional poverty has profound policy implications. It underscores that economic growth alone does not guarantee improved quality of life if structural issues like inadequate sanitation, education, and healthcare persist. For the open data community, this case demonstrates the value of integrating diverse datasets to create holistic indicators. Such transparency enables society to hold institutions accountable and directs resources toward addressing the specific, non-monetary deprivations that perpetuate inequality, ensuring that development strategies are inclusive and effective.
