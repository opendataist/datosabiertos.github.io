Government may warn against DeepSeek over China’s data tracking risks

Government may warn against DeepSeek over China’s data tracking risks

The Indian government is preparing to issue a cautionary advisory regarding the Chinese AI tool DeepSeek, citing serious data privacy and cyber espionage risks. Officials highlight that the application’s ability to track extensive user behavior, device data, and keystrokes poses a significant threat to national security. This move underscores a broader governmental discomfort with allowing foreign entities, particularly those lacking accountability under Chinese law, access to sensitive Indian citizen data. This development is crucial for the open data community as it illustrates the tension between the benefits of open-source artificial intelligence and the imperative of sovereign data protection. While tools like DeepSeek offer cost-effective innovation, their underlying architecture often prioritizes data collection over user privacy. The lack of global legal accountability for Chinese tech firms means that open data initiatives may inadvertently expose users to surveillance, challenging the fundamental open data principle of trust and transparency. Consequently, the advisory reflects a global trend where nations are reassessing the integration of foreign AI models into official infrastructure. With several countries already banning the tool due to security concerns, this incident emphasizes the need for robust data governance frameworks in open data ecosystems. It serves as a critical reminder that open access to technology must be balanced with stringent security protocols to prevent the misuse of personal information and maintain public trust in digital services.
