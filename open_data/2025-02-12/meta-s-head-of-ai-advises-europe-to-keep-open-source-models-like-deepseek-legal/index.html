Meta's head of AI advises Europe to keep open-source models — like DeepSeek — legal

Meta's head of AI advises Europe to keep open-source models — like DeepSeek — legal

Yann LeCun warns that Europe risks falling behind in the global AI race if it restricts open-source artificial intelligence. He argues that developing models in secret stifles innovation, whereas open collaboration accelerates progress. By allowing free access to software, societies can benefit from faster advancements and avoid dependence on a few powerful entities in the US or China. This stance directly challenges political moves in some regions that view open source as a security threat, labeling such restrictions as costly strategic errors. The relevance of this debate to open data is profound, as open-source AI serves as a foundational pillar for transparency and equitable technological growth. LeCun highlights that breakthroughs, such as those by Chinese startup DeepSeek, often stem from building upon existing open frameworks. This demonstrates how shared data and code create a cumulative advantage, fostering a competitive environment where diverse contributors can innovate. Restricting access to these models would effectively halt the iterative improvements that drive the field forward. Consequently, maintaining open models is presented not just as a technical preference, but as an economic necessity for Europe. Regional companies like Mistral and Aleph Alpha already thrive within this ecosystem, criticizing regulations that might hinder their ability to compete with global giants. The narrative suggests that protecting open access is essential for preventing monopolistic control and ensuring that AI development remains inclusive. Ultimately, the article advocates for policies that support open sharing to secure long-term technological sovereignty and innovation.
