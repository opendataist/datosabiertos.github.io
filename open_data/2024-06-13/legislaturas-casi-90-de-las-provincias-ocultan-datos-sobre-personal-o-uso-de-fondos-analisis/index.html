Legislaturas: casi 90% de las provincias ocultan datos sobre personal o uso de fondos | Análisis

Legislaturas: casi 90% de las provincias ocultan datos sobre personal o uso de fondos | Análisis

A revealing investigation shows that most provincial legislatures in Argentina systematically conceal critical information regarding contracting and the management of funds. This lack of transparency undermines accountability and facilitates irregular practices, such as the payment of double salaries, the discretionary use of public resources, and the hiring of unverified personnel, thereby evidencing a serious erosion of democratic integrity and public management. Institutional secrecy is so pervasive that the vast majority of jurisdictions either fail to respond to freedom of information requests or provide only partial responses, even preventing the judiciary from accessing basic data during criminal investigations. The absence of clear records on attendance, salaries, and reserved expenses turns the legislative branch into an opaque space where it is impossible to discern the actual use of resources, facilitating the creation of fictitious employment structures and nepotism under the protection of administrative anonymity. This case is crucial for the open data movement because it illustrates the direct consequences of administrative opacity: corruption and citizen distrust. By highlighting that transparency is not merely a good practice but a prerequisite for combating corruption, the article underscores the urgency of standardizing and proactively publishing public data. Without access to accurate and comprehensive information on public spending and personnel, civil society cannot exercise its right to oversight, making it imperative to promote robust open data laws and systems that ensure genuine scrutiny.
