Meta y la Privacidad de Datos: Lo que debes saber sobre el entrenamiento de su IA

Meta has begun using public data from Facebook and Instagram to train its artificial intelligence models, a practice already in operation in the United States but which has sparked intense privacy concerns in Europe. The main conclusion is that, although the company claims to comply with local regulations and anonymize the information, the implementation of this policy exposes structural tensions between technological innovation and user protection, particularly in the absence of clear mechanisms to prevent data collection in regions with more permissive legislation. The relevance to the field of open data lies in how this strategy redefines the boundary between what is “public” and what is commercially usable. By treating open posts as raw material for AI development, Meta demonstrates a model in which nominal transparency does not guarantee individual control. This raises significant ethical and legal challenges, as the lack of robust opt-out options, particularly in the U.S., suggests that the availability of public data does not automatically imply informed consent for algorithmic processing, thereby questioning the foundations of current information management frameworks. Furthermore, the European response, although it provides a formal avenue for objection, reveals the fragility of digital rights in the face of corporate power asymmetry. The fact that Meta reviews and may reject opt-out requests indicates that “opt-out” is not an effective right, but rather an exception subject to corporate discretion. For the open data community, this case underscores the urgent need for more rigorous standards that distinguish between public access and secondary use for AI training, ensuring that the reuse of information does not undermine citizens’ autonomy and privacy.
