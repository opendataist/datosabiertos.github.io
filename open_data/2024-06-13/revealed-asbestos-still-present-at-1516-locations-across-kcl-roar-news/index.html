REVEALED: Asbestos Still Present at 1,516 Locations Across KCL - Roar News

REVEALED: Asbestos Still Present at 1,516 Locations Across KCL - Roar News

This article highlights a critical gap between regulatory transparency and public safety in higher education infrastructure. Information obtained through the Freedom of Information Act reveals that King’s College London maintains significant, potentially hazardous asbestos across its major campuses and associated accommodations. The presence of this banned material underscores the lingering legacy of pre-1999 construction standards, where asbestos was widely utilized for its thermal properties before health risks were fully recognized. While current exposure levels are deemed managed and non-immediate, the data indicates that many locations have not undergone substantial remediation for over two decades. The university relies on external contractors for removal only when materials are damaged or disturbed, suggesting a reactive rather than proactive approach to eliminating legacy hazards. This contrasts with recent efforts at other institutions, where redevelopment projects have prioritized the complete removal of asbestos to ensure long-term safety for students and staff. The relevance to open data lies in the power of FOI requests to expose hidden institutional risks and hold universities accountable for their physical environments. By making inspection records and risk assessments public, transparency forces organizations to justify their safety protocols and accelerates the timeline for necessary repairs. This case demonstrates how accessible information drives public scrutiny, ensuring that older educational facilities are not left with unresolved health liabilities that affect thousands of individuals daily.
