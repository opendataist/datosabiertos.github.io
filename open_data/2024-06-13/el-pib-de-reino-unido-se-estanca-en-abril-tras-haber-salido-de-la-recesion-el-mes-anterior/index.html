El PIB de Reino Unido se estanca en abril tras haber salido de la recesión el mes anterior

The United Kingdom’s economy stagnated in April, with gross domestic product remaining flat after a brief recovery in the previous month. While experts anticipated this lack of growth, the data highlights a divergence between strong service sector performance and significant declines in manufacturing and construction. This mixed performance suggests that the initial momentum from the first quarter was insufficient to sustain a robust expansion, leaving the economy vulnerable to further slowdowns despite not entering an immediate recession. Economists emphasize that while monthly figures can be volatile, broader indicators like activity PMIs suggest the economy is still expanding. However, the stagnation casts a shadow over the current administration ahead of elections, with critics arguing that underlying issues such as inflation, insurance costs, and public insecurity are stifling consumer spending. The consensus is that fiscal tightening and delayed interest rate effects will likely result in a steady, rather than spectacular, recovery over the coming year, requiring structural changes to achieve significant growth. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of high-quality, transparent, and timely statistical releases, such as those provided by the UK’s Office for National Statistics. Reliable public data allows diverse stakeholders—including analysts, politicians, and civil society—to independently verify economic trends, debate policy effectiveness, and hold governments accountable.
