Wirepoints reveals that Illinois’ education system suffers from critically low student performance despite rising costs and minimal accountability. Analysis of major school districts shows that while proficiency rates in reading and math remain abysmal for most demographics, high graduation rates and nearly universal positive teacher evaluations obscure the underlying academic failures. This disconnect highlights a systemic lack of rigorous standards and meaningful consequences for underperformance. Financially, the state burdens taxpayers with the nation’s highest property taxes while spending more per student than most other states. Even as enrollment declines, tax revenues and per-pupil spending have surged significantly over the last decade. This trend indicates that increased financial investment has not translated into better educational outcomes, suggesting inefficiencies or misaligned priorities within the funding structure. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how accessible, granular public statistics can expose systemic governance failures. By aggregating state and census data into accessible fact sheets, organizations can drive public awareness and demand for transparency. It underscores the value of democratizing data to challenge official narratives and inform policy discussions regarding fiscal responsibility and educational equity.
