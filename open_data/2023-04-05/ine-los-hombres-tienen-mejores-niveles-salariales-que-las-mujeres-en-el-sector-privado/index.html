INE: Los hombres tienen mejores niveles salariales que las mujeres en el sector privado

The report confirms a persistent gender wage gap in Bolivia’s private sector, where men consistently earn higher nominal salaries and remuneration than women across most occupational categories. This structural inequality highlights systemic disparities in labor compensation, affecting professionals, managers, and skilled workers, while showing only negligible differences in specific unskilled roles. The data underscores that despite overall economic fluctuations, the fundamental imbalance in pay equity remains a defining characteristic of the current labor market. This issue is critically relevant to open data initiatives, as it demonstrates the power of transparent, publicly accessible statistical analysis in revealing deep-seated social inequalities. By making granular salary and remuneration data available, government institutions enable researchers and civil society to monitor progress toward gender equality and hold employers accountable. Without such rigorous, open datasets, these widespread disparities might remain obscured or ignored by policymakers. Ultimately, the availability of this information fosters informed public debate and supports evidence-based policy design. Open data serves as a vital tool for advocacy, allowing stakeholders to identify specific sectors with the widest gaps and target interventions effectively. Ensuring that high-quality labor statistics remain free and accessible is essential for driving meaningful social change and promoting fairer economic structures in developing economies.
