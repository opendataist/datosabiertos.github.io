The article concludes that government initiatives, specifically the "Ruta del Arroz," failed to reduce the consumer price of rice despite significant tariff reductions. Statistical analysis reveals that while inflation rates slowed, prices did not drop, contradicting the administration's primary economic promise. This outcome highlights a critical disconnect between policy design and market reality, suggesting that lowering import barriers alone is insufficient to translate into lower costs for households without addressing other structural factors like international transport costs and global market trends. The lack of price reduction has had severe negative implications for the local agricultural sector. Domestic producers argue that the influx of cheaper imported rice, facilitated by the new tariffs, has dismantled the national industry, forcing many small and medium-sized farmers out of business. Consequently, rural unemployment has risen, and local food security is jeopardized as domestic production capacity erodes. This scenario illustrates the complex trade-offs in trade liberalization, where short-term consumer gains or political promises can lead to long-term structural damage to domestic livelihoods and local supply chains. This case is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, statistically rigorous data can serve as an essential tool for accountability and public scrutiny. The credibility of the National Institute of Statistics and Censuses (INEC) allowed independent experts and citizens to verify policy failures against official metrics, challenging government narratives with empirical evidence. It underscores the necessity of accessible and trustworthy open data ecosystems in democratic societies, enabling the public to evaluate the actual impact of governmental decisions rather than relying solely on political rhetoric.
