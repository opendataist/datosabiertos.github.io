NDP raises alarm after Sask. ambulances unavailable hundreds of times this year | CBC News

NDP raises alarm after Sask. ambulances unavailable hundreds of times this year | CBC News

Recent Freedom of Information requests have exposed critical shortages in Saskatchewan’s ambulance services, revealing hundreds of instances where the Saskatchewan Health Authority lacked available vehicles to respond to emergency calls in early 2023. These gaps in coverage have led to dangerous delays, including cases where patients waited excessively long for life-saving transport or gave birth roadside due to staffing crises. Although the provincial government acknowledges the severity of the situation and cites increased funding as a remedial measure, opposition leaders argue that these investments are insufficient band-aid solutions that fail to address the systemic nature of the crisis. This incident is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the power of proactive transparency in holding public institutions accountable. The ability of political opponents and the public to access granular performance metrics allowed for the immediate identification of service failures that might otherwise have remained obscured within administrative silos. By making this data public, stakeholders could verify official narratives against empirical evidence, demonstrating how accessible, high-quality datasets are essential for identifying systemic inefficiencies and driving necessary policy reforms in public health infrastructure.
