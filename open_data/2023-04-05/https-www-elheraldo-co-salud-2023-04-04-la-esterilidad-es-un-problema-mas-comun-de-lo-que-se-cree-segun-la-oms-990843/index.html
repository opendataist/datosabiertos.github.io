The World Health Organization reveals that infertility is a widespread global health issue affecting one in six adults, without significant disparities based on region or wealth. This condition often leads to severe mental health challenges, social stigmatization, and economic hardship, highlighting the urgent need for accessible treatments. Many families struggle because fertility procedures are prohibitively expensive and frequently excluded from health insurance, leaving them unable to access necessary care. Data limitations prevent precise conclusions about whether infertility rates are rising or if one gender is more susceptible, as comparable studies remain scarce. While lifestyle factors and age contribute to the issue, the exact impact of environmental pollution on reproductive health remains unclear. Consequently, many cases lack a definitive explanation, underscoring the complexity of diagnosing and treating infertility effectively on a global scale. This article is relevant to open data because it emphasizes the critical need for standardized, comparable datasets to accurately assess public health trends. The current lack of sufficient, unified data hinders the ability to draw firm conclusions, illustrating why transparent, open, and comprehensive data sharing is essential for developing effective policies, securing funding, and ensuring equitable access to healthcare solutions.
