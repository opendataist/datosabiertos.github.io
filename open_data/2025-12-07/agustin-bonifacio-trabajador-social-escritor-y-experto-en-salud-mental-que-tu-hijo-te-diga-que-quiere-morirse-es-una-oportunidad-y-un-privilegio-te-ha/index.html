The article highlights a critical public health crisis in which suicide is the leading cause of death among Spanish youth, driven by systemic precarity and structural violence. This situation reveals a profound intergenerational disconnect, as families often underestimate the severe emotional burdens and uncertainty faced by young people today. Crucially, the text underscores how digital technologies exacerbate mental health struggles. Artificial intelligence has become a primary coping mechanism for adolescents seeking to avoid suicide, while social networks promote superficiality and toxic narratives, deepening feelings of isolation and despair among youth who lack genuine human connection. This report is vital for open data because it illustrates the urgent need for granular, accessible mental health statistics to guide effective public policies. Transparent data allows researchers and policymakers to identify specific risk factors, such as digital dependency and socioeconomic inequality, enabling targeted interventions that address the root causes of this growing crisis rather than just its symptoms.
