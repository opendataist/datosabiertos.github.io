El eslabón perdido entre la tasa de paro y la evolución del empleo

El eslabón perdido entre la tasa de paro y la evolución del empleo

The article questions the reliability of the National Statistics Institute, arguing that its methodologies—particularly those used in the Active Population Survey—are susceptible to manipulation and generate confusion. By adjusting the criteria for what constitutes unemployment, the data can be interpreted in multiple ways, allowing politicians to present official figures as triumphs even when the underlying reality is more complex. This critical lack of transparency in public statistics is a central problem for open data, as it undermines trust in the public information sources necessary for effective social scrutiny. Furthermore, the article highlights the disparity between the decline in declared unemployment and the actual drop in the number of net jobs registered with the Social Security system. Evidence suggests that job creation has become precarious, with an increase in temporary and public-sector contracts, while permanent positions have decreased. This contradiction between the subjective metric of job-seeking behavior and the objective reality of labor affiliation reveals a significant gap between political narratives and tangible economic indicators, making it difficult to accurately assess social well-being. The relevance to open data lies in the urgency of ensuring that statistics are standardized, transparent, and internationally comparable. Without robust, manipulation-free data, it is impossible to properly contextualize national economic performance relative to other countries or to measure the real impact of public policies. Methodological opacity not only hinders informed debate but also obscures persistent structural problems, such as high youth unemployment, demonstrating that the quality of open data is fundamental to democratic accountability.
