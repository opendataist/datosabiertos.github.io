Open data, AI, Idris Odunewu | Homeland Security Newswire

Open data, AI, Idris Odunewu | Homeland Security Newswire

The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence necessitates an evolution of the open data movement rather than its retreat. While AI offers transformative potential across various sectors, this progress must occur within a framework that safeguards the public’s right to access its own information. Ignoring these rights risks undermining a foundational pillar of democratic governance and public innovation, as the integrity of data-driven progress depends on equitable access. A critical concern is the growing tendency for governments to treat valuable public datasets as monetizable resources rather than public goods. As private firms seek exclusive access to train their models, agencies face increasing financial and political pressure to restrict availability. This shift threatens to erode decades of progress in open data policy, potentially compromising the societal benefits that stem from transparent and freely available government information. Restrictive licensing and tiered access models risk entrenching inequalities, favoring large corporations while excluding smaller entities, journalists, and researchers. For open data advocates, this article highlights the urgent need to resist commercialization pressures that limit public access. Ensuring that data remains a public good is essential to maintaining fair competition and supporting the diverse ecosystem of civil society and academic research that relies on unrestricted information.
