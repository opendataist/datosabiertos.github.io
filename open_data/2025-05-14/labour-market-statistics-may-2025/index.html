Labour Market Statistics May 2025

Labour Market Statistics May 2025

Northern Ireland’s labor market shows a complex picture where nominal wages have risen significantly while workforce participation has weakened. Despite year-over-year increases in earnings, the employment rate has declined, and economic inactivity has surged. This divergence suggests that wage growth may be outpacing actual job creation, potentially indicating structural shifts in the local economy rather than broad-based prosperity. The stability of the unemployment rate masks deeper underlying trends, as the rise in inactivity offsets gains in joblessness metrics. Concurrently, redundancies have dropped to levels comparable to those seen before the pandemic, signaling a stabilization in job cuts. However, the simultaneous rise in economic inactivity raises concerns about the health of the labor force, as fewer people are actively seeking or available for work. This data is relevant to open data initiatives as it demonstrates the value of transparent, multi-source statistical reporting. By combining HMRC payroll data with survey-based metrics, open datasets provide a comprehensive view of economic health that single metrics might obscure. Such transparency allows policymakers and researchers to identify nuances, like the gap between pay growth and employment rates, fostering more informed public discourse and decision-making based on accessible, detailed evidence.
