Herd Learning of False Lessons

Herd Learning of False Lessons

The article uses the concept of AI "model collapse" as a metaphor for political and social groups that become trapped in echo chambers. When communities exclusively consume and generate content from within their own ideological bubbles, they lose touch with objective reality. This feedback loop leads to a degradation of discourse and decision-making, mirroring how AI models degrade when trained solely on their own synthetic data. This phenomenon is presented as a universal human trait, applicable to religious, scientific, and political groups alike. The author argues that Democrats and Republicans are both suffering from a broken connection with reality, though to varying degrees. By ignoring external evidence and relying on internal consensus, these groups create distorted beliefs that no longer serve the actual needs or interests of the population, resulting in a catastrophic break with voters and societal cohesion. For open data advocates, this highlights the critical importance of diverse, high-quality, and externally validated information sources. Data systems, like human societies, require exposure to reality to remain accurate and useful. Relying on biased or insular data leads to "model collapse," where insights become increasingly noise and less signal. Ensuring access to grounded, diverse data is essential to prevent the degeneration of truth in algorithmic and societal contexts.
