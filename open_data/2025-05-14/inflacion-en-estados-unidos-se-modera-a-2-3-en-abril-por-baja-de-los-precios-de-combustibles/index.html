Inflación en Estados Unidos se modera a 2.3% en abril por baja de los precios de combustibles

Inflación en Estados Unidos se modera a 2.3% en abril por baja de los precios de combustibles

US inflation eased in April, dropping to 2.3% year-over-year, primarily driven by declining fuel costs. This moderation, however, slightly exceeded analyst expectations and occurred amidst concerns that recent tariff policies might soon trigger higher consumer prices. While current figures mask underlying inflationary pressures, economists warn that these effects are temporary. The decline in energy prices has temporarily hidden the inflationary impact of high import duties. Analysts anticipate that as inventories deplete and tariff costs are fully passed on to consumers, inflation will likely accelerate in the coming months. There is a consensus that the current low figures do not reflect the long-term trend, with expectations for inflation to rise above 3% as trade barriers continue to influence market dynamics. This article is relevant to open_data as it highlights how transparent, official economic statistics allow for critical analysis of policy impacts. By making inflation metrics publicly accessible, researchers and the public can independently verify narratives about trade wars and price controls. This transparency fosters accountability, enabling stakeholders to understand how specific government actions, such as tariffs, directly affect everyday economic conditions through verifiable data rather than speculation.
