Facilidad para alterar datos de inmuebles en Registros Públicos, atenta a la seguridad jurídica - Nacionales - ABC Color

The article highlights a critical flaw in the integrity of public property records, where the arbitrary alteration of real estate data generates legal insecurity and forces citizens into lengthy litigation to correct administrative errors. The case illustrates how inconsistencies in property identification, such as the change of a property’s parcel number without valid technical justification, disconnect physical reality from the digital registry, leaving owners vulnerable to legal disputes and potential usurpations. This situation reveals the absence of transparency and robust verification mechanisms in land management systems. When public institutions fail to ensure the accuracy and accessibility of their data, trust in the legal system erodes, creating an environment conducive to irregularities, such as the alleged “rosca” (a term referring to entrenched networks of influence or corruption), where opacity facilitates questionable practices. The system’s inability to self-correct in a timely and reliable manner demonstrates structural deficiencies in the governance of public information. This case is relevant to open data because it underscores the urgent need for open, standardized, and auditable data in public administration. The lack of interoperability and the failure to publish accurate digital records hinder citizen oversight and efficient conflict resolution. Promoting openness and quality in registry data not only protects property rights but also strengthens accountability and enables civil society to detect and correct anomalies in real time, thereby preventing corruption and systemic injustice.
