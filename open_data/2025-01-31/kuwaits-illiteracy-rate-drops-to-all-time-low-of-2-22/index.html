Kuwait’s illiteracy rate drops to all-time low of 2.22%

Kuwait’s illiteracy rate drops to all-time low of 2.22%

Kuwait has achieved a marked reduction in illiteracy, with overall rates dropping significantly as educational initiatives gain traction. This progress underscores the nation’s strong commitment to sustainable development and public awareness. The data reveals a clear distinction between citizens and expatriates, highlighting where targeted interventions remain most critical for social equity and inclusion. The gap between Kuwaiti nationals and non-Kuwaiti residents is the most pressing issue. While national literacy is near universal, expatriates face substantially higher illiteracy rates. This disparity suggests that current educational frameworks may not adequately serve the migrant workforce, limiting their integration and access to opportunities. Addressing this divide is essential for ensuring that education benefits all segments of society, not just the local population. This report is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistics can drive policy adjustments. By making these metrics public, stakeholders can identify specific demographic vulnerabilities, such as gender and nationality gaps. Open data facilitates evidence-based decision-making, allowing governments to refine literacy programs and allocate resources more effectively to support lifelong learning and digital education for underserved groups.
