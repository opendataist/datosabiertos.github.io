Why blocking China’s DeepSeek from using US AI may be difficult

Why blocking China’s DeepSeek from using US AI may be difficult

Concerns have emerged in Washington that China’s DeepSeek achieved competitive AI performance by utilizing “distillation,” a technique where one model learns from another. This approach allows developers to leverage the massive computational investments of US rivals without incurring the associated costs, potentially violating the terms of service of major American tech companies like OpenAI. While some industry leaders view this as standard competitive intelligence, others argue it constitutes the misappropriation of protected intellectual property, prompting calls for stricter regulatory oversight. The difficulty of policing these activities highlights a significant vulnerability in the current open technology ecosystem. Because many models are publicly accessible or available through user interfaces, detecting unauthorized distillation is nearly impossible, especially when only small amounts of data are required to make substantial improvements. Consequently, technical countermeasures are proving insufficient against sophisticated actors who can bypass restrictions, creating a challenging regulatory environment that struggles to keep pace with rapid technological evolution. This situation is critical to open_data because it illustrates the inherent tension between transparency and intellectual property protection. Open-source models facilitate innovation and accessibility but simultaneously provide the raw materials for competitive advantages that may infringe on proprietary rights. As governments consider stricter controls, the debate underscores the need for clear governance frameworks in open data initiatives to balance the benefits of shared knowledge with the sustainability of investment-driven research.
