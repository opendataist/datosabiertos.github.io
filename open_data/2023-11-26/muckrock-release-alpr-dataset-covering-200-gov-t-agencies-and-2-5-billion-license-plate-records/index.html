MuckRock Release ALPR Dataset Covering 200 Gov't Agencies And 2.5 Billion License Plate Records

MuckRock’s nationwide survey reveals that nearly all license plate scans are collected from individuals not suspected of any crime, creating a massive, indiscriminate database of daily life. This highlights a critical transparency gap in open data initiatives, as such extensive tracking occurs without public knowledge or consent, fundamentally challenging the principle that government-held data regarding citizens should be accessible and accountable. The lack of standardized oversight exacerbates privacy risks, as laws and retention policies vary wildly across states and agencies. When billions of records are shared across a network of law enforcement entities, these conflicting regulations vanish, allowing indefinite storage and unrestricted access. This fragmentation demonstrates how open data efforts can be undermined by opaque institutional practices, leaving citizens with little control over their digital footprints. Ultimately, the data is heavily centralized in the hands of private manufacturers who refuse to release comprehensive records. This reliance on corporate entities to manage public surveillance data underscores a significant failure in open governance. True openness requires that such sensitive information be managed by public authorities with clear accountability, rather than being locked away by private companies, thereby urging a reevaluation of how public surveillance data is collected, stored, and made accessible to the public.
