The Mexican Senate has formally complied with the Supreme Court’s directive regarding the pending appointments of commissioners for the National Institute of Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI). By concluding the voting procedures for two specific candidate slates that failed to secure the necessary majority, the Senate demonstrated adherence to constitutional protocols for selecting these critical oversight roles. To resolve the impasse, the Senate approved agreements to terminate the current selection processes and initiate a new public call for candidates. This strategic shift aims to foster the political consensus previously lacking, ensuring that future appointments reflect broader agreement among legislative factions rather than remaining stalled by partisan divisions. This development is vital for open data advocacy because the INAI is the primary guardian of citizens' right to access public information. Effective appointments ensure the institution can vigorously defend transparency and protect personal data, thereby maintaining the integrity of democratic accountability and enabling the public to hold government officials accountable through unrestricted access to information.
