JDUC blueprints change, impact not disclosed by the AMS or University - The Queen's Journal

Students at Queen’s University are facing significant uncertainty regarding space allocations in the new John Deutsch University Centre, as current blueprints remain undisclosed. Initial architectural plans obtained through an Information Act request were confirmed by student governments to be obsolete preliminary sketches rather than the final working design. This discrepancy highlights a lack of transparency from university administration, leaving students unaware of how their dedicated spaces will actually materialize in the redeveloped facility. Financial implications of this redevelopment have led to increased mandatory fees for both undergraduate and graduate students. These rising costs are attributed to enhanced building features, such as improved climate control and extended operating hours, which require greater operational investment. Despite approving the project via referendum, students are now bearing higher financial burdens to fund these upgrades, even while critical details about the final spatial layout remain unresolved and uncommunicated. This situation is highly relevant to open data principles because it illustrates the failure to provide accessible, accurate, and timely information to stakeholders. The use of obsolete documents and the withholding of current floor plans demonstrate a lack of transparency in public institution planning. For open data to be effective, institutions must proactively release accurate, up-to-date data that allows communities to make informed decisions, rather than relying on outdated or partial information that obscures the true scope and impact of infrastructure projects.
