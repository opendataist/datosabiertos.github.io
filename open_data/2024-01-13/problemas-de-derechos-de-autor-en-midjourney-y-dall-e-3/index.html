Problemas de derechos de autor en Midjourney y DALL-E 3

Generative image AI platforms, such as Midjourney and DALL-E 3, are facing serious allegations of visual and textual plagiarism. Recent studies show that these models can reproduce copyrighted content almost identically, including famous characters and scenes, even when not explicitly requested. This capability reveals that the systems were trained on materials without consent, exposing users to legal risks from unintentional infringement and raising serious concerns about the privacy of the data used in training datasets. This phenomenon creates a fundamental ethical and legal dilemma, as evidenced by lawsuits such as *The New York Times* vs. OpenAI. The automatic reproduction of creative works suggests that artists' labor is being commercially exploited without compensation or authorization. Technology companies must improve transparency regarding data sources and develop technical solutions that prevent the generation of infringing content, balancing innovation with respect for the intellectual property rights of original creators. This issue is relevant to the open data movement because it underscores the critical need for transparency and licensing in the datasets used to train AI. The principles of open access should extend to model training, ensuring that educational and creative resources used are properly licensed, fostering an ecosystem where technology advances without sacrificing the moral and economic rights of authors. Ethical regulation is essential to protect the integrity of shared knowledge.
