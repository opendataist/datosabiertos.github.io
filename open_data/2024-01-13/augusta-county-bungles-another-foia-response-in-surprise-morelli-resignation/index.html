Augusta County bungles another FOIA response in surprise Morelli resignation

Augusta County bungles another FOIA response in surprise Morelli resignation

The article highlights significant failures in governmental transparency regarding the sudden resignation of a local supervisor. Despite a Freedom of Information Act request seeking records related to the resignation and alleged sexual harassment, the county withheld key communications and provided incomplete data. This selective disclosure suggests an institutional effort to obscure the internal dynamics and motivations behind the leadership change, rather than adhering to open records laws. Crucially, the missing evidence includes text messages between the resigning supervisor and other board members that occurred during closed meetings. These omitted messages reveal informal discussions and emotional reactions to the event, contradicting the official narrative of a quiet and procedural departure. The gap between the requested records and those actually provided indicates that the government is leveraging legal exemptions, such as attorney-client privilege, to protect private administrative deliberations from public scrutiny. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates because it exposes how "data" in government is often incomplete or curated before reaching the public. True transparency requires not just the release of documents, but the accountability for what is excluded. When agencies withhold contextual communications, they prevent citizens from understanding the full picture of governance, undermining trust and making it difficult to hold officials accountable for their decisions and conduct.
