Authors & Copyright Scholars Back 'Internet Archive' in Landmark Legal Battle * TorrentFreak

Authors & Copyright Scholars Back 'Internet Archive' in Landmark Legal Battle * TorrentFreak

The legal dispute between the Internet Archive and major publishers centers on the validity of Controlled Digital Lending, a practice where libraries scan owned physical books to lend digital copies. A recent lower court ruling favored the publishers, declaring the Internet Archive’s self-scanning service copyright infringement. This decision threatens to dismantle a critical infrastructure for digital preservation and public access, raising significant concerns about the future availability of knowledge in the digital age. Supporting the Internet Archive are numerous authors, copyright scholars, and civil liberties organizations who argue that the ruling ignores the principles of fair use and the public interest. These groups contend that publishers have exerted disproportionate market control by restricting library acquisitions through restrictive licenses rather than outright sales. They assert that the Internet Archive’s model balances these interests by ensuring broad public access to cultural heritage, whereas the publishers’ approach consolidates power and limits researcher and reader opportunities. This case is vital to open data and open knowledge ecosystems because it defines the legal boundaries of library digitization and digital lending. A precedent against the Internet Archive could establish a restrictive framework where access to information is entirely dictated by commercial licensing agreements rather than ownership or fair use. Preserving the Internet Archive’s operations ensures that libraries can continue to act as open archives, maintaining the free flow of information and preventing the privatization of public cultural resources.
