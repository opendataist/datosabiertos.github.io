Electricity bills in Chile are rising significantly, imposing a financial burden on consumers. This situation underscores the urgent need for transparency in utility services, as access to data reveals systemic inefficiencies rather than external factors such as weather conditions. A comprehensive analysis of fourteen years of outage records shows that utility companies are primarily responsible for the majority of power cuts. Operational errors and inadequate maintenance account for a significant share of these failures, directly contradicting public assumptions that attribute the main disruptions to natural causes. This investigation highlights the critical role of open data in holding corporations accountable. By leveraging transparency laws, citizens can access previously hidden information, thereby driving demand for improved service quality and more equitable energy pricing through evidence-based oversight.
