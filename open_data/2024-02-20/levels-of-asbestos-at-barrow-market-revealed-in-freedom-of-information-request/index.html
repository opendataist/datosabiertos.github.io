Levels of asbestos at Barrow Market revealed in Freedom of Information request

Asbestos contamination has prompted the temporary relocation of traders from Barrow Market to ensure safety during extensive regeneration works. The discovery of high-risk asbestos materials in various areas of the building necessitated these precautionary measures, with unaffected traders compensated or moved to refurbished units nearby. This shift prioritizes health and safety, allowing the council to proceed with necessary removals without endangering the public or businesses. The underlying infrastructure assessment reveals significant hazards, including the presence of carcinogenic asbestos varieties across multiple levels of the market. Although the most dangerous type was not detected, the prevalence of other forms requires immediate intervention. Consequently, professional contractors are scheduled to remove these materials this summer, a critical step that must be completed before the broader modernization efforts can continue effectively. This situation highlights the importance of transparent environmental data in urban planning and public trust. By making survey results and risk assessments public, authorities demonstrate accountability in managing heritage sites and public spaces. This openness ensures that stakeholders understand the rationale behind closures and relocations, fostering cooperation during community-led regeneration projects funded by significant government schemes.
