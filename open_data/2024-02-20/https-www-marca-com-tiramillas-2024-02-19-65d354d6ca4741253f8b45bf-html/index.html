The article reveals that Spanish households spend nearly 40% of their income on housing, pushing many into severe poverty. This historic crisis stems from decades of failed public policy, with consecutive governments neglecting affordable housing initiatives despite early warning signs. The situation highlights a systemic failure to regulate rent-to-income ratios, leaving vulnerable tenants disproportionately affected by inflation. Without intervention, families remain trapped in precarious conditions, demonstrating how the lack of structural housing policies exacerbates economic inequality and social instability. This case is relevant to open data because transparent, accessible statistics from institutions like the Bank of Spain are crucial for diagnosing these trends. Open data empowers researchers and activists to hold policymakers accountable, proving that data-driven transparency is essential for identifying and addressing systemic social failures in real time.
