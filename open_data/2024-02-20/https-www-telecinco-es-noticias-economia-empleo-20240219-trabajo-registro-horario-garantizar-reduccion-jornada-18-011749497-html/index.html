The Spanish Ministry of Labor is pushing to modernize the mandatory working hour tracking system by digitizing it, thereby eliminating paper records that are prone to manipulation and difficult to audit. This shift aims to enhance transparency and allow remote access for labor inspectors, though the proposal acknowledges the need for flexibility to support small and medium-sized enterprises during the transition. Simultaneously, the government intends to strengthen penalties for labor violations by shifting the focus from company-wide fines to individual worker sanctions. This structural change seeks to make penalties more dissuasive and effective, ensuring that non-compliance is personally attributable rather than absorbed as a general corporate cost. These measures are part of a broader agenda that also includes a gradual reduction of the standard workweek, with negotiations underway to determine the specific timeline and mechanisms for adaptation. This development is highly relevant to open data as it highlights the critical intersection between regulatory compliance and digital infrastructure. The mandate for digital records creates an opportunity to establish standardized, machine-readable data formats for labor statistics. If implemented with open standards, this digitalization could facilitate better transparency, enable more rigorous independent analysis of working conditions, and serve as a model for how government-mandated data collection can improve both accountability and the quality of public datasets.
