National Census of Fatal Occupational Injuries Released by BLS and EHS Resources to Protect Workers

Rising workplace fatalities underscore the critical need for transparent safety data. This surge highlights the limitations of current reporting, which focuses narrowly on deaths while excluding the millions of non-fatal injuries and illnesses. Consequently, stakeholders face a fragmented view of occupational health, obscuring the full scope of workplace risks and hindering comprehensive public health analysis. The data reveals persistent hazards, particularly in high-risk sectors, emphasizing the urgency of robust environmental and health safety programs. By failing to capture the broader spectrum of worker harm, existing metrics provide an incomplete picture of occupational dangers. This gap necessitates more inclusive data collection methods that integrate both fatal and non-fatal incidents to accurately reflect worker safety realities. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the consequences of siloed or incomplete datasets in public policy. Improving open data standards to include comprehensive injury and illness statistics would empower researchers, policymakers, and safety professionals to develop more effective interventions. Transparent, holistic data is essential for driving systemic change and ensuring safer working environments for all employees.
