Ordena INAI a la FGR transparentar carpetas contra de Fox, Calderón, EPN y AMLO

The INAI ordered the FGR to disclose data on investigations against former and current presidents, rejecting the agency’s claim of confidentiality based on protecting reputation or presumption of innocence. This decision underscores that security classifications cannot be absolute barriers when public interest outweighs potential harms to individuals. Transparency principles must prevail over administrative secrecy, ensuring citizens can monitor legal actions against public servants. By prioritizing the right to access information, the ruling reinforces accountability in government operations and supports the societal benefit of understanding how authorities handle allegations against political figures. This case is vital to open data because it challenges the misuse of classification to withhold public records. It establishes a precedent where transparency obligations override broad exemptions, promoting a culture where government data remains accessible and subject to public scrutiny, thereby strengthening democratic oversight and institutional trust.
