La frontera financiera: pagos digitales y datos abiertos para decisiones libres

La frontera financiera: pagos digitales y datos abiertos para decisiones libres

The article argues that Mexico’s reliance on cash, despite the availability of advanced digital alternatives, represents a missed economic opportunity. This situation stems from fragmented strategies rather than technological limitations. It emphasizes that without coordinated incentives and interoperability standards among financial actors, digital payment systems fail to achieve the critical mass necessary for widespread adoption, leaving consumers to bear hidden costs and safety risks. The core proposal is a unified national strategy that mandates open payment infrastructure and educates users, mirroring Brazil’s successful model. This shift is essential to transition from simple digital transactions to Open Finance, a framework that allows individuals to securely share their financial data across institutions. By granting users control over their information, the system fosters greater competition and transparency, enabling personalized financial products and significantly improving consumer welfare. This transition is directly relevant to open_data because Open Finance relies on the secure, standardized sharing of personal financial data through APIs. It demonstrates how regulated data openness can dismantle information asymmetry, empower consumers with real-time insights, and stimulate market innovation. Ultimately, the article illustrates that unlocking the full potential of the digital economy requires not just technology, but a collaborative, data-driven regulatory approach that prioritizes user agency and interoperability.
