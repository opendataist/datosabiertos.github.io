¿Público o privado?: revelan sector dónde más gente cobra por debajo del salario mínimo - Economía - ABC Color

¿Público o privado?: revelan sector dónde más gente cobra por debajo del salario mínimo - Economía - ABC Color

The analysis reveals stark disparities in wage compliance across different occupational categories, highlighting significant structural vulnerabilities within the labor market. While public sector employees largely earn above the legal minimum, private sector workers and those in domestic employment face severe deficiencies. A substantial portion of private employees earns below the statutory threshold, a situation driven by informality and partial compliance, whereas domestic workers suffer from systemic neglect, with nearly all earning significantly less than the minimum wage. This indicates that despite legislative progress, regulatory frameworks fail to protect the most precarious segments of the workforce effectively. The broader data confirms that income distribution in the private sector is highly unequal and compressed at the lower end. Even among the middle-income deciles, a large majority of workers still fail to reach the minimum wage reference point. This suggests that the legal floor does not adequately support a significant portion of the salaried population, reinforcing economic inequality. Only those in the highest income brackets consistently exceed these standards, underscoring a rigid hierarchy where wage growth is stagnant for the majority despite overall economic indicators. This article is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how granular, disaggregated datasets expose systemic inequities that aggregate statistics often obscure. By analyzing wage distributions by sector and income deciles, open data initiatives provide the transparency necessary to identify specific groups vulnerable to labor exploitation. Such detailed evidence is essential for policymakers and civil society to design targeted interventions, ensuring that data-driven insights translate into concrete improvements in labor rights and social equity.
