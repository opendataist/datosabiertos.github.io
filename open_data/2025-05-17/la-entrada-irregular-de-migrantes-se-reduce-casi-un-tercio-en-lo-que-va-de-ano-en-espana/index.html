La entrada irregular de migrantes se reduce casi un tercio en lo que va de año en España

Spain’s irregular migration entries fell significantly in early 2025, largely due to a sharp decline in arrivals via the perilous Atlantic route to the Canary Islands. This overall decrease contrasts with a slight rise in Mediterranean crossings and mixed trends in the northern African enclaves, underscoring shifting migration pressures. This data is vital for open-data initiatives, as it illustrates the value of transparent, standardized government statistics in monitoring complex societal changes. By making this information accessible, citizens and researchers can assess policy effectiveness and humanitarian needs without relying on fragmented sources. Ultimately, the article highlights how public data enables evidence-based discourse on migration management. It reinforces the importance of reliable open datasets in promoting accountability and understanding the real-world impacts of border control strategies and international cooperation efforts.
