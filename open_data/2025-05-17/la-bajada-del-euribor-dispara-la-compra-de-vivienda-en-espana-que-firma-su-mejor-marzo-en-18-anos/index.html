La bajada del Euríbor dispara la compra de vivienda en España, que firma su mejor marzo en 18 años

La bajada del Euríbor dispara la compra de vivienda en España, que firma su mejor marzo en 18 años

The recent surge in property transactions in Spain highlights a critical dynamic within the real estate market, driven primarily by lower interest rates and high rental costs that incentivize buying over renting. While this activity signals economic optimism and reaches its highest levels in nearly two decades, it exacerbates the existing scarcity of housing supply, thereby pushing prices upward and complicating access for many residents. This paradox reveals a market increasingly polarized, where the overwhelming majority of sales consist of free-market properties rather than affordable protected housing. Consequently, the lack of adequate affordable options forces many potential buyers into the competitive free market, fueling speculation and further inflating prices. The regional disparities in this trend underscore how monetary policies and local housing stock availability interact to create uneven outcomes across different autonomous communities. This article is highly relevant to open data because it relies on official statistical registers, such as the INE’s Transmissions of Property Rights Statistics, to track these market movements. Analyzing this granular, publicly available data is essential for understanding the true state of the housing crisis, identifying regional exceptions like Navarra, and evaluating the effectiveness of current economic policies. Transparent data enables citizens and policymakers to move beyond anecdotal evidence and address the structural imbalances in housing accessibility.
