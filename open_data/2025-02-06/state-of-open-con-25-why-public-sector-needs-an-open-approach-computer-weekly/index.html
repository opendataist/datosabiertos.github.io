State of Open Con 25: Why public sector needs an open approach | Computer Weekly

State of Open Con 25: Why public sector needs an open approach | Computer Weekly

The article argues that open government data is essential to rebuild democratic engagement and improve public sector efficiency. By making processes transparent, governments can counter democratic disengagement and authoritarian trends, ensuring citizens understand and trust public services. This openness allows for better collaboration across agencies, reducing the friction citizens currently face when navigating complex bureaucracy. A key illustration of this potential is the integration of open-source AI tools like Caddy, which leverages public data to enhance support services. Furthermore, the UK is moving toward mandatory open data standards in procurement, inspired by successful models elsewhere. This shift aims to create a more flexible market, enabling small businesses to participate while helping departments negotiate better prices and identify supply chain risks through shared insights. Ultimately, treating the government as an "open system" allows for the simulation of policies and the integration of diverse public contributions. By embedding open data into legislation and workflows, the state can foster innovation and accountability. This approach ensures that economic growth and anti-corruption efforts are driven by accessible, actionable information rather than opaque internal processes. This topic is critical to open data because it demonstrates the transition from data availability to data utility. It highlights how open data standards and legislation are not just technical requirements but foundational tools for modernizing governance, enhancing economic equity, and restoring civic trust through radical transparency.
