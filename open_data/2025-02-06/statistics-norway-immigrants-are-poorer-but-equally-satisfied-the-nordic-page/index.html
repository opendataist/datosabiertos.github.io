Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions, including lower income and higher rates of mental health challenges and loneliness, yet report satisfaction levels comparable to the general population. This paradox highlights a complex social dynamic where structural disadvantages do not necessarily translate into personal dissatisfaction, suggesting that subjective well-being is influenced by factors beyond objective socioeconomic status. A key driver of this resilience appears to be the immigrants' strong institutional trust. Despite having lower interpersonal trust than natives, immigrants exhibit higher confidence in political, judicial, and police institutions. This strong affiliation with state bodies fosters a sense of security and belonging that mitigates the negative impacts of socioeconomic inequality, reinforcing social cohesion within the Nordic model. This article is crucial for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the value of detailed, disaggregated datasets in revealing nuanced societal truths. By making such granular statistical information publicly accessible, open data enables researchers and policymakers to move beyond aggregate averages. This transparency allows for a deeper understanding of marginalized groups, facilitating targeted interventions that address specific mental health needs while leveraging the positive institutional trust that supports integration.
