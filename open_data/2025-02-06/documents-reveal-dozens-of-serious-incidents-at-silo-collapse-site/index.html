Documents reveal dozens of serious incidents at silo collapse site

Manildra Group has been formally notified by SafeWork NSW following a surge in dangerous incidents and serious injuries at its Shoalhaven Starches plant. The regulator’s intensified monitoring strategy highlights a critical need for sustained improvements in workplace safety compliance, as the company faces ongoing investigations into severe structural failures and fires. This situation underscores the urgent necessity for robust enforcement mechanisms to ensure that industrial operators prioritize worker well-being over operational continuity, preventing potential fatalities in high-risk environments. The article reveals significant flaws in current regulatory oversight, with unions arguing that the frequency of improvement notices without meaningful consequences has failed to deter unsafe practices. The lack of transparent communication from both the company and the safety authority has eroded community trust and sparked fears of future disasters. This highlights the importance of open data in holding institutions accountable, as limited public access to safety records prevents stakeholders from understanding the true scale of operational risks and regulatory effectiveness. For the open data community, this case illustrates how transparency drives accountability in industrial safety. When incident reports are obscured or delayed, it hinders public scrutiny and allows repeat offenders to avoid stricter penalties. Enhancing the availability of granular safety data would empower unions, local communities, and policymakers to demand tangible corrective actions. Ultimately, accessible information is vital for fostering a culture where safety violations are promptly addressed, ensuring that no company operates above the law and that public confidence is restored.
