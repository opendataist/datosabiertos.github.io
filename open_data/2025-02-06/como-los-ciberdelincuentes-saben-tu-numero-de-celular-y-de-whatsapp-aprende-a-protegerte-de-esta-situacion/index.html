Cómo los ciberdelincuentes saben tu número de celular y de WhatsApp: aprende a protegerte de esta situación

The article concludes that phone numbers are rarely private, as cybercriminals exploit various data leakage channels to obtain them. These methods include phishing campaigns that deceive users into submitting personal details, malicious applications that secretly extract data from devices, and significant security breaches within third-party companies or social media platforms. Consequently, individual privacy is frequently compromised not just by direct user error, but by systemic vulnerabilities in the digital infrastructure we rely on. Understanding these mechanisms highlights the critical importance of digital hygiene and skepticism. Users are advised to avoid interacting with unsolicited communications, restrict privacy settings on social networks, and strictly download applications from verified sources. The narrative emphasizes that even when users take precautions, their data may still be exposed through large-scale corporate leaks or random number generation techniques used by attackers for mass scams. This topic is highly relevant to open_data because it underscores the dangers inherent in the intersection of public information ecosystems and personal privacy. It illustrates how data intended for utility or connectivity can be repurposed for harm when security measures fail. By analyzing these leak pathways, the open_data community can better advocate for robust data protection standards, transparency in data handling, and user-centric privacy controls that prevent personal information from becoming a commodity for malicious actors.
