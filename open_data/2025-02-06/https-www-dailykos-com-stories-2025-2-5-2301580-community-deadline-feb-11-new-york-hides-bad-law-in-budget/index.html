Open Data
ES EN

A proposed legislative amendment in New York threatens to severely restrict public access to vital records, effectively ending decades of open data practices. By extending embargo periods for birth, marriage, and death records to nearly a century, the state aims to lock away essential historical information. This move prioritizes operational convenience over transparency, ignoring long-standing backlogs and failing to address the public’s fundamental right to access their own heritage and legal documentation. Financial barriers are simultaneously being raised through exorbitant fee hikes, transforming genealogical research from a affordable public service into a luxury commodity. The elimination of basic indexes further obstructs discovery, making it nearly impossible for individuals to verify the existence of records before paying prohibitive costs. These changes undermine the principle that government-held documents should remain accessible to citizens, signaling a broader trend of deprioritizing public service in favor of bureaucratic efficiency and revenue generation. This issue is critical to the open data movement because it exemplifies the active effort to reverse public access rights rather than protect them. It highlights the urgent need for civil society organizations to monitor legislative processes closely, as significant restrictions are often hidden within large budget bills. The campaign against this proposal demonstrates that without constant vigilance and public pressure, vital information can be swiftly removed from the public domain, threatening the integrity of historical preservation and digital transparency efforts worldwide.
