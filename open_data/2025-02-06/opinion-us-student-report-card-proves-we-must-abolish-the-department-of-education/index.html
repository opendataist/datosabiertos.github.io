Opinion - US student report card proves we must abolish the Department of Education

Opinion - US student report card proves we must abolish the Department of Education

The article argues that American education has deteriorated despite having the inherent capacity to produce skilled professionals, as evidenced by historical success in one-room schoolhouses. Recent data reveals that students, particularly those already struggling, are falling further behind in reading and mathematics since the pandemic. The author attributes this decline not to student capability but to the failure of modern educational leadership and excessive bureaucratic interference, suggesting that a return to classic, fundamental teaching methods is essential for recovery. This perspective is highly relevant to open data because it challenges the efficacy of massive government spending and centralized control in education. The text implies that current federal metrics and budget allocations are mismanaged or ineffective, highlighting a critical need for transparent, localized data to identify where resources are wasted. By questioning the utility of large-scale federal interventions, the piece underscores the value of independent, accessible information that allows states and communities to evaluate actual outcomes rather than relying on aggregated national statistics that may obscure local realities. Ultimately, the author concludes that the U.S. Department of Education should be abolished, advocating for state-led systems that empower teachers and eliminate bureaucratic overhead. This call for decentralization suggests that open, granular data is necessary to build accountability at the local level, ensuring that educational policies are driven by practical results and community needs rather than federal mandates or ideological agendas.
