Julen Rementería, leader of the National Action Party senators, alleges that the prolonged inoperativeness of the National Institute of Transparency is the result of a deliberate strategy by Morena to conceal alleged government misconduct. By blocking the appointment of the remaining commissioners, the legislative majority has kept the institute without a quorum, preventing its normal functioning and, consequently, limiting its capacity for public oversight. This behavior is not viewed as an administrative failure but rather as an intentional omission designed to block access to information that could reveal misappropriations or irregularities in the management of public funds. This conflict is fundamentally significant for open data, as the INAI serves as the institutional guarantor of citizens’ right to know about government management. When the regulatory body becomes inoperative, the transparency ecosystem is weakened, making it more difficult for citizens to access official data on the use of public money. The lack of transparency not only undermines immediate accountability but also erodes trust in democratic institutions and limits civil society’s ability to use public information as a tool for monitoring and participation. Consequently, the INAI’s inability to hold sessions represents a direct threat to the culture of open data in Mexico. Without an active body demanding and protecting access to information, the flow of data to citizens is severely restricted. This situation underscores the critical importance of keeping transparency institutions operational, as they are essential to ensuring that public information is accessible, verifiable, and useful for combating corruption and fostering a more transparent and accountable administration.
