Panama is confronting a rapidly escalating crisis of childhood obesity and overweight, with prevalence rates tripling over the past decade. This alarming public health trend underscores the critical responsibility of parents to cultivate healthy habits and active lifestyles within their households, rather than relying exclusively on external interventions. Consequently, inspections of various schools revealed widespread non-compliance with national laws that mandate healthy food standards in educational settings. These findings indicate that, while parental influence is paramount, schools are also failing to provide adequate nutritious options, thereby exacerbating unhealthy trends among students. This situation highlights the vital role of open data in addressing public health challenges. Transparent, accessible statistical information is essential for holding institutions accountable and driving policy enforcement, ensuring that data not only informs the public but also compels systemic change in how communities and schools promote wellness.
