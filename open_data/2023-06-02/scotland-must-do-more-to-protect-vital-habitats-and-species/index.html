Scotland ‘must do more’ to protect vital habitats and species

Scotland’s protected natural features are predominantly in good or recovering condition, yet recent years reveal a slow decline driven by overgrazing, invasive species, and climate change. This trend highlights the urgent need for transformative restoration efforts to reverse biodiversity loss, despite long-term historical improvements. The Scottish government acknowledges this as an emergency, responding with significant investment in nature restoration funds and the establishment of new protected areas. A forthcoming biodiversity strategy aims to deliver landscape-scale change backed by statutory targets, emphasizing that collaborative action across sectors is essential for sustainable environmental resilience. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of transparent, evidence-based monitoring for accountability. Accessible environmental statistics empower stakeholders to track progress, identify emerging threats like climate impacts, and validate policy decisions, ensuring that restoration initiatives are grounded in reliable, publicly available information rather than isolated observations.
