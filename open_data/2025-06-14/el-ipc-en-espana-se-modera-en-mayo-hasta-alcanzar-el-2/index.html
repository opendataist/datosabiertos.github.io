El IPC en España se modera en mayo hasta alcanzar el 2%

El IPC en España se modera en mayo hasta alcanzar el 2%

Spain’s Consumer Price Index (CPI) stabilized at 2% in May, marking the lowest rate since October and extending a three-month trend of moderation. This decline was primarily driven by a significant drop in the cost of tourism packages and a slower increase in electricity prices compared to the previous year. Although the official figure was slightly higher than initially expected, the overall trajectory points to easing inflationary pressure, supported by favorable developments in service sectors such as air travel and transportation. Underlying core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy items, also decelerated, reflecting a gradual normalization of persistent price pressures. However, structural price increases remain evident in certain goods, including chocolate, coffee, and eggs, while essential commodities like olive oil experienced historic price drops, partially offsetting broader trends. Monthly data further highlight seasonal shifts: prices rose for clothing and fresh fruits, while tourism and fuel costs declined, illustrating the complex interplay between seasonal demand and energy market dynamics. This report is particularly valuable for the open data community, as it underscores the importance of verifying official statistics against preliminary estimates, given that the final data from the National Statistics Institute (INE) diverged from initial forecasts. It also highlights the value of granular, sector-specific data in understanding nuanced economic shifts, such as the distinct performance of food versus energy prices. By accessing and analyzing these detailed breakdowns, researchers and developers can better model economic behaviors, assess the impact of policy measures, and provide more accurate tools for tracking the real-world effects of inflation on consumers.
