The article argues that Finsbury Park is being quietly repurposed into a commercial venue, prioritizing the council’s financial interests over public welfare. This shift results in the steady decline of public facilities, with repairs delayed or ignored outside of major event seasons, effectively treating the space as a liability rather than a communal asset. This exploitation highlights a failure of local governance, where the council acts more like a proprietor than a trustee. The author contends that municipal authorities have proven unfit to manage public spaces responsibly, necessitating their removal from direct control to ensure true public benefit and prevent further degradation of the environment for commercial gain. Relevance to open_data lies in the demand for transparency regarding how public assets are managed and monetized. The case underscores the need for open, accessible data on council spending, contract details, and maintenance schedules to hold authorities accountable. Without such visibility, citizens cannot verify claims of trust or challenge decisions that prioritize revenue over community well-being, making data openness crucial for civic oversight.
