The State of Open Data report 2023: Support for researchers still lacking

The eighth State of Open Data report reveals a persistent gap in infrastructure, with nearly three-quarters of researchers lacking the necessary support to share data openly. This deficit highlights a critical need for the research community to collaborate more effectively and develop robust tools that facilitate sustainable open science practices. Without adequate assistance, the transition to open data remains hindered by systemic logistical challenges, preventing many from fully participating in the open research ecosystem. Credit recognition remains a significant barrier, as a majority of researchers feel undervalued for their contributions, an issue that has persisted for eight years. Additionally, the study emphasizes that a nuanced, global approach is required for data management, as attitudes and requirements vary significantly across regions and disciplines. Contrary to common assumptions, career stage does not dictate openness, suggesting that current outreach efforts are insufficiently inclusive and must be broadened to engage all levels of the academic community. Finally, while awareness of artificial intelligence in data processing is growing, it has not yet translated into practical application. This finding is relevant to open data because it identifies an emerging opportunity to leverage AI for managing and sharing research outputs more efficiently. Understanding these motivations and technological gaps is essential for stakeholders to design policies and tools that truly support accessibility, usability, and the reusability of research data globally.
