Here's how Supreme Court performed after CJP Isa took over

The Supreme Court of Pakistan has published its inaugural quarterly report, marking a significant step toward judicial transparency under the constitutional right to information. By disclosing detailed metrics on case disposal and pendency, the court aims to fulfill its duty to serve the public better and address historical criticisms regarding the lack of accessible data. This move establishes a precedent for openness, suggesting that the judiciary is committed to demystifying its operations and providing citizens with clear insights into how the legal system functions. The report highlights a notable reduction in pending cases during the last quarter, reversing a decade-long trend of increasing backlog. Although the total number of pending cases has risen significantly over recent years, the court emphasizes that this recent decline demonstrates improved efficiency. By openly sharing these statistics, the Supreme Court allows for public verification of its performance, thereby enhancing accountability and rebuilding trust with a populace that has long been concerned about judicial delays. Furthermore, the document details landmark rulings on critical national issues, including the constitutionality of the Practice and Procedure Act, general election timelines, and the nullification of civilian trials in military courts. These decisions underscore the court’s role in upholding democratic principles and individual rights. This transparency is crucial for open data initiatives, as it proves that judicial outcomes and procedural histories are becoming increasingly accessible, enabling civil society to engage in informed discourse and hold institutions to higher standards of accountability.
