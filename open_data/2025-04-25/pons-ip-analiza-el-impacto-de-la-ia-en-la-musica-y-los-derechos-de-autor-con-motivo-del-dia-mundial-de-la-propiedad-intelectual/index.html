PONS IP analiza el impacto de la IA en la música y los derechos de autor con motivo del Día Mundial de la Propiedad Intelectual

PONS IP analiza el impacto de la IA en la música y los derechos de autor con motivo del Día Mundial de la Propiedad Intelectual

The article highlights a critical tension in the music industry, where rapid advancements in artificial intelligence challenge existing intellectual property frameworks. Experts argue that current regulations, such as the European AI Act, fail to adequately protect creators, potentially leading to significant income losses and insufficient compensation for the use of human-generated works in training AI models. This regulatory lag creates an urgent need for a cultural and legal shift that prioritizes the protection of creative rights amidst technological disruption. A key conclusion is the necessity to adapt intellectual property laws to ensure fair remuneration and transparency when algorithms use copyrighted material. The discussion underscores that while technology offers new opportunities for visibility, it must not come at the expense of creators' ability to control and profit from their work. Strengthening legal mechanisms is presented as essential to maintain a balanced ecosystem where artists can assert their rights against platforms and AI developers without stifling innovation. This topic is highly relevant to open data because it addresses the provenance and licensing of data used in machine learning. Just as music copyright requires clear attribution and compensation, open data initiatives must establish robust frameworks for how datasets are collected, shared, and reused. Ensuring that AI models respect data ownership and ethical guidelines is fundamental to maintaining trust and sustainability in the broader open data and AI landscapes.
