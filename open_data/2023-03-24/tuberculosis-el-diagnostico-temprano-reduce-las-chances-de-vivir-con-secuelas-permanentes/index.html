Tuberculosis: el diagnóstico temprano reduce las chances de vivir con secuelas permanentes

Tuberculosis represents a serious global public health threat, having experienced an increase in morbidity and mortality after years of decline. This resurgence underscores the urgency of improving detection systems, as delayed diagnosis not only compromises individual health but also facilitates community transmission and the development of drug resistance, drastically complicating treatment. It is crucial to ensure universal access to rapid and sensitive diagnostic methods capable of identifying not only infection but also drug resistance. Implementing these technologies enables timely and effective therapy initiation, reducing the chain of transmission and preventing the disease from progressing to multidrug-resistant forms, which require more costly and complex interventions. This article is relevant to the open data movement because it highlights the need for transparency in epidemiological information to combat misinformation and stigma. Access to accurate and up-to-date data allows authorities to design evidence-based health policies, while clear communication of the medical reality helps educate the population, encouraging early care-seeking and treatment adherence.
