Hate crimes in Saskatchewan have increased since pandemic, Statistics Canada reports | Globalnews.ca

Hate crime incidents in Canada have surged significantly since the onset of the pandemic, with Saskatchewan experiencing a particularly sharp rise. This trend highlights how health crises can exacerbate existing social tensions and discrimination, leading to increased hostility and targeted violence against marginalized groups. The data reveals that attacks targeting race, ethnicity, and religion drove much of this upward trajectory. While some specific categories showed slight fluctuations, the overall pattern indicates a systemic deterioration in social cohesion and safety for vulnerable populations across major urban centers. This report is crucial for open data advocates as it demonstrates the value of transparent, standardized government statistics. By making such detailed records publicly accessible, researchers and policymakers can identify emerging patterns of discrimination, hold institutions accountable, and develop evidence-based strategies to combat rising societal prejudice.
