Tomgram: Andrea Mazzarino, Anger and the MAGA Movement

The article critiques the United States’ massive national security expenditure, highlighting the paradox that despite spending more than the next nine countries combined, the military has failed to secure safety or win significant conflicts since the 9/11 attacks. This financial focus has coincided with a dramatic increase in global terrorism and domestic instability, suggesting that the current defense strategy is not only ineffective but potentially counterproductive to national well-being. Simultaneously, the author identifies a deepening crisis of social isolation and loneliness, particularly among rural and older populations, which correlates with rising anger and political polarization. The narrative argues that this societal fracture is exacerbated by a culture that prioritizes militarization over human connection, leaving many citizens feeling alienated and unsafe. The personal anecdotes provided illustrate how fear and isolation drive individuals toward extreme behaviors, further fragmenting the social fabric. This analysis is crucial for open data initiatives because it underscores the importance of measuring social well-being and community health with the same rigor as defense spending. By exposing the gap between vast military budgets and deteriorating social outcomes, the article advocates for data-driven policy shifts that prioritize addressing the root causes of insecurity, such as loneliness and alienation, rather than continuing to fund ineffective military interventions.
