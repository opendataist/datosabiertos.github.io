Internet Archive Fails to Dismiss Record Labels' Copyright Lawsuit * TorrentFreak

The ongoing legal battle between the Internet Archive and major music labels highlights the tension between digital preservation efforts and copyright enforcement. The Archive’s “Great 78 Project” aims to save decaying audio recordings for future generations, asserting that this work constitutes fair use. However, record labels argue that unauthorized copying deprives artists of compensation and undermines the commercial value of music, seeking substantial damages for the widespread distribution of these protected works. A recent court ruling denied the Internet Archive’s motion to dismiss the lawsuit, rejecting the defense that the claims were barred by the statute of limitations. Although the Archive pointed to an earlier industry warning letter, the judge determined that the document lacked specificity regarding particular recordings. Consequently, the court found it premature to dismiss the case, allowing the litigation to proceed to address the merits of the alleged infringments and the validity of fair use defenses. This case is critically relevant to the open_data community because it challenges the legal framework surrounding the long-term preservation of public and historical information. It underscores the risks institutions face when digitizing and sharing cultural heritage without explicit permission, potentially setting a precedent that could restrict access to historical data. The outcome will likely influence how organizations balance their missions to preserve human knowledge against strict copyright obligations, impacting the sustainability of open digital archives worldwide.
