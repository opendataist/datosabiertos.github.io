Peligro en puerta: ¿Son seguros controles de accesos biométricos?

The growing deployment of biometric controls in residential and corporate buildings poses significant challenges for the protection of personal data, as legislation classifies this information as sensitive. The relevance to open data lies in the need for transparency and ethical governance when handling data that reveal physical, ethnic, or health-related characteristics, requiring organizations to prioritize the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of information under strict information security principles. It is essential to ensure that consent is explicit and that non-biometric access alternatives are always available, safeguarding user autonomy against technological coercion. This requirement for multiple options and informed consent reinforces the accountability of data-handling entities, promoting more inclusive systems that respect digital rights, which is crucial for building trust in both public and private infrastructure. Furthermore, the vulnerability to cyberattacks and biometric data theft underscores the urgency of protecting devices with robust software and restricting access to sensitive information solely to authorized personnel. By implementing these best practices, serious risks such as identity theft and unauthorized access are mitigated, demonstrating that technical security must go hand in hand with clear communication and respect for privacy to prevent incidents that could affect both the physical and digital security of the community.
