The Open Source Initiative is launching global workshops to define what constitutes "open source" artificial intelligence, addressing concerns that traditional software licenses do not adequately cover the unique nature of machine learning models and datasets. This initiative aims to establish clear standards that promote transparency, collaboration, and permissionless innovation, ensuring stakeholders maintain agency over emerging technologies. The effort highlights a critical tension in the tech industry: while many AI systems are released under open licenses, the legal terminology of existing open source definitions often fails to accurately describe the structure and usage of AI assets. By soliciting public input on a draft definition, the OSI seeks to adapt core principles to this new domain, recognizing that AI requires a distinct framework to ensure the original intent of open access is preserved. This development is highly relevant to open_data as it establishes precedents for how shared resources and collaborative standards can apply to non-traditional software artifacts. The outcome will likely influence how datasets and trained models are governed, impacting the broader ecosystem of open information. Ultimately, it underscores the urgent need to evolve governance frameworks to match technological realities, ensuring that openness remains viable in the age of artificial intelligence.
