Billionaire real estate investor Frank McCourt wants to buy TikTok — but he's not interested in the algorithm

Frank McCourt is assembling a consortium to acquire TikTok’s US business, aiming to transform the platform into an open-source model. This initiative, driven by Project Liberty, prioritizes user data control and privacy over the proprietary algorithmic features that currently define the app’s success. By rejecting the core recommendation engine, the bid seeks to establish a new architectural standard for social media that places authority back in the hands of creators and users. This approach highlights a critical shift in how social platforms manage user information, directly impacting open data principles. McCourt’s vision challenges the prevailing closed-loop data economy by advocating for transparency and decentralization. If successful, this model could serve as a blueprint for future digital ecosystems, demonstrating how open standards can coexist with viral social networks while protecting individual privacy rights. The relevance to open data lies in the potential to democratize access to the mechanisms that shape online behavior. By proposing a transparent, user-centric infrastructure, the bid offers an alternative to the opaque algorithms currently dominating the digital landscape. This effort underscores the growing demand for ethical data practices and highlights how open-source methodologies can provide a viable path toward more accountable and equitable social media platforms.
