Ordenan a Pemex informar sobre agua contaminada en BJ

The article highlights that Pemex is competent to provide data on water contamination, rejecting its initial claim of incompetence. This ruling underscores the operator’s responsibility to address public concerns about polluted water, demonstrating that state-owned enterprises must actively respond to transparency demands regarding environmental risks in residential areas. It asserts that access to clean water is a constitutional human right, requiring the state to guarantee safety and transparency. By linking public health to legal obligations, the text emphasizes that withholding such data violates citizens’ rights, thereby reinforcing the imperative for open data policies in the environmental and public health sectors. Finally, a judicial suspension allows the Mexico City government to release previously restricted information. This development is relevant to open data, as it illustrates how legal mechanisms can enforce transparency, ensuring that critical environmental information remains accessible to the public and promotes accountability in government operations.
