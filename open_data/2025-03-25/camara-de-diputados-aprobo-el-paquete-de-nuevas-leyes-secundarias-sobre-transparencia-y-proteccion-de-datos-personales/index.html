Cámara de Diputados aprobó el paquete de nuevas leyes secundarias sobre transparencia y protección de datos personales

Cámara de Diputados aprobó el paquete de nuevas leyes secundarias sobre transparencia y protección de datos personales

The Mexican Chamber of Deputies has approved significant legislation that harmonizes transparency and personal data protection with constitutional principles. This approval establishes new general laws governing access to public information and data protection for public entities, alongside reforms to private data protection. By consolidating these frameworks, the state aims to standardize the rules and procedures governing access to information, ensuring that citizens’ rights are uniformly protected across all levels of government. A key implication of this legislative shift is the restructuring of oversight authorities. The traditional National Transparency System is being replaced to create a more cohesive administrative body focused on transparency and anti-corruption. This reorganization distributes responsibilities among various control organs and defines clear competencies for handling data requests and enforcing privacy regulations, thereby strengthening institutional capacity to safeguard citizens’ information rights against arbitrary interference. This development is crucial for open data advocates because it legally mandates higher standards for how public institutions handle and release information. By aligning transparency laws with privacy protections, the legislation seeks to balance the right to know with individuals’ right to control their personal data. This framework encourages the responsible sharing of non-sensitive public data while establishing robust safeguards, which is essential for fostering a transparent society where open data can be used ethically and effectively to promote civic engagement and accountability.
