La minifalda de Vox, por Isabel Garcia Pagan

La minifalda de Vox, por Isabel Garcia Pagan

The article challenges the narrative propagated by Vox regarding the alleged "Islamization" of Terrassa, highlighting that such claims rely on anecdotal observations rather than concrete data. By focusing on superficial cultural shifts, such as clothing choices, political actors attempt to frame social diversity as a security threat, despite the absence of public statistics supporting these fears. This approach prioritizes emotional resonance over factual accuracy, creating a distorted perception of local realities. Crucially, the text demonstrates that actual crime rates and immigration statistics in Terrassa contradict the urgency and scale portrayed by right-wing rhetoric. While Vox amplifies stories of gang violence and radicalization to gain media traction, objective indicators show decreasing criminality and immigration levels below the national average. This discrepancy reveals that the political strategy is not based on empirical evidence but on the strategic selection of isolated incidents to justify a broader ideological agenda. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the danger of allowing political narratives to replace transparent, accessible statistical information. When reliable data is ignored in favor of sensationalized claims, public discourse suffers, and citizens lose the ability to make informed decisions based on reality. Ensuring the availability and prominence of open data is essential to counter misinformation and maintain an informed democracy, proving that facts must anchor political debates.
