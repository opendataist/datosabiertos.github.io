DOGE Keeps Trying to Dodge the Freedom of Information Act. So We’re Suing.

DOGE Keeps Trying to Dodge the Freedom of Information Act. So We’re Suing.

The Intercept has filed a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit against the Trump administration to force the Department of Government Efficiency to disclose records, challenging the agency’s claim that it is exempt from federal transparency laws. This legal action is critical for open data because it confronts the administrative strategy of using executive orders to bypass statutory accountability. By refusing to release communications and operational data, the administration creates a significant barrier to public scrutiny, undermining the fundamental principle that government activities should be accessible and verifiable by citizens and the press. Federal judges have increasingly ruled that DOGE functions as an agency subject to FOIA and other statutes, noting the administration’s inconsistent legal stance. Courts have identified substantial independent authority being exercised by DOGE, contradicting claims that it is merely an advisory body without formal power. These judicial findings highlight a dangerous precedent where the government selectively applies legal definitions to avoid oversight, which directly impacts the integrity of public information systems and the enforceability of data access rights. The relevance to open data extends beyond this specific case, as it establishes whether newly created entities can evade transparency requirements. If DOGE is deemed an agency, it must adhere to rigorous disclosure standards, setting a vital precedent for future government operations involving private sector collaborators. This lawsuit ensures that mechanisms for data access remain robust, preventing the establishment of opaque structures that hinder democratic accountability and public trust in governmental efficiency initiatives.
