EL CALLAO - El Imparcial de Oaxaca

EL CALLAO - El Imparcial de Oaxaca

The most significant development for open data is the dismantling of the INAI, Mexico’s autonomous transparency oversight body. This independent institute, which successfully uncovered major corruption cases, has been replaced by a subordinate system under the Anti-Corruption Secretariat. This structural shift fundamentally weakens institutional checks and balances, threatening the independence essential for effective access to information and accountability. It represents a regression in the country’s transparency framework, prioritizing political control over the unrestricted flow of public information. Parallel efforts aim to centralize data management and surveillance. The government is advancing constitutional reforms to digitalize all administrative procedures and establishing new national security databases. While presented as tools for efficiency, these systems consolidate vast amounts of citizen and institutional data under executive control. Simultaneously, legislation to remove legislative immunity and the creation of new intelligence registries suggest a broader strategy to minimize obstacles to state power, potentially undermining the traditional safeguards that protect data integrity and individual rights. This centralization of data power is critical for open data advocates because it redefines transparency as a state-managed function rather than a citizen’s right. By merging anti-corruption oversight with security intelligence and administrative digitalization, the state risks creating opaque structures where access to information depends on political alignment. The erosion of the INAI demonstrates how institutional design directly impacts the availability and trustworthiness of public data, making this consolidation of authority a pivotal concern for democratic transparency and data governance.
