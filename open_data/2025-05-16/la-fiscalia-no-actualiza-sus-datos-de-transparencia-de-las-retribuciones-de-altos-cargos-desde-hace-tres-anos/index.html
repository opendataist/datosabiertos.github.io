La Fiscalía no actualiza sus datos de transparencia de las retribuciones de altos cargos desde hace tres años

La Fiscalía no actualiza sus datos de transparencia de las retribuciones de altos cargos desde hace tres años

The article highlights a critical irony: state institutions, currently embroiled in legal controversies over the protection of private data, fail to uphold basic transparency standards themselves. The Public Prosecutor’s Office is criticized for leaving its transparency portal stagnant for years, thereby obscuring crucial financial information about high-ranking officials. This failure to update the portal violates legal mandates designed to ensure accountability and public trust in governmental operations. Similarly, major political entities such as the PSOE are noted for maintaining incomplete or inactive transparency sections, particularly regarding the salaries of their top leaders. Despite legislative requirements for periodic updates, these portals remain effectively frozen, preventing citizens from accessing vital data on institutional remuneration and potential conflicts of interest. These omissions persist even when factual changes in leadership or legislation occur, indicating a systemic disregard for open governance principles. This situation is highly relevant to open data, as it demonstrates how public institutions can actively hinder the flow of essential public information. When entities that enforce legal transparency simultaneously neglect their own obligations, it undermines the fundamental premise of an open society. True open data requires not merely the existence of portals, but the active, accurate, and timely release of information, ensuring that transparency laws are applied universally rather than selectively.
