Public Information Officer Not Legally Bound To Start Investigative Process U/S 7 Of RTI Act: Kerala High Court

Public Information Officer Not Legally Bound To Start Investigative Process U/S 7 Of RTI Act: Kerala High Court

The court ruled that a Public Information Officer is not legally obligated to investigate document authenticity when processing Right to Information requests, provided they follow statutory procedures. It clarified that without specific allegations of forgery, such officers lack the power or duty to initiate independent inquiries, protecting them from negligence claims based on isolated incidents. This decision affirms that due process in appointment approvals cannot be overturned by minor procedural disputes unrelated to the core selection criteria. By rejecting the withdrawal of appointment based on alleged past negligence, the judgment strengthens procedural fairness and prevents administrative retaliation against officials acting within their statutory mandates. For open data enthusiasts, this case underscores the boundaries of information disclosure duties. It highlights that transparency initiatives require clear definitions of official responsibilities to prevent abuse. Understanding these legal limits ensures that open data frameworks respect the practical constraints of information officers while maintaining accountability, fostering a more robust and legally sound approach to public information accessibility.
