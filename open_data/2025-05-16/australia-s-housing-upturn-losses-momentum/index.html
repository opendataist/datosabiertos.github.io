Australia's housing upturn "losses momentum"

Australia's housing upturn "losses momentum"

Australian housing finance shows its first quarterly decline since early 2023, signaling a significant loss of momentum despite recent interest rate cuts. Owner-occupier, investor, and first-home buyer lending have all slowed, leading to moderated price growth and smaller average loan sizes across major cities. This deceleration suggests that the previous housing upturn is fading, with weak construction loans indicating that recent approval gains may not sustain. This slowdown highlights critical affordability barriers that continue to suppress market activity even as monetary policy eases. While financial markets anticipate further rate reductions to improve repayment capacity, the current landscape reveals a sector lacking clear direction. The interplay between falling lending volumes and stagnant growth underscores the fragility of the current recovery phase. Relevance to open_data lies in the availability of granular ABS housing finance metrics and Cotality’s dwelling value indices. These publicly accessible datasets allow analysts to track real-time lending trends and price dynamics transparently. By providing detailed quarterly breakdowns, such open resources empower researchers and the public to monitor economic shifts and policy impacts accurately.
