Work commute rising in Canada as more people going into office - National | Globalnews.ca

Canadian work commutes have surged past pre-pandemic levels, signaling a significant shift back to in-office employment. Although the return is partly driven by rapid population growth and overall employment increases, the data highlights a substantial decline in remote work arrangements. This resurgence of on-site labor demonstrates that many sectors are prioritizing physical presence, reshaping daily routines and challenging the entrenched hybrid models established during the pandemic era. The mode of transportation reveals a heavy reliance on private vehicles, which dominates the commute landscape despite economic pressures like high car prices and supply shortages. While public transit use has seen a notable rise, active commuting methods such as walking or cycling have slightly declined. This vehicle-centric trend contributes to longer travel times and increased congestion, illustrating how current infrastructure and commuting habits are struggling to adapt to the renewed volume of daily office-bound workers. By making this granular data publicly available, transparency initiatives allow researchers, urban planners, and policymakers to analyze emerging trends in labor mobility. Such access enables evidence-based decision-making regarding transportation infrastructure and housing policies, ensuring that public services evolve in alignment with the actual behaviors and needs of the population.
