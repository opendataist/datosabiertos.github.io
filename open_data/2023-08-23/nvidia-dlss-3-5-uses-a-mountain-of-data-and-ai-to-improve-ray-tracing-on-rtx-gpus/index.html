NVIDIA DLSS 3.5 uses a mountain of data and AI to improve ray tracing on RTX GPUs

NVIDIA’s announcement of DLSS 3.5 marks a significant evolution in AI-driven graphics processing, primarily through its new Ray Reconstruction feature. By leveraging substantially more training data, this technology fundamentally enhances the quality of ray-traced images, delivering crisper reflections and more accurate global illumination. This advancement demonstrates how deep learning algorithms can now solve complex rendering problems that traditional methods struggle with, setting a new benchmark for visual fidelity in real-time applications. The relevance to open data lies in the underlying data ecosystems that make such AI models viable. The massive datasets used to train these neural networks are critical infrastructure for modern graphics, highlighting the importance of accessible, high-quality data sources. As developers and creators increasingly rely on these AI upscaling techniques, the need for transparent, standardized, and potentially open datasets for training and benchmarking becomes evident. This shift drives demand for better data governance and sharing practices within the tech industry to ensure equitable access and innovation. Ultimately, DLSS 3.5 extends beyond gaming to empower creative professionals in real-time rendering workflows. Its integration into cloud platforms like GeForce NOW and professional tools signals a broader industry trend toward AI-augmented productivity. For the open data community, this underscores the growing intersection of proprietary AI technologies and public data resources. It encourages ongoing dialogue about how open datasets can support the development of more efficient, accurate, and accessible AI tools for both entertainment and professional creation, fostering a more collaborative technological landscape.
