La Comunitat Valenciana se posiciona como la región con más viviendas vacías de España

The recent publication of Spain’s population and housing census, after a decade-long gap, reveals a significant increase in vacant properties, highlighting a critical disconnect between housing supply and active market availability. While the total number of empty homes has risen, the percentage of owners with vacant units has slightly decreased, suggesting that most individuals still use their property as a primary residence. This shift underscores the urgency of addressing housing inefficiencies to better align stock with demand, particularly in regions like the Valencian Community, where vacancy rates remain disproportionately high. Owners predominantly maintain empty homes due to structural uncertainties rather than speculative hoarding. The primary drivers are the need for rehabilitation works and unresolved inheritance issues, followed by fears of tenant defaults or property damage. These concerns stem from a lack of legal security, leading many landlords to prefer inactivity over the risks associated with renting. Consequently, policies that incentivize rehabilitation and provide greater legal protection are viewed as essential to unlock these dormant assets and reintegrate them into the rental market. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how granular, publicly accessible statistics can reveal the underlying causes of social and economic inefficiencies. By moving beyond aggregate figures to analyze owner motivations and regional disparities, open datasets enable policymakers and researchers to design targeted interventions. Understanding the specific barriers to housing availability allows for more effective governance, ensuring that statistical transparency directly contributes to solving real-world challenges in housing security and urban planning.
