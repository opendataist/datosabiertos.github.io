Appellate court narrows FOIA exemption for police investigations

The Arkansas Court of Appeals issued a significant ruling that limits law enforcement’s ability to broadly withhold records under the "undisclosed investigation" exemption. By rejecting the argument that investigations remain open until a trial concludes, the court established that the status of an ongoing probe is a specific question of fact requiring individualized review. This shifts the burden from automatic denial to a case-by-case assessment, ensuring agencies cannot hide behind vague definitions of ongoing proceedings to block public access. This decision is crucial for open_data because it demands rigorous, record-specific scrutiny rather than blanket exemptions. The court clarified that the exemption applies only to internal investigative work product, not all documents related to a person with pending charges. Consequently, data advocates can now challenge withholding practices more effectively, forcing agencies to justify why specific records qualify for secrecy. This promotes greater transparency by preventing the indefinite suppression of information simply because legal proceedings have not yet finished. Ultimately, this ruling empowers citizens to access critical information about police actions, such as officer identities in sensitive incidents, much earlier than before. It sets a precedent that transparency should not be delayed indefinitely by administrative arguments about trial dates. For open_data initiatives, this means data holders must now prove the investigatory nature of each document, fostering a culture where accountability and public interest outweigh procedural delays.
