Las multas por circular en patinete por vías interurbanas aumentan más de un 70 % en Cataluña

Las multas por circular en patinete por vías interurbanas aumentan más de un 70 % en Cataluña

This article highlights critical traffic regulations for Personal Mobility Vehicles (PMVs) in Spain, emphasizing that these devices are prohibited on interurban roads, highways, and sidewalks. Enforcement campaigns reveal a rising trend of illegal usage, posing significant safety risks to riders. By clarifying these restrictions and the associated fines, the piece underscores the urgent need for better regulatory compliance and safer urban planning to protect vulnerable road users. The text also addresses the ongoing debate regarding helmet usage and age restrictions. While national law permits helmet mandates, they remain non-mandatory at the federal level, although local municipalities have already implemented stricter rules. Furthermore, minors under the age of sixteen are banned from operating these vehicles, with legal liability falling on their parents. These varying local ordinances create a fragmented legal landscape that confuses users and complicates enforcement efforts across different regions. This discussion is relevant to open data because it illustrates the complexity of aggregating fragmented regulatory information. Understanding how local ordinances diverge from national guidelines requires transparent, accessible datasets on municipal regulations and enforcement statistics. Open data initiatives can help visualize these disparities, improving public awareness and aiding policymakers in creating more cohesive, data-driven traffic safety strategies that accurately reflect local realities and enforcement trends.
