Entra en vigor en NY la Ley de Transparencia Salarial: he aquí lo que debes saber

New York’s new law mandates the disclosure of salary ranges in all job postings, promotions, and transfers, with the aim of closing economic inequality gaps. By requiring employers to honestly define the limits of their budgets before publishing a vacancy, the regulation seeks to eliminate the opacity that has historically favored wage discrimination. This measure goes beyond simply publishing data; it establishes a transparency standard that empowers candidates during labor negotiations. The impact of this regulation extends across multiple formats of job advertisements and categories of workers, covering both full-time employees and independent contractors, regardless of whether the work is performed on-site or remotely. By including employment agencies and excluding only certain specific temporary help firms, the law creates a more equitable information ecosystem. This ensures that applicants receive clear financial data from the first point of contact, reducing information asymmetry between employers and candidates. This legislative evolution is fundamental to the open data movement, as it demonstrates how policy can transform labor market structures through the standardization of information. By requiring organizations to publish structured and verifiable salary data, it encourages the creation of large public datasets that enable the analysis of compensation trends and the detection of systemic biases. This initiative serves as a precedent for other jurisdictions to implement similar frameworks, promoting corporate accountability and public access to critical information about working conditions.
