C-LM registró la quinta tasa más alta de demandas de disolución matrimonial en 2023

In 2023, Spain experienced a broad decline in marital dissolution proceedings, with overall demand falling by three percent compared to the previous year. This decrease was consistent across all procedural types, including consensual and non-consensual divorces, separations, and nullities. Regional analysis revealed significant disparities, with the Basque Country, Castilla y León, and Madrid recording the lowest incidence rates per capita, while other autonomous communities showed markedly higher figures, indicating varying legal and social dynamics across the national territory. At the same time, the landscape of post-separation legal adjustments showed divergent trends depending on parental status. Demand for modifying measures in existing marriages generally fell, reflecting a slight cooling in ongoing disputes among married couples. Conversely, there was a notable increase in demands concerning custody, care, and support for children born outside of marriage, both in consensual and contested cases, highlighting growing complexity in family law structures beyond traditional marriage. These statistics are crucial for open data initiatives, as they provide transparent, granular insights into societal shifts regarding family structures and legal engagement. By making such detailed judicial metrics accessible, stakeholders can analyze the impact of family law on public life, monitor regional legal burdens, and support evidence-based policy decisions that address the evolving needs of modern families.
