Bright Simons chases Fidelity Bank for KMA’s ‘missing’ GH¢3.6 Million - The Herald ghana

This article highlights a critical intersection between public accountability and financial transparency, illustrating how Right to Information mechanisms can challenge opaque banking practices involving public funds. By demanding full disclosure from a private bank that manages state-related resources, the case underscores the necessity of treating institutions handling public money as quasi-public entities subject to open data mandates. The core dispute revolves around significant discrepancies in the disbursement and accounting of loan facilities for a major urban redevelopment project. While the bank asserts that no funds are missing and claims full repayment of the approved facility, local authorities allege that substantial sums were disbursed beyond the authorized amount without clear documentation. This conflict emphasizes the importance of accessible, detailed financial records to verify claims and prevent potential misappropriation of resources intended for community development. Relevance to open data lies in the legal argument that commercial secrecy cannot shield transactions involving public interest. The lawyers’ insistence on treating the bank as a relevant body under information access laws challenges the barrier between private banking confidentiality and public right to know. Ultimately, this case advocates for a framework where financial interactions involving government bodies are transparent, ensuring that taxpayers can trace the use of their funds and hold all parties, including private partners, accountable for their actions.
