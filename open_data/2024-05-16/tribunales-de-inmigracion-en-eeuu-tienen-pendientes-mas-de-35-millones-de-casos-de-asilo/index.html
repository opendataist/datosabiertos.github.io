Tribunales de inmigración en EEUU tienen pendientes más de 3,5 millones de casos de asilo

The massive backlog of asylum cases in the United States highlights a critical strain on the judicial system, where courts cannot keep pace with the influx of new petitions. This delay creates prolonged legal uncertainty for millions of migrants, while the low rate of legal representation during deportation proceedings raises significant concerns about due process and fairness within the immigration framework. Simultaneously, the U.S. government has intensified efforts to curb irregular migration through diplomatic pressure and targeted restrictions on entities facilitating unauthorized crossings. By imposing visa bans on certain transport company executives and collaborating with regional governments to regularize migration paths, officials aim to dismantle exploitative networks. These measures reflect a broader strategy to reduce irregular flows by addressing root causes and holding private actors accountable for profiting from vulnerable populations. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, publicly accessible datasets to monitor migration trends and policy impacts. Reliable data compiled from public records enables civil society and researchers to verify official claims, assess the efficiency of legal processes, and advocate for systemic reforms. Without open access to such information, it remains difficult to hold institutions accountable or to develop evidence-based solutions that effectively protect migrant rights.
