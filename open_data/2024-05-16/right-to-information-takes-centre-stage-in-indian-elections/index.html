Right to information takes centre stage in Indian elections

The Indian Supreme Court’s landmark decision to strike down the Electoral Bonds Scheme serves as a powerful vindication of the Right to Information Act. By ruling that anonymous political donations violate citizens’ rights to information and fair elections, the court affirmed that transparency in political financing is essential for a functioning democracy. This verdict not only invalidated a mechanism designed to conceal corporate influence but also restored public confidence in the judiciary as a guardian of constitutional rights against state overreach. The ruling has triggered the disclosure of vast sums of money exchanged between corporations and political parties, revealing significant potential for corruption and quid pro quo arrangements. These revelations demonstrate how opacity in funding can undermine the level playing field among political entities and allow illicit lobbying to flourish unchecked. The exposure of these financial flows underscores the critical role of data transparency in identifying misconduct and holding both state and non-state actors accountable for their influence on public policy. For Bangladesh, this Indian experience highlights the transformative potential of robust Right to Information laws when actively utilized by civil society and the judiciary. The case illustrates that strong legal frameworks alone are insufficient; they require active enforcement and judicial interpretation to effectively check corruption and protect democratic integrity. Consequently, this article is relevant to open data advocates as it provides a compelling model for how transparency initiatives can disrupt opaque power structures and empower citizens to demand greater accountability in governance.
