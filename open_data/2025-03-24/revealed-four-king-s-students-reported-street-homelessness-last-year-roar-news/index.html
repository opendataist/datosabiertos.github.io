REVEALED: Four King's Students Reported Street Homelessness Last Year - Roar News

REVEALED: Four King's Students Reported Street Homelessness Last Year - Roar News

The investigation reveals a critical housing crisis at King’s College London, where rising private rents and insufficient student loans are driving students toward homelessness. Despite small absolute numbers, the upward trend in rough sleeping highlights a systemic failure to protect vulnerable students from financial insecurity. This situation underscores the urgent need for universities to address the gap between state funding and actual living costs, which increasingly forces students into unsafe housing or street homelessness. Student welfare is significantly compromised by restrictive support eligibility and a lack of institutional empathy. Many students spend the majority of their maintenance loans on rent, relying on overdrafts to survive, while reporting inadequate assistance from university housing teams. The disparity in accommodation costs between subsidized options and the private market exacerbates this inequality, leaving low-income and postgraduate students disproportionately affected. This indicates that current support structures are reactive rather than preventive, failing to provide stable living conditions necessary for academic success. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how Freedom of Information requests can expose hidden societal issues within educational institutions. By making specific metrics on homelessness and hardship funding transparent, the investigation drives accountability and informs policy debates regarding student welfare. It highlights the power of data transparency in revealing the real-world implications of financial policies, encouraging broader discussions on how universities should utilize and share data to improve equity and access for all students.
