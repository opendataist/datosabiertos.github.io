The resurgence of inflation in Mexico at the end of 2023, driven by rising food and service prices, has solidified a macroeconomic context characterized by persistent upward pressure on prices. This phenomenon has exceeded market expectations and kept the inflation rate above the institutional target for more than two years, prompting analysts to project a more gradual slowdown for the following period. The situation reflects the complexity of balancing purchasing power against global external shocks, suggesting that while the disinflation process continues, monetary policies must be implemented with caution. The relevance of this article to the field of open data lies in the critical need for transparent and up-to-date economic information. For analysts, citizens, and private sector actors to correctly interpret indicators such as core and non-core inflation, it is essential to have access to high-quality open datasets published by institutions like the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI). Opacity or delays in releasing detailed component-level data hinder public scrutiny and reduce the efficiency of investment and consumption decisions based on empirical evidence. Furthermore, the illustrative case of salmonella in melons demonstrates how transparency in health and agri-food data is vital for consumer confidence and food safety. When health authorities and companies publish analysis results in an accessible and verifiable manner, it contributes to accountability. Therefore, promoting a culture of open data not only enhances economic understanding but also strengthens public health governance, enabling more agile and evidence-based responses to international health crises.
