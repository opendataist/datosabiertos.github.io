Amnezia launches 'Amnezia Pulse' — a live map that tracks internet shutdowns as they happen

Amnezia VPN has publicly launched Amnezia Pulse, an open data initiative that tracks real-time internet disruptions, slowdowns, and whitelist restrictions. By utilizing anonymized traffic data from their own servers, the tool provides a live map of network stability, primarily focusing on Russia but with expanding potential for global coverage. This shift from an internal monitoring tool to a public resource highlights the growing importance of independent, infrastructure-level data in understanding digital censorship. The platform operates by establishing baselines of normal traffic patterns to automatically flag significant outages, eliminating the need for user-reported incidents. This method offers immediate clarity for individuals distinguishing between local technical failures and state-imposed shutdowns. Furthermore, the planned open API for researchers and human rights organizations creates a new dataset for analyzing censorship trends, fostering transparency and enabling technical experts to study the mechanics of internet control more effectively. This release is crucial for the open data community as it democratizes access to critical internet freedom metrics that were previously proprietary. By providing verified, aggregated data on connectivity issues, Amnezia Pulse supports global efforts to document and resist digital authoritarianism. It underscores how open access to network performance data can empower civil society, offering a vital layer of accountability and evidence for advocates working to protect online rights and ensure uninterrupted digital connectivity worldwide.
