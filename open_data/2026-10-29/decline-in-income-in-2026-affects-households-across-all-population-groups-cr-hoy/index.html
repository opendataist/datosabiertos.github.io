Decline in income in 2026 affects households across all population groups | CR Hoy

Decline in income in 2026 affects households across all population groups | CR Hoy

The National Household Survey reveals that the decline in income across all Costa Rican households is intrinsically linked to the drop in employment, affecting all five income quintiles. This widespread decline indicates that the health of the labor market is the primary determinant of household economic well-being, beyond the specific distribution of resources. The demographic and economic structure shows that lower-income households account for a larger share of the total population, while higher-income households concentrate the majority of national income, although this concentration has decreased slightly. This dynamic demonstrates that vulnerabilities are not confined to a single sector; rather, the reduction in employment impacts the purchasing power of society as a whole, particularly among segments with lower financial resilience. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how official open statistics enable tracking of the causal correlation between macroeconomic indicators, such as employment, and the microeconomic well-being of households. Transparency in these data facilitates the identification of structural inequalities and the formulation of evidence-based public policies, allowing citizens and analysts to audit the effectiveness of state interventions in response to labor crises.
