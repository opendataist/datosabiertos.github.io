Millions of free graves, but Mexico's cemeteries have run out of space

Millions of free graves, but Mexico's cemeteries have run out of space

Although national data indicate widespread availability of burial plots in Mexico, this capacity is unevenly distributed, leading to critical saturation in many urban areas. The geographic distance between rural surpluses and urban demand prevents the problem from being resolved through aggregated statistics, forcing families to face high costs and a lack of local alternatives for burying their loved ones. This spatial imbalance requires urgent territorial planning that goes beyond the construction of new cemeteries. Authorities must manage not only physical space but also the economic sustainability of funeral services and legal clarity regarding usage rights. Evidence shows that current infrastructure is at its limit, highlighting the need to integrate precise data on actual plot occupancy to make informed and fair decisions. The relevance to open data lies in the need to move beyond aggregated figures toward disaggregated and georeferenced information. Only through access to open data on the exact location, maintenance status, and costs of each burial plot can civil society and governments identify real gaps and promote transparent management. The visibility of these data is crucial to guarantee the right to a dignified farewell and prevent the management of memory from becoming a privilege with limited access.
