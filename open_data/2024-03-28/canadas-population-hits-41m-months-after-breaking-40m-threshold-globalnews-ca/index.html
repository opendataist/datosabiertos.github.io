Canada’s population hits 41M months after breaking 40M threshold | Globalnews.ca

Canada’s population has recently surpassed 41 million, marking its fastest annual growth rate in over six decades. This surge is primarily driven by a significant influx of temporary immigrants, which accounts for the majority of the increase. While experts note that these new residents fill critical labor market gaps and bring high levels of skill, the rapid expansion has intensified pressure on housing supply and infrastructure, prompting the government to establish stricter targets to ensure sustainable growth and alleviate cost-of-living challenges. Simultaneously, there is a notable shift in internal migration patterns within the country. Alberta has experienced the largest net gain in interprovincial migration in decades, reversing previous trends and attracting residents from other provinces. Conversely, Ontario and British Columbia have seen substantial population losses as residents move elsewhere. This redistribution highlights changing regional dynamics, with Atlantic provinces also seeing modest gains, reflecting broader economic and lifestyle preferences influencing where Canadians choose to live. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical role of official statistical agencies in providing timely, transparent, and granular demographic insights. By releasing live population trackers and detailed migration reports, governments enable researchers, economists, and policymakers to analyze trends, identify emerging issues like housing stress, and make evidence-based decisions. The availability of such open data is essential for monitoring the social and economic impacts of population changes and for fostering accountability in public policy formulation.
