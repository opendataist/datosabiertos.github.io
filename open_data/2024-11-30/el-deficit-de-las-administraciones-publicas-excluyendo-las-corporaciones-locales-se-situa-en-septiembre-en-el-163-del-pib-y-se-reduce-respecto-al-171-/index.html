El déficit de las Administraciones Públicas, excluyendo las Corporaciones Locales, se sitúa en septiembre en el 1,63% del PIB y se reduce respecto al 1,71% del mismo periodo de 2023

The article reveals a structural shift in Spanish public finances, where deficit figures are heavily influenced by accounting adjustments rather than solely by primary economic activity. Specifically, the state’s deficit is significantly affected by the final settlements of the 2022 financing system, which were completed much later than in previous years. This highlights a critical challenge in open data: financial statistics often reflect administrative timing and retrospective corrections. Without transparent access to these underlying accounting mechanisms, raw deficit numbers can be misleading, obscuring the true fiscal health and introducing noise into economic analysis. Revenue growth is driven by increased tax collections and social security contributions, indicating some degree of economic recovery, while expenditure expands due to salary adjustments and higher pension payments. The interplay between the central administration, autonomous communities, and social security funds demonstrates a complex web of transfers. For open data advocates, this underscores the necessity of granular, interconnected datasets that link these entities. Policymakers and citizens need to see not just the net deficit, but the flow of resources between different levels of government to understand who is bearing the fiscal burden and how revenue is distributed. Ultimately, the relevance to open data lies in the demand for methodological clarity and historical comparability. When large portions of financial results are explained by one-off accounting events—such as delayed settlements or changes in mechanisms like the Intergenerational Equity Mechanism—longitudinal analysis becomes complicated. Open data initiatives must prioritize explaining these contextual factors alongside the raw numbers. This ensures that stakeholders can distinguish between genuine structural improvements and statistical artifacts, fostering more accurate public debate and better-informed economic decisions.
