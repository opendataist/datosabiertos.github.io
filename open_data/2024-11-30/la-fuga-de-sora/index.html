La fuga de Sora

Artists selected for Sora’s closed beta have leaked their model to Hugging Face, complaining that they felt exploited rather than valued as creative partners. This direct action reflects a serious rift in the relationship between AI developers and artistic communities, where a lack of transparency and a perceived mercantilist treatment have led to a breach of confidentiality. The incident has forced OpenAI to temporarily suspend access, highlighting the inherent risks of closed betas when participants do not feel respected or secure in the evaluation process. This conflict underscores the critical importance of ethical governance and communication in the development of disruptive technologies. When companies fail to legitimately acknowledge concerns about labor or creative impact, they create a breeding ground for distrust and resistance. The loyalty of evaluators depends on an implicit social contract based on mutual respect; without it, even legal agreements cannot guarantee the stability of a launch, as the tool’s reputation becomes tarnished in the eyes of a critical mass of dissatisfied users. For the open data community, this case is relevant because it illustrates how opacity and a lack of dialogue undermine trust in digital tools—a fundamental pillar for open collaboration. Data and model management is not only technical but also relational: the sustainability of these platforms depends on actively integrating affected communities, prioritizing ethics over deployment speed. Ignoring these social dynamics jeopardizes long-term adoption and the legitimacy of artificial intelligence, reminding us that open access must be accompanied by responsible and transparent management.
