Finding skilled labour, input costs reported as pressing obstacles for N.S. businesses: StatCan | CBC News

Finding skilled labour, input costs reported as pressing obstacles for N.S. businesses: StatCan | CBC News

Nova Scotia businesses anticipate that securing skilled labor and managing rising input costs will be their primary hurdles in the near future. This dual pressure creates significant operational challenges, particularly in construction where skill shortages lead to delays and reduced productivity. Simultaneously, sectors like agriculture and hospitality struggle with escalating expenses for materials such as fertilizers and packaging, driven by global supply chain dynamics and increased demand for e-commerce logistics. These economic pressures are not merely temporary inconveniences but structural issues affecting industry viability. When input costs rise continuously, businesses face thinning profit margins, forcing some to operate at a loss or break even. The scarcity of trained workers further exacerbates these financial strains by slowing production and increasing overhead, making it difficult for companies to maintain competitiveness and sustainable growth in a challenging market environment. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical need for accessible, real-time economic indicators. Publicly available statistics on labor shortages and cost inflation enable researchers, policymakers, and private sector analysts to identify trends, forecast risks, and develop targeted interventions. By understanding these specific regional challenges through open datasets, stakeholders can better allocate resources and design programs that address the root causes of economic stagnation and workforce gaps.
