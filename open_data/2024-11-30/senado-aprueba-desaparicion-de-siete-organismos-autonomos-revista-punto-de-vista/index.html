Senado aprueba desaparición de siete organismos autónomos | Revista Punto de Vista

Senado aprueba desaparición de siete organismos autónomos | Revista Punto de Vista

The dissolution of key autonomous bodies in Mexico, such as the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), centralizes their functions within government secretariats. This restructuring, justified by the ruling party as a measure to improve administrative efficiency, concentrates state power and eliminates independent oversight mechanisms, sparking intense debate about the country’s democratic sustainability. The elimination of entities responsible for transparency and public policy evaluation poses a significant risk to accountability. By transferring these competencies to executive branches, external oversight capacity is weakened, potentially facilitating opacity in public management and hindering the detection of irregularities or corruption, thereby limiting citizen scrutiny of government actions. This reform is particularly relevant to open data, as effective transparency depends on the independence of data and its public accessibility. Without autonomous bodies ensuring the integrity and availability of information, the open data ecosystem is compromised, undermining the ability of civil society and researchers to rely on unmanipulated, trustworthy government information for the public good.
