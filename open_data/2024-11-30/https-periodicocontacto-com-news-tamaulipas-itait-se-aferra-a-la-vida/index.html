The Tamaulipas Transparency Institute faces imminent dissolution but continues to operate while awaiting legislative approval of its budget. Its leadership advocates for dialogue to preserve the agency, emphasizing its critical role in ensuring transparency at the state level, even as parallel national autonomous bodies cease operations. This situation highlights the fragility of Mexico’s open data infrastructure, where legal uncertainties threaten institutional continuity. The institute’s ongoing processing of review requests and complaints demonstrates that the demand for public information remains active, regardless of administrative instability or impending structural changes. The article underscores the vulnerability of open data ecosystems during political transitions. It reveals how the potential loss of autonomy and funding could undermine citizens’ access to information, serving as a cautionary example of the risks posed to transparency mechanisms when legal frameworks fail to protect these essential democratic institutions from abrupt dismantling.
