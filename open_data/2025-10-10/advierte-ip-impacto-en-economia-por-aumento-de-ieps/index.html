Advierte IP impacto en economía por aumento de IEPS

Advierte IP impacto en economía por aumento de IEPS

Business leaders and industry associations strongly reject the federal government’s 2026 revenue proposal, arguing that increasing taxes on sugary drinks, tobacco, and digital platforms will not achieve public health goals. Instead, these measures are expected to devastate small businesses, trigger severe liquidity crises for SMEs, and disproportionately impact the informal economy, thereby exacerbating poverty rather than alleviating it. The core concern is that steep tax hikes will fuel the expansion of illicit markets currently controlled by organized crime, particularly in the tobacco sector. Stakeholders emphasize that raising prices without addressing broader dietary habits or alternative revenue sources is irrational, warning that such policies would drive legal operations underground. This shift would undermine formal economic activity, reduce tax collection in the long term, and harm consumers by eliminating affordable legal options. This article is crucial for open data advocates because it highlights the tension between opaque fiscal enforcement and digital transparency. The proposed real-time access to digital platforms’ operations raises significant privacy and constitutional concerns, illustrating how lack of clear, open regulatory frameworks can lead to disproportionate surveillance. Understanding these dynamics is essential for promoting policies that balance revenue needs with data protection, transparency, and the sustainability of the digital economy.
