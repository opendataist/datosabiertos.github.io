Canadian music industry leaders ask government for copyright protections against AI | CBC Music

Canadian music industry leaders ask government for copyright protections against AI | CBC Music

The music industry urges the government to treat unauthorized generative AI training as copyright infringement, aiming to establish a robust licensing framework. This regulatory push seeks to transition the sector from an unregulated phase to a structured market where creators receive fair compensation and attribution. Key demands include mandatory transparency regarding data ingestion, ensuring rights holders can identify and negotiate for their protected works. Without disclosure, creators cannot verify usage or participate effectively in the emerging economic landscape, leaving them vulnerable to uncompensated exploitation by technology firms. This movement is vital for open_data governance, as it highlights the urgent need for ethical data sourcing and intellectual property respect. By advocating for licensing regimes similar to those in digital streaming, the industry promotes a sustainable model where AI development coexists with, rather than exploits, human creative output.
