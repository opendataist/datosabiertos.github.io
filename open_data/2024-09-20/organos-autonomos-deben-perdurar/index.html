Órganos autónomos deben perdurar

Órganos autónomos deben perdurar

The article warns that proposed constitutional reforms to abolish Mexico’s Autonomous Constitutional Organs threaten democratic stability and institutional integrity. These bodies, such as the electoral and central banking institutes, operate independently to ensure fair elections, economic stability, and transparency. Removing this autonomy exposes critical functions to political interference, risking a regression in recent democratic advancements and undermining public trust in essential state services. Relevant to open data, these institutions generate the independent statistical and informational backbone necessary for evidence-based policymaking. Entities like INEGI and INAI guarantee that data remains objective, accessible, and protected from partisan manipulation. If centralized or eliminated, the quality and reliability of this public information would degrade, compromising the ability to evaluate policies effectively and limiting citizen access to truthful, unbiased records. Ultimately, preserving these organs is vital for combating corruption and inequality through robust, independent oversight. Strengthening their resources and autonomy ensures that institutions serve society rather than political interests. This highlights the open data principle that independent, high-quality public information is foundational for accountability and informed citizenship, making the protection of these data-generating bodies crucial for a healthy democracy.
