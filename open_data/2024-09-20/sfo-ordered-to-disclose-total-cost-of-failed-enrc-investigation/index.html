SFO ordered to disclose total cost of failed ENRC investigation

SFO ordered to disclose total cost of failed ENRC investigation

The First-Tier Tribunal has ordered the Serious Fraud Office (SFO) to disclose the total cost of its investigation into Eurasian Natural Resources Corporation (ENRC). Despite the SFO’s refusal based on potential prejudice to law enforcement, the tribunal ruled that the strong public interest in transparency outweighs the exemption. This decision mandates disclosure within 42 days, highlighting that government bodies cannot automatically withhold financial data from serious criminal probes without rigorous justification. This ruling carries significant weight for open data advocacy, as it challenges the tendency of investigative agencies to use national security or crime prevention arguments to obscure spending details. By forcing the release of these costs, the judgment reinforces the principle that public funds warrant public scrutiny, even when investigations conclude without prosecution. It sets a precedent that specific evidence of harm is required to justify secrecy, rather than relying on generic claims of operational risk. The context involves a contentious decade-long inquiry that was dropped due to insufficient evidence, amidst broader legal battles concerning the SFO’s conduct. With previous judgments noting the SFO’s active role in potential wrongdoing, this transparency order serves as a critical check on institutional power. It demonstrates how open data mechanisms can enforce accountability within powerful regulatory bodies, ensuring that the public understands the financial scale of failed or controversial state investigations.
