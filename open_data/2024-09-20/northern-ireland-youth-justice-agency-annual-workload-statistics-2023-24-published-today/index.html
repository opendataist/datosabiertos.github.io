Northern Ireland Youth Justice Agency Annual Workload Statistics 2023/24 Published Today

The Department of Justice released comprehensive workload statistics for the Northern Ireland Youth Justice Agency, offering a detailed analysis of trends in youth justice services over the past five years. This publication standardizes data across the eleven Local Government Districts, ensuring consistency for annual comparisons and facilitating integration with other datasets like police crime records. By including demographic breakdowns and new referral scheme details, the report supports a deeper understanding of youth justice dynamics and demographic disparities. Key findings highlight a significant rise in referrals to Youth Justice Services, driven largely by diversionary programs, while custody numbers and total custody days decreased. Males continue to represent the majority of referrals and those in custody, with Belfast recording the highest volume of activity. These shifting trends suggest evolving engagement patterns within the justice system, emphasizing the need for monitoring how different interventions affect various demographic groups and regions. This data is crucial for the open data community as it exemplifies transparent public sector reporting on sensitive social issues. By making detailed statistics on youth justice accessible, it enables independent researchers and policymakers to analyze systemic inequalities, evaluate the effectiveness of rehabilitation versus punitive measures, and hold agencies accountable. Open access to such granular, longitudinal data fosters evidence-based discussions on youth welfare and judicial efficiency.
