LinkedIn is scraping your data to train AI — here’s how to opt-out

LinkedIn is scraping your data to train AI — here’s how to opt-out

LinkedIn has integrated numerous AI tools to enhance user experience for job seekers and recruiters, signaling a broader industry trend where tech platforms leverage artificial intelligence to remain competitive. While these features offer practical benefits, their implementation has exposed significant gaps in corporate transparency regarding data usage. The core issue lies in the automated collection of profile information for AI training without explicit prior consent, which has led to widespread user backlash and feelings of being blindsided by the company's data practices. This situation highlights critical concerns about digital privacy and the lack of informed consent in social media data management. Although users can manually opt out of future data sharing for generative AI improvements, the initial non-transparent collection has eroded trust. The incident serves as a stark reminder for users to scrutinize platform terms and actively manage their privacy settings, as passive engagement often allows companies to utilize personal information in ways that may not align with individual expectations or values. The article is highly relevant to open_data because it underscores the tension between corporate data exploitation and user sovereignty. It demonstrates how "open" access to user-generated content for AI development can occur without adequate user agency, challenging the principles of ethical data stewardship. Furthermore, the mention of GDPR protections for EU citizens illustrates how regulatory frameworks can successfully restrict such data practices, offering a potential model for how open data ecosystems might balance innovation with stringent privacy standards and user rights.
