Release information to public, it’s their right - RTI tells public officers

The Right to Information Commission emphasizes that accessing information is a fundamental constitutional right, obligating public officers to release data unless it falls under strict legal exemptions. This mandate ensures that transparency is maintained in governance, as withholding information without legal justification is unlawful. By clarifying these obligations, the Commission reinforces the principle that public records belong to the citizenry, thereby establishing a clear framework for accountability where officials must comply with legitimate requests or face regulatory consequences. Enforcement mechanisms are increasingly robust, with the Commission empowered to impose financial penalties on institutions that refuse compliance. Recent actions, including significant fines against government bodies, demonstrate a tangible commitment to protecting this right and deterring future violations. These sanctions serve as a critical deterrent, ensuring that the promise of transparency is not merely theoretical but is backed by real administrative and financial repercussions for non-compliance. This active enforcement shifts the dynamic from passive availability to active protection of citizen access. For open data initiatives, this development is highly relevant as it establishes a legal backbone for data availability and institutional accountability. The push to decentralize commission operations further democratizes access, making it easier for citizens to exercise their rights across all regions. Ultimately, this strengthens the ecosystem for open data by creating a culture where transparency is enforced, corruption is deterred, and citizens are empowered to demand accountability through established legal channels.
