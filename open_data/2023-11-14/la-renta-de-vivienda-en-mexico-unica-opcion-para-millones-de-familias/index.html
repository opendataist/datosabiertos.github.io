La renta de vivienda en México, única opción para millones de familias

The article highlights a significant disparity between official statistics and the actual size of Mexico's rental housing market, suggesting that the sector is much larger than government data indicates. This growth is driven by rapid urbanization and soaring home purchase prices, which force more people to rent. Consequently, the rental sector generates a substantial economic impact, contributing millions to the national GDP, while bank financing for home purchases declines. The relevance to open data lies in the critical need for accurate, granular datasets that reflect real-world behaviors rather than relying solely on traditional censuses. Official figures often lag behind dynamic market shifts, leading to underestimations of demand and economic activity. To support informed policy-making and investment, open data initiatives must incorporate real-time indicators and alternative data sources that capture the true scale of the rental economy, especially in high-demand urban areas. Ultimately, the narrative argues that renting is a superior real estate investment due to its liquidity and steady returns. By acknowledging the limitations of current official metrics, stakeholders can better understand the sector's potential. Improved transparency and accessible data would allow investors and policymakers to recognize the rental market's true contribution to economic stability and growth, fostering a more robust and transparent real estate environment.
