The INAI’s advisory council has called for an investigation into former commissioner Óscar Guerra Ford, who is accused of using institutional funds for inappropriate expenditures at a venue linked to human trafficking. This revelation exposes a cover-up that has lasted nearly a decade, implicating current commissioners who authorized keeping the information hidden. President López Obrador criticized the institute as a mere simulation of transparency, thereby reinforcing public skepticism toward such government bodies. This incident is highly relevant to open data, as it demonstrates how institutions can actively suppress or obscure financial records, thereby preventing public accountability. It underscores the critical need for rigorous open data standards and real-time transparency to detect the misuse of public resources. Ultimately, it serves as a stark reminder that without accessible and truthful data, democratic oversight remains compromised.
