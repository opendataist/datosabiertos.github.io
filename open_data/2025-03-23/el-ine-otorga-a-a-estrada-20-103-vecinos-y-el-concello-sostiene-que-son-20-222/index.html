The article highlights inherent discrepancies in official demographic statistics, illustrating how differing data collection methods between national institutes and local governments can produce conflicting population figures. These inconsistencies compel local administrations to actively challenge and adjust reported numbers in order to accurately reflect their true resident population. Such inconsistencies underscore the challenges of standardizing data sources, revealing that high residential mobility significantly affects assessments of population stability. By comparing local census records with national estimates, the study exposes gaps that require thorough administrative review to finalize official demographic records. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical need for transparent, accessible, and harmonized datasets. It emphasizes that users must understand data provenance and methodological limitations when relying on public statistics, advocating for clearer communication among data providers to ensure reliability and accurate interpretation.
