Publican reglamento tras extinción del INAI y crean nuevo órgano de transparencia | Noticias de México | El Imparcial

Publican reglamento tras extinción del INAI y crean nuevo órgano de transparencia | Noticias de México | El Imparcial

Mexico has replaced the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) with a new transparency body under the Secretariat of the Interior, marking a significant shift in governance structures. This change introduces a new regulatory framework that centralizes authority while suspending certain procedures to facilitate the transition, ensuring continuity only for essential information requests. The reorganization aims to align transparency mechanisms with anti-corruption goals, transferring resources and redefining operational competencies. By establishing a single head with non-delegable powers, the government seeks to streamline oversight, although this consolidation raises concerns about the independence previously guaranteed by the former autonomous institute. This development is crucial for open data advocates because it fundamentally alters the institutional landscape of transparency in Mexico. The reduction of an independent watchdog to a subordinate secretariat impacts data accessibility and accountability, requiring citizens and developers to adapt to new bureaucratic pathways for obtaining public information.
