Indemnización legal para trabajadores del INAI, afirma Buenrostro

Indemnización legal para trabajadores del INAI, afirma Buenrostro

The dissolution of Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI) represents a significant shift in the country’s governance architecture, raising critical questions about the future of public accountability. This institutional extinction is not merely an administrative change but a structural overhaul that challenges existing models of data transparency and citizen oversight. The government has launched a transition plan that includes the creation of “Transparency for the People,” a new entity designed to decentralize information guarantees. By replacing the single centralized body with multiple independent entities, the state aims to restructure access to information. However, this reorganization involves reducing operational functions and reassigning personnel, creating uncertainty about the continuity and effectiveness of current transparency mechanisms. This development is highly relevant to open data, as it directly impacts the standardization, accessibility, and governance of public information. Historically, centralizing data oversight under a single independent body has ensured robust data protection and uniform standards. Fragmenting these responsibilities among various new institutions may complicate data interoperability and weaken the unified protection of personal data. The shift prioritizes administrative efficiency over institutional independence, potentially altering how open data ecosystems are regulated and maintained in Mexico.
