Cívicos cruceños anuncian que “cobrarán” al INE los resultados del Censo 2024

Cívicos cruceños anuncian que “cobrarán” al INE los resultados del Censo 2024

The National Institute of Statistics (INE) is facing intense scrutiny for failing to deliver the final results of the 2024 Census on time, despite prior commitments. Civic leaders in Santa Cruz argue that this delay constitutes a procedural failure and political maneuvering, claiming that the partial data previously released significantly undercounted their region’s population. This perceived injustice has fueled organized protests, with demonstrators demanding accountability from INE leadership and the release of the complete database by August. The situation highlights critical governance issues regarding transparency and public trust in official statistics. The government’s reliance on incomplete data raises concerns about how national planning and resource allocation are determined, particularly for the country’s most populous department. Citizens view the prolonged release as a strategic delay rather than a technical necessity, intensifying regional tensions and political dissatisfaction with the central administration. This delay is highly relevant to the open data movement, as it exemplifies the risks of non-transparent, incomplete, or delayed public data release. For open data to be effective, information must be timely, complete, and accessible to ensure democratic oversight. When statistical bodies withhold or slow down data, they undermine accountability and prevent stakeholders from verifying claims, demonstrating why strict adherence to open data principles is essential for maintaining public confidence and ensuring equitable representation.
