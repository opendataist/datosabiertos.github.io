CNN Made FOIA Request About DOGE—Only to Learn FOIA Staff Was Fired - Alaska Native News

The article highlights a concerning trend in federal transparency, illustrated when the Office of Personnel Management dismissed its privacy and FOIA processing staff. This action effectively blocked a journalist’s request for security clearance details regarding Elon Musk and his associates, suggesting that reducing personnel dedicated to public records directly impedes accountability. By eliminating the teams responsible for ensuring legal compliance and managing public information, the agency limits outside access to sensitive government records. This move mirrors strategies employed by Musk at Twitter, where media relations and privacy functions were similarly purged to control narratives and data access. The parallel indicates a deliberate shift away from institutional safeguards that protect public trust. When agencies lack dedicated staff to handle privacy and freedom of information requests, they become less accountable to the public, undermining the democratic principle that government operations should remain open and scrutinized. The incident is critical to open data advocates because it demonstrates how administrative restructuring can be used to obscure information rather than share it. It underscores the vital importance of maintaining independent privacy and FOIA teams within federal agencies. Without these dedicated resources, the mechanism for public transparency is weakened, allowing those in power to restrict access to records with minimal oversight, thereby eroding the foundational commitment to open government.
