County OKs release of more info on airport probe

County OKs release of more info on airport probe

The Delta County Board recently partial released documents requested by former Airport Manager Andrea Nummilien, balancing transparency demands with legal privileges. While most records were disclosed, a subset remained withheld under attorney-client privilege, sparking a debate over the limits of confidentiality in government investigations. This split decision highlights the ongoing tension between public accountability and legal protection during internal probes. The controversy centers on an investigation into Nummilien’s activities, which she claims was retaliatory for her prior harassment complaints. Commisioner Christine Williams strongly argued that the Freedom of Information Act mandates full disclosure of official acts to ensure democratic participation. She emphasized that public officials and employees must be held accountable, asserting that the public’s right to know supersedes efforts to shield internal communications from scrutiny. This case is highly relevant to open data and transparency advocates because it illustrates the practical challenges of implementing access laws when legal exemptions are invoked. It underscores how subjective interpretations of privilege can hinder the flow of public information. The ongoing struggle to define what must be shared versus what can be hidden serves as a critical example of the barriers that still exist in achieving complete government openness.
