INEI: 100,000 jóvenes perdieron su trabajo en 2024

INEI: 100,000 jóvenes perdieron su trabajo en 2024

Youth employment in Peru has significantly declined, with a substantial number of young people losing their jobs last year. This downturn is compounded by extremely high rates of labor informality and a severe mismatch between academic training and market demands. Consequently, many graduates struggle to find relevant work, creating significant barriers to professional stability and economic development for this demographic. This situation highlights critical gaps in current policy effectiveness. While government initiatives aim to facilitate labor insertion through internships and technical training, their impact is limited by poor oversight and prevalent abuses. The persistence of unemployment despite these efforts indicates that existing measures are insufficient to address the multifactorial causes of youth joblessness, such as lack of experience and educational misalignment. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, high-quality statistical information to drive effective public policy. Accessible data on youth employment trends, informality rates, and educational outcomes allows for better accountability and informed decision-making. By analyzing such datasets, stakeholders can identify systemic failures and advocate for reforms that bridge the gap between education, labor markets, and government interventions.
