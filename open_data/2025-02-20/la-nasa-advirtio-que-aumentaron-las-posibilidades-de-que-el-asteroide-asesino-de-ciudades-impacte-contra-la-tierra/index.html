La Nasa advirtió que aumentaron las posibilidades de que el asteroide “asesino de ciudades” impacte contra la Tierra

The recent update regarding asteroid 2024 YR4 highlights a significant shift in planetary risk assessment, raising the probability of a potential impact in 2032 to approximately 3%. While this figure marks the highest probability recorded for a near-Earth object in modern history, experts emphasize that immediate alarm is unnecessary. The probability is expected to fluctuate before likely dropping to zero as more observational data is gathered. This situation underscores the importance of robust, continuous monitoring systems in identifying and tracking potential hazards before they escalate into crises. The relevance of this event to open data lies in the critical role of transparent, accessible scientific information in managing public perception and coordinating global responses. The asteroid’s trajectory and characteristics are determined by publicly available observations from multiple international entities, including the James Webb Space Telescope. By making orbital mechanics and impact probabilities accessible to the scientific community and the public, authorities can demystify the risk. This openness allows for independent verification and fosters trust, demonstrating how shared data infrastructure is essential for distinguishing between genuine threats and statistical anomalies in planetary defense. Ultimately, the case of 2024 YR4 serves as a practical test for international collaboration and technological preparedness. Although classified as a "city killer" due to its potential localized devastation, the event provides ample time for mitigation strategies, such as deflection missions, to be planned and executed. The transparency of data regarding the asteroid’s path enables global agencies to align their efforts and prepare appropriate countermeasures. This demonstrates that open access to astronomical data is not merely an academic exercise but a vital component of modern planetary security, ensuring that humanity is informed and prepared for potential extraterrestrial challenges.
