La Nación / IPS niega acceso a información pública de Lilian Samaniego

The article highlights a significant conflict between transparency laws and privacy regulations in Uruguay, where the Social Security Institute (IPS) denied access to the employment history of former official and current Senator Lilian Samaniego. By classifying these records as personal data, the IPS relied on credit protection laws rather than transparency statutes, asserting that disclosure requires the senator's explicit written consent. This interpretation contradicts the views of several legal professionals who argue that public sector employment records should remain accessible to ensure accountability and openness. The core implication is the potential erosion of public oversight regarding government officials' professional conduct. The requested information included detailed histories of positions held, salaries, performance evaluations, and disciplinary records. The IPS’s refusal suggests a prioritization of individual privacy over the public’s right to know how public resources and authority have been used by elected representatives. This stance creates a legal gray area where sensitive information about public service can be shielded from view, potentially allowing questionable practices to go unexamined by citizens and media. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates because it illustrates the tension between data protection frameworks and the fundamental principles of governmental transparency. It underscores the urgent need for clear legal guidelines that define which public employee data must be routinely disclosed to prevent abuse of power. The involvement of oversight bodies to issue a binding judgment emphasizes that defining the boundaries of open data is critical for maintaining trust in democratic institutions and ensuring that transparency mechanisms function effectively against corporate or bureaucratic resistance.
