Inflación: este jueves se conoce el dato de noviembre y para las consultoras podría superar el 2,3%

Inflación: este jueves se conoce el dato de noviembre y para las consultoras podría superar el 2,3%

The upcoming release of November’s inflation data is critical for evaluating the Argentine government’s economic trajectory, as private forecasts suggest a continued acceleration rather than the expected stabilization. With indicators pointing to monthly increases between 2.3% and 2.5%, the government faces pressure regarding its inflation-control promises. This trend, already evident in Buenos Aires, implies that the initial momentum achieved earlier in the year is weakening, driven significantly by rising food costs and adjustments in regulated utilities. This development highlights the persistent challenge of "repressed inflation" stemming from delayed price adjustments in essential services. Although recent policy shifts aim to reduce subsidies and align energy and transport tariffs, the lingering effect of artificially low prices continues to distort market signals. The necessity of implementing structural reforms, such as the scheduled subsidy cuts for 2026, underscores the gap between official macroeconomic stability goals and the lived reality of rising household expenses. The relevance of this report to open data lies in the ongoing tension between official statistics and independent estimates. The significant divergence between INDEC’s methodology and the consensus among various economic consultancies illustrates the fragmentation of transparency in economic reporting. Analyzing these contrasting datasets is essential for understanding the true state of the economy, as reliance on a single official source may obscure the complex realities of price formation and the effectiveness of current fiscal policies.
