El reto del envejecimiento en España y el auge de la atención domiciliaria como modelo sostenible

El reto del envejecimiento en España y el auge de la atención domiciliaria como modelo sostenible

Spain is undergoing a profound demographic shift characterized by increasing longevity and declining birth rates, resulting in an aging population that challenges traditional healthcare and social systems. This reality forces a reevaluation of care models, highlighting the urgent need to adapt to a society where the majority of older adults wish to age in place. The transition from institutionalization to home-based care is no longer just a preference but a structural necessity to manage the growing number of elderly individuals, particularly those over eighty. The solution lies in the professionalization of domiciliary care, supported by technology and clinical coordination. Modern families often lack the time and expertise to handle complex medical needs and emotional support, leading to caregiver burnout. By integrating digital monitoring tools with human expertise, the sector can provide personalized, preventive care that maintains patient autonomy while reducing hospital admissions. This approach combines specialized training with technological advances to ensure safety and well-being without isolating the individual. This article is relevant to open data because the effectiveness of this new care model depends on the interoperability and sharing of health and social care information. To prevent complications and coordinate efficiently between families, primary care, and home services, data must flow seamlessly across different platforms. Open data principles enable the creation of connected ecosystems where real-time information can support decision-making, improve resource allocation, and ultimately allow for a more sustainable and human-centered care system for an aging population.
