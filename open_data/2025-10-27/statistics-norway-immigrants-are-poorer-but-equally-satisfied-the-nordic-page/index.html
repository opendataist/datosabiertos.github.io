Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions, including lower income, employment challenges, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness compared to the general population. Despite these objective disadvantages, they report satisfaction levels equal to native residents and demonstrate strong affiliation with their new home. This paradox highlights a complex resilience where material hardship does not diminish subjective well-being or national identity. A crucial finding is that immigrants exhibit higher confidence in key institutions, such as the political system, judiciary, and police, than the rest of the population. While interpersonal trust remains lower among immigrants, their robust faith in state structures suggests a unique social dynamic. This indicates that marginalized groups may rely more heavily on institutional support systems to navigate their lives, viewing the state as a stabilizing force despite personal struggles. This article is vital to open_data because it illustrates how transparent, comprehensive government surveys can reveal nuanced social realities that simple metrics might miss. By publishing detailed, disaggregated data on health, income, and trust, Statistics Norway enables researchers and policymakers to understand the disconnect between objective living standards and subjective satisfaction. Such open access to granular data fosters evidence-based policy interventions and promotes social cohesion by accurately representing diverse population experiences.
