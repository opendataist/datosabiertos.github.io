Una nueva era en la protección de datos personales en México

Una nueva era en la protección de datos personales en México

Mexico’s new Federal Law on the Protection of Personal Data Held by Private Parties reflects a significant institutional restructuring, following the dissolution of the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) and the transfer of its functions to the Secretariat for Anti-Corruption and Good Governance. This change not only alters the channels for oversight and dispute resolution but also redefines the balance of power between data subjects and organizations, requiring greater individual control over the use of their personal data. Among the most significant legal implications is the elimination of the possibility to process data for “compatible or analogous” purposes, which obligates organizations to obtain the data subject’s express authorization again for any change in purpose. Moreover, the regulatory framework strengthens the obligation to maintain confidentiality at every stage of the data processing chain and simplifies certain transparency requirements in privacy notices, although it retains the need for consent in most cases. For the open data ecosystem, this law is crucial because it establishes the boundary between privacy and accessibility. The absence of specific regulations on data portability and openness in the new law highlights the urgency for organizations to voluntarily adopt advanced international standards. This fosters an environment where proactive information governance enables a balance between privacy protection and interoperability and ethical data handling, which is essential for building trust in the digital era.
