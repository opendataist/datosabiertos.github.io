INDEC: el 42% de los trabajadores argentinos no está registrado

INDEC: el 42% de los trabajadores argentinos no está registrado

The recent INDEC report significantly expands labor market data by quantifying informal employment among non-salaried workers, moving beyond previous metrics that focused almost exclusively on employees. This shift provides a more comprehensive view of economic precariousness, revealing that nearly half of urban workers lack formal labor rights. Understanding this broader landscape is vital for open data initiatives, as it highlights the necessity of disaggregated datasets to accurately reflect the true structure of the national workforce and the vulnerability of diverse employment categories. Key disparities emerge when analyzing specific demographics and sectors, with young people and those in domestic service facing significantly higher rates of informality compared to older workers or those in the industrial sector. Educational attainment also serves as a strong protective factor, demonstrating a clear inverse relationship between higher education levels and informal employment conditions. These insights underscore how data can expose systemic inequalities, allowing policymakers and researchers to identify which groups are most excluded from social protections and how structural barriers persist across different age and education groups. This study is particularly relevant to open data because it democratizes access to information about informal work, a sector previously obscured by limited statistical frameworks. By making granular details about non-salaried workers publicly available, it fosters transparency and enables civil society to advocate for better labor regulations. Ultimately, such data serves as a critical tool for holding institutions accountable and driving evidence-based reforms aimed at reducing informality and promoting social equity in the labor market.
