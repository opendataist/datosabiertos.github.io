Keep promise of transparency

Michigan’s transparency remains critically low, with the governor’s office and legislature still exempt from Freedom of Information Act requirements despite repeated promises and legislative efforts. The state lags far behind national standards, undermining public accountability and fueling a crisis of trust in government institutions. This prolonged failure highlights a systemic gap in open governance, where elected officials operate without the same disclosure obligations as other state agencies. The core issue lies in the persistent refusal to remove statutory exemptions that shield these powerful branches from public scrutiny. Although multiple bills have passed one chamber with strong bipartisan support, they consistently stall in committee, reflecting deep-seated resistance to accountability. This stagnation occurs even amid high-profile scandals that have eroded citizen confidence, demonstrating that political will is insufficient without institutional commitment to open data principles. This situation is vital to open data advocates because it illustrates how legal exemptions can block essential public access to government records. Without these records, citizens cannot effectively monitor decision-making or hold officials accountable. Expanding FOIA coverage to all state branches is a fundamental step toward true transparency, ensuring that data about governance is freely available to the public rather than hidden behind bureaucratic barriers.
