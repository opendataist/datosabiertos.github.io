Maybe – transparency coming to state government

Michigan lawmakers appear poised to finally subject themselves and the governor to the same Freedom of Information Act requirements that already apply to other state agencies. This potential legislative change addresses a long-standing exemption that allowed the state’s most powerful officials to keep their records private, distinguishing Michigan from nearly every other state. Although the proposed bills include some limitations, such as protecting constituent communications, they represent a significant step toward transparency by mandating the disclosure of interactions with lobbyists. This shift ensures that high-level decision-making is more open to public scrutiny, thereby strengthening the accountability mechanisms that help prevent government misconduct. This development is highly relevant to open_data advocates because it demonstrates how legislative action can close critical loopholes in data access laws. By extending disclosure mandates to the executive and legislative branches, Michigan moves closer to a model where all government-generated data is accessible, ensuring that transparency guards apply uniformly to those with the greatest power.
