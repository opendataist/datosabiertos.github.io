Ni Madrid ni Barcelona: estas son las 3 provincias con más kilómetros de carreteras de España

The article reveals that Spain’s road network is managed through a fragmented administrative structure, with jurisdiction split among the central government, autonomous communities, and provincial councils. This division highlights how infrastructure ownership is decentralized, creating a complex landscape where local entities hold significant responsibility for regional connectivity. The distribution of mileage further illustrates that territorial size, rather than economic power, largely dictates road volume, challenging assumptions about Madrid and Barcelona’s dominance. These findings are particularly relevant to open data initiatives because they underscore the necessity of aggregating disparate datasets from multiple governance levels to achieve a comprehensive view. Without coordinated data sharing and standardized formats across these administrative bodies, creating a unified, accurate national map becomes challenging. The variance in nomenclature and management styles between regions emphasizes the need for interoperable data structures to bridge these informational gaps. Ultimately, the analysis demonstrates that understanding infrastructure requires navigating this multi-layered ownership model. For developers and researchers working with open data, the key implication is the importance of integrating heterogeneous sources. Recognizing these structural complexities allows for more robust data models that accurately reflect the reality of Spain’s diverse and divided road management system.
