Difunden más de 115 mil fotos de ciudadanos argentinos que fueron robadas del Renaper

The leak of more than 116,000 citizen photographs from Argentina’s National Registry of Persons highlights critical vulnerabilities in the protection of public personal data. This breach, facilitated by compromised credentials and potentially linked to prior unauthorized accesses by state entities, demonstrates how easily sensitive biometric information can be extracted and monetized. The incident underscores the fragility of current institutional security protocols and the ease with which bad actors can exploit government databases for illicit purposes. The implications of this data exposure are severe, as the photographs allow for the precise linkage of identities to faces, a key requirement for bypassing digital identity verification systems in the fintech sector. This facilitates serious cybercrimes such as identity theft and social engineering, posing a direct threat to financial security and individual privacy. The absence of watermarks on the files further enables their reuse across various platforms, amplifying the potential for long-term harm to the affected citizens and eroding trust in digital authentication mechanisms. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the urgent need for transparent governance and robust accountability when managing public information systems. It reveals the disconnect between existing cybersecurity recommendations and the actual enforcement of reporting obligations, leaving citizens unaware of the risks. Strengthening legal frameworks for data breach notification and fostering collaboration between cybersecurity and data protection authorities are essential to mitigate future threats and ensure that public data remains secure and trustworthy.
