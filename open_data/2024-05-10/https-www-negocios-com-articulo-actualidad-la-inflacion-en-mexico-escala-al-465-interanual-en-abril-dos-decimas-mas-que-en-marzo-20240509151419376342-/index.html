Mexico’s annual inflation rate rose to 4.65% in April, slightly exceeding expectations just before the central bank’s interest rate decision. This increase has prompted adjustments in monetary policy, with the central bank signaling further minor tweaks while maintaining a firm stance against inflationary pressures. Core inflation also declined on a monthly basis, presenting mixed signals across different economic sectors. Education and other services recorded the highest annual increases, underscoring persistent price pressures in specific areas despite an overall moderation in food and beverage costs. This data is relevant to open data initiatives because transparent, high-quality economic statistics are essential for independent verification and analysis. Open access to such indices enables researchers and citizens to objectively monitor inflation trends, fostering accountability and informed public discourse on the effectiveness of monetary policy.
