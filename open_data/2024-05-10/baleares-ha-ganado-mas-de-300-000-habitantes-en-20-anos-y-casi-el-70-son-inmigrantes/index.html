Baleares ha ganado más de 300.000 habitantes en 20 años y casi el 70 % son inmigrantes

The demographic landscape of the Balearic Islands has undergone a profound transformation, characterized by a massive population surge driven primarily by international migration. Over the past two decades, the region has expanded significantly, with the foreign-born population growing at a rate far exceeding that of the total resident count. This shift indicates that the islands are evolving from a locally stable community into a globally connected territory where international residents constitute a substantial and increasing portion of society. The growth is unevenly distributed across the archipelago, with smaller islands like Formentera experiencing exponential increases that disrupt local dynamics, while major hubs like Mallorca approach historical population thresholds. This demographic explosion is not merely a numerical increase but a structural change, as new arrivals reshape social fabrics and urban demands. The intensity of this influx suggests a permanent alteration in the islands' identity, moving away from historical patterns toward a model defined by continuous global mobility and diverse cultural inputs. This article is highly relevant to open data as it highlights the critical role of official statistics in monitoring complex societal shifts. By making granular migration and demographic data publicly accessible, institutions enable researchers, policymakers, and citizens to understand the implications of globalization on local communities. Such transparency fosters informed debates on urban planning, resource allocation, and social integration, demonstrating how open data serves as a vital tool for navigating the challenges and opportunities of modern demographic transformation.
