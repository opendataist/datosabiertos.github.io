‘The SML scandal will not die’ – Manasseh chides Akufo-Addo over KPMG report – - Ghanamma.com

The Ghanaian presidency’s refusal to release a full KPMG audit report on the SML scandal highlights a critical tension between government transparency and administrative secrecy. By classifying the document as exempt deliberative material, the administration prioritizes the protection of executive decision-making processes over public accountability. This stance raises significant concerns about the integrity of revenue mobilization agreements and suggests a potential effort to obscure controversial details from public scrutiny. This episode underscores the vital importance of Right to Information laws in maintaining democratic checks and balances. Activists argue that such restrictions echo historical patterns of opacity, contrasting sharply with past administrations that prioritized public access to sensitive audits. The rejection demonstrates how legal exemptions can be leveraged to withhold information, potentially undermining citizen trust and hindering effective oversight of public funds and government contracts. For open data advocates, this incident serves as a stark reminder that legal frameworks alone are insufficient without robust political will. It emphasizes the need to challenge broad exemptions that shield substantive findings from public view. Ensuring that audit results remain accessible is essential for fostering an environment where transparency drives accountability, allowing civil society to effectively monitor government actions and demand justice for potential corruption.
