Pobreza se eleva casi a niveles de pandemia: 29% de peruanos afectados, informó el INEI

The National Institute of Statistics and Informatics (INEI) confirmed that monetary poverty in Peru rose to 29% in 2023, reversing previous gains and returning to pre-pandemic levels. This increase, driven by a decline in real income, indicates that the country has regressed 13 years in its poverty reduction efforts. Experts warn that returning to pre-pandemic poverty rates will take more than two decades under current economic growth projections, highlighting a significant structural challenge in social welfare. The data reveal stark regional disparities, with departments heavily populated by indigenous communities, such as Cajamarca and Loreto, bearing the highest burdens, while areas like Ica and Arequipa show much lower rates. Urban poverty grew significantly, whereas rural areas saw a slight decline, although absolute numbers remain high. These geographic divides underscore the uneven impact of economic pressures and suggest that national averages mask deep-seated inequalities that targeted local interventions may need to address. This situation is critical for the open data community because it illustrates the vulnerability of statistical transparency to political pressure. The initial attempt by the executive branch to suppress the report before it was forced into the public domain serves as a stark reminder that data independence is essential for accountability. Open data advocates must prioritize the protection of statistical agencies from political interference to ensure that citizens have access to unbiased information necessary for informed public discourse and policy-making.
