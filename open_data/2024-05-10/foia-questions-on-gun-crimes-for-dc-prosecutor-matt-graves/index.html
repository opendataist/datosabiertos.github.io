FOIA Questions on Gun Crimes for DC Prosecutor Matt Graves

The article argues that U.S. Attorney Matthew Graves has fostered a culture of impunity in Washington, D.C., by strategically avoiding federal prosecution for gun crimes. By keeping cases in local Superior Court rather than utilizing federal jurisdiction, Graves enables lighter sentences and high rates of charge reductions. This policy choice is presented as a primary driver of the city’s rising homicide rate, as federal laws typically result in significantly longer prison terms for firearms offenses. The core issue lies in the disparity between local and federal judicial outcomes. Local prosecutors frequently decline charges, drop cases, or plea bargain felon-in-possession cases down to misdemeanors, allowing many armed criminals to avoid serious consequences. In contrast, federal courts consistently impose substantial prison sentences for similar crimes. The article contends that Graves’ office deliberately bypasses this stricter federal avenue, thereby undermining public safety and accountability through lenient charging practices. This narrative is crucial to open data advocates because it illustrates how fragmented and opaque criminal justice data can obscure systemic policy failures. The call for Freedom of Information Act requests highlights the necessity of transparent, granular data on charging decisions and court jurisdictions. Without such detailed public records, it is difficult to assess prosecutorial performance or hold officials accountable, demonstrating that open data is essential for verifying claims about public safety and justice system effectiveness.
