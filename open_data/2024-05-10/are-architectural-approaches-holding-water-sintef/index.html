Are architectural approaches holding water? - SINTEF

Research is urgently addressing how increasingly severe weather patterns impact housing infrastructure, specifically aiming to prevent moisture damage in buildings. By developing a comprehensive toolbox for climate adaptation, the project supports architects and the construction industry in predicting and mitigating these risks. This collaborative effort involves home developers, material suppliers, and academic institutions, ensuring that design choices are scientifically grounded rather than reliant on unverified assumptions about minimalist architecture. The initiative highlights a critical gap in current regulatory frameworks, where climate adaptation is assumed rather than explicitly mandated. Consequently, builders currently bear the burden of determining what constitutes adequate protection against intense rainfall. This research provides the necessary data and validation methods to clarify these standards, helping stakeholders transition from uncertainty to informed decision-making. It allows professionals to test specific design features, such as roof overhangs, under simulated extreme weather conditions to identify effective solutions. This work is highly relevant to the open_data community as it underscores the importance of accessible, standardized data in enabling robust public and private decision-making. By creating shareable procedures, checklists, and risk assessments, the project promotes transparency in building standards and insurance viability. Ultimately, it demonstrates how open, evidence-based tools can drive innovation, reduce financial risk, and ensure sustainable infrastructure that is resilient to the challenges of a changing climate.
