The book challenges the traditional, simplified narrative of Mexican political history by focusing on the unofficial operators who truly shaped power dynamics. Rather than centering solely on presidents or elected officials, it examines the behind-the-scenes architects—such as intelligence agents and political negotiators—who managed crises, built alliances, and controlled information. This perspective reveals that power in Mexico was not just exercised from official offices but was constructed through hidden agreements, silence, and unspoken codes within a complex authoritarian system. A crucial implication of this analysis is the persistence of these political practices in modern Mexico. Although institutions and parties have changed names and appearances, the underlying mechanisms of control, loyalty, and information management remain. The book argues that understanding current political realities requires recognizing how these historical structures survived transitions, demonstrating that the methods used to maintain stability and authority continue to influence present-day governance, regardless of superficial democratic reforms. This work is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical role of information control in political power. It highlights how much of history is determined by what remains secret, undocumented, or obscured by myth, rather than by transparent records. By distinguishing between verified facts and legendary narratives, the text advocates for the importance of accessing and verifying diverse sources. It serves as a reminder that true understanding of political systems often depends on uncovering the hidden, fragmented, and non-transparent data that official histories routinely omit.
