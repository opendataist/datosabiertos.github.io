Inflación liga 2 meses a la baja, en México; fue de 4.58% en septiembre

Mexico’s annual inflation rate moderated to 4.58 percent in September, marking a second consecutive monthly decline. This deceleration brings price pressures closer to the target range set by the central bank, signaling that monetary tightening measures are beginning to take effect without causing an economic contraction. The stabilization suggests a potential return to normalcy in consumer pricing dynamics, offering relief to households and businesses that have faced persistent cost increases over the previous year. The underlying trend shows that core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy items, has fallen to its lowest level since early 2021. This sustained reduction in underlying prices reflects improved supply chain conditions and effective transmission of monetary policy. Meanwhile, the significant drop in non-core inflation was primarily driven by sharp declines in fresh produce prices, highlighting the role of seasonal agricultural factors in smoothing overall consumer basket costs. This data is relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of transparent, granular, and timely statistical releases for public accountability. Reliable access to disaggregated price indices allows researchers, journalists, and civil society to verify government performance and analyze the real impact of inflation on specific demographics. Enhanced open data standards in national statistics empower stakeholders to hold institutions accountable and foster informed public debate on the effectiveness of economic policy.
