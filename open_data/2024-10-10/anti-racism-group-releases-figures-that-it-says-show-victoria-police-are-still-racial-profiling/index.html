Anti-racism group releases figures that it says show Victoria Police are still racial profiling

Research by the Centre Against Racial Profiling reveals that Victorian police are significantly more likely to search First Nations and minority individuals compared to white Australians. Although official bans exist, the absence of a government-led monitoring scheme has forced community groups to use Freedom of Information requests to uncover these disparities. This highlights a critical gap in transparency, as police data often lacks the necessary ethnic details required for official oversight, effectively shielding the institution from accountability. The study underscores the urgent need for independent, state-run monitoring bodies to detect and address systemic bias. Current deficiencies in data collection suggest that police are not adhering to mandatory reporting standards, which prevents accurate assessment of racial profiling. Without independent verification, it remains difficult to validate claims of equitable policing or hold agencies accountable, leaving marginalized communities vulnerable to discriminatory practices that lack robust external scrutiny. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how citizen-led data extraction can expose institutional opacity when official records are incomplete or biased. It illustrates the transformative power of FOI requests in democratizing access to information, allowing civil society to fill monitoring voids. Furthermore, it emphasizes that open data initiatives must prioritize data completeness and accuracy to be truly effective in driving social justice and policy reform.
