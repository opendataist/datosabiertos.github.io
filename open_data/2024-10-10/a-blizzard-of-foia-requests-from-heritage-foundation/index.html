A Blizzard of FOIA Requests from Heritage Foundation

A Blizzard of FOIA Requests from Heritage Foundation

The Heritage Foundation and its affiliates have adopted an aggressive strategy of filing thousands of Freedom of Information Act requests to map and potentially target federal civil servants. This approach, closely linked to the conservative Project 2025, seeks to identify career employees for potential removal under a future administration. By gathering detailed records on personnel communications and organizational structures, these groups aim to undermine job protections and dismantle the administrative state, raising significant concerns about the politicization of government oversight and the erosion of institutional stability. The relevance of this trend to open data lies in the transformation of FOIA from a tool for public accountability into a mechanism for political opposition research. While transparency initiatives typically serve to inform the citizenry and empower journalists, these requests are instead designed to facilitate partisan personnel changes. This shift demonstrates how open data infrastructure can be weaponized, turning legally accessible government records into weapons for ideological campaigns rather than sources of democratic transparency, thereby complicating the ethical use of public information. Furthermore, the use of affiliated entities and pseudonymous requesters to bypass scrutiny highlights vulnerabilities in current data tracking systems. The coordination between think tanks, news outlets, and oversight projects allows for large-scale data extraction that obscures the true actors behind the requests. This underscores the need for more robust oversight of how open data is aggregated and used, ensuring that the principles of transparency do not inadvertently enable the erosion of democratic norms or the targeting of public servants based on their political beliefs.
