APYMEC destaca el mes de julio que sitúa la compraventa de viviendas por encima del año pasado

The article reveals that while property transactions in Cuenca recently reached a five-year high, this surge is overwhelmingly driven by the resale of existing homes rather than new construction. This structural imbalance prevents the construction sector from truly revitalizing, as reliance on second-hand sales fails to stimulate the broader economic subsectors associated with new developments. Consequently, the current market dynamics do not translate into substantial job creation or long-term industrial growth. The core implication is that sustainable economic recovery requires a strategic shift toward new housing projects, including both protected and private developments. Without addressing the chronic shortage of new homes, the region cannot resolve its housing affordability crisis or attract younger populations. The dominance of the secondary market leaves critical needs unmet, highlighting that statistical gains in sales volume are insufficient indicators of sectoral health if they do not reflect actual construction activity. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how aggregated statistics can mask critical structural deficiencies. Open datasets must capture granular details, such as housing type and the nature of transactions, to reveal these nuances. Relying solely on high-level volume metrics risks misrepresenting the health of the sector, underscoring the need for transparent, detailed data to inform effective public policy and economic planning.
