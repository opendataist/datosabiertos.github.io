INAI y Órganos Estatales solicitan diálogo sobre el Plan C con Claudia Sheinbaum | Noticias de México | El Imparcial

Mexican transparency bodies, led by the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) and twenty-four state-level guarantor agencies, have formally requested an audience with incoming President Claudia Sheinbaum. They aim to defend the independence of autonomous institutions against the proposed “Plan C,” which seeks to dissolve these entities and absorb their functions into federal government departments. This move threatens the structural integrity of rights related to access to information and data protection, prompting an urgent call for dialogue to prevent the erosion of democratic safeguards. The core argument presented in their public letter emphasizes that transparent and accountable institutions are essential for protecting citizens’ rights and improving living conditions. These bodies argue that independence is not merely administrative but serves as a critical counterweight, ensuring that the state remains open and accountable to society. By maintaining separate, robust organizations, the system ensures that transparency laws are actively enforced rather than merely regulated, preserving the checks and balances necessary for a healthy democracy. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the institutional framework that guarantees public access to information. The potential elimination of these autonomous bodies risks centralizing data control and weakening the mechanisms that enforce transparency and protect personal data. The outcome of these negotiations will determine whether Mexico maintains strong, independent structures for open data governance or shifts toward a more vulnerable, government-controlled system, directly impacting the availability and reliability of public information for citizens and researchers.
