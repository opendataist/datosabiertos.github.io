Dos premios con un trabajo de fin de máster

Dos premios con un trabajo de fin de máster

Natalia Crespo Lera’s master’s thesis successfully bridges forest science and data technology by automating the assignment of national forest inventory parcels to municipalities using linked open data. Her work addresses the challenge of fragmented, dispersed forestry data across provincial boundaries, creating a unified dataset that simplifies local and cross-regional analysis. This innovation allows researchers to easily filter and extrapolate information, overcoming the traditional difficulties of handling multiple files for interprovincial studies. The project is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the practical power of integrating disparate public datasets to enhance accessibility and utility. By linking forest inventory data with municipal boundaries, Crespo transformed complex, hard-to-use information into a streamlined resource. This approach highlights how open data initiatives can solve real-world problems by making scientific information more interoperable and user-friendly for diverse applications. Crespo’s achievement was recognized with prestigious awards from the Spanish Society of Forest Sciences and the Aragon Research Institute, validating the innovative potential of this method. Her success underscores the importance of interdisciplinary skills in managing and analyzing open data. The case study of the Picos de Europa illustrates how these integrated datasets facilitate comprehensive environmental monitoring, proving that open data can significantly advance sustainable forest management and scientific research.
