The article argues that the Freedom of Information Act has long functioned as a mechanism for obfuscation rather than transparency, highlighting systemic efforts by federal agencies to withhold records. It illustrates this pattern through examples of bureaucratic delays and absurd denials, suggesting that the legal framework intended to ensure government accountability is frequently manipulated to conceal damaging information from the public and investigators. This systemic lack of openness is exemplified by the recent congressional scrutiny of Dr. Anthony Fauci and the NIH. Testimony reveals significant evasions regarding critical pandemic decisions, including Wuhan lab funding and the rationale for social distancing mandates. The narrative emphasizes that officials often claim ignorance or provide conflicting explanations, raising serious questions about whether data-driven policies were actually used to shape the initial public health response. These issues are highly relevant to open_data because they underscore the critical importance of accessible, unredacted government records for public accountability. The article demonstrates how opaque data practices and the destruction or concealment of digital communications can undermine trust in scientific and political leadership. It suggests that without robust mechanisms for data transparency, it remains nearly impossible to verify official claims or understand the true basis of major policy decisions.
