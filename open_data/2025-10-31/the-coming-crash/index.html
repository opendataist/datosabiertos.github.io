The coming crash

The coming crash

The article argues that the current artificial intelligence investment bubble is dangerously unsustainable, driven by irrational scaling of infrastructure rather than proven profitability. While some optimists view this excess as a necessary precursor to future innovation, the reality is that massive capital is being deployed into technologies that lack clear financial returns. This reckless over-investment poses a significant risk to the broader economy, particularly because a handful of dominant tech firms now represent a substantial portion of major market indices, making the entire financial system vulnerable to their potential collapse. The implications for global stability are severe, as the anticipated market crash coincides with unprecedented geopolitical and economic disorder. The fragility of the financial sector is exacerbated by weakening international rule of law, escalating conflicts, and shifting trade dynamics that challenge established global institutions. Furthermore, the emerging move to diversify away from the US dollar as the primary reserve currency adds another layer of uncertainty, suggesting that any economic downturn will not occur in isolation but within a turbulent international landscape that could amplify its duration and depth. This narrative is critical to the open data community because it highlights the precarious nature of the digital infrastructure upon which modern data initiatives depend. Open data projects often rely on the cloud computing power and hardware investments of the very corporations described here, which are currently fueled by speculative bubbles rather than stable economic foundations. If these major tech entities face severe financial downturns, the availability, cost, and reliability of the underlying data platforms could be jeopardized, threatening the continuity of open access projects that rely on their ecosystem.
