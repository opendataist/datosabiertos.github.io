The article highlights critical failures in public works governance, where regional authorities in Junín and Ayacucho face accusations of concealing stalled projects and ignoring severe labor safety violations. These instances reveal a systemic lack of transparency and accountability, as officials contradict oversight bodies, leaving citizens and workers exposed to risks without effective remediation. Regarding civil registration and electoral integrity, the closure of the electoral roll in Loreto underscores the necessity of accurate, accessible, and up-to-date citizen databases. The reliance on RENIEC’s data for democratic participation demonstrates how open and well-maintained public records are foundational to ensuring legitimate and inclusive elections, preventing disenfranchisement due to administrative gaps. Furthermore, proposed technological interventions in Cusco, such as facial recognition for SIM card purchases, raise questions about the effectiveness of digital controls versus underlying structural issues. While technology offers new layers of regulation, its success depends on the availability and reliability of underlying data infrastructure. Ultimately, these diverse reports emphasize that open data is not merely an informational asset but a prerequisite for enforcing accountability, protecting rights, and ensuring the integrity of public services and democratic processes across Peru.
