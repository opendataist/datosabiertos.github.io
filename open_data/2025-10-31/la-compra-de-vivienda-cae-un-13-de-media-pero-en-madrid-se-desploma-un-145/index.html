La compra de vivienda cae un 1,3% de media pero en Madrid se desploma un 14,5%

The Spanish real estate market exhibits a clear bifurcation, with apartment transactions declining while single-family home sales and mortgage lending volumes increase. This shift indicates a changing consumer preference toward larger properties and suggests that financing remains a crucial enabler for homebuyers, despite rising overall costs. Geographically, the market is highly fragmented. While some autonomous communities experience significant growth in both sales volume and prices, others face sharp contractions. This regional disparity highlights that national averages can obscure local realities, as economic conditions and demand vary drastically across different provinces. This report is vital for open data initiatives as it underscores the necessity of granular, localized datasets. High-level aggregations fail to capture the nuanced regional dynamics driving the housing crisis. Accessible, detailed public statistics allow researchers and policymakers to identify these disparities accurately, fostering evidence-based decisions rather than relying on misleading generalizations.
