Missing RTI info to be restored, website says, after years of data disappears

The Indian government’s RTIOnline portal has restored missing archival data, highlighting significant technical vulnerabilities in the infrastructure managing public records. This incident underscores critical governance failures regarding digital transparency, as users previously experienced unexplained data loss during repeated maintenance cycles. Such instability threatens the integrity of historical public disclosures, raising concerns about the reliability of state-maintained information systems for citizen accountability. Beyond technical glitches, recent legislative amendments and institutional shortcomings are weakening the Right to Information framework. New restrictions on disclosing personal information and increased governmental control over the Information Commission create barriers to accessing evidence of public interest matters. These changes, combined with severe backlogs and vacancies within appeal bodies, diminish the RTI Act’s effectiveness as a tool for auditing government operations and ensuring administrative transparency. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocates, as it demonstrates how both technical negligence and restrictive legal frameworks can erode public trust in open information systems. When data disappears or becomes harder to access, the fundamental promise of government transparency is compromised. Understanding these systemic failures is crucial for developing robust, secure, and legally protected open data ecosystems that truly empower citizens and hold authorities accountable.
