Open Data
ES EN

Uruguay 2100 emerged as the winner of a national Open Climate Data Challenge, demonstrating how publicly accessible environmental data can be transformed into actionable tools for civic engagement and policy formulation. Supported by major tech and development organizations, the initiative successfully bridged the gap between raw government datasets and community-driven solutions, proving that open data is not merely a bureaucratic requirement but a vital resource for fostering transparency and collective action against climate change. The project’s significance lies in its demonstration of data ecosystem mapping as a strategic practice for innovation. By training diverse participants—including students, activists, and developers—to identify key data sources and value flows, the challenge highlighted the importance of connecting disparate information assets. This approach empowers local innovators to visualize complex climate scenarios, such as coastal erosion or agricultural footprints, making abstract scientific risks tangible and understandable for the general public. This initiative is highly relevant to open data advocates because it validates the practical application of open data principles in critical societal challenges. It illustrates how structured mentorship and funding can accelerate the development of minimal viable products that serve the public interest. By showcasing successful models where data journalism, awareness applications, and policy insights converge, the case study provides a replicable framework for other governments seeking to leverage open data for climate resilience and democratic participation.
