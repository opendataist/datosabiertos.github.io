La inflación de diciembre fue de 5,1 % y cerró el 2022 en 94,8 %

Argentina concluded 2022 with its highest inflation rate in thirty-two years, surpassing the previous peak recorded in 1991. This economic milestone signifies a severe deterioration in purchasing power and financial stability. The accumulation of prices over the twelve-month period nearly doubled the rate of the previous year, creating a critical environment that challenges the sustainability of current social and commercial contracts. The data reflects a structural crisis where monetary issuance and currency devaluation have driven costs beyond historical limits, signaling a departure from the relative stability achieved during the Convertibility era. The drivers behind this surge include significant exchange rate gaps, substantial utility adjustments, and high monetary emission to cover treasury needs. While specific sectors like clothing and hospitality saw extreme increases, government agreements managed to moderately slow prices in food and apparel due to reduced consumption. This dynamic highlights the complex interplay between policy interventions, market scarcity, and household income erosion. The divergence between official forecasts and actual results underscores the difficulty in controlling inflationary expectations amidst a constrained import environment and rising public debt obligations. This information is crucial for open data because it illustrates how independent statistical reporting, such as that provided by INDEC, serves as an essential tool for verifying official narratives and enabling public accountability. Access to disaggregated, transparent consumer price indices allows analysts, journalists, and citizens to cross-check government projections against empirical evidence. Reliable, free access to such fundamental economic indicators fosters informed public debate, supports the creation of independent economic analyses, and strengthens democratic oversight by preventing the monopolization of factual reality by state entities.
