Las subidas de tipos ya se notan: la compraventa de viviendas se hunde un 8,2% en noviembre

The Spanish housing market experienced a significant contraction in November, marked by a sharp decline in property transactions and mortgage approvals. This downturn was widespread, with sales dropping across almost all autonomous regions, indicating a broad weakening of demand rather than isolated regional issues. The reduction in volume suggests that fewer buyers are entering the market, reflecting potential economic uncertainty or tightening credit conditions that discourage purchasing activity. Despite the drop in sales volume, house prices continued to rise, albeit at a more moderate pace. This divergence highlights a complex market dynamic where affordability constraints are limiting transactions, yet underlying value persists in many areas. Certain regions saw substantial price increases, while others experienced slight declines, creating a fragmented landscape. The fact that prices are rising even as transaction volumes fall points to structural supply-demand imbalances rather than pure market overheating. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, timely, and granular real estate statistics for public analysis. By making such detailed information accessible, researchers and policymakers can better understand market trends, assess housing affordability, and design effective urban policies. Open access to these datasets empowers citizens to make informed decisions and fosters accountability in the housing sector, ensuring that market dynamics are visible and understood by all stakeholders.
