Is it 'treason' to support free trade?

A federal advisory panel’s recommendation to label critics of the Jones Act as traitors exposes a deep conflict between regulatory oversight and industry interests. This extreme stance highlights how protectionist policies prioritize domestic shipping monopolies over consumer welfare, resulting in higher prices and slower disaster recovery for affected regions. The incident reveals a concerning pattern of collusion between maritime unions and the government agencies meant to regulate them. Such behavior undermines the principles of transparency essential for effective open data initiatives. When regulatory bodies align closely with private interests, they risk obscuring data that reflects negative outcomes for the public. This lack of openness prevents stakeholders from accurately assessing the true economic impact of protectionist laws. The relevance to open data lies in the necessity of unrestricted access to governmental communications. Without FOIA-enabled scrutiny, collusive practices remain hidden from public view. Ensuring that such documents are publicly available allows for independent analysis, fostering accountability and enabling informed debates on trade policy and regulatory effectiveness.
