UPenn researchers developing taxpayer-funded AI tool to target vaccine skeptics in social media – NaturalNews.com

UPenn researchers developing taxpayer-funded AI tool to target vaccine skeptics in social media – NaturalNews.com

University of Pennsylvania researchers are developing an AI tool, funded by U.S. taxpayer dollars, to identify and target social media users expressing skepticism or negative views regarding the HPV vaccine. This initiative, led by the National Cancer Institute, aims to create an "inoculation" mechanism that flags vaccine-related content for intervention, regardless of whether the statements are factually true or false. The project represents a significant investment in using automated systems to monitor and potentially correct public discourse surrounding vaccination safety and efficacy. The development of this technology highlights a growing reliance on algorithmic monitoring to manage public health narratives and address vaccine hesitancy. By training machine learning models to recognize specific patterns of skepticism, researchers are attempting to preemptively counter concerns about side effects and industry motives before they spread further. This approach shifts the burden of information verification onto automated systems, raising questions about how digital tools influence individual autonomy and public trust in medical guidance. This article is relevant to open data as it underscores the critical importance of transparency in AI-driven public health interventions. The use of taxpayer funds for surveillance tools that categorize dissent as misinformation requires public scrutiny and access to the underlying algorithms and criteria. Open data advocates emphasize that understanding how these systems classify "misinformation" is essential for ensuring accountability, preventing abuse, and maintaining democratic discourse in the digital age.
