Presentan un proyecto para derogar el decreto del Gobierno que restringe el acceso a la información pública

Legislators introduced a bill to repeal the executive decree restricting access to public information, arguing that it violates constitutional and international standards. This action underscores the critical need to prevent regulatory overreach that undermines transparency laws, ensuring that restrictions are legally defined rather than arbitrarily imposed by the executive branch. The initiative highlights the tension between government authority and the fundamental right to information, emphasizing that exceptions must be narrowly interpreted. This legislative challenge serves as a vital precedent for open data advocates, reinforcing the principle that transparency frameworks must remain robust against administrative encroachments that could obscure public accountability. Ultimately, this effort protects the integrity of data accessibility laws by asserting that only the legislature can limit information rights. It demonstrates how political opposition plays a key role in safeguarding open data principles, ensuring that public information remains available and that citizens’ right to know is not diminished by temporary regulatory measures.
