INE demostrará datos en mesa técnica y Santa Cruz confirma “paro pacífico”

INE demostrará datos en mesa técnica y Santa Cruz confirma “paro pacífico”

The National Institute of Statistics (INE) is finalizing the preliminary dissemination of the 2024 Census results across Bolivia’s departments, with plans to convene a national technical commission to demonstrate the methodological consistency of the process. This administrative step aims to validate the integrity of the data and address growing skepticism regarding the accuracy of the demographic figures released in March. Simultaneously, significant social unrest has emerged, particularly in Santa Cruz, where civic leaders have organized protests against the census outcomes. Critics, including local authorities, argue that the data lacks reliability and demand an audit, refusing to accept the results for resource distribution. This tension highlights a deep disconnect between official statistics and local perceptions of governmental accountability and service delivery. This situation is crucial for open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent data governance to build public trust. When citizens question the validity of statistical data, the availability of raw datasets for independent verification becomes essential. By allowing public review of questionnaires and international validation, institutions can mitigate distrust. Ultimately, open data practices serve as a bridge between technical rigor and democratic participation, ensuring that official statistics are not only accurate but also perceived as legitimate by the society they serve.
