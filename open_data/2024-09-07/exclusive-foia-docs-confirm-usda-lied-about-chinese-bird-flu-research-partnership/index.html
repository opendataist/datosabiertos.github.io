EXCLUSIVE: FOIA Docs Confirm USDA Lied About Chinese Bird Flu Research Partnership

The article asserts that the USDA misled Congress regarding ongoing financial partnerships with Chinese institutions for gain-of-function research on avian influenza. Through Freedom of Information Act documents, it claims that U.S. funding continues to support high-risk virus experiments at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, despite previous biosecurity controversies. The narrative centers on the allegation that federal officials provided false testimony about these collaborations, suggesting a breach of public trust and potential legal violations. A primary concern raised is the persistence of funding for research capable of creating more lethal pathogens, which critics argue poses an existential threat similar to the origins of the global pandemic. The author contends that this continued engagement with foreign entities for dangerous biological studies represents a dangerous continuation of policies that previously resulted in widespread economic and health crises. The text implies that the lack of immediate consequences for these actions enables potentially catastrophic outcomes for public safety. This content is relevant to open data discussions because it highlights the critical role of transparency and accountability in government operations. The reliance on FOIA requests to uncover hidden financial ties and official misleading statements underscores how open data mechanisms serve as essential tools for civic oversight. It illustrates the tension between classified or opaque institutional activities and the public’s right to access information regarding national security and biological research funding.
