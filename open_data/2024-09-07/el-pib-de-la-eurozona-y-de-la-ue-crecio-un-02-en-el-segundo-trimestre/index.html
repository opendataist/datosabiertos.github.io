El PIB de la eurozona y de la UE creció un 0,2% en el segundo trimestre

The European economy experienced a modest slowdown in the second quarter of 2024, with growth in both the eurozone and the wider EU declining slightly compared to the previous quarter. While year-on-year figures indicate continued expansion, the deceleration highlights underlying fragility. Crucially, this stagnation was driven by a drop in household consumption, which was offset by increased public spending, while significant contractions in fixed capital formation suggest reduced investment confidence across member states. Despite the broader regional slowdown, Spain demonstrated notable resilience, emerging as one of the top performers for economic growth within the bloc. This positive domestic performance occurred alongside mixed labor market trends, where overall employment in the eurozone saw slight gains despite declining labor productivity. The divergence between Spain’s robust results and the stagnation in major economies like Germany underscores the uneven recovery trajectory within the European Union. This analysis is relevant to open data because it relies on Eurostat’s standardized, transparent statistical updates, which are essential for rigorous cross-country comparisons. Access to such high-quality, granular economic indicators allows researchers and policymakers to accurately assess regional disparities and the impact of fiscal policies. Open data facilitates the scrutiny of these complex macroeconomic dynamics, ensuring that decisions regarding economic strategy are based on verified, accessible, and comparable evidence rather than opaque reporting.
