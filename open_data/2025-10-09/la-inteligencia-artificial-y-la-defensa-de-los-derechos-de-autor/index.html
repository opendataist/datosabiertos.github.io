La Inteligencia Artificial y la defensa de los derechos de autor

La Inteligencia Artificial y la defensa de los derechos de autor

The article highlights a critical legislative debate in the UK regarding the use of artistic works by artificial intelligence, emphasizing the urgent need to protect copyright laws. Major musicians have strongly opposed proposed initiatives that fail to safeguard creators' rights, warning that unchecked corporate access threatens artistic integrity and exploits both living and deceased artists. This collective resistance demonstrates a unified stance against the potential commodification of creativity, urging governments to prioritize intellectual property protections to maintain cultural leadership and prevent economic exploitation by tech giants. The relevance to open data lies in the tension between data accessibility for AI training and the ethical boundaries of intellectual property. While open data promotes transparency and innovation, this scenario illustrates the risks when such data includes copyrighted creative works without proper consent or compensation. The debate underscores the necessity of balancing technological advancement with legal frameworks that respect creator rights, serving as a cautionary example for other nations, including Mexico, facing similar challenges. Ultimately, the article argues that proactive policy-making is essential to address these emerging technological threats before they become widespread issues. It calls for robust legal structures that define clear boundaries for data usage in AI, ensuring that the benefits of open and accessible data do not come at the expense of artists’ livelihoods. This perspective encourages stakeholders to consider the long-term implications of data policies on creative industries, fostering a more equitable digital ecosystem.
