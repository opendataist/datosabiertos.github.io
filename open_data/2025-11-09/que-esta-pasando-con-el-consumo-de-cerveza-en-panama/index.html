¿Qué está pasando con el consumo de cerveza en Panamá?

¿Qué está pasando con el consumo de cerveza en Panamá?

Panama is experiencing a significant contraction in alcohol consumption, marked by a notable decline in the production of beer and other distilled spirits. This trend, evident since late 2024, is attributed to a combination of substantial increases in selective consumption taxes and a broader economic slowdown that has reduced households’ purchasing power. The most affected categories include rum, gin, and seco, while even non-alcoholic products have seen a drop in sales. Analysts point out that this reduction is not isolated but part of a wider cultural and economic shift. The new generation of consumers prioritizes health and moderation, shifting toward non-alcoholic or craft beverages, while financial pressures have forced many to cut back on recreational spending in favor of basic needs. Bars and nightclubs report lower foot traffic, reflecting declining consumer confidence and high unemployment, factors that are cooling activity in the leisure sector. This article is relevant to open_data because it illustrates how macroeconomic indicators, such as figures from the National Institute of Statistics and Census (INEC) and tax revenue collections, directly interact with private sector data and market perceptions. Transparency in these government statistics enables analysts and businesses to understand the real impacts of fiscal policies, such as tax increases, on consumer behavior. Moreover, the correlation between official production data and business decisions, such as layoffs, underscores the importance of integrating diverse data sources to assess the social and economic impact of public regulations.
