ICIR tasks journalists on utilizing FOI Act

ICIR tasks journalists on utilizing FOI Act

The Freedom of Information Act serves as a critical tool for investigative journalism, enabling reporters to demand transparency and hold public officials accountable for corruption and abuse of power. By actively requesting data from government bodies, journalists can empower citizens with the necessary information to make informed decisions, thereby fostering open governance. This legal framework is essential for reducing impunity in the public sector, shifting the burden of enforcement from civil society alone to the media, who must champion its usage to ensure effective implementation. However, significant structural barriers hinder the full realization of these benefits. Public agencies often struggle with cumbersome internal processes and lack the competency or willingness to comply with information requests, leading to non-compliance without consequence. The current regulatory environment allows institutions to withhold information under various exemptions, claiming protection of public or private interests. Without robust enforcement mechanisms or sanctions against non-compliant entities, the right to access information remains largely theoretical, leaving many crucial records inaccessible to the public and the press. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the gap between legislative intent and practical accessibility of public information. It underscores that open data initiatives cannot succeed through passive availability alone; they require active legal enforcement and institutional accountability. The discussion emphasizes the need for standardized procedures and proactive disclosure to bridge the divide between opaque bureaucracy and the public’s right to know, ensuring that data is not just legally accessible but practically available for use in journalism and civic engagement.
