Violation of Arkansas Constitution? Restraining order requested against new FOIA law

The core conflict centers on Act 7, a legislative measure that attempts to exempt state police records regarding the governor’s security from public disclosure. This law was drafted specifically to invalidate a prior Freedom of Information lawsuit filed by attorney Matthew Campbell. By seeking to shield these records, the legislation represents a significant threat to government transparency, prioritizing privacy for high-ranking officials over the public’s right to access information held by the state. Legal experts argue that Act 7 is likely not in effect due to procedural violations regarding its emergency clause. Constitutional requirements dictate that such clauses must be passed via a separate roll-call vote, a step allegedly skipped during the special session. Consequently, the law may not take effect for ninety days, allowing Campbell’s original legal challenge to proceed. If the courts uphold this interpretation, the attempt to retroactively nullify transparency obligations would fail, preserving the citizen’s ability to seek information that was legally accessible before the new statute was proposed. This case is critically relevant to open data because it illustrates how legislative bodies may exploit legal mechanisms to restrict data access rather than improve it. It highlights the vulnerability of transparency frameworks to sudden political maneuvering and underscores the importance of judicial oversight in protecting public records. The outcome will determine whether specific categories of government data can be arbitrarily excluded from public view, setting a precedent for how far officials can go to limit accountability and maintain secrecy in governance.
