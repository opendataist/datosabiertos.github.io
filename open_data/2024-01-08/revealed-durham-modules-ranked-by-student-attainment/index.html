Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

By utilizing data obtained through a Freedom of Information Request, this analysis reveals significant disparities in undergraduate academic performance across various disciplines. The findings highlight that certain fields, such as physics and English, consistently demonstrate higher attainment levels, while foundational courses and specific modern foreign languages show considerably lower results. These variations suggest that subject-specific challenges, assessment types, and student backgrounds heavily influence grading outcomes, raising questions about the standardization and fairness of university evaluation systems. The article further breaks down performance by academic year, identifying specific modules that either excel or struggle in awarding top honors. For instance, while some first-year design courses saw no first-class degrees awarded, business analytics modules achieved remarkably high success rates. Similarly, upper-level studies show stark contrasts, with certain philosophy and classics modules outperforming theology and language courses. This granular level of detail provides students and educators with a clearer understanding of which areas of study present greater academic hurdles or advantages, facilitating more informed educational planning. This data is particularly relevant to the open_data movement as it demonstrates the practical value of transparent, publicly accessible government and institutional information. When universities release detailed grading statistics, it empowers stakeholders to scrutinize educational quality and accountability. Open access to such metrics fosters a culture of transparency, allowing for public debate on pedagogical effectiveness and resource allocation. Ultimately, it underscores how releasing raw data can drive meaningful insights and improvements in higher education policy and practice.
