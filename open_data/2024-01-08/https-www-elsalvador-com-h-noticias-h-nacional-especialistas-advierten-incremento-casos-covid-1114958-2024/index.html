El Salvador is experiencing a significant surge in cases of COVID-19 and other respiratory illnesses, primarily attributed to the spread of the JN.1 variant and increased tourist movement. Specialists warn that this subvariant is more contagious and partially evades prior immunity, leading to a notable increase in healthcare facility visits, particularly in urban areas and among vulnerable groups such as children and older adults. The situation is complicated by the lack of up-to-date epidemiological data, as the surveillance system does not include genomic sequencing to accurately identify circulating variants in the country. This lack of precise information, combined with delays in publishing health bulletins, hinders a context-specific response and limits experts’ ability to monitor the true evolution of the epidemic beyond general clinical reports. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates how opacity in the publication of critical epidemiological data—such as viral sequencing and timely updates to statistics—obstructs transparency and informed decision-making. The lack of access to accurate and up-to-date data not only complicates public understanding of the health crisis but also weakens the accountability of health institutions, underscoring the urgent need for robust and updated open data systems to protect public health.
