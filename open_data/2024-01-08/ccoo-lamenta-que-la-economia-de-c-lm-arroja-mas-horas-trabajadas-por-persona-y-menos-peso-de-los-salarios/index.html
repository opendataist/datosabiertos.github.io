CCOO lamenta que la economía de C-LM arroja "más horas trabajadas por persona y menos peso de los salarios"

The article highlights a structural inequality in Castilla-La Mancha’s economy, where the growth of labor compensation significantly lags behind the expansion of business surpluses. This divergence has reduced the share of wages in the regional Gross Domestic Product, indicating that wealth generated by workers is not being equitably distributed to them. Instead, profits are accumulating at a faster rate than employee earnings, widening the gap between capital returns and labor income. Furthermore, the region faces broader economic challenges, including sluggish GDP growth and low per capita income compared to national averages. Workers are increasingly contributing more hours on average, yet this increased effort does not translate into proportional job creation or wage growth. The disparity is particularly pronounced in sectors like commerce and hospitality, where hour increases outpace employment figures, suggesting a trend of intensifying labor without corresponding improvements in job quality or stability. This analysis is crucial for open data because it demonstrates how accessible economic indicators can reveal deep social inequities that aggregate national statistics often obscure. By making granular regional data transparent, researchers and policymakers can challenge narratives focused solely on corporate profits and advocate for metrics that prioritize well-being. Ultimately, open access to such detailed socioeconomic evidence is essential for driving policies that ensure fair wealth distribution and sustainable labor conditions.
