ACLU of N.H. investigating reports that Berlin prison will be used for immigration detainees

The ACLU of New Hampshire is utilizing Freedom of Information Act requests to uncover federal plans for detaining immigration detainees at a state prison. This legal action targets both the Bureau of Prisons and the Department of Homeland Security, seeking records that would confirm an agreement to house hundreds of immigrants in a medium-security facility. The initiative arises from media reports suggesting significant preparations are underway, including financial agreements and staff relocations, despite official silence from government agencies. Central to this effort is the concern that civil immigration violators could be confined in isolation from legal counsel and support networks. The ACLU argues that such measures amount to cruelty, particularly because these individuals face civil, not criminal, charges. By demanding transparency, the organization aims to prevent the concealment of policies that separate immigrants from their families and advocates, ensuring that local authorities and citizens are aware of potential impacts on their community. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how FOIA requests serve as critical tools for accessing government information that is otherwise hidden. It highlights the intersection of legal transparency and public accountability, showing that withholding operational details about detention facilities obstructs civic oversight. Ultimately, it reinforces the principle that citizens have a right to data regarding government actions affecting civil liberties, emphasizing that transparency is essential for democratic engagement and policy evaluation.
