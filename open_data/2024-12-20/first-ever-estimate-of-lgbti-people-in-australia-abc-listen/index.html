First ever estimate of LGBTI+ people in Australia - ABC listen

First ever estimate of LGBTI+ people in Australia - ABC listen

For the first time, the Australian Bureau of Statistics has released an estimate of the LGBTQI+ population, addressing a long-standing gap in national datasets where sexuality questions were historically excluded. This milestone is significant for open data initiatives because it demonstrates how aggregated survey data can effectively fill voids in official census statistics, providing a more complete and inclusive picture of societal demographics without requiring intrusive direct questioning in every national count. The availability of this data is crucial for evidence-based policy and resource allocation, ensuring that public funding for health and aged care reaches communities based on actual need rather than invisibility. By confirming that LGBTQI+ individuals reside in every region and sector of Australia, the estimates enable governments to design more equitable services. This underscores the core open data principle that measurable populations are visible populations, allowing for efficient and targeted public expenditure. Furthermore, the findings reveal a generational shift, with younger demographics identifying as LGBTQI+ at significantly higher rates than older ones, reflecting changing social attitudes and safety. This data validates the lived experiences of marginalized groups, offering "dignity in being known" and supporting community acceptance. Ultimately, this release highlights the vital role of transparent data in fostering social inclusion and proving that diverse communities are an integral part of the national fabric.
