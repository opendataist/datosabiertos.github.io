Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly worse living conditions regarding employment, housing, and income compared to the general population. Despite these socioeconomic disadvantages and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness, they report satisfaction with life in Norway that is equal to or greater than native citizens. This paradox highlights that material hardship does not necessarily translate to lower subjective well-being, suggesting that cultural and social factors play a crucial role in their overall happiness and integration experience. The data reveals a complex relationship between immigrants and societal institutions. While trust in interpersonal relationships is lower among immigrants, they exhibit notably higher confidence in the political system, judiciary, and police than the rest of the population. This strong institutional trust and affinity for Norway indicate a resilient integration process, where reliance on state structures compensates for personal or community-level challenges, fostering a sense of security and belonging despite individual hardships. This report is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the power of comprehensive, publicly accessible statistics to challenge common narratives about immigrant integration. By making detailed demographic and well-being metrics available, researchers and policymakers can move beyond simplistic economic indicators to understand the multidimensional nature of social cohesion. Such transparency encourages evidence-based discussions on how to improve living conditions while acknowledging the unique strengths and adaptive strategies of immigrant communities.
