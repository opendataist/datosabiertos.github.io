Nace Transparencia para el Pueblo; gastará 500 mdp menos que el Inai

Nace Transparencia para el Pueblo; gastará 500 mdp menos que el Inai

The article details the structural replacement of the autonomous Institute for Transparency (INAI) with two new units under the Secretariat of Anti-Corruption and Good Government. This administrative shift aims to centralize oversight while claiming significant budget efficiency, asserting that the new configuration can perform the majority of previous functions with substantially reduced resources. The leadership transition marks a definitive end to the previous autonomous model in favor of a centralized bureaucratic approach. The new framework divides responsibilities between access to information and personal data protection, managed by distinct specialized units. By consolidating these roles, the government intends to streamline processes and focus on the effectiveness of resolutions rather than their volume. This centralization implies that technical autonomy will now reside within the executive branch rather than an independent body, fundamentally altering how transparency obligations and data rights are enforced for both public institutions and private entities. This development is critical for open data because it challenges the traditional separation between transparency advocacy and data regulation. Moving these functions into the Secretariat suggests a potential conflict of interest, as the same entity responsible for good governance now regulates the data practices of other powerful state organs. This consolidation raises concerns about the independence of data protection, potentially weakening safeguards for citizens’ rights and impacting the impartiality of open access to government information, which are foundational principles for a robust open data ecosystem.
