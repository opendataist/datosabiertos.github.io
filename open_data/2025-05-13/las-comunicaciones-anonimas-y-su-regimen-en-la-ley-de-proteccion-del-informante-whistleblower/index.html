Las comunicaciones anónimas y su régimen en la Ley de protección del informante (“whistleblower”)

Spain’s Law 2/2023 on the protection of whistleblowers permits anonymous reports, aligning with the European Directive, although such reports are recommended to be treated as an exceptional channel. This approach prioritizes the safety of the whistleblower against the fear of retaliation, enabling individuals to disclose serious violations without exposing their identity. However, anonymity introduces clear limitations, particularly when the justice system requires knowledge of the whistleblower’s identity to safeguard the accused’s right to a defense, which may necessitate revealing the whistleblower’s identity in judicial proceedings. Managing anonymous communications presents significant operational challenges during the investigative phase. Without direct contact with the whistleblower, organizations face difficulties in verifying the accuracy of the reported facts or requesting clarifications, which can lead to the closure of investigations due to insufficient information. This practical limitation contrasts with the fundamental right not to be discriminated against for having reported wrongdoing, requiring organizations to adopt reasonable measures to avoid automatically dismissing anonymous reports, even though their processing is more complex. This article is relevant to open data because it establishes the ethical and legal foundations for the secure handling of sensitive information. The need to protect the whistleblower’s identity through advanced technologies and strict legal frameworks—such as the use of encrypted networks and severe penalties for leaks—demonstrates how privacy can coexist with institutional transparency. This provides a critical model for balancing public accountability with the protection of individual rights in digital governance systems.
