Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer socioeconomic conditions compared to the general population, including lower incomes, worse housing, and employment challenges. Despite these material disadvantages, they report high satisfaction with life in Norway and demonstrate stronger confidence in national institutions like the judiciary and police. This paradox highlights a complex integration dynamic where objective hardship does not necessarily correlate with subjective dissatisfaction or alienation from the state. Mental health disparities are pronounced, with immigrants experiencing twice the rate of mental problems and loneliness as the average citizen. Specific national origins show varying vulnerability, yet the overall trend indicates a significant burden of psychological distress and isolation within immigrant communities. These findings underscore the urgent need for targeted mental health support services that address the unique stressors faced by this demographic, beyond general public health initiatives. This article is highly relevant to open data because it exemplifies the power of transparent, government-published statistics to reveal nuanced social realities. By making detailed demographic data available, such reports foster evidence-based policymaking and public discourse on integration and equality. Open access to this information allows researchers and civil society to identify hidden inequalities, ensuring that social progress is measured not just by economic indicators but by holistic well-being and trust.
