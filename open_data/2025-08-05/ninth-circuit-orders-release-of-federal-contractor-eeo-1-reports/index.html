Ninth Circuit Orders Release of Federal Contractor EEO-1 Reports

Ninth Circuit Orders Release of Federal Contractor EEO-1 Reports

The Ninth Circuit ruled that the Department of Labor must release federal contractor EEO-1 reports, rejecting the claim that they qualify as confidential commercial information under FOIA exemptions. The court determined that these documents contain only aggregated workforce demographics and job categories, failing to reveal trade secrets, pricing strategies, or operational details specific to a company’s commercial activities. Consequently, the data does not describe the exchange of goods or services for profit, rendering it ineligible for protection against public disclosure. This decision clarifies the scope of exemptions for sensitive business data, establishing a precedent that generic statistical information regarding employee diversity is not inherently commercial. By distinguishing between proprietary business insights and general workforce composition, the ruling limits the ability of federal contractors to withhold basic employment data under the guise of protecting competitive advantage or trade secrets. For open data advocates, this case is highly relevant as it reinforces transparency in government contracting and diversity tracking. It ensures that public funds and contractor obligations are visible to the public, promoting accountability in labor practices. The release of this data empowers researchers and journalists to analyze employment equity across industries, supporting broader goals of societal transparency and informed public discourse on corporate responsibility.
