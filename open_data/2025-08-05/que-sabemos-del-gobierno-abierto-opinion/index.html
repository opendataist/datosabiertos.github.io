…¿Qué sabemos del Gobierno Abierto?… | Opinión

…¿Qué sabemos del Gobierno Abierto?… | Opinión

Peru joined the Open Government Partnership with the aim of strengthening public transparency, combating corruption, and enhancing citizen participation. However, the country’s series of biennial government plans have largely failed to achieve these objectives due to inconsistent implementation and a lack of genuine involvement from civil society. Rather than fostering a transparent and accountable state, these initiatives have become examples of unfulfilled legal commitments, leaving significant issues regarding political integrity and budgetary responsibility unresolved. The core problem lies in the exclusion of key stakeholders, such as the private sector and organized civil society, from the design and implementation of these strategies. True government openness requires broad institutional collaboration to embed transparency principles into the fabric of public administration. Without this inclusive approach, the plans lack legitimacy and fail to transform public servants into effective advocates for openness, rendering the commitments largely symbolic and ineffective in practice. This case is crucial for open data because it highlights how policy failures directly undermine data utility and public trust. When governments do not commit to genuine participation and accountability, open data remains underutilized or manipulated, failing to serve as a tool for citizen oversight. Understanding this disconnect is vital for voters, as it underscores the need for leaders who prioritize verifiable transparency and are prepared to defend open governance principles, ensuring that data serves as a mechanism for real democratic engagement rather than mere bureaucratic formalism.
