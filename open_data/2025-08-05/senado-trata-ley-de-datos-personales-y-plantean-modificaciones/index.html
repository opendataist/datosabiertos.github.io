Senado trata ley de datos personales y plantean modificaciones

Senado trata ley de datos personales y plantean modificaciones

The Argentine Senate is moving toward approving, with amendments, the Personal Data Protection Law, a regulation that has been under debate for more than five years. This step is crucial for establishing a robust legal framework that guarantees citizens’ constitutional rights, with special emphasis on the protection of children and adolescents. The reduction in the number of articles in the bill reflects a consensus between the Legislation and Science committees, seeking to balance legislative efficiency with the legal certainty required for digital society. The proposed amendments aim to strengthen the autonomy and transparency of the future National Agency for Personal Data Protection. The agency is required to be autonomous and independent from the Ministry of Technologies, thereby avoiding institutional overload and ensuring operational independence. Additionally, it is proposed that the agency’s director report directly to Congress through annual reports, introducing a crucial accountability mechanism and preventing the concentration of power in a single executive or technical authority. The relevance of this text for the field of open data lies in its definition of the ethical and legal boundaries for data processing. By establishing clear rules regarding international transfers and the creation of offenses, the law not only protects individual privacy but also structures the environment in which data can be shared or released. This is fundamental for ensuring that government transparency initiatives and public information access operate within a system that respects the dignity and security of data subjects.
