Best of OSH25: Reviewing MOSAIC | Aero-News Network

Best of OSH25: Reviewing MOSAIC | Aero-News Network

The FAA’s new MOSAIC regulation fundamentally transforms the Light Sport Aircraft category by replacing rigid weight limits with performance-based criteria. This shift allows for significantly more capable aircraft, including those with higher speeds, retractable gear, and alternative propulsion systems like electric power. Crucially, it embraces emerging aviation technologies such as eVTOLs, signaling a regulatory framework that encourages innovation rather than stifling it with outdated constraints. Sport Pilot privileges are substantially expanded, granting access to a wider array of aircraft and introducing limited commercial operations such as aerial photography and search and rescue. By permitting night flying and reducing bureaucratic hurdles through a streamlined Statement of Compliance process, the rule aims to make aviation more accessible, particularly for individuals unable to obtain traditional medical certificates. This approach seeks to alleviate pilot shortages by lowering barriers to entry while maintaining safety standards through updated training requirements. This development is highly relevant to open data as it necessitates the creation of transparent, accessible databases for aircraft compliance, performance metrics, and pilot certification records. As the industry shifts toward flexible, performance-based definitions, stakeholders require standardized, machine-readable data to verify eligibility across diverse aircraft types. Open, interoperable data sets will be essential for manufacturers, pilots, and regulators to navigate the complex new landscape, ensuring that the promised innovation translates into practical, safe, and widely adopted aviation solutions.
