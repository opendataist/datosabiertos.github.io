En México, 1 de cada 5 usuarios de internet vivió alguna situación de ciberacoso en 2023

The INEGI’s 2023 Cyberbullying Module reveals that a significant portion of Mexico’s internet users experienced harassment, highlighting the urgent need for digital safety literacy. The data shows that victims, on average, spent more time online than non-victims, suggesting that increased connectivity may correlate with higher exposure to digital threats. This finding underscores the importance of understanding usage patterns to design more effective interventions. Security behaviors remain a critical area of concern, as the vast majority of users rely primarily on passwords while fewer invest in robust software protections like antivirus programs. The study indicates that while basic security measures are widespread, they may be insufficient against the sophisticated methods employed by aggressors, who often hide behind false identities. This gap between perceived safety and actual vulnerability points to a need for more advanced digital education. This report is relevant to open data because it exemplifies how large-scale, standardized statistical releases can drive public policy and awareness. By making detailed demographic and behavioral information accessible, institutions like INEGI empower researchers, policymakers, and citizens to identify risks and develop evidence-based strategies. Open access to such granular data fosters transparency and enables collaborative efforts to mitigate the social impacts of digital violence.
