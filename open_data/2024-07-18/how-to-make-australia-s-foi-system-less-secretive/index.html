How to make Australia's FOI system less secretive

The article highlights a critical disconnect in Australian freedom of information practices, revealing a bureaucratic culture steeped in secrecy rather than openness. A recent investigation into three states confirms that current systems are overly reactive, treating information release as an exception rather than the norm. This entrenched opacity stems from a lack of proactive disclosure mandates, where decisions about sharing information are delayed until a specific request is made, significantly hindering public access to government data. To rectify this, the research argues that proactive release must be legally enshrined as the default practice, transforming FOI requests into a last resort. This shift requires urgent improvements in digital record management and funding for FOI teams, as fragmented systems and staff shortages create impossible bottlenecks for officers who are otherwise knowledgeable and committed. Furthermore, streamlining third-party consultations and protecting officers from political interference are essential to ensuring timely and efficient access, thereby empowering these teams to function effectively as facilitators of transparency. This report is highly relevant to open data because it exposes the systemic barriers that prevent government information from being truly accessible and usable. By advocating for proactive disclosure and modernized records management, the findings underscore that open data cannot thrive under a regime of reactive secrecy. Addressing these structural flaws is crucial for rebuilding public trust and ensuring that government information is available by default, not just upon demand, which is a foundational principle for a healthy, transparent open data ecosystem.
