Dinero: La “llave china” - La Jornada San Luis

The article highlights Mexico’s severe trade imbalance with China, where imports vastly outweigh exports, prompting the imposition of defensive tariffs. This economic disparity is crucial for open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible trade statistics to hold governments accountable and ensure equitable negotiations. Without clear data on legal and illicit flows, policymakers cannot effectively address systemic imbalances or protect domestic industries from predatory practices that exploit information asymmetries. Additionally, the piece critiques the lack of legal consequences for architects and engineers involved in unsafe building practices, particularly following major earthquakes. This transparency gap is vital for open data initiatives, as public access to project records and regulatory decisions can expose corruption and negligence. By making such data available, citizens can demand accountability, preventing future disasters caused by corrupt real estate cartels and ensuring that public safety standards are rigorously enforced. Finally, the article discusses societal reactions to crime and sports decisions, emphasizing how individual accountability affects public trust. While these examples are distinct, they collectively illustrate that open data serves as a foundation for justice and fairness across multiple sectors. Whether in trade, construction safety, or social justice, the availability of accurate, unbiased information empowers society to make informed decisions and resist manipulation, fostering a more transparent and equitable democratic environment.
