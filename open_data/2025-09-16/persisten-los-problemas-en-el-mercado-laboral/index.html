Persisten los problemas en el mercado laboral

Persisten los problemas en el mercado laboral

The article reveals that while Paraguay’s employment figures increased in 2025, this growth primarily reflects a deepening of labor precarity rather than genuine economic progress. The majority of new jobs entered the informal sector, characterized by excessively long working hours and significantly lower income levels. Consequently, despite aggregate macroeconomic growth, real wages have stagnated, failing to improve purchasing power or allow workers to escape poverty, indicating that broader economic gains are not translating into better living conditions for the workforce. The relevance to open data lies in the critical necessity of disaggregated, high-quality statistics to expose these hidden disparities. By analyzing detailed datasets on income, sector, and gender, it becomes evident that the wage gap disproportionately affects women, who dominate sectors like domestic work with incomes far below the minimum wage. Open data initiatives must therefore prioritize transparency in labor metrics to highlight how standard aggregates can mask severe inequalities, enabling policymakers and civil society to demand accountability for the actual quality of employment rather than just the quantity. Ultimately, the report underscores that current labor trends show no recovery in job quality, with social security coverage remaining scarce. The data illustrates a systemic failure where economic indicators mask the reality of stagnant real incomes and entrenched gender biases. This highlights the vital role of open data in driving evidence-based policy reforms. Without accessible and detailed labor data, society cannot accurately assess the impact of economic policies on vulnerable populations, making transparency essential for advocating for fair wages, better working conditions, and inclusive growth.
