Centre LSD, OPG condemn ‘obnoxious bill’ by national assembly to scuttle transparency

Centre LSD, OPG condemn ‘obnoxious bill’ by national assembly to scuttle transparency

The article highlights a critical intersection between legislative threats and open governance efforts in Nigeria, where civil society groups condemned bills aimed at restricting freedom of association and expression. These legislative challenges directly undermine the foundational principles of transparency and civic participation essential for robust open data ecosystems. By targeting non-governmental organizations, such measures threaten the very partners who hold governments accountable and advocate for information accessibility, creating a hostile environment for the collaborative dialogue necessary for effective open data implementation. The Open Government Partnership serves as a vital platform where government and civil society function as equal partners to advance transparency commitments. However, participants noted that fulfilling these obligations remains a significant challenge, requiring both sides to maintain unity against attempts to weaken collective stances. The relevance to open data lies in this joint partnership model; without a stable, cooperative relationship between state actors and independent watchdogs, the mechanisms for publishing, accessing, and utilizing public data remain vulnerable to political interference and lack of enforcement. Ultimately, the workshop emphasized the necessity of holding government accountable for its transparency pledges and actively utilizing laws like the Freedom of Information Act. Stakeholders urged civil society to adopt collaborative approaches with media and government to expose non-compliance and drive action on national action plans. This collective vigilance is crucial for open data because it ensures that transparency initiatives are not merely procedural but result in tangible access to information, fostering public trust and enabling citizens to leverage data for improved governance and accountability.
