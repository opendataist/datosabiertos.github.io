El precio del alquiler sube un 13,1%: ¿dónde se pagan las rentas más altas?

El precio del alquiler sube un 13,1%: ¿dónde se pagan las rentas más altas?

The article highlights a critical structural imbalance in Spain’s housing market, where rent prices have reached record highs due to a severe shortage of available long-term rentals. This scarcity is driven by insufficient new construction and the diversion of existing housing stock toward more profitable short-term tourist rentals. Consequently, demand significantly outpaces supply, forcing tenants to pay substantially more than in previous years, with most autonomous communities and provinces recording significant annual increases. This trend underscores a systemic crisis rather than temporary fluctuations, as the limited inventory persists despite seasonal demand variations. The data reveals a nationwide phenomenon where nearly all analyzed capitals and provinces experience rising costs, indicating that the issue is deeply rooted in supply-side constraints rather than localized market dynamics. The concentration of the most expensive rentals in major urban centers and tourist destinations further exacerbates accessibility issues for local residents. This report is highly relevant to open data, as it demonstrates how transparent, granular real estate statistics can diagnose complex socio-economic challenges. By providing accessible metrics on price trends and supply levels, such data enables researchers, policymakers, and civic organizations to monitor housing affordability gaps. It facilitates evidence-based advocacy for regulatory reforms and highlights the urgent need for data-driven solutions to address housing inequality and market distortions.
