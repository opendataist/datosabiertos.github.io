Alrededor del 13% de la población joven en Paraguay “ni trabaja ni estudia” - Economía - ABC Color

Alrededor del 13% de la población joven en Paraguay “ni trabaja ni estudia” - Economía - ABC Color

The report from the National Institute of Statistics reveals a critical structural gap in Paraguay’s labor market, where young people face high levels of informality and underemployment. Precarious working conditions prevent most young people from accessing formal contracts or basic social benefits, such as health insurance, exacerbating the exclusion of one-quarter of the national population. This situation reflects a disconnect between companies’ demand for technical skills and the supply of young talent, who often lack the specific technical and soft skills needed to successfully integrate into the modern labor ecosystem. The relevance of this article to open data lies in its demonstration of how official data can highlight systemic inequalities that require more precise public policy interventions. By breaking down variables such as school attendance, informal employment, and urban-rural gaps, these data underscore the need for transparency and accessibility in statistical information to diagnose complex social problems. The analysis enables the identification not only of the volume of unemployment but also of its root causes, such as lack of experience and inadequate training, thereby promoting evidence-based management. Furthermore, the report emphasizes the importance of using open data to foster dialogue between the public and private sectors. By making talent gaps and labor market characteristics public, it facilitates the coordination of more effective educational and employment strategies. Transparency in statistical information is essential for creating genuine inclusion opportunities, ensuring that institutional decisions are based on verifiable realities of youth rather than general assumptions, thus driving a fairer and more sustainable economic development.
