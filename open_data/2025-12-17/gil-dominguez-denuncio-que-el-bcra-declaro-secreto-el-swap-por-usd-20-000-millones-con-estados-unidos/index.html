Gil Domínguez denunció que el BCRA declaró "secreto" el swap por USD 20.000 millones con Estados Unidos

Gil Domínguez denunció que el BCRA declaró "secreto" el swap por USD 20.000 millones con Estados Unidos

Andrés Gil Domínguez alleges that the Central Bank of the Argentine Republic (BCRA) classifies information about a key financial agreement as secret, arguing that this decision is arbitrary and violates the constitutional principles of transparency. By withholding the details of the transaction, the institution limits the ability of citizens and the Legislative Branch to oversee the use of public resources, creating a significant gap between state management and the right to know. This opacity prevents an assessment of the actual terms of the deal and its impact on the national economy, affecting legal certainty and the country’s risk profile. The refusal to share even partially anonymized data reveals a lack of commitment to accountability, prioritizing institutional discretion over the public interest and democratic access to government information. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the barriers faced by open data initiatives when financial institutions justify secrecy under broad stability criteria. The fight for transparency in high-value agreements underscores the urgency of robust regulations that prevent abuses in the classification of information, ensuring that citizens can exercise real oversight over financial decisions that profoundly affect their future economic well-being.
