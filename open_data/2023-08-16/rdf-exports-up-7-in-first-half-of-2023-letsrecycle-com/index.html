RDF exports up 7% in first half of 2023 - letsrecycle.com

England saw a significant rise in Refuse-Derived Fuel exports in early 2023, reversing previous declines and signaling market recovery. This growth is driven by reduced European waste production and energy security concerns, with Sweden and the Netherlands remaining primary importers. The increase highlights how shifting geopolitical and economic factors directly influence international waste trade flows, emphasizing the interconnectedness of global resource management. However, the overall picture reveals a stark divergence between RDF and Solid Recovered Fuel sectors. While municipal-focused RDF exports surged, industrial-bound SRF shipments plummeted due to soaring shipping costs and limited reach. This contrast demonstrates that not all waste-to-energy markets are recovering uniformly; specific sectors face unique vulnerabilities dependent on their end-use industries and transportation logistics. This data is crucial for open data initiatives in sustainability and circular economy sectors. Transparent tracking of waste movements allows stakeholders to identify market inefficiencies and geopolitical dependencies. By making such granular trade data accessible, researchers and policymakers can better understand the resilience of supply chains and develop strategies to optimize local processing capacities, reducing reliance on volatile international markets.
