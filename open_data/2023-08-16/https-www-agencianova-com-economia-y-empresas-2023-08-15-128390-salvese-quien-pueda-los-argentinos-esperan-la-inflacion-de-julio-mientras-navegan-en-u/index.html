The Central Bank implemented a significant devaluation of the exchange rate and a substantial increase in the interest rate, measures that are reshaping the national economic environment. This monetary volatility acts as a key trigger for domestic prices, generating uncertainty about the future stability of the currency and citizens’ purchasing power. Inflation is expected to accelerate in the coming months, driven by the devaluation and the rising cost of basic goods such as meat. Experts project double-digit figures, contradicting the moderation expectations observed in previous months. This scenario reflects the complex interplay between exchange rate policies, food supply, and tax pressure, highlighting the fragility of the price system in the face of external shocks. This analysis is relevant to the field of open data because it underscores the urgency of accessing timely and verifiable official statistical information. In contexts of high volatility, transparency in price indices enables citizens and researchers to compare official figures with private estimates. The availability of high-quality open data thus becomes an essential tool for monitoring the real impact of macroeconomic decisions and fostering effective accountability.
