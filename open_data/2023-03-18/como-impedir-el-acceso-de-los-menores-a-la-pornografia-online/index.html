Cómo impedir el acceso de los menores a la pornografía online

The article examines the implementation of new age-verification systems in France and Spain, concluding that the only technically viable solution requires the involvement of a trusted third party to validate majority status without compromising user privacy. It highlights that current methods, such as self-declaration, are ineffective because they are easily circumvented, while proposals like the use of digital certificates or third-party accounts pose significant risks regarding the retention of personal data by platforms. The relevance to open data and privacy lies in the critical balance between security and information protection. The text emphasizes that AI-based verifications, such as selfies analyzed by external companies, can be unreliable and discriminatory. Moreover, using third-party service accounts to access sensitive content leads to digital intrusion, as these platforms obtain detailed records of users’ activities, which contradicts the data minimization principles essential to the open information ecosystem. Finally, the article argues that true technological openness should not depend on extreme parental controls or the submission of identity documents to commercial websites, but rather on protocols that guarantee anonymity during the verification process. The main challenge is to design infrastructures that enable authorities and citizens to exercise protection rights without granting private actors access to biometric or browsing data, thereby preserving the integrity and confidentiality of personal data in the digital age.
