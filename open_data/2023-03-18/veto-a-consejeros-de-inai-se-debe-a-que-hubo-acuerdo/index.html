Veto a consejeros de INAI se debe a que hubo “acuerdo”

Veto a consejeros de INAI se debe a que hubo “acuerdo”

The president vetoed the appointment of new members to the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), denouncing a partisan agreement that he claimed compromises the institution’s integrity. This action underscores the current political tensions surrounding the autonomy of the agency, a central issue for transparency and access to public information in Mexico. Additionally, the administration defended its energy management record and attributed past irregularities to external actors, downplaying the direct responsibility of current officials. This narrative aims to consolidate a discourse of good governance and administrative integrity in the face of both internal and external criticism. The relevance to open data lies in how political pressure on the INAI affects the public’s ability to access government data, while actions against fentanyl and weapons reflect security priorities that may indirectly impact transparency in health records and border control registries.
