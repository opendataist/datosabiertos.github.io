Economic output statistics

The Northern Ireland Statistics and Research Agency reveals a divergent economic recovery trajectory following the pandemic. The services sector has emerged as the primary driver of growth, surpassing pre-pandemic levels and reaching series highs. This robust performance, supported by gains in business, finance, and wholesale trade, highlights the sector's resilience and suggests a structural shift toward service-based economic activities that are better positioned to withstand recent global disruptions compared to traditional industrial outputs. In contrast, the production sector shows a more mixed picture, experiencing recent declines in manufacturing and energy supply, despite remaining slightly above 2019 levels. While certain subsectors like engineering and food products have grown, others such as chemicals have contracted significantly. This volatility underscores the fragility of Northern Ireland’s industrial base and its vulnerability to supply chain pressures and energy costs, indicating that sustained recovery in this area requires targeted support to stabilize output against ongoing economic headwinds. Retail output remains notably weak, staying below pre-pandemic volumes and lagging behind broader regional trends. This persistent shortfall in consumer spending power signals ongoing challenges for local businesses and consumers alike, reflecting broader economic pressures that have not yet been fully resolved. For the open data community, this case illustrates the critical importance of granular, timely economic indicators. It demonstrates how disaggregated data allows policymakers and analysts to identify specific sectoral vulnerabilities and track recovery nuances that aggregate national statistics might obscure, ensuring more effective and localized interventions.
