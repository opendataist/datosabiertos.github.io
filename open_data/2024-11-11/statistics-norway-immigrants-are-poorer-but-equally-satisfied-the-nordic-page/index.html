Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions compared to the native population, encompassing lower incomes, worse employment, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Despite these material and psychological challenges, they report satisfaction levels equal to the rest of the society, supported by notably high trust in key political institutions and the judiciary. This paradox highlights a complex social dynamic where structural disadvantages do not necessarily translate into social alienation or dissatisfaction. This analysis is crucial for open data communities because it demonstrates the value of comprehensive, standardized social surveys in revealing nuanced societal truths that might otherwise be overlooked. By making such detailed statistical reports accessible, open data initiatives can foster deeper public understanding of integration challenges and resilience. It underscores the importance of transparency in government statistics, allowing researchers and citizens to examine the interplay between economic disparity and social trust. Furthermore, it illustrates how open data can facilitate evidence-based policy discussions regarding immigrant integration and mental health support. The availability of granular data allows for targeted interventions and a more accurate assessment of social cohesion. Ultimately, this case reinforces the role of open data in promoting accountability and informed dialogue, ensuring that marginalized groups' experiences are visible and considered in broader societal conversations.
