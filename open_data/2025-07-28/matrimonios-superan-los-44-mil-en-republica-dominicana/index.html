Matrimonios superan los 44 mil en República Dominicana

The article analyzes the dynamics of marriage and divorce in the Dominican Republic using official statistics. It highlights a significant concentration of unions in urban centers, particularly the Distrito Nacional and Santo Domingo, while rural regions like Enriquillo show notably lower registration rates. This geographic disparity reveals how demographic distribution directly influences social institutional data, emphasizing that local context is crucial for accurate societal analysis. Furthermore, the data uncovers a high rate of marital dissolution, with divorces approaching sixty percent of new marriages annually. The prevalence of separations in major metropolitan areas contrasts with rural stability, suggesting that urban environments may correlate with different social pressures or legal accessibility. These trends indicate that marriage stability is not uniform across the country, but rather shaped by specific regional socio-economic factors and legal frameworks. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how disaggregated, public statistics allow for the visualization of complex social phenomena. By making this data accessible, governments and researchers can identify inequalities in legal access and social stability. Open access to such granular information fosters transparency, enabling policymakers to address regional disparities and understand the real-life implications of civil institutions beyond mere numerical totals.
