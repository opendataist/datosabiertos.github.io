Study: Drug endangerment West Virginia child abuse cases four times the national average - WOAY-TV

West Virginia faces a severe child welfare crisis, with one in ten children experiencing abuse and neglect rates significantly exceeding national averages, often compounded by the opioid epidemic. The prevailing approach frequently fails due to outdated laws and a lack of physical evidence, resulting in low prosecution and conviction rates despite forensic interviews. This systemic failure highlights the urgent need for legislative reform and a shift toward trauma-informed care that prioritizes the child’s immediate safety and emotional healing over immediate legal accountability. The article underscores the critical role of specialized child advocacy centers like Just for Kids, which provide forensic interviewing, therapy, and family safety plans when state intervention is insufficient. By focusing on early intervention and comprehensive support, these organizations help children cope with trauma and build resilience, even when their abusers are not prosecuted. This model demonstrates that helping victims recover is a vital public health objective, independent of the criminal justice system’s ability to secure convictions, emphasizing the necessity of accessible therapeutic resources for vulnerable youth. This issue is highly relevant to open data because improving child protection outcomes requires transparent, accessible statistics to drive policy change and resource allocation. Currently, opaque or siloed data hampers efforts to understand the true scale of abuse and the effectiveness of interventions. Making detailed abuse statistics, prosecution outcomes, and service impacts openly available would empower researchers, policymakers, and community advocates to identify gaps, demand evidence-based reforms, and create data-driven strategies that truly protect children and address the root causes of neglect and addiction.
