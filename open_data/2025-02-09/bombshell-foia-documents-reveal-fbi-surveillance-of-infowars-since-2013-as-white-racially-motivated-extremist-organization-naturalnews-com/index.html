BOMBSHELL: FOIA documents reveal FBI surveillance of InfoWars since 2013 as “White racially-motivated extremist organization” – NaturalNews.com

Alex Jones claims that Freedom of Information Act requests have exposed long-term federal surveillance of his platform, InfoWars. The documents suggest the FBI classified the organization as a white racially motivated extremist threat, granting agents broad powers to monitor the group without evidence of criminal activity. This designation, initiated in 2013, allegedly allowed for extensive intelligence gathering, including the use of informants and database access, raising serious questions about the justification and ongoing nature of these investigations. The revelation highlights deep concerns regarding the weaponization of government agencies to suppress dissenting voices. Jones argues that this surveillance is politically motivated, serving to silence conservative and independent media rather than addressing genuine security threats. By drawing parallels to previous controversies involving the targeting of political groups, the narrative suggests a systemic pattern of overreach aimed at undermining critics and controlling public discourse through legal and intelligence frameworks. This case is critically relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparency mechanisms like FOIA can uncover hidden government activities, even amidst heavy redaction. It underscores the importance of public records in holding law enforcement accountable for how classification systems are applied to media organizations. The incident serves as a case study for the intersection of press freedom, government surveillance, and the public’s right to access information about state actions against non-state actors.
