Así puedes identificar la suplantación de identidad; Condusef pide estar alerta | El Universal

Financial fraudsters impersonate legitimate institutions by altering logos and names, using digital platforms and messaging apps to extract personal data and demand unauthorized upfront payments for loans. This modus operandi exploits user trust, leading victims to lose significant sums without receiving any financial services, as the scammers vanish after receiving the funds. The primary implication is that consumers must rigorously verify the legitimacy of any financial provider through official regulatory registers before engaging in transactions. Legitimate entities never require advance payments or sensitive data via informal channels, making skepticism toward such requests a critical defense mechanism against identity theft and financial loss. This issue is relevant to open data because transparency in regulatory databases empowers citizens to independently validate institutional claims. By leveraging open, accessible records of authorized financial entities, users can cross-reference service providers against official sources, thereby reducing information asymmetry and enhancing accountability within the financial sector.
