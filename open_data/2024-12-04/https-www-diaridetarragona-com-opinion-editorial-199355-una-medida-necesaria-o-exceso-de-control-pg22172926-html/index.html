The new mandatory registration platform for travelers in Spain requires the collection of extensive personal data from tourists, with the aim of enhancing security against fraud and irregular immigration. This regulatory change obliges accommodation providers to transmit detailed information to the authorities, fundamentally altering how private entities collect and store personal data. The implementation raises significant concerns about data privacy and proportionality. Collecting such a broad range of identifiers risks undermining fundamental rights, as individuals may feel excessively monitored without a clear justification for the use of their data. This highlights the critical need for transparency in how sensitive information is handled to prevent potential abuses. This article is highly relevant to the open data discourse because it illustrates the tension between state-controlled data collection and individual privacy. It underscores the importance of strict governance frameworks to ensure that mandatory reporting does not compromise data protection principles, serving as a cautionary example of how to balance security initiatives with ethical data stewardship in an increasingly regulated digital environment.
