Open Data
ES EN

The exposure of personal data on the dark web represents a critical threat that extends beyond public figures, affecting any individual who has interacted with external providers or online platforms. This article highlights how cybercriminals obtain sensitive information through third-party data breaches, phishing, or malware, and then sell it on anonymous markets. The relevance to open data lies in the inherent vulnerability of systems that store and process public information; if government institutions and policies do not adequately safeguard their data, transparency can become a national and personal security risk, eroding public trust. The implications of these attacks include facilitating identity fraud and disinformation campaigns through social engineering, demonstrating that digital privacy is a fundamental component of democratic security. By analyzing how leaked data integrates into criminal ecosystems, the urgency of applying data protection principles by design in all governance initiatives becomes clear. This involves not only protecting identifiable information but also assessing how open data can be exploited by malicious actors if robust anonymization and access controls are not implemented. To mitigate these risks, proactive measures are emphasized, such as multi-factor authentication, the use of disposable email addresses, and strict management of one’s digital footprint. From the perspective of open data, this underscores the importance of balancing transparency with privacy, ensuring that the availability of public information does not compromise citizens’ security. The central lesson is that data protection is a shared responsibility among technology providers, governments, and users, requiring an integrated digital security culture to preserve information integrity in the digital age.
