As of Nov 30th: Cotton arrivals stand at 5.19m bales: PCGA

As of Nov 30th: Cotton arrivals stand at 5.19m bales: PCGA

Pakistan’s cotton sector is facing a critical decline in production, ranking significantly lower globally than its historical standing. This sharp drop creates a supply gap that forces the nation to rely heavily on costly imports to sustain its vital textile industry. The situation highlights a systemic failure where domestic agriculture can no longer meet industrial demand, threatening economic stability and increasing foreign exchange pressure. Addressing this crisis requires immediate, tangible policy interventions rather than administrative gestures. Experts argue that establishing guaranteed support prices for farmers, investing in climate-resilient seeds, and funding research institutions are essential steps. Furthermore, reforming tax structures and banning duty-free cotton imports can protect local growers from exploitation and encourage the textile sector to prioritize domestic sourcing, thereby revitalizing the entire value chain. This narrative is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as transparent, accurate agricultural statistics are foundational for effective policy-making. Without accessible and reliable data on crop yields, import dependencies, and market flows, stakeholders cannot design evidence-based solutions. Open data enables the scrutiny of trade patterns and the verification of claims regarding foreign influence, ensuring that interventions are driven by ground realities rather than political or external agendas.
