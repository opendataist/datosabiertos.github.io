Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions than the general population, including lower income, employment disparities, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Despite these structural disadvantages and socio-economic challenges, immigrants report life satisfaction levels comparable to native-born Norwegians. This contrast highlights a complex reality where objective hardship does not necessarily correlate with subjective well-being, challenging simplistic narratives about integration success or failure. Furthermore, immigrants demonstrate a distinctively high trust in public institutions, such as the political system, judiciary, and police, which exceeds that of the general population. This strong institutional confidence suggests a resilient social contract and a positive orientation toward Norwegian governance, even amid personal struggles. This finding is crucial for understanding social cohesion, as it indicates that immigrants may view the state as a reliable partner despite experiencing marginalization in other areas of daily life. This article is highly relevant to open data because it showcases the power of publicly available statistics to reveal nuanced societal dynamics that qualitative accounts alone might miss. By making detailed survey data accessible, Statistics Norway enables researchers and policymakers to analyze the interplay between structural inequality and individual satisfaction. Open access to such granular data fosters evidence-based dialogue, allowing for more targeted interventions to address specific vulnerabilities, such as mental health and loneliness, while leveraging the strong community trust in public institutions to drive inclusive policy improvements.
