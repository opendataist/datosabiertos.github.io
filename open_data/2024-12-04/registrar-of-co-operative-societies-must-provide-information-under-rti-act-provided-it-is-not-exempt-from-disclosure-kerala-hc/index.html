Registrar Of Co-Operative Societies Must Provide Information Under RTI Act Provided It Is Not Exempt From Disclosure: Kerala HC

Registrar Of Co-Operative Societies Must Provide Information Under RTI Act Provided It Is Not Exempt From Disclosure: Kerala HC

The High Court affirmed that the Registrar of Co-operative Societies qualifies as a public authority under the Right to Information Act, obligating them to disclose specific documents held by co-operative societies. The ruling emphasizes that unless information is explicitly exempt under confidentiality provisions, the Registrar must leverage their statutory power to collect such records and provide them to applicants. This decision reinforces the principle that public authorities cannot hide behind the private nature of an entity’s internal agreements when those records fall within the Registrar’s purview. The judgment clarifies the hierarchy between sector-specific legislation and transparency laws, establishing that the RTI Act takes precedence over the Co-operative Societies Act regarding information disclosure. By dismissing arguments that certain documents were protected as trade secrets or fiduciary data, the Court upheld the right of citizens to access information that aids in ensuring accountability. This ensures that public interest outweighs claims of private secrecy when the information pertains to public activities or contractual obligations managed by registered entities. This case is vital to open data initiatives as it expands the scope of accessible government-held information to include co-operative societies, which are critical components of local infrastructure and economy. It establishes a legal precedent that strengthens transparency by treating these organizations as subjects of public scrutiny, thereby enhancing civic oversight. For open data advocates, this ruling validates the inclusion of cooperative sector data in public domains, promoting greater accountability and reducing opacity in public-financed or regulated activities.
