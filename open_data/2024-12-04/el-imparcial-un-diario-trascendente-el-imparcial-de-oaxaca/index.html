EL IMPARCIAL, un diario trascendente - El Imparcial de Oaxaca

The article reflects on the precarious current state of transparency in Mexico, where constitutional reforms and the dismantling of key oversight bodies threaten the right to access public information. In this context of regression, it is vital to remember the historical struggle that established these rights, particularly the role of the Oaxaca Group. This collective pioneered the legislative framework for transparency, shifting the paradigm from government secrecy to the presumption of public access, a foundation that is now fragile but essential to recall. Central to this achievement were the strategic alliances between journalists, civil society, and institutions like *El Imparcial* de Oaxaca and the Mexican Association of Editors. Their active lobbying and editorial advocacy were decisive in passing the 2002 Federal Transparency Law and creating state-level guarantee bodies. By bridging the gap between legislative intent and political reality, these entities helped institutionalize the concept that information is a fundamental democratic right rather than a privilege granted by the state. This narrative is highly relevant to open data because it highlights that transparency infrastructure relies on sustained civic pressure and institutional memory, not just legal texts. Understanding the historical efforts required to overcome resistance to openness provides a crucial lesson for current advocates. It underscores that maintaining and modernizing open data frameworks requires the same proactive engagement and political will that were necessary to create them initially, ensuring that technological advancements do not outpace the democratic foundations of information access.
