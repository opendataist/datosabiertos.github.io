Lo que sigue

Lo que sigue

Mexico’s Senate approved a constitutional reform abolishing the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI) and transferring its functions to the Secretariat for Anticorruption and Good Governance. This significant institutional shift threatens the independence of mechanisms that have historically safeguarded citizens’ right to access public information. By moving oversight away from an autonomous body, the reform risks weakening the checks and balances necessary to prevent government opacity and potential abuses of power. The transition period requires careful legal design and the inclusion of diverse stakeholders, including experts, academics, and current INAI personnel, to ensure continuity. Specialized knowledge must be preserved to maintain effective protection of personal data and transparency. A major challenge lies in informing the public about the new responsible entities and ensuring that the new structure does not dilute accountability or create confusion regarding who guarantees these fundamental rights. This development is critically relevant to open data because transparent governance relies on robust, independent institutions that enforce data availability and privacy standards. If the new framework fails to provide adequate guarantees, it could hinder the open sharing of government information, reducing civic engagement and innovation. Ultimately, the success of open data initiatives in Mexico depends on whether the new institutional design truly reinforces, rather than undermines, the legal guarantees for transparency and data protection.
