En Veracruz, mujeres pasan el doble de tiempo que hombres en labores no remuneradas

En Veracruz, mujeres pasan el doble de tiempo que hombres en labores no remuneradas

The article highlights a significant gender disparity in unpaid labor, revealing that women in Veracruz devote substantially more time to domestic duties and caregiving than men. This imbalance underscores a systemic issue where female participation in non-remunerated work remains disproportionately high compared to their male counterparts. This pattern is relevant to open data as it demonstrates how national statistical agencies like INEGI can quantify social inequalities through time-use surveys. By making such detailed data publicly available, authorities enable researchers and policymakers to identify specific areas of concern and develop targeted interventions. Consequently, this transparency fosters accountability and supports evidence-based decision-making. It allows society to recognize the invisible burden carried by women, promoting discussions on equity and resource allocation. Ultimately, accessible data empowers communities to advocate for policies that reduce these persistent gaps in the distribution of domestic responsibilities.
