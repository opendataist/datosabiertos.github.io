Ley de Transparencia Social: 34 organizaciones de la sociedad civil alertan debilitamiento a sus estructuras y anuncian demanda de inconstitucionalidad

Ley de Transparencia Social: 34 organizaciones de la sociedad civil alertan debilitamiento a sus estructuras y anuncian demanda de inconstitucionalidad

Thirty-four civil society organizations have filed an unconstitutionality lawsuit against the newly enacted Social Transparency Law in Ecuador. They argue that the legislation severely undermines public trust and threatens the continuity of these groups by presenting them as suspect entities without empirical evidence. The official discourse used during the legislative debates stigmatized NGOs, potentially deterring private and international funding essential for sustaining community and human rights projects. The law imposes disproportionate administrative and economic burdens, such as requiring quarterly financial reports with standards equivalent to commercial corporations. These requirements are deemed unviable for grassroots and rural organizations operating with minimal budgets and voluntary work. Furthermore, the statute includes unrelated regulatory topics and grants authorities broad discretionary power to dissolve organizations for vague administrative infractions, which disproportionately restricts freedom of association. This situation is relevant to open data because it highlights how transparency legislation can be weaponized to suppress civil society rather than enhance accountability. When data disclosure requirements are overly burdensome or lack technical consensus, they can effectively silence the very actors needed for democratic development. This case demonstrates the critical need for inclusive, evidence-based policy-making to ensure that transparency mechanisms strengthen, rather than dismantle, the institutional framework necessary for a healthy civil society.
