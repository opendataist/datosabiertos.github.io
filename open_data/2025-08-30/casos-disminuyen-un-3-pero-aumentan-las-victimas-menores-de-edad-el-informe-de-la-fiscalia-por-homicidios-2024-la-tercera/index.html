Casos disminuyen un 3%, pero aumentan las víctimas menores de edad: el informe de la fiscalía por homicidios 2024 - La Tercera

Casos disminuyen un 3%, pero aumentan las víctimas menores de edad: el informe de la fiscalía por homicidios 2024 - La Tercera

The National Prosecutor’s Office report on homicides in 2024 reveals a complex statistical reality: while the total number of recorded cases appears to have decreased, there has been a sustained increase in fatal victims since 2016. The observed percentage reduction is primarily due to the inclusion of attempted crimes and uncompleted frauds, underscoring the importance of distinguishing between completed offenses and attempts when assessing public safety. This distinction is crucial for understanding the true scale of the violent crime problem, preventing aggregated data from obscuring the upward trend in actual deaths. The demographic profile of victims and perpetrators shows significant structural changes, particularly the rise in child homicides and the growing involvement of foreign nationals both as victims and aggressors. Moreover, the geographic distribution of these crimes is concentrated in specific areas, highlighting critical hotspots of violence and the influence of organized crime in certain territories. These patterns indicate that violence is not uniform but is shaped by social, demographic, and territorial factors that require detailed analysis to be effectively addressed. This article is relevant to the open data community because it illustrates the challenges involved in integrating, standardizing, and visualizing public security data. To be useful, open data must overcome methodological discrepancies among different agencies, such as those between prosecutor’s offices and observatories, and provide clear indicators that enable citizens and policymakers to analyze long-term trends. Transparency in case classification, demographics, and context is essential for generating evidence-based public policies that are robust and accessible.
