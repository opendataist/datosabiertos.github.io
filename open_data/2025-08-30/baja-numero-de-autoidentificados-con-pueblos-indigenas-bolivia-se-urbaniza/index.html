Baja número de autoidentificados con pueblos indígenas; Bolivia se urbaniza

Baja número de autoidentificados con pueblos indígenas; Bolivia se urbaniza

The 2024 Bolivian Census reveals a significant demographic shift, highlighting a progressive decline in indigenous self-identification and a steady trend toward urbanization. The data indicates that less than half of the population now identifies with indigenous origins, a notable decrease from previous decades. Concurrently, the majority of citizens reside in urban areas, reflecting a sustained migration pattern over the last twenty years. This transformation suggests evolving social dynamics and regional distribution challenges that require updated policy frameworks. Technological access further illustrates the urban-rural divide, with mobile phones achieving near-universal coverage across the country. While cellular connectivity bridges some gaps, access to computers and fixed internet remains disproportionately low in rural regions. These disparities underscore a digital inequality that affects information access and economic opportunities, particularly for those living outside major cities. The dominance of mobile technology over traditional infrastructure marks a distinct change in how the population communicates and accesses services. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the value of transparent, high-quality demographic statistics in measuring social progress. By publishing detailed census results, the National Institute of Statistics enables researchers and policymakers to analyze trends in identity, migration, and digital inclusion. Open access to this data fosters accountability, allowing society to evaluate the effectiveness of public policies and address emerging inequalities through evidence-based decision-making.
