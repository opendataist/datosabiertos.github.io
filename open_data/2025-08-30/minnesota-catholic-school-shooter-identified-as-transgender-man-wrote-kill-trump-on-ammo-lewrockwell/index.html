The relevance to open data lies in how publicly available digital footprints, such as social media posts, video uploads, and legal name change records, are used to construct public narratives about criminal motives. These open sources provide investigators and the media with immediate access to the perpetrator’s intentions, beliefs, and history. However, this reliance on unverified online content raises critical questions about data accuracy, context, and the potential for bias in how open information shapes public understanding of complex social issues. Ultimately, the article illustrates the dual nature of open data as both a tool for transparency and a catalyst for polarized interpretation. While open records can enhance accountability and understanding, they also allow for selective storytelling that may reinforce existing societal divides. This case underscores the importance of critically evaluating open data sources, recognizing that readily available information can be interpreted in multiple ways, often influenced by the reader’s preexisting beliefs rather than objective facts alone.
