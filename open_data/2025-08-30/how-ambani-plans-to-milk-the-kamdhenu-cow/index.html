How Ambani plans to milk the Kamdhenu cow

How Ambani plans to milk the Kamdhenu cow

Reliance Industries is positioning itself as a central driver of India’s artificial intelligence revolution by launching Reliance Intelligence, a new subsidiary dedicated to building next-generation AI infrastructure. This initiative aims to make advanced computing power and deep-tech solutions accessible to the broader Indian market, mirroring the company’s past success in democratizing digital connectivity. By establishing gigawatt-scale, green-energy-powered data centers, Reliance seeks to create the foundational hardware necessary to support national-scale AI training and inference. The strategy relies heavily on strategic partnerships with global tech leaders like Google and Meta to integrate open-source models and cloud capabilities into the Indian ecosystem. These collaborations are designed to deliver sovereign, enterprise-ready AI services that are both affordable and compliant with local regulations. By combining international technological expertise with Reliance’s extensive domestic reach, the conglomerate intends to empower businesses and consumers alike, ensuring that AI adoption is resilient, scalable, and tailored to India’s specific needs. This development is highly relevant to the open data and open technology movement because it explicitly leverages open-source AI models and fosters collaborative innovation. By making tools like Meta’s Llama accessible to Indian entrepreneurs and developers, Reliance promotes a decentralized approach to technology creation. This shift not only accelerates local innovation but also supports the open data principle by encouraging the widespread availability and adaptability of core technologies, thereby lowering barriers to entry for diverse creators and small businesses across the country.
