Perú advierte sobre peligros de usar varias aplicaciones móviles de préstamos de dinero

Perú advierte sobre peligros de usar varias aplicaciones móviles de préstamos de dinero

The Peruvian National Authority for Personal Data Protection warns that numerous mobile lending applications fail to comply with data protection laws. Crucially, these apps lack essential privacy policies and fail to inform users that downloading them grants unauthorized access to their entire contact lists and device data. This transparency gap exposes users to severe privacy risks by allowing unknown entities to harvest sensitive personal information. While Google asserts it prohibits harmful apps and can disable non-compliant developer accounts, the regulatory body notes that effective verification remains challenging. The authority identified specific applications that not only miss privacy requirements but also provide false contact addresses, further undermining user trust and legal accountability in the digital marketplace. This case is vital for open data discussions as it highlights the friction between proprietary platform control and public regulatory oversight. It underscores the necessity for transparent data handling practices in fintech. By exposing how private apps evade information requirements, this incident emphasizes the need for stronger mechanisms to ensure algorithmic and data transparency, protecting citizen rights against opaque commercial data extraction.
