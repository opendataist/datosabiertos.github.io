A Michigan judge has ruled that the Attorney General’s office must release exhibits introduced during the open preliminary hearings of the Governor’s attempted kidnapping case. The court determined that once documents are presented in public judicial proceedings, they lose their status as confidential investigative records and must be made accessible to the public under Freedom of Information Act laws, rejecting the AG’s claim that they remained protected attorney work product. This decision highlights a significant contradiction between the Attorney General’s public advocacy for government transparency and her office’s actual practice of withholding high-profile case documents. By enforcing the release of these records without additional redactions, the ruling challenges the notion that ongoing legal strategies or internal reviews justify secrecy for materials already visible to the public, thereby reinforcing the principle that government operations should remain open to scrutiny. The outcome is crucial for open_data initiatives because it establishes a clear legal precedent regarding the public’s right to access evidentiary materials in criminal cases. It confirms that transparency commitments cannot be selectively applied and ensures that citizens and journalists can obtain critical information necessary for understanding government actions and maintaining accountability in high-stakes political matters.
