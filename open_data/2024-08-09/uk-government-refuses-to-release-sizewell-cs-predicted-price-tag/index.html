UK Government refuses to release Sizewell C’s predicted price tag

UK Government refuses to release Sizewell C’s predicted price tag

The UK government’s refusal to disclose Sizewell C’s costs under commercial sensitivity exemptions highlights a significant transparency gap in major infrastructure projects. By withholding financial data, officials limit public scrutiny of potential budget overruns and funding uncertainties. This lack of openness contradicts the principles of open data, which advocate for accessible information to ensure accountability. The decision obscures the true economic impact of the nuclear plant, preventing stakeholders from evaluating the validity of state investments. When cost data remains confidential, it becomes difficult to assess whether public resources are being managed efficiently. This opacity hinders informed public debate and reduces trust in governmental decision-making processes regarding large-scale energy developments. Relevant to open data, this case illustrates how “commercial sensitivity” is often used to justify non-disclosure of public interest matters. Advocating for greater transparency requires challenging such exemptions when they impede democratic oversight. Ensuring that critical infrastructure costs are publicly available supports better policy analysis and fosters a more engaged, informed citizenry capable of holding institutions accountable.
