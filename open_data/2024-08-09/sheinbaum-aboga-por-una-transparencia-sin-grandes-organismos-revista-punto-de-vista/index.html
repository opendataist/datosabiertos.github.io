Sheinbaum aboga por una transparencia sin grandes organismos | Revista Punto de Vista

Sheinbaum aboga por una transparencia sin grandes organismos | Revista Punto de Vista

Claudia Sheinbaum questions the necessity of autonomous transparency bodies, arguing that bureaucratic size does not prevent corruption. This perspective suggests a shift away from specialized independent institutions toward internal government mechanisms, implying that transparency can be managed more efficiently without complex structural overlays. Her stance contrasts with previous defenses of such entities, highlighting a potential redesign of accountability frameworks. The debate centers on whether independent oversight is essential for democratic rights or merely an inefficient layer. Sheinbaum’s focus on direct dialogue and internal responsibility signals a move toward consolidating transparency functions within existing ministries. This approach risks undermining the separation of powers that autonomous institutions provide, potentially affecting how data access and rights are protected by the state. This development is critical for open data because it impacts the independence of data governance. If autonomous oversight is dissolved or weakened, the reliability and neutrality of public information may be compromised. Open data initiatives rely on transparent, verifiable sources free from political interference. Changes to these structures directly influence how citizens and developers access and trust government data for civic engagement and innovation.
