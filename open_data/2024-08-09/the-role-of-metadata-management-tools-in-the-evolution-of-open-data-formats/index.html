The role of metadata management tools in the evolution of open data formats

The role of metadata management tools in the evolution of open data formats

The data platform landscape is undergoing a significant shift as organizations transition from proprietary formats to open table standards like Iceberg and Delta Lake. While major vendors currently hold influence over these technologies, the industry is moving toward coexistence rather than immediate consolidation. The critical challenge lies in metadata management, as the underlying data formats share common on-disk structures, but their metadata layers remain fragmented and largely incompatible across different vendors. This evolution highlights that the "source of truth" in modern data architectures is no longer just the raw data, but the metadata that governs it. Technical, operational, and semantic layers must be unified to ensure cohesive and secure data processing. However, current tools struggle to seamlessly integrate these different types of metadata, particularly when supporting complex operations like knowledge graphs alongside traditional SQL databases. The industry is still in the early stages of developing universal, open-source environments that can handle these diverse requirements without locking users into specific vendors. This topic is vital to open_data because it underscores the necessity of standardization and interoperability. As the field matures, the demand for open, universal metadata catalogs will drive the adoption of open formats, ensuring that data remains accessible and usable across multiple engines. By advocating for open-source capabilities and universal compatibility, the open_data community helps prevent vendor lock-in and fosters a more flexible, resilient data ecosystem for the future.
