Condenan a 3 años de prisión al ex secretario de Comercio de CFK

Condenan a 3 años de prisión al ex secretario de Comercio de CFK

The recent conviction of Guillermo Moreno for abuse of authority, but not for statistical manipulation, illustrates the complex relationship between political governance and the technical integrity of data. Although the courts acknowledged the methodological accuracy of the figures produced by INDEC (the National Institute of Statistics and Censuses), the case underscores how government interference in statistical agencies can generate systemic distrust, even when numerical results are not artificially altered. This event highlights the vulnerability of public statistics to political pressure—a central challenge for the open data movement. Distrust stemming from perceptions of institutional unmanageability or bias hinders the adoption of official data as reliable tools for social and economic analysis. Technical independence must be safeguarded through procedural transparency and independent audits, ensuring that citizens can trust the information without questioning its legitimacy for political reasons. The relevance to open data lies in the need to establish rigorous standards for publication and methodological explanation. By making accessible the processes by which indicators are generated, external verification is enabled and the scope for accusations of fraud is reduced. This case demonstrates that mere data availability is insufficient; trust is built through clear communication about the underlying processes, ensuring that transparency is perceived as genuine rather than as an institutional facade.
