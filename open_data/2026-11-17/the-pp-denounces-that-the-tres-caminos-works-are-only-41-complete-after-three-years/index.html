The PP denounces that the Tres Caminos works are only 41% complete after three years

The article highlights the severe management and transparency crisis surrounding the construction of the Tres Caminos interchange in Cádiz. Despite having received substantial financial certifications, the project has barely surpassed 40% of its execution. Leaders of the People’s Party emphasize that the lack of detailed information regarding final costs and timelines for resumption reflects an institutional “obscurantism,” which fuels uncertainty about the safety and viability of an infrastructure essential for regional mobility. An urgent independent audit is being demanded to clarify the causes of the accumulated delays dating back to the project phase, accusing the central government of mismanagement and neglect, especially in contrast to the efficiency demonstrated by the regional administration in other projects. The declared priority is to ensure that the completion of the interchange is carried out with full technical guarantees, calling for political accountability and tangible solutions that restore confidence in state-dependent public infrastructure planning. The relevance of this text for open data lies in how public data, obtained through transparency, become key tools for citizen and political oversight. It illustrates the critical value of accessible information for detecting budgetary deviations and delays, demonstrating that without robust mechanisms for data publication and monitoring, accountability in projects with significant social impact is compromised due to the lack of specificity in official responses.
