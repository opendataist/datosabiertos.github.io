Government ordered to hand over reports on migrant minors in the Canary Islands

Government ordered to hand over reports on migrant minors in the Canary Islands

The Council for Transparency and Good Governance has ordered the Ministry of Inclusion, Social Security and Migration to provide reports on unaccompanied migrant minors in the Canary Islands, rejecting the ministry’s previous refusal. The initial administration argued that disclosure could harm an ongoing judicial proceeding and infringe upon the privacy of the minors. However, the ruling states that the mere existence of a legal process does not automatically justify administrative secrecy, requiring concrete evidence of harm that the Ministry failed to substantiate. This case highlights the tension between procedural confidentiality and public transparency, establishing that administrative reports, even when submitted to the Supreme Court, remain documents subject to the right of access. The transparency body emphasizes that the public interest in understanding how judicial measures are implemented and how migration policies are managed outweighs generic protection arguments, especially since the administration had already shared this information with other public entities. The relevance for open data lies in the consolidation of a precedent that limits the abusive use of judicial status to withhold information of public utility. Additionally, administrative inefficiency is criticized due to the six-month delay in responding, which contravenes legal timeframes. This decision strengthens state accountability in sensitive matters, ensuring that citizens can verify the government’s compliance with its obligations toward vulnerable groups.
