Diario HOY | Biometric Data: “The risk is much higher than losing a password

The collapse of Ueno Bank has exposed the severe vulnerability of Paraguayan citizens to the indiscriminate use of biometric and personal data. The normalization of submitting sensitive information for routine procedures has created an environment where digital identity is easily spoofed, with irreversible consequences since, unlike passwords, biometric characteristics cannot be changed. The accumulation of behavioral information gives these data significant hidden economic value, placing the nation’s digital assets in the millions. However, regulatory oversight has been insufficient, leaving massive databases without effective controls to ensure their security or clearly define access and use by financial entities and the guarantee system. This crisis underscores the urgent need to reform current legislation, which was weakened by state cuts, to establish an independent regulatory authority and impose robust sanctions. The relevance to open data lies in the critical need to balance transparency and the use of public information with strict legal frameworks that protect individual privacy, preventing the availability of data from becoming a tool for mass exploitation or fraud.
