This text explores the hidden mechanisms of power in twentieth-century Mexico, arguing that true political influence extended far beyond elected officials to include intelligence operatives, bureaucrats, and behind-the-scenes negotiators. By examining these “architects,” the author reveals a complex system that balanced authoritarian control with political negotiation, demonstrating how decisions were often made in private offices rather than public forums. This perspective challenges traditional historical narratives that focus solely on presidential biographies, offering a more nuanced understanding of how the Mexican political machine actually functioned. The work emphasizes the necessity of distinguishing between documented facts, probable events, and legends when studying opaque sectors such as intelligence and security. It advocates for a critical approach that avoids reducing historical figures to simplistic heroes or villains, instead analyzing their contradictions and the specific circumstances that shaped their actions. By acknowledging that myths often reflect political reality as much as truth, the text encourages readers to understand the logic, fears, and codes that guided these operatives, providing deeper insight into the enduring culture of loyalty and secrecy that influenced national outcomes. This analysis is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical importance of accessing information that is typically excluded from official records, such as unpublished agreements, intelligence reports, and private testimonies. In open governance, the struggle is not just for existing public data, but for the transparency of those informal networks and decision-making processes that truly shape policy. Understanding these “architects” requires overcoming the opacity of state secrets, reminding us that a complete picture of democracy depends on exposing the informal structures and hidden actors that have historically operated in the shadows, often leaving gaps in the public historical record.
