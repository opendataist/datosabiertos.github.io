Modi degree case: RTI’s purpose not to satisfy curiosity, Delhi University tells High Court

The Delhi High Court recently addressed a significant legal interpretation of the Right to Information Act, centered on whether public institutions must disclose third-party educational records. The Solicitor General argued that while the Act mandates transparency, its purpose is not to satisfy curiosity but to ensure accountability. He emphasized that personal academic data, when requested by unauthorized third parties, falls under exemptions designed to protect individual privacy rights. This case highlights the tension between public interest and personal data protection in open data ecosystems. The university contends that revealing student roll numbers, marks, and pass/fail status to anyone other than the individuals concerned violates privacy laws. This stance suggests that even in public institutions, certain categories of sensitive information should remain restricted to maintain trust and legal compliance, challenging the assumption that all institutional data is inherently open. The relevance to open data lies in defining the boundaries of accessibility. While promoting transparency is crucial for democratic oversight, this ruling underscores the necessity of balancing it with robust privacy safeguards. It serves as a critical reminder that open data initiatives must carefully navigate legal exemptions to avoid misuse, ensuring that the push for information freedom does not inadvertently compromise the personal rights of citizens involved in public records.
