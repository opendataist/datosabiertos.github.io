EU Eyes Next Moves on China Medical Device Bias

EU Eyes Next Moves on China Medical Device Bias

The European Commission has concluded that China systematically discriminates against EU medical device producers in public procurement, restricting their access to government contracts. This finding stems from the first investigation under the EU’s International Procurement Instrument, which identifies unfair practices that hinder European suppliers across all medical device categories. The report confirms that these barriers are widespread and significantly impair market entry for EU companies within China. In response, the Commission is assessing potential countermeasures to restore a level playing field. While the EU remains open to constructive dialogue to eliminate discriminatory practices, it is prepared to implement restrictions on Chinese bidders in EU public tenders if no acceptable solution is reached. These actions aim to enforce reciprocity, leveraging the openness of the EU’s own market, which has seen a surge in Chinese medical exports, to pressure China into fairer trade conditions. This development is crucial for open_data advocacy as it highlights the necessity of transparent, accessible public procurement data to identify market barriers and justify trade interventions. By documenting specific discrimination, the report underscores how data-driven evidence supports efforts to ensure equitable global access. It reinforces the principle that open, non-discriminatory markets depend on rigorous monitoring and the ability to take proportional, evidence-based actions when reciprocity fails, ultimately promoting fairness in international economic relations.
