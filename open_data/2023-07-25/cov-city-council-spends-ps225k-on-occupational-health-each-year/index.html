Cov City Council spends £225k on occupational health each year

Coventry City Council demonstrates that robust occupational health services provide significant economic value by substantially reducing sick leave and enhancing employee performance. By investing in in-house ergonomics and physiotherapy, the council addresses prevalent workstation injuries, proving that proactive health management is a cost-effective strategy for public bodies rather than merely an expense. The article highlights a critical gap in national accessibility, with less than half of employees having access to similar comprehensive care. Experts warn that prolonged device use causes preventable strain, emphasizing the urgent need for proper workstation setups and regular breaks. This underscores the broader societal responsibility to mitigate physical health risks associated with modern digital lifestyles. This case is relevant to open data because it showcases how Freedom of Information requests can reveal hidden municipal spending and health outcomes. By making these financial and health metrics transparent, governments can drive accountability and encourage other organizations to adopt evidence-based, cost-efficient health policies. This transparency empowers citizens to demand better workplace standards and promotes systemic improvements in public sector well-being initiatives.
