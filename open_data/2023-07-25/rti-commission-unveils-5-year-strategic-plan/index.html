RTI Commission unveils 5-year strategic plan

The Right to Information Commission has introduced a comprehensive five-year strategic plan to deepen its regional presence and sustain public sensitization regarding access rights. This initiative aims to foster greater transparency and accountability among public institutions by ensuring the law is operationalized through the collaborative efforts of the public, information officers, and the commission. By targeting strategic areas such as gender, youth, and disability, the plan seeks to overcome historical cultural barriers where public discourse was previously viewed as secretive. The effectiveness of this framework is evident in the growing number of information requests focused on critical sectors like the economy, infrastructure, and public safety. As more citizens engage with the system, the commission is actively sanctioning non-compliant institutions, signaling a shift toward a culture of openness. This engagement demonstrates that Ghanaians are increasingly embracing their right to know, validating the necessity of structured mechanisms to facilitate and protect these disclosures in a manner that holds government entities to account. This article is highly relevant to open data as it illustrates the institutional backbone required to transform raw government information into accessible public resources. It highlights that open data initiatives cannot succeed solely through technology; they require robust legal mandates, dedicated regulatory oversight, and active public participation to ensure data is not just published, but meaningfully utilized. The strategic emphasis on resource mobilization and multi-stakeholder collaboration provides a vital roadmap for maintaining the infrastructure that supports transparent, open, and usable public information ecosystems.
