Inestabilidad en el terreno mantiene en alerta a Quetame y a otros tres municipios

The sudden death of 26 people and the subsequent instability in Quetame highlight a critical gap in local risk management and disaster preparedness. The event underscores the urgent need for transparent, accessible data regarding geological risks and infrastructure resilience. In contexts where informal settlements and remote rural areas are frequently overlooked, the lack of comprehensive, real-time open data regarding terrain stability and historical flood patterns prevents authorities from implementing effective preventive measures, ultimately leading to tragic, predictable losses. Beyond immediate rescue efforts, the ongoing threat of landslides and the necessity for preventive evacuations reveal systemic vulnerabilities that are often obscured by fragmented reporting. By making geospatial data, seismic activity records, and infrastructure integrity reports publicly available, civil society and independent researchers can cross-reference these with official responses. This transparency is essential for holding institutions accountable and ensuring that resource allocation for mitigation and infrastructure repair is based on accurate, verifiable information rather than reactive political decisions. Furthermore, the disruption of critical transportation links between regions illustrates how infrastructure failures impact broader economic and social networks, affecting access to services and market connectivity. Open data initiatives can model these interdependencies, allowing for better planning of alternative routes and resource distribution during crises. Ultimately, prioritizing open data in disaster management fosters community resilience, enabling affected populations to understand their risk exposure and participate actively in reconstruction efforts, ensuring that recovery strategies are inclusive, informed, and sustainable.
