Tariff hike: Nigeria’s internet users decline – NCC Report |

Tariff hike: Nigeria’s internet users decline – NCC Report |

Nigeria’s telecommunications sector recently faced a significant downturn in internet adoption following a substantial tariff hike on voice, data, and SMS services. This pricing adjustment triggered a sharp decline in monthly data consumption and caused a temporary loss of internet users, illustrating how immediate economic pressures can directly suppress consumer demand for digital connectivity. Although a slight recovery in usage occurred later, overall habits remained cautious, highlighting the fragile balance between affordability and accessibility for subscribers. Despite the struggles in internet services, the broader telecommunications market demonstrated resilience through continued growth in telephone lines. Operator market shares remained largely stable, with major players maintaining dominance while smaller entities faced subscriber attrition. This divergence reveals that while high costs may restrict data-heavy applications, basic voice connectivity remains essential and less susceptible to immediate contraction, allowing operators to sustain overall subscriber bases even as internet engagement wanes. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the critical need for transparent, accessible industry statistics to monitor consumer behavior and market health. Publicly available data on tariffs, usage patterns, and subscriber movements enables researchers, policymakers, and civil society to analyze the real-world impact of pricing regulations on digital inclusion. Such transparency is essential for advocating policies that ensure telecommunications services remain affordable and equitable for all citizens.
