Fabrizio Peralta: “Se sabe que en la Bahía se venden datos personales”

Fabrizio Peralta: “Se sabe que en la Bahía se venden datos personales”

The article emphasizes that legal frameworks alone are insufficient to change the prevailing culture of data negligence, where personal information is often wrongly treated as an organizational asset rather than a private right. The core issue is that existing laws lack detailed directives on how entities should practically protect data, leaving a gap between legal intent and operational security. Consequently, organizations must shift their paradigm by implementing robust technical, organizational, and administrative measures to ensure confidentiality, moving beyond simple confidentiality agreements. The relevance to open data lies in the critical importance of protecting personal information within publicly accessible or state-managed ecosystems. The narrative demonstrates that without strict security protocols and a cultural shift towards privacy by design, the openness of data can lead to severe harms, such as identity theft and financial fraud. It underscores that transparency must be balanced with security, ensuring that data handling practices do not expose individuals to risk, which is a fundamental principle for maintaining trust in digital systems. Finally, the effectiveness of data protection relies heavily on institutional capacity and adequate resources. The recently established supervisory body faces significant challenges due to understaffing and limited budgets, which delay investigations and enforcement. This illustrates that regulatory oversight requires sustained investment and specialized training to properly monitor compliance. Without these resources, even well-intentioned laws remain ineffective, leaving citizens vulnerable to misuse of their personal data by both private and public entities.
