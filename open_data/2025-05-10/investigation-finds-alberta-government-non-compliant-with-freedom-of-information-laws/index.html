Investigation finds Alberta government non-compliant with freedom of information laws

Investigation finds Alberta government non-compliant with freedom of information laws

Alberta’s government has been found non-compliant with freedom of information laws after an investigation revealed that internal policies routinely allowed employees to wrongfully deny public records requests. The watchdog determined that departments violated their legal duty to assist applicants by refusing requests based on insufficient detail, a practice that placed an unreasonable burden on citizens who cannot possibly know the specific contents of government archives. This systemic failure demonstrates how bureaucratic obstacles can effectively obstruct transparency, undermining the fundamental principle that access to information should be a right exercised by the public, not a privilege negotiated with the state. The report highlights that the government’s strategy of narrowing request scopes to manage workloads was unlawful, as the responsibility to clarify and locate records lies with the public body, not the applicant. This finding is critically relevant to the open data movement because it exposes how institutional resistance can stall the availability of public information. For open data initiatives to succeed, governments must move beyond mere compliance and actively facilitate access rather than creating administrative barriers. Ensuring that data is accessible by default, rather than requiring complex justification from citizens, is essential for fostering true accountability and transparency in public administration.
