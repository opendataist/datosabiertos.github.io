RTI saga: Millions paid as penalty - MyJoyOnline

RTI saga: Millions paid as penalty - MyJoyOnline

Ghana’s Right to Information Act faces significant implementation challenges, as many critical governance institutions are either refusing or failing to provide requested data to citizens. This systemic non-compliance has resulted in substantial fines imposed by the Right to Information Commission. However, the collection of these penalties is complicated by widespread unpaid debts and ongoing litigation, with many public entities contesting the charges or delaying payment, thereby undermining the law’s immediate enforcement power. The financial penalties levied against state institutions are funded by taxpayers rather than targeting the specific individuals responsible for withholding information. This dynamic weakens the deterrent effect, as institutional heads face no personal consequences for non-compliance. Consequently, the fines act merely as revenue for the commission and a cost for the institution, failing to address the root cause of secrecy or encourage individual accountability within the bureaucratic structures. This situation is vital for the open data community because it highlights the gap between legal frameworks and actual transparency practices. The lack of detailed implementation regulations and unclear criteria for penalties further complicates efforts to standardize proactive disclosure. True open data initiatives require not just the right to request information, but active institutional willingness to publish data routinely, ensuring that transparency is enforced through accountability rather than just financial penalties.
