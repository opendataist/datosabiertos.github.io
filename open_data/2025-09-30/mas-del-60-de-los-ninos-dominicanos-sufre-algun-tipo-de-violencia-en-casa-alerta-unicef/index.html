Más del 60% de los niños dominicanos sufre algún tipo de violencia en casa, alerta Unicef

Más del 60% de los niños dominicanos sufre algún tipo de violencia en casa, alerta Unicef

A recent UNICEF report highlights that domestic violence affects a significant majority of children in the Dominican Republic, revealing a severe crisis in child protection. This alarming prevalence indicates that societal structures currently fail to guarantee basic safety and well-being for minors, necessitating an urgent shift away from normalizing abuse toward robust protective frameworks. Beyond violence, the data exposes systemic risks such as early unions, adolescent pregnancies, and hazardous child labor. These interconnected challenges underscore how socioeconomic inequalities and weak enforcement of laws continue to truncate the potential of young people, highlighting critical gaps in social services and legal safeguards that require immediate policy intervention. This article is vital for open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistics drive public awareness and policy reform. By making these indicators publicly available, stakeholders can identify trends, hold governments accountable, and collaborate on evidence-based solutions. Open data transforms raw vulnerability metrics into actionable insights, empowering civil society to advocate effectively for children’s rights and improved social outcomes.
