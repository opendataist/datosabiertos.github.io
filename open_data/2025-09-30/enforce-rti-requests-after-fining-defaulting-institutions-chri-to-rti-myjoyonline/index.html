Enforce RTI requests after fining defaulting institutions — CHRI to RTI - MyJoyOnline

Enforce RTI requests after fining defaulting institutions — CHRI to RTI - MyJoyOnline

The Commonwealth Human Rights Initiative argues that imposing financial penalties on institutions for denying information is insufficient without mandating the actual release of those records. Their investigation reveals that many government bodies, including the Police Service and Judiciary, treat fines as a mere cost of doing business rather than a mechanism to enforce transparency. This systemic failure undermines the core purpose of the Right to Information Act, allowing institutions to avoid accountability while continuing to withhold public data. This dynamic creates a significant barrier to effective open data practices and civic engagement. When transparency laws are circumvented through monetization of non-compliance, citizens cannot access critical information regarding justice, administration, or public services. Consequently, the legal framework fails to deliver substantive accountability, leaving vital records locked away despite official violations being acknowledged and penalized. The lack of enforced disclosure perpetuates opacity and erodes public trust in state institutions. For the open data community, this case highlights the urgent need for robust monitoring and enforcement mechanisms beyond punitive measures. It underscores that true transparency requires active compliance and continuous oversight to ensure data is not just declared available but is genuinely accessible. Strengthening the follow-up duties of regulatory bodies is essential to transform theoretical rights into practical access, ensuring that open data initiatives result in real-world accountability rather than procedural failures.
