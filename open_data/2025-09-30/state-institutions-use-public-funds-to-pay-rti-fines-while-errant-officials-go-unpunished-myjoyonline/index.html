State institutions use public funds to pay RTI fines while errant officials go unpunished - MyJoyOnline

State institutions use public funds to pay RTI fines while errant officials go unpunished - MyJoyOnline

The implementation of the Right to Information Act faces a critical accountability gap where public institutions systematically deny citizens access to information while utilizing taxpayer funds to settle fines imposed by the RTI Commission. This financial arrangement effectively shields individual officials from personal or professional consequences, as penalties are absorbed by the state budget rather than being charged to the specific decision-makers responsible for withholding data. Consequently, the punitive intent of the law is neutered, turning fines into mere administrative inconveniences rather than serious sanctions for misconduct. This practice creates a loophole that undermines the deterrent effect of transparency laws, allowing culpable officials to retain their positions and salaries without suffering any tangible hardship. By forcing the public to involuntarily fund the penalties for their own denied rights, the system fosters a culture of impunity. This dynamic risks reducing the RTI Act from a functional tool for accountability into a symbolic piece of legislation, thereby obstructing journalists, researchers, and citizens from demanding transparency and holding power structures accountable for public contracts and spending. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the structural barriers preventing the free flow of public information, which is essential for effective data advocacy and transparency initiatives. Open data ecosystems rely on institutional compliance and genuine willingness to share information; however, when non-compliance is financially uncoupled from individual responsibility, there is little incentive for officials to release data proactively. To preserve open data integrity, advocates must push for mechanisms that enforce personal liability and surcharges against obstructive officials, ensuring that transparency is a enforced duty rather than a voluntary gesture shielded by public funds.
