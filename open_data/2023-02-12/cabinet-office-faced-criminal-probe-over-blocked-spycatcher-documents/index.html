Cabinet Office faced criminal probe over blocked Spycatcher documents

The Information Commissioner’s Office investigated a complaint regarding the Cabinet Office’s handling of Freedom of Information requests about Peter Wright’s *Spycatcher* files. While the inquiry found that officials provided inconsistent and contradictory reasons for withholding the documents, it ultimately determined there was insufficient evidence of criminal misconduct. The review concluded that the legal justification for keeping the records classified was sound, despite the poor administrative conduct and misleading statements made to the requester. This case highlights significant issues with government transparency and the integrity of the Freedom of Information process. The complaint follows broader criticisms of the Cabinet Office’s lack of transparency and poor handling of information requests across the government. The discrepancy between the legal validity of withholding records and the deceptive methods used to justify them raises serious questions about trust in public institutions and the effectiveness of oversight mechanisms in ensuring accountability. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the friction between established secrecy norms and the push for greater data accessibility. It demonstrates how even when data is legally exempt from release, the manner in which that exemption is communicated can undermine public trust. For open data advocates, this underscores the importance not just of availability, but of honesty and procedural integrity in how governments manage and justify the withholding of information, ensuring that opaque decision-making does not masquerade as valid legal defense.
