Estudio: Más de 250 municipios en Bolivia son inviables y requieren fusión para sobrevivir

Estudio: Más de 250 municipios en Bolivia son inviables y requieren fusión para sobrevivir

A study based on recent census data reveals that Bolivia’s municipal viability is critically threatened, as the vast majority of municipalities lack the minimum demographic and economic thresholds required for sustainable operation. This structural fragility stems from historical decentralization policies that created an atomized administrative map without regard for sustainable population criteria. Small municipalities, particularly those in rural areas, cannot cover their basic operational costs or maintain essential services like education and infrastructure. The ongoing rural-to-urban migration exacerbates this crisis by depleting the active population and local tax bases, leaving many local governments unable to function effectively or contribute to regional investment projects. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how analyzing high-quality public statistics can expose systemic governance failures and drive necessary policy reforms. By making census data transparent and accessible, citizens and experts can advocate for evidence-based solutions, such as municipal mergers, ensuring equitable access to public services and promoting sustainable local development across the country.
