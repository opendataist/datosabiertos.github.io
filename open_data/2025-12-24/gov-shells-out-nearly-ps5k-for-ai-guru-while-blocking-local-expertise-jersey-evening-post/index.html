Gov shells out nearly £5k for AI guru while "blocking" local expertise - Jersey Evening Post

Gov shells out nearly £5k for AI guru while "blocking" local expertise - Jersey Evening Post

The article reveals a stark contradiction in government spending, where a public health department paid a celebrity tech writer nearly £4,700 for an exclusive AI workshop while simultaneously denying funding to Digital Jersey’s academy, the island’s central hub for digital upskilling. This disparity highlights a critical failure in resource allocation, suggesting that instead of building internal capacity, officials are opting for expensive, short-term external interventions. This approach undermines the sustainability of digital literacy efforts and raises questions about the strategic prioritization of workforce development within the public sector. Critics argue that the government is actively blocking its own digital experts from training civil servants, despite claims that free online resources are sufficient. The refusal to fund local initiatives that have successfully educated hundreds of residents indicates a disconnect between policy decisions and practical needs. By sidelining established organizations capable of delivering tailored education, the administration risks leaving its workforce ill-equipped to handle the opportunities and risks posed by rapid technological advancements, particularly regarding misinformation and data integrity. This situation is highly relevant to open data because effective data governance and utilization depend heavily on a workforce that understands digital tools and data ethics. Without comprehensive, accessible training, civil servants cannot fully leverage open data initiatives to improve public services or foster transparency. The current trend of underinvesting in digital skills creates a barrier to adopting open data practices, potentially leading to misuse of information or missed opportunities for innovation. Ultimately, the lack of strategic investment in human capital threatens the successful implementation of any open data framework.
