Las pernoctaciones extrahoteleras en Castilla-La Mancha superan las 78.000 en febrero, un 3,7% menos

The article presents a detailed analysis of February tourism data in Castilla-La Mancha, highlighting a distinct preference for apartments over other accommodation types. The main conclusion is that while the region participates in a national upward trend for extra-hotel lodging, its performance is significantly outpaced by autonomous communities with higher international appeal, such as the Balearic Islands, which dominate the market in occupancy and volume. This disparity underscores the importance of accessible, high-quality regional statistics for benchmarking local performance against national leaders and identifying specific growth or stagnation areas. The data reveals divergent trends across different lodging categories, with apartments and campings showing growth while rural tourism suffers a decline. For open data initiatives, this breakdown is crucial as it demonstrates the necessity of granular, disaggregated datasets that separate domestic from international travelers and track occupancy rates across various sector segments. Such detailed data allows stakeholders to understand not just overall volume, but the shifting behaviors of different traveler demographics and their impact on specific local economies. Relevance to open data lies in the transparency required to drive informed decision-making in tourism policy and investment. By making these metrics publicly available, governments and businesses can accurately assess the health of the sector, allocate resources effectively, and promote sustainable tourism strategies. The availability of comparable, structured data across regions fosters a competitive yet cooperative environment, enabling the identification of best practices and facilitating evidence-based planning for future tourism development.
