The article highlights the political pressure and institutional challenges facing transparency authorities, specifically noting the INAI’s current vacancy crisis. This underscores the fragility of democratic oversight mechanisms when institutions are deprived of key leadership positions. Despite these adversities, the newly appointed president of the Integrity Network outlines a strategic plan focused on global cooperation, external alliances, and sharing best practices. The collective goal is to combat corruption and impunity by strengthening ethical standards in public administration across member nations. This content is relevant to open data because transparency and integrity are foundational to data governance. Without robust, independent institutions guaranteeing access to information, open data initiatives cannot ensure accountability or foster public trust. The article emphasizes that protecting these rights is essential for maintaining functional, data-driven democratic systems.
