JNE sobre voto digital para 2026: "No resulta viable su aplicación inmediata", tras auditoría técnica

JNE sobre voto digital para 2026: "No resulta viable su aplicación inmediata", tras auditoría técnica

The National Electoral Jury (JNE) has officially rejected the use of digital voting in the 2026 General Elections, concluding that the proposed technology lacks the necessary security, transparency, and data protection standards required for a free and authentic electoral process. This decision follows a rigorous technical audit that identified insufficient mechanisms to guarantee the integrity of votes and the accuracy of the final tally within the current electoral timeline, effectively halting immediate implementation despite prior interest from the National Office of Electoral Processes (ONPE). This outcome underscores a critical intersection between civic rights and technological governance, which is central to open data discussions. It highlights the essential role of independent technical verification in validating digital systems that handle sensitive personal information and public trust. By prioritizing rigorous security audits over rapid modernization, the ruling emphasizes that any digital electoral framework must demonstrate robust cybersecurity and transparency before deployment, ensuring that data integrity is never compromised for convenience. The relevance to open data lies in the imperative for accountability and public confidence in digital infrastructure. The rejection serves as a reminder that open, verifiable, and secure data systems are prerequisites for democratic innovation, not optional features. It reinforces the principle that transparency in technology design and operation is vital for protecting citizen rights, suggesting that future electoral reforms must adhere to strict open standards and independent oversight to ensure they are legally sound and publicly trustworthy.
