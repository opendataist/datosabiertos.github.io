'We all have duty to protect children from abuse': NSPCC urges public to use helpline

This article highlights the alarming prevalence of child sexual abuse, noting that recorded cases represent only a fraction of actual incidents due to significant under-reporting. The data suggests a vast gap between reported offenses and real-world victimization, emphasizing that police statistics fail to capture the full scale of this societal crisis. Relevance to open data lies in the necessity of transparent, accessible, and comprehensive data collection to drive effective policy and public awareness. Without clear, disaggregated data, stakeholders cannot accurately assess risk factors or allocate resources to vulnerable demographics, such as pre-teens and teenagers. Open, standardized reporting mechanisms are essential to bridge the visibility gap and hold institutions accountable. The NSPCC’s campaign underscores the critical role of public engagement and professional support systems. By leveraging funded helplines and educational resources, the initiative aims to encourage community vigilance and provide safe channels for reporting concerns. This approach demonstrates how combining empirical evidence with accessible support services can foster a more protective environment for children, reinforcing the value of data-informed public health strategies.
