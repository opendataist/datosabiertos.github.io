CEOE CEPYME Cuenca señala que el padrón sitúa a la provincia cerca de los habitantes que había en 2018

The recent demographic data reveals a promising reversal in Cuenca’s long-standing depopulation trend, with the 2023 population rising to levels nearing the peak recorded in 2018. This marks the first annual growth in over a decade, ending a prolonged period of decline and signaling a potential stabilization for the region. Crucially, this slight recovery improves population density metrics across Castilla-La Mancha, suggesting that recent development efforts in industrial zones and specific municipalities are successfully attracting residents. Although the province remains below the threshold typically used to classify areas as severely depopulated, the consistent upward trajectory indicates that localized economic growth is effectively counteracting rural flight. This article is relevant to open_data as it demonstrates how freely accessible government statistics can drive regional policy and business strategy. By analyzing these transparent datasets, organizations can identify emerging demographic shifts, validate the effectiveness of anti-depopulation measures, and make informed decisions about resource allocation and infrastructure investment in rural Spain.
