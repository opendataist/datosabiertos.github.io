Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical character recognition transforms static image data into machine-readable text, addressing critical challenges in managing unstructured digital information. By automating the extraction of text from scans, businesses eliminate manual intervention, enabling seamless integration with analytics and workflow automation tools. This shift is vital for converting traditional print media into actionable digital assets. The technology’s most significant implication lies in identity verification and digital services. When combined with biometric data from photos on documents like passports, OCR facilitates robust identity binding and verification processes. This capability streamlines operations for companies handling sensitive personal data, enhancing security and operational efficiency through automated form completion and data analysis. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the necessity of converting legacy physical records into standardized, accessible digital formats. Effective OCR practices ensure that previously inaccessible paper-based information becomes searchable and interoperable, thereby expanding the pool of usable public and organizational data for transparency and innovation.
