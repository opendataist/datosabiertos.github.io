Un piso en Baleares cuesta lo mismo que cuatro en Extremadura o tres en Murcia

Un piso en Baleares cuesta lo mismo que cuatro en Extremadura o tres en Murcia

The housing crisis in the Balearic Islands underscores a severe affordability emergency, in which high costs and the dominance of foreign buyers exclude local residents from the property market. This exodus illustrates how unregulated real estate dynamics can adversely affect community stability and social equity. This situation is critical for open data, as transparent and accessible statistics are essential for exposing such inequalities. Reliable public data enables researchers and policymakers to analyze market distortions, understand the correlation between foreign investment and local displacement, and monitor the true cost of living for residents. Consequently, open data serves as a vital tool for advocacy and informed decision-making. By making these statistics publicly available, society can better understand the mechanisms driving the housing emergency and advocate for sustainable urban policies that protect vulnerable populations from being priced out of their homes.
