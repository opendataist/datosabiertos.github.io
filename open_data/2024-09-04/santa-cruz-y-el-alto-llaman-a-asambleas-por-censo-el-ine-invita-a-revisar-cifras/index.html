Santa Cruz y El Alto llaman a asambleas por Censo; el INE invita a revisar cifras

The recent release of census data by Bolivia’s National Institute of Statistics (INE) has sparked significant political and social unrest in major urban centers. Authorities and universities in Santa Cruz and El Alto are rejecting the initial findings, citing discrepancies between the reported population figures and local realities. This controversy stems from concerns about methodological transparency, specifically the lack of error margins and coverage surveys, which have prompted calls for technical reviews and potential mobilizations by regional assemblies. In response to these criticisms, the INE has promised to provide technical explanations and host debate forums across all departments. While the institute acknowledges the need to address doubts through international and national oversight, the tension highlights a broader crisis of trust in public statistical processes. The disagreement underscores the critical importance of verifying data quality and ensuring that demographic estimates accurately reflect the lived experiences and administrative records of municipal entities. This situation is highly relevant to open data principles, as it illustrates the friction that arises when published datasets are perceived as opaque or unverified. It emphasizes that open data alone is insufficient if the underlying methodology is not accessible or trusted by stakeholders. The episode serves as a vital case study on the necessity for proactive communication, methodological transparency, and robust engagement with local authorities to ensure that public data fosters confidence rather than conflict.
