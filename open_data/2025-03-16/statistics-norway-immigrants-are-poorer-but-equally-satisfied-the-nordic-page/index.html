Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway experience significantly poorer living conditions, including lower incomes, worse housing, and higher rates of mental health issues compared to the general population. Despite these tangible disadvantages, they report satisfaction levels with life in Norway that are equal to those of native residents. This paradox highlights a complex social reality where objective hardship does not necessarily translate into subjective dissatisfaction, suggesting that other factors may buffer the impact of socioeconomic struggles on well-being. The data reveals that while trust in interpersonal relationships is lower among immigrants, their confidence in major public institutions such as the political system, judiciary, and police is notably higher. This strong institutional trust and affiliation with Norway indicate a successful integration into the civic fabric, even when economic and social disparities persist. Understanding this dynamic is crucial for policymakers aiming to maintain social cohesion and leverage institutional credibility to support marginalized communities. This report is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the power of transparent, large-scale statistical surveys to uncover nuanced social trends. By making detailed metrics on living conditions and attitudes publicly available, such data enables researchers and civil society to critically assess integration outcomes. It underscores the importance of accessible, high-quality datasets in fostering informed debates about social equity and the effectiveness of immigration policies, ensuring that systemic issues are addressed through evidence-based solutions.
