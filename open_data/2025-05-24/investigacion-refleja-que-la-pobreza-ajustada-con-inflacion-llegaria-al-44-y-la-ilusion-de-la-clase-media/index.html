Investigación refleja que la pobreza ajustada con inflación llegaría al 44% y la ‘ilusión’ de la clase media

Investigación refleja que la pobreza ajustada con inflación llegaría al 44% y la ‘ilusión’ de la clase media

Research by the Jubileo Foundation reveals that official poverty figures significantly underestimate reality, creating an illusion of social stability. By adjusting for inflation, the true poverty rate is substantially higher than reported, indicating that current measurement lines are outdated and fail to reflect actual living conditions. This discrepancy highlights a systemic invisibility of deprivation, particularly affecting rural populations who face extreme hardship. Furthermore, the study demonstrates that the middle class is overestimated due to overly broad income thresholds. When corrected for inflation, the percentage of people identified as middle class drops, exposing the fragility of this status. Many individuals lack the financial resilience to maintain their standing, risking a rapid slide back into poverty due to minor economic shocks or health issues. This analysis is vital for open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accurate, and frequently updated datasets. Reliable public statistics are essential for designing effective social policies and holding institutions accountable. Without correcting for inflation and refining metrics, decision-makers risk implementing strategies based on flawed premises, ultimately hindering efforts to reduce inequality and improve welfare for vulnerable communities.
