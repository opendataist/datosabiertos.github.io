Constituida en Toledo la Conferencia de Órganos Garantes de Transparencia y el Derecho de Acceso a la Información

Constituida en Toledo la Conferencia de Órganos Garantes de Transparencia y el Derecho de Acceso a la Información

Spain’s regional transparency councils and the national body have established a collaborative framework to strengthen the right of access to public information. This agreement represents a significant step toward unified cooperation, aiming to foster reciprocity among the autonomous communities and reinforce transparency standards. By creating a shared platform, these institutions seek to systematically improve governance and accountability mechanisms across the country, ensuring that access to information remains a guaranteed right for citizens rather than an administrative burden. A key outcome of this consensus is the approval of a presidency rotation schedule extending until 2028, involving major regions such as Catalonia, the Canary Islands, and Murcia. This structure facilitates ongoing dialogue and the exchange of best practices among the guarantor bodies. The initiative emphasizes building a culture of transparency that benefits local administrations and officials, promoting efficient organization over duplicated efforts. This cooperative model serves as a democratic safeguard against illiberal trends that seek to restrict access to information, thereby legitimizing liberal democratic systems through increased civic engagement and trust. This development is highly relevant to open data, as it institutionalizes the infrastructure necessary for effective information sharing and interoperability. By fostering collaboration among regional entities, the framework reduces fragmentation and standardizes practices, which are essential prerequisites for a cohesive open data ecosystem. It signals a political commitment to treating transparency not merely as a matter of compliance, but as a strategic tool for democratic resilience. Consequently, open data initiatives benefit from a more aligned regulatory environment, where improved access to information naturally supports the publication and use of structured, reusable data for the public good.
