Pollo, lo que pegó más duro a la inflación en la quincena

Pollo, lo que pegó más duro a la inflación en la quincena

The suspension of Brazilian poultry imports amid concerns about avian influenza directly impacted May’s inflation, driving chicken prices up significantly. This surge, along with rising costs for tomatoes and housing, contributed to a 4.22% annual inflation rate for the first half of the month. Conversely, substantial price drops in electricity due to seasonal tariffs, along with decreases in tequila and lemon prices, helped mitigate overall inflationary pressure. These negative contributors partially offset the upward trends in essential food and housing sectors, stabilizing the general price index. This data is relevant to open data because it highlights the importance of transparent, high-frequency statistical tracking. Access to granular, real-time indicators allows researchers to analyze immediate supply chain shocks and policy impacts, fostering evidence-based economic decision-making and public accountability.
