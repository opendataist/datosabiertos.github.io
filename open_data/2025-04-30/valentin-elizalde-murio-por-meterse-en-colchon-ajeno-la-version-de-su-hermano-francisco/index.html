The article suggests a paradigm shift in the understanding of Valentín Elizalde’s 2006 assassination, challenging the long-held belief that he was killed for singing a specific narcocorrido. Instead, new testimonies from his brother propose that personal motives, likely involving jealousy over a relationship with a woman connected to organized crime, may have been the true catalyst. This recontextualization implies that the violence was rooted in private conflicts rather than professional artistic choices or public defiance. Furthermore, the narrative introduces significant skepticism regarding the official account provided by Fausto Elizalde, the sole survivor of the attack. By casting doubt on the reliability of the primary witness’s version of events, the text highlights the fragmented and contested nature of historical truth in high-profile criminal cases. This uncertainty underscores how survivor testimony, often accepted as definitive, can be subject to manipulation or confusion. This case is relevant to open data as it illustrates the critical importance of data integrity and source verification in digital journalism. It demonstrates how unverified narratives can solidify into public "facts," and how releasing new, conflicting information disrupts established datasets. For open data advocates, this serves as a reminder that data surrounding historical events is often fluid, requiring rigorous auditing and transparency to prevent misinformation from becoming entrenched in public memory.
