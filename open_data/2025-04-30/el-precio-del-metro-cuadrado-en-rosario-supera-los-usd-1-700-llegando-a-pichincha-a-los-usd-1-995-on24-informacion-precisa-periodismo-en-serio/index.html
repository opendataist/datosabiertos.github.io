The Rosario housing market shows a pronounced imbalance: sales listings are abundant while rental options are scarce, reflecting structural challenges in housing accessibility. Although transaction volumes are showing signs of moderate recovery, the limited availability of rentals creates intense competition among seekers, highlighting a critical supply-demand disconnect that undermines local residents’ ability to secure stable housing. Prices have risen sharply in prime areas, yet rental increases lag behind broader inflation indices, suggesting a complex interplay between market forces and economic pressures. This disparity indicates that while property values are appreciating, the immediate cost of living for renters remains under strain, revealing gaps in how housing costs correlate with general economic indicators and consumer purchasing power. This data is crucial for open-data initiatives, as it underscores the need for transparent, granular real estate statistics to inform public policy. Access to such detailed metrics enables researchers and citizens to monitor affordability trends, identify drivers of inequality, and advocate for effective urban planning solutions that address the housing crisis through evidence-based decision-making.
