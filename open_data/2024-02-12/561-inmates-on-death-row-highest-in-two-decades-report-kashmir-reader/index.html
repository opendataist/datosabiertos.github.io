561 inmates on death row, highest in two decades: Report - Kashmir Reader

561 inmates on death row, highest in two decades: Report - Kashmir Reader

The article highlights a critical divergence in India’s capital punishment system, where trial courts increasingly impose death sentences while appellate courts rigorously scrutinize and reject them. This widening gap suggests systemic flaws in lower court proceedings, including poor investigative quality and procedural lapses, which result in a rising death row population despite fewer confirmed executions. Furthermore, the report points to troubling judicial practices at the trial level, such as the failure to consider crucial mitigating factors like probation reports or psychiatric evaluations. With a significant majority of new sentences issued for sexual offenses and often delivered immediately after conviction, the data indicates a rush toward capital punishment that contradicts established legal safeguards and the Supreme Court’s mandate for careful, individualized sentencing. This trend is particularly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible statistical records to hold the justice system accountable. By making such detailed annual statistics publicly available, civil society and legal researchers can analyze patterns of judicial error, advocate for policy reforms like those proposed in the new penal code, and ensure that the administration of the death penalty is subject to rigorous public scrutiny rather than remaining opaque.
