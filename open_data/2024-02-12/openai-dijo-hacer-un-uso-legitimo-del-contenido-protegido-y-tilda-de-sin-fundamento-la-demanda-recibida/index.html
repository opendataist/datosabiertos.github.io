OpenAI dijo hacer un uso legítimo del contenido protegido y tilda de “sin fundamento” la demanda recibida.

OpenAI dijo hacer un uso legítimo del contenido protegido y tilda de “sin fundamento” la demanda recibida.

The New York Times’ lawsuit against OpenAI, alleging the use of copyrighted content in the training of AI models, has been dismissed by the artificial intelligence company as baseless. OpenAI argues that access to such materials is essential for developing useful systems, contending that restricting training data to public domain works would hinder the creation of advanced and competitive technologies. This stance reflects a central conflict in data governance: the tension between the legal protection of creative content and the technical need for large volumes of diverse information to train algorithms. From an open data perspective, this case is significant because it challenges the definition of what constitutes accessible and usable data for the common good or technological innovation. OpenAI defends its use of publicly available and copyrighted data as “fair use,” a practice that many countries and jurisdictions are beginning to accept or regulate to foster AI development. The dispute underscores the urgency of clarifying intellectual property laws in the digital age, where data are not merely static resources but dynamic inputs for generating new knowledge. Beyond its legal defense, OpenAI highlights voluntary collaboration initiatives, such as opt-out options for publishers and strategic partnerships with media outlets. These efforts aim to balance access to information with respect for creators, promoting an ecosystem in which AI tools support journalism rather than engaging in destructive competition. The case illustrates how transparency and cooperation in data management can serve as viable alternatives to legal confrontation, setting precedents for how protected resources can be integrated into open data systems in an ethical and sustainable manner.
