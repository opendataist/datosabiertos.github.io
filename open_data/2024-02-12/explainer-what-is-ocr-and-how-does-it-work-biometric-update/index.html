Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical character recognition transforms static image files into editable, machine-readable text, bridging the gap between physical documents and digital analysis. This capability is essential for modern data workflows, as it allows organizations to efficiently process information from print media, including sensitive identity documents that often contain biometric elements. By automating the extraction of textual data from these images, businesses eliminate manual intervention and enable seamless integration with broader analytical software systems. The technology operates through hardware scanning and software analysis, converting images into distinct foreground and background layers to isolate characters. It employs algorithms like pattern matching or feature extraction to identify glyphs, ultimately generating digital text files. These outputs support various applications, such as form automation and operational streamlining, ensuring that visual data becomes actionable intelligence for enterprises seeking to enhance productivity and accuracy. This article is highly relevant to open data as it highlights a critical infrastructure component for converting unstructured visual information into structured, accessible datasets. Effective OCR facilitates the digitization of public records and identity documents, making them searchable and analyzable. This process supports transparency and interoperability, enabling researchers and developers to utilize standardized text data for public benefit, policy analysis, and innovative service development.
