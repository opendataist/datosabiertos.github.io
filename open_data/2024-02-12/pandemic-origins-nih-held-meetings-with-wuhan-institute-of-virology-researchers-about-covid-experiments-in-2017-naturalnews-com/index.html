Pandemic origins: NIH held meetings with Wuhan Institute of Virology researchers about COVID experiments in 2017 – NaturalNews.com

FOIA documents reveal that US-funded research at the Wuhan Institute of Virology involved deliberate misrepresentation of risks associated with gain-of-function studies. This highlights a critical transparency gap in international scientific collaboration, where potential biosecurity threats were obscured to secure funding. The revelation underscores the urgent need for rigorous ethical oversight and open disclosure in virology research to prevent future biological hazards and maintain public trust in scientific institutions. The integration of civilian and military objectives at the Wuhan lab complicates origin investigations, suggesting dual-use research programs that blur institutional boundaries. This ambiguity challenges the current frameworks for monitoring high-risk biological activities, indicating that existing international norms are insufficient for addressing the complexities of modern virological research. Clearer distinction and accountability between civilian and military research entities are essential for effective global health security. Open data initiatives are crucial for resolving the fragmented intelligence assessments regarding the virus's origins. Access to unredacted records and comprehensive datasets would allow for independent verification of claims, moving beyond speculative narratives to evidence-based conclusions. Enhancing data accessibility ensures that public health decisions are grounded in transparent, verifiable information rather than political or institutional biases, fostering a more robust global response to emerging pandemic threats.
