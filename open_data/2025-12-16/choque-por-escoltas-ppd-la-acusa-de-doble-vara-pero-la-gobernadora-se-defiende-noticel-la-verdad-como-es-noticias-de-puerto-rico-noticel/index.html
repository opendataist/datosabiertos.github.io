Choque por escoltas: PPD la acusa de doble vara, pero la gobernadora se defiende - NotiCel - La verdad como es - Noticias de Puerto Rico - NOTICEL

Choque por escoltas: PPD la acusa de doble vara, pero la gobernadora se defiende - NotiCel - La verdad como es - Noticias de Puerto Rico - NOTICEL

The Governor of Puerto Rico vetoed legislation that sought to eliminate public bodyguards for former officials, including those with criminal records, arguing that altering an already-acquired right violated prior court rulings. Instead, she introduced a measure that restricts this benefit specifically to individuals convicted of serious crimes, aiming to balance fiscal savings with compliance with the existing legal framework. The Popular Democratic Party denounced a "double standard" in the handling of public affairs, pointing out the contradiction of rejecting cost-saving measures while extending deadlines for access to information. This critical stance highlights the perception of governmental opacity, as the opposition claims that the administration limits transparency and protects undue privileges for political figures questioned for corruption. This case is relevant to open data because it directly links fiscal accountability with access to public information. The controversy underscores how the management of government data and public spending must be aligned with principles of transparency, enabling citizens to verify the use of state resources and assess the integrity of officials—essential elements for building trust in democratic institutions.
