Análisis de las Enmiendas a la Ley de Transparencia Gubernamental de Puerto Rico (Ley 141-2019) - El Sol de la Florida

Análisis de las Enmiendas a la Ley de Transparencia Gubernamental de Puerto Rico (Ley 141-2019) - El Sol de la Florida

Recent amendments to Puerto Rico’s Transparency Law have sparked deep concern by undermining the fundamental right to access public information. By imposing additional bureaucratic hurdles, significantly extending response deadlines, and eliminating key protections for the identity of requesters, these modifications render transparency a slow and discouraging process. This legal regression threatens democratic accountability, favoring governmental opacity over the openness envisioned by the original legislation. Freedom of the press is directly affected by these restrictions, as investigative journalism critically depends on obtaining public data in a timely manner and in analyzable formats. By allowing agencies to deliver information in non-digital or limited formats, it becomes difficult to analyze large datasets necessary for detecting patterns of corruption. This technical limitation acts as a form of censorship by delay, where vital information arrives too late or in an unmanageable form, losing its journalistic relevance and oversight capacity. This issue is relevant to the field of open data because it illustrates how legal infrastructure determines the practical utility of public information. Real transparency requires not only the publication of data but also its accessible, processable, and timely availability. When regulations prevent swift access to data structures, they degrade the ability of civil society and the media to transform government information into actionable knowledge, weakening the democratic ecosystem that depends on verifiable and public data.
