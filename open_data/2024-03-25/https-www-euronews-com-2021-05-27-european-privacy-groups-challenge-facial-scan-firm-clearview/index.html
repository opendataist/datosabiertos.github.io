Privacy advocates have launched legal challenges in multiple European jurisdictions against Clearview AI, alleging the unauthorized collection of facial images violates stringent data protection regulations. The companies argue that scraping billions of images without consent exceeds legal boundaries for biometric data processing, raising fears of excessive surveillance capabilities in Western democracies similar to those observed in China. Although the firm claims to have no current EU customers, the complaints highlight the global reach of its data stockpile. Activists emphasize that extracting unique facial features from public websites without user awareness fundamentally breaches privacy expectations, urging regulators to enforce strict compliance with European laws regarding the lawful basis for data handling. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical tension between the availability of publicly accessible information and individual privacy rights. It illustrates that just because data is open does not mean its use, particularly for sensitive biometric identification, is unrestricted, necessitating careful legal frameworks to balance transparency with fundamental human rights.
