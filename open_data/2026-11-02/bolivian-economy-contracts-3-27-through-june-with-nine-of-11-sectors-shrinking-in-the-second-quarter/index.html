Bolivian economy contracts 3.27% through June, with nine of 11 sectors shrinking in the second quarter

Bolivia’s economy experienced a significant contraction in the first half of 2026, driven by a multifaceted crisis that combined pre-existing structural difficulties with the direct impact of social unrest and road blockades. This widespread deterioration affected most productive sectors, revealing a loss of economic dynamism that notably accelerated during the second quarter. The interplay between disruptions in the flow of goods and political instability has exacerbated the inherent vulnerabilities of the country’s productive model. Four key sectors—construction, transportation, commerce, and manufacturing—were primarily responsible for the decline in Gross Domestic Product (GDP). Construction suffered the sharpest drop due to the suspension of construction projects and shortages of materials, while transportation and commerce were severely constrained by fuel shortages and logistical disruptions. This concentration of negative impacts highlights that activities linked to the production and distribution of goods are the most sensitive to infrastructure and supply crises. This report is relevant to the open data community because it illustrates how the quality and context of statistical data are crucial for public policy analysis. Providing sector-disaggregated metrics enables researchers and citizens to unravel the root causes of economic fluctuations, distinguishing between temporary external shocks and deep-seated structural problems. Transparency in these data facilitates accountability and helps clarify the complexity of economic challenges beyond national aggregates.
