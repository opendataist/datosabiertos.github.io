Periodista de Acento obtiene segundo lugar en concurso de Datos Abiertos

The recognition of Dominican journalists at the National Data Journalism Award highlights the transformative power of investigative journalism grounded in quantitative evidence. By leveraging verified information from transparency portals, media outlets not only fulfill their informational role but also act as critical filters that reveal the efficiency and shortcomings of public management. This process transforms raw data into accessible narratives, enabling citizens to accurately understand how state resources are used and which areas require immediate intervention. The relevance of this approach to the open data movement lies in its ability to operationalize the right to access information. Beyond mere technical availability, the award validates the full transparency cycle: the release of data by the state, followed by its rigorous analysis by civil society and the press. This demonstrates that open data are not an end in themselves, but essential tools for accountability, corruption prevention, and strengthening governmental integrity through external oversight. Furthermore, the launch of the National Open Data Plan marks an institutional step toward standardizing and continuously improving the quality of public information. By engaging diverse social sectors in identifying priority data, the government seeks to ensure that available information addresses the real needs of citizens. This collaborative participation reinforces the idea that transparency is a two-way dialogue, where technology serves to enhance governance and public trust, ensuring that data are useful, relevant, and easily reusable by any social actor.
