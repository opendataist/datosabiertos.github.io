This article highlights how gender-disaggregated data is essential for addressing persistent global inequalities that hinder women’s full economic participation. By examining the World Bank’s Gender Data Portal, it underscores that violence, labor disparities, and child marriage remain widespread obstacles. These issues not only violate human rights but also impose significant social and economic costs, effectively limiting women’s potential and contributing to broader development challenges. The narrative emphasizes that without robust, sex-disaggregated data, policymakers cannot accurately understand the prevalence or root causes of these problems. Consequently, evidence-based interventions cannot be designed or adapted to support those most affected. The lack of progress in closing the labor force participation gap over decades illustrates the urgency of generating high-quality statistics to drive effective, gender-sensitive policy changes and improve economic opportunities for women. This content is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the transformative power of accessible, reliable statistics in advancing social justice. It argues that investing in national statistical systems and making gender data openly available empowers advocacy campaigns and informs development policies. Ultimately, transparent data serves as a critical tool for tracking progress, holding institutions accountable, and accelerating the achievement of gender equality in economic and social spheres.
