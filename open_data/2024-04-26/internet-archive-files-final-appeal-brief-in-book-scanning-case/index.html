Internet Archive Files Final Appeal Brief in Book Scanning Case

The Internet Archive is appealing a lower court ruling that declared its Controlled Digital Lending (CDL) program a copyright infringement, arguing that the practice is a transformative fair use that simply modernizes traditional library lending. The core contention is that allowing patrons to access the same physical book they own in a digital format expands utility and efficiency without harming the market, as it preserves the one-to-one lending model essential to library services. This case is critically relevant to open data and digital preservation because it determines whether nonprofit institutions can maintain permanent, accessible archives of cultural heritage independent of proprietary licensing restrictions. The outcome will establish whether libraries can legally digitize and lend materials to protect patron privacy and ensure long-term access, or if they are forced to rely on corporate-controlled e-book platforms that may withdraw content or compromise data ownership. A ruling against the Internet Archive would set a precedent limiting digital access for nonprofits, potentially forcing libraries into restrictive commercial agreements. Conversely, a victory would affirm the right of public institutions to digitize owned physical collections, ensuring equitable access to knowledge and protecting the integrity of open archival practices against claims that such activities are inherently commercial or harmful to creative industries.
