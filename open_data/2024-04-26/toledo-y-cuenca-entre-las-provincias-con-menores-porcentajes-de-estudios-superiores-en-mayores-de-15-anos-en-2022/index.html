Toledo y Cuenca, entre las provincias con menores porcentajes de estudios superiores en mayores de 15 años en 2022

The article highlights significant regional disparities in educational attainment within Spain, contrasting areas with high university graduation rates against those with lower percentages. This data underscores a broader national trend where a substantial portion of the population holds advanced degrees, yet access remains unevenly distributed across different municipalities and provinces. A key finding reveals a gender shift, with women now surpassing men in higher education levels, particularly among younger demographics. Furthermore, nationality significantly influences educational outcomes, as immigrants from certain countries exhibit much higher rates of university degrees compared to those from others. These distinctions highlight the complex intersection of migration and human capital development in contemporary Spain. This information is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates how granular, annually updated administrative records can replace traditional decennial censuses. By providing detailed sociodemographic insights down to the census section level, this approach enhances transparency and allows for more precise, real-time analysis of social inequalities and workforce potential.
