Aug 2022: High processing fees for freedom of information requests slammed

Aug 2022: High processing fees for freedom of information requests slammed

Manitoba Justice’s request for nearly $32,000 to process freedom of information requests regarding a secret RCMP investigation into former Winnipeg Mayor Sam Katz has sparked significant controversy. Experts and politicians argue that these exorbitant fees act as a deliberate barrier to public access, effectively using financial hurdles to suppress transparency rather than facilitating legitimate information sharing. This approach is viewed as an abuse of legislative intent, prioritizing government opacity over democratic accountability. The core issue extends beyond immediate costs to a systemic failure in managing public records. Critics emphasize that citizens already fund the creation and storage of government data; therefore, charging excessive fees for retrieval represents a double taxation of public resources. When institutions cannot efficiently produce requested documents, the burden should not fall on the requester. This practice suggests a broader pattern where governments treat access laws as inconveniences to be minimized rather than rights to be upheld. This situation is critical to the open data movement because it highlights how bureaucratic friction can undermine the principles of transparency and accountability. If governments can legally charge prohibitive fees to hide information, the promise of open access becomes illusory. True open data requires not just the availability of information, but the removal of structural barriers that prevent citizens from understanding how their institutions operate, ensuring that transparency is a practical reality rather than a theoretical concept.
