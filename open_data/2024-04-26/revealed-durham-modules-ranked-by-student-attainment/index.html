Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

This article demonstrates the transformative power of data transparency in higher education by leveraging Freedom of Information requests to expose institutional performance metrics. The main conclusion is that releasing granular attainment data allows for significant public scrutiny of academic standards, revealing stark disparities in grading practices across different departments and course levels. By making this information accessible, universities are encouraged to uphold accountability and consistency in their assessment processes, fostering a culture of openness that benefits students and stakeholders alike. The analysis highlights that attainment varies dramatically by discipline, with Physics and English showing the highest average marks, while foundation year and certain modern language modules lag significantly behind. This variation underscores the importance of contextualizing data; without understanding factors like class size or exam formats, raw grades can be misleading. However, publishing these details invites a deeper dialogue about equity in grading and ensures that students can make informed decisions about their academic paths based on transparent, evidence-based outcomes rather than opaque institutional reputations. This resource is highly relevant to open data advocates as it exemplifies how government-mandated transparency can be repurposed for public benefit beyond simple compliance. It illustrates that when raw educational data is shared openly, it empowers the community to identify trends, question outliers, and demand higher standards. Such initiatives promote the principle that public institutions should operate with clear visibility, ensuring that data serves not just internal administration but also the broader societal goal of educational improvement and democratic oversight.
