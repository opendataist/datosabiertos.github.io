Tras resolución de SCJN, titular de OGAIPO afirma que mantendrá cargo

The President of Oaxaca’s transparency authority asserts that its commissioners will remain in office despite a Supreme Court ruling declaring unconstitutional the legislative article used to appoint them. This legal challenge, initiated by federal oversight bodies, argued that the state legislature overstepped its authority by dictating the selection process for the new autonomous institution. Although the Court invalidated the specific procedural mechanism, it did not nullify the legitimacy of the current commissioners themselves, clarifying that the dismissal was not due to misconduct but because the legal basis for their appointment was superseded by a new organic law. The core implication is that the current administration maintains legal continuity based on the interpretation that their mandates were tied to the previous legislation, which has since been repealed. By distinguishing between a procedural flaw in appointment and a substantive loss of eligibility, the dissenting vote within the Court supports the view that the commissioners’ tenure is protected under general transparency laws. This nuance prevents an immediate vacuum of authority, allowing the institution to continue functioning without the disruptive effect of a full leadership turnover, preserving operational stability during the transition. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates as it underscores the fragility of institutional independence when local legislatures attempt to redefine the selection criteria for oversight bodies. It highlights the critical role of federal constitutional safeguards in protecting autonomous transparency agencies from political interference at the state level. For open data ecosystems, this confirms that while local laws may change, the fundamental right to access information and the structural integrity of guarantor institutions are resilient, provided they are shielded by robust constitutional jurisprudence.
