RTI Law: Minister wants sanctions against non-complying institutions - The Ghanaian Chronicle

RTI Law: Minister wants sanctions against non-complying institutions - The Ghanaian Chronicle

The article highlights the critical intersection of government transparency and public accountability through the enforcement of the Right to Information Act. A primary concern is the significant non-compliance among public institutions, with a large portion failing to submit mandatory annual reports. This widespread neglect underscores the urgent need for administrative penalties to ensure that transparency mechanisms are not merely symbolic but are actively enforced to maintain public trust. Despite these compliance gaps, the data reveals a positive trend in how information requests are handled, with a majority of applications being approved. However, the article emphasizes that systemic barriers, such as bureaucratic inertia and limited resources, continue to impede the full realization of information access rights. Addressing these structural challenges is essential for moving beyond initial compliance toward a culture of genuine openness and efficiency within public service delivery. The initiative is highly relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates the power of legislative frameworks in driving institutional change. By proposing an online records management system and hosting global conferences on universal access to information, the government signals a commitment to modernizing data infrastructure. This shift supports the broader open data movement by promoting digital accessibility and standardizing how public information is stored and shared, thereby enhancing citizen engagement and oversight.
