Crece percepción de inseguridad - Plaza de Armas | Querétaro

The INEGI report reveals a significant disparity between national public security statistics and local realities in Querétaro. While most of Mexico perceives high levels of insecurity, the state exhibits a divergent trend: perceptions of safety are relatively low compared to the national average, despite a slight recent increase. This data highlights how localized governance affects citizen sentiment and underscores the need to rely on granular, state-level statistics rather than solely on aggregate national figures for effective policy-making. Public trust in security forces varies drastically by institution, with military branches receiving significantly higher approval ratings than municipal police. Moreover, gender plays a critical role in perceptions of safety, as women report feeling markedly more vulnerable in public spaces and transportation than men. These findings illustrate that security data must be disaggregated by gender and institution to accurately reflect the diverse experiences of the population and to design inclusive public safety strategies. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the value of high-resolution, accessible statistical data in challenging official narratives and informing local political discourse. By making specific metrics on security perceptions and institutional trust publicly available, INEGI enables citizens and policymakers to scrutinize government performance beyond general statements. This transparency fosters evidence-based decision-making and holds authorities accountable, proving that open data is essential for building trust and driving targeted improvements in public services.
