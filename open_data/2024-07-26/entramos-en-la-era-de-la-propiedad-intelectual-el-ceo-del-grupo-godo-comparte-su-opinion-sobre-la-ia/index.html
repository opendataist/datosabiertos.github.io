Entramos en la era de la propiedad intelectual: El CEO del Grupo Godó comparte su opinión sobre la IA

Entramos en la era de la propiedad intelectual: El CEO del Grupo Godó comparte su opinión sobre la IA

The article outlines Grupo Godó’s strategic evolution from a traditional print conglomerate to a diversified media and marketing enterprise, emphasizing sustainability through diversified revenue streams. By integrating specialized marketing agencies and focusing on vertical content niches, the group reduces reliance on volatile digital advertising, aiming for a balanced structure where media and marketing divisions are independently profitable. Central to this strategy is the prioritization of paid digital subscriptions over free traffic metrics, viewing them as the truest indicator of reader trust and brand value. The group employs a "fair paywall" model and leverages a subscriber benefits club to enhance retention. This approach not only secures financial stability but also reinforces the publication’s commitment to pluralistic, high-quality journalism that respects its audience’s critical thinking. Crucially, the piece highlights the urgent need for regulatory frameworks regarding artificial intelligence and intellectual property. As tech companies utilize journalistic content to train language models without compensation, the industry faces a threat to its economic viability. The article argues for a global consensus, similar to historical copyright levies, ensuring that tech platforms fairly compensate media creators, thereby safeguarding the existence of reliable democratic discourse. This is relevant to open data because it underscores the conflict between proprietary journalistic content and open data practices in AI. It illustrates how the lack of clear data governance and compensation models for original information creators threatens the sustainability of high-quality media, a key provider of structured, reliable data, while benefiting open-access AI training methods.
