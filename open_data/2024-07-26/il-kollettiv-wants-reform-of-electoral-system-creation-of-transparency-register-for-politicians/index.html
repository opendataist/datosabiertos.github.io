Il-Kollettiv wants reform of electoral system, creation of transparency register for politicians

The article highlights a critical call by Il-Kollettiv for constitutional reforms in Malta, triggered by the European Commission’s Rule of Law report. The organization argues that current governance lacks the transparency and representativeness required for a modern democracy. By reactivating stalled reform processes, Malta can align its legal framework with democratic standards, ensuring that state institutions truly reflect societal diversity and uphold the rule of law effectively. A central theme is the urgent need for increased information accessibility and institutional accountability. The group emphasizes that citizens and journalists are currently deprived of vital public documents, hindering open participation in the legislative process. Implementing a transparency register for politicians and taking Freedom of Information requests seriously are proposed as essential steps to combat opacity. These measures aim to foster a culture where government actions are visible and subject to public scrutiny, reducing opportunities for corruption. Relevance to open_data lies in the demand for systemic transparency and the protection of whistleblowers. The article advocates for robust legal frameworks, such as an updated Whistleblower Act, to safeguard those exposing wrongdoing. Furthermore, the push for immediate implementation of inquiry recommendations and the establishment of independent oversight bodies like a Human Rights Commission underscores the necessity of structured data availability. Without such open data mechanisms and legal protections, meaningful democratic engagement and anti-corruption efforts remain severely compromised.
