Mercado hipotecario: los préstamos a tipo variable ganan peso en 2023

The Spanish mortgage market experienced a significant contraction in 2023, characterized by a decline in the volume of loans granted and a widespread increase in monthly payments. This phenomenon, primarily driven by the rise in the Euribor and interest rates, has drastically reduced households' purchasing power, excluding many prospective homebuyers from the market and forcing others to take on debts with larger variable components and shorter terms to maintain financial viability. The shift toward variable-rate and hybrid mortgage products reflects the adaptation of banks and consumers to economic uncertainty, although competition among financial institutions has partially mitigated the negative impact on the approval of new loans. This situation has altered the market structure, with an increase in self-financed investments and a sharper decline in mortgages compared to property sales, highlighting how the cost of borrowing has become the main barrier to housing access in a high-interest-rate environment. The relevance of this article for open data lies in the critical importance of transparency and public access to official data, such as that provided by the National Statistics Institute (INE), to understand macroeconomic dynamics and their social impact. A detailed analysis of these time series enables citizens, researchers, and policymakers to assess the effectiveness of financial measures, identify gaps in housing affordability, and make informed, evidence-based decisions, thereby promoting a fairer and more comprehensible market.
