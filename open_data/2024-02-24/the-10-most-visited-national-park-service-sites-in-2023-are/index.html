The 10 most visited National Park Service sites in 2023 are …

The National Park Service reported a significant recovery in 2023, with visitation numbers approaching pre-pandemic peaks. This surge reflects a growing public demand for outdoor recreation and historical engagement, indicating that national parks remain a central pillar of American cultural identity. The data suggests that the sector is not only rebounding but stabilizing at higher participation levels, signaling strong ongoing interest in public lands. A shift in visitor patterns emerged, with increased attendance during off-peak seasons and record-breaking visits to lesser-known sites. This diversification highlights that interest extends beyond the traditional summer months and famous landmarks, reaching deeper into the system’s diverse offerings. Visitors are increasingly exploring "hidden gems," which broadens the distribution of tourism benefits and reduces pressure on iconic, overcrowded destinations. This trend is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the value of transparent, accessible statistics in tracking societal engagement with public resources. By making visitation data available, agencies like the NPS enable researchers, planners, and the public to understand usage trends, identify emerging sites, and inform policy decisions. Such openness supports better resource management and ensures that the narrative of shared history is accessible to a wider, more diverse audience.
