Indaga INAI al Presidente por dar datos de periodista

The Mexican Institute of Transparency has launched an investigation into President López Obrador for publicly disclosing a journalist’s phone number during a press conference. This act, presented as retaliation for a critical New York Times report, raises serious concerns regarding the violation of data protection laws and the safety of media professionals. The incident highlights the tension between government transparency initiatives and the fundamental right to privacy. The revelation of personal contact information has triggered condemnation from press freedom organizations and media alliances. They argue that such actions endanger journalists by exposing them to potential harassment and undermine democratic norms. The controversy emphasizes that protecting personal data is not merely a technical issue but a critical human rights safeguard for those reporting on sensitive matters like corruption. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the dual nature of transparency: while access to public information is vital, it must not compromise individual privacy rights. It serves as a cautionary example of how state actors can misuse open information mechanisms to harass critics, stressing the need for robust legal frameworks that balance accountability with the protection of personal data against abuse.
