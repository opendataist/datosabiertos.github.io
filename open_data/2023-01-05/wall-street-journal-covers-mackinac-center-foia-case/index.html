Wall Street Journal covers Mackinac Center FOIA case

The ruling challenges Michigan’s public transparency by excluding educational materials from FOIA requirements if not directly held by the district. This creates a dangerous precedent where public employees’ work products remain hidden from taxpayers, undermining the fundamental link between public funding and public accountability. Open data advocates argue that true transparency requires accessible information about how public resources are utilized in education. Excluding curriculum from open records obscures what is taught in schools, preventing meaningful oversight and hindering the democratic process of informed civic engagement. This case highlights the critical need to define “public records” comprehensively in digital and educational contexts. It serves as a warning that narrow interpretations of transparency laws can significantly weaken citizen oversight, making it essential to ensure all taxpayer-funded work remains visible and accessible for public scrutiny.
