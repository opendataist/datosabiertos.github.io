John Horgan's last text messages as premier deleted - BC News

The British Columbia government’s admission that all text messages from Premier John Horgan’s final three days in office were deleted raises significant concerns about transparency and accountability. Authorities justified the deletion by labeling the communications as casual and non-substantive transitory records. However, this decision highlights the inherent subjectivity in determining what constitutes "transitory" data, creating an opportunity for abuse where officials might arbitrarily discard records to obscure important political decisions or communications. This incident is particularly striking given the historical context and the improbability of a premier making no significant decisions via text during their final 72 hours. The situation echoes past criticisms by Horgan himself regarding previous administrations that engaged in similar data destruction, now contrasting with his government’s actions. Furthermore, the selective release of other correspondence and the substantial severance packages for senior staff suggest a pattern of managing information and resources in ways that may undermine public trust. This article is crucial to the open data movement as it illustrates the challenges of implementing true transparency in practice versus rhetoric. It underscores the need for robust, objective standards for record retention and the dangers of allowing political convenience to dictate access to public information. The case serves as a reminder that without strict enforcement of open data principles, governments can easily create narratives of secrecy, eroding the democratic value of accessible government records.
