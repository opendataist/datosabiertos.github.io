Argentina has officially activated the exchange of financial information with the United States under the FATCA agreement, marking a significant shift toward greater tax transparency. The process begins with selective requests for specific account data, while full automatic information exchange is scheduled to commence later in 2024. This move aims to ensure that assets held abroad by Argentine residents are properly reported to local authorities, enhancing the state's ability to monitor international wealth. To facilitate compliance, the tax authority has established a complementary international operations reporting regime. Financial institutions are now obligated to implement due diligence procedures to identify and report account details, including holder identities and balances, to the government. This regulatory framework imposes strict documentation requirements on banks and other financial entities, ensuring that both active and previously closed accounts from the previous year are captured within the new reporting structure. This initiative is highly relevant to open data because it represents a large-scale, structured exchange of sensitive personal financial information between sovereign governments. While currently restricted for national security reasons, such massive datasets often serve as foundational models for future public transparency initiatives. Furthermore, the agreement includes a voluntary disclosure mechanism that encourages individuals to repatriate undeclared assets, potentially leading to richer, more accurate economic data regarding foreign holdings once these assets are brought back into the formal financial system.
