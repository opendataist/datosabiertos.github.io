Guía sobre el tope a la subida del alquiler: ¿Cuánto más me pueden subir al mes? ¿A quién afecta el índice de referencia?

The Spanish government’s housing law introduces a new rental reference index, aiming to stabilize rent increases for new contracts signed after May 2023. This shift marks a significant change in how rental prices are regulated, replacing previous temporary inflation caps with a structured, law-defined mechanism. The core conclusion is that the state now actively controls rental inflation for the majority of the market, distinguishing between newer agreements and legacy contracts. For contracts governed by this new rule, annual increases are strictly capped at a low percentage, calculated using inflation data and underlying economic indicators. Tenants benefit from predictable, limited costs, while landlords must adhere to these ceilings unless both parties agree otherwise. However, this protection does not extend to older agreements, which remain tied to the general Consumer Price Index. This creates a dual system where the financial burden of inflation affects different tenants based on when they signed their leases, highlighting the transitional complexity of the legal framework. This development is highly relevant to open data because it relies on the transparent publication of statistical indices to enforce legal standards. The INE’s role in releasing this index publicly ensures that the limits are objective and verifiable, fostering trust in the housing market. Open access to this data allows tenants, landlords, and researchers to monitor trends, verify compliance, and understand the economic impact of housing policies. Without such transparent data streams, enforcing these complex regulations would be significantly more difficult.
