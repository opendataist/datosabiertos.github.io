Nvidia unveils robot training tech, new gaming chips, Toyota deal at CES 2025 - ET CIO

Nvidia is expanding its AI infrastructure into the consumer and robotics sectors by introducing Cosmos, a foundation model that generates synthetic, photo-realistic video for training autonomous vehicles and robots. This innovation significantly reduces the cost of gathering real-world training data, marking a strategic shift toward democratizing physical-world AI development. By offering these models under an open license, Nvidia aims to replicate the widespread adoption seen in enterprise language models, positioning itself as the foundational platform for next-generation robotics and industrial automation. Simultaneously, Nvidia is bolstering its presence in personal computing with its new RTX gaming chips and Project DIGITS desktop. These products leverage data-center-grade AI technology to deliver superior graphics and enable individual developers to test AI systems locally. While the gaming hardware targets short-term sales growth through enhanced visual fidelity, the developer-focused desktop underscores the company’s broader goal of bringing high-performance AI capabilities to the edges of the network, allowing for more accessible and immediate experimentation with complex models. This release is highly relevant to open data as it demonstrates the critical value of open, synthetic datasets in overcoming traditional data bottlenecks. By providing open licenses for these generative models, Nvidia facilitates a more collaborative and efficient data ecosystem, reducing reliance on proprietary, expensive data collection methods. This approach accelerates innovation across robotics and AI fields, ensuring that high-quality training data remains accessible to a wider range of researchers and developers, thereby fostering greater transparency and standardization in the open data community.
