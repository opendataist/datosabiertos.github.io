SAISD spent thousands to have attorneys review media outlets' requests. Here’s what KSAT Investigates asked for.

The San Antonio Independent School District spent significant funds on legal fees to review public records requests from media outlets, often exceeding the cost of basic operational expenses like teacher salaries. This high expenditure highlights a systemic reliance on external attorneys to process information that should ideally be handled internally, creating unnecessary financial burdens on the public. Transparency advocates argue that routing numerous public records through outside counsel defeats the purpose of open government laws, which prioritize prompt access to presumed open information. The extensive legal review process raises concerns about administrative inefficiency and suggests that the district may be unnecessarily complicating simple requests rather than facilitating public oversight. This situation is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of reducing friction in public information access. When agencies incur high costs to withhold or delay data, it signals a lack of proactive transparency. True open data initiatives require institutions to minimize barriers and legal overhead, ensuring that citizens can easily obtain the information they are legally entitled to without prohibitive costs or delays.
