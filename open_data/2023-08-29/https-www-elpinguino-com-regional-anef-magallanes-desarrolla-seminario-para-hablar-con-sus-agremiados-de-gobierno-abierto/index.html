The seminar highlights that ignorance of legal frameworks and inaccessible justice systems hinder the rights of citizens and officials. Participants emphasized the urgent need to democratize legal knowledge, challenging the perception of a two-tiered justice system based on wealth. By fostering dialogue on penal processes, the initiative aims to empower fiscal employees and ensure equitable access to legal information. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical link between transparency and effective citizenship. Accessible, clear information acts as a vital tool for empowering marginalized groups, such as state employees. It demonstrates how open data initiatives can bridge the gap between complex legal systems and the public, promoting justice through knowledge dissemination rather than exclusive institutional access. Ultimately, the event illustrates that sharing structured legal data helps combat systemic inequality. When governments and associations proactively distribute essential legal insights, they enable better governance and individual agency. This approach reinforces the principle that open data is not just about availability, but about ensuring that information is understandable and usable for all stakeholders in the justice system.
