Leading airports in Africa sharing from the $201bn cargo revenue

The article highlights that air cargo is a vital component of global trade, with Africa playing an increasingly significant role in exporting high-value agricultural produce and other goods. This growth underscores the continent's potential to leverage air freight for economic development, moving beyond traditional ground transport limitations to reach international markets more efficiently. However, the distribution of this capacity is highly concentrated. The top ten cargo airports are dominated by major hubs in Egypt, Kenya, South Africa, and Ethiopia, while Nigeria, despite having numerous aerodromes, has only one entry. This disparity reveals structural inefficiencies, such as poor infrastructure, security concerns, and lack of governmental support, which hinder broader participation and limit the scalability of Africa’s logistics network. For open data initiatives, this report emphasizes the critical need for transparent, standardized, and accessible data on African aviation infrastructure. Reliable datasets regarding cargo volumes, airport capacities, and export commodities are essential for policymakers and investors to identify bottlenecks, allocate resources effectively, and promote equitable development across the continent’s diverse aviation sectors.
