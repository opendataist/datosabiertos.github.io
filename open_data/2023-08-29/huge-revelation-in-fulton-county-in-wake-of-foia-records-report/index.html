HUGE REVELATION IN FULTON COUNTY IN WAKE OF FOIA RECORDS: REPORT

Recent FOIA disclosures from Fulton County, Georgia, suggest that election officials deputized hundreds of activists opposed to Donald Trump to process absentee ballots in 2020. These individuals, allegedly affiliated with the ACLU, were reportedly given authority to cancel absentee ballot applications via iPads without direct county supervision. This revelation has intensified scrutiny of the election administration in the jurisdiction where former President Trump faces criminal charges related to election interference. The timing of these findings is significant given the ongoing legal proceedings against Trump and his allies. While state officials have maintained their diligence regarding voter fraud claims, the new information fuels narratives regarding the integrity of the 2020 vote count. If verified, these operational details could influence the trajectory of the Georgia case, adding a layer of procedural complexity to the accusations surrounding phone calls made to state election officials. This article is highly relevant to open_data because it demonstrates the tangible impact of Freedom of Information Act requests on public discourse and legal accountability. It highlights how transparent access to government records can uncover specific administrative practices that might otherwise remain hidden. The case underscores the necessity of data transparency in elections, showing how released documents can trigger new lines of inquiry and potentially reshape political narratives by providing raw, unfiltered evidence of administrative actions.
