Inteligencia artificial y derechos de autor

Inteligencia artificial y derechos de autor

This article highlights the ethical complexities that arise when artificial intelligence tools rapidly repurpose copyrighted creative works without explicit permission. It illustrates how easily algorithms can animate static images from existing films, raising immediate concerns about intellectual property rights and the necessity for creators to verify source material and acknowledge original authors. The text emphasizes that while AI can enhance imagination, human responsibility remains paramount in respecting artistic origins and maintaining transparency in the creative process. The core argument centers on the blurred boundaries between human and machine authorship, questioning who truly owns a work when AI is involved. By referencing economic theories on creative destruction, the piece suggests that unchecked AI adoption risks undermining the ethical and economic foundations of authorship. It argues that without proper regulation, the efficiency of AI could lead to exclusion rather than widespread benefit, potentially devaluing the unique human spark that drives genuine artistic creation. Relevance to open data lies in the urgent need to address data provenance, consent, and licensing in algorithmic training. As AI systems increasingly rely on large datasets containing copyrighted or protected content, the debate mirrors open data challenges regarding attribution and usage rights. Understanding these ethical frameworks is crucial for developing responsible data practices that balance innovation with the protection of original creators, ensuring that technological progress does not come at the cost of artistic integrity or fair compensation.
