The World Bank’s 2022 report reveals that external debt in low- and middle-income countries surged significantly in 2020, outpacing economic growth and raising sustainability concerns. While the global health crisis necessitated massive stimulus packages to protect vulnerable populations, these efforts exacerbated pre-existing financial vulnerabilities. The resulting debt burden has grown to levels that threaten long-term stability, prompting urgent calls for comprehensive management strategies to mitigate risks and prevent future distress. To address this crisis, international bodies have implemented temporary suspension mechanisms and frameworks for restructuring unsustainable debts. However, leaders emphasize that these measures must be complemented by debt reduction and improved transparency to ensure genuine recovery. The narrative highlights the critical need for policymakers to anticipate potential debt crises as financial conditions fluctuate, underscoring that sustainable borrowing is essential for poverty reduction and broader economic health.
