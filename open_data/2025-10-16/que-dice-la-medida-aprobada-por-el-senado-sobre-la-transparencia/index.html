¿Qué dice la medida aprobada por el Senado sobre la transparencia?

¿Qué dice la medida aprobada por el Senado sobre la transparencia?

The proposed legislation significantly restricts public access to information by extending response deadlines and requiring personal data, which critics argue enables bureaucratic delays and intimidates citizens. This shift prioritizes governmental control over transparency, effectively normalizing opacity and hindering the public’s ability to scrutinize state actions. Such measures risk lowering Puerto Rico’s global standing in transparency indexes, potentially placing it below nations with poor human rights records. By allowing broader confidentiality claims and providing data in unusable formats, the bill undermines the principle that information belongs to the people, not the state. This development is crucial for open data advocates, as it highlights the fragility of digital rights when legislative frameworks favor secrecy. It demonstrates how procedural changes can erode accountability, emphasizing the urgent need for robust protections against state-controlled information barriers to maintain democratic integrity.
