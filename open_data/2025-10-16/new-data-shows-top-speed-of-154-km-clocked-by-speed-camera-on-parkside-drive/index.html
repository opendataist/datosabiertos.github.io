New data shows top speed of 154 km clocked by speed camera on Parkside Drive

Freedom of Information data reveals extreme speeding on Toronto’s Parkside Drive, with vehicles reaching speeds up to 154 km/h in a 40 km/h zone. This alarming evidence highlights severe safety risks, including past fatal collisions, and underscores the urgent need for effective enforcement. The findings demonstrate that despite ongoing construction, drivers are consistently exceeding safe limits, resulting in dangerous conditions for pedestrians and motorists alike. The Parkside speed camera has generated significant revenue but faced relentless vandalism, leading to its temporary removal. While city leadership insists automated enforcement reduces speeding, the camera’s destruction reflects public resistance and political controversy. Premier Doug Ford’s threats to ban such cameras as a “cash grab” clash with local officials and safety advocates who view them as essential tools for reducing severe traffic violations and protecting community safety. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparent access to enforcement records can validate safety concerns and fuel policy debates. By making violation statistics publicly available, the city allows communities to verify the effectiveness of traffic measures. Open data thus serves as a critical mechanism for accountability, enabling advocates to challenge political narratives and demand solutions based on empirical evidence rather than speculation.
