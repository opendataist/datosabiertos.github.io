¿Tus apps protegen tu privacidad o te espían? El informe que revela riesgos ocultos en Colombia - Pulzo

¿Tus apps protegen tu privacidad o te espían? El informe que revela riesgos ocultos en Colombia - Pulzo

The article highlights a significant disconnect between Colombian data protection laws and the reality of mobile app usage. Despite existing regulations mandating informed consent, numerous applications collect excessive personal data without proper justification. This practice exploits user ignorance, as most individuals install apps without reading terms and conditions, thereby compromising their digital privacy and security against potential misuse or leaks. This issue is critical for open data because it illustrates the urgent need for transparency and accountability in data handling. When private entities obscure data collection practices, it undermines public trust and hinders the creation of reliable, ethical data ecosystems. Strengthening digital literacy and enforcing strict oversight are essential to bridge the gap between legal frameworks and actual technological implementation, ensuring that data usage remains ethical and user-centric. Ultimately, the report calls for a cultural shift where both developers and users assume greater responsibility for privacy. Users must actively monitor and restrict unnecessary permissions, while companies must provide genuine control over data sharing. This dynamic is vital for open data initiatives, as secure and consensual data environments are foundational for generating trustworthy insights that benefit society without violating individual rights.
