La recesión que se negó demasiado tiempo

La recesión que se negó demasiado tiempo

The article concludes that Bolivia’s prolonged technical recession is not a temporary anomaly but the inevitable result of a structurally exhausted economic model. The government’s reliance on public spending and subsidies, funded by dwindling gas exports, has proven unsustainable. This approach masked underlying fragilities until reserves vanished, leaving the economy vulnerable to shocks and unable to correct its deep-seated imbalances. A central theme is the critical failure of transparency and institutional credibility. By delaying data releases and dismissing international warnings, authorities prioritized political narratives over factual economic reality. This lack of openness eroded trust among investors and citizens, exacerbating inflation and currency instability. The narrative underscores that concealing the true state of public finances prevents effective policy responses, turning manageable adjustments into severe crises. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the tangible dangers of non-transparent governance. When official statistics are withheld or manipulated to align with political agendas, society cannot accurately assess risks or hold leaders accountable. True economic recovery requires more than technical fixes; it demands a commitment to open, timely, and accurate data. Only through transparency can institutions rebuild the trust necessary for sustainable growth and informed decision-making.
