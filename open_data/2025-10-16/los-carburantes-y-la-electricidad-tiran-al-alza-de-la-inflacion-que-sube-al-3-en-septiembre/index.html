Los carburantes y la electricidad tiran al alza de la inflación, que sube al 3% en septiembre

Spain’s inflation rose to three percent in September, reaching its highest level since February. This increase resulted from smaller declines in fuel and electricity prices compared to the previous year, alongside notable rises in transport and housing costs. The data reflects underlying economic pressures despite broader moderation efforts. Underlying inflation remained stable at 2.4 percent, aligning with the European Central Bank’s target trajectory. This stability suggests that core price pressures are manageable, even as specific sectors like housing continue to drive overall consumer price indices upward across different regions. This information is relevant to open data because it highlights how public statistics track economic health through granular indicators. Access to such detailed, official datasets allows citizens and analysts to verify government claims, understand regional disparities, and assess the real impact of monetary policy on everyday living costs.
