En septiembre, una familia tipo necesitó más de $1.175.000 para no ser pobre

This report illustrates the critical role of national statistical institutions in monitoring socioeconomic well-being through transparent data on inflation and poverty lines. By defining the cost of basic food baskets and total consumption needs, INDEC provides essential metrics that allow researchers and citizens to independently verify living conditions. This transparency is fundamental to open data, as it empowers users to analyze real purchasing power without relying solely on government narratives. The data reveals a steady increase in both the cost of living and inflation rates, highlighting how essential goods and services have become progressively more expensive. These figures demonstrate the importance of accessible, high-quality datasets in understanding economic trends and their impact on vulnerable populations. Open access to this granular information enables a deeper comprehension of how macroeconomic indicators translate into daily hardships for families, fostering informed public debate. Ultimately, the relevance to open data lies in the ability to validate official statistics and create independent analyses. When datasets regarding poverty thresholds and price indices are freely available, they support accountability and encourage civic engagement. This openness ensures that societal challenges, such as rising costs, are addressed with evidence-based solutions, reinforcing the democratic value of transparent governance.
