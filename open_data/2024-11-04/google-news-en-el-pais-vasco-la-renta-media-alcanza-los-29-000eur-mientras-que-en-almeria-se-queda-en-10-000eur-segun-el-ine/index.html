The analysis of recent economic data reveals a profound territorial asymmetry in Spain, where the north and major metropolitan areas exhibit significantly higher incomes than the south. This disparity not only reflects differences in living standards but also structural inequalities in economic opportunities, creating a landscape in which geographic location plays a more decisive role in income-generating capacity than post-pandemic recovery. The relevance of this article to open data lies in the critical importance of the granularity and accessibility of statistical information. By demonstrating that inequalities exist even among neighboring neighborhoods, the report underscores the need for disaggregated datasets that enable the identification of micro-inequalities. Transparency in the publication of local indicators facilitates precise monitoring of social justice and the effectiveness of public policies, preventing national averages from masking extreme local realities. Understanding this detailed distribution is essential for fostering evidence-based governance. When data are open and analyzed from a geospatial perspective, public officials can design more precise and equitable interventions. This transforms statistics from a mere historical record into an active tool for reducing inequality gaps, ensuring that information serves to close structural disparities between communities.
