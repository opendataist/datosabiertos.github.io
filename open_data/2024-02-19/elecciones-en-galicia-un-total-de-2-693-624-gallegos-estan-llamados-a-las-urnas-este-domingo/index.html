Elecciones en Galicia |Un total de 2.693.624 gallegos están llamados a las urnas este domingo

The definitive census of Galician voters residing abroad shows a slight increase in the number of registered individuals, highlighting the growing demographic weight of the diaspora in regional politics. Unlike in previous general elections, these voters can now cast their ballots without having to submit a prior request, a change intended to simplify participation. This structural shift underscores the critical role that emigrants play in determining the composition of the autonomous government—a factor that has historically been decisive in close races, where marginal shifts can alter majority outcomes. Geographically, the influence is concentrated in specific countries and provinces, with Argentina hosting the largest bloc of potential voters, followed by Cuba, Brazil, and Uruguay. Within Galicia, the provinces of A Coruña and Pontevedra account for the highest numbers of expatriate electors. This distribution highlights how international migration patterns directly correlate with political representation, suggesting that the foreign vote is not merely peripheral but a core component of the electoral landscape that political parties must strategically engage with to secure power. Voting mechanisms have also evolved, with a significant rise in mail-in ballots compared to pre-pandemic levels, although there has been a notable drop from the record highs seen during the 2020 health crisis. This trend indicates a maturing electoral process in which remote participation is normalized yet fluctuates based on recent historical contexts. For open data, this scenario illustrates the necessity of transparent, accessible, and timely demographic and electoral statistics. Understanding these granular data points enables better civic analysis, ensuring that the voices of dispersed populations are accurately counted and that electoral integrity remains robust across borders.
