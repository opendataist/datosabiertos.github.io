Radiografía del tractor en Castilla y León

The article highlights how European farmers are using tractors as powerful symbols in their protests against agricultural policies, bureaucratic burdens, and environmental regulations. This movement has paralyzed major cities, demonstrating the sector’s deep dissatisfaction with the current regulatory framework. The visual impact of these vehicles in urban centers underscores the urgency of addressing the socio-economic pressures facing the farming community. Behind the protests lies a complex statistical reality regarding the agricultural machinery fleet. While official registers indicate a high number of tractors, significant discrepancies exist due to poor deregistration practices, meaning the actual count of functional vehicles may be lower. Furthermore, the fleet is aging rapidly as sales plummet, leading to reduced productivity and increased environmental impact. This trend reflects a sector struggling with profitability, where the cost of new, technologically advanced machinery is increasingly prohibitive for many farmers. This narrative is relevant to open data because it reveals the critical gap between registered statistics and real-world usage. Accurate open data is essential to understand the true state of agricultural infrastructure and inform effective policy-making. Without reliable, accessible information on machinery circulation and deregistration, policymakers cannot adequately address issues of safety, environmental compliance, or the economic viability of the sector, perpetuating the cycle of crisis and protest.
