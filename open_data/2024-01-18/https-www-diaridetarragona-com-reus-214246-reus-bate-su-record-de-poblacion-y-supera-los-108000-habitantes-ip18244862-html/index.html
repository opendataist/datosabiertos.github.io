Reus has surpassed its historical population peak, reaffirming its status as a major urban center with over 108,000 residents. This growth is driven primarily by net immigration rather than natural demographic increase, as the region experiences a natural population decline due to higher mortality rates than birth rates. Consequently, the city’s expansion relies heavily on the continuous influx of people moving from other areas or countries, highlighting migration as the key engine behind its current demographic vitality. The resilience of this migratory trend, even amidst global crises like the pandemic, underscores the region's attractiveness and stability. Unlike previous economic downturns that triggered significant emigration, current conditions have maintained positive migration flows, allowing Reus to consistently grow. This sustained movement indicates that the area offers a compelling quality of life, with balanced housing costs and strong connectivity compared to neighboring cities, making it a preferred destination for new residents seeking both economic opportunity and urban convenience. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical importance of detailed demographic indicators, such as birth, death, and migration balances, to understand true population dynamics. It emphasizes how open statistical data allows researchers and policymakers to distinguish between natural growth and migratory changes, enabling more informed urban planning and resource allocation. By making these granular datasets accessible, society can better analyze the underlying causes of urban trends and ensure that development remains sustainable and responsive to the actual composition of the community.
