Recent crime statistics for the Sligo-Leitrim division reveal a significant rise in violent offenses, including sexual offenses, assaults, robberies, and property damage. This upward trend contrasts with decreases in thefts, fraud, and drug-related incidents, highlighting a complex shift in local criminal activity patterns that demands careful interpretation by community stakeholders and policymakers. Crucially, these figures represent the first release following a suspension triggered by identified irregularities in how gardai recorded crimes on the PULSE system. The Central Statistics Office resumed publication only after completing a comprehensive review of data quality, acknowledging historical issues where a significant portion of reported crimes were either unrecorded, misclassified, or incorrectly categorized as non-crimes. This article is vital to open data because it illustrates the critical importance of data governance and transparency in public sector statistics. It underscores how administrative errors in data collection can distort reality, emphasizing the need for robust validation processes. For open data advocates, it serves as a case study on the challenges of ensuring data integrity and the necessity of clear explanations when resuming published datasets after quality interventions.
