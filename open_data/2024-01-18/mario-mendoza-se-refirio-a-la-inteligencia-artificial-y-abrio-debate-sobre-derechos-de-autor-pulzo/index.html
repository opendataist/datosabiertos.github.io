Mario Mendoza se refirió a la inteligencia artificial y abrió debate sobre derechos de autor - Pulzo

The article highlights how artificial intelligence can now mimic renowned literary and musical styles with such precision that human experts struggle to distinguish AI-generated content from authentic works. This technological leap demonstrates the immense creative potential of AI, challenging traditional boundaries in art and media by producing outputs that are virtually indistinguishable from human creation. Legally, this raises significant questions about copyright ownership, as current international jurisprudence generally denies copyright protection to works generated entirely by algorithms, emphasizing the necessity of human authorship. While some jurisdictions recognize human oversight as a basis for rights, others strictly exclude AI-generated material, creating a complex and evolving landscape for intellectual property that lacks unified global standards. This topic is relevant to open data because the training of these AI models relies heavily on vast datasets of copyrighted works. Understanding the legal implications of data usage, attribution, and ownership is crucial for open data initiatives, as it impacts how creative content can be legally accessed, shared, and utilized in the public domain without infringing on intellectual property rights.
