El PIB de China superó el objetivo oficial al crecer un 5,2 % en 2023 - Mundo - ABC Color

China’s economy closed the year with a 5.2% GDP growth, surpassing both the previous figure constrained by pandemic-related restrictions and the initial official target. This expansion reflects a recovery with positive momentum following the pandemic, although it remains below some more optimistic international forecasts. The growth was primarily driven by the services sector, while industry and agriculture showed more moderate advances, consolidating the country’s productive base amid resistance to external pressures. However, the economic performance reveals significant structural challenges, such as business caution and weak household confidence. Despite exceeding the annual target, the recovery proved uneven and less vigorous than initially expected, as evidenced by the sluggish growth in the last quarter. Experts note that gradual stimulus measures have been insufficient to reverse this trend, highlighting that uncertainty and the complexity of the external environment continue to negatively affect domestic economic dynamics. This analysis is relevant to the field of *open data* because it illustrates how transparency in macroeconomic indicators allows for comparing official expectations with empirical reality. By making detailed sectoral and quarterly data publicly available, authorities enable independent analysts to identify deviations and risks, such as the real estate crisis or deflation. This availability of open information is crucial for critically evaluating public policies and understanding the limitations of the recovery, promoting evidence-based governance that is accessible and verifiable.
