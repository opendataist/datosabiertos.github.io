The article warns that attacks on the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) represent a direct threat to transparency and accountability in Mexico. This institution is essential for ensuring public access to information and limiting governmental discretion; its dismantling undermines democratic principles and historically acquired citizen rights. At the local level, the construction of an integrated waste management center is examined, highlighting that the project’s success depends on effective community engagement. Local resistance should not be ignored or politicized, but rather addressed through dialogue and consultation, involving municipal authorities to overcome obstacles and build consensus around sustainable waste management. The relevance of this text for open data lies in the interdependence between institutional transparency and active citizen participation. The availability of public data, safeguarded by guaranteeing bodies such as the INAI, forms the foundation for society to exercise its right to know and to hold authorities accountable. Without real access to information, citizens’ capacity to voice opinions, raise questions, and participate in public decisions—such as the siting of infrastructure—is severely limited.
