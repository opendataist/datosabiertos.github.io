The Senate rejected the nomination for the INAI commissioner, failing to secure the required supermajority. This outcome highlights the constitutional challenges in appointing key officials for transparency institutions, which are vital for safeguarding open data governance in Mexico. The failure necessitates a new proposal to fill the vacancy, underscoring the political complexity of maintaining independent oversight bodies. Such delays can hinder effective access to information policies and data protection frameworks. Concurrently, discussions continued on reforms barring certain offenders from public office, though the session was suspended. These parallel processes reflect the broader legislative struggle to balance institutional integrity with political realities in a democracy committed to openness.
