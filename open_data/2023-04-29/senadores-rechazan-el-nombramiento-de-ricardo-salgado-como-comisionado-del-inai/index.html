Senadores rechazan el nombramiento de Ricardo Salgado como comisionado del Inai

The Senate plenary rejected the proposal to appoint a commissioner to the National Institute of Transparency and Access to Public Information (INAI), failing to meet the quorum required for approval. This failure has paralyzed legislative sessions, as the opposition, composed of various political parties, has announced it will block any further votes until the panel of commissioners is fully constituted—a necessary condition for the autonomous body to function and hold sessions effectively. The situation sparked intense political and physical conflict within the legislative chamber, highlighting a profound lack of trust among parliamentary blocs. Opposition senators accused the ruling majority of deceiving them, claiming that the majority had supposedly agreed to support the appointment but ultimately voted against it. These incidents underscore the urgent need for transparent mechanisms and serious agreements in the management of key positions, as opacity and political maneuvering delegitimize the legislative process and hinder democracy. This article is of fundamental relevance to the field of open data, given that a fully constituted and operational INAI is an essential guarantee of government transparency. Without the required quorum, the agency lacks the legal capacity to effectively sanction institutions that withhold information, thereby violating citizens’ right to access public data. The political blockade not only delays the legislative agenda but also directly weakens accountability and the integrity of the country’s open data ecosystem, impairing society’s ability to monitor the use of public resources and policies.
