New Commission Raises Questions Among Civil Society - La strada International

The new European Commission structure has sparked significant concern among civil society groups, who argue that centralizing migration, labor, and equality portfolios under security or crisis management roles signals a decline in commitment to human rights. Critics fear that treating migration primarily as a security threat undermines the protection of migrant workers, while merging equality issues with crisis management suggests women’s rights are being deprioritized in favor of administrative efficiency. This reallocation raises critical implications for open data, as the framing of these portfolios dictates how data is collected, categorized, and utilized to shape policy. When labor rights and gender equality are subsumed under broader, less specialized mandates, there is a risk that disaggregated data essential for monitoring discrimination, exploitation, and inequality may be obscured or lost. Transparent, accessible data becomes less effective if the political will to analyze it through a dedicated rights-based lens is weakened by structural downsizing. Consequently, the relevance to open data lies in the potential erosion of the evidence base required to hold institutions accountable. If the new Commission prioritizes border control and crisis response over dedicated social protections, the metrics for success may shift away from individual rights and toward aggregate security outcomes. This structural shift highlights the importance of ensuring that open data initiatives can independently track these policy changes, preventing the loss of visibility for marginalized groups whose rights depend on specific, targeted data analysis.
