Oman’s bank lending rises 3.4% in July, deposits jump 12.5%

Oman’s banking sector demonstrated robust expansion in mid-2024, driven primarily by significant growth in credit and deposits. Islamic banking entities emerged as the key growth engine, recording substantially higher lending and asset increases compared to their conventional counterparts, which showed only modest gains. This divergence highlights a structural shift in the market, where faith-based finance is rapidly gaining share and outperforming traditional models in attracting capital. Simultaneously, the overall deposit base expanded vigorously, with households and private corporations maintaining the largest shares of new funds. The surge in liquidity suggests strong confidence among individuals and businesses in the banking system. The data reveals that conventional banks continued to dominate total deposit volumes, yet Islamic banks achieved the highest growth rates, indicating a successful transition of funds from traditional to Sharia-compliant products. This information is relevant to open_data initiatives as it exemplifies the value of transparent, granular financial reporting. By making detailed statistics on sectoral credit distribution and banking performance publicly accessible, central banks enable analysts, researchers, and the public to monitor economic health and financial inclusion trends. Such data transparency fosters accountability and allows for better informed decision-making regarding economic policy and investment strategies.
