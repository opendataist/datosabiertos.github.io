INE: tasa de desocupación nacional se situó en 8,9% - El Clarin de Chile

The Chilean labor market shows a nuanced recovery, characterized by modest improvements in employment levels and a slight decline in unemployment. Although the total number of employed individuals has increased, this positive trend is offset by a rise in the number of people actively seeking work after losing their jobs, indicating that the labor market’s capacity to absorb workers is not yet fully robust. A persistent challenge is the high rate of informal employment, which has grown notably, particularly among self-employed workers and in sectors such as commerce. This expansion of informality affects both men and women, pointing to structural weaknesses in job quality and security. Despite gains in female labor force participation, women continue to experience higher unemployment rates than men, underscoring ongoing gender disparities in the labor market. This report is relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the need for transparent, high-quality statistical dissemination to support sound policy-making. Accessible raw data enables more in-depth analysis of complex trends, such as the relationship between informal work and gender gaps. By making these metrics openly available, stakeholders can better assess economic conditions and advocate for inclusive labor policies, ensuring that statistical insights lead to tangible social improvements.
