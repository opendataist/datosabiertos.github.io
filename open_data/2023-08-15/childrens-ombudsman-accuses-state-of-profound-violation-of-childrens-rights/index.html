Children’s ombudsman accuses State of ‘profound violation of children’s rights’

The Ombudsman for Children has formally criticized the State for a profound violation of children’s rights regarding mental health and disability care. The core conclusion is that the Health Service Executive has seriously failed to uphold children's right to healthcare, resulting in significant geographical disparities where location often determines access to essential therapeutic services. This systemic failure has left many children waiting excessively long periods for initial appointments or receiving inadequate care once they enter the system. A critical implication of this critique is the severe lack of interagency cooperation and organizational silos within the health sector. The Ombudsman highlights that the absence of joint work between relevant departments prevents effective early intervention, while administrative inefficiencies, such as reliance on paper records, exacerbate the risk of vulnerable children being lost to follow-up. This fragmentation directly undermines the best interests of children, creating a environment where families feel forced to abandon their children to access necessary support due to the sheer inadequacy of existing provisions. This article is highly relevant to open data because the identified failures—ranging from lost patient cases to service waiting lists—highlight the urgent need for transparent, accessible, and integrated public health information. By exposing the "silos" and poor service quality, the report underscores how data transparency can serve as a vital tool for accountability in public administration. Without open access to accurate service metrics and funding allocations, systemic issues in healthcare delivery remain obscured, preventing the public and policymakers from effectively addressing the structural deficits that harm vulnerable populations.
