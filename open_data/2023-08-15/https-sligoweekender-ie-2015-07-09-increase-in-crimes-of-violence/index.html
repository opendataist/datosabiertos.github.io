The article highlights a concerning rise in violent crimes and property damage within the Sligo-Leitrim region, despite decreases in theft and fraud. More critically, it underscores significant data integrity issues within the Garda recording system. Irregularities were discovered where reported crimes were not logged, misclassified, or recorded as non-crimes, revealing substantial inaccuracies in official statistics. This disclosure is vital to open data because it demonstrates that public datasets often require rigorous validation before being trusted for analysis or policy-making. The suspension and subsequent resumption of crime figure publication illustrate the necessity of transparency regarding data quality limitations. It serves as a reminder that raw data may contain systemic errors that distort the true picture of societal conditions. Ultimately, the relevance to open data lies in the need for critical evaluation of source reliability. Stakeholders must recognize that even government-published statistics can be flawed due to administrative inconsistencies. This case emphasizes the importance of metadata that clearly indicates data processing history and known limitations, ensuring users can interpret the information with appropriate caution and context.
