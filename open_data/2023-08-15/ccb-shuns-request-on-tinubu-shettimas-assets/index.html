CCB shuns request on Tinubu, Shettima’s assets

The Code of Conduct Bureau’s refusal to disclose the asset declarations of the President and Vice President highlights a critical conflict between statutory obligations and executive transparency. Despite explicit provisions in the Freedom of Information Act requiring public access to records of public interest within a specified timeframe, the Bureau ignored these mandates. This non-compliance suggests an institutional resistance to accountability, raising significant concerns about whether government bodies prioritize legal transparency or administrative secrecy when handling sensitive information about top leadership. Legal experts emphasize that this refusal constitutes a clear violation of democratic principles, obstructing the public’s right to hold high-ranking officials accountable. The denial of such information undermines trust in governmental integrity and fuels suspicions of potential corruption, as asset declarations are fundamental tools for verifying the wealth and honesty of public servants. By withholding these records, the Bureau not only breaches statutory duties but also erodes the foundational tenets of good governance, demonstrating a failure to uphold the mechanisms designed to ensure official conduct aligns with public service ethics. This case is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it illustrates the gap between data availability laws and their actual enforcement in practice. It reveals how legal frameworks can be rendered ineffective if judicial interpretations or institutional attitudes do not strictly enforce disclosure mandates. The incident underscores the urgent need for stronger legal precedents and cultural shifts within government institutions to ensure that open data policies translate into tangible transparency, thereby empowering citizens with the information necessary to monitor public officials effectively.
