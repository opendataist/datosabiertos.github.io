Researchers find no evidence that game play time limits for minors in China reduce longer play

A recent study reveals that China’s strict limits on minors’ online gaming time failed to reduce heavy gaming rates. Analyzing vast datasets, researchers found that the percentage of players engaging in excessive daily play actually increased after mandates were introduced, suggesting that regulatory caps on play duration are ineffective at curbing addictive behaviors. This finding challenges the assumption that simple time restrictions can successfully manage digital consumption among young people. The data indicates that government interventions, whether limiting specific games or general screen time, do not necessarily alter user habits. Consequently, policymakers may need to reconsider their strategies, as current top-down approaches appear insufficient to address the underlying drivers of excessive gaming. This research is crucial for open_data advocates, as it highlights the power of large-scale, anonymized digital traces to inform public policy. It underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible data to evaluate the real-world impact of regulations. By leveraging such datasets, researchers can provide evidence-based insights that help governments design more effective, rather than merely restrictive, digital health policies for minors.
