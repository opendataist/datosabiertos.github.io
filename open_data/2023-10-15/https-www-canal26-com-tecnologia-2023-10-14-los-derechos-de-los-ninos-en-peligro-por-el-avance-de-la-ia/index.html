A recent study reveals that global artificial intelligence guidelines are failing to protect children’s rights. Although general ethical principles such as privacy and fairness are widely recognized, the research points to a critical omission regarding the specific needs of minors. This gap suggests that current governance is insufficient to ensure truly inclusive and safe technological development for all generations. Moreover, analyses show that most existing regulations remain at an abstract level, lacking practical methods for implementation and legally binding frameworks. This lack of concreteness hinders the necessary transparency in the deployment of automated systems. On the other hand, there is a clear geographic disparity in the authorship of these documents, with significant underrepresentation from regions such as Latin America, Africa, and Asia, which distorts the global perspective on technology ethics. This finding is crucial for the open data community, as it highlights the need to include diverse data and multiple voices in the creation of algorithms and policies. Opacity in authorship and the dominance of certain languages in current standards perpetuate structural biases. Promoting accessibility to information on AI governance and fostering participation from underrepresented regions is essential to building more equitable and transparent systems, ensuring that the benefits of technology are justly distributed.
