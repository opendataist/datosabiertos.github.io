Freedom of Information data shows number of schools that still contain asbestos

Asbestos remains a pervasive hazard in English educational facilities, with the vast majority of schools still containing this carcinogen despite its decades-long ban. Although undamaged materials pose minimal risk, the potential for fiber release during disturbance creates a severe health threat, leading to incurable cancers and lung diseases that can take decades to manifest. This persistent presence highlights a critical gap in long-term safety protocols, where management strategies are often prioritized over the urgent need for complete remediation. The human cost of this exposure is staggering, evidenced by thousands of deaths among former staff and students linked to historical asbestos use in schools. Unions and health committees are increasingly calling for immediate, government-funded removal of all asbestos from educational buildings, arguing that current management plans are insufficient to protect future generations. The demand for phased removal within specific timeframes reflects a growing consensus that reliance on containment is a flawed approach to a lethal material. This issue is highly relevant to open data initiatives because transparency in identifying affected sites is fundamental to accountability and public safety. Publishing detailed lists of schools containing asbestos empowers parents, staff, and advocacy groups to demand action and monitor compliance. Open data serves as a crucial tool in bridging the information gap, ensuring that communities can access vital health risk information and driving policy changes toward safer educational environments.
