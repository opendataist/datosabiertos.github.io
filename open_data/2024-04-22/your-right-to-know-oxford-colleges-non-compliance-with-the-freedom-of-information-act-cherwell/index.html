Your right to know: Oxford colleges’ non-compliance with the Freedom of Information Act - Cherwell

The investigation reveals a significant compliance gap within Oxford University’s collegiate system regarding Freedom of Information Act requests. While the Act mandates transparency from public authorities, many individual colleges consistently fail to respond to inquiries from student journalists. This lack of responsiveness undermines the legislative intent of the FOIA, which was designed to break traditional secrecy and empower the public with access to information. The high rate of non-compliance suggests that the decentralized structure allows specific institutions to evade accountability, distorting the overall picture of institutional openness. Relevance to open data lies in the critical role of transparency in maintaining trust and ensuring accurate information dissemination. When data is withheld or obscured, it prevents stakeholders from making informed decisions and hinders the ability to hold power to account. The findings highlight that simply having laws for data access is insufficient if enforcement mechanisms are weak. True open data practices require not just legal rights, but a cultural commitment from institutions to actively share information rather than obstructing it through neglect or unjustified exemptions. Addressing these compliance failures is essential for restoring public trust and ensuring that open data initiatives function as intended. Institutions must move beyond viewing FOI requests as burdensome obstacles and instead recognize them as opportunities for accountability. Strengthening internal systems and accepting consequences for non-compliance are necessary steps toward a more transparent society. Ultimately, reliable access to public information fosters a healthier relationship between governing bodies and the communities they serve, ensuring that openness remains a fundamental pillar of modern governance.
