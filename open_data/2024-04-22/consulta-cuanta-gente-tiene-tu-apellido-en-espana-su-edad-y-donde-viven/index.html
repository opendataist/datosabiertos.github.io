Consulta cuánta gente tiene tu apellido en España, su edad y dónde viven

The INE’s updated data reveals that Spanish surnames are far more than simple identifiers; they serve as historical markers reflecting centuries of migration, conquest, and cultural diversity. The persistence of patronymic endings and specific regional distributions demonstrates how demographic records preserve the nation’s complex social fabric and regional identities, offering a living archive of collective history rather than just static statistics. Furthermore, the analysis of naming trends highlights significant generational shifts and regional nuances, such as unique local favorites in the Canary Islands or the Balearic Islands. By examining the aging patterns of traditional names versus emerging ones, the data underscores how cultural evolution is encoded in personal identifiers. This provides a dynamic view of societal changes, illustrating how language and heritage adapt over time while maintaining deep-rooted connections to specific geographic areas. This resource is highly relevant to open data because it exemplifies how large-scale, publicly accessible government statistics can be transformed into interactive tools for civic engagement. By making this demographic information searchable and visualized, it empowers citizens to explore their personal heritage within the broader national context. Such initiatives demonstrate the value of open data in fostering transparency, encouraging public participation in cultural discovery, and enhancing the utility of administrative datasets for everyday users.
