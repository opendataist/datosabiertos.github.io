Open Data Day celebrated at Strathmore University - KachTech Media

Open Data Day celebrated at Strathmore University - KachTech Media

The celebration of International Open Data Day in Kenya highlights a critical paradox: while high-quality data exists, its limited accessibility and poor contextualization hinder its effective use for policy advocacy and research. The main conclusion is that data must be more than just available; it requires strategic packaging and a cultural shift toward data-driven decision-making to truly benefit society. Without these improvements, the potential of open data to inform governance and drive sustainable development remains largely untapped, despite increased internet penetration. Collaboration is identified as essential to overcoming these barriers, requiring concerted efforts from both government entities and civil society. Stakeholders emphasize that open data should not be solely the government’s responsibility but a shared initiative that empowers citizens to act as effective watchdogs. By safeguarding data privacy and ensuring enforcement mechanisms are robust, stakeholders aim to create a trustworthy ecosystem where information flows freely and securely. This collective approach is necessary to bridge the gap between data generation and practical application in public service delivery. This event is relevant to open data because it underscores the global movement toward utilizing data as a tool for social good and accountability. It illustrates the evolution of open data from a niche advocacy topic to a community-driven practice focused on solving complex societal issues. By bringing together diverse partners to showcase platforms and discuss challenges, the event reinforces the importance of transparency, citizen engagement, and multi-sectoral partnerships. Ultimately, it demonstrates how open data fosters a more informed and empowered public, driving meaningful policy reforms and sustainable development outcomes in Kenya and beyond.
