Accidentes de ciclistas: Seis meses después, Costanera Norte sigue siendo trampa mortal - Nacionales - ABC Color

Accidentes de ciclistas: Seis meses después, Costanera Norte sigue siendo trampa mortal - Nacionales - ABC Color

Six months after severe accidents caused by the poor infrastructure of Asunción’s Costanera Norte, the area remains a lethal trap for cyclists and pedestrians. Critical deficiencies, such as large potholes, broken sidewalks, overgrown vegetation, and inadequate lighting, persist without proper repair. This state of abandonment continues to endanger users, despite previous incidents that nearly cost lives, highlighting a systemic failure in maintaining public safety infrastructure. The responsibility for this dangerous situation lies primarily with the local municipality, which assumed maintenance duties years ago but has failed to execute them effectively. While technical bodies constructed the works, the handover to the city government has been marked by administrative disputes and a lack of coordinated action. Officials have acknowledged neglect, yet no substantial improvements have been implemented to address the structural defects that lead to violent crashes and injuries. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of transparent, accessible public information regarding infrastructure maintenance and institutional accountability. When data on municipal repairs, work handovers, and incident reports is hidden or disorganized, citizens cannot hold authorities responsible. Open data enables the tracking of these neglected obligations, allowing communities to demand transparency and ensuring that safety metrics drive administrative action rather than bureaucratic evasion.
