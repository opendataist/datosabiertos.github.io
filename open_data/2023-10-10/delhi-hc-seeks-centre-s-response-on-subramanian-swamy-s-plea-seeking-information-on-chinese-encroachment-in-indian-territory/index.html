Delhi HC seeks Centre's response on Subramanian Swamy's plea seeking information on 'Chinese Encroachment' in Indian Territory

The article highlights the challenges in accessing sensitive state information through the Right to Information Act, specifically regarding national security concerns like territorial encroachments. It underscores the tension between government transparency and bureaucratic deflection, where legitimate inquiries are often shuffled between departments to avoid providing substantive answers. The legal battle illustrates how RTI mechanisms can be stalled by administrative inertia, leaving citizens without effective remedies. This case serves as a critical reminder of the limitations faced when seeking information on matters deemed sensitive by multiple ministries, exposing gaps in the accountability framework. This is relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates that even with legal provisions for disclosure, structural barriers can prevent the release of vital public interest information. It emphasizes the need for stronger enforcement of transparency laws and highlights the ongoing struggle to balance security claims with the public's right to know, a core principle in the open data movement.
