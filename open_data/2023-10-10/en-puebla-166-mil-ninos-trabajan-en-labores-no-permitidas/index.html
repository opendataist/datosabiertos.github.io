En Puebla 166 mil niños trabajan en labores no permitidas

The article reveals that child labor remains a significant issue in Puebla, with a substantial number of minors engaged in prohibited and hazardous occupations. This situation severely impacts children's physical and psychological development by depriving them of their rights and potential, highlighting a critical social challenge that persists despite slight statistical improvements over recent years. Economic precarity is evident, as the vast majority of working children earn minimum wage or nothing at all, while many are excluded from the education system. The concentration of minors in risky sectors, such as agriculture and commerce, underscores systemic vulnerabilities where vulnerable populations are exploited, often trapped in cycles of poverty that prevent social mobility and secure futures. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how statistical transparency can expose deep societal inequalities and inform public policy. By making these metrics accessible, governments and organizations can better monitor progress, allocate resources effectively, and advocate for stronger legal protections. Open data thus serves as a crucial tool for accountability, enabling stakeholders to identify gaps in child protection and drive evidence-based interventions to safeguard minors' rights and well-being.
