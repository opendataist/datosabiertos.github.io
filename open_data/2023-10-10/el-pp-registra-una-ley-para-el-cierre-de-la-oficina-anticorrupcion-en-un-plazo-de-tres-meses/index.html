El PP registra una ley para el cierre de la Oficina Anticorrupción en un plazo de tres meses

The legislative proposal to dissolve the Anti-Corruption Office, supported by Vox and opposed by the PP, represents a significant shift in governance transparency. The opposition views this move as a political vendetta designed to shield party members from scrutiny, arguing that eliminating independent oversight allows corrupt practices to go unchecked. By removing the office, the government risks dismantling essential checks and balances that previously ensured accountability for public officials. Consequently, the responsibilities for monitoring conflicts of interest and asset declarations are transferred to Parliament and the Court of Accounts. This structural change raises serious concerns about the future of preventive measures against corruption. Critics argue that centralizing these functions within legislative bodies may dilute effectiveness, as internal controls are often less rigorous than those provided by specialized, independent agencies tasked with investigating irregularities. This development is highly relevant to open data because it directly impacts the public’s right to access information regarding political integrity. Reducing the scope of transparent data collection and independent auditing diminishes civic oversight and trust. As the rules governing the disclosure of official assets become less visible, the transparency mechanisms crucial for verifying the integrity of public administration are significantly weakened, limiting the availability of open information for citizens and researchers.
