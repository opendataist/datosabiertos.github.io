Sierra Leone’s Freedom of Information Act needs activating

The enactment of Sierra Leone’s Freedom of Information Law represents a critical legal milestone for government transparency, yet its practical value remains nullified by administrative inaction. The primary concern is that without immediate establishment of enforcement mechanisms and a clear implementation plan, the legislation fails to empower citizens or hold officials accountable. This gap transforms the law from a tool for open governance into a symbolic gesture, highlighting the urgent need for structural readiness alongside legislative approval. Effective open data relies not merely on the existence of access rights, but on the institutional capacity to enforce them and educate the public on their use. The article emphasizes that a collaborative framework between government and civil society is essential to define milestones, secure funding, and establish the necessary commission. Without such coordinated effort, citizens remain disenfranchised from their right to information, undermining the democratic potential of the law and stalling progress toward a truly transparent society. Furthermore, the article identifies a significant inefficiency in maintaining overlapping agencies with similar mandates, such as the Open Government Initiative and the proposed Freedom of Information Commission. Consolidating these roles would prevent resource waste and ensure a unified, powerful enforcement body. For the open data ecosystem to function, governments must eliminate redundant structures and focus on creating a single, authoritative entity capable of guaranteeing access to information and driving genuine accountability.
