Statistics from the Sligo-Leitrim region reveal a significant rise in violent crimes and property damage, including assaults, robberies, and arson, alongside a surge in drink-driving offenses. Conversely, non-violent thefts, fraud, and drug-related incidents have decreased, indicating a shift in criminal activity patterns within the division. These figures are now available following a suspension caused by irregularities in police data recording. A comprehensive review resolved issues where crimes were either unrecorded, misclassified, or incorrectly categorized as non-crime incidents. This restoration of trust in the administrative data quality ensures that future crime statistics reflect a more accurate operational reality. This development is vital for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates the importance of data governance and validation. Transparent, high-quality datasets are essential for public accountability and accurate sociological analysis. Without rigorous auditing of recording systems, open data platforms risk propagating errors, undermining their utility for researchers and citizens who rely on truthful public information to understand societal trends.
