City of Casey quadruples open data engagement

The City of Casey’s Open Data Exchange has experienced a dramatic surge in community engagement by shifting its focus from raw data publication to user-centric visualization. By prioritizing plain-language insights, dashboards, and mapping tools, the council transformed complex information into accessible formats. This approach proved that making data understandable is more effective for citizen engagement than simply increasing the volume of available datasets, leading to a fivefold increase in platform visits. This strategic pivot has delivered significant practical benefits for both residents and council operations. Tools like the Councillor Transparency page and community service directories empower citizens to independently access essential information, addressing hidden needs such as food security and kindergarten availability. Concurrently, the self-service model has drastically reduced manual inquiries, allowing staff to redirect their efforts toward more complex issues while ensuring that critical data, such as building permits, remains transparent and easily digestible for the general public. This case is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it challenges the traditional notion that data release alone equals transparency. It demonstrates that true openness requires intentional design and storytelling to bridge the gap between technical availability and public utility. For organizations worldwide, Casey’s success offers a blueprint for enhancing civic trust and operational efficiency by treating data as a narrative tool rather than just a technical asset, ensuring it serves everyday community needs effectively.
