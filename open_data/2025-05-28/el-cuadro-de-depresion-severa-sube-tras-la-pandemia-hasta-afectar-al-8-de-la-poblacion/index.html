El cuadro de depresión severa sube tras la pandemia hasta afectar al 8 % de la población

The recent Spanish Health Survey reveals a concerning rise in mental health issues, particularly severe depression, which has increased significantly across all age groups since 2020. The data highlights a disproportionate impact on women, older adults, and those unable to work, suggesting that socioeconomic factors and gender disparities play critical roles in psychological well-being. This pattern indicates a widening gap in public health that requires targeted social interventions rather than just medical treatment. Physically, the population remains in relatively good health, yet chronic conditions and obesity rates continue to be prevalent challenges. While tobacco consumption has declined, alcohol intake and heavy drinking have risen, pointing to a shift in risk behaviors among the adult population. These trends underscore the complexity of public health management, where mental health crises intersect with lifestyle-related physical ailments, demanding integrated policy responses that address both mental and physical aspects of wellness simultaneously. This article is highly relevant to open data because it exemplifies how large-scale, standardized government datasets can uncover nuanced societal trends when disaggregated by demographics. By making this granular information accessible, open data enables researchers and policymakers to move beyond aggregate statistics, identifying specific vulnerable groups and geographical hotspots. Such transparency is essential for designing evidence-based interventions and holding institutions accountable for the holistic health outcomes of the population.
