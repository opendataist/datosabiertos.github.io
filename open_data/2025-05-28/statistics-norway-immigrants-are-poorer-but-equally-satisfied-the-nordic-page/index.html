Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Norway’s latest data reveals a paradox where immigrants face significantly poorer living conditions, including lower incomes, higher unemployment, and worse health outcomes, yet report satisfaction levels equal to the native population. This discrepancy highlights the complexity of well-being, suggesting that subjective happiness may not correlate directly with objective socioeconomic status or material living standards in this context. Despite these hardships, immigrants demonstrate notably higher confidence in Norwegian institutions, such as the police and judiciary, and a strong attachment to the country. This contrasts with their lower interpersonal trust and elevated rates of mental health issues and loneliness. The findings underscore a resilient civic engagement among immigrant groups, who rely on state structures for stability even while experiencing personal distress and social isolation. This report is vital for open data initiatives as it provides granular, disaggregated statistics necessary to understand marginalized communities accurately. By making these detailed insights accessible, researchers and policymakers can move beyond aggregate averages to address specific gaps in housing, employment, and mental health services. Such transparency fosters evidence-based social inclusion strategies and ensures that the unique experiences of immigrant populations are visible and addressed in public discourse.
