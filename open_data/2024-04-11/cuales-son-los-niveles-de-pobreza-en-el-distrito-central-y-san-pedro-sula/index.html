¿Cuáles son los niveles de pobreza en el Distrito Central y San Pedro Sula?

A substantial government investment failed to significantly reduce poverty in Honduras, with only a one percent decrease over eleven years despite massive financial allocation. This outcome highlights a critical disconnect between fiscal policy and social results, revealing that two-thirds of the population remain in poverty. The data underscores that monetary inputs alone do not guarantee effective poverty reduction when strategies lack impact or proper implementation, signaling a systemic failure in addressing national welfare needs. The analysis distinguishes between relative and absolute poverty, showing that major urban centers like Tegucigalpa and San Pedro Sula concentrate these vulnerabilities due to differing economic structures. The capital suffers from limited job creation linked to a lack of industrial development, whereas San Pedro Sula has a higher concentration of businesses. These disparities illustrate how urban planning and economic focus directly influence living standards, emphasizing that regional development imbalances exacerbate inequality. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistics can expose policy inefficiencies and hold institutions accountable. By leveraging official data from the National Institute of Statistics, civil society can verify government claims and advocate for evidence-based reforms. Ultimately, it proves that open data is essential for diagnosing socio-economic problems and driving the structural changes necessary to improve quality of life.
