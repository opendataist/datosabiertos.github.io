Home sales resume their decline, plunging 13.4% in September

Home sales resume their decline, plunging 13.4% in September

The Spanish real estate market is showing a clear slowdown, with a significant decline in the volume of property transactions, breaking the dynamic trend of previous years. This moderation reflects a loss of momentum driven by the rising cost of credit and high prices, which reduce the purchasing power of lower-income households. This is not a collapse, but rather a necessary technical adjustment to restore market accessibility, consolidating a correction phase following an exceptional period. Recent trends indicate that demand still exists, but it has become more selective and dependent on financial solvency. Experts warn that, unless restrictive monetary policies are implemented that could worsen the situation, the market will not experience a widespread drop in prices. Instead, a stabilization with lower transaction volumes is expected, benefiting buyers with greater savings and higher incomes, while potential demand contracts due to the imbalance between salaries and real estate prices. This analysis is relevant to the open data community because it demonstrates the importance of integrating and cross-referencing multiple statistical sources, such as property registries and mortgage data, to understand the structural dynamics of the market. Beyond individual figures, the transparency of these data allows for the identification of macroeconomic trends, the evaluation of the impact of monetary policies on housing access, and the provision of citizens and policymakers with a precise and well-founded view of the health of the real estate sector.
