62 RTI Applications In Two Months? Karnataka High Court Flags Misuse Of Law To Harass Officials, Quashes Penalty On Information Officer

62 RTI Applications In Two Months? Karnataka High Court Flags Misuse Of Law To Harass Officials, Quashes Penalty On Information Officer

The Karnataka High Court highlighted a growing trend of misusing the Right to Information Act to harass Public Information Officers for extraneous motives, rather than for genuine access to public records. By setting aside a penalty imposed on a Public Information Officer, the Court emphasized that the legislation is intended solely for obtaining information for valid and bona fide purposes. This ruling serves as a critical check against frivolous litigation that burdens public servants and distracts from the Act’s original democratic objective. The judgment specifically addressed an applicant who filed an excessive number of applications in a short period, demonstrating a clear lack of legitimate intent. The Court noted that the Information Commission had failed to adequately consider the officer’s explanations and the context of the applications. By recognizing this pattern of abuse, the judiciary affirmed that procedural delays caused by repetitive or malicious filings should not result in punitive actions against officials who are merely processing an unreasonable volume of requests. This decision is highly relevant to open data advocates because it clarifies the boundaries of the RTI framework. It reinforces that while transparency is paramount, the system must protect information officers from being weaponized against by bad-faith actors. Understanding these legal precedents helps stakeholders distinguish between legitimate requests for open data and misuse, ensuring the integrity of information governance remains intact while preventing the erosion of trust in public institutions.
