U.S. corn ending stocks up 35% from last year

U.S. corn ending stocks up 35% from last year

This USDA report highlights a significant surge in old crop corn stocks, reflecting a substantial increase in available supply compared to the previous year. In contrast, soybean and wheat inventories experienced declines, indicating tighter markets for these grains. These diverging trends suggest varying levels of demand and production efficiency across major agricultural commodities. The data reveals that off-farm corn storage grew much faster than on-farm reserves, pointing to shifts in how grain is held and distributed within the supply chain. Meanwhile, wheat stocks decreased across both farm and commercial locations, coinciding with reduced production areas and lower yields. This contraction in wheat availability signals potential pressures on future pricing and distribution for end-users relying on these specific crops. For open data enthusiasts, this report exemplifies the critical role of high-quality, standardized agricultural statistics in global market analysis. By providing granular details on stock locations and crop types, such datasets enable researchers and policymakers to track resource flows and predict economic impacts. Accessible raw data allows for deeper investigative journalism and algorithmic modeling, fostering transparency in how food security and commodity values are shaped by production realities.
