The government has approved an increase in the minimum wage to €1,286, which will remain tax-free

The government has approved an increase in the minimum wage to €1,286, which will remain tax-free

The government has approved salary increases and new labor transparency measures, highlighting the end of pay secrecy in companies. This initiative requires organizations to detail their compensation-setting criteria, eliminating hidden barriers and ensuring that workers know the essential terms of their contracts from the outset, thereby strengthening pay equity between men and women. At the same time, the minimum wage has been increased, along with tax deductions designed to prevent income from being subject to personal income tax (IRPF), aiming to reduce inequality and in-work poverty. Although employer associations have questioned the electoral timing of these decisions, the administration insists on their technical nature and their usefulness in combating the gender gap in the labor market, extending contractual rights beyond short-term temporary contracts. This legislation is fundamental to the open data agenda because it institutionalizes the obligation to publish structured data on remuneration and equality criteria. By mandating proactive transparency in human resources management, it creates an ecosystem where public information is accessible, verifiable, and usable by citizens, facilitating social auditing and accountability regarding equity in employment.
