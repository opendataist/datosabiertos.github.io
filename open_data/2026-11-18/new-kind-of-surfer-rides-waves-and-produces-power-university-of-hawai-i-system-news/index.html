New kind of surfer rides waves—and produces power | University of Hawaiʻi System News

New kind of surfer rides waves—and produces power | University of Hawaiʻi System News

The deployment of the SURF-WEC represents a significant step toward closing critical knowledge gaps in marine energy commercialization. By moving from theoretical modeling to real-world ocean testing, this project addresses the chronic shortage of empirical data that has historically hindered the industry's growth. This transition from laboratory to open-water deployment demonstrates that small-scale, accessible devices can effectively generate the practical insights necessary to advance the field. A key contribution of this initiative is its commitment to open data and collaborative expertise. Recognizing that previous deployments often kept valuable information isolated, the researchers made their design, performance data, and operational lessons freely accessible. This approach not only shares technical details like the hydraulic power take-off system but also highlights how interdisciplinary partnerships between national laboratories and academic institutes can successfully overcome the complex engineering challenges of marine energy. This project is highly relevant to open data because it prioritizes transparency and community learning over proprietary control. By providing a live data feed and a publicly available final report, the team ensures that other innovators can build upon these findings without repeating past mistakes. Ultimately, the SURF-WEC serves as a model for how shared, open-source information can accelerate innovation and reduce barriers to entry for future renewable energy technologies.
