La España vacía recibe solo el 14% de la inversión de las empresas públicas estatales

The recent publication of raw data by the Ministry of Finance reveals a marked territorial inequality in the investment made by state-owned public companies. Regions affected by depopulation receive less than 14% of these funds, while Madrid, Catalonia, and Andalusia account for nearly half, highlighting a structural imbalance in the distribution of resources between wealthy and depopulated regions. This imbalance is crucial to the debate on fiscal balances, indicators that measure each region’s net contribution to public coffers. The disparity in public investment serves as a key piece of evidence in the arguments put forward by regions such as Catalonia, which allege a persistent fiscal deficit. The lack of a unified official methodology and the provisional nature of these data reflect the complex interplay between territorial policies and economic equity. For the open data movement, this case underscores the urgency of gaining access to detailed, geographically disaggregated fiscal information. Transparency regarding how state revenues and expenditures are distributed enables citizens and civil society to verify national redistribution dynamics, fostering an informed public debate grounded in concrete, accessible evidence.
