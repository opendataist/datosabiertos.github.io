FOIA bills move along in Senate, transparency bills to come in Michigan House

Michigan House Democrats have introduced a comprehensive package of legislation aimed at increasing government transparency and accountability. A primary focus is regulating "dark money" by requiring political entities tied to officials to register with the Secretary of State. This initiative seeks to address the opacity surrounding 501(c)4 non-profits, which currently do not disclose donors, thereby making it difficult to hold elected officials accountable for inappropriate financial activities. The proposals also introduce stricter ethics rules, including a one-year cooling-off period before lawmakers can become lobbyists and enhanced disclosure requirements for gifts and travel paid for by non-lobbyist groups. By closing these loopholes, the legislation ensures that the public can identify who is funding political influence, addressing long-standing concerns about hidden financial interests and fostering greater trust in the political process. This effort is highly relevant to open data because it expands the scope of required public disclosures beyond traditional campaign finance. By mandating the registration of previously opaque groups and clarifying gift disclosures, the legislation generates more accessible, structured data for citizens and journalists. This shift moves the state toward true transparency, allowing for better monitoring of political spending and strengthening the infrastructure of open government through increased data availability and accountability mechanisms.
