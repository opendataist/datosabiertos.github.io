Sunshine Week: Support local journalism

Sunshine Week highlights the critical role of freedom of information laws in sustaining democratic self-governance by ensuring public access to government actions. This annual celebration underscores how transparent information allows citizens to hold leaders accountable, make informed electoral decisions, and participate effectively in civic life. The initiative traces its roots to the idea that sunlight acts as a disinfectant against corruption, emphasizing that an informed public is essential for a healthy society. However, the ability to access and disseminate this information is increasingly threatened by a severe decline in local journalism. The shrinking number of newsroom professionals has created vast "news deserts" where communities lack basic oversight, leading to reduced coverage of local institutions like city councils and school boards. This scarcity means fewer journalists are available to investigate government waste or corporate abuse, leaving significant gaps in the public’s understanding of local affairs and undermining the checks and balances necessary for accountability. This article is vital to the open data movement because it illustrates that raw data alone is insufficient without the interpretive capacity provided by investigative journalists. As government data becomes more abundant, the human infrastructure required to analyze, contextualize, and report on that data is disappearing. Supporting local news organizations is therefore a direct way to preserve the open data ecosystem, ensuring that the public can actually utilize available information to drive remedial action rather than remaining in the dark.
