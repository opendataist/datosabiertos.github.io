Effective policing requires close community ties, social intelligence, and professionalization. Speakers emphasized that trust is built through proximity and that reliable results depend on understanding local contexts and ensuring fair treatment of officers. This human-centric approach fosters confidence and improves security outcomes by aligning police operations with community needs. International experiences highlight the necessity of adapting strategies to cultural and legal frameworks. Successful models, such as those in El Salvador, demonstrate that initial community engagement facilitates better criminal registration. This suggests that rigid universal recipes are less effective than context-aware approaches that integrate citizens into the safety process through shared intelligence and transparent record-keeping. The exchange of information across all investigative agencies is critical for creating accessible, comprehensive databases. Collaborative intelligence centers allow diverse departments to share data, enhancing analytical capabilities and operational efficiency. This open-data perspective ensures that security decisions are informed by real-time, integrated information rather than isolated silos. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores how accessible, shared intelligence databases and cross-agency collaboration are fundamental to modern, effective policing.
