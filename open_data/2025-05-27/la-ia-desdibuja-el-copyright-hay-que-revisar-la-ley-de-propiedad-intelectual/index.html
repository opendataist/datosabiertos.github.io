La IA desdibuja el 'copyright', ¿hay que revisar la ley de propiedad intelectual?

La IA desdibuja el 'copyright', ¿hay que revisar la ley de propiedad intelectual?

The article underscores the urgent need to reassess intellectual property laws in the era of generative artificial intelligence, which challenges traditional definitions of human authorship. While some tech leaders advocate abolishing copyright protections, legal experts argue for a nuanced approach. The core implication is that existing regulations, many dating back to the 1990s, are outdated and struggle to address algorithmic creation, necessitating a careful balance between protecting human creators and fostering technological innovation. Regarding authorship, the prevailing consensus maintains that human involvement remains essential for copyright eligibility. Experts suggest that while AI should not be granted legal personhood or independent rights, new frameworks could protect algorithmic outputs under industrial property regimes or establish specific licensing models. This ensures that human creators retain control while allowing for the legitimate use of AI tools, preventing the legal system from becoming a bottleneck for creative and technological progress. This debate is crucial for open data as it directly impacts the legal use of copyrighted works for training AI models and the transparency of data sources. The international landscape shows fragmented responses, from Canada’s emphasis on consent and compensation to the UK’s push for transparency requirements. For open data advocates, these developments underscore the necessity of clear, technology-neutral regulations that define permissible data usage, ensuring that the benefits of AI development do not come at the expense of creators’ rights or public access to information.
