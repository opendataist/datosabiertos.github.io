Cash row: SC rejects RTI plea for in-house panel report, CJI's letter to Prez, PM - OrissaPOST

Cash row: SC rejects RTI plea for in-house panel report, CJI's letter to Prez, PM - OrissaPOST

The Supreme Court Administration has rejected a Right to Information request regarding the inquiry report into Justice Yashwant Varma and his communications with political leaders. This decision underscores the critical tension between judicial transparency and the protection of official confidentiality, highlighting how sensitive internal mechanisms can shield proceedings from public scrutiny under current interpretations of parliamentary privilege. A three-member committee confirmed allegations of a suspicious cash discovery at the judge’s residence, prompting the Chief Justice to initiate impeachment procedures by informing the President and Prime Minister. The judge’s refusal to resign despite these findings illustrates the limitations of internal judicial accountability, as the burden of final action shifts entirely to the executive and legislative branches rather than being resolved within the judiciary itself. This case is highly relevant to open data discussions as it demonstrates the barriers preventing public access to critical information about judicial conduct and integrity. When key documents are classified as confidential, it limits the ability of researchers and citizens to analyze accountability frameworks, raising concerns about whether open data principles can effectively apply to high-level governance structures where secrecy is often invoked to maintain institutional stability.
