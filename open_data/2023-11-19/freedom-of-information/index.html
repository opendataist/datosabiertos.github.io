Freedom of Information

Web functionality relies on essential cookies for user preferences and service requests, which cannot be disabled without breaking site features. While these do not store personal identification, other technical storage may be used for anonymous statistics or, controversially, for tracking users across sites for targeted advertising profiles. This distinction is critical for open data advocates, as it highlights the tension between necessary service operations and intrusive marketing practices. Understanding these mechanisms helps users and developers discern when data collection serves legitimate, non-identifiable purposes versus when it violates privacy norms. Recognizing these differences supports the development of transparent, privacy-respecting digital ecosystems. It encourages the separation of functional necessity from commercial exploitation, ensuring that open initiatives prioritize user autonomy and data minimalism over surveillance capitalism.
