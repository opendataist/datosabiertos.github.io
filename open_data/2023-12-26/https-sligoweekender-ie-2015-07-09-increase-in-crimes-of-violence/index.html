Recent crime statistics for the Sligo-Leitrim region reveal a complex shift in criminal activity, characterized by notable increases in violent offenses like assaults, sex crimes, and robberies, alongside significant rises in property damage and drink driving. Conversely, non-violent crimes such as thefts, fraud, and drug offenses have decreased during the same period. This divergence highlights changing patterns in local criminal behavior, suggesting that while some areas of public safety are deteriorating, others may be seeing some improvement. The resumption of these official figures is particularly significant given the previous suspension due to data irregularities identified in Garda recording practices. The Central Statistics Office only restored publication after completing a comprehensive review of the data quality, addressing issues where reported crimes were unrecorded or misclassified. This restoration underscores a critical commitment to transparency and accuracy, ensuring that policymakers and the public can rely on valid administrative data for analysis rather than flawed or incomplete records. This development is directly relevant to the open data community as it emphasizes the necessity of rigorous data governance and validation before publication. It serves as a cautionary tale for open data initiatives, demonstrating that raw public sector data often requires cleaning and verification to be truly useful. By highlighting the consequences of poor data classification and the efforts made to correct them, this article reinforces the importance of trust, accountability, and methodological integrity in releasing government statistics to the public domain.
