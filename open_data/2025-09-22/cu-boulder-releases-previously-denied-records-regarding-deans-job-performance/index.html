CU Boulder releases previously denied records regarding dean’s job performance

CU Boulder releases previously denied records regarding dean’s job performance

Public agencies frequently misapply personnel file exemptions to withhold records regarding professional conduct and job performance, citing privacy rights that legally protect only personal details like home addresses or financial data. Legal precedents establish that performance evaluations, disciplinary actions, and feedback reports are distinct from private personal information. Consequently, these documents remain accessible to the public, and the repeated incorrect denial of such requests highlights a systemic misunderstanding of open records law among government institutions. This distinction is critical for open data because it ensures transparency in public administration. When employees are funded by public dollars, the public has a legitimate interest in knowing how well they perform their duties, particularly when issues like retaliation or misconduct are involved. Clarifying that job performance metrics are not exempt preserves the integrity of open data systems, preventing institutions from using broad privacy interpretations to obscure accountability and operational insights that stakeholders need to evaluate public service effectiveness. In the specific case involving the University of Colorado Law School, records were eventually released after legal intervention clarified that performance-related documents were not shielded by the personnel exemption. While the university maintained its initial legal stance, the release of feedback and disciplinary records demonstrated that withholding such data without proper justification undermines public trust. This outcome reinforces the necessity for accurate interpretation of open data laws to ensure that public interest in government performance is not unnecessarily obstructed by misapplied privacy exemptions.
