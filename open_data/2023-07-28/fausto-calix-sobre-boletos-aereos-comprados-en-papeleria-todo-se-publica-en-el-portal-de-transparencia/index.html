Fausto Cálix sobre boletos aéreos comprados en papelería: “Todo se publica en el portal de transparencia”

The Honduran Customs Administration publicly claims to fully disclose its information on its transparency portal. However, a journalistic investigation reveals a significant disconnect between these assertions and the actual quality of the data available to citizens. While the institution asserts total transparency, the reality is that the uploaded information is often incomplete, lacking essential supporting documents such as invoices and competitor bids for numerous transactions. Specifically, substantial expenditures on air travel and hotel services by a stationery supplier were found without adequate justification or full documentation in the public records. The investigation highlights that while some recent expenses are properly documented, previous high-value purchases remain lacking in their supporting files. This inconsistency suggests that merely listing transaction amounts is insufficient for genuine accountability, as the absence of detailed evidence prevents effective oversight of public spending. This case is highly relevant to open data principles because it demonstrates that publishing data does not equal transparency if the information is fragmented or missing critical context. True open data requires completeness and usability, allowing citizens to verify how public funds are managed. When institutions release partial records, they undermine the core purpose of open data: enabling informed public scrutiny and ensuring that government actions are visible, verifiable, and accountable to society.
