Set up units to facilitate access to information — Yaw Sarpong Boateng

Public institutions in Ghana are urged to establish dedicated information units to ensure citizens can effectively access data, thereby strengthening democratic accountability. By appointing trained officers to manage requests, these units facilitate the generation, preservation, and dissemination of accurate information, directly supporting the goals of the Right to Information Act. This structural shift aims to transform transparency from a theoretical principle into a functional reality for public engagement. The legislation imposes strict procedural obligations on officials, requiring timely responses to information requests and penalizing non-compliance as gross misconduct. This legal framework empowers individuals to scrutinize government actions, fostering a culture of openness. Consequently, public bodies must not only process inquiries efficiently but also actively publish updated information manuals, ensuring that transparency is maintained as a routine operational standard rather than an exceptional measure. This development is highly relevant to open_data because it establishes a legal mandate for proactive information disclosure. By requiring institutions to compile and publish data regularly, the law creates a foundational ecosystem where open data initiatives can thrive. It underscores the necessity of institutional mechanisms to support data availability, highlighting that sustainable open governance relies on both legislative enforcement and the practical infrastructure of information management within public sectors.
