Sánchez Henríquez propone regulación contra la violación de datos personales

Dominicans face significant anxiety due to the vulnerability of their personal data protection and a weak regulatory framework. Victims currently endure lengthy legal processes with limited recourse, highlighting the urgent need for effective, rapid sanctions and proper compensation mechanisms. The article is highly relevant to open data as it underscores the critical gap between data accessibility and individual privacy rights in the digital age. Without robust safeguards, open data initiatives risk exploiting citizens rather than serving them, emphasizing the necessity for comprehensive legal structures that balance transparency with security. To address this, a dedicated Data Protection Agency and updated laws are proposed. These measures aim to align domestic regulations with international standards, specifically covering emerging technologies like artificial intelligence. This expansion ensures that all data interactions, beyond just banking, are securely governed, fostering trust in digital ecosystems.
