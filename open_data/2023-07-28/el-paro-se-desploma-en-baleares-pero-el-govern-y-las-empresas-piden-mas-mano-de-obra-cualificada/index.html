El paro se desploma en Baleares pero el Govern y las empresas piden más mano de obra cualificada

The Balearic Islands have reached a historic record of occupancy in the second quarter of 2023, with the fastest growth in Spain. This scenario of de facto full employment, however, has exposed serious tensions in the labor market. Companies, especially small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), face critical difficulties in filling their staff positions due to a lack of available labor. This shortage directly affects productivity, forcing adjustments in shifts and temporary closures, revealing a disconnect between aggregate employment statistics and the actual operations of businesses. The crisis is exacerbated by structural factors such as the exorbitant cost of housing and insufficient public transportation to accommodate the working hours required by the tourism industry. Unions warn that these figures do not mask the precariousness of jobs, pointing to excessive workloads, illegal overtime, and deteriorating worker health. The discussion goes beyond simple numbers, focusing the debate on the quality of employment, the distribution of benefits, and the need to guarantee decent working conditions in the face of the tourism boom. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates that statistical transparency, although necessary, is insufficient on its own. For open data to have true social value, it must be complemented by qualitative contexts that reveal the hidden vulnerabilities behind positive aggregates. The openness of data should facilitate the detection of these disparities between macrostatistics and the lived experiences of workers and businesses, driving public policies more closely aligned with the actual socioeconomic reality.
