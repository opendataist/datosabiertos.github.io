La mitad de los niños de Europa ya están expuestos a olas de calor frecuentes, según UNICEF

A UNICEF report reveals that half of the children in Europe and Central Asia are exposed to frequent heatwaves, a rate double the global average. This alarming statistic highlights that vulnerable youth are disproportionately affected by the intensifying climate crisis, with projections indicating universal exposure to extreme heat by 2050. The findings underscore an urgent need for governments to implement comprehensive mitigation plans, adapt health and education services, and prioritize investments in preventive care for heat-related illnesses among young populations. The relevance of this article to open data lies in the critical importance of transparent, accessible climate and demographic datasets for effective public policy. Accurate, freely available data allows organizations to identify at-risk regions, monitor trends, and hold governments accountable for their adaptation commitments. By making such granular health and environmental information open, stakeholders can better coordinate interventions, allocate resources efficiently, and drive the systemic changes required to protect children’s welfare against escalating climate threats.
