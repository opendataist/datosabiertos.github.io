“Cantabria crea empleo por debajo de la media nacional y no reduce el número de parados” - El Faradio | Periodismo que cuenta

The article reveals a paradoxical labor market dynamic in Cantabria: while total employment has risen significantly, the region fails to meet national averages for job creation and sees a slight increase in unemployment, contrary to the country's overall downward trend. This divergence highlights a structural lag in Cantabria’s economy, where the generation of jobs has not kept pace with the growth of the active population, resulting in a stagnant reduction of the unemployment rate compared to the rest of Spain. A crucial implication is the shift toward higher-quality employment, driven largely by the recent labor reform. There is a marked increase in permanent, full-time contracts, particularly benefiting women who have driven most of the employment growth. However, gender disparities persist, with women still disproportionately represented in part-time roles and facing lower rates of permanent employment than men. Furthermore, the gains are concentrated in services and construction, while traditional sectors like industry are contracting, raising concerns about economic sustainability. This data is relevant to open_data as it demonstrates how disaggregated statistics are essential for uncovering hidden inequalities and structural weaknesses. By analyzing detailed variables such as gender, contract type, and sector, researchers can move beyond aggregate indicators to understand the true quality and inclusivity of economic recovery. It underscores the importance of transparent, granular datasets in evaluating policy effectiveness and identifying specific areas requiring targeted intervention to ensure equitable labor market outcomes.
