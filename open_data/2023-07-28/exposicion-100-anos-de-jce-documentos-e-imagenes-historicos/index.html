Exposición 100 años de JCE: documentos e imágenes históricos

The opening of the “100 Years JCE” exhibition highlights the Dominican Central Electoral Board’s century-long evolution, emphasizing its historical resilience and its pivotal role in consolidating democratic institutions in Latin America. The event serves not merely as a retrospective of electoral procedures but as a crucial educational tool designed to foster civic consciousness. By showcasing the transition from authoritarianism to democracy, the exhibition aims to contextualize the current political landscape for new voters, illustrating how historical struggles directly impact the exercise of political power today. A central theme of the narrative is the profound impact of gender inclusion on democratic legitimacy. The exhibition pays specific homage to women suffragists and their fight for citizenship rights, marking the milestone when Dominican women first exercised the right to vote. This focus underscores the broader societal shift from exclusion to active participation, demonstrating that authentic democracy requires the full engagement of all citizens. It frames the electoral process as an inclusive struggle for rights rather than just a technical administrative function. This content is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it illustrates the importance of making historical institutional records accessible and interpretable. By transforming archival documents, such as rare images and civil registration timelines, into public educational materials, the JCE exemplifies how transparent data can drive civic education and accountability. It reinforces the principle that open government archives are not just static repositories but active resources that empower citizens to understand their rights and the history of their democratic participation.
