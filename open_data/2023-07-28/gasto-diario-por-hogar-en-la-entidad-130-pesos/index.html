The article reveals significant economic disparities in Chiapas, where households allocate nearly half of their spending to basic food needs, highlighting deep vulnerability. In contrast, wealthier regions like Baja California Sur and Mexico City enjoy substantially higher income levels, illustrating a stark national inequality in living standards. This data exposes the precarious financial reality faced by many families who prioritize sustenance over other essential services. Understanding this distribution of household expenditure is critical for open data initiatives focused on social justice. By making such granular economic information transparent, researchers and policymakers can identify regional gaps in resources and quality of life. The visibility of these spending patterns allows for more accurate assessments of poverty and inequality, moving beyond abstract statistics to concrete human impacts. Consequently, this dataset supports the development of targeted public policies aimed at reducing regional disparities. Open access to this information empowers civil society to hold institutions accountable and advocate for equitable resource distribution. Ultimately, transparency in economic data fosters informed dialogue and drives efforts toward a more balanced and just society for all citizens.
