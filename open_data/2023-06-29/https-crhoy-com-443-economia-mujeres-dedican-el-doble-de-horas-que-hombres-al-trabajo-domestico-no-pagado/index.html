Research confirms that women continue to bear the majority of the burden of unpaid domestic work and caregiving, dedicating nearly twice as many hours per week as men. Although the gender gap has decreased slightly over the past five years, due to a reduction in women’s hours and a modest increase in men’s hours, structural inequality persists. This disparity is not static; rather, it reflects a deeply asymmetric distribution of time that continues to place women at a temporal and labor disadvantage. The analysis details that inequality is more pronounced in tasks traditionally associated with women, such as cleaning and food preparation, where differences in actual hours are stark. However, there are also nuances in specific activities, such as household shopping, where men’s participation is higher, although the time invested by both sexes is balanced. This shows that co-responsibility is not uniform; while some areas show small advances in male participation, others maintain considerable rigidity in assigned roles, further exacerbated in rural areas. This study is fundamental to the field of open data because it transforms the visibility of social inequalities into quantifiable and public information. By generating accessible statistics on caregiving time and unpaid work, it enables researchers, public officials, and citizens to monitor progress toward equity. These data are crucial for designing evidence-based public policies that promote social co-responsibility, allowing for the evaluation of intervention effectiveness and the promotion of a fairer society through informational transparency.
