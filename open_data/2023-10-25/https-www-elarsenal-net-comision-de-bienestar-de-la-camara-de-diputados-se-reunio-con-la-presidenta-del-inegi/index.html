The meeting between the Welfare Commission and the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) highlights the critical importance of statistical indicators for grounding budgetary discussions and evaluating social policies in Mexico. The legislature seeks to gain a deep understanding of INEGI’s methodologies, particularly those of the National Household Income and Expenditure Survey (ENIGH), in order to make informed decisions about the federal budget and measure the real impact of government actions on reducing poverty and improving household well-being. Significant structural challenges have been identified, such as the gender wage gap, the lack of resources for caregivers, and regional disparities in income. The president of INEGI emphasized that the information generated is vital for designing effective laws and improving decision-making, underscoring that public trust in the institute and methodological rigor are essential for data to accurately reflect the country’s socioeconomic reality, including occupational and infrastructure characteristics. This event is relevant to the open data movement because it illustrates the direct link between official statistical transparency and democratic accountability. When governments produce and share robust, reliable data, they not only justify public spending but also empower an informed civil society capable of demanding more equitable policies. The availability and understanding of these metrics enable citizens and legislators to monitor whether allocated resources are actually closing social gaps, thereby consolidating data science as a fundamental tool of governance.
