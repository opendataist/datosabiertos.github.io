La SGAE alerta de que la IA podría reducir hasta un 28% los ingresos por derechos de autor de la música en España

La SGAE alerta de que la IA podría reducir hasta un 28% los ingresos por derechos de autor de la música en España

The report highlights that artificial intelligence represents a structural threat to the Spanish music ecosystem, with projections of a significant short-term decline in royalty revenues. This trend not only jeopardizes the economic sustainability of composers and producers but also fundamentally transforms the relationships between creators and production processes, generating profound uncertainty regarding authorship and intellectual property in a sector traditionally reliant on legal protection for its works. The widespread adoption of these tools, already used by a considerable portion of industry professionals, reflects both the dependence of AI on existing copyrighted works and the competitive pressure to reduce costs. This phenomenon raises critical ethical and legal debates, as the success of algorithms is based on the decomposition of prior creative works, often without obtaining the corresponding authorization. Massive investments in technological development are forcing the circumvention of traditional copyright rules, exacerbating fears of human displacement and irresponsible use of technology. This analysis is crucial for the field of open data because it illustrates the tension between the scalability of algorithms and the need for transparency in intellectual property management. The lack of clear regulatory frameworks defining what constitutes AI-assisted versus AI-autonomous music complicates the creation of reliable and accessible data registries. To ensure the survival of human creativity, it is imperative to establish registration and licensing systems that protect the integrity of cultural data, ensuring that technological innovation does not erode the fundamental attribution and compensation mechanisms that sustain creative biodiversity.
