Mapping danger: Experts build a free open-source model to assess landslide risks

Mapping danger: Experts build a free open-source model to assess landslide risks

Recent devastating landslides in India highlight the urgent need for accessible, granular data to mitigate climate risks. While national statistics reveal thousands of incidents over two decades, static publications like the Landslide Atlas often lack the detailed, actionable datasets required for effective planning. This gap underscores how the absence of timely, open data hinders the development of precise mitigation strategies, leaving vulnerable communities exposed to recurrent disasters in ecologically fragile regions like the Western Ghats and Himalayas. In response, the Climate Software Lab developed an open-source landslide risk assessment model that leverages publicly available remote sensing data to provide high-resolution susceptibility maps. This tool moves beyond coarse global models by incorporating local factors such as terrain slope and forest loss, demonstrating the practical value of open data in building localized solutions. By making this software freely available, the initiative encourages policymakers and developers to utilize open datasets for better risk visualization, proving that private entities can create innovative tools when high-quality public data is accessible. Although the current model estimates static susceptibility rather than predicting imminent events, it serves as a critical foundation for future predictive systems. The developers emphasize that integrating real-time rainfall data is essential for moving from risk assessment to actual prediction. This case reinforces the necessity for governments to routinely release raw geological and climate data, as such transparency is the only way to foster continuous model improvement and empower diverse stakeholders to address environmental hazards effectively.
