Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Despite facing significant socioeconomic disadvantages, including lower incomes, poorer housing, and higher rates of mental health issues, immigrants in Norway report life satisfaction levels comparable to the native population. This counterintuitive finding highlights a resilience that challenges assumptions linking material conditions directly to subjective well-being, suggesting that other factors buffer the impact of structural inequality. Furthermore, this group exhibits notably high trust in Norwegian institutions, such as the political system, judiciary, and police, surpassing the trust levels found among the general population. While interpersonal trust remains lower, this strong institutional confidence indicates a robust social contract and a deep sense of belonging, which are critical for social cohesion in diverse societies. This article is vital for open data initiatives because it demonstrates the necessity of disaggregating statistics by demographic background to reveal nuanced societal dynamics. It underscores that aggregate data can obscure significant disparities in living conditions, while also exposing complex emotional realities that policymakers must address. Comprehensive, accessible data allows for a more accurate understanding of integration successes and ongoing challenges.
