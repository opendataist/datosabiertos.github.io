Los concursos de acreedores se quintuplican hasta los 24

Judicial statistics reveal a significant shift in how economic distress is manifested, highlighting that official data is a vital tool for understanding regional and national economic realities. The core finding is not a general collapse of businesses, but a sharp surge in individual insolvency procedures, indicating that ordinary citizens are increasingly utilizing legal mechanisms to manage personal debt. This trend suggests that the underlying liquidity problems may stem from deeper, longer-standing issues rather than immediate external shocks. This phenomenon is primarily driven by a new legal awareness rather than a sudden spike in corporate failures. The rise in filings reflects individuals learning to use these procedures to discharge unpaid liabilities, a strategy that was previously rare but is now becoming widespread. While total case numbers are at their highest since the last major crisis, professional and corporate insolvencies remain stable or even decreasing, underscoring that the current crisis is predominantly a personal, rather than industrial, challenge. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent access to judicial records provides real-time, granular insights into socio-economic health that traditional indicators might miss. By tracking these specific legal filings, analysts can identify emerging social vulnerabilities and the evolving impact of legislative changes, such as recent reforms regarding free legal aid. This data enables policymakers and researchers to move beyond aggregate figures and understand the human dimension of economic hardship.
