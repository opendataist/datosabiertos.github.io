Stress-related absences at Pembrokeshire County Council sees three-year increase

Pembrokeshire County Council’s data reveals that while stress-related absences have increased, the total full-time equivalent days lost have decreased significantly. This trend indicates that employees are recovering and returning to work more quickly, despite the rising frequency of short-term mental health-related leave. The council attributes this efficiency to a proactive mental health strategy that reduces stigma and encourages early intervention. By fostering an environment where staff seek preventative support, such as talking therapy, the authority has successfully lowered the average duration of absence. This cultural shift is supported by manager training and a network of mental health champions, resulting in lower overall absence rates compared to other Welsh local authorities. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates as it demonstrates the value of transparent, FOI-released public sector statistics. It shows how accessible data can be analyzed to evaluate policy effectiveness, revealing nuanced outcomes that raw absence counts might obscure. Such transparency allows for better public scrutiny and highlights successful organizational practices in managing employee wellbeing.
