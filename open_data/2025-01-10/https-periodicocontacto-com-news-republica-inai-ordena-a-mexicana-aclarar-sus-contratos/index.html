The INAI ruled that contracts between the state-owned airline of Mexico State and Petrus Aero Holdings must be disclosed, rejecting claims of confidentiality. This decision underscores that public entities must uphold their transparency obligations, even in the context of international legal disputes. The ruling reinforces the principle that contractual information remains public data, regardless of ongoing litigation, thereby ensuring accountability in government operations. By prioritizing open access, authorities demonstrate that transparency is a fundamental duty, not contingent upon legal sensitivities. This case highlights the role of open data in preventing opacity within state-controlled organizations. It establishes a precedent that public interest takes precedence over commercial secrecy, encouraging broader compliance with information freedom laws in corporate governance.
