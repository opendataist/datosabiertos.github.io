La compraventa de viviendas crece un 9% y el precio sube casi un 6% en noviembre

The Spanish real estate market is showing a strong recovery, fueled by lower interest rates that have significantly boosted transaction volumes across most regions. This surge in demand, combined with a persistent shortage of available housing inventory, has put upward pressure on prices, revealing market dynamics in which affordability is increasingly strained by limited supply. A key structural shift concerns the financing of these purchases, as most buyers are now paying in cash rather than relying on mortgages. This trend indicates a change in the availability or preference for traditional credit, with nearly half of all transactions occurring without debt. As a result, the health of the real estate sector is becoming less dependent on the growth of mortgage lending and more reliant on the purchasing power of individuals who can make upfront payments. This data is crucial for open-data initiatives, as it underscores the need for transparent and accessible housing statistics to monitor market health and the impact of policies. By analyzing such detailed regional and transactional data, researchers and policymakers can better understand the interplay between financing mechanisms and pricing trends, thereby fostering more informed decisions on housing affordability and urban planning strategies.
