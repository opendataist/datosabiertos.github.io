Michigan appeals court says Taylor must comply with ACLU FOIA request

Michigan appeals court says Taylor must comply with ACLU FOIA request

The Michigan Court of Appeals ruled that the City of Taylor must release police misconduct records, rejecting claims that the request was too broad. This decision reinforces the principle that government transparency mechanisms must remain accessible even when the specifics of requested documents are initially unknown to the requester. By ordering the city to comply with the Freedom of Information Act request, the court affirmed that public entities cannot easily evade disclosure by arguing vagueness. This ruling ensures that accountability for allegations of discrimination and excessive force remains enforceable, preventing administrative hurdles from blocking public access to critical information. This case is vital to open data advocacy as it sets a precedent for how broadly defined transparency laws apply to sensitive government records. It demonstrates that legal interpretations favoring open access can successfully challenge local governments’ attempts to withhold potentially controversial data, strengthening the framework for public oversight of law enforcement activities.
