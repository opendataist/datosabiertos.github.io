IA amenaza derechos de autor de artistas

More than 11,000 creators, including prominent figures in music and literature, have issued a joint warning against the unlicensed use of copyrighted works to train generative artificial intelligence models. They consider this practice an unjust threat to their livelihoods and demand a ban on the extraction of creative data without compensation, arguing that the technology industry invests heavily in infrastructure while avoiding payment for the human-generated content essential to their algorithms. This protest highlights a critical power asymmetry, as individual artists lack the regulatory and financial influence wielded by large tech corporations backed by major conglomerates. There is concern that intellectual property policies may be distorted in favor of AI companies due to their promises of investment, leaving creators vulnerable to the mass copying of their works without attribution or adequate compensation. This could saturate the market with machine-generated content and devalue human effort. This conflict is fundamental to the open data movement, as it challenges the notion that creative information should be treated as a free input for technological development. The letter underscores the urgent need to redefine ethical and legal standards for data handling, promoting transparency and informed consent. To advance toward a truly open and sustainable data ecosystem, it is essential to balance innovation with copyright protections, ensuring that creators are recognized and compensated, thereby establishing crucial precedents for the responsible governance of datasets used in AI.
