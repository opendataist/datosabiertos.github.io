Nuevo sistema de salud digital de Trump genera preocupaciones éticas y legales en EE.UU.

Nuevo sistema de salud digital de Trump genera preocupaciones éticas y legales en EE.UU.

The Trump administration launched an initiative to integrate medical records into a digital ecosystem managed by private technology companies, promising to facilitate access to health information and improve wellness monitoring. This system aims to eliminate current barriers through tools such as mobile applications and artificial intelligence, enabling patients to quickly access their medical histories and allowing providers to gain a more comprehensive view of their patients’ health status. Proponents argue that this centralization empowers users and improves clinical outcomes, particularly in the management of chronic diseases such as diabetes and obesity. By connecting isolated data from various sources, including hospital records and lifestyle information collected by apps, the initiative seeks to optimize diagnosis and treatment. The involvement of major technology corporations and hospital systems suggests a collaborative approach to modernizing healthcare. However, this proposal raises profound ethical and legal concerns regarding the privacy of personal data. Critics warn that inadequate regulation turns this initiative into an opportunity for the improper monetization of sensitive information, thereby jeopardizing public trust. This news is relevant to open data because it illustrates the fundamental tension between the potential of open data to improve public health and the inherent risks of exposing sensitive personal information to unregulated commercial actors.
