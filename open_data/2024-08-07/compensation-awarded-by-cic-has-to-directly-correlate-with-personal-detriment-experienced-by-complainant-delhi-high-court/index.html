Compensation Awarded By CIC Has To Directly Correlate With Personal Detriment Experienced By Complainant: Delhi High Court

The Delhi High Court clarified that compensation under the Right to Information Act must strictly correlate with the personal detriment suffered by the specific complainant, rather than losses incurred by third parties. This ruling establishes a necessary causal link between the public authority’s non-compliance and the direct financial or other harms experienced by the information seeker. Consequently, the Central Information Commission’s authority to award damages is confined to redressing these individual grievances, ensuring that punitive measures remain within the statutory scope of the law. This judgment is critical for open data governance as it sets a legal precedent for accountability in information disclosure. By emphasizing that compensatory powers must be applied judiciously, the court reinforces the principle that transparency mechanisms are designed to protect individual rights against obstruction. This interpretation prevents the misuse of compensation provisions for unrelated disputes, thereby strengthening the integrity of the RTI framework as a tool for enforcing public accountability. The relevance to open data lies in the enforcement of timely and complete information access. When public authorities withhold crucial details, they hinder citizens’ ability to pursue legal remedies or make informed decisions. By validating compensation based on tangible delays and associated costs, the ruling incentivizes proactive transparency. It signals that obstructing data release has concrete consequences, encouraging institutions to adopt more efficient and open data practices to avoid legal liabilities.
