La nueva Ley de Infancia de Castilla y León potenciará los acogimientos familiares e incidirá en la transición a la vida independiente

The proposed legislation represents a paradigm shift in regional governance, moving from a passive welfare model to one that recognizes children as active subjects with both rights and responsibilities. This evolution balances legal protections with a framework of duties, emphasizing social co-responsibility and the prevention of violence, particularly in emerging digital environments. By prioritizing family-based foster care over institutionalization, the state aims to provide dignified, individualized development for vulnerable minors, ensuring equality of opportunity through targeted incentives and specialized support structures. A critical component of this reform is the systematic preparation for independent life, beginning at age fourteen. This phase focuses on fostering autonomy through socio-educational and labor support, ensuring that adolescents in care or with disabilities transition into adulthood with sufficient skills for self-management. Furthermore, the law streamlines adoption processes by prioritizing family guardianship and introducing open adoption concepts, thereby avoiding prolonged institutional stays and promoting stable, permanent family units for children awaiting final judicial authorization. This initiative is highly relevant to open data initiatives because the preliminary text is publicly accessible via the government’s open portal, inviting citizen scrutiny and feedback. Such transparency allows civil society and data analysts to examine the legal framework directly, facilitating public debate and ensuring that the resulting policies align with community needs. This process exemplifies the core tenets of open government by making legislative drafts available for public observation, fostering accountability and collaborative policy-making before the final enactment of the law.
