Marriage age increases as ratio of couples aged 25-39 rises

Marriage age increases as ratio of couples aged 25-39 rises

China’s marriage landscape is shifting toward older ages, with the proportion of couples in their thirties rising significantly while youth registration declines. This demographic transition reflects the pressures of urbanization, extended education, and employment struggles facing newer generations. Consequently, overall marriage rates have hit historic lows, prompting government interventions aimed at fostering a more marriage-friendly society. These trends are deeply relevant to open data because they highlight the urgent need for transparent, accessible demographic statistics. Open data initiatives enable researchers and policymakers to accurately track these shifting social patterns, ensuring that evidence-based solutions address real-time challenges rather than relying on outdated assumptions. By making such statistical bulletins openly available, society can better understand the structural drivers behind declining birth rates and delayed marriages. This transparency supports the development of targeted support policies in housing, education, and finance, ultimately helping to stabilize family structures and address national population pressures through informed, data-driven governance.
