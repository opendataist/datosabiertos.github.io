Right to Information Month launched to increase awareness

This article highlights Ghana’s renewed commitment to the Right to Information Act, emphasizing the transformative role of digital platforms in democratizing access to public data. By focusing on the online space as a catalyst for transparency, the RTI Commission aims to shift citizens from passive recipients to active participants in the democratic process. This strategic pivot underscores how technology can redefine the relationship between government agencies and the public, fostering deeper accountability through enhanced digital engagement. The celebration serves as a vital deliberative platform to share best practices among pioneers of transparency legislation. It addresses critical challenges inherent in the digital age, such as ensuring information accuracy, combating misinformation, and safeguarding privacy. By promoting digital literacy and open dialogue, the initiative seeks to balance the rapid flow of information with the need for authenticity, ensuring that the benefits of open data are realized without compromising security or trust. Relevance to open data lies in its demonstration of how legal frameworks must evolve to harness digital opportunities for civic empowerment. The article illustrates that successful open data implementation requires not just legislative backing but also active public education on navigating online information ecosystems. It advocates for a future where technology supports not only the availability of data but also its integrity and accessibility, setting a precedent for how nations can leverage digital infrastructure to strengthen governance and citizen rights through transparent, verifiable public information.
