McBroom introduces bill to protect FOIA access

McBroom introduces bill to protect FOIA access

Michigan Senator Ed McBroom has introduced legislation to strengthen Freedom of Information Act protections, aiming to prevent government officials from evading public scrutiny through obscure communication tactics. The proposal responds to a specific incident where an email from a lobbyist to the Governor’s office used Greek letters to hide sensitive content, potentially bypassing automated searches for key terms like "Flint." By closing this loophole, the bill seeks to ensure that all government communications remain transparent and accessible, regardless of formatting tricks or technical glitches that might otherwise obscure their meaning from automated public records systems. The core legislative measure prohibits agencies from avoiding disclosure by employing code words, symbols, or non-standard letters. It also increases penalties for failures to comply with transparency laws. This approach addresses concerns that such methods could be used intentionally to suppress information or inadvertently due to software errors, both of which undermine public trust. By clarifying the intent of the law and establishing stricter consequences, the bill reinforces the principle that government accountability must not be compromised by technical workarounds or ambiguous language. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates, as it highlights the critical need for robust digital transparency frameworks. It underscores how technical vulnerabilities in communication systems can threaten the integrity of public records, affecting the reliability of open data initiatives. The legislation signals a growing recognition that protecting public access requires addressing not just intentional secrecy, but also the structural and technical weaknesses that allow information to be hidden inadvertently. Ensuring that data remains accessible despite formatting issues is essential for maintaining trust in government openness and facilitating informed civic engagement.
