Centenarios gallegos destapan sus secretos para vivir más de un siglo

The article highlights a group of Galician centenarians whose longevity is attributed not to medical miracles, but to consistent daily engagement, social connection, and a positive philosophical outlook. Key factors include maintaining physical activity through work or hobbies, staying mentally stimulated via reading and games, and valuing strong family bonds and humor to navigate life’s changes. From an open data perspective, this narrative underscores the importance of contextualizing demographic statistics. While institutions like the INE provide aggregate counts of centenarians, such data often lacks the qualitative depth needed to understand the human experiences behind the numbers. The story illustrates how demographic trends are shaped by individual behaviors and social contexts, rather than being mere statistical abstractions. This relevance extends to the open data movement’s goal of transparency and meaningful accessibility. By making detailed, anonymized personal narratives available alongside hard data, researchers and policymakers can better understand the drivers of healthspan and longevity. It advocates for datasets that integrate qualitative insights, fostering a more holistic view of public health and societal well-being beyond raw percentages.
