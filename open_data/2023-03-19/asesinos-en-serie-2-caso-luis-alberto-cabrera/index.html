Asesinos en serie (2). Caso Luis Alberto Cabrera

The article examines the case of a Dominican serial killer convicted in 2002, highlighting the insufficiency of available biographical data to understand the factors that shaped his criminal personality. This lack of detailed information prevents a comprehensive study of the underlying causes, underscoring the critical need to collect and preserve complete historical records for forensic and criminological analysis. The text emphasizes the connection between psychopathy, crime planning, and the retention of objects as trophies, suggesting a high likelihood of recidivism despite existing sentences. Given that the prognosis for rehabilitation in such disorders is discouraging, the article argues that the current criminal justice system is inadequate. Therefore, it proposes the implementation of life sentences without parole and permanent public registries of released murderers, models already in place in other jurisdictions to ensure community safety. This discussion is relevant to open data because it highlights the urgency of transparency in violent crime records. The creation of accessible, open databases on serial killer profiles and their histories would enable better public monitoring and criminological analysis. The availability of such structured data facilitates academic research and the development of more effective prevention policies, breaking the institutional silence that currently hinders a deep understanding of these criminal phenomena.
