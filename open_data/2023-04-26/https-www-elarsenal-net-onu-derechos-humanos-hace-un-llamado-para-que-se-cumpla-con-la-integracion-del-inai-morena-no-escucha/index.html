The article asserts that the prolonged failure to integrate Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) constitutes a significant breach of constitutional rights. By withholding the appointment of commissioners, the President and the ruling Senate majority are obstructing citizens' fundamental ability to access public information and protect their personal data. This political inaction effectively paralyzes a key democratic mechanism, preventing the proper exercise of these liberties despite their explicit constitutional guarantee. This delay undermines the core principles of open data and government transparency, which are essential for democratic accountability and fighting corruption. The text criticizes the administration’s narrative that frames such autonomous institutions as neoliberal or conservative obstacles, arguing instead that they are vital safeguards against power abuse. When these bodies lack full membership, the state fails to uphold its obligation to ensure transparency, thereby weakening the checks and balances necessary for a healthy republic. The article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights how institutional paralysis directly impacts data rights. Without a fully functional INAI, mechanisms for enforcing the right to information and protecting personal data remain ineffective. This situation demonstrates that open data initiatives depend not just on technical infrastructure, but on the political will to maintain independent oversight bodies that guarantee citizen access to government information.
