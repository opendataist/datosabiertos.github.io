Dos de cada tres españoles asegura que es feliz siempre o casi siempre: ¿qué ocurre con el resto?

Dos de cada tres españoles asegura que es feliz siempre o casi siempre: ¿qué ocurre con el resto?

Public sector surveys reveal significant variations in citizen well-being, highlighting that happiness is deeply influenced by socioeconomic status and demographic factors. By analyzing health and quality of life data, researchers identify specific trends across gender, age, and income levels, showing that financial stability and employment are strong predictors of self-reported satisfaction. The data indicates that younger men with higher incomes report the highest happiness levels, while women face distinct societal pressures that reduce their sense of well-being, particularly during their childbearing years. Conversely, older adults report higher satisfaction with leisure time despite experiencing loneliness, suggesting that the subjective perception of free time differs significantly across life stages. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how government statistics can uncover complex social dynamics and inform public policy. Transparent access to such datasets enables sociologists and policymakers to address inequality, support vulnerable groups, and design interventions that improve overall quality of life, proving that data-driven insights are essential for societal progress.
