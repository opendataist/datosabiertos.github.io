India será el país más poblado del mundo por delante de China a finales de abril

The United Nations confirms that India is surpassing China as the world’s most populous country, marking a historic demographic shift. China’s population has begun to decline after recently peaking, driven by an aging demographic structure and the lasting effects of restrictive birth policies, while India’s population continues to grow steadily, as it has for several decades. This transition highlights diverging future trajectories: China faces a shrinking workforce, whereas India contends with a young, expanding population that requires significant employment opportunities. The imprecision in predicting the exact moment of this crossover underscores the limitations of current demographic modeling. Since India has not conducted an official census since 2011, data relies heavily on estimates and projections rather than precise counts. The UN acknowledges the uncertainty surrounding these timelines, reflecting the inherent challenges in tracking large-scale population movements without recent, comprehensive official records to validate projections. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical importance of transparent, accessible, and timely statistical data for informed global decision-making. The reliance on estimates due to delayed censuses creates a data vacuum that hinders accurate policy formulation and public scrutiny. Highlighting these gaps emphasizes the need for governments to provide complete, verifiable datasets, ensuring that societal challenges such as economic planning and resource allocation are addressed based on reality rather than speculation.
