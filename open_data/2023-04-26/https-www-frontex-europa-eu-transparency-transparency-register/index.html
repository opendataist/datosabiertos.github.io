Frontex’s Transparency Register illustrates the critical balance between institutional accountability and individual privacy in open data ecosystems. By processing personal data solely to prevent conflicts of interest and ensure lobbying transparency, the agency demonstrates how public bodies can maintain integrity without overreaching into unnecessary surveillance. This approach underscores that openness does not require abandoning data protection standards but rather integrating them into governance frameworks. The public disclosure of stakeholders’ functions highlights a broader implication for open government: transparency must be standardized and accessible to be effective. When individuals acting on behalf of third parties or the agency are identified in a consistent format, it builds public trust by clarifying who influences decision-making processes. This standardization serves as a model for other organizations seeking to make their interactions with external parties more visible and understandable to citizens. This article is relevant to open_data because it addresses the ethical and legal foundations of publishing sensitive information. It shows that open data initiatives must carefully define purposes, limit retention periods, and provide clear avenues for data subject rights. Understanding such mechanisms helps advocates and practitioners design open data policies that respect privacy while maximizing societal value, ensuring that transparency serves democracy without compromising individual rights.
