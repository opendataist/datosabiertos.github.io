Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

Transparency in academic performance metrics represents a significant step toward open data practices within higher education. By utilizing Freedom of Information requests to publish granular attainment statistics, institutions can move beyond opaque grading systems, allowing students, researchers, and the public to scrutinize educational outcomes. This approach demonstrates how public accountability mechanisms can unlock valuable datasets that were previously inaccessible, fostering a culture of evidence-based inquiry into teaching quality and student success. The analysis reveals significant disparities in attainment across different disciplines and academic levels, highlighting the complexity of standardizing evaluation methods. While subjects like Physics and English demonstrate high achievement rates, others, such as foundation years and specific language modules, show notably lower results. These variations underscore the necessity of contextualizing data when interpreting open datasets, as factors like module type and student cohort size heavily influence averages. Recognizing these nuances is essential for anyone analyzing open educational data to avoid misleading generalizations about overall institutional performance. Ultimately, publishing this detailed breakdown empowers stakeholders to make informed decisions regarding course selection and academic support. It challenges the traditional secrecy surrounding grading curves and encourages a more democratized view of university excellence. For the open data community, this case study illustrates the tangible benefits of data liberation: it transforms raw statistics into actionable insights that can drive institutional improvement and enhance public trust in educational quality assurance.
