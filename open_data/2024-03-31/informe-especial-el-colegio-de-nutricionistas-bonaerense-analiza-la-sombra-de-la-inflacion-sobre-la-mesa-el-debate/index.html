Informe Especial: el Colegio de Nutricionistas bonaerense analiza “La sombra de la inflación sobre la mesa” - EL DEBATE

Rising costs for essential food items and medications have significantly worsened economic inequality, pushing more families below the poverty line and threatening their ability to afford a nutritious diet. This financial strain forces many to abandon varied, healthy eating habits, directly compromising public health by increasing vulnerability to chronic diseases linked to malnutrition and limited access to quality nutrition. The crisis extends beyond food, as skyrocketing drug prices and reduced sales hinder the treatment and control of non-communicable diseases, which account for the majority of national deaths. These health disparities predominantly affect low-income populations, highlighting how economic instability erodes the fundamental right to food security and medical care, transforming basic necessities into privileges accessible only to some. This situation is crucial to open data because it demonstrates how transparent statistics on prices and poverty levels are essential for monitoring social rights and guiding effective policy interventions. By making this data accessible, professionals and civil society can build evidence-based networks, advocate for nutritional rights, and ensure that the link between economic indicators and human health is clearly understood and addressed in public debates.
