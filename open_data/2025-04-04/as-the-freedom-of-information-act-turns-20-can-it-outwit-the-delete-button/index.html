As the Freedom of Information Act turns 20, can it outwit the delete button?

As the Freedom of Information Act turns 20, can it outwit the delete button?

The Freedom of Information Act has significantly enhanced government transparency, empowering citizens and the media to hold public bodies accountable. By revealing everything from policy advice to operational failures, the law has made officials more answerable for their actions, demonstrating that open data mechanisms are vital for democratic oversight. The steady rise in requests confirms that the public actively uses these tools to scrutinize governance, proving that access to information remains a powerful mechanism for civic engagement. However, a significant tension exists between the law’s intent and political resistance, often termed the "chilling effect." While evidence suggests civil servants have improved record-keeping, senior politicians frequently employ delays, sparse documentation, and the deletion of communications to evade scrutiny. This strategic obfuscation undermines the spirit of the law, creating an environment where transparency is actively hindered by those in power rather than facilitated by institutional compliance. The relevance to open data is critical as informal communication channels like WhatsApp and private emails are increasingly used for official business. These non-digital or ephemeral formats fall outside traditional transparency frameworks, risking the loss of vital public records. This evolution necessitates a modernization of open data policies to ensure that digital communication habits do not erode accountability, highlighting the urgent need to adapt legal frameworks to contemporary technological realities.
