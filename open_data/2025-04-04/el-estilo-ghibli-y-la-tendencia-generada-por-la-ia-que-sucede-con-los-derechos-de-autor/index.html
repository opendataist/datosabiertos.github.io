El estilo Ghibli y la tendencia generada por la IA: ¿qué sucede con los derechos de autor?

El estilo Ghibli y la tendencia generada por la IA: ¿qué sucede con los derechos de autor?

The recent viral spread of a new OpenAI feature that transforms images into the aesthetic style of Studio Ghibli has reignited the intense debate over the ethical and legal boundaries of artificial intelligence in artistic creation. This phenomenon highlights the tension between the ease of access to such tools and the protection of authorship, revealing how the replication of protected visual styles may constitute a distinct legal infringement under Japanese law, separate from the prior training of AI models. Professionals in the sector express concern about the replacement of human creative roles and the lack of adequate regulatory protection, noting that current laws do not effectively address the commercial exploitation of specific artistic styles. While some artists view AI as a useful collaborative tool for initial composition, others insist that the true essence of art lies in narrative depth and human intention—qualities that algorithmic replicas cannot genuinely replicate. This article is crucial for the open data community, as it illustrates the practical challenges of data governance in the era of generative models. It underscores the urgent need to align legal frameworks with technical realities, particularly concerning copyright and consent for the use of training data, directly impacting the sustainability and ethics of both the technology and creative industries.
