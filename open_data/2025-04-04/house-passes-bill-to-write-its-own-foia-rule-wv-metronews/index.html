House passes bill to write its own FOIA rule - WV MetroNews

House passes bill to write its own FOIA rule - WV MetroNews

West Virginia legislators narrowly approved a bill that would allow the House of Delegates to create its own rules for disclosing public records, effectively removing them from the state’s general Freedom of Information Act. Supporters argue this shift is necessary to clarify access parameters and reduce the administrative burden on staff caused by frivolous or external requests. They maintain that transparency is already inherent in the legislative process through public visibility of votes and debates, suggesting that self-regulated rules can better manage resources while maintaining accountability to voters. However, opponents and media advocates warn that removing external oversight creates a significant risk to public accountability. Critics argue that without the "guard rails" of state FOIA laws, future legislatures could pass restrictive rules that significantly limit public access to documents like correspondence. The concern is that this move undermines the principle that all government branches should operate under the same transparency standards, potentially shielding decision-making processes from journalists, researchers, and citizens who rely on open records to understand how laws are made. This issue is critically relevant to open data because it highlights the fragility of institutional transparency frameworks. It demonstrates how legislative bodies can attempt to centralize control over information release, shifting the burden of proof and access from statutory rights to internally defined rules. For open data advocates, this serves as a cautionary tale about the importance of independent, standardized data access laws that prevent government entities from arbitrarily restricting the flow of public information, ensuring that transparency remains a guaranteed right rather than a privilege subject to political whims.
