Desaparición vs privacidad: el embate por nuestros datos biométricos y la lucha por preservar la información

Desaparición vs privacidad: el embate por nuestros datos biométricos y la lucha por preservar la información

The article highlights a critical tension between national security initiatives and digital privacy in Mexico. It warns that proposed legislation aimed at improving the search for missing persons could effectively dismantle existing data protections by abolishing the autonomous body responsible for overseeing personal information rights. Without this independent oversight, individuals would lose their ability to exercise fundamental rights regarding access, rectification, cancellation, and opposition to their personal data. Furthermore, the initiative mandates broad cooperation from both public and private entities, including banks, universities, and digital platforms, requiring them to surrender personal and biometric information upon simple reports of disappearance, often without judicial authorization. This shift transfers control of sensitive biometric data, such as fingerprints and facial images, to the Ministry of the Interior, raising significant concerns about data security and the potential vulnerability of these centralized databases to cyberattacks or unauthorized foreign access. This issue is highly relevant to open data, as it illustrates the risks of centralizing sensitive information under administrative control without robust, independent oversight mechanisms. It underscores the urgent need for transparent legal frameworks and strong institutional guarantees to protect citizens' rights when data is collected for public interest purposes, ensuring that security measures do not come at the expense of fundamental privacy and democratic freedoms.
