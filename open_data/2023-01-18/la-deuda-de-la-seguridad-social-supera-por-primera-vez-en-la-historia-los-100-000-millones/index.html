La deuda de la Seguridad Social supera por primera vez en la historia los 100.000 millones

The article reveals that Spain’s Social Security debt has reached a historic high, surging by over one hundred billion euros. This dramatic increase signals deep structural challenges regarding the system’s long-term sustainability, particularly as the government raises pensions without having finalized the necessary reforms promised to European authorities. Concurrently, total public debt has hit new records, exceeding one and a half trillion euros. While economic forecasts have been revised upward due to moderating energy prices and improved data, experts predict a significant slowdown in growth next year as households lack savings buffers. This divergence between rising liabilities and slowing future growth highlights a precarious fiscal balance. This situation is crucial for open data advocates because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and timely government statistics. Reliable open data allows citizens and analysts to monitor public debt trends and policy impacts effectively. Without clear data visibility, holding institutions accountable for unsustainable fiscal practices becomes impossible, making data openness essential for democratic oversight and informed economic debate.
