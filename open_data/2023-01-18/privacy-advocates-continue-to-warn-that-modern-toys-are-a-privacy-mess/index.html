Privacy Advocates Continue To Warn That Modern Toys Are A Privacy Mess

The article highlights the persistent privacy and security vulnerabilities in smart toys and educational technology, arguing that manufacturers routinely over-collect and monetize children’s data under the guise of ineffective anonymization. Despite decade-long warnings from privacy advocates, the industry continues to prioritize profit over safety, allowing for the creation of detailed profiles on minors without transparent consent or adequate security measures. This neglect extends beyond toys to EdTech products endorsed by governments, which often surveil children in ways that infringe on their rights. This situation is critically relevant to open data because it exposes the dangerous gaps between regulatory frameworks and technological reality. The widespread failure to protect sensitive personal information demonstrates how open or accessible data practices, when poorly regulated, can enable mass surveillance and exploitation. It underscores the urgent need for robust data governance that prioritizes individual privacy rights over corporate data harvesting, particularly when dealing with vulnerable populations like children. Ultimately, the piece illustrates that current legal structures, such as COPPA and FTC enforcement, are insufficient to address modern digital risks. The lack of meaningful accountability for companies that compromise user security signals a systemic failure in protecting digital rights. This serves as a cautionary tale for the broader open data movement, emphasizing that transparency and accessibility must be balanced with strict security protocols and ethical standards to prevent harm rather than enable it.
