China pierde población en 2022 por primera vez en más de 60 años

For the first time since 1961, China experienced an official contraction in its total population in 2022, driven primarily by a historic decline in birth rates and a simultaneous rise in the number of deaths. This demographic reversal marks the end of decades of steady population growth and confirms earlier trends of accelerated aging, as the share of the working-age population shrinks while the cohort of older adults expands. Authorities had already forecast this structural shift, which reflects the ineffectiveness of recent measures to boost fertility in the face of economic and social burdens. The relevance of this article to open data lies in the critical importance of transparency and quality in census data when confronted with external factors not captured by official statistics. The contrast between official figures, based on a survey conducted before the removal of health restrictions, and data that were only acknowledged late regarding COVID-related mortality, illustrates how opacity or delays in publishing statistical information can distort the immediate understanding of a health and social crisis. For researchers and citizens, these data underscore the need to verify the temporal and methodological sources when analyzing sensitive indicators. At the macroeconomic level, population decline represents a fundamental shift in China’s growth projections, moving the engine of economic expansion away from demographic dividends toward productivity and efficiency. Having passed this inflection point, the implications for domestic demand and the structural labor force are profound, necessitating a reassessment of public policies. This case demonstrates how open and up-to-date demographic data are crucial not only for anticipating social changes but also for foreseeing global economic realignments, such as India potentially surpassing China.
