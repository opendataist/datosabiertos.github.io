China disminuye por primera vez su población en más de 60 años

China has experienced its first population decline in decades, marking a demographic inflection point with more deaths than births. This structural trend, driven by the aging of the workforce, threatens the sustainability of economic growth and the long-term stability of public finances. Despite recent adjustments to birth policies, the decline is consolidating as a persistent challenge. This profound shift redefines the country’s future projections, indicating that the contraction period could extend regardless of immediate government efforts to reverse the situation. This case is crucial for the open data movement, as it highlights the critical importance of transparent, real-time access to official demographic indicators. Statistical transparency enables civil society and researchers to rigorously analyze the socioeconomic impacts of these structural changes, fostering a deeper, evidence-based understanding of global population dynamics.
