Open Data
ES EN

The article highlights that open data, specifically INEGI’s annual records of rheumatoid arthritis cases, reveals a notable decline in morbidity recently, particularly among women of working age. By making this health data publicly accessible, authorities can better track regional trends, such as the specific decreases observed in Oaxaca compared to previous years. This transparency allows for the analysis of long-term epidemiological patterns, even in the absence of current prevalence figures. The data supports the understanding of the disease’s significant impact on women, reinforcing the need for targeted public health strategies based on accurate, granular statistical evidence. The relevance of open data lies in its ability to expose gender disparities and geographic variations in chronic diseases. Accessible, standardized datasets enable researchers and policymakers to identify risk factors, such as genetic predisposition combined with environmental triggers, thereby facilitating more effective prevention and care initiatives.
