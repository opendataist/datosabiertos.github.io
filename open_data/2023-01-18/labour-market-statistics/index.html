Labour Market Statistics

Labour Market Statistics

Northern Ireland’s labor market demonstrates a slow but steady recovery, characterized by rising payroll numbers and significant increases in median earnings over the past year. While the overall employment rate and total hours worked have improved substantially compared to the previous year, the sector has not yet fully returned to pre-pandemic levels, with specific age groups and overall workforce participation metrics still lagging behind historical benchmarks. A notable shift occurs in unemployment indicators, where the claimant count has risen for the fourth consecutive month, reversing recent downward trends. However, this increase coexists with record-low annual redundancy figures, suggesting a complex economic environment where job security remains relatively stable despite slight upticks in those seeking benefits. This duality highlights the nuanced nature of the current recovery, balancing improved hiring with persistent claims for support. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it provides a transparent, granular view of regional economic health, enabling researchers and policymakers to analyze long-term workforce trends. By making these statistical releases openly available, stakeholders can track the efficacy of economic interventions and understand the structural shifts in employment, ensuring that public discourse on labor issues is grounded in accessible, verifiable facts rather than speculation.
