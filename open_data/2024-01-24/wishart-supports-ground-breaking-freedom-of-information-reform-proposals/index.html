Wishart supports ‘ground-breaking’ Freedom of Information reform proposals

Proposed reforms to Scotland’s Freedom of Information legislation aim to extend transparency requirements to all entities delivering public services, including outsourced organizations. This expansion addresses critical gaps where private bodies currently operate outside oversight, ensuring that essential community services remain accountable to the public. The initiative seeks to restore trust by mandating proactive information publication and closing loopholes that allow sensitive communications to escape scrutiny. The relevance to open data lies in establishing robust legal frameworks that guarantee public access to government-held information. By enforcing recording duties and preventing the deletion of digital records, these reforms ensure that data remains available for public examination rather than being concealed. This approach aligns with international standards, promoting a culture where transparency is the norm, not the exception, thereby empowering citizens with reliable information for informed decision-making. Support from political figures and information commissioners highlights the urgent need to modernize governance structures. As digital communication evolves, legal protections must adapt to prevent information suppression in high-stakes areas like healthcare and infrastructure. Ultimately, strengthening these laws reinforces the principle that open data is fundamental to democratic integrity, ensuring that public services operate with clarity and accountability for the benefit of all citizens.
