The article highlights a significant downward trend in narcomenudeo (small-scale drug trafficking) cases in Ciudad Juárez, marking the third consecutive year of decline. Despite this local improvement, Juárez remains among the highest-ranking municipalities nationally for this crime, reflecting persistent challenges in border cities. This disparity underscores that while local enforcement or reporting changes may lower specific counts, the broader regional context still presents severe security issues. Analyzing open data from national security systems reveals these nuances, showing that Juárez’s figures are part of a larger pattern affecting multiple border municipalities. The data illustrates how methodological changes and regional dynamics influence reported statistics, providing a more accurate picture of criminal incidence over time. Understanding these trends requires access to transparent, publicly available datasets that allow for longitudinal analysis and comparative studies across different locations. This information is relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the value of accessible, granular crime statistics in evaluating public safety policies. Open data enables researchers and citizens to scrutinize government performance, identify emerging patterns, and hold institutions accountable. By making such detailed records available, society can better understand the complexity of security challenges and advocate for evidence-based solutions that address the root causes of crime rather than just its symptoms.
