Consumidores mexicanos cerraron el 2024 más pesimistas: Inegi

Mexican consumers ended 2024 in a state of heightened pessimism, with confidence levels slipping for two consecutive months despite a slight annual improvement. This decline follows a period of relative optimism under the new administration, suggesting that recent judicial reforms and political shifts have dampened economic sentiment among households. The drop was driven primarily by a reduced willingness to purchase durable goods such as furniture and appliances, alongside a worsening perception of the current national economic situation. While future household expectations remained stable, the broader trend indicates that uncertainty regarding fiscal consolidation and external geopolitical factors, such as political changes in the United States, are significantly influencing consumer behavior. This trend is critical for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how transparent, real-time socioeconomic indicators can reveal the immediate human impact of policy changes. By tracking these shifts in sentiment, researchers and policymakers can better understand the correlation between governance actions and public trust, emphasizing the value of accessible data in assessing societal well-being beyond traditional economic metrics.
