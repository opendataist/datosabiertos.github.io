Turismo: mes a mes, los datos de la caída de visitantes a Mendoza

Turismo: mes a mes, los datos de la caída de visitantes a Mendoza

The article highlights a persistent decline in tourism in Mendoza throughout early 2025, characterized by a steady reduction in both visitor numbers and average length of stay. While the government anticipates a recovery driven by winter holidays and local attractions, there is a significant disconnect between official optimism and the reality reported by the hotel sector. This divergence underscores a broader national crisis within the tourism industry, where economic pressures and shifting consumer behaviors are fundamentally altering travel patterns. The relevance to open data lies in the transparency and verification of these statistical discrepancies. Publicly accessible data from the National Institute of Statistics and Censuses reveals that tourism metrics are not only decreasing but also becoming shorter in duration, with occupancy rates dropping significantly compared to historical averages. By making this granular, month-by-month data available, authorities enable independent stakeholders to validate claims about regional economic health, fostering a more accountable and evidence-based public discourse. Ultimately, this situation illustrates the critical importance of accessible, high-quality public datasets in diagnosing sectoral crises. When official narratives conflict with ground-level observations from industry leaders, open data serves as the neutral ground for truth. It allows policymakers and businesses to move beyond speculative estimates and engage with concrete evidence, ensuring that strategic decisions regarding tourism promotion and infrastructure are based on accurate, verifiable realities rather than political optimism.
