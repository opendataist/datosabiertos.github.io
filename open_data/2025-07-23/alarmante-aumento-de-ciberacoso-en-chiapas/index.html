Alarmante aumento de ciberacoso en Chiapas

Alarmante aumento de ciberacoso en Chiapas

Chiapas is experiencing a significant rise in digital violence, signaling a critical failure in the local cybersecurity culture. With fewer than three-quarters of users adopting basic security measures, the state falls behind national standards, leaving residents exposed to high levels of harassment despite relatively lower internet usage. This gap highlights a systemic vulnerability that demands immediate public policy intervention and improved educational resources. The data reveals stark gender disparities, as women face higher rates of abuse and severe threats, such as extortion, compared to men. Young adults are identified as the most vulnerable demographic, frequently subjected to deceptive identities and offensive messaging. These patterns underscore that digital harassment is not random but follows predictable, harmful trends that disproportionately affect specific groups, necessitating targeted protective strategies. This report is crucial for open-data initiatives, as it exposes the urgent need for transparent and accessible statistics on digital safety. By making such detailed information publicly available, society can hold institutions accountable and drive informed decision-making. Open data serves as a foundational tool for visualizing these risks, empowering communities to demand effective solutions against the growing tide of online aggression.
