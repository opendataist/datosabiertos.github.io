Authors & Copyright Scholars Back 'Internet Archive' in Landmark Legal Battle * TorrentFreak

Authors & Copyright Scholars Back 'Internet Archive' in Landmark Legal Battle * TorrentFreak

The Internet Archive’s Controlled Digital Lending program faces a critical legal challenge, with a lower court ruling against it in a suit brought by major publishers. The non-profit is now appealing this decision, arguing that its practice of scanning owned physical books to lend digital copies constitutes fair use. This dispute highlights a fundamental tension between copyright enforcement and the public interest in maintaining accessible digital archives. The case is significant for open data and digital preservation because it questions the extent to which libraries can own and circulate digital knowledge. Publishers argue the service disrupts market equilibrium, while supporters contend that the current ebook licensing model grants publishers excessive control, effectively preventing libraries from owning true copies of digital works. If upheld, the lower court’s ruling could severely limit the ability of cultural institutions to preserve and provide access to information in a digital-native world. Amici curiae briefs from authors, scholars, and advocacy groups emphasize that fair use serves as a necessary check against market failures where publishers dominate access terms. They argue that restricting library lending harms readers, researchers, and authors by reducing visibility and perpetuating power imbalances. This battle is crucial for defining the future of open access, ensuring that digital libraries can continue their mission to preserve history and democratize knowledge rather than succumbing to restrictive commercial licensing regimes.
