Record Labels: 'Hisses & Crackles' Are No License to Copy & Digitize Old Records * TorrentFreak

The ongoing legal battle between the Internet Archive and major record labels over the preservation of obsolete 78-rpm records highlights the critical tension between cultural heritage and copyright enforcement. The core dispute centers on whether digitizing these decaying physical artifacts, including their inherent audio imperfections, constitutes fair use or illegal distribution. This case is particularly relevant to the open data community because it challenges the legal boundaries of preserving digitized cultural assets, questioning whether archival efforts that maintain the authenticity of historical data sources can withstand claims of widespread copyright infringement. A significant legal hurdle involves the statute of limitations, with the Archive arguing that many claims are time-barred due to the labels' prior awareness of the project. Simultaneously, the debate extends to the philosophical value of preserving audio flaws, which the Archive views as essential cultural context, while labels dismiss these imperfections as evidence of degradation rather than artistic intent. This disagreement underscores the complexities of defining "authentic" digital copies when physical mediums deteriorate, a concern that resonates across various data archiving disciplines where fidelity to original formats is paramount. Ultimately, the outcome of this lawsuit will set a precedent for how digital libraries can operate in an era where physical media is becoming obsolete. If the Archive’s defense is upheld, it reinforces the viability of open access initiatives that rely on the fair use doctrine to maintain historical records against commercial interests. Conversely, a ruling against the Archive could restrict the ability of non-profit institutions to freely share and preserve open-access cultural data, potentially creating legal risks for future preservation projects involving copyrighted materials that are no longer commercially available.
