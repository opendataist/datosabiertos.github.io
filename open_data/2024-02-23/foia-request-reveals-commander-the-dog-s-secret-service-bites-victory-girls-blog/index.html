FOIA Request Reveals Commander The Dog's Secret Service Bites - Victory Girls Blog

The release of extensive documents via a Freedom of Information Act request exposes a significant gap between the Biden administration’s proclaimed commitment to transparency and its actual operational practices. Although initial reports downplayed the severity of incidents involving the presidential dog, Commander, the uncovered records reveal over two dozen biting attacks and serious safety risks for Secret Service agents. This disparity highlights how reliance on public information alone can obscure critical details, demonstrating that proactive disclosure is far more effective than reactive responses to legal requests. The documents indicate that senior leadership was aware of the dog’s aggression long before its removal, yet allowed it to roam freely off-leash within the White House. The resulting safety hazards compelled security personnel to alter their protocols, raising concerns about whether their ability to protect the President was compromised by the need to avoid the animal. These findings underscore the critical importance of accountability in government operations, where internal records often provide a more accurate picture of institutional failures and risks than official statements. This incident is highly relevant to the open data movement as a cautionary tale regarding data suppression and the necessity of robust freedom of information laws. It illustrates how governments may manage narratives by releasing burdensome volumes of data only when compelled, rather than voluntarily sharing actionable information. By ensuring that such records are accessible, open data advocates can help prevent the erosion of public trust and ensure that accountability mechanisms function effectively regardless of political optics.
