CPI regularly prepared and published and is in conformity with recommended international standard - Stabroek News

The Bureau of Statistics refutes claims that Guyana’s Consumer Price Index is missing or unreliable, affirming that the data is systematically compiled and publicly available. The CPI is calculated using a rigorous methodology aligned with international standards from organizations like the UN and IMF. This process involves a detailed Household Budget Survey to determine a weighted basket of goods, ensuring that price fluctuations in minor items do not disproportionately skew the overall index, thereby maintaining statistical integrity and objectivity. A key distinction is made between the CPI and the Cost-of-Living Index, clarifying common misconceptions about economic measurement. While the letter conflated these metrics, the CPI reflects the weighted average of prices for fixed goods and services, whereas the Cost-of-Living Index measures expenditure required to maintain a specific standard of living. Furthermore, the Bureau confirms the regular publication of Gross Domestic Product data, noting that Gross National Income figures are derived from separate balance of payments records managed by the central bank. This correspondence is relevant to open data because it highlights the necessity of transparent, accessible statistical education. By clarifying methodologies and correcting public misunderstandings through open channels, statistical agencies build trust in official datasets. The Bureau’s commitment to publishing unbiased data according to global standards demonstrates how open access to clear, accurate information empowers citizens and stakeholders to interpret economic indicators correctly, fostering greater confidence in public statistics.
