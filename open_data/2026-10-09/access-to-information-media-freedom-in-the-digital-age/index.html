Access to information, media freedom in the digital age

Access to information, media freedom in the digital age

The article asserts that access to information and media freedom are foundational to democracy, requiring legal protections that balance free expression with responsibilities like accuracy and ethical reporting. It emphasizes that while these rights are fundamental, they are not absolute and must navigate complex limitations to serve the public interest without inciting harm or violating privacy. This dynamic is significantly amplified in the digital age, where intermediaries such as social media platforms and internet service providers act as critical gatekeepers for information. These platforms democratize content creation but also introduce risks, including state censorship and the spread of disinformation, necessitating a re-evaluation of how digital spaces are governed to protect citizens while maintaining open access. Relevant to open data, the piece highlights the tension between protecting free expression and ensuring online accountability. It suggests that robust governance frameworks must actively safeguard access to information and media freedom, treating them as essential rights that require policy protection. This underscores the need for transparent data policies that support democratic engagement while mitigating the risks of digital manipulation and abuse.
