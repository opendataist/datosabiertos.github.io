La NASA publicó imágenes inéditas de Artemis II: los hallazgos del lado oculto de la Luna

La NASA publicó imágenes inéditas de Artemis II: los hallazgos del lado oculto de la Luna

NASA has opened a vast collection of scientific data derived from the Artemis II mission to public access, consolidating the first set of planetary observations made by humans in more than five decades. This archive, which includes high-resolution images, recordings, and field notes, represents a crucial milestone in revitalizing crewed lunar exploration and providing raw material for global scientific analysis. Among the notable findings are direct observations of minor impacts on the far side of the Moon, visually confirmed by astronauts from significant distances. These detections enable researchers to calibrate models regarding the frequency and nature of micrometeorite impacts, offering essential data for understanding the surface environment characterized by the absence of a protective atmosphere. This release is fundamental for the open-data community, as it democratizes access to cutting-edge information on space exploration. By making gigabytes of planetary science available, it fosters transparency and allows researchers worldwide to analyze risks to future lunar infrastructure, accelerating the development of mitigation technologies based on real evidence rather than solely on simulations.
