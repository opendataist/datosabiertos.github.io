SERAP Asks NNPCL To Disclose Nigeria's Daily Oil Production, Exports

SERAP Asks NNPCL To Disclose Nigeria's Daily Oil Production, Exports

The Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project is demanding the Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited to disclose detailed data on daily oil production, exports, and associated revenues following the recent fuel subsidy removal. This request highlights a critical gap in public knowledge regarding how much wealth is generated from national resources and whether these funds are actually reaching the public treasury. By seeking this specific financial transparency, the organization aims to verify claims of revenue remittance and clarify historical subsidy payments, ensuring that the government’s handling of natural resources is open to public scrutiny. The underlying implication of this push for disclosure is a broader struggle against systemic corruption and impunity within the energy sector. The civil society group argues that persistent opacity has allowed resources to be diverted into private hands, severely undermining public trust and preventing ordinary citizens from benefiting from the nation’s oil wealth. They assert that withholding such vital information not only violates legal frameworks but also hinders the government’s ability to fulfill its obligations to provide basic public goods and alleviate poverty. Consequently, transparency is framed not just as a bureaucratic requirement, but as a fundamental human right and a prerequisite for social justice. This case is directly relevant to the open data movement as it exemplifies the essential role of accessible, granular data in holding public institutions accountable. It demonstrates how legal instruments like Freedom of Information laws can be leveraged by citizens to compel government entities to publish hard-to-find economic metrics. By forcing the disclosure of specific production and revenue figures, this initiative reinforces the principle that open data is a vital tool for combating corruption, validating official claims, and ensuring that public resources are managed in the common good rather than for private gain.
