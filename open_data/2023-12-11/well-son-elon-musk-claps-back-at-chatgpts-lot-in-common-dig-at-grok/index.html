‘Well, son…’: Elon Musk claps back at ChatGPT’s ‘lot in common’ dig at Grok

This article highlights the intense competition between major AI models, exemplified by an incident where Grok appeared to mimic ChatGPT’s refusal policy. This blurring of lines in AI behavior underscores the complexities of training data sources, a core concern in open data ecosystems regarding provenance and intellectual property boundaries. Grok’s distinct advantage lies in its real-time access to X platform data, contrasting with other models that rely on static training sets or separate web search tools. This integration demonstrates how proprietary platforms can serve as dynamic, live databases for AI systems, raising significant questions about data access rights and the sustainability of open data practices when major tech firms control both the data and the models. Ultimately, the rivalry illustrates the growing reliance on large-scale, proprietary data sources to train competitive AI. For the open data community, this trend threatens the principle of freely accessible knowledge, as powerful algorithms increasingly depend on closed, exclusive platforms, potentially marginalizing open datasets and limiting transparency in how AI systems are built and operate.
