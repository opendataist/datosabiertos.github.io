The article highlights a severe mental health crisis in the Quindío region, where rising suicide rates reflect deep-seated social despair, economic exclusion, and inadequate psychological support, particularly in rural areas. This trend underscores the urgent need for comprehensive, proactive public policies that address the root causes of distress rather than merely reacting to tragedies. Open data is crucial here because it allows for the precise tracking of suicide attempts and deaths across municipalities, revealing geographic hotspots and temporal trends that might otherwise remain invisible. By making this granular information accessible, researchers and policymakers can identify vulnerable populations and allocate resources more effectively to where they are needed most. Ultimately, transparent data visualization helps combat the "Werther effect" by informing responsible media reporting and fostering community dialogue. It transforms isolated statistics into actionable insights, enabling coordinated efforts to dismantle stigma and build stronger social safety nets. Without this data-driven approach, interventions remain fragmented and fail to sustainably improve collective well-being.
