AI used in UK defence review stays unnamed, but is “segregated” from networks - Army Technology

AI used in UK defence review stays unnamed, but is “segregated” from networks - Army Technology

The UK government’s refusal to disclose the specific AI tools used in its Strategic Defence Review highlights a critical tension between operational security and the need for transparency in public administration. By invoking qualified exemptions to protect strategic capabilities, the state effectively keeps the provenance and potential biases of the algorithms hidden from public scrutiny. This lack of disclosure undermines the principles of open data, where citizens should ideally understand how automated systems influence significant policy decisions affecting national security and public funds. Despite withholding vendor identity and contract details, officials confirmed the AI operates within a strictly isolated, segregated cloud architecture. This technical measure is intended to ensure data integrity and security by preventing external connectivity. However, this emphasis on closed ecosystems creates a "black box" scenario where the inner workings of the decision-support system remain opaque. For open data advocates, this illustrates a trend where security concerns are used to justify limited data accessibility, complicating efforts to audit algorithmic fairness or verify the integrity of the review process. This situation is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how critical government data is becoming automated and proprietary. As AI increasingly mediates between vast datasets and policy outcomes, the absence of transparency regarding these tools threatens democratic accountability. The review serves as a case study for the broader implications of implementing opaque technology in public sectors, suggesting that without clear frameworks for algorithmic disclosure, public trust in data-driven governance may erode significantly.
