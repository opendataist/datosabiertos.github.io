Government Contractors Should Act Now to Protect EEO-1 Reports From Public Disclosure

Federal contractors must urgently address a new Freedom of Information Act request for their 2021 EEO-1 Component 1 reports. The U.S. Department of Labor’s Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs has received requests from public interest groups seeking demographic data from Type 2 consolidated reports. Consequently, contractors are notified that their data may be disclosed unless they file timely objections, a situation mirroring previous mass disclosures that have already resulted in released information and ongoing litigation for those who did not contest. The core implication for organizations is the critical need to assess the confidentiality of their submitted data. Companies must determine if their EEO-1 data qualifies as protected trade secrets or commercially sensitive financial information. To avoid automatic release, contractors must submit detailed justifications for their objections, demonstrating how disclosure could harm their economic interests or violate promises of confidentiality. This process requires a rigorous internal review to identify any specific grounds for exemption under federal disclosure laws. This event is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the fragile boundary between public transparency and corporate privacy. While open data advocates often push for broader access to employment statistics to promote equity and accountability, this legal conflict demonstrates the significant friction and resistance from regulated entities when such data becomes public. It underscores the importance of clear legal frameworks and robust privacy protections in open data ecosystems to balance transparency with legitimate business concerns.
