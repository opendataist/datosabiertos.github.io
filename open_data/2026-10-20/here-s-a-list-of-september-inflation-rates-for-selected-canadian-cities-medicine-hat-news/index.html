Canada’s national inflation reached 3.1 percent in September, though regional figures varied significantly. Statistics Canada warned that city-specific data relied on small samples, leading to potential fluctuations that limit their immediate statistical reliability. This highlights the importance of data quality and transparency in open datasets. While aggregated national metrics provide a broad overview, granular local data often requires clearer methodological context to prevent misinterpretation by the public. Understanding these limitations is crucial for researchers and policymakers relying on open government statistics. It underscores the need for robust metadata explanations to ensure that consumers of open data can accurately assess the confidence levels of localized economic indicators.
