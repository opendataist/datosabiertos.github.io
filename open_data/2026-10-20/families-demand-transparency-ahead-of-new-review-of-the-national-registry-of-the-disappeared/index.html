Families Demand Transparency Ahead of New Review of the National Registry of the Disappeared

Families Demand Transparency Ahead of New Review of the National Registry of the Disappeared

Family members of the disappeared and search groups question the independence and transparency of the new review of the National Registry of Missing Persons. They demand to know the objectives, methodology, and criteria for appointing experts before any changes are implemented, fearing that this effort will serve only to manipulate official figures without addressing the real crisis. Past experience shows that a lack of transparency in similar processes has led to disputes over data by fragmenting categories, resulting in a dehumanizing management approach in which loved ones are treated as variable statistics. Families warn that this approach takes an emotional toll on their members and that criteria must be clear from the outset, not after they have already been applied. It is crucial to include families in this process, as no one better understands the real challenges they face. This situation underscores the importance of open data in human rights contexts, where transparency in government data is vital to prevent the concealment of the true scale of disappearances. Community participation ensures that data reflect social reality and protect the memory of victims, guaranteeing that databases serve justice rather than concealment.
