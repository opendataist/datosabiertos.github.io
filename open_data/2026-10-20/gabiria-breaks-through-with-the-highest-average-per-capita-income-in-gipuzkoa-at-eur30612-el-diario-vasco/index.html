Gabiria breaks through with the highest average per capita income in Gipuzkoa at €30,612 | El Diario Vasco

Gabiria breaks through with the highest average per capita income in Gipuzkoa at €30,612 | El Diario Vasco

The analysis reveals that economic disparities do not necessarily reflect an extreme concentration of wealth, but rather statistical distortions stemming from reduced census samples and the presence of high-wage industries in small municipalities. This anomaly demonstrates how demographic structure and local economic activity, far from being homogeneous, generate high average incomes in specific areas that distort the overall perception of wealth. Socio-spatial segregation is evident in the gap between urban centers and their metropolitan belts, as well as in the duality between residential tourist zones and traditional industrial areas. While core areas with advanced services and residential appeal maintain high incomes, localities tied to their industrial or port heritage record significantly lower earnings, highlighting a territorial fragmentation where geographic proximity does not guarantee economic equality. This case is crucial for open data because it underscores the critical importance of understanding the methodologies used to calculate public indicators. By showing how a simple metric can mask structural inequalities and demographic biases, the article calls for transparency regarding the origin of data and emphasizes the need to critically analyze statistical sources to avoid misinterpretations of the population’s actual well-being.
