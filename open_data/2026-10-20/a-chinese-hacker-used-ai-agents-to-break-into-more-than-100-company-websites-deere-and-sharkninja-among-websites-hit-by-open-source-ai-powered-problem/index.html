A Chinese hacker used AI agents to break into more than 100 company websites — Deere and SharkNinja among websites hit by open-source AI-powered problem-solving platform demo

Chinese-speaking hackers are increasingly leveraging AI agents to exploit exposed open-source platforms, successfully probing and breaching numerous major corporations. This shift signifies a move beyond manual scripting toward autonomous cyberattacks, where AI tools like Carin are used to independently identify vulnerabilities and execute malicious commands with minimal human intervention. The primary implication is that attackers can now deploy unsupervised agents to scale their operations significantly. By issuing brief instructions, hackers can direct AI to scour digital records, locate weak points, and force command execution across thousands of websites. This autonomy allows attackers to manage massive, coordinated campaigns with limited personal oversight, dramatically increasing the volume and efficiency of their efforts. This development is critical for the open data community because it highlights the severe security risks of leaving AI infrastructure publicly accessible. It underscores the urgent need for robust exposure management and stricter access controls for open-source tools. As open platforms become prime targets for autonomous exploitation, ensuring their secure deployment is vital to preventing widespread data breaches and protecting sensitive corporate information from sophisticated, AI-driven threats.
