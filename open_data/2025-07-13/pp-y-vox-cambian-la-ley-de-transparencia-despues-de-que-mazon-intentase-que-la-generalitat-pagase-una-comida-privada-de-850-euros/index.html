PP y Vox cambian la ley de transparencia después de que Mazón intentase que la Generalitat pagase una comida privada de 850 euros

PP y Vox cambian la ley de transparencia después de que Mazón intentase que la Generalitat pagase una comida privada de 850 euros

The modification of transparency laws in the Valencian Community significantly restricts public officials' obligation to disclose the specific reasons for expenses. By removing the requirement to justify meals and attendees, the changes create substantial opacity regarding public spending, contradicting claims of increased clarity. This lack of detail hinders effective monitoring of potential conflicts of interest, particularly concerning high-cost events. Consequently, citizens cannot adequately scrutinize whether public funds are used appropriately, undermining the core principles of accountability that define a healthy democratic system. This case is crucial for open data as it illustrates how legal loopholes can degrade data quality and availability. When laws allow vague categorization of expenses, it becomes impossible to generate meaningful, granular datasets for public oversight, directly impacting the utility and trust inherent in transparent government data initiatives.
