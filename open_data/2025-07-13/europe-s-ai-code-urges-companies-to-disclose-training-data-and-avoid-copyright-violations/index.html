Europe's AI code urges companies to disclose training data and avoid copyright violations

Europe's AI code urges companies to disclose training data and avoid copyright violations

The EU has established a comprehensive legal framework for AI regulation, accompanied by a voluntary code of practice to help major tech companies comply with the new AI Act. While enforcement is pending, this initiative sets a critical precedent for global AI governance by demanding greater accountability from industry leaders like OpenAI and Google. The code emphasizes transparency, copyright respect, and safety, requiring developers to disclose training data sources and energy usage. These mandates clash with current industry practices, as companies resist revealing proprietary data and argue that strict copyright compliance could stifle innovation. This tension highlights the ongoing struggle to balance technological advancement with legal and ethical standards. This development is vital for open data, as it forces AI developers to be more explicit about their training datasets. By promoting transparency and respecting data ownership rights, the regulations encourage a more open and accountable ecosystem. This shift supports the principles of open data by challenging the opacity of large-scale model training and fostering better practices for data sourcing and intellectual property.
