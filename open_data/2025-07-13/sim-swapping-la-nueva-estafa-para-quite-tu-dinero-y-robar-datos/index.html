SIM SWAPPING: La nueva estafa para quite tu dinero y robar datos

SIM SWAPPING: La nueva estafa para quite tu dinero y robar datos

The article highlights the emerging threat of SIM swapping, a technique in which cybercriminals exploit personal data to duplicate a victim’s SIM card, thereby hijacking their mobile identity. This method enables attackers to bypass security measures, access confidential information, and commit financial fraud, such as unauthorized transfers or loans, often leaving the victim with a sudden loss of signal as the primary indicator. Prevention relies on robust digital hygiene, including frequent password updates, enabling two-step verification, and implementing strict privacy controls on social media to limit data exposure. By avoiding suspicious links and never sharing sensitive details through unverified channels, users can significantly reduce the risk of identity theft and maintain control over their digital footprint against sophisticated social engineering attacks. This topic is critical for open data discussions, as it illustrates the real-world consequences of data exposure. When personal information is easily accessible through public networks or poorly secured digital practices, it empowers malicious actors to manipulate essential services such as telecommunications and banking. Protecting open data ecosystems requires not only transparency but also strong privacy safeguards to prevent such vulnerabilities from compromising individual security and trust in digital infrastructure.
