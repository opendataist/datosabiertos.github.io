Albacete. El 33,6% de los hogares albacetenses aparca el vehículo fuera de su edificio o en la calle

The analysis reveals significant disparities in parking access in Albacete, heavily influenced by income and household size. Lower-income residents and single-person households are far more likely to park on the street, exposing their vehicles to climate-related damage and higher maintenance costs. Conversely, those with greater financial means predominantly enjoy secure, in-building garages. This disparity highlights how socioeconomic factors dictate access to infrastructure and vehicle preservation. Public policy must address this inequity, as street parking inflicts disproportionate wear and tear on the assets of less privileged citizens. Ensuring equitable parking availability is crucial for reducing the economic burden on vulnerable populations and promoting fair urban mobility standards. This study is vital for open data advocates, as it demonstrates how leveraging freely available national microdata can uncover hidden social inequalities. By transforming raw government statistics into actionable insights, researchers can drive accountability and inform policies that improve living conditions and resource distribution for all citizens.
