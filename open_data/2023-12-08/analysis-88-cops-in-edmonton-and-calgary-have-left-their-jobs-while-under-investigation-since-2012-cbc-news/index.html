ANALYSIS | 88 cops in Edmonton and Calgary have left their jobs while under investigation since 2012 | CBC News

Recent statistics reveal that dozens of police officers in Alberta’s major cities retired or resigned while under investigation for misconduct, effectively terminating disciplinary proceedings. This practice is widely criticized as a significant accountability loophole, allowing officers to escape internal sanctions without consequence. The lack of legislative barriers enables these individuals to potentially continue working in law enforcement elsewhere, undermining public trust and institutional integrity. The primary concern is the inability to complete investigations into alleged misconduct, particularly those involving discreditable conduct or deceit. Although recent amendments to the Police Act introduced independent oversight and diversity mandates, they failed to address this specific escape hatch. Experts argue that this omission is a critical failure, noting that without mandatory continuation of disciplinary processes until conclusion, the justice system remains compromised and accountability remains elusive. This issue is highly relevant to open data because transparency is essential for identifying and rectifying systemic flaws in policing. By obtaining and analyzing freedom of information data, researchers and the public can expose patterns of misconduct and evaluate the efficacy of reform measures. The current data gap, where police services restrict detailed disclosures, highlights the urgent need for greater openness to ensure that policing is professionalized and that accountability mechanisms function as intended.
