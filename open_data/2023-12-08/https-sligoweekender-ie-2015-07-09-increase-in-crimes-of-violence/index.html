Crime statistics in the Sligo-Leitrim region reveal a concerning rise in violent offenses, including sexual crimes, assaults, and robberies, alongside increased property damage and drink driving incidents. Conversely, categories such as non-violent thefts, fraud, and drug offenses have declined. This divergent trend highlights shifting patterns in local criminal activity, suggesting that while some deterrence measures may be working, serious violent crimes are becoming more prevalent. These figures are notable because they mark the return of official crime data publication after a suspension triggered by significant data recording irregularities found by the Garda Inspectorate. The Central Statistics Office resumed publishing these statistics only after conducting a comprehensive review of the Police Using Leading Systems Effectively system, addressing issues where crimes were either unrecorded, misclassified, or improperly categorized as non-crimes. This restoration signals a commitment to improving the reliability and accuracy of national crime records following previous administrative failures. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it underscores the critical importance of data integrity and transparency in public sector statistics. For open data to be trusted and useful for analysis, stakeholders must ensure that underlying systems are robust and that historical anomalies are acknowledged and corrected. It serves as a case study for how data governance, quality assurance, and clear communication about methodological changes are essential for maintaining public confidence in published datasets.
