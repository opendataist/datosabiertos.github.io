¿Cuáles son los plazos de la Ley de Protección de Datos? Experto explica lo que viene tras ser publicada este viernes en el Diario Oficial | Diario Financiero

¿Cuáles son los plazos de la Ley de Protección de Datos? Experto explica lo que viene tras ser publicada este viernes en el Diario Oficial | Diario Financiero

Chile has officially enacted its new Personal Data Protection Law, marking a historic milestone by aligning its standards with the European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). This legislation not only establishes clear obligations and penalties for businesses and public entities but also creates a dedicated Data Protection Agency tasked with overseeing compliance. This structural change aims to ensure that the processing of personal information respects fundamental rights, thereby enhancing digital trust and legal certainty across the entire ecosystem. The implementation process will unfold in strategic phases, beginning with the issuance of a general regulation that will detail complaint procedures and transparency requirements. Concurrently, autonomous bodies will establish their own internal rules, while the council members of the new Agency are appointed. This phased approach allows institutions to adapt their operations progressively, ensuring that oversight mechanisms and privacy policies are robust before the law fully takes effect. This regulation is crucial for the open data movement because it establishes the necessary balance between transparency and privacy. By defining clear limits on the use of information and creating a supervisory authority, the law protects citizens without hindering the exchange of data essential for innovation. For open-source and public-access communities, this legal framework provides the legal certainty needed to share data, knowing that a solid structure exists to protect the identities of the individuals involved.
