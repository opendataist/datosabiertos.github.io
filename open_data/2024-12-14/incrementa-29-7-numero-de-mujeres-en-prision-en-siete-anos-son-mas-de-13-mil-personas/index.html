Incrementa 29.7% número de mujeres en prisión en siete años, son más de 13 mil personas

Incrementa 29.7% número de mujeres en prisión en siete años, son más de 13 mil personas

The article highlights a significant rise in Mexico’s female prison population, driven by the disproportionate use of incarceration as a primary crime-deterrence strategy. This trend reveals deep systemic failures, where judicial delays and harsh sentencing criteria leave thousands of women imprisoned without final convictions, exposing them to structural violence and undermining their fundamental rights within the penal system. Beyond incarceration rates, the narrative exposes severe human rights violations, including physical and sexual abuse by authorities, restricted access to education and work, and the denial of identity documentation. These infractions are exacerbated by a double standard regarding sexuality and family reunification, which severely limits the ability of incarcerated mothers to maintain bonds with their children and violates international agreements on maternal and child welfare. This report is crucial for open data initiatives because it underscores the urgent need for transparent, granular data on gender, justice, and prison conditions. By documenting the intersection of legal frameworks with lived realities—such as mental health crises and discriminatory policies—open data can help identify these systemic gaps, advocate for evidence-based reforms, and hold institutions accountable for protecting the rights of vulnerable women in the justice system.
