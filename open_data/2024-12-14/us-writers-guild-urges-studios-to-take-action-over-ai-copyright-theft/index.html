US writers guild urges studios to take action over AI copyright “theft”

The Writers Guild of America is urging major Hollywood studios and streaming services to initiate legal action against technology giants accused of illegally using copyrighted screenplays to train artificial intelligence models. This strategic move addresses the unauthorized extraction of vast libraries of film and television dialogue, framing the tech industry’s actions as a form of intellectual property theft that undermines the hard-won protections secured by writers during recent labor disputes. Central to this conflict is the Guild’s collective bargaining agreement, which explicitly obligates studios to defend copyrights on behalf of its members. By holding certain rights in trust for writers, studios have a fiduciary duty to prevent unauthorized commercial exploitation of creative works. The Guild argues that allowing tech companies to build profitable AI services on stolen labor constitutes a breach of this obligation, demanding that studios stop being passive observers and actively protect their creative assets. This development is critical to open data discussions as it highlights the growing tension between the free flow of information required for AI training and the legal rights of content creators. It underscores the urgent need for transparent data provenance and clear licensing frameworks in the open data ecosystem. As the industry grapples with these copyright implications, this standoff illustrates the broader challenge of balancing innovation with intellectual property protection, setting a precedent for how creative content can or cannot be included in publicly accessible or commercially trained datasets.
