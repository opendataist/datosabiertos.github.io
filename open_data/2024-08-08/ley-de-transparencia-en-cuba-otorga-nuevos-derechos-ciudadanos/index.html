Ley de transparencia en Cuba otorga nuevos derechos ciudadanos

Ley de transparencia en Cuba otorga nuevos derechos ciudadanos

Cuba has approved its first Transparency and Access to Public Information Law, a legislative milestone designed to guarantee citizens’ right to request accurate and timely data from government institutions. This law aims to combat corruption, promote institutional efficiency, and protect fundamental freedoms, marking a significant shift after decades of secrecy and legal gaps that restricted access to public information. However, the law has sparked controversy over its practical implementation, particularly regarding the costs of digital reproduction and the absence of an independent body capable of overseeing compliance and sanctioning violations. The ministry responsible for enforcing the law also retains other ministerial functions, raising doubts about its impartiality. Moreover, exceptions related to national security and sovereignty may significantly limit the law’s practical utility. This legislation is relevant to the field of open data because it establishes the legal framework for the public availability of information, but its real impact will depend on media autonomy and political will to overcome the “besieged fortress” culture. True transparency will require not only the publication of data but also a structural change in communication management and greater citizen participation in government decision-making.
