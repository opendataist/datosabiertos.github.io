Commentary: Spartanburg County released its records, but secrets remain

Selective police recordkeeping undermines accountability, preventing oversight bodies from inspecting reports, denying victims legal recourse, and hindering supervisor evaluations of officer conduct. This opacity allows agencies to showcase contraband seizures while concealing unconstitutional practices, such as false drug dog alerts and coercive consent tactics. Mass enforcement programs that use traffic stops as a pretext for criminal investigations violate the Fourth Amendment by prioritizing ulterior motives over genuine public safety. By accepting vague behavioral cues as probable cause, officers create rigged systems that target innocent individuals without reasonable suspicion, effectively bypassing constitutional protections against unreasonable searches and seizures. This content is critical to open_data initiatives because transparent, comprehensive incident records are essential for identifying and preventing systemic civil rights abuses. Without accessible and accurate data, society cannot verify whether law enforcement agencies are operating within legal bounds or engaging in discriminatory pretextual stops, making data transparency a fundamental requirement for justice and trust in policing.
