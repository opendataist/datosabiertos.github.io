Qué asuntos ponen en riesgo la protección legal de datos personales

The article emphasizes that ignoring personal data protection regulations carries severe legal and financial consequences for individuals and organizations across the EU and the US. It highlights how common practices, such as using biometrics for gym access or processing images with AI tools without explicit consent, often violate the General Data Protection Regulation. These oversights expose entities to heavy fines and civil liabilities, underscoring the critical need for proactive legal advice and strict adherence to transparency and consent principles. A central implication is the distinction between mere data collection and illicit processing, particularly regarding sensitive images or video surveillance in public and private spaces. The text warns that technologies like AI-driven image conversion or digital intercoms must be implemented with robust safeguards, such as impact assessments, to prevent unauthorized access or privacy infringements. Failure to comply not only triggers administrative sanctions but also opens doors to collective actions for damages, mirroring recent high-profile cases in the United States. This content is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the legal boundaries that govern personal information, which often underpins many open datasets. While open data promotes transparency and innovation, this article clarifies that personal data cannot be freely used or shared without rigorous consent and security measures. Understanding these compliance requirements is essential for anyone working with data ecosystems to ensure that openness does not come at the cost of fundamental rights or legal exposure.
