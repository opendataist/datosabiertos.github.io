Informalidad devora el empleo formal en México

Informalidad devora el empleo formal en México

Mexico’s labor market is undergoing a structural shift toward informality, with formal employment declining while informal jobs increase. This trend reflects a diminished capacity to generate secure employment, leaving the majority of workers without social protection or legal safeguards. The data underscores a significant gap in job quality and long-term economic stability for the population. This imbalance exacerbates social inequality and restricts retirement options for millions. In the absence of sufficient formal job creation, the economy fails to adequately absorb new entrants into the labor force. Experts stress that attracting investment is essential to reversing this pattern and building a more resilient labor market that provides genuine security. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparent statistical reporting by institutions such as INEGI reveals critical socioeconomic realities. Accessible, high-quality data enables analysts and policymakers to identify structural weaknesses, advocate for evidence-based reforms, and hold government agencies accountable for labor market conditions.
