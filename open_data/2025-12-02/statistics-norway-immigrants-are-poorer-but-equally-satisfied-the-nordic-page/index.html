Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Norwegian immigrants experience significantly poorer living conditions, including lower income, worse health outcomes, and higher levels of loneliness, compared to the general population. Despite these socioeconomic challenges and increased mental health struggles, they report equal or even greater satisfaction with life in Norway. This paradox highlights a complex social reality where objective disadvantages do not necessarily translate into subjective dissatisfaction, suggesting that factors beyond material wealth contribute to overall well-being. Furthermore, immigrants demonstrate unusually high trust in national institutions such as the political system, judiciary, and police, often exceeding the trust levels found among native-born Norwegians. This strong institutional confidence contrasts with lower interpersonal trust, indicating a specific type of social integration that relies on systemic support rather than community cohesion. Understanding this distinction is crucial for policymakers aiming to foster social stability and support for diverse populations within Nordic welfare states. This article is highly relevant to open data because it showcases how public statistics can reveal nuanced social dynamics that simple metrics might miss. By making detailed survey data available, platforms enable researchers to identify disparities in health and housing while simultaneously uncovering unexpected strengths like institutional trust. Open data thus facilitates a more comprehensive understanding of immigrant integration, encouraging evidence-based policy decisions that address both material needs and psychological well-being.
