COVID-19: Inflation Hits 2-year High

COVID-19: Inflation Hits 2-year High

Nigeria’s inflation reached a two-year high in April, driven primarily by the closure of land borders in 2019. This policy severely restricted the supply of essential commodities like rice and vegetable oil, creating a sustained upward pressure on prices that has persisted into the current economic climate. The pandemic exacerbated these challenges, as lockdowns disrupted supply chains and increased the cost of food staples. While inflation rates varied by region, the composite food index continued to rise, reflecting broader economic strains. These price hikes illustrate how external shocks and policy decisions can rapidly destabilize local economies and increase the cost of living for citizens. This data is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, publicly available statistical reports are crucial for accountability and informed decision-making. By making detailed inflation metrics accessible, citizens and economists can critically evaluate government policies, such as border closures and lockdowns, to assess their true social and economic impacts beyond official narratives.
