En el olvido quién es el motor de la economía mexicana

En el olvido quién es el motor de la economía mexicana

The article argues that Mexico’s twelve-month decline in fixed investment, driven primarily by a collapse in private sector activity, signals a structural economic stagnation. This trend reveals a profound lack of business confidence caused by insecurity, judicial instability, and unfavorable fiscal policies. Consequently, the economy faces a low growth ceiling because private investment is the essential engine for productivity, technological adoption, and quality job creation, rather than merely a supplementary factor. While public investment remains stable through specific infrastructure projects, its limited scale prevents it from substituting for the private sector or driving growth rates above three percent. The text highlights that despite favorable macroeconomic adjustments like lower interest rates, private capital continues to flee due to enduring institutional risks. This reliance on state-led initiatives, which often lack broad productivity impacts, fails to replicate the success of emerging economies that sustained high growth through dynamic private sectors supported by certainty. This analysis is highly relevant to open_data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible statistics to diagnose such economic imbalances. Open data enables stakeholders to monitor investment trends, assess the impact of policy changes, and hold institutions accountable for fostering the trust required for private capital inflow. Without clear, reliable data on economic drivers like private versus public investment, designing effective strategies to attract technology-driven industries and reverse stagnation remains nearly impossible.
