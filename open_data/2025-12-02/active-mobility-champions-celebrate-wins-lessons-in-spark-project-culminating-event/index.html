Active mobility champions celebrate wins, lessons in SPARK project culminating event

Active mobility champions celebrate wins, lessons in SPARK project culminating event

The SPARK Project demonstrates how combining tactical urbanism with open data can effectively transform car-centric Philippine cities into inclusive, climate-resilient environments. By implementing temporary street interventions in Quezon City and Pasig City, the initiative provided a practical framework for testing people-centered mobility solutions. This approach allowed local governments to observe real-world impacts and gather tangible evidence to support permanent infrastructure changes, proving that low-cost, rapid prototypes are vital for shifting urban planning paradigms. A critical outcome of the project was the successful integration of open data tools to drive decision-making. The deployment of Eco-Counter devices enabled the continuous collection of pedestrian and cyclist traffic data, which was essential for validating the effectiveness of mobility interventions. This emphasis on data-driven insights ensures that future policies are not based on assumptions but on reliable, real-world metrics, thereby increasing the accountability and efficacy of public transportation investments. This article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the practical application of transparent, community-accessible data in achieving sustainable urban goals. The project highlights how open data serves as a bridge between citizen engagement, government action, and environmental sustainability. By making mobility metrics available and understandable, stakeholders can collaboratively design safer streets, ensuring that data serves as a public good that empowers communities and guides equitable urban development.
