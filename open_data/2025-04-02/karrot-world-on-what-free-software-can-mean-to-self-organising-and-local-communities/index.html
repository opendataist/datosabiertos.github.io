Karrot.world on what free software can mean to self-organising and local communities

Karrot exemplifies the intersection of technology and grassroots community resilience by offering a free, open-source tool designed for local, autonomous coordination. Rather than relying on rigid corporate structures, the software facilitates face-to-face interactions among groups engaged in activities like food saving, co-housing, and gardening. The core conclusion is that digital infrastructure must align with social values, prioritizing equity, consent-based decision-making, and non-hierarchical governance to truly empower communities rather than just managing them. The project highlights a critical shift in how community tools are funded and sustained. Supported by the European Commission’s NGI Zero initiative, Karrot explores alternative economic models such as community-supported software and open collective funding. This approach moves away from traditional hourly billing toward need-based distribution, demonstrating that open-source projects can achieve economic sustainability while remaining deeply embedded in the communities they serve. It underscores the necessity of financial independence for maintaining the integrity and accessibility of open tools. This article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates that open technology extends beyond code to include open governance and financial transparency. It shows how open-source ecosystems can challenge the exclusivity of Big Tech by fostering inclusive, diverse, and equitable digital spaces. By decentralizing ownership and encouraging groups to host their own instances, Karrot promotes a distributed model of trust and cooperation that is essential for a truly free and open internet.
