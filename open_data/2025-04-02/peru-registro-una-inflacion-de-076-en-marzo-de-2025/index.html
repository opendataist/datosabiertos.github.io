Perú registró una inflación de 0,76% en marzo de 2025

Perú registró una inflación de 0,76% en marzo de 2025

The recent inflation report highlights a moderate increase in consumer prices, driven primarily by the cost of education and food items. The surge in educational expenses is largely attributed to the start of the school year, as private institutions raised tuition and enrollment fees. Simultaneously, significant price hikes in basic goods like eggs, chicken, and vegetables reflect supply-side pressures and seasonal fluctuations, indicating a direct impact on household budgets for essential needs. Interestingly, while essential goods became more expensive, transport costs decreased, largely due to lower fares and fuel prices. This divergence suggests that while daily living costs are rising for many families, mobility expenses are becoming slightly more affordable. The underlying inflation, excluding volatile food and energy sectors, also showed an upward trend, signaling persistent price pressures across the broader economy that are not solely dependent on primary commodity fluctuations. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it underscores the importance of transparent, granular economic indicators. Access to such detailed breakdowns allows researchers, journalists, and citizens to analyze specific trends, verify official statistics, and understand the nuanced factors driving inflation. By providing structured, accessible information on price variations across diverse categories and regions, open data fosters greater accountability and enables more informed public discourse on economic policies and their social impacts.
