La pobreza en Argentina se reduce al 38,1% en medio de críticas y desconfianza

The article highlights a critical dispute over poverty statistics in Argentina, illustrating the dangers of using incomplete or manipulated data in public discourse. Experts argue that the reported decline in poverty rates is misleading because the measurement methodology excludes rural populations and ignores rising costs for essential public services. This selective reporting creates a distorted picture of reality, masking the severe economic hardships faced by a majority of the population. This case underscores the necessity of transparent, comprehensive, and methodologically sound open data. When government agencies or political actors selectively publish metrics to support specific narratives, it erodes public trust and hinders informed decision-making. True open data must encompass all relevant dimensions of social welfare, ensuring that vulnerabilities in rural areas and access to basic services are accurately reflected in official statistics. The relevance to open data lies in demonstrating how incomplete datasets can be weaponized for political propaganda. By hiding structural issues behind optimistic but partial figures, policymakers can obscure the true extent of social crises. Therefore, advocating for full, unbiased data publication is essential to hold leaders accountable and ensure that economic policies are based on reality rather than statistical fiction.
