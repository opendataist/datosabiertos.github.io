Paul McCartney y Elton John le pidieron al gobierno británico que proteja a los artistas de la IA

Elton John and Paul McCartney have warned the British government about the risks that artificial intelligence poses to artists’ copyright. The current legislative proposal would allow companies to use creative works to train their models without explicit permission, unless creators actively opt out. Musicians argue that this structure shifts control and profits away from the original authors, enabling large technology corporations to access human talent for free to compete in the market. This situation is crucial for the open data movement because it illustrates the tension between the availability of information for training algorithms and the protection of intellectual property. Data generated by creative activity is not neutral; it represents the effort and identity of individuals. If access to this data becomes normalized without clear consent and fair compensation, it undermines the principle that knowledge and culture should be respected and economically valued by their creators, thereby affecting the sustainability of future cultural production. The debate extends beyond legal issues and touches on the ethics of technological development. While tools such as AI have enabled the completion of historical works, as in the case of the Beatles, this should not serve as an excuse to deprive creators of their basic rights. The relevance for the open data community lies in establishing precedents regarding how creative datasets are shared and used. It is essential to ensure that data systems respect creators’ autonomy, guaranteeing that technological progress is not achieved at the expense of exploiting human intelligence and art.
