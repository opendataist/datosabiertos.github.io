La Inteligencia Artificial en Argentina: ¿suficiente con buenas intenciones?

The article concludes that Argentina’s new AI transparency resolution, while formally establishing a program aligned with international OECD standards, risks being merely symbolic without concrete management and investment. The main argument is that good intentions alone are insufficient; robust implementation, continuous oversight, and significant resource allocation are essential to translate regulatory frameworks into effective protection against the ethical and technological risks posed by artificial intelligence. A critical gap identified is the current absence of preventive measures and effective data protection mechanisms within the public sector, which suffers from frequent security breaches and reactive rather than proactive control. Simultaneously, the private sector drives economic growth through AI adoption but lacks binding, clear regulations to ensure responsible practices. Therefore, bridging this divide requires compelling both sectors to adopt standardized, safe practices through mandatory training and clear rules of engagement, rather than relying solely on aspirational guidelines. This discussion is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the tension between the transparency of public information and the protection of personal privacy in the age of advanced algorithms. It underscores that open data initiatives cannot exist in a vacuum; they must be integrated with rigorous data governance, security protocols, and ethical AI frameworks to maintain public trust. Without strong management and investment, the openness of data may expose citizens to new risks, making the balance between accessibility and protection a fundamental challenge for democratic societies.
