Economic Output Statistics

Northern Ireland’s economic landscape in mid-2023 reveals a complex recovery pattern distinct from the broader UK. While the services and production sectors have successfully surpassed their pre-pandemic output levels, indicating strong growth in these areas, the retail sector continues to lag behind. This divergence highlights that economic rebound is uneven, with consumer-facing retail struggling to regain its former peak while industrial and service-based activities show robust performance. The narrative of growth is driven primarily by the production and business services sectors, which have expanded significantly compared to the previous year. In contrast, the wholesale and retail trade component within services, along with certain utility sectors, has experienced declines. This suggests that while Northern Ireland is outperforming the UK in overall production and services recovery, specific sub-sectors face ongoing challenges, creating a mixed picture of economic health that differs markedly from national trends. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the value of granular, region-specific statistical transparency. By making detailed breakdowns of sector performance available, agencies allow researchers and policymakers to move beyond aggregate national figures. This level of detail enables more accurate analysis of local economic resilience, helps identify underperforming industries for targeted intervention, and supports evidence-based decision-making that accounts for the unique structural differences within the Northern Ireland economy.
