Anwar: Enactment of Freedom of Information Act approved in principle

The Malaysian government has agreed in principle to enact a Freedom of Information Act, marking a significant step toward establishing clear guidelines for public access to government information. This initiative aims to enhance transparency and empower citizens by allowing them to view data held by public bodies. By creating a legal framework that defines access parameters, the state seeks to dismantle traditional barriers to information, fostering a more open and accountable administrative system. Concurrently, the Official Secrets Act will be amended to align with these new transparency standards. This legislative shift is intended to improve public service delivery and encourage greater citizen involvement in national policy formulation. The decision reflects a broader commitment to good governance, moving away from secrecy toward a culture where openness is the norm. This change is crucial for building trust between the government and the public, ensuring that citizens are active participants rather than passive observers in the democratic process. Further reinforcing these efforts, the government plans to enact a Government Procurement Bill with a strong emphasis on transparency and accountability. By introducing complaint mechanisms and adhering to international standards, the legislation aims to professionalize the administration of statutory bodies and subsidiaries. These combined legal reforms demonstrate a serious commitment to institutionalizing integrity, which is vital for open_data advocates seeking predictable, accessible, and reliable government information.
