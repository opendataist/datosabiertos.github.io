Panama’s executive branch withdrew a controversial bill aimed at replacing the existing transparency and access to information law. This decision came in response to significant opposition from civil society and media organizations, which argued that the current legal framework did not require reform. The administration’s move demonstrates a commitment to dialogue and democratic engagement rather than unilateral action. The withdrawal underscores the critical role of stakeholder engagement in shaping legislation that affects public rights. By heeding the concerns of journalists and freedom advocates, the government highlighted the importance of maintaining robust legal protections for transparency. This event serves as a vital reminder for open data advocates: legal frameworks must reflect broad consensus to remain effective and legitimate. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how political pressure can either safeguard or undermine access to information. It reinforces the need for transparent governance structures that prioritize citizen rights over arbitrary executive changes. Ultimately, it shows that protecting open data requires active collaboration between the state and independent media to ensure enduring freedom of information.
