The INEGI report highlights a modest increase in the number of public servants within state civil protection units, alongside significant voluntary participation in thirteen entities. This data reveals the human resources available for managing diverse emergencies, ranging from floods to earthquakes, indicating an evolving operational structure across Mexico. The figures underscore the collaborative effort required to maintain safety standards in the face of recurring natural disasters. This information is crucial for open data initiatives, as it provides transparent, quantifiable metrics on government staffing and resource allocation. By making these census results publicly accessible, stakeholders can analyze trends in the distribution of the civil protection workforce. Such transparency enables more effective scrutiny of how resources are deployed to safeguard citizens against both natural and human-induced risks, thereby fostering accountability in public service management. Furthermore, understanding these dynamics supports the broader objectives of the General Law on Civil Protection. Open access to this data facilitates research into prevention and response efficiency, helping communities cultivate a culture of self-protection. Ultimately, leveraging this open information empowers society to participate more actively in emergency preparedness, ensuring that the legal framework translates into tangible, protective actions for the population.
