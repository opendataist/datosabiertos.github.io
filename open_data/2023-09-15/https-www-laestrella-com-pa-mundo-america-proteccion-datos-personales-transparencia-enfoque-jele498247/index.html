Open Data
ES EN

The article argues that transparency and data protection are not contradictory forces but complementary pillars essential for responsible information management in the digital age. Rather than viewing open-data advocates and privacy protectors as adversaries, the text posits that both concepts are necessary to build public trust and ensure ethical governance. True accountability arises when citizens can scrutinize government actions while simultaneously ensuring that personal privacy is safeguarded against misuse by state or corporate entities. A key implication for open data is the critical need to clearly distinguish between public administrative information and private personal data. The author highlights that current regulations in Panama contain restrictions on government transparency—such as secrecy surrounding cabinet discussions—that may hinder citizen oversight. These legal boundaries require rigorous review to prevent the abuse of secrecy clauses, ensuring that the default position of government operations remains accessible to the public, thereby fostering a culture of rationality and effectiveness in decision-making. This discussion is relevant to open data because it challenges practitioners to navigate the nuanced intersection of legal frameworks governing public access and individual privacy. Open-data initiatives must operate within this delicate balance, ensuring that while governmental data is published to promote efficiency and reduce corruption, personal information remains strictly protected. Ultimately, achieving this equilibrium requires ongoing debate, legal refinement, and heightened sensitization among journalists and officials to clarify the specific scope where open access applies and where privacy rights take precedence.
