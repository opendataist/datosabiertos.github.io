Northern Ireland Youth Justice Agency Annual Workload Statistics 2022/23 published today

The report analyzes five years of workload data from Northern Ireland’s Youth Justice Agency, offering a standardized view of referrals and custody trends across local districts. By aligning statistics with standard geographic boundaries, the publication enables robust longitudinal comparisons with other datasets, such as police crime records and population demographics. This methodological consistency is vital for open data ecosystems, as it ensures that justice metrics remain interoperable and comparable across different government departments and analytical frameworks. Key findings indicate a slight decrease in Youth Justice Service referrals alongside a modest rise in custodial admissions, with male youths and older adolescents representing the majority of those involved. The data highlights significant demographic disparities, particularly regarding religious background and care status, while pinpointing specific local districts with higher activity rates. These insights reveal structural patterns in youth justice intervention, demonstrating how aggregated, granular data can uncover systemic trends that individual case records might obscure. This publication underscores the importance of transparent, validated official statistics in maintaining public trust and facilitating evidence-based policy. By releasing detailed workload analytics, the Department of Justice supports open data principles that allow researchers and citizens to scrutinize government performance. Ultimately, such accessible reporting promotes accountability, enabling stakeholders to evaluate the effectiveness of youth justice strategies and advocate for improvements based on clear, empirical evidence rather than anecdotal observation.
