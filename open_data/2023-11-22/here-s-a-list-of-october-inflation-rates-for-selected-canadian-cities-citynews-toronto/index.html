Here's a list of October inflation rates for selected Canadian cities | CityNews Toronto

Statistics Canada reports a national inflation rate of 3.1 per cent, highlighting significant volatility in city-level data due to small statistical samples. This variability underscores the critical importance of sample size transparency in public economic datasets to ensure accurate interpretation and reliable decision-making for policymakers and researchers. The wide fluctuations in local figures suggest that aggregated national trends may obscure underlying regional economic realities. Open data initiatives must prioritize methodological clarity regarding sample limitations, enabling stakeholders to assess data reliability and understand the potential for misleading localized conclusions when granular statistics are analyzed without adequate context. Relevant to open data, this situation demonstrates the need for robust metadata and statistical caveats in published datasets. Providing clear guidance on sample constraints helps users navigate uncertainty, fostering trust in public statistics and promoting more nuanced, evidence-based analysis of regional economic performance across diverse geographic areas.
