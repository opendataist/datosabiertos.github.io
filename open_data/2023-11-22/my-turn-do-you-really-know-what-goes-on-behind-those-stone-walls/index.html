My Turn | Do you really know what goes on behind those stone walls?

This article highlights a significant transparency gap in the governance of major collegiate athletic conferences, using a Freedom of Information Act request for the Big Ten’s bylaws as a case study. The author argues that the conference operates as a powerful entity that influences public education while evading public scrutiny. By refusing to disclose foundational documents, the conference obscures critical decision-making processes, such as revenue distribution and voting rules, which directly impact public universities and their stakeholders. The relevance to open data lies in the argument that the Big Ten should be considered a public entity subject to disclosure requirements. Despite its non-profit status, the conference performs essential government functions, including research and teaching, and receives indirect public funding through state-backed bonds and tax exemptions. This financial and operational entanglement with the public sector suggests that its internal records are not private matters but public concerns, warranting the same level of transparency as other government bodies. Ultimately, the article underscores the necessity of accessible data in ensuring accountability within organizations that wield substantial public influence. Just as historical legal precedents protect citizens' right to question how tax-supported institutions allocate resources, modern open data principles should extend to entities like the Big Ten. Ensuring access to bylaws and financial records allows the public to understand and challenge the structures that govern their educational institutions, reinforcing the link between transparency and democratic oversight.
