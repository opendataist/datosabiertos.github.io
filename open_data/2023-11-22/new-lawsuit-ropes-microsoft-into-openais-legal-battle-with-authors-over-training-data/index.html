New Lawsuit Ropes Microsoft Into OpenAI’s Legal Battle With Authors Over Training Data

A new copyright lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft highlights the critical legal vulnerabilities surrounding the commercialization of generative AI. By alleging that Microsoft provided essential computing infrastructure and actively participated in the unlicensed scraping of copyrighted works, the case shifts scrutiny from OpenAI to its major partner. This direct involvement challenges the industry’s long-standing assertion that AI training merely develops abstract parameters rather than copying specific content, suggesting instead that the companies facilitated the mass reproduction of protected materials to build their profitable models. The legal strategy leverages recent Supreme Court rulings to argue against fair use, emphasizing the commercial nature of the defendants' actions and the economic harm caused to creators. Unlike previous dismissals where courts argued AI models do not store literal copies, this suit points to explicit confirmations that specific nonfiction texts were included in training data. The plaintiffs contend that this "manifestly unfair use" deprives authors of licensing revenue and allows users to substitute purchased works with AI-generated summaries, thereby undermining the traditional market for creative expression. This case is highly relevant to open data because it defines the boundaries of lawful data ingestion for AI development. It forces a reckoning with whether open availability of information on the internet equates to permission for commercial use, directly impacting policies on dataset licensing. As AI firms rely heavily on vast amounts of publicly accessible text, this lawsuit illustrates the growing tension between open information sharing and intellectual property rights, potentially reshaping how organizations source and utilize public data for machine learning.
