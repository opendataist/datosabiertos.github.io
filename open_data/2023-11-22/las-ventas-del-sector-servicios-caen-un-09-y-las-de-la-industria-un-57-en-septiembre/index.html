Las ventas del sector servicios caen un 0,9% y las de la industria un 5,7% en septiembre

The article highlights a mixed economic landscape in which the services sector experiences a slight annual decline in sales, contrasting with modest recovery signs in specific sub-sectors such as hospitality and retail repairs. At the same time, industrial business volumes continue to contract, driven largely by falling energy production and reduced exports to the Eurozone, indicating persistent structural challenges in manufacturing. Despite these revenue declines, employment in the services sector shows resilient growth across all regions, suggesting that companies are retaining staff even amid lower turnover. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how raw statistical indicators from national institutes can be transparently disaggregated to reveal nuanced economic realities. By making this granular information publicly accessible, policymakers, researchers, and the general public can independently analyze regional disparities and sectoral trends. This transparency fosters accountability and enables evidence-based decision-making, allowing stakeholders to identify which geographic areas or industries are truly thriving or struggling without relying on aggregated media narratives. Ultimately, the availability of such detailed datasets empowers citizens to understand the real impact of economic shifts on their local communities. It underscores the importance of maintaining high-quality, freely accessible statistical infrastructure as a public good. When data is open, it facilitates deeper societal engagement with economic realities, promoting a more informed democracy in which decisions are grounded in verified, comprehensive facts rather than vague generalizations.
