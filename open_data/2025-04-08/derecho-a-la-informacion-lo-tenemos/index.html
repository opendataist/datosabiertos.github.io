Derecho a la Información. ¿Lo tenemos?

Derecho a la Información. ¿Lo tenemos?

The article argues that effective political communication must shift from commercial government marketing to robust government communication grounded in policy justification and citizen rights. This transition highlights the necessity for governing bodies to clearly articulate the logic behind their decisions, relying on the constitutional right of citizens to access information. As regulatory frameworks evolve to restrict promotional advertising, the emphasis moves toward transparency and the substantive explanation of public policies, ensuring that governance is understood rather than merely marketed. Central to this approach is the alignment of communication with a clear political project. A coherent strategy requires communicators to translate complex policies into understandable narratives that foster public acceptance and trust. This process demands a deep understanding of the social context and the specific objectives of the administration. Without a defined political vision, communication becomes ambiguous; therefore, the primary goal is to ensure the public knows what is being done, accepts it as beneficial, and comprehends the reasoning behind administrative actions. This analysis is highly relevant to open data because transparent policy communication relies fundamentally on the availability and clarity of public information. Open data serves as the "hard data" backbone that justifies governmental decisions, enabling citizens to verify claims and understand the impact of programs. By leveraging open data, governments can move beyond superficial messaging to provide verifiable evidence of their effectiveness, thereby strengthening democratic accountability and facilitating informed public discourse based on facts rather than rhetoric.
