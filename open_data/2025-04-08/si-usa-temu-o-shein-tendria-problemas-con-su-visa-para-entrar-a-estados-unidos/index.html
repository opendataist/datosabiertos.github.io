¿Si usa Temu o Shein tendría problemas con su visa para entrar a Estados Unidos?

The US government has revoked tax exemptions for small packages from China, primarily targeting e-commerce giants like Shein and Temu. This decision aims to curb their market dominance and address concerns regarding personal data security and the potential harm to local industries in partner countries. The move introduces tariffs that may disrupt global supply chains and increase costs for consumers seeking affordable goods. While rumors suggest these platforms could negatively impact visa applications, officials clarify that no such immigration penalties exist. The primary consequences are commercial, involving stricter inspections and higher import duties rather than legal restrictions on users. This highlights a distinction between economic policy enforcement and immigration regulations, reassuring applicants that owning these apps does not affect their eligibility for US visas. This development is relevant to open_data as it underscores the critical intersection between international trade policies and data privacy rights. The US concerns about data harvesting by foreign platforms illustrate why transparency and accountability in digital services are essential. It emphasizes the need for robust open data standards to protect user privacy while navigating complex global commerce dynamics, showing how policy changes can directly impact data governance and consumer trust.
