Cómo el enfriamiento de la inflación está haciendo que muchos en EE.UU. reciban un “aumento salarial” de forma indirecta

The article concludes that inflation in the United States has begun to decline significantly, allowing workers’ purchasing power to improve for the first time since the health crisis. As real wages grow faster than prices, Americans experience indirect economic relief, marking a shift in trend after a period of broad-based price increases. This phenomenon is relevant to the open data movement because it illustrates the critical importance of having access to reliable, public, and real-time labor and inflation statistics. Without transparency in data from the Bureau of Labor Statistics and other government agencies, it would be impossible to accurately monitor economic well-being, distinguish between temporary fluctuations and structural trends, or assess the real impact of macroeconomic policies on the population. However, the analysis also highlights that these advances are relative, as prices remain higher compared to pre-pandemic levels. This underscores the need for open data to enable in-depth longitudinal analysis. The ability to compare historical indicators with current ones is essential to determine whether the recovery is genuine and sustainable, or merely a correction following a temporary distortion, thereby facilitating informed decision-making by both citizens and policymakers.
