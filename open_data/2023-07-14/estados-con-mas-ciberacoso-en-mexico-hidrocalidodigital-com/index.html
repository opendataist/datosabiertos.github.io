Estados con más ciberacoso en México | HidrocalidoDigital.com

According to INEGI data, the prevalence of cyberbullying among internet users in Mexico decreased slightly in 2022, with women disproportionately affected compared to men. The study highlights that victims often used digital platforms more intensively, suggesting a correlation between high online engagement and exposure to harassment. Notably, a majority of these incidents occurred anonymously, complicating accountability and victim support mechanisms while obscuring the identities of aggressors. Gender-specific patterns reveal that women faced significantly higher rates of sexual insinuations and exposure to sexual content, whereas men were more frequently targeted through fake identities. Social media, particularly Facebook, emerged as the primary conduit for publishing private information and media, indicating that platform design and privacy settings play a crucial role in facilitating or preventing abuse. These distinctions underscore the need for targeted interventions that address the unique ways different genders experience online violence. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how standardized, publicly available statistics can uncover systemic digital safety issues. By making detailed demographic and behavioral data accessible, researchers and policymakers can identify vulnerable populations and specific risks, such as anonymous harassment or gender-based violence. This transparency encourages evidence-based public health strategies and holds tech companies accountable for user safety, proving that open data is essential for diagnosing and mitigating modern societal harms.
