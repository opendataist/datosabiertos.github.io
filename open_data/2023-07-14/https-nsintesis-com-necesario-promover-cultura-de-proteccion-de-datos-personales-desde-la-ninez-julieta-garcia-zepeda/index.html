The article underscores the critical need to foster a culture of data privacy among children to prevent cybercrimes and protect minors from digital exploitation. By empowering young people through participatory mechanisms, society can help reduce the power imbalance between citizens and authorities, thereby fostering a more balanced relationship with government institutions. This emphasis on child participation and transparency is directly relevant to open data, as it promotes inclusive governance in which diverse voices can influence policy. Open data initiatives must ensure that public information is both accessible and secure, enabling active citizen oversight. Ultimately, this approach strengthens accountability and ensures that open government practices genuinely serve the public interest by preventing the misuse of personal data.
