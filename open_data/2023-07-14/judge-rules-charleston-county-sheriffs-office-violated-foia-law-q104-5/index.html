Judge rules Charleston County Sheriff’s Office violated FOIA law | Q104.5

A judge ruled that the Charleston County Sheriff’s Office violated state transparency laws by withholding jail call recordings from a suspect in a fatal DUI case. This decision affirms the critical role of the Freedom of Information Act in ensuring government accountability, emphasizing that citizens have a right to understand the actions of public officials during active investigations. The ruling highlights that general concerns about interfering with justice do not automatically justify withholding records without citing specific legal exemptions. By rejecting the sheriff’s office argument, the court reinforces the principle that transparency must outweigh administrative convenience, ensuring that information is only denied when strictly permitted by law rather than by broad institutional preference. This case is significant for the open data community as it sets a precedent for accessing sensitive law enforcement data. It underscores the necessity of robust legal challenges to protect access to public records, demonstrating that agencies cannot arbitrarily withhold information even in high-profile criminal matters without facing judicial scrutiny and mandatory disclosure.
