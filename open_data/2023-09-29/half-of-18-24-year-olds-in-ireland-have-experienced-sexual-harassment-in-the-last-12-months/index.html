Half of 18-24 Year-Olds in Ireland Have Experienced Sexual Harassment in the Last 12 Months

The Irish Central Statistics Office’s recent survey reveals alarming rates of sexual harassment, particularly among young adults, highlighting a pervasive issue that significantly impacts victims' freedom and safety. The data underscores that harassment is not isolated to specific locations but occurs across private venues, public spaces, and online platforms, creating an environment where more serious offenses can escalate or be excused. A critical finding is the high rate of non-disclosure among male victims, suggesting that societal stigma prevents many from reporting their experiences. This lack of transparency obscures the true scale of the problem and hinders effective support mechanisms. The significant proportion of incidents occurring in social settings and digital spaces indicates that harassment is deeply embedded in daily life, necessitating broader cultural shifts rather than just targeted interventions. This article is relevant to open data as it demonstrates how public datasets can drive essential societal dialogue and policy reform. By making such sensitive information transparent, governments empower advocates and educators to demand accountability and implement preventative measures. Open data initiatives in this context are crucial for exposing hidden trends, validating lived experiences, and fostering evidence-based strategies to combat sexual violence and support victims.
