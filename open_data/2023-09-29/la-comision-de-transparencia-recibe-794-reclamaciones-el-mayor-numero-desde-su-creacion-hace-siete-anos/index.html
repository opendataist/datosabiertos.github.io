La Comisión de Transparencia recibe 794 reclamaciones, el mayor número desde su creación hace siete años

The 2022 Transparency Commission report highlights a significant cultural shift toward public accountability, marked by a substantial increase in citizen requests for information. This surge indicates that citizens are increasingly exercising their right to access public data, driven by greater trust in digital channels and a desire to monitor government actions. Consequently, the article underscores the importance of transparent systems as a cornerstone of advanced democracy, where open access serves not just as a legal obligation but as a vital tool for civic engagement and institutional legitimacy. However, the implementation of these transparency norms faces structural challenges, particularly regarding the quality and accessibility of published data. The report recommends that public administrations simplify technical language and adopt visual aids like graphs to make information comprehensible to all citizens, regardless of their expertise. Furthermore, it points out a disparity in compliance capabilities; while larger entities demonstrate strong adherence, smaller local governments struggle due to limited resources. This highlights the need for better support mechanisms to ensure equitable transparency standards across all administrative levels. Relevance to open data lies in the tension between high volume and practical usability. Although compliance rates are generally high and administrative silence is decreasing, the persistence of unresolved complaints and the need for clearer, user-friendly data formats reveal gaps in the open data ecosystem. The article illustrates that true transparency requires more than just publishing data; it demands proactive communication strategies and adequate resources for smaller entities to ensure that open information is genuinely accessible and actionable for the public.
