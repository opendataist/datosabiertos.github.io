Scottish government called upon to commit to FOI reform

The article highlights a critical push for comprehensive Freedom of Information reforms in Scotland, driven by persistent issues of secrecy and outdated legislation. It argues that the current system fails to hold providers of public services accountable, creating significant loopholes that undermine democratic transparency and public trust. This situation is highly relevant to open data because effective open data policies rely on strong, enforceable legal frameworks. Without extending access rights to all public service providers, critical data remains hidden, preventing the full realization of open government principles and limiting civic engagement. Ultimately, the call for modernized reforms aims to align Scottish practices with global standards for universal information access. Strengthening these rights ensures that public information flows freely, empowering citizens and fostering a more accountable, transparent digital ecosystem aligned with open data ideals.
