Reporta México el número más alto de divorcios en los últimos 10 años

This data underscores the vital role of open statistics in tracking complex societal shifts, demonstrating how transparent datasets enable researchers and policymakers to identify critical demographic trends without bias. By making marriage and divorce records publicly accessible, institutions such as INEGI foster accountability and support evidence-based decisions regarding family law and social welfare. The significant rise in divorces alongside increased marriage rates reflects evolving social dynamics, particularly the growing prevalence of no-fault separations. This pattern highlights changing attitudes toward marital stability and the increasing autonomy of individuals in ending unions, signaling a broader cultural transformation in how relationships are valued and maintained in contemporary society. Furthermore, the rise in the age at marriage and the increase in same-sex unions illustrate progressive social acceptance and changing life timelines. These insights from open data are essential for understanding long-term structural changes in the family unit, allowing society to adapt policies that support diverse household structures and ensure that legal frameworks remain responsive to the real needs of the population.
