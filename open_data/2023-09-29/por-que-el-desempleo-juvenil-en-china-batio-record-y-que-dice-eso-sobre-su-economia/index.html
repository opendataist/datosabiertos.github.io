¿Por qué el desempleo juvenil en China batió récord y qué dice eso sobre su economía?

China has suspended the publication of youth unemployment statistics after recording a record high of 21.3%, an official decision justified by the need to adjust the data. This measure reflects a slower-than-expected economic slowdown, exacerbated by the real estate sector crisis, a fundamental pillar of GDP. The lack of transparency in labor indicators has generated deep anxiety among university graduates, who feel that their qualifications do not align with market demands, thereby questioning the traditional promise of social mobility through higher education. High unemployment and economic uncertainty are affecting key life decisions, such as marriage and family formation, due to pressure for material prosperity within a traditional context. Despite concerns raised on social media and among experts, authorities and some academics view this situation as a temporary difficulty rather than a permanent structural change. However, the gap between young people’s expectations and the current labor reality has fostered feelings of disillusionment and cynicism, highlighting internal tensions in the transition toward a new development model less dependent on construction. This phenomenon is relevant to open data because the deliberate suspension of official youth unemployment data hinders transparency, independent analysis, and accountability. By withholding critical information, the ability of researchers, journalists, and citizens to understand the true state of the economy and its social impacts is limited. The lack of access to timely and verifiable data prevents the development of evidence-based solutions, weakens trust in institutions, and complicates global monitoring of labor trends in one of the world’s most important economies.
