Leaked screenshot shows Amazon is now tracking individual employee office attendance records, reversing its anonymized data policy

Amazon’s shift from aggregated to individual office attendance tracking signals a critical evolution in corporate transparency and worker surveillance. By replacing anonymized data with specific "Badge Reports" for individual employees, the company moves away from broad space-planning metrics toward granular behavioral monitoring. This reversal underscores a strategic pivot where personal productivity metrics become central to management oversight, highlighting the tension between corporate mandates and employee privacy rights in the modern workplace. This policy change has significant implications for open data discussions, as it illustrates how organizations can repurpose aggregated insights into invasive, individual-level tracking tools. The introduction of visible individual attendance records, despite exemptions for international regions, demonstrates the potential for data transparency to be weaponized for compliance enforcement rather than collaborative improvement. It raises ethical questions about the balance between organizational visibility and the fundamental right to privacy, suggesting that current open data norms may need stricter boundaries to prevent misuse in employment contexts. Ultimately, this development reflects the growing scrutiny of remote work policies and the digitalization of workplace accountability. As Amazon forces a return to office through increasingly rigid enforcement, the detailed tracking serves as a deterrent rather than a supportive tool. This case study is relevant to open data advocates because it exposes the risks of data visibility when applied to personal performance, emphasizing the need for clear guidelines that protect individual autonomy against corporate overreach in an era of remote work transition.
