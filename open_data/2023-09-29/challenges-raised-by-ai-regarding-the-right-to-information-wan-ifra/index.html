Challenges raised by AI regarding the right to information - WAN-IFRA

The article argues that the rapid integration of AI into journalism poses a fundamental threat to the right to information by blurring the lines between reality and fabrication. As generative AI lowers the cost of content creation, the sheer volume of synthetic material makes verifying authenticity increasingly difficult, while language models often produce plausible but false information. This environment undermines the foundational journalistic principle of truth, forcing media organizations to struggle with maintaining integrity amidst a flood of undetectable deepfakes and algorithmic hallucinations. Furthermore, the economic and operational incentives within the AI era disproportionately favor reach and engagement over ethical standards. AI-driven recommendation systems and search algorithms prioritize sensationalist content to maximize attention, compelling media outlets to automate editorial decisions based on engagement metrics rather than journalistic value. This shift creates a structural bias where transparency is hindered by the "black box" nature of these technologies, and media entities become dependent on proprietary platforms that do not necessarily align with public interest or professional ethics, effectively silencing nuanced or critical reporting in favor of viral noise. This discussion is critical to open data because it highlights how algorithmic opacity and proprietary data practices can restrict public access to reliable information. If media ecosystems are dominated by closed AI systems that obscure their training data and prioritize commercial interests, the open flow of information is compromised. Ensuring open data principles requires advocating for transparency in AI design, protecting journalistic independence from opaque algorithmic gatekeepers, and recognizing that without ethical guardrails, the democratization of information technology may inadvertently erode the quality and trustworthiness of the public discourse.
