N.S. government orders review of freedom of information, privacy legislation | CBC News

Nova Scotia is launching a comprehensive review of its information and privacy legislation, marking the first significant update since 1999. Justice Minister Brad Johns aims to modernize laws to balance public access with digital-age privacy protections. This initiative involves consulting various stakeholders, including government bodies and journalists, to produce legislative recommendations by 2025. The review follows a political shift, as Premier Tim Houston previously criticized similar proposals while in opposition, favoring direct reforms over studies. The province continues to grapple with the authority of the privacy commissioner, whose recommendations remain non-binding. This tension highlights the ongoing debate between government discretion and independent oversight, reflecting broader challenges in maintaining transparent public institutions amidst changing political landscapes. This development is crucial for open data advocates, as it addresses the legal frameworks governing public sector transparency. Effective modernization can enhance accountability by ensuring government information remains accessible and adaptable to technological advancements. Strengthening these laws supports the public’s right to know, fostering trust in democratic processes and ensuring that privacy concerns do not obstruct the free flow of essential public information.
