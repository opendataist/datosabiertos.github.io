N.S. to review freedom of information legislation

Nova Scotia is initiating a critical review of its freedom of information laws to address long-standing complaints about excessive redactions and a weak, ineffective appeals process. This legislative overhaul aims to modernize transparency rules, responding to years of criticism that citizens face undue barriers in accessing public records despite existing privacy protections. The necessity for reform is underscored by past failures to empower the privacy commissioner with actual decision-making authority. Previous recommendations to prevent costly court battles and expedite document releases have been repeatedly ignored, leading to a decline in compliance by government bodies and leaving applicants frustrated by protracted legal disputes. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates because it signals a potential shift toward greater government accountability. By potentially granting the privacy office order-making powers, the province could establish a more robust framework for data release, ensuring that transparency initiatives are not merely symbolic but are enforced with tangible consequences for non-compliant agencies.
