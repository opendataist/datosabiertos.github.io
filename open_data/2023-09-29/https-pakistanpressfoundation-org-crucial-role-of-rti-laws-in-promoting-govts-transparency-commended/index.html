Article 19-A of the Pakistani Constitution establishes access to information as a fundamental right, serving as a cornerstone for democratic accountability. By enabling citizens to scrutinize government actions, this legal framework reduces institutional opacity and empowers the electorate to make informed decisions. The Pakistan Information Commission highlights that robust Right to Information laws are essential for fostering transparency and responsive governance. These mechanisms allow the public to hold authorities accountable, ensuring that state institutions remain open to scrutiny and responsive to the needs of the people they serve. This stance is highly relevant to open_data as it underscores the legal imperative for public institutions to proactively share data. The emphasis on constitutional rights and institutional commitment signals a strong political will to support data openness, which is crucial for building trust and facilitating meaningful civic engagement through accessible government information.
