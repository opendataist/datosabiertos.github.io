EU trade deficit with China on the rise - EUbusiness.com | EU news, business and politics

EU trade volumes expanded significantly in late 2026, shifting the overall balance from a surplus to a deficit due to growing trade gaps with major partners like China and Norway. This reversal highlights evolving supply chain dependencies and market dynamics within global commerce, emphasizing the need for robust monitoring of external economic relationships. The United States remains the primary export destination, while China dominates as the top import source. These structural imbalances underscore the critical role of trade data in assessing economic resilience and negotiating international agreements, particularly regarding strategic dependencies on key suppliers. Such granular trade statistics are vital for open data initiatives, as they provide transparent, evidence-based insights into global economic flows. By making this information accessible, policymakers and researchers can better analyze trade trends, enhance accountability in international relations, and support data-driven decision-making for sustainable economic growth.
