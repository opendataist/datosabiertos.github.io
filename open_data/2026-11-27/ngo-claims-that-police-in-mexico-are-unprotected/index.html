NGO claims that police in Mexico are unprotected

NGO claims that police in Mexico are unprotected

The study reveals severe labor precarity among Mexican municipal police forces, where most officers lack living wages, basic social security, and essential benefits. This situation contradicts the need for minimum conditions to ensure an effective and loyal public service, as incomes are often insufficient to support families, and many agents do not even have protection against occupational risks or death. The absence of common labor standards results in high staff turnover, weakening the operational continuity of the corporations responsible for the majority of police actions nationwide. Local governments must implement a minimum standard of conditions that includes adequate funding, ensuring not only fair salaries but also access to healthcare, housing, and support for officers' families, as required by current regulations. This information is relevant to the open data movement because it underscores the urgency of making information about labor conditions and budget allocations accessible and transparent. The availability of detailed data enables civil society and researchers to monitor compliance with state obligations, fostering accountability and evidence-based decision-making to improve public safety and labor rights.
