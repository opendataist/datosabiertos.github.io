US lawmakers raise worries about China in Microsoft deal with Emirati AI firm - ET Telecom

US lawmakers raise worries about China in Microsoft deal with Emirati AI firm - ET Telecom

Republican lawmakers are urging the Biden administration to assess national security risks before Microsoft proceeds with its significant investment in the UAE-based AI firm G42. The primary concern centers on the potential transfer of highly sensitive, export-restricted technologies, including advanced semiconductor chips and sophisticated AI model weights. Lawmakers fear that without robust oversight, this technology could eventually reach U.S. adversaries, particularly China, due to G42’s historical ties to Chinese entities and recent diplomatic engagements between the UAE and Beijing. This political push highlights a critical gap in current open data and technology governance frameworks. There are currently no comprehensive regulations governing the export of proprietary or closed-source AI models, allowing major tech companies to share powerful tools with foreign entities without sufficient government scrutiny. The lack of clear rules creates a precarious environment where critical infrastructure and reasoning-capable AI could be disseminated globally, raising urgent questions about how open data practices intersect with national security and intellectual property protection. The article is relevant to open data because it exposes the tension between the free flow of information and the need for controlled access to high-impact digital assets. As open data initiatives expand, this case demonstrates that unrestricted data sharing can have severe geopolitical consequences. It suggests that the future of open data may require stricter international cooperation and regulatory standards to prevent sensitive technological knowledge from being exploited by hostile actors, balancing transparency with security imperatives.
