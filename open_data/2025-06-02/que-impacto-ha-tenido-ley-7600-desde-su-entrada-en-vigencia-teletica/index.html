¿Qué impacto ha tenido Ley 7600 desde su entrada en vigencia? | Teletica

¿Qué impacto ha tenido Ley 7600 desde su entrada en vigencia? | Teletica

The article argues that despite decades of legal frameworks, true inclusion in Costa Rica is hindered by a persistent cultural misconception that views disability as a medical condition rather than a societal barrier. This narrow understanding reduces accessibility to physical modifications like ramps, ignoring the deeper attitudinal and environmental obstacles that prevent full participation. The core conclusion is that society must shift from viewing disability as an individual burden to recognizing it as a collective responsibility, where the environment, not the person, is what needs to be adapted. This perspective is crucial for open data because transparent, accessible information systems are foundational to dismantling these barriers. When data regarding accommodations, employment quotas, and rights is openly available and easily understandable, it empowers individuals to claim their entitlements and holds institutions accountable. Without open, clear data, the "reasonable adjustments" required for inclusion remain abstract concepts rather than actionable mandates, perpetuating the exclusion of vulnerable populations who lack the resources to navigate opaque bureaucratic processes. Furthermore, the text highlights the urgent need to integrate disability considerations into emerging technologies and data policies, including artificial intelligence. As public and private sectors increasingly rely on digital tools for employment and services, ensuring these systems are universally accessible is no longer optional but a legal and ethical imperative. The relevance to open data lies in the necessity of designing inclusive data infrastructures from the start, ensuring that technological advancements facilitate autonomy and equal opportunity rather than creating new forms of digital exclusion.
