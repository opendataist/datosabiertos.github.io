Making the Public Record Public | Harvard Magazine

Harvard Law School’s Caselaw Access Project represents a transformative shift in legal research by releasing seven million judicial decisions into the public domain. This initiative moves beyond simple preservation to create a searchable, analyzable database that allows researchers and the general public to explore legal history in unprecedented ways. By dismantling the barriers of traditional physical reporters, the project democratizes access to the nation’s legal foundation, enabling complex inquiries into historical patterns and judicial behaviors that were previously impossible to conduct manually. This achievement highlights a successful collaboration between public institutions and private entities, where corporate investment facilitated the digitization of vast archives in exchange for temporary exclusive access. The model demonstrates how private sector resources can be leveraged to produce enduring public goods. Once the private partner recouped its investment and was acquired, the data was fully released, proving that strategic partnerships can effectively bridge the gap between commercial viability and open scholarly resource creation. The release is particularly significant for the advancement of open data in artificial intelligence, as the database permits unrestricted downloading for training large language models. This availability helps counter the spread of misinformation by providing AI systems with a robust, authoritative source of legal truth. Consequently, the Caselaw Access Project not only serves legal professionals but also strengthens the integrity of emerging AI technologies, ensuring they are grounded in accurate, accessible, and comprehensive public legal records.
