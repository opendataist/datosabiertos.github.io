14 Barriers to Using Open Data for Better Development Decisions - ICTworks

Open data in Africa holds significant promise for enhancing governance and socio-economic development, yet current practices fall short of achieving optimal impact. The central issue is not merely the availability of data, but the systemic barriers preventing its effective utilization. Without robust legal frameworks and binding continental guidelines, institutions remain hesitant to share information due to fears of legal repercussions and privacy violations. This regulatory vacuum creates an environment of uncertainty, stifling the sustainability of open data initiatives and limiting the ability of governments and stakeholders to confidently publish data that drives meaningful change. Beyond policy, the quality and accessibility of data present critical challenges. Poor data management practices, such as releasing static PDFs rather than raw, interoperable formats, severely limit usability. Furthermore, the digital divide exacerbates inequality, as limited internet access and technical skills restrict who can actually benefit from open data. The lack of sustainable funding and coordination among stakeholders leads to fragmented efforts and duplicate data collection, while structural inequalities often result in the exploitation of communities without adequate recognition or benefit for those providing the data. This article is crucial for the open data community as it highlights that technical solutions alone are insufficient. True progress requires addressing the socio-political context, including building data literacy, establishing secure institutional policies, and ensuring ethical data practices. For open data to fulfill its potential in Africa, advocates must move beyond simple publication to foster an ecosystem where data is accessible, usable, and equitably shared, ensuring that development outcomes are inclusive and sustainable for all populations.
