New NHC school board policy doesn't align with the spirit of public records law

The proposed policy change granting school board members early access to public agenda documents before the general public raises significant transparency concerns. Although proponents argue that this lead time allows for error correction and better preparation, critics and legal experts contend that delaying public release violates open records laws. State law mandates that documents, including drafts, be accessible immediately upon creation, and granting officials a head start undermines the principle of equal access to government information. This disparity creates a situation where citizens are unaware of specific documents they might have the right to request, effectively hiding legally public records from view. Even if residents attempt to file public records requests later, the vague statutory timeline for responses does not guarantee timely access, leaving the public at a disadvantage compared to elected officials. This lack of synchronization between official review and public availability erodes trust and limits the community’s ability to engage meaningfully with local governance. Relevance to open data lies in the fundamental right of citizens to access raw, unfiltered information to hold institutions accountable. When data release is intentionally delayed for internal vetting, it contradicts the spirit of open government, which favors broad access and narrow exemptions. Ensuring simultaneous public and official access to data prevents the obscuring of government actions, fosters informed civic participation, and supports a democratic system where transparency, rather than privileged early access, drives policy decisions.
