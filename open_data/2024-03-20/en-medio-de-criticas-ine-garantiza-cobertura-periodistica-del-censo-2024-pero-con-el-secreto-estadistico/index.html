En medio de críticas, INE garantiza cobertura periodística del Censo 2024, pero con el “secreto estadístico”

The core issue revolves around balancing statistical confidentiality with the media’s right to transparency during Bolivia’s 2024 Census. The National Institute of Statistics clarified that while individual data remains protected under statutory secrecy to ensure honest public participation, this protection does not justify restricting journalistic access to general logistical operations. Journalists are permitted to observe the process from a distance, provided they respect the privacy of individual households and the specific rules of the statistical law. Relevance to open data lies in the tension between data privacy and public accountability. The Institute insists that anonymized information suffices for legal compliance, yet journalists argue that opacity regarding budget, logistics, and workforce management undermines the impartiality required of official statistics. This dispute highlights a critical challenge in open governance: defining the boundary between protecting sensitive personal identifiers and ensuring that the administrative processes behind data collection are visible and verifiable by the public and media. Ultimately, the conflict underscores the necessity of clear protocols in public data initiatives. By mandating credentialing and limiting official spokespersons, the Institute aims to control narrative flow, whereas the press demands broader access to verify the integrity of the census execution. This interaction serves as a case study for open data advocates, illustrating how legal frameworks must carefully delineate what is considered confidential personal data versus what constitutes public interest information, ensuring that efforts to protect privacy do not inadvertently suppress necessary public oversight.
