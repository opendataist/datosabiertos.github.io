Censo 2022: Un tercio de los habitantes de Necochea no tiene vivienda propia – Diario Necochea

The recent census data highlights that while a majority of households own their homes, a significant portion faces housing insecurity due to limited access to credit, high rental costs, and an influx of temporary rentals. This situation reflects a broader regional trend where economic stagnation and inflation exacerbate the difficulty of securing stable, permanent housing. The findings underscore the urgent need for more effective public policies to ensure housing rights, treating adequate shelter not just as a service but as a fundamental human right essential for personal and social development. The availability of open data from this census is crucial for democratic governance and informed policy-making. By providing granular details on housing conditions, from basic services to ownership status, the data allows researchers, journalists, and citizens to independently verify the government’s narrative and identify specific areas requiring intervention. Transparency in this sector empowers communities to demand accountability and supports evidence-based advocacy for social rights, ensuring that statistical information serves the public interest rather than merely bureaucratic reporting. Furthermore, the dataset reveals a stark contrast between high connectivity and infrastructure standards, such as water and internet access, and the lack of financial inclusion regarding property ownership. This disparity illustrates how open data can uncover complex socio-economic realities that simple percentages might obscure. Analyzing these specific indicators helps stakeholders understand that improving housing quality involves more than just physical infrastructure; it requires addressing systemic barriers to property acquisition and rental stability, making comprehensive data analysis vital for holistic social planning.
