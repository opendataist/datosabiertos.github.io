PLA Daily reveals training data of China's three carriers; expert says statistics demonstrate far seas capabilities

PLA Daily reveals training data of China's three carriers; expert says statistics demonstrate far seas capabilities

China’s recent commissioning of the Fujian solidifies its status as a major naval power with three operational aircraft carriers. Official data reveals that these vessels are not merely experimental platforms but fully combat-ready assets engaged in rigorous far-seas training. The Liaoning and Shandong have demonstrated sustained operational tempo, while the Fujian achieved immediate success with its electromagnetic catapults, signaling a significant leap in technological maturity and tactical proficiency for the Chinese Navy. This rapid advancement highlights the effectiveness of extensive simulation and rigorous preparation, allowing new carriers to integrate advanced aircraft like the J-35 and KJ-600 seamlessly. The ability to conduct daily combat sorties and successfully manage complex launch operations indicates that China has overcome previous learning curves. Consequently, the overall maritime defense capability of the fleet is strengthening, ensuring that each carrier can independently and effectively project power in distant waters. This development is highly relevant to open_data because it underscores the critical role of transparent data dissemination in verifying military capabilities. By publishing specific training metrics and operational achievements, the PLA provides verifiable evidence of progress, moving beyond vague assertions to concrete, data-backed claims. For analysts and observers, such open access to structured performance data is essential for accurate assessment of naval modernization trends, technological adoption rates, and strategic intent in the broader context of global maritime security.
