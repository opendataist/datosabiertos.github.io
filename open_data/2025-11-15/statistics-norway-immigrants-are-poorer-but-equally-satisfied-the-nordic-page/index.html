Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions than the native population, including lower incomes, worse employment and housing standards, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Despite these structural disadvantages, they report equal satisfaction with life and exhibit stronger trust in key institutions like the judiciary and police. This paradox highlights a complex reality where objective hardship coexists with subjective well-being and civic engagement. This data is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, publicly available statistics can reveal nuanced social dynamics that simplistic narratives often miss. By making detailed socioeconomic and psychological metrics accessible, open data initiatives encourage deeper analysis of integration success and policy effectiveness. It allows researchers and policymakers to move beyond surface-level indicators to understand the true lived experiences of diverse communities. Furthermore, the report underscores the value of standardized, longitudinal surveys in tracking societal trends over time. Openly sharing such comprehensive datasets fosters evidence-based decision-making, enabling stakeholders to identify specific challenges faced by different immigrant groups. Ultimately, this transparency supports more targeted interventions and promotes a more inclusive society by ensuring that the voices and conditions of marginalized groups are visible, measurable, and addressed in public discourse.
