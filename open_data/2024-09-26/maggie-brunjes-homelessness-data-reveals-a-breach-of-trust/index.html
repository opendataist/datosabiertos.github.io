Maggie Brünjes: Homelessness data reveals a breach of trust

Scottish homelessness statistics reveal a deepening crisis, marked by record numbers of children in temporary housing and rising applications for assistance. These figures highlight a significant breach of trust between the government and the housing sector, driven by policy failures and funding cuts that undermine progressive housing rights. The situation is exacerbated by external economic pressures, yet remains solvable through aligned fiscal and housing strategies that prioritize prevention and permanent solutions over temporary measures. For open data advocates, this scenario underscores the critical importance of transparent, high-quality public statistics in driving social accountability. Without accurate and accessible data on homelessness trends, it is difficult to monitor policy effectiveness or hold decision-makers accountable for resource allocation gaps. The data serves as an urgent evidence base, illustrating the real-world consequences of underinvestment and proving that statistical visibility is essential for mobilizing public opinion and guiding effective legislative action. Ultimately, resolving this emergency requires treating data not merely as retrospective reporting but as a tool for proactive intervention. By leveraging comprehensive datasets, advocates can demonstrate the failure of current systems and demand adequate funding for local authorities to implement rapid rehousing plans. This narrative reinforces the open data principle that transparency enables civic engagement, ensuring that the voices of those affected by homelessness are amplified through undeniable, publicly available evidence of systemic neglect.
