INAI garantiza pagos a trabajadores previo a extinción | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

INAI garantiza pagos a trabajadores previo a extinción | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The extinction of Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI) has triggered an immediate restructuring of its final fiscal operations. To ensure institutional survival through April 2025, the remaining council members approved a budget reallocation that prioritizes payroll and essential services. This strategic shift aims to maintain critical infrastructure, such as the National Transparency Platform, despite a drastic reduction in federal funding. This temporary financial scheduling represents a transitional phase toward a new transparency model rather than a permanent operational framework. By securing resources for staff salaries and core administrative functions, the institution seeks to uphold its human rights mandate until its final days. The move highlights the fragility of autonomous bodies facing legislative dissolution and the urgent need to preserve institutional knowledge and service continuity during political transitions. This situation is profoundly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the vulnerability of public information systems when political will shifts. It underscores the necessity of establishing robust, decentralized open data frameworks that survive changes in governance. If transparency institutions can be dismantled or defunded so easily, the sustainability of free public information becomes dependent on citizen demand and resilient technical architectures, rather than solely on the existence of specific government bodies.
