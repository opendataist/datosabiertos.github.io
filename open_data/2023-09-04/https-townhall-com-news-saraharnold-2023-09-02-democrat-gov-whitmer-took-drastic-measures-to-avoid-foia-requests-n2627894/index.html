This article highlights a concerning practice where Michigan officials used coded messaging to evade public records requests. By employing Greek letters and other strategies, the administration allegedly concealed critical information regarding water safety issues from the public. This direct circumvention of transparency protocols undermines democratic accountability and public trust in government operations. The relevance to open data lies in the deliberate manipulation of communication channels to avoid disclosure. When government entities actively obscure data or use encryption-like methods to bypass FOIA laws, it creates significant barriers for researchers, journalists, and citizens seeking to understand policy impacts. Such actions demonstrate how institutional resistance can corrupt the open data ecosystem, preventing the free flow of essential information. Ultimately, this case serves as a stark warning about the fragility of information transparency when political will favors secrecy over openness. It emphasizes the urgent need for robust technological and legal safeguards to ensure that digital communications remain accessible. Protecting open data requires vigilant oversight to prevent governments from using technical workarounds to hide material facts from their constituents, ensuring that truth remains available for public scrutiny.
