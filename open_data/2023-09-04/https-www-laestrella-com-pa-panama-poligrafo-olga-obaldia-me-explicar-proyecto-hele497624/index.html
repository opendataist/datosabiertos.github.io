The article highlights deep concerns regarding a proposed law in Panama that aims to replace current transparency regulations. The main conclusion is that this draft bill significantly weakens democratic accountability by expanding the categories of confidential information and removing effective sanctions for officials who withhold public data. This shift prioritizes government secrecy over citizens' right to know, potentially shielding corrupt practices and reducing the state's obligation to report on the use of public funds. From an open data perspective, this legislation is critically relevant because it threatens the foundational principles of transparency and institutional independence. By transforming the oversight authority into a political instrument rather than an impartial guarantor, the proposal dismantles the separation of powers necessary for robust oversight. Furthermore, it removes the ability of individuals to access justice without legal representation, creating administrative barriers that effectively silence citizen scrutiny and prevent the free flow of essential public information. Ultimately, the project is criticized for being drafted without genuine public consultation and for establishing a bureaucratic labyrinth that obscures responsibility. The expansion of exceptions to confidentiality and the elimination of specific public interest tests mean that judicial rulings and administrative processes could become secret. This regression undermines the rule of law and the cultural shift toward open governance, leaving citizens unable to exercise control or demand accountability from their government institutions.
