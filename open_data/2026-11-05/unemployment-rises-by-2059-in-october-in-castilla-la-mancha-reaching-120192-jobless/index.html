Unemployment rises by 2,059 in October in Castilla-La Mancha, reaching 120,192 jobless

The article highlights the rise in registered unemployment in Spain during October, a phenomenon that has historically recurred in this month, driven primarily by the services sector and individuals without prior employment experience. At the regional level, Castilla-La Mancha mirrors this national trend with significant increases, particularly among young people under 25 and across all its provinces. This dynamic suggests a structural seasonality in the Spanish labor market, where temporary hiring remains high while permanent contracts have relatively declined in some autonomous communities. The relevance of this report for the open data movement lies in the transparency and accessibility of official employment statistics. By publishing detailed breakdowns by autonomous community, province, gender, age, and economic sector, institutions enable citizens, researchers, and developers to audit and analyze the health of the labor market with precision. This openness of data facilitates the identification of structural inequalities, such as the differentiated impact by gender or the vulnerability of young people, fostering a more informed society capable of exerting evidence-based pressure. Moreover, the availability of these data in open formats drives civic innovation, enabling the creation of tools and visualizations that make complex trends—such as benefit coverage or the historical evolution of unemployment—easier to understand. The ability to cross-reference hiring and unemployment data in near real-time empowers civil society to demand more effective public policies tailored to local realities. Ultimately, free access to this information is essential for government accountability and the development of technological solutions that improve labor market integration and social equity.
