Crimes related to fuel theft in Mexico surge 207%, according to INEGI data

Crimes related to fuel theft in Mexico surge 207%, according to INEGI data

The National Census on the Administration of Justice reveals an explosive increase in fuel theft (huachicol) and hydrocarbon robbery at the federal level, marking the largest annual jump recorded. This trend reverses a previous decline, once again positioning these offenses among the most frequent in the country, surpassed only by arms-related and tax-related crimes. The data show that economic losses to the public treasury during the current administration have already significantly exceeded those of previous governments. This phenomenon not only affects tax revenue from fuel but also generates massive embezzlement through the direct theft of crude oil, severely impacting public finances. The relevance of this article to open data lies in the need for transparency and access to high-quality public information. Accurate tracking of crime and financial indicators is crucial for citizen accountability. Ensuring that censuses and independent analyses are accessible enables effective monitoring of national security and the use of public resources, fostering an informed and vigilant society.
