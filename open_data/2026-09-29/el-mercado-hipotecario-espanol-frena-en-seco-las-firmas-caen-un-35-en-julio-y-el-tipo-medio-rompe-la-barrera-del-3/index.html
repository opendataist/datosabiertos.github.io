El mercado hipotecario español frena en seco: Las firmas caen un 3,5% en julio y el tipo medio rompe la barrera del 3%

The Spanish mortgage market has undergone a sharp reversal, shifting from growth to contraction as interest rates surpass psychological thresholds. This sudden slowdown, driven by the European Central Bank’s monetary policy, indicates that rising borrowing costs are effectively cooling demand. The data reveals a market in transition, where housing affordability is becoming increasingly precarious for prospective buyers. A critical dynamic is the simultaneous rise in average loan amounts despite a decline in the number of contracts signed. This trend highlights a dual pressure: while financing is becoming more expensive, housing prices remain high, forcing families to seek larger loans to afford homes. This combination significantly reduces purchasing power and creates a restrictive environment for housing access, particularly affecting those adjusting their payments due to floating rates or fixed-term renewals. This situation underscores the importance of open data, as it demonstrates how transparent, timely official statistics are essential for understanding complex socioeconomic trends. By making this data freely available, stakeholders can analyze the real-time impact of monetary policy on household finances. Such visibility empowers individuals and policymakers to make informed decisions and assess the true health of the real estate sector, ensuring that economic signals are not obscured by opaque financial practices.
