Europe and UAE plan $1 billion orbital data center with 50 connected "computers" and a central "LLM store" that allows users to select what open source model to use on data collected by satellite sensors

Marlan Space and Loft Orbital have secured a billion-dollar initiative to expand their Altair Earth observation constellation from ten to fifty satellites. This expansion, backed by UAE investment and utilizing Mistral AI’s models, aims to shift the paradigm of space-based data processing. By equipping satellites with onboard AI, the system interprets sensor data in orbit before it reaches the ground, dramatically reducing the latency associated with traditional raw imagery transmission. The architecture functions as an orbital data center, offering a centralized application store where users can select specific large language models to process satellite-collected information. This approach addresses the critical bottleneck of processing vast amounts of Earth observation data on the ground. Instead of downloading hours of footage, customers receive immediate alerts and analyzed results, effectively turning the constellation into a fleet of connected compute units that provide near-real-time insights for global monitoring and security. This development is highly relevant to the open data community as it raises pivotal questions about data accessibility and model transparency. The proposed AI application store allows users to choose their processing models, yet it remains unclear whether this ecosystem will support open-source or open-weight solutions. As space-based computing becomes more centralized, ensuring that these platforms do not create new proprietary barriers to Earth observation data is essential for maintaining the principles of openness and equitable access to critical planetary information.
