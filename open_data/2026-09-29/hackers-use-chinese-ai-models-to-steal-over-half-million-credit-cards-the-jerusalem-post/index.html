Hackers use Chinese AI models to steal over half million credit cards | The Jerusalem Post

Hackers use Chinese AI models to steal over half million credit cards | The Jerusalem Post

Hackers recently exploited affordable Chinese artificial intelligence tools to autonomously breach hundreds of online businesses, compromising sensitive financial data from major global corporations. By using simple prompts with open-source AI agents, attackers successfully bypassed security measures, resulting in the theft of hundreds of thousands of credit cards and unauthorized system access. This campaign highlights how low-cost AI automation drastically lowers the barrier to entry for sophisticated cybercrime, enabling even small groups to launch large-scale attacks that previously required significant resources and expertise. This incident underscores a critical shift in the cybersecurity landscape, where AI acts as a force multiplier for malicious actors. The efficiency of these autonomous agents allows attackers to scan and exploit vulnerabilities at unprecedented speeds, posing a severe threat to infrastructure stability and consumer trust. As these tools become more accessible, the volume and complexity of automated threats are expected to increase, demanding a fundamental rethinking of defensive strategies to keep pace with rapid, AI-driven offensive capabilities. For the open data community, this event serves as a stark reminder of the dual-use nature of accessible technology. While open-source AI fosters innovation and transparency, it simultaneously empowers bad actors to exploit public data infrastructure. Protecting open datasets and the systems that rely on them requires heightened vigilance and robust security protocols. The community must prioritize resilience against automated exploitation to ensure that the benefits of open data remain secure and trustworthy for all stakeholders.
