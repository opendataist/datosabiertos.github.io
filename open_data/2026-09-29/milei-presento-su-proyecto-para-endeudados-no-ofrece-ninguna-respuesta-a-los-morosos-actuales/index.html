Milei presentó su proyecto para endeudados: no ofrece ninguna respuesta a los morosos actuales

The bill proposed by the ruling party aims to establish a common framework of transparency and protection for consumer credit, focusing primarily on future transactions rather than current delinquency. By synthesizing multiple initiatives, the legislation prioritizes financial education and the fight against digital fraud, reaffirming the position that lack of training is a key factor in debt problems. This preventive approach marks a shift in focus toward responsible financial inclusion through digital literacy. The legislation requires strict disclosure of financial costs, ensuring that the CFTEA (Total Effective Annual Cost) is visible and not obscured by promotions, and obliges fintech companies to clearly break down taxes and fees. Free access to credit risk profiles is guaranteed, and a ten-day regret button is implemented to allow contract cancellation. These measures seek to empower consumers, facilitating the comparison of offers and portability to more convenient alternatives, thereby fostering fair competition in the market. This article is relevant to open data because its success depends on the availability and clarity of structured financial information. The requirement for radical transparency in costs and the facilitation of portability necessitate that financial data be accessible, standardized, and shareable across platforms. Without open and comparable data, the mechanisms for informed choice and fraud prevention proposed by the law would lack practical effectiveness, highlighting the importance of data interoperability in digital regulation.
