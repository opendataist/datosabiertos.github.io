Kathleen Hanna, Tegan and Sara, More Back Internet Archive in $621 Million Copyright Fight

Kathleen Hanna, Tegan and Sara, More Back Internet Archive in $621 Million Copyright Fight

Over 300 musicians have united to oppose a massive copyright lawsuit against the Internet Archive, arguing that the legal challenge prioritizes corporate profits over the survival of artistic heritage. The signatories contend that major record labels are misusing resources to threaten a vital digital library, rather than addressing the genuine financial struggles faced by working artists. This collective action highlights a growing rift between industry gatekeepers and creators who rely on open access to cultural history for preservation and inspiration. The lawsuit, seeking hundreds of millions in damages, targets the Archive’s digitization of historical recordings, labeling the effort as infringement rather than preservation. Supporters view this as an existential threat to public access and the safeguarding of diverse cultural narratives from being erased by digital decay. By framing the Archive as a research library, defenders emphasize its role in maintaining a comprehensive historical record that benefits both the public and artists, contrasting this value against the restrictive practices of mainstream commercial entities. This conflict is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of preserving digital heritage against proprietary control. It illustrates how open access initiatives serve as essential infrastructure for cultural memory, ensuring that information remains available for future research and creativity. The artists’ demand for systemic reform rather than litigation highlights a broader need for fair data practices that support creators and libraries, ensuring that historical records are not lost to corporate interests or legal intimidation.
