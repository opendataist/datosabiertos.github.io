Inflación baja a 4.55% en noviembre, el nivel más bajo desde 2020

Mexico’s inflation moderated in November, stabilizing at an annual rate of 4.55 percent following a recent uptick. This decline was driven significantly by falling prices for fresh produce and electronics, which offset spikes in electricity costs resulting from the end of seasonal tariff programs. The core inflation rate also reached its lowest level since April 2020, indicating a broader trend of price stabilization across less volatile sectors. The distinction between core and non-core inflation highlights that while food and energy prices remain volatile, underlying economic pressures are easing. Prices for services continue to rise, whereas prices for goods are declining, suggesting complex market dynamics. This divergence implies that while immediate consumption costs for staple goods may fluctuate, fundamental inflationary pressures are cooling, allowing for more predictable economic planning. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, high-frequency statistical monitoring for economic policy. Reliable, accessible inflation metrics enable researchers and citizens to scrutinize government effectiveness and market health. By making such granular data available, societies can foster informed debate on the impact of the cost of living, ensuring that economic indicators serve public accountability rather than remaining opaque institutional metrics.
