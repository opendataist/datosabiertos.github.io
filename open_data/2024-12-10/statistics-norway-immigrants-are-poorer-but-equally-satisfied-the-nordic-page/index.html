Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway experience significantly poorer living conditions compared to the general population, facing challenges in employment, housing, health, and income. Despite these substantial socioeconomic disadvantages and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness, this demographic reports equal life satisfaction. This paradox highlights a complex social reality where objective hardship does not necessarily translate into subjective dissatisfaction, offering a crucial perspective on well-being beyond mere economic metrics. Furthermore, immigrants demonstrate remarkably high confidence in state institutions, including the political system, judiciary, and police, often exceeding the trust levels of native Norwegians. This strong institutional affiliation suggests a deep integration into the societal framework, contrasting with lower interpersonal trust. Understanding these divergent trust dynamics is vital for policymakers aiming to foster social cohesion and effective governance in diverse communities. This report is relevant to open data as it exemplifies how transparent, accessible statistical information can reveal nuanced societal trends. By making detailed findings on health, income, and social trust publicly available, such data empowers researchers and civil society to analyze integration outcomes critically. It underscores the importance of comprehensive data collection in shaping informed, equitable public policies that address both material conditions and social well-being for all residents.
