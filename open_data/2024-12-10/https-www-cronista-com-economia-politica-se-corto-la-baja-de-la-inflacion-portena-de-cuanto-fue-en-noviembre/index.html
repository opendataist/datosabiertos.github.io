Inflation in Buenos Aires maintained its monthly level, consolidating a year-on-year deceleration that, although still high, has generated optimism for the national government. This data suggests a relative stabilization compared to market expectations, marking a turning point in the recent downward trend. The main impact comes from essential services such as housing, transportation, and health, reflecting structural and seasonal adjustments that continue to pressure the daily cost of living for residents. The composition of spending reveals that subsidies and regulated tariffs, along with energy and transportation, are the most significant drivers of price increases. In contrast, food experienced mixed dynamics, with some declines in basic products partially offsetting increases in other sectors. This heterogeneity demonstrates that inflation is not uniform but is driven by specific categories with different elasticities and weights in the household basket, complicating generalized control policies. This report is relevant to the field of open data because it illustrates the importance of disaggregating macroeconomic statistics into detailed series by sector and type of good. By making specific components and their weights public, citizens and analysts can better understand the underlying forces behind general indices. Transparency in these microdata allows for more rigorous public scrutiny, facilitating the creation of visualization and analysis tools that contribute to more effective accountability and a more accurate public understanding of the local economy.
