Piratas informáticos secuestran datos del ayuntamiento de Sils

The Sils Town Council has been the victim of a ransomware attack that encrypted files and compromised personal data, a situation that underscores the critical vulnerability of public infrastructure to cybercrime. As with other serious incidents, this case demonstrates how a lack of digital resilience can paralyze essential services and expose citizens’ sensitive information, resulting in a privacy breach that requires legal intervention and immediate notification to data protection authorities. The relevance to open data lies in the vital importance of the technological architecture used to ensure operational continuity. In this scenario, the use of a cloud-based case management system allowed public services to continue operating almost normally, unlike what would have happened a few years ago. This highlights that decentralization and data storage redundancy are not just efficiency tools, but fundamental safeguards that keep public service channels open and accessible even when local systems fail. However, the incident also reveals that dependence on specific systems can create blind spots; procedures that require direct access to local databases, such as certain urban planning documents, remain affected. This distinction between general continuity and the punctual accessibility of information underscores the need for administrations to adopt truly robust open data strategies. Transparency and access to information must not be vulnerable to isolated security failures, but rather designed to withstand attacks aimed at seizing or manipulating public information.
