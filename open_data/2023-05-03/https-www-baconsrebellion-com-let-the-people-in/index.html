The Virginia Supreme Court reinforced the principle that citizens possess an unequivocal right to physically attend public meetings, rejecting a school board’s attempt to restrict access to a virtual feed. The ruling emphasizes that statutory language requires actual physical presence unless normal accommodations are insufficient, establishing that local governments cannot artificially limit in-person attendance or claim emergency powers to bypass transparency laws after restrictions have expired. This decision is highly relevant to open data advocates because it underscores that transparency is not merely about providing data or digital access, but about guaranteeing physical accessibility and public presence at the source of decision-making. It clarifies that convenience or remote viewing does not satisfy legal obligations, ensuring that the public retains the right to witness deliberations firsthand, which is crucial for maintaining trust in government operations. The article also highlights the ongoing tension between necessary private deliberation and public oversight. While historical precedents like the Constitutional Convention justify secrecy for candid debate, modern legal frameworks prioritize openness, ruling that elected officials cannot claim exceptional privacy rights for routine administrative retreats. Ultimately, this case serves as a stern reminder that transparency laws are binding, and local entities must prioritize public access over their desire for unobserved discussions.
