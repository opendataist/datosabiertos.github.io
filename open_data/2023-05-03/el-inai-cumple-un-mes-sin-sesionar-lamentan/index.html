El INAI cumple un mes sin sesionar, lamentan

El INAI cumple un mes sin sesionar, lamentan

The failure to appoint a senator as a commissioner of the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) prevents the body from reaching the quorum required to hold sessions. This absence effectively paralyzes the institute’s ability to resolve public complaints, leaving citizens without a mechanism to defend their rights to information and data protection against government entities. Consequently, the democratic guarantee of transparency is severely compromised. Although authorities must still publish information and respond to requests, the lack of a full council means individual grievances cannot be adjudicated, undermining the constitutional safeguards intended for the Mexican population. This situation is critical for open data advocates because it highlights the fragility of institutions that ensure data accountability. When the governing body cannot function, the enforcement of transparency laws becomes theoretical rather than practical, threatening the very foundation of accessible and protected public information systems.
