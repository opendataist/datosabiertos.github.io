Open Data
ES EN

The exposure of personal data online represents a critical vulnerability, transforming private information into a valuable asset for malicious actors. Underestimating this risk allows third parties, both corporate and criminal, to collect and exploit sensitive details using advanced technologies, including the use of artificial intelligence to personalize and scale attacks. This underscores the urgent need to protect digital identity from non-consensual data collection. Personal data is the driving force behind various cyber threats, ranging from financial fraud and identity theft to ransomware and corporate espionage. The commercialization of this information in clandestine markets and its use for social engineering demonstrate that privacy is not merely an individual concern but an essential component of overall security. The lack of protection facilitates unauthorized access to accounts and systems, amplifying the potential harm to victims. This topic is relevant to open data because it illustrates the inherent tension between transparency and privacy protection. While open data fosters innovation and accountability, its large-scale collection and analysis can facilitate the re-identification of individuals and their exploitation. Understanding how personal data integrates into broader ecosystems helps develop governance frameworks that balance public benefit with individual security, preventing data openness from becoming a tool for surveillance or organized crime.
