Despite recent declines in consumer borrowing and mortgage rates, millions of households remain in financial distress, struggling to cover essential costs. Many are forced into further debt just to survive, revealing that superficial economic improvements mask a deeper crisis of widespread problem debt. This situation highlights the urgent need for targeted government support to protect low-income families from eroding financial resilience. The charity warns that without intervention, rising living costs will continue to push more people into unsustainable borrowing cycles, threatening overall economic stability. Open data is crucial here, as transparent statistics on borrowing trends and debt levels allow for accurate assessment of household financial health. By making such data accessible, researchers and policymakers can better identify vulnerable groups, evaluate the impact of economic policies, and design effective interventions to address systemic debt issues.
