The Welsh Government is moving to modernize outdated laws that mandate statutory notices be published in local newspapers, a requirement that forces authorities to spend hundreds of thousands of pounds on media with dwindling and often untracked circulations. An investigation revealed that local councils are spending significant public funds on print advertisements while frequently lacking data on the actual reach or cost-efficiency of these publications, highlighting a systemic inefficiency in how public information is disseminated. This legislative shift proposes replacing the print requirement with digital publication on council websites, accompanied by measures to ensure accessibility for those unable to use online facilities. The implication is a move toward greater transparency and fiscal responsibility, allowing governments to direct resources away from obsolete media practices and toward more effective, measurable digital communication channels that better serve modern citizens. This case is highly relevant to the open data movement as it underscores the critical need for transparency in public spending and the measurement of information accessibility. It illustrates how rigid legal definitions can obscure the true value of public expenditures and demonstrates the potential for data-driven advocacy to push for open, efficient, and cost-effective methods of civic communication.
