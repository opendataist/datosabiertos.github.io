Local authorities failing to accelerate EV infrastructure investment

Local authorities failing to accelerate EV infrastructure investment

Local authorities are failing to meet the urgent need for electric vehicle infrastructure, with installation rates rising only marginally despite the critical 2030 government targets. This stagnation highlights a significant infrastructure gap, as current progress is nowhere near the pace required to support the transition to electric mobility. The disparity in efforts across different regions further emphasizes the inconsistency in public sector commitment to this essential technological shift. The shortfall in public provision is not being remedied by private sector investment, creating a stalemate that hinders widespread adoption. Investors remain hesitant due to unclear market demand, high connection costs, and complex regulatory requirements, while consumers are reluctant to switch to electric vehicles without reliable charging access. This mutual uncertainty creates a barrier that neither public funding alone nor private initiative can easily overcome, threatening the viability of broader electrification goals. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocates because the lack of a specific legal duty on local authorities results in inconsistent and often invisible reporting of progress. Without standardized, transparent data requirements, the public cannot effectively hold institutions accountable for their performance. Enhancing open data standards and mandating clear reporting duties would provide the visibility needed to drive policy changes and ensure equitable infrastructure development across all regions.
