Economía mexicana crece 0.9% en el segundo trimestre del año: Inegi

The recent Mexican GDP estimates reveal an economy that exceeded expert expectations by maintaining steady growth in the second quarter of 2023. This resilience was primarily driven by robust consumer spending, accelerated construction activity, and a stable manufacturing sector benefiting from demand in the United States. Notably, the services sector led this expansion, supported by rising employment and wages amid declining inflationary pressures. These findings are significant for open data initiatives, as they underscore the critical importance of timely, high-frequency economic indicators such as INEGI’s preliminary GDP estimate. Such data enable researchers, journalists, and citizens to monitor the real-time state of the economy without having to wait for final, lagged reports. The transparency of this information empowers the public to understand complex macroeconomic trends through accessible, granular datasets that reflect immediate sectoral performance. Furthermore, the upcoming transition to a new national accounts base highlights the necessity of tracking methodological updates in public data systems. For open data advocates, this shift emphasizes the need for clear documentation and historical comparability as standards evolve. Ultimately, the article demonstrates how accessible, accurate statistical releases foster informed public discourse and accountability regarding national economic policies and their societal impacts.
