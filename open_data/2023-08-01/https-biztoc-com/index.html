Open Data
ES EN

Global debt has surged to a record high, intensifying financial instability as borrowing costs rise. This economic strain highlights the critical need for transparent, accessible data to monitor systemic risks and inform policy responses effectively. Open data initiatives can provide the empirical foundation necessary for stakeholders to assess debt sustainability and market volatility accurately. Concurrently, rapid advancements in quantum computing and artificial intelligence introduce significant security and regulatory challenges. The FBI’s investigation into AI-driven malware and the potential misuse of synthetic technologies underscore the urgency of open standards. Transparency in AI development and data handling is essential to build trust and ensure these powerful tools are deployed responsibly and safely. Furthermore, geopolitical and regulatory shifts, including new export restrictions and AI oversight proposals, are reshaping global governance. Open data serves as a vital resource for understanding these complex policy landscapes. By making government and financial information publicly available, societies can better navigate the interplay between technological innovation, economic stability, and international regulation.
