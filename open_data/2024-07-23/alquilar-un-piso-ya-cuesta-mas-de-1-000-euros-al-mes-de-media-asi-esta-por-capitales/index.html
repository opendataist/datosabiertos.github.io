Alquilar un piso ya cuesta más de 1.000 euros al mes de media: así está por capitales

Alquilar un piso ya cuesta más de 1.000 euros al mes de media: así está por capitales

Spain has reached a historic milestone: the average monthly rent for an 80-square-meter apartment has permanently surpassed one thousand euros. This sharp increase, driven by a significant contraction in the available housing supply and intense demand, imposes an unsustainable financial burden on tenants. Rent now consumes nearly half of the average national salary and over 60% for young people, highlighting a severe mismatch between income levels and housing costs that deviates drastically from recommended financial safety margins. Regional disparities reveal a deep geographic divide in housing affordability. Major economic hubs such as Madrid and Barcelona command the highest prices per square meter, while regions like Extremadura and Castilla-La Mancha remain relatively affordable. This demonstrates that access to decent housing is heavily dependent on location. This polarization suggests that the rental crisis is not uniform across the country but is concentrated in urban centers, where competition for housing is most fierce, leaving residents in other areas with more manageable, though still rising, costs. This data is crucial for open data initiatives, as it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and standardized housing statistics for public policy and civic engagement. By exposing the structural imbalances between supply, demand, and affordability, open data enables researchers and citizens to analyze trends beyond surface-level figures. It facilitates the development of evidence-based solutions to the housing crisis, fostering accountability and empowering communities to demand interventions that address the root causes of skyrocketing rents and housing insecurity.
