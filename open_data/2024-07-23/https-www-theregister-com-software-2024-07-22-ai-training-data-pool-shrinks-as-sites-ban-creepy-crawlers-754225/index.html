Open Data
ES EN

The rapid expansion of generative AI has triggered a significant shift in web accessibility, as websites increasingly restrict automated data collection through updated robots.txt files and terms of service. This backlash against AI crawlers suggests a growing tension between the open nature of the web and the restrictive measures taken to protect intellectual property. The study highlights that this change is not merely a technical adjustment but a fundamental challenge to the assumption that public availability implies consent for commercial data scraping. A primary concern for the open_data community is the resulting fragmentation of information access. As major tech companies are blocked from thousands of domains, the datasets used to train AI models become less comprehensive and potentially biased. This dynamic risks creating a more closed web ecosystem, where high-quality data held by news and social media platforms is shielded from both AI developers and benign academic or archival projects. The "collateral damage" to non-commercial crawlers underscores how current enforcement mechanisms lack the nuance to distinguish between harmful commercial exploitation and legitimate data preservation. Furthermore, the study reveals that existing protocols like robots.txt are ill-equipped to handle the complexities of AI data provenance. The misalignment between technical blocks and legal terms of service creates ambiguity, leaving many site owners and data scientists confused about permissions. For open data advocates, this underscores the urgent need for clearer, standardized frameworks that define ethical data usage. Without such improvements, the web may evolve into a more restrictive environment, hindering not only AI development but also the free exchange of information that underpins open knowledge initiatives.
