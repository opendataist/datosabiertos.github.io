More than 35k Scots have left NHS dentists to go private since 2019

Scottish Labour warns that a massive surge in patients abandoning NHS dentistry for private care signals the effective privatisation of public health services. Freedom of information data reveals that tens of thousands of Scots have left NHS registers since 2019, a trend that has accelerated dramatically in recent years. This exodus suggests that systemic failures within the public system are driving citizens toward a two-tier model, where access to essential care depends on financial capacity rather than medical need. The implications highlight a deepening inequity in healthcare access, particularly during a cost-of-living crisis. While private fees are uncapped and can be quadruple the cost of public treatment, many patients are forced to pay these premium rates to relieve severe pain. This divide creates a scenario where those who can afford to pay secure timely treatment, while others suffer without access to basic dental services. The situation underscores the critical importance of maintaining a robust, universally accessible public health infrastructure to prevent essential services from becoming commodities reserved for the wealthy. This article is highly relevant to open_data because it demonstrates how freedom of information requests can expose hidden societal trends and hold governments accountable. By accessing and analyzing specific datasets regarding patient registration changes, political opponents and researchers can quantify the impact of policy decisions and advocate for systemic reform. The transparency provided by such data allows for evidence-based debates on healthcare funding and workforce strategies, ensuring that the true scale of service deterioration is visible to the public and policymakers alike.
