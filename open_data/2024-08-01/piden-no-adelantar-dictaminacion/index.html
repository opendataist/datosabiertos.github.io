Piden no adelantar dictaminación

Civil society organizations are calling on the Mexican Congress to guarantee meaningful public participation in the debate over the proposal to abolish autonomous bodies such as the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI). They warn that hastening this discussion breaches the principles of an open parliament by sidelining civil society and academic institutions from essential scrutiny of transparency reforms. This acceleration undermines the government’s commitment to avoid fast-tracking executive initiatives, raising concerns about democratic deficits and diminished accountability. The move transfers oversight functions away from an independent institute, potentially weakening citizens’ rights to access information and protect personal data through bureaucratic absorption rather than transparent, participatory governance processes. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights threats to the institutional independence that is crucial for safeguarding data rights. When transparency bodies lose their autonomy, the integrity and accessibility of public data may be compromised, affecting citizens’ ability to access and use open government data for accountability and civic engagement.
