Biden y Lula exigen a Venezuela que publique todas las actas electorales

Biden y Lula exigen a Venezuela que publique todas las actas electorales

U.S. and Brazilian leaders demand electoral transparency in Venezuela to ensure democratic legitimacy. By calling for the full publication of voting records, these heads of state emphasize that only the detailed and verifiable disclosure of data can resolve disputes over the results and restore confidence in the process. This joint diplomatic pressure represents a coordinated effort to prevent impunity and ensure that electoral authorities are held accountable for the alleged official victory. The discrepancy between the official results and the data collected by the opposition has triggered a deep crisis and violent repression. The refusal to publish complete documentation has escalated social tensions, sparking mass protests and deaths, underscoring the urgency of accessing the original information. The lack of transparency not only fuels suspicions of fraud but also hinders any legal or political mechanism to resolve the conflict, exacerbating regional instability. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates that merely publishing a final result is insufficient for democracy. Legitimacy depends on the availability of granular data, such as individual polling station records, which enable citizen audits and independent verification. Without open and detailed access to these electoral records, accountability is impossible, making data transparency a fundamental requirement for peace and legitimate governance.
