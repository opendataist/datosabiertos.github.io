Credit in Latin America: Brazil leads with 75.1% of GDP while Venezuela falls to 3.5% - Deciphered

Credit in Latin America: Brazil leads with 75.1% of GDP while Venezuela falls to 3.5% - Deciphered

Venezuela is experiencing extreme financial paralysis, with private sector credit at minimal regional levels, far below those of its neighbors. This situation reflects a structural strangulation of the local economy, where businesses lack the necessary financing to operate or expand, severely limiting the country's economic development. The main cause lies in the strict monetary policies implemented to contain hyperinflation and currency devaluation. By imposing extremely high legal reserve requirements, the central bank drastically reduced banks' capacity to extend loans, prioritizing temporary exchange rate stability over the productive liquidity of the market. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparency in key economic indicators, such as the credit-to-GDP ratio, enables analysis of the real impact of public policies. The visibility of these data facilitates the evaluation of regulatory decisions and highlights the need for clear metrics to understand systemic crises and the lack of financial intermediation in contexts of macroeconomic instability.
