U.S. Secretary of Homeland Security threatens to prosecute those who vote illegally this Tuesday without U.S. citizenship, vowing to pursue legal action to the fullest extent against violators

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security has intensified surveillance of the voter rolls, warning that it will pursue legal action against any non-citizen who attempts to vote in the midterm elections. This stance emphasizes that participation in elections is reserved exclusively for citizens, aiming to safeguard the integrity of the democratic process by detecting and penalizing illegal voting. The significance of these measures extends beyond administrative concerns, as electoral outcomes will determine control of Congress and governability during the presidential term. In a context where immigration policy remains a central axis of public debate, the intervention of the security agency reflects the direct connection between immigration status, national security, and political participation in the United States. This scenario is critical for open data studies, as it illustrates how transparency in the management of civic records and accessibility to demographic data can serve as fundamental tools for auditing electoral inclusion and legality. The ability to monitor and analyze these information flows is essential to ensure that digital platforms and government systems protect participation rights without compromising the security of democratic infrastructure.
