More of the Same Expensive Dependence on Fossil Fuels in TEP and APS 2026 Energy Plans - CleanTechnica

More of the Same Expensive Dependence on Fossil Fuels in TEP and APS 2026 Energy Plans - CleanTechnica

Arizona utilities have released long-term energy plans that prioritize continued investment in fossil fuels, specifically natural gas and coal, rather than accelerating a transition to clean energy. Despite including some renewable resources, these strategies lock in expensive infrastructure, with major expansions planned for gas capacity and extended operations for aging coal plants. This approach contradicts public desire for a rapid shift to renewables and delays the retirement of polluting facilities, effectively prolonging reliance on carbon-intensive sources for decades. This reliance on fossil fuels carries significant economic and health implications for ratepayers. By approving new gas projects and extending coal contracts, utilities risk increasing electricity bills for residents who already face high energy costs. The plans also fail to meet previously stated carbon reduction goals, thereby exacerbating climate impacts and air pollution. Consequently, customers continue to pay the price for outdated infrastructure, missing out on potential savings and health benefits associated with a modern, clean energy grid. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the necessity of transparent utility planning and public accountability. The proposed plans are subject to public comment and regulatory review, underscoring the importance of accessible, comprehensive data regarding energy mix, costs, and emission trajectories. Open data enables communities to scrutinize these proposals, challenge misleading narratives, and demand evidence-based decisions that prioritize affordability, public health, and genuine sustainability over corporate profit.
