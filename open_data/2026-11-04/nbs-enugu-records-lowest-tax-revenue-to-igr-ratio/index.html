NBS: Enugu records lowest tax Revenue-to-IGR ratio

NBS: Enugu records lowest tax Revenue-to-IGR ratio

The analysis reveals a stark divergence in how Nigerian states generate internal revenue, highlighting a critical dependency on tax versus non-tax sources. States like Abia and the FCT derive their entire internally generated revenue from taxes, whereas others, such as Enugu, rely primarily on non-tax streams. This disparity underscores the varying economic structures and governance priorities across different regions, challenging the assumption that revenue generation follows a uniform model nationwide. Governor Peter Mbah of Enugu addressed misconceptions regarding this revenue mix, arguing that the state’s growth stems from institutional reforms and the monetization of dormant assets rather than excessive taxation. By digitizing collections and recovering underutilized state properties, the administration significantly boosted its financial capacity. This approach suggests that sustainable revenue expansion is achievable through efficiency and asset optimization, rather than simply increasing the tax burden on citizens. This data is highly relevant to the open data community because it demonstrates the necessity of granular, disaggregated public financial information. Without transparent access to specific components of state revenue—such as separating tax from non-tax sources—observers risk misinterpreting fiscal health and policy effectiveness. Open data initiatives that facilitate detailed tracking of these figures empower citizens and researchers to hold governments accountable for transparency, efficiency, and equitable resource management.
