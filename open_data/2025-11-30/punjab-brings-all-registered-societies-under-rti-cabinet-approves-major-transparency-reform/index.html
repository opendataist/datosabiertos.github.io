Punjab Brings All Registered Societies Under RTI: Cabinet Approves Major Transparency Reform

Punjab Brings All Registered Societies Under RTI: Cabinet Approves Major Transparency Reform

The Punjab government has amended the Societies Registration Act to bring all registered non-profit organizations under the Right to Information Act, establishing a critical precedent for open data in the charitable sector. This legislative shift mandates that these entities, which often handle public funds and tax-exempt resources, must now disclose their records to citizens. By removing the veil of secrecy, the reform ensures that financial audits, donor lists, and decision-making minutes are accessible to the public, fundamentally altering the transparency landscape for non-governmental organizations. Beyond information access, the law introduces strict procedural safeguards to prevent asset misappropriation and deceptive practices. Societies can no longer transfer or dispose of immovable property without prior Registrar approval, and they face mandatory re-registration to verify active compliance. These measures empower local authorities to intervene in cases of mismanagement, ensuring that institutions operate responsibly. The focus on preventing misleading names and enforcing accountability creates a structured environment where ethical governance is legally enforced rather than voluntary. This development is highly relevant to open data because it institutionalizes the flow of civic information from non-state actors to the public sphere. It demonstrates how policy can be leveraged to enhance civic oversight, allowing journalists, activists, and beneficiaries to scrutinize the use of charitable resources. The success of this model depends on citizens actively utilizing RTI rights, highlighting the symbiotic relationship between legislative transparency mandates and public engagement. Ultimately, it promises cleaner administration and renewed trust in social welfare institutions through verified data.
