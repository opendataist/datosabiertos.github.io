Propiedad intelectual en tiempos de IA: ¿quién es el dueño de lo que inventa la tecnología?

The article examines the legal challenge of defining authorship and patent rights when generative artificial intelligence plays a significant role in the creative process. As AI tools become ubiquitous in generating ideas and solutions, traditional intellectual property frameworks struggle to adapt, raising fundamental questions about whether machines can be recognized as creators or if human oversight remains the sole basis for ownership. The United States Patent and Trademark Office has clarified this ambiguity by establishing that AI must be legally classified strictly as a tool, similar to a microscope or software, rather than an inventor. Consequently, only human beings can hold the conception of an invention required for patent protection. This ruling eliminates the possibility of co-authorship with AI, ensuring that credit and legal rights always rest with the individual who directed the creative and technical process, even if the machine performed substantial computational work. This distinction is crucial for open data and AI governance, as it sets a precedent for how autonomous systems interact with legal frameworks governing information and innovation. By firmly categorizing AI as an assistive instrument rather than an independent agent, the decision protects the integrity of human-centric intellectual property laws while highlighting the urgent need for updated regulations as AI autonomy increases. It underscores that despite advanced capabilities, the "spark" of originality remains legally exclusive to humans, shaping future debates on digital ownership and data usage.
