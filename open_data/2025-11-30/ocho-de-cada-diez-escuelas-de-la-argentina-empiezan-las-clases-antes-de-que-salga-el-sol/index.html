Ocho de cada diez escuelas de la Argentina empiezan las clases antes de que salga el sol

Argentina’s inaugural National Map of Secondary School Start Times reveals a systemic mismatch between educational schedules and adolescent biology. The study identifies 7:31 AM as the typical start time, a historical norm that often forces students to attend class in darkness. This practice exacerbates sleep deprivation and social jet lag, as teenagers’ natural chronotypes shift toward later sleep patterns during puberty. Consequently, this misalignment negatively impacts cognitive performance, mental health, and academic achievement, highlighting a critical gap between current policies and scientific understanding of student well-being. The research emphasizes that while start times vary slightly by province, they largely ignore geographical and biological realities. Experts argue that delaying school starts to align better with daylight and circadian rhythms leads to measurable improvements in grades, attendance, and graduation rates, alongside reduced traffic accidents. The study also suggests that adopting Argentina’s natural geographic time zone would naturally shift school hours to daylight, offering a structural solution to the problem without merely adjusting school bells. This article is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the power of consolidating dispersed administrative data into actionable public insights. By creating the first centralized map of school schedules, researchers transformed fragmented jurisdictional records into a cohesive national picture. This case illustrates how transparent, aggregated open data can drive evidence-based policy changes, empowering citizens and policymakers to advocate for educational reforms grounded in scientific rigor rather than tradition.
