Conwy Council answering just 54 of FOI requests on time

Conwy Council answering just 54 of FOI requests on time

The article highlights a critical failure in public transparency, specifically noting that Conwy County Council missed statutory response targets for Freedom of Information requests by a significant margin. This lapse underscores a broader challenge within open data initiatives: having a strategic framework is insufficient if operational execution lacks efficiency and consistent training. The council’s reliance on disjointed systems and limited staff preparedness directly hinders its ability to provide timely public information, eroding trust during a period of rising taxes and closed-door meetings. The implications extend beyond individual bureaucratic failures to the systemic necessity of robust data governance. Without standardized processes, benchmarking, and transparent disclosure logs, organizations struggle to measure performance or identify areas for improvement. The review suggests that integrating formal training and unified digital systems is essential to bridge the gap between policy intent and actual delivery, ensuring that public bodies can meet their legal obligations effectively. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates that technical infrastructure alone does not guarantee transparency. Sustainable open data practices require cultural shifts, consistent staff education, and active benchmarking against established standards. By addressing these operational gaps, public bodies can transform from opaque entities into responsive institutions, thereby fulfilling the core purpose of open data legislation to enhance accountability and public engagement.
