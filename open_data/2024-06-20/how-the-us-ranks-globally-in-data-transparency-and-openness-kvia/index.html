How the US ranks globally in data transparency and openness - KVIA

How the US ranks globally in data transparency and openness - KVIA

The United States has experienced a significant decline in data transparency since its peak in 2016, dropping from the ninth to the twenty-second global rank. This downward trend, marked by reduced coverage of social and health statistics, was exacerbated by policy shifts during the pandemic that disrupted standard data collection practices. The erosion of these systems has undermined public trust and hindered evidence-based decision-making, highlighting how political interference can compromise the integrity of vital public health information. Globally, data openness correlates loosely with wealth but is more heavily influenced by institutional robustness and press freedom. While Nordic countries lead in transparency, some high-income nations lag significantly, whereas certain lower-income countries outperform their richer counterparts through strong statistical institutions. This disparity demonstrates that financial resources alone do not guarantee data accessibility; political will and freedom of the press are critical enablers for ensuring that collected data remains accessible and usable for accountability. For the open data community, this article underscores the fragility of statistical infrastructure and the urgent need to protect data integrity from political manipulation. It illustrates that transparency is not merely a technical metric but a foundational element of democratic accountability and public health resilience. Understanding these dynamics is essential for advocates working to establish resilient, independent data systems that serve the public interest rather than political agendas.
