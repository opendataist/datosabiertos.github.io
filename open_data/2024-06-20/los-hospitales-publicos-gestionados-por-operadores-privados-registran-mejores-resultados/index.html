Los hospitales públicos gestionados por operadores privados registran mejores resultados

Los hospitales públicos gestionados por operadores privados registran mejores resultados

A comprehensive bibliographic and official data analysis reveals that public-private partnerships in healthcare do not increase avoidable mortality, refuting political claims that question this model. On the contrary, evidence shows that hospitals managed under such agreements typically record efficiency and patient satisfaction indicators above the public sector average, demonstrating competitive performance in critical areas such as surgical waiting lists. The research contrasts the quality of available scientific evidence with recent political interpretations, demonstrating that international studies cited to justify restrictions on the private sector lack a clear causal relationship between outsourcing and poorer health outcomes. By showing that other factors, such as management capacity and the institutional environment, determine the success of these models, the study underscores the need to base healthcare policies on rigorous and verifiable data. This finding is crucial for the open data movement, as it illustrates how transparency in access to public information allows ideological narratives to be tested against empirical realities. By democratizing knowledge through accessible health indicators, citizens and academia can audit the actual performance of health services, fostering accountability and evidence-based decision-making grounded in tangible and open data.
