¿Cuántos puestos de trabajo se perdieron en la construcción?

The article concludes that President Javier Milei’s decision to halt public works has caused a severe contraction in the construction sector, leading to the loss of approximately 100,000 formal jobs in just a few months. This economic shock stems primarily from the near-total paralysis of state-funded infrastructure projects, despite initial assurances that advanced contracts would be maintained. The rapid decline highlights the immediate and drastic impact of austerity measures on employment, creating a significant gap in labor market stability. The narrative emphasizes that the private sector cannot currently compensate for the lack of public investment, as private infrastructure funding rarely exceeds 15% of the total globally, even in economically stable countries. Consequently, the burden has shifted to provincial governments operating with their own funds and large private enterprises, which are the only forces sustaining the remaining workforce. This disparity reveals a structural vulnerability where reliance on private capital alone is insufficient to maintain industry volume or prevent widespread unemployment in regions dependent on state contracts. This content is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical importance of timely and accessible statistical reporting in understanding economic shifts. The article notes that official data from the National Institute of Statistics and Censuses is lagging by three months, delaying the confirmation of job losses. This delay hinders real-time policy analysis and public accountability, underscoring the need for more transparent, immediate data dissemination to accurately assess the true impact of governmental decisions on the labor market.
