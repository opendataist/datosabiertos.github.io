Microsoft admits no guarantee of sovereignty for UK policing data | Computer Weekly

Microsoft’s admission that it cannot guarantee the sovereignty of UK policing data hosted on its public cloud infrastructure exposes a critical gap between corporate service models and strict legal requirements. The company acknowledges that data processing and support inherently involve international transfers, directly conflicting with laws mandating that law enforcement data remain within the UK. This revelation highlights the fundamental incompatibility between hyperscale cloud architectures and the specific data residency obligations imposed on public sector entities. The implications for open data and public accountability are significant, as this breach undermines trust in the integrity of digital evidence systems. When governments cannot verify the physical location or legal jurisdiction of sensitive information, the reliability of public records is compromised. This situation suggests that current open data initiatives relying on commercial cloud providers may inadvertently facilitate unlawful data handling, eroding the transparency and security foundations necessary for effective civic oversight. This case serves as a vital warning for the broader open data community regarding the hidden complexities of cloud computing. It demonstrates that technical infrastructure choices have profound legal consequences, often obscured by generic terms of service that fail to address specific national regulations. Consequently, organizations must rigorously audit cloud providers to ensure they do not violate statutory data protection laws, as assuming compliance based on general privacy policies is no longer sufficient.
