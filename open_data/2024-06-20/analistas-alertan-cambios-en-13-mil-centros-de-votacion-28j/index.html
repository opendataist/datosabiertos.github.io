Analistas alertan cambios en 13 mil centros de votación 28J

A study reveals that most polling centers in Venezuela face moderate or high risks, threatening the integrity and transparency of the elections. The study highlights two critical dimensions: administrative decisions by the electoral authority that facilitate fraud, such as the creation of small polling centers or unexplained changes in voters’ locations, and atypical electoral patterns. These include abstention rates exceeding 50 percent or abnormally high turnout, which suggest a possible lack of freedom in exercising the right to vote. The findings indicate a direct correlation between increased electoral risk and higher votes for the ruling party. This suggests that the administrative and statistical conditions identified as risky systematically favor one of the main political forces, while the opposition has less presence in high-risk areas. The concentration of these factors in border and southern states amplifies concerns about democratic legitimacy in key regions. This analysis is crucial for the open data movement, demonstrating how the critical use of public electoral data can expose systemic vulnerabilities. By transforming administrative and historical records into evidence of risk, it promotes accountability and citizen oversight. The ability to identify biased patterns through information transparency is essential for defending electoral freedom and ensuring authentic political processes in contexts of high political tension.
