Más de 100.000 personas se censaron digitalmente en los primeros tres días - Diario Hoy En la noticia

The National Institute of Statistics (INE) reports a positive start to the 2023 Census, with significant citizen engagement during the initial digital phase. This mass participation highlights a strong societal willingness to contribute to national data collection, a critical foundation for reliable demographic tracking. The enthusiasm suggests that effective communication strategies and the convenience of online access are successfully driving public involvement in this mandatory exercise. The primary objective of this census extends far beyond mere population counting; it serves as the essential empirical basis for designing equitable public policies. Accurate data regarding household distribution and living conditions directly informs decision-making processes, such as determining where to allocate resources for schools and community centers. Without this comprehensive dataset, the government would lack the necessary evidence to address territorial inequalities and social needs effectively. This data is fundamentally relevant to the open data movement because it represents the largest structured dataset concerning the population’s structure and needs. Transparent and accessible census data enables researchers, journalists, and civil society to scrutinize government planning and advocate for informed policy adjustments. By publishing these findings, the state supports an evidence-based ecosystem where citizens can verify how public services are distributed, fostering greater accountability and societal trust in institutional data governance.
