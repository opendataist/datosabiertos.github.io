Policing bill for most recent street party near Dalhousie campus was $82K | CBC News

High policing costs associated with unauthorized street parties near Dalhousie University have sparked public concern regarding the strain on municipal resources and emergency services. These events, which draw thousands of attendees, result in significant expenditures for law enforcement and highlight a tension between student social activities and community safety, raising questions about the equitable allocation of public funds. The incident underscores the critical role of open data in ensuring institutional accountability. By obtaining policing costs and internal communications through freedom of information requests, residents and journalists were able to scrutinize the university’s response and financial transparency. This access to information allowed the public to challenge the administration’s narrative, revealing discrepancies between official statements and the reality on the ground, and demonstrating how data transparency can empower communities to demand better governance. Ultimately, while authorities noted some improvements in safety compared to previous years, the persistence of these issues over six years suggests that current mitigation strategies, such as alternative campus events, are insufficient. The situation illustrates that without rigorous data collection and open sharing of operational metrics and outcomes, universities may continue to rely on repetitive public relations messaging rather than implementing effective, evidence-based solutions to complex social problems.
