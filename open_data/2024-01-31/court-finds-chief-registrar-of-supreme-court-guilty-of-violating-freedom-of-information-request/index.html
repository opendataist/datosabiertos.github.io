Court Finds Chief Registrar Of Supreme Court Guilty Of Violating Freedom Of Information Request

The Federal High Court ruled that the Chief Registrar of the Supreme Court violated the Freedom of Information Act by failing to respond to a request within the mandated timeframe. While the court acknowledged the breach, it declined to impose the statutory fine, interpreting the term “conviction” as strictly applicable to criminal cases rather than civil suits. This discrepancy has prompted the petitioner to appeal, arguing that civil judgments should carry financial consequences to ensure accountability and remedy for the injured party. The ruling highlights a critical legal ambiguity regarding enforcement mechanisms in civil litigation involving public institutions. By separating the finding of guilt from the application of penalties, the court created a precedent where institutions may be declared wrongdoers without facing tangible repercussions. This raises significant concerns about the efficacy of transparency laws if they lack robust deterrents, potentially undermining the intent to hold powerful bodies answerable for administrative delays or non-compliance. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates because it tests the enforceability of access-to-information laws in Nigeria’s highest judicial body. It underscores the urgent need for legal frameworks that ensure consistent accountability, demonstrating that declaring an entity’s fault is insufficient without meaningful sanctions. Strengthening these mechanisms is essential to maintain public trust and ensure that transparency rights are not merely theoretical but practically enforceable against even the most prominent state institutions.
