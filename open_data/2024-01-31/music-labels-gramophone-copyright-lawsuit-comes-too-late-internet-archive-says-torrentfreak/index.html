Music Labels 'Gramophone' Copyright Lawsuit Comes Too Late, Internet Archive Says * TorrentFreak

Music Labels 'Gramophone' Copyright Lawsuit Comes Too Late, Internet Archive Says * TorrentFreak

The lawsuit against the Internet Archive regarding the Great 78 Project highlights a critical tension between digital preservation efforts and copyright enforcement. This case is vital to the open data community because it tests whether non-profit archiving of obsolete formats qualifies as fair use, potentially setting a precedent for how cultural heritage can be preserved without commercial permission. If the archive’s actions are deemed infringing, it threatens the ability of institutions to maintain access to historically significant, non-commercialized data assets. The Internet Archive argues that these recordings are at risk of physical decay and must be preserved to maintain cultural memory, asserting that their digitization serves a transformative, educational purpose. By claiming fair use, the organization emphasizes that the unique audio qualities of these obsolete medium constitute part of the public’s shared history. This perspective challenges the notion that all digital reproductions require explicit licensing, advocating for a broader interpretation of user rights in the context of historical preservation. Furthermore, the dispute centers on legal technicalities, specifically the statute of limitations, with the archive arguing that the plaintiff’s delay in filing renders many claims invalid. This procedural defense aims to significantly narrow the scope of potential damages and liability. For open data advocates, this outcome is crucial as it determines whether proactive takedown notifications sufficiently notify rights holders of infringement, or if immediate legal action is mandatory, thereby influencing future strategies for managing open-access repositories.
