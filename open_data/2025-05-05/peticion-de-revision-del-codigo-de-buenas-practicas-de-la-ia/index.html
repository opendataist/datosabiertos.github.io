Petición de revisión del Código de Buenas Prácticas de la IA

Petición de revisión del Código de Buenas Prácticas de la IA

The Spanish Minister of Culture has formally requested a review of the draft Code of Good Practice for General AI, arguing that the current text fails to align with European intellectual property laws and fundamental rights. By reducing provider obligations to mere "reasonable efforts," the draft effectively renders compliance voluntary, thereby undermining legal standards and the protection of creators' rights. This critique highlights a critical tension in open data practices, where the transparency of AI training sources is insufficiently mandated. The lack of strict requirements for disclosing data origins creates legal uncertainty and weakens the enforcement of copyright, posing significant risks to the cultural sector. This approach contradicts the spirit of recent AI regulations that aim to ensure fairness and accountability in technological development. The article is relevant to open data because it underscores the urgent need for rigorous transparency regarding the data used to train AI systems. Without clear mandates on data provenance, open data initiatives risk being exploited to circumvent intellectual property laws. Ensuring that AI developers openly disclose their training sources is essential for maintaining trust, protecting creative rights, and establishing a fair, legally compliant framework for digital innovation across Europe.
