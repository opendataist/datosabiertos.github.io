55º Periodo de Sesiones de la Comisión de Estadística de la ONU

The article highlights the United Nations Statistical Commission’s pivotal decision to expand its membership to ensure more equitable geographical representation, particularly for developing nations. This structural shift aims to enhance the inclusivity and global legitimacy of international statistical governance, reflecting a broader commitment to diverse participation in shaping global data standards. A central focus is the refinement of indicators supporting the 2030 Sustainable Development Agenda. Delegates emphasized maintaining statistical stability to avoid overburdening national systems while improving data inclusivity. There is a strong recognition that reliable, timely, and disaggregated social and demographic statistics remain a significant challenge, especially for developing countries, requiring sustained international cooperation to strengthen national statistical capacities. Furthermore, the text underscores the critical role of census data in effective development planning and policy monitoring. It calls for collective efforts to resolve conflicts hindering data collection in unstable regions and announces Equatorial Guinea’s upcoming census. This is relevant to open data because it reinforces the necessity of standardized, high-quality, and accessible official statistics as the foundation for transparent governance, evidence-based policymaking, and global accountability.
