5 things to know for May 22: Embassy shooting, Budget cuts, Trump-Ramaphosa meeting, Qatar jet, Police reform

The article highlights a critical legal battle over transparency regarding the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE). The Trump administration is seeking Supreme Court intervention to determine if DOGE can withhold public records under executive privilege, bypassing the Freedom of Information Act. This dispute arises because DOGE has drastically reduced the federal workforce and cut agency budgets, prompting numerous lawsuits. For open data advocates, this represents a significant threat to government accountability, as the potential exemption of such a powerful new entity from disclosure laws could obscure decision-making processes and hinder public oversight of federal operations. In parallel, the administration is moving to dismiss federal consent decrees with major police departments, effectively ending federal oversight of misconduct. While the article covers various political and diplomatic events, the erosion of these monitoring systems compounds the concerns regarding transparency. The combination of reduced workforce oversight and withdrawn federal mandates creates an environment where institutional knowledge and operational data may be lost or hidden, making it increasingly difficult for researchers and the public to access reliable information about government performance and ethical standards. This context is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the fragility of public access to information when executive power is expanded without legislative checks. If agencies like DOGE succeed in shielding their records, the resulting data vacuum will impede academic research, journalistic inquiry, and citizen engagement. Protecting open data frameworks is essential to ensuring that significant structural changes to the government remain visible, auditable, and subject to democratic scrutiny, rather than concealed behind claims of executive privilege.
