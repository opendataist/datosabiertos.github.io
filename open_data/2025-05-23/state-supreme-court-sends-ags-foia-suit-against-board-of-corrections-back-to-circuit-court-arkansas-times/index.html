State Supreme Court sends AG’s FOIA suit against Board of Corrections back to circuit court - Arkansas Times

State Supreme Court sends AG’s FOIA suit against Board of Corrections back to circuit court - Arkansas Times

The Arkansas Supreme Court overturned a lower court’s dismissal of Attorney General Tim Griffin’s lawsuit, ruling that the trial judge abused his discretion by penalizing Griffin for failing to comply with an impossible order. The high court determined that the Attorney General lacked the legal authority to unilaterally authorize funding for outside counsel without approval from the Governor or Legislative Council. Consequently, the initial directive for Griffin to facilitate this hiring was legally unenforceable, rendering the subsequent dismissal of the case erroneous and necessitating a return to trial proceedings. At its core, this dispute highlights significant tensions between executive oversight and independent state entities regarding transparency and legal representation. Griffin alleged that the Board of Corrections violated open-government laws by hiring outside counsel without public notice, while the Board claimed the Attorney General had a conflict of interest. By reversing the dismissal, the court ensures that these allegations of procedural violations can be heard on their merits, rather than being terminated on technical grounds related to funding jurisdiction. This ruling is relevant to open_data and open_government principles because it reinforces the accountability of state agencies under Freedom of Information Act statutes. It clarifies that procedural hurdles or jurisdictional disputes cannot be used to shield administrative actions from scrutiny. By allowing the suit to proceed, the decision supports the broader goal of ensuring that government operations, particularly those involving significant legal expenditures and hiring decisions, remain transparent and subject to judicial review under the law.
