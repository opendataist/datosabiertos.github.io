Generative AI Needs to Respect Artists

Generative AI Needs to Respect Artists

Governments in the US and UK are currently debating whether to allow generative AI firms to use copyrighted creative works for training data without compensation. While US courts remain skeptical of tech companies’ fair use claims, the UK government appears inclined to support the industry, highlighting a significant global divergence in how intellectual property rights are interpreted in the context of artificial intelligence development. This regulatory ambiguity creates profound transparency challenges for open data initiatives, as AI training datasets remain largely opaque. Without mandatory disclosure of source materials, it is nearly impossible for individual artists to verify if their work was incorporated or to seek appropriate remuneration. This lack of visibility undermines the core principles of open data, which rely on clear attribution, verifiability, and the ability for creators to control how their digital assets are utilized by commercial entities. Ultimately, the article argues that AI development must respect copyright holders by either compensating creators or honoring opt-out requests. The push for dataset transparency is essential to ensure accountability and fair treatment of original authors. For the open data community, this case underscores the urgent need to balance technological innovation with ethical standards, ensuring that data usage does not exploit creative labor while failing to provide the transparency necessary for trust and reuse.
