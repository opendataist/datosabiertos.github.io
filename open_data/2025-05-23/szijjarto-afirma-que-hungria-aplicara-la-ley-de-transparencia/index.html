Szijjártó afirma que Hungría aplicará la ley de transparencia

Szijjártó afirma que Hungría aplicará la ley de transparencia

Hungary is advancing a Transparency Law aimed at restricting foreign funding for NGOs and the press, framing it as a defense against external interference rather than a suppression of civil society. The government argues that such measures are necessary to protect national sovereignty from what they describe as aggressive espionage and propaganda, particularly citing Ukrainian activities, despite European concerns about the law’s potential to stigmatize and financially destroy independent organizations. This initiative highlights the tension between state control and journalistic independence, raising critical questions about the health of democratic institutions and free speech within the European Union. The controversy underscores how legislative tools can be repurposed to target dissent, creating a chilling effect on transparency and accountability mechanisms that are essential for open societies and robust public discourse. The article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the political risks associated with information accessibility. When governments restrict funding for organizations that often rely on or produce transparent data to monitor public affairs, they undermine the ecosystem of independent scrutiny. This dynamic threatens the availability of unbiased information, complicating efforts to maintain a free flow of data necessary for civic engagement and democratic oversight.
