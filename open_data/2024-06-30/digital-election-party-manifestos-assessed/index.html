Digital election: party manifestos assessed

The recent election manifestos highlight a significant regulatory gap, as the anticipated data protection reform bill was abandoned. With no party proposing immediate changes to the current UK GDPR framework, the future of information governance remains uncertain. This stagnation creates a complex environment for data professionals, who must navigate an evolving landscape without clear legislative guidance or the streamlined, business-friendly system previously planned by the outgoing government. Liberal Democrats stand out by advocating for robust statutory AI regulation and strict privacy protections, including bans on bulk communications data collection and biometric surveillance. In contrast, the Conservatives favor a non-statutory, sector-specific approach focused on innovation, while Labour promises targeted regulation for powerful AI models and improved data sharing through a National Data Library. These divergent strategies reflect competing visions for balancing technological advancement with individual rights and public safety. Relevance to open data lies in the tension between transparency and controlled access. While the Liberal Democrats propose enhancing governmental transparency through immediate messaging records, the general absence of Freedom of Information proposals across all major parties signals a potential retreat from openness. This lack of commitment to FOI and varied approaches to data interoperability suggest that open data initiatives may face headwinds, as political priorities lean toward security and innovation rather than the broad, unhindered access to public information.
