La CE envía una solicitud de información a Meta en virtud del Reglamento de Servicios Digitales

La CE envía una solicitud de información a Meta en virtud del Reglamento de Servicios Digitales

The European Commission is ramping up its scrutiny of Meta, demanding greater transparency regarding how the company ensures public access to data and maintains independent tools for monitoring electoral discourse. This investigation arises from concerns that Meta’s handling of political disinformation and its failure to provide adequate electoral oversight may violate the Digital Services Act in the run-up to the European elections. Meta recently expanded its CrowdTangle monitoring capabilities but later rolled back to a less robust version, rekindling criticism from Brussels. The regulator is seeking detailed information about Meta’s APIs and content libraries to verify compliance with open data obligations, stressing the need for reliable, independent mechanisms to track public discourse and prevent misinformation from influencing democratic processes. This development is significant for open data advocates, as it highlights the tension between platform opacity and regulatory demands for transparency. It underscores the urgent need for tech giants to provide meaningful, accessible data interfaces for public scrutiny. The potential for severe sanctions if Meta fails to cooperate demonstrates the legal weight behind open data principles, setting a precedent for holding powerful platforms accountable for the information they control and for how that information is shared with the public and researchers.
