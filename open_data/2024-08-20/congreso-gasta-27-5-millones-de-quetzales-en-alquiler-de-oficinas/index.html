Congreso gasta 27.5 millones de quetzales en alquiler de oficinas

Congreso gasta 27.5 millones de quetzales en alquiler de oficinas

Guatemala’s Legislative Body is facing a structural crisis due to its reliance on 34 rental contracts to compensate for the insufficiency of its own facilities, incurring millions of dollars in expenses spread across several dispersed buildings. This operational fragmentation not only entails high costs but also unequal working conditions and security risks, highlighting the urgent need to consolidate into a single headquarters that guarantees adequate and secure infrastructure for parliamentary activities. The relevance of this case for open data lies in the proactive transparency of public budgetary and contractual information. By making detailed information accessible regarding the amounts, validity periods, and terms of these agreements, civil society can audit public management. The accessibility of this data facilitates scrutiny over spending efficiency and the proper allocation of resources, demonstrating how the open publication of administrative information is fundamental to accountability and the detection of structural inefficiencies. In conclusion, the dependence on external rentals underscores the pressing need to construct a dedicated headquarters, a project that has been delayed for decades due to logistical and land-related challenges. Transparency in disclosing these current expenses and conditions provides the necessary evidence to drive public policies that prioritize investment in permanent legislative infrastructure, ensuring that public resources are used sustainably, securely, and efficiently in the long term.
