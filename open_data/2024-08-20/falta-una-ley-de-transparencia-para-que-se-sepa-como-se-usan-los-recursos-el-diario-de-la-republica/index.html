“Falta una ley de transparencia para que se sepa cómo se usan los recursos” | El Diario de la República

“Falta una ley de transparencia para que se sepa cómo se usan los recursos” | El Diario de la República

The article underscores the urgent need for judicial financial autonomy to guarantee institutional independence and operational effectiveness. Currently, the judiciary suffers from severe fiscal dependence on the executive branch, with frozen budgets failing to keep pace with inflation. This lack of fiscal self-sufficiency hinders the recruitment of necessary staff and the implementation of competitive salaries, directly compromising the quality of justice and citizens’ access to legal services. Transparency and direct resource allocation are presented as essential solutions to break the cycle of political negotiation over judicial funds. By mandating that a specific portion of provincial coparticipation revenues flows directly to the judiciary, the system could achieve greater predictability and fairness. This approach ensures that courts have stable resources to function properly, reducing administrative bottlenecks and allowing the institution to operate without being held hostage by executive budget approvals or delays. This case is highly relevant to open data, as it highlights the critical link between fiscal transparency and institutional integrity. The argument that public resources belong to citizens and require transparent tracking implies that open data initiatives should include detailed, accessible information on government spending and revenue distribution. Without open, real-time visibility into how these funds are managed and allocated, society cannot effectively monitor whether essential state functions, such as justice, are being adequately funded, thereby hindering democratic accountability and informed public debate.
