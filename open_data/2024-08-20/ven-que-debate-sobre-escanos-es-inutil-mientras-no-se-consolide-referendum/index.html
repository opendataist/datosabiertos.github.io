Ven que debate sobre escaños es inútil mientras no se consolide referéndum

The article concludes that the distribution of parliamentary seats in Bolivia will legally be determined by the latest census data, rejecting the President’s proposal to hold a referendum on this issue. Political analysts, electoral authorities, and opposition sectors agree that the Constitution mandates redistributing seats based on population figures, meaning any deviation requires a complex constitutional amendment. Consequently, the government’s attempt to delay or alter this process through a referendum is viewed as politically irresponsible and legally unfeasible without first following strict procedural norms. Critics argue that the President is using the referendum proposal as an excuse to postpone the official release of census results and to manipulate political representation in favor of allied regions. This strategy is seen as an evasion of administrative responsibility that ignores the clear legal framework established by the Constitution. Experts warn that altering the number of deputies or their distribution without proper constitutional reform will likely trigger significant regional conflicts and dissatisfaction among departments expecting fair representation based on their actual population size. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the tension between statistical evidence and political convenience. The article underscores that census data, once collected and made public, carries mandatory legal weight in governance decisions. Ignoring these transparent, objective metrics in favor of arbitrary political maneuvers undermines the rule of law and public trust in democratic institutions, demonstrating that open data is not just a technical resource but a critical foundation for equitable political representation and legal compliance.
