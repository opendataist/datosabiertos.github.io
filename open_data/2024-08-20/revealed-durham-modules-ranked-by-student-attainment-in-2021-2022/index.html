Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

This article highlights how Freedom of Information requests can unlock granular academic data, revealing significant disparities in student attainment across different disciplines. By analyzing undergraduate module results, the report identifies specific departments, such as Physics and English, that lead in average marks and first-class honors. Conversely, it exposes areas like foundation years and certain foreign language modules where student performance lags considerably. This transparency allows stakeholders to scrutinize institutional effectiveness and identify potential gaps in teaching support or assessment difficulty. The analysis further breaks down performance by year level, uncovering stark contrasts between specific modules. For instance, while some first-year courses struggle to award a single first-class grade, others in the same cohort achieve near-universal success. This level of detail demonstrates that aggregate university rankings often mask underlying issues within individual subjects. Understanding these micro-level variations is crucial for identifying whether low attainment stems from student preparedness, pedagogical challenges, or assessment design. This dataset is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it exemplifies the practical value of accessible public sector information. When educational institutions release raw performance metrics, it empowers prospective students, parents, and researchers to make informed decisions based on evidence rather than reputation. Furthermore, it encourages accountability within higher education, suggesting that open data can drive meaningful improvements in educational outcomes by fostering greater scrutiny of how resources and teaching quality are distributed across different fields of study.
