Emergency Care Waiting Time Statistics (April - June 2023)

The Department of Health’s latest statistics reveal a complex picture of Northern Ireland’s emergency care system, highlighting both operational pressures and structural shifts in patient pathways. A significant finding is the emergence and impact of new urgent care services, such as PhoneFirst and Urgent Care Centres, which successfully diverted thousands of patients away from traditional Emergency Departments. This diversion suggests a strategic improvement in resource allocation, ensuring patients receive appropriate care outside of high-pressure ED environments while slightly increasing direct ED attendances compared to the previous year. Performance metrics against waiting time targets indicate mixed results, with notable disparities across different types of emergency departments. While smaller facilities maintained high efficiency rates, major hospitals struggled to keep patient throughput within the desired four-hour window, resulting in a rise in patients waiting over twelve hours. Although median times for triage and treatment initiation showed modest improvements, the extended duration for admitted patients points to systemic bottlenecks that require further investigation and potential policy intervention to streamline the patient journey. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it exemplifies the critical need for transparent, accessible, and longitudinal health statistics. By making granular details about waiting times, attendance trends, and service usage publicly available, stakeholders can better analyze healthcare efficiency and equity. Such transparency empowers researchers, policymakers, and citizens to hold health providers accountable and drive evidence-based improvements in public health infrastructure.
