Economía de México sigue creciendo, 3,6% interanual en segundo trimestre

Mexico’s economy showed continued positive momentum in the second quarter of 2023, driven primarily by strong performance in the services sector. While the government celebrates these results as evidence of stability, independent analysts caution that current growth rates remain modest compared to pre-pandemic levels and lag behind those of regional peers. This divergence highlights that, despite recent successes, the economy has not yet fully realized its potential or recovered to previous historical peaks. Significant macroeconomic risks persist, particularly concerning high interest rates intended to curb inflation. These elevated borrowing costs are expected to dampen short-term investment and consumption, creating uncertainty for future economic expansion. Although inflation has moderated, it remains above official targets, prompting the central bank to maintain a restrictive monetary stance. This policy trade-off suggests that while price stability is being pursued, it may simultaneously hinder dynamic economic activity and limit consumer purchasing power in the near term. This article is relevant to open data because it relies on official statistics from Mexico’s National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) and monetary reports from the central bank. Understanding how raw economic data is collected, seasonally adjusted, and interpreted is crucial for distinguishing between official narratives and analytical critiques. Access to such transparent, granular datasets allows citizens and researchers to verify claims, assess the true health of specific sectors, and evaluate the real-world impact of monetary policies on society.
