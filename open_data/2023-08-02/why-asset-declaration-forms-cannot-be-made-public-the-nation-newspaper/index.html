‘Why asset declaration forms cannot be made public’ | The Nation Newspaper

The Court of Appeal clarified that while public interest groups possess the legal standing to challenge government transparency issues, the Freedom of Information Act does not grant unrestricted public access to public officers' asset declarations. The court ruled that the Code of Conduct Bureau holds the exclusive authority to verify these documents, meaning citizens cannot independently access or audit them without specific authorization from the Bureau. This decision establishes that privacy protections and institutional mandates override general public curiosity regarding personal financial details of officials. This ruling is highly relevant to the open data community as it delineates the boundaries of public information rights in governance. It highlights that transparency initiatives are often mediated by specific regulatory bodies rather than being fully open by default. Consequently, advocates for open data must navigate the legal distinction between general public records and sensitive, verification-specific documents, understanding that access may require formal collaboration with designated state agencies rather than direct public retrieval.
