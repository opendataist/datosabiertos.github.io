Las pernoctaciones extrahoteleras suben un 9,1% y son un 7,4% más que en 2019

Las pernoctaciones extrahoteleras suben un 9,1% y son un 7,4% más que en 2019

Non-hotel tourism accommodation in Spain saw overall growth in the first half of the year compared to the previous year, surpassing 2019 levels. However, June experienced a decline, primarily driven by a sharp drop in domestic travelers. Although international visits increased slightly, the average length of stay decreased across all sectors, indicating a shift in traveler behavior toward shorter trips. A clear divergence emerged between different types of accommodation: apartments and campsites faced lower occupancy rates and fewer stays, while rural tourism experienced modest growth. Prices rose across the board in these sectors, suggesting that demand remains resilient despite lower volumes, particularly among non-resident travelers, who continue to dominate the market share for private rentals and camping sites. This data is relevant to open data initiatives as it underscores the need for granular, sector-specific statistical analysis. By disaggregating data by nationality, accommodation type, and region, policymakers can better understand shifting tourism dynamics and price pressures. Access to such detailed, structured datasets enables more accurate economic forecasting and targeted infrastructure planning, ensuring that the benefits of tourism are distributed effectively across different geographical areas and business models.
