New Brunswick Education Department received no complaints about pronouns kept secret - New Brunswick | Globalnews.ca

Freedom of information requests have revealed a stark contradiction regarding recent policy changes in New Brunswick’s education system. While the Education Minister justified a review of gender identity protocols by claiming to receive hundreds of parental complaints, official records show no such written concerns existed. This absence of documented feedback suggests the stated rationale for the policy overhaul was unjustified and likely exaggerated, highlighting a disconnect between public claims and administrative reality. The revised policy restricts students under sixteen from changing their names or pronouns without parental consent and limits bathroom access, moving away from the previous student-centered approach. Critics and youth advocates argue these changes are discriminatory and lack evidence of widespread support, as only a negligible number of tangential complaints were ever recorded. This shift prioritizes parental authority over student autonomy, potentially creating a hostile environment for LGBTQ youth without addressing any verified safety or educational concerns. This case is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency mechanisms can expose political narratives that lack factual grounding. By making internal records publicly accessible, researchers and advocates can verify government claims and hold leaders accountable for decisions based on fabricated or misinterpreted data. Ultimately, it underscores the vital role of open data in ensuring that public policy is driven by genuine community feedback rather than political expediency.
