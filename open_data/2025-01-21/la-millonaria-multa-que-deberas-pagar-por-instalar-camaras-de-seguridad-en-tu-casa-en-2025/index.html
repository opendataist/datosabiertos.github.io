La millonaria multa que deberás pagar por instalar cámaras de seguridad en tu casa en 2025

La millonaria multa que deberás pagar por instalar cámaras de seguridad en tu casa en 2025

The article warns that installing home security cameras in Mexico can result in severe fines under federal data protection laws, especially when footage is shared publicly. Authorities stress that revealing the faces of individuals—whether they are perpetrators or innocent bystanders—poses significant privacy risks. As a result, the law imposes strict regulations to prevent unauthorized dissemination of data, highlighting the tension between personal security measures and the right to privacy. Relevant to open data, this situation underscores the critical importance of controlling personal information within digital ecosystems. Even private entities must comply with regulations that restrict how personal identifiers are collected and shared, demonstrating that data governance extends beyond public institutions. This case serves as a reminder that unregulated data distribution can have serious legal consequences, reinforcing the need for ethical data-handling practices in both the public and private sectors to protect citizens’ rights. To avoid penalties, residents are advised to configure cameras to record only from the outside inward and to display clear signage notifying people that they are being filmed. These measures align with best practices for transparency and minimal intrusion, which are foundational principles in open data frameworks. By respecting these boundaries, individuals contribute to a culture of responsible data use, ensuring that technological tools enhance security without compromising fundamental privacy standards.
