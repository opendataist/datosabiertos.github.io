Residentes que viven en urbanizaciones de Menorca todo el año: «No lo cambio por nada»

Residentes que viven en urbanizaciones de Menorca todo el año: «No lo cambio por nada»

The article highlights a significant demographic shift in Menorca, where residential urbanizations such as Cala en Porter and Cales Piques now have more registered inhabitants than traditional town centers. This trend reflects a growing preference for coastal living, driven by the desire for tranquility and the economic benefits offered by the tourism sector, particularly during the off-season. Although these areas have fewer permanent residents than urban nuclei, they have evolved into substantial communities, challenging the conventional hierarchy of population distribution on the island. Residents acknowledge the lack of essential public services, such as reliable transportation, commercial amenities, and adequate infrastructure maintenance. However, these inconveniences are outweighed by the quality of life they provide, characterized by peace, natural beauty, and a strong sense of community. The decision to live in these areas is often a conscious trade-off, where individuals prioritize silence and proximity to the sea over the convenience of urban life, accepting limitations such as poor internet coverage or limited winter mobility as part of a desirable lifestyle. This phenomenon is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of granular, location-specific datasets for effective municipal planning. Standard urban metrics may fail to capture the unique needs of dispersed tourist-residential hubs, leading to inadequate service allocation. By analyzing precise registration and usage data in these specific locations, administrators can better understand the real-time demands for infrastructure, transport, and safety, ensuring that digital governance tools reflect the actual living conditions of these emerging communities rather than relying on outdated or aggregated population figures.
