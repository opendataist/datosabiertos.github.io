En octubre, Chaco tuvo un aumento interanual de 390% en transferencias no automáticas

An analysis of the distribution of non-automatic transfers across provinces reveals a model of discretionary public spending that operates outside predetermined fiscal mechanisms. The consulting firm Politikon Chaco highlights that these allocations, which are linked to specific circumstances such as climate emergencies or infrastructure needs, have experienced real growth significantly outpacing inflation, suggesting a political priority distinct from the automatic transfers regulated by law. The relevance to open data lies in the importance of monitoring these opaque or less-regulated financial flows. By making visible the amounts and disproportionate increases in certain jurisdictions, such as La Pampa or Buenos Aires, it becomes possible to audit the transparency of state spending. This visibility is crucial for distinguishing between legal obligations and subjective political decisions, facilitating more rigorous public scrutiny of the equity and efficiency of the nation’s common fund. In conclusion, these exposed data underscore the need for transparency platforms that break down the origin and justification of discretionary resources. Only through open access to this detailed information by province and category can civil society and researchers assess whether the state is adequately responding to genuine emergencies or simply distributing resources arbitrarily, thereby ensuring more responsible and democratic fiscal management.
