2023 será "casi seguro" el año más caluroso desde que hay datos, según Copernicus

October 2023 set global temperature records, indicating that the year is likely to be the hottest on record. This unprecedented heat, driven by factors including El Niño, underscores the accelerating pace of climate change and the critical need for urgent, ambitious action at the upcoming COP28 conference. The data reveals significant environmental impacts, such as record-low ice coverage in Antarctica and the Arctic, alongside extreme weather events like flooding in Europe and droughts in other regions. These findings highlight the increasing volatility of global weather patterns and the tangible consequences of rising temperatures on ecosystems and human populations. This article is relevant to open_data because the conclusions rely on massive, diverse datasets gathered from satellites, ships, and weather stations worldwide. Copernicus demonstrates how integrating billions of open data points through cybernetic analysis provides essential, transparent evidence for scientific understanding and policy-making, showcasing the power of accessible data in addressing global challenges.
