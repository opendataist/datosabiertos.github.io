Uno de cada cuatro alumnos repite curso antes de los 15 años

The article highlights a critical paradox in Castilla y León’s education system, where high international test scores contrast sharply with a persistent one-in-four student repetition rate among compulsory education ages. This discrepancy reveals that while overall academic competence is strong, systemic retention remains a significant structural issue, suggesting that standardized testing metrics may not fully capture the daily challenges students face in progressing through school levels. The persistence of repetition, particularly during primary education, challenges the legislative intent of the new educational law, which positions grade retention as a last resort rather than a standard practice. High failure rates indicate that current pedagogical strategies and resource allocation are insufficient to support at-risk students effectively. Consequently, there is a pressing need for more individualized attention and reduced class sizes to ensure students master material before advancing, rather than carrying gaps in their knowledge forward. This data is vital for open data advocates because it exposes the gap between aggregate quality indicators and granular student outcomes. By making detailed repetition and promotion statistics transparent and comparable, researchers can better analyze the effectiveness of educational policies. Such transparency allows for evidence-based reforms that prioritize early intervention and personalized support, ensuring that high-level achievements do not mask systemic inequalities in student progression.
