Un 59,6% de los españoles ve insuficientes los datos del apagón

Un 59,6% de los españoles ve insuficientes los datos del apagón

The article highlights a significant crisis of confidence in government transparency during the April 28 blackout, with the majority of citizens judging official communications as inadequate. This perception of insufficient information underscores the critical role of open data and proactive public communication in maintaining trust during emergencies, suggesting that timely, clear, and accessible information is essential for effective crisis management and mitigating public anxiety. Interestingly, traditional media, particularly radio, emerged as a reliable source of information when digital infrastructure failed, with most listeners rating the broadcast content positively. This finding emphasizes the importance of maintaining resilient, multi-channel communication strategies that do not solely depend on internet connectivity. It also reveals that while the digital sphere is often prioritized, analog backups remain vital for ensuring that open information reaches the entire population, regardless of technological access or failure. Furthermore, the survey points to a gap between citizen awareness of emergency preparedness and actual readiness, as many lacked essential supplies like radios or backup power sources. This disconnect illustrates why open data initiatives regarding safety protocols and infrastructure vulnerabilities are crucial. By making data on grid reliability and emergency guidelines transparently available, governments can better inform the public, fostering a more prepared society and reducing the fear and confusion associated with sudden system failures.
