Se reduce 22.6 % la percepción de inseguridad

Se reduce 22.6 % la percepción de inseguridad

Recent statistics from Tuxtla Gutiérrez reveal a significant decline in perceived urban insecurity, dropping notably below the national average. While citizens still express fear in specific public zones like ATMs, the overall trend suggests improving safety conditions. This data highlights how open data initiatives can effectively track societal well-being and measure the tangible impact of public policies on citizen perception over time. Public trust varies drastically among security forces, with the national military enjoying overwhelming support compared to local police services. These contrasting confidence levels underscore the importance of transparent, accessible statistics. Open data allows communities to scrutinize institutional performance, fostering accountability and enabling residents to make informed judgments about the effectiveness of different government agencies in maintaining order. Beyond security, residents face numerous municipal challenges, including infrastructure deficits and service failures, which dominate public concern. The prevalence of these issues demonstrates that safety is only one component of urban quality of life. By making such diverse datasets publicly available, authorities encourage civic engagement and data-driven dialogue, ensuring that resource allocation addresses the multifaceted realities faced by the population in an increasingly transparent manner.
