The article uses official population statistics to highlight the evolution of Spanish naming conventions, revealing that García remains the most common surname while Nonó is among the rarest. This contrast underscores how demographic data can uncover significant social trends, such as the gradual disappearance of traditional names like Afrodisia and Frumencio, which are nearing extinction as their elderly bearers pass away. These findings demonstrate the power of open data in preserving cultural heritage and tracking societal shifts. By making registry information publicly accessible, institutions enable citizens and researchers to observe linguistic diversity and migration patterns, such as the prevalence of foreign-origin surnames in specific regions. This transparency is crucial for open data initiatives, as it transforms raw statistics into meaningful insights about national identity. It encourages the use of public datasets to understand human behavior and history, fostering a deeper appreciation of how data reflects the dynamic nature of population demographics and cultural integration.
