The Internet Archive case: implications for India's copyright landscape

The ruling against the Internet Archive in *Hachette Book Group, Inc. v. Internet Archive* establishes a critical precedent by prioritizing publisher market interests over public access, explicitly rejecting the application of the first sale doctrine to digital lending. This decision underscores the legal complexity of treating digital copies as equivalent to physical ones, warning that mass digitization without transformative value constitutes copyright infringement rather than fair use. For open data initiatives, this case highlights the fragility of digital preservation and free access models globally. In jurisdictions like India, the lack of clear legal precedents for digital exhaustion means that similar restrictive interpretations could significantly limit the operations of digital libraries and educational resources. This threatens equitable access to knowledge for underprivileged communities and imposes heavy licensing burdens on institutions seeking to provide affordable educational materials. Consequently, open data advocates must focus on redefining "transformative use" to protect digital repositories. To ensure compliance and sustainability, digital libraries may need to add significant analytical value to digitized works rather than merely reproducing them. Furthermore, the ruling’s implications extend to emerging technologies like AI, suggesting that future access to digitized content for training and research may face stricter legal hurdles unless robust frameworks for fair dealing are established.
