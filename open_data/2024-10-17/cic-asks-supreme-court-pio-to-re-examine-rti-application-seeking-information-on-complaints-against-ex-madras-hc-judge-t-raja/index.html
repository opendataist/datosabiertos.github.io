CIC Asks Supreme Court PIO To Re-examine RTI Application Seeking Information On Complaints Against Ex-Madras HC Judge T Raja

CIC Asks Supreme Court PIO To Re-examine RTI Application Seeking Information On Complaints Against Ex-Madras HC Judge T Raja

The Central Information Commission recently directed the Supreme Court’s Public Information Officer to re-examine an RTI application concerning complaints against a former High Court judge. This directive underscores the critical importance of transparency within India’s judicial appointment system. By requiring the disclosure of complaint counts, the ruling reinforces the principle that judicial accountability mechanisms must remain accessible to public scrutiny, ensuring that the integrity of the judiciary is not shielded from necessary oversight. However, the Commission clarified the boundaries of this transparency by denying access to details regarding the specific actions taken on those complaints. The decision established that such procedural outcomes constitute personal information exempt from disclosure under the RTI Act, particularly when the requester is not the original complainant. This distinction highlights a significant legal tension: while the existence of complaints must be revealed to maintain institutional trust, the granular details of their resolution remain protected to safeguard individual privacy rights. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates as it defines the precise scope of publicly accessible judicial records. It demonstrates that while raw metrics like the volume of grievances can be made open to promote accountability, the associated qualitative data remains restricted. For the open data community, this serves as a vital precedent for navigating the complex intersection of public interest and privacy, illustrating that full transparency in governance often requires balancing conflicting legal obligations rather than absolute disclosure.
