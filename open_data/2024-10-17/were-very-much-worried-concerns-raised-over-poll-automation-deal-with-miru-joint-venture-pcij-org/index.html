'We’re very much worried.’ Concerns raised over poll automation deal with Miru Joint Venture - PCIJ.org

'We’re very much worried.’ Concerns raised over poll automation deal with Miru Joint Venture - PCIJ.org

The sudden departure of St. Timothy Construction Corp. from the Miru Joint Venture has raised serious doubts about its ability to fulfill the multi-billion peso contract for automating the May 2025 elections. Advocacy groups are demanding full transparency regarding the remaining partners' financial capacity to deliver essential electoral hardware. Without the original partner’s reported financial backing, there are legitimate concerns that the consortium may lack the resources to complete the complex installation, threatening the integrity and timeliness of the upcoming national vote. Central to these concerns is whether the contract still adheres to the Government Procurement Law’s 60-40 equity requirement favoring Philippine nationals. With one major local partner exiting, the remaining entities must prove they can individually and collectively meet the substantial funding obligations mandated by law. Transparency advocates argue that disclosure is the only way to ensure the consortium is not operating as a "phantom entity" and that the remaining Filipino stakeholders retain sufficient financial power to sustain the project without violating legal equity standards. This situation is highly relevant to open_data because it highlights the critical intersection between government procurement, public trust, and the right to information. The coalition’s push for disclosure underscores that in high-stakes public contracts, withholding financial and operational data undermines democratic accountability. Ensuring that citizens can access detailed information about contractor capabilities and legal compliance is essential for verifying that electoral processes remain secure, lawful, and trustworthy in the face of changing corporate partnerships.
