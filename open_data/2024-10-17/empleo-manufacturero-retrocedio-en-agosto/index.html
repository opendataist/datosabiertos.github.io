Empleo manufacturero retrocedió en agosto

The manufacturing sector saw a slight monthly decline in employment in August, driven by reductions in both dependent and non-dependent staff, despite a minor increase in total hours worked. This data underscores shifting labor dynamics within the industry, with some subsectors contracting while others expanded their workforces. Remuneration trends diverged from employment figures, showing an increase in real average wages for both dependent and non-dependent workers. Social benefits and contributions also rose, indicating that although headcount stabilized or dipped slightly, compensation levels improved for workers during this period. This information is relevant to open data because it illustrates how granular statistical indicators from national institutes provide essential transparency for economic analysis. Access to such detailed datasets enables researchers and policymakers to monitor labor market health, understand wage disparities, and assess the impact of industrial policies with greater precision and accountability.
