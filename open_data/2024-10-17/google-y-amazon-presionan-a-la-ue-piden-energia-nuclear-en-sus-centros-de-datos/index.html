Google y Amazon presionan a la UE: piden energía nuclear en sus centros de datos

Google y Amazon presionan a la UE: piden energía nuclear en sus centros de datos

The race to lead in artificial intelligence is driving major technology companies to secure stable and massive energy supplies, prompting firms like Google and Microsoft to advocate for the revitalization of nuclear energy. Given the unprecedented computational capacity required to process enormous volumes of data, the tech sector identifies nuclear power—particularly through small modular reactors (SMRs)—as the key solution to meet these demands without compromising decarbonization goals. This pressure extends to Europe, where corporations are seeking to influence the European Commission to foster policies that facilitate nuclear development, contrasting with the current uncertainty in countries such as Germany and Spain, which are closing their reactors. While the United States is consolidating its advantage through direct alliances with the nuclear industry, the European Union is attempting to follow suit with initiatives like the Industrial Alliance on Small Modular Reactors, although there is concern that the continent is losing ground in this crucial technological transition. This article is relevant to the field of open data because it exposes the invisible physical infrastructures that underpin the data era: processing centers and the energy that powers them. Understanding these dynamics is vital for debating the sustainability, governance, and accessibility of artificial intelligence systems that generate, store, and analyze public and private data, highlighting that the availability of information is intrinsically linked to strategic energy resources and transnational political decisions.
