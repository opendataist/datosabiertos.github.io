Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly disadvantaged living conditions compared to the general population, including lower incomes, poorer health outcomes, and higher rates of mental distress and loneliness. Despite these structural challenges, this group maintains satisfaction levels equal to native-born citizens, indicating a resilient sense of well-being amidst socioeconomic hardship. Interestingly, immigrants exhibit stronger institutional trust and national affiliation than the broader population. While personal social trust is lower, their confidence in the political system, judiciary, and police is notably higher. This paradox highlights a complex social integration where citizens may feel personally isolated yet remain deeply committed to and trusting of Norwegian state institutions. This data is crucial for open_data as it reveals hidden societal dynamics through standardized, accessible statistics. It demonstrates how public data can uncover nuanced truths about social cohesion and inequality. By making such comprehensive datasets available, governments enable researchers to move beyond simple metrics and understand the multifaceted realities of integration, fostering evidence-based policy decisions.
