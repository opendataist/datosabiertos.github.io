El Parlamento húngaro retrasa ley de transparencia

El Parlamento húngaro retrasa ley de transparencia

Hungary’s proposed transparency law, which requires the disclosure and monitoring of foreign funding for media outlets and non-governmental organizations (NGOs), is facing significant delays despite concerns raised by the European Union. The legislation aims to combat perceived disinformation but has been widely criticized as unconstitutional and a threat to the independence of civil society. Critics argue that it grants the state excessive power over financial data, potentially stifling free speech and democratic pluralism. The European Union has formally called for the withdrawal of the bill, warning that its current form violates EU principles. While the Hungarian government maintains that its focus is on protecting national sovereignty, ongoing debates highlight deep divisions within political and professional sectors over the specific legal instruments required. This tension underscores the struggle between state control and journalistic freedom in the region. This case is crucial for open data, as it illustrates the risks of state-mandated data transparency being weaponized for political censorship rather than public accountability. It demonstrates how opaque regulatory frameworks can undermine trust in data ecosystems and threaten the operational integrity of independent organizations, reinforcing the need for robust data protection standards in legislative processes.
