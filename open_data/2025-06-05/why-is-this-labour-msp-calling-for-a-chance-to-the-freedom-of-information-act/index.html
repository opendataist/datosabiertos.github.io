Why is this Labour MSP calling for a chance to the Freedom of Information Act?

Why is this Labour MSP calling for a chance to the Freedom of Information Act?

Scottish officials and ministers, including former First Minister Nicola Sturgeon, admitted to deleting WhatsApp messages related to government business during the pandemic, despite being explicitly warned that such communications are discoverable under Freedom of Information laws. This widespread deletion has raised significant concerns regarding transparency and accountability, particularly as it obscures crucial details about critical decision-making processes during a global health crisis. The revelation highlights a dangerous precedent where digital convenience overrides legal obligations to maintain public records, undermining trust in governmental institutions. To address these vulnerabilities, new legislation proposes criminalizing the deliberate or reckless destruction of materials subject to public scrutiny, even before specific information requests are made. This legislative push aims to modernize Scotland’s existing Freedom of Information framework, ensuring it remains robust against the evolving methods of digital communication. By removing the First Minister’s power to override information commissioner rulings and introducing strict penalties for data deletion, the proposed bill seeks to enforce stricter compliance and prevent future attempts to evade transparency obligations through informal messaging platforms. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the urgent need for legal frameworks that explicitly recognize informal digital communications as public records. It illustrates how traditional transparency mechanisms often fail when adopted technologies operate outside official archival systems. For open data advocates, this case study emphasizes that ensuring accessibility to public information requires not just technical standards, but also robust legislative safeguards that close loopholes allowing officials to circumvent disclosure requirements through personal or unofficial messaging apps.
