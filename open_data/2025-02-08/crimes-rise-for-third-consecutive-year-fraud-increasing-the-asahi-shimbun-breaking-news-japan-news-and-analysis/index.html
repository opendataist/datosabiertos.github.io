Crimes rise for third consecutive year, fraud increasing | The Asahi Shimbun: Breaking News, Japan News and Analysis

Japan’s reported crime levels are rising, returning to pre-pandemic peaks after two decades of decline. This shift challenges the long-held narrative of inherent national safety, with significant increases in thefts, fraud, and violent offenses. The surge is driven partly by economic hardship and new social media recruitment tactics, signaling a complex deterioration in public security rather than simple statistical noise. Perceptions of safety are plummeting, with a majority of citizens now believing neighborhoods are less secure than they were ten years ago. Fraud and cybercrimes dominate public concern, eroding trust in community stability. This growing anxiety reflects a tangible change in the social fabric, as ordinary citizens engage in increasingly diverse criminal activities, from minor thefts to sophisticated financial scams. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it highlights the urgent need for transparent, accessible crime statistics to foster public trust. Open datasets allow researchers and policymakers to analyze trends in fraud and cybercrime, informing better security strategies. By making these insights publicly available, governments can address the root causes of rising insecurity and empower communities to adapt to evolving safety challenges effectively.
