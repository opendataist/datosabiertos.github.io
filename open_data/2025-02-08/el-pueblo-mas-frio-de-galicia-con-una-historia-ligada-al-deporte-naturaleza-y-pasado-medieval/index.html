El pueblo más frío de Galicia con una historia ligada al deporte, naturaleza y pasado medieval

The article highlights Cabeza de Manzaneda, a remote settlement in Galicia with only five residents, illustrating the extreme demographic fragmentation and depopulation affecting rural Spain. This case underscores the challenge of maintaining sustainable community structures in areas with minimal human presence, where geographic isolation significantly impacts social and economic viability. Despite its tiny population, the area leverages its unique high-altitude environment and historical ski infrastructure to sustain tourism. The focus shifts from traditional residential communities to service-oriented locations, demonstrating how specific geographical advantages can support localized economies even when permanent habitation is negligible. This is relevant to open data as it reveals critical gaps in spatial datasets regarding micro-settlements. Accurate mapping of such entities requires granular, updated geospatial information to understand administrative boundaries and service needs, emphasizing the necessity for high-resolution data that captures the reality of dispersed, shrinking rural populations rather than relying on generalized municipal aggregates.
