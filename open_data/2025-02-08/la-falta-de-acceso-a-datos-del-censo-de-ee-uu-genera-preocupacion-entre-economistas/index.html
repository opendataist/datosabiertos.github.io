La falta de acceso a datos del Censo de EE. UU. genera preocupación entre economistas

The sudden unavailability of key U.S. Census datasets, including critical demographic and economic reports, highlights the fragility of public data infrastructure. Experts warn that blocking access to such routine information disrupts the transparency necessary for informed decision-making across government and business sectors. This incident underscores how political pressures can directly hinder the flow of essential statistics, creating uncertainty about the motives behind these restrictions. The exclusion of fundamental data compromises the quality of monetary and fiscal policies, as well as private investment strategies. When accurate, timely information is withheld, policymakers and economists lose the empirical foundation required to address societal challenges effectively. This disruption serves as a stark reminder that open data is not merely a technical convenience but a vital component of democratic accountability and economic stability. This event is highly relevant to open data advocacy because it demonstrates how administrative decisions can instantly erode public trust and accessibility. It reinforces the urgent need for legal safeguards ensuring that government data remains independent, unrestricted, and freely available to all citizens. Protecting open data access is crucial to maintaining the integrity of public institutions and enabling evidence-based governance in an increasingly complex world.
