Editores y autores franceses demandan a Meta por uso de obras en entrenamiento de IA

Editores y autores franceses demandan a Meta por uso de obras en entrenamiento de IA

French publishers and authors have filed a lawsuit against Meta in Paris, alleging that the tech giant used protected literary works without authorization to train its generative artificial intelligence models. This legal action underscores the growing tension between creative industries and technology companies over data ownership and intellectual property rights in the digital age. The plaintiffs seek the complete removal of Meta’s unauthorized datasets and argue that AI systems are parasitically appropriating cultural heritage. They also express concern about the emergence of false AI-generated books that compete with authentic human creations, threatening the economic and artistic viability of traditional authors and publishers who rely on exclusive rights to their content. This case is highly relevant to open data discussions, as it highlights the critical balance between training large-scale AI models and respecting copyright laws. It illustrates the broader global challenge of ensuring transparency and legal compliance when using publicly available digital content for commercial AI development, setting a potential precedent for how open data practices must adapt to protect creators’ rights amidst technological advancement.
