Cunde el miedo entre los alemanes a que se ocupen sus segundas residencias en Mallorca

The article highlights a significant shift in Mallorca’s real estate market, where rising housing costs, inflation, and the fear of squatting are driving away German residents, a demographic that previously represented a major portion of foreign investment. This reversal underscores the instability of the local property sector, as legal hurdles and safety concerns deter potential buyers and force current owners to sell, thereby altering the dynamic of foreign ownership in the Balearic Islands. Simultaneously, the local population faces severe difficulties in securing housing, as construction rates fail to meet demand and speculative foreign purchases contribute to an exodus of residents and young professionals. The situation has evolved from affecting only the unemployed to impacting the working class, sparking public debate on how to limit foreign acquisition of properties. This crisis is not merely economic but social, as it compromises the quality of life and safety for locals, creating a hostile environment for long-term community stability. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the intersection of demographic trends, housing policy, and public safety, all of which rely on transparent data for effective governance. Understanding the correlation between foreign investment patterns, squatting incidents, and local displacement requires accessible, high-quality datasets. Open data initiatives can help policymakers and researchers analyze these complex socio-economic factors, fostering informed decisions that balance tourism-driven real estate growth with the rights and needs of local citizens.
