The rapid advancement of quantum computing poses an existential threat to classical cryptographic standards, necessitating an urgent transition to quantum-resistant protocols. This shift is critical for protecting sensitive infrastructure, particularly electronic machine-readable travel documents like passports, which remain vulnerable to future quantum attacks. The article highlights that without immediate intervention, current security measures will become obsolete, undermining the integrity of digital identities and cross-border travel systems globally. A major European initiative, PQC4eMRTD, addresses this vulnerability by driving the standardization and promotion of quantum-resistant cryptographic technologies. Coordinated by industry leaders and supported by the European Union, the project aims to bridge the gap between theoretical research and practical implementation. By fostering collaboration among government bodies, private sector experts, and academic institutions, the consortium seeks to create a comprehensive blueprint for Europe’s transition to post-quantum cryptography, ensuring a unified and robust defense against emerging digital threats. This development is highly relevant to the open data community as it demonstrates how transparent, collaborative standardization efforts can secure digital identities and personal information in an evolving technological landscape. Open data initiatives often rely on trust and security; therefore, adopting quantum-resistant standards ensures that public data infrastructures remain protected and resilient. By advocating for these protocols, the project sets a precedent for how open, interoperable, and secure frameworks can be established, safeguarding citizen data against future computational power while maintaining public trust in digital governance systems.
