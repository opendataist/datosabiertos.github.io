The Peruvian economy showed continued, albeit slowing, expansion, marked by five months of growth driven by strong performance in mining, construction, and tourism. This recovery highlights the resilience of specific industries while underscoring that national economic health is unevenly distributed, with significant declines in agriculture, fishing, and financial services tempering overall progress. This analysis is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how granular, sector-specific statistics provide a more nuanced understanding of economic realities than aggregate figures alone. By accessing and comparing detailed public datasets, analysts can identify which drivers are propelling growth and which areas require intervention, enabling more precise policy decisions and business strategies based on evidence rather than general trends. Ultimately, the article emphasizes the critical importance of transparency in economic reporting. Open access to detailed statistical data allows stakeholders to verify official narratives, track discrepancies across sectors, and ensure accountability. This transparency fosters informed public discourse and supports the development of robust, data-driven solutions for sustainable economic development.
