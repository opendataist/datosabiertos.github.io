The South African Artificial Intelligence Association has challenged LinkedIn’s practice of using local user data to train AI models without explicit consent. This action highlights critical tensions between rapid technological innovation and established privacy regulations, specifically questioning whether corporate data utilization aligns with the country’s strict protection laws. This conflict underscores the urgent need for clear guidelines on data ownership and usage rights in the digital age. By forcing a regulatory investigation, the association emphasizes that protecting individual privacy is essential, ensuring that personal information is not exploited merely because it holds commercial value for large tech entities. Relevance to open data lies in the debate over transparency and consent in data ecosystems. While open data advocates for accessibility, this case warns against unchecked exploitation of public information. It reinforces that ethical open data practices must prioritize individual rights and regulatory compliance, ensuring that data sharing respects legal boundaries and human-centric principles.
