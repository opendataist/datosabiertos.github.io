Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly worse living conditions than the general population, including lower income, poorer employment outcomes, and substandard housing. Furthermore, they experience higher rates of mental health challenges and loneliness, particularly among specific national groups. These objective socioeconomic and psychological disparities highlight systemic inequalities in social integration and resource distribution within the country. Despite these substantial disadvantages, immigrants report satisfaction levels equal to native Norwegians and demonstrate stronger confidence in key state institutions like the judiciary and police. This paradox suggests that subjective well-being does not always correlate with material circumstances, indicating that immigrants may prioritize societal stability or institutional fairness over personal economic status. Such nuanced data challenges simplistic narratives about immigrant discontent. This report is vital for open data advocates because it exemplifies the power of transparent, comprehensive national statistics to reveal complex social realities. By making detailed demographic insights accessible, such datasets enable researchers and policymakers to move beyond superficial metrics. It underscores how open government data can drive evidence-based interventions to address mental health disparities and improve housing standards, fostering more inclusive and equitable policy frameworks.
