Ley de Transparencia Social busca prevenir lavado de activos y fortalecer trazabilidad financiera

Ley de Transparencia Social busca prevenir lavado de activos y fortalecer trazabilidad financiera

The article highlights a critical legislative push to enhance transparency in Ecuador’s economic and social sectors, aiming to stabilize public finances while combating illicit activities. By simplifying tax regulations on dividends and strengthening the regulatory framework for social organizations, the government seeks to foster economic growth through increased business confidence and clearer fiscal rules. This approach balances the need for financial stability with the imperative to prevent money laundering and terror financing, ensuring that transparency serves as a cornerstone of modern state governance. A central theme is the necessity of robust public registers to ensure legal security and prevent economic crimes. Experts emphasize that entities currently operating without adequate oversight, such as certain civil societies and trusts, must be incorporated into formal control systems. Establishing comprehensive registries is presented not merely as an administrative task, but as a fundamental requirement for preventing illegal operations and maintaining the integrity of the legal framework governing businesses and associations. This initiative is highly relevant to open data advocates because it underscores the direct link between public registries and societal security. The proposal argues that transparency cannot exist without accessible, controlled public records, reinforcing the principle that open data on organizational structures and financial flows is essential for democratic accountability. By mandating better data collection and disclosure, the legislation supports a broader ecosystem where open information empowers citizens to monitor institutional compliance and detect irregularities, thereby strengthening trust in public institutions.
