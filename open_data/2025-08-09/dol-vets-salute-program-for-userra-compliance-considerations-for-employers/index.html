DOL/VETS SALUTE Program for USERRA Compliance: Considerations for Employers

DOL/VETS SALUTE Program for USERRA Compliance: Considerations for Employers

The Department of Labor’s Veterans’ Employment and Training Service has introduced the SALUTE program to assist employers in navigating the complex reemployment rights and anti-discrimination mandates of USERRA. This initiative allows companies to seek proactive technical guidance on specific military service-related employment scenarios, potentially clarifying ambiguous statutory requirements. By offering a formal channel for advisory opinions, the program addresses the nuanced and open-ended nature of veteran employment protections, which often leave employers vulnerable to liability due to the lack of clear administrative prerequisites or statutes of limitations. However, the program presents significant legal and privacy risks that employers must carefully evaluate. Requests for assistance require detailed disclosures that may be published publicly, potentially exposing confidential business information or trade secrets. There is a notable danger that these advisory opinions could be weaponized in subsequent litigation; if an employer ignores the guidance, it may serve as evidence of willful non-compliance, leading to increased financial penalties. Conversely, adhering to the advice could help defend against claims of willfulness, creating a precarious situation where the level of engagement with regulators directly impacts legal exposure. This development is highly relevant to open data principles because it highlights the tension between transparency and privacy in government operations. By mandating the public availability of employer-specific inquiries and responses, the program prioritizes open access to regulatory interpretations but at the cost of individual privacy. For open data advocates, this case illustrates the critical need for robust privacy guardrails when publishing administrative records, demonstrating how well-intentioned transparency can inadvertently compromise sensitive commercial and personal information.
