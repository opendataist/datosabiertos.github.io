CCOO CLM urge una ley regional que apueste por la movilidad sostenible al trabajo - Tercera Información

CCOO CLM urge una ley regional que apueste por la movilidad sostenible al trabajo - Tercera Información

The article highlights a critical imbalance in regional commuting, where the vast majority of workers rely on private vehicles rather than public transport for their daily travel. This heavy dependence on cars is identified as a primary source of air and noise pollution, which significantly contributes to health issues in urban areas. Furthermore, it exacerbates the risk of traffic accidents during commutes, positioning mobility safety as a serious occupational hazard that threatens the well-being of the workforce. Beyond environmental and safety concerns, the dominance of private transport creates substantial social inequalities. The rising costs of vehicle ownership disproportionately affect lower-income workers, reducing their employability and increasing economic precarity by limiting access to certain jobs. This dynamic not only strains personal finances but also underscores the urgent need for policies that ensure equitable access to employment opportunities, independent of one’s ability to afford a car. This text is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible statistics to drive policy change and collective bargaining. By relying on official data from the National Statistics Institute, advocates can substantiate their arguments for sustainable mobility laws and negotiate better transport solutions. Open data empowers citizens and unions to hold institutions accountable, transforming raw numbers into actionable evidence for creating healthier, safer, and more equitable urban environments.
