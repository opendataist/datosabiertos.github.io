Argentina, un país donde con un empleo no se llega a fin de mes

The article illustrates a severe deterioration of the labor market in Argentina under the current administration, characterized by a significant rise in informal employment and economic precarity. This trend is not limited to low-skilled sectors; it heavily impacts qualified professionals, retirees, and researchers who are forced to seek secondary income sources to survive. The data indicates a shift away from formal, secure jobs toward unstable alternatives, reflecting a broader "economic stress" where official unemployment rates mask widespread vulnerability and reduced purchasing power. This situation highlights a critical gap in official statistics and public discourse regarding the true state of employment quality. By documenting how individuals with higher education and stable public sector jobs are turning to the informal gig economy, the report exposes limitations in standard labor indicators that fail to capture underemployment and income erosion. Understanding these nuanced shifts is essential for accurately assessing social welfare and economic health beyond simple job creation metrics. For open data advocates, this case underscores the necessity of granular, multidimensional datasets that track income stability, informal sector participation, and the real value of wages. It emphasizes the importance of accessible, high-quality data to validate citizen experiences and hold institutions accountable. Reliable open data can help reveal hidden crises like those described, ensuring that policy decisions are based on comprehensive evidence rather than isolated metrics that may obscure deepening social inequalities.
