AG rules Association of Coastal Towns violated FOIA requirements, must act as public body

The Delaware Attorney General determined that the Association of Coastal Towns is a public body, ruling that its collective structure and the public nature of its member municipalities mandate compliance with Freedom of Information Act requirements. This decision underscores that organizations formed by governmental entities must operate transparently, regardless of their self-proclaimed status as voluntary associations. Consequently, the AG ordered the group to rectify procedural failures by re-conducting decisions taken in private, specifically regarding offshore wind project discussions. This enforcement action highlights the critical importance of adhering to open meeting laws and maintaining accessible records to ensure community involvement and accountability in regional governance matters. This ruling is highly relevant to open data because it establishes that collective bodies representing public interests must ensure their deliberations and decisions are accessible. It reinforces the principle that data transparency and public participation are not optional for such organizations, but legal obligations essential for maintaining democratic integrity in coastal management and resource planning.
