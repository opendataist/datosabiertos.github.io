Report reveals WA government knew regional jetty was unviable prior to fix worth almost half a million dollars

A Freedom of Information report reveals that Western Australian authorities proceeded with a half-million-dollar refurbishment of a Bunbury jetty despite possessing clear engineering assessments indicating it was extensively damaged by wood borers and nearing the end of its viable life. The decision to prioritize an election promise over structural integrity resulted in the structure being closed less than three years after opening, effectively rendering the significant public expenditure as a total loss rather than a sustainable infrastructure investment. The revelation has sparked calls for accountability from the state opposition and local constituents, who view the spending as a misuse of taxpayer funds given the known risks. While authorities maintain that the degradation occurred faster than expected and that demolition was always part of the long-term plan, the community remains skeptical about future promises. Local fishers argue that repeatedly patching up deteriorating assets instead of building new facilities demonstrates a failure to listen to the needs of the recreational fishing community and highlights a pattern of broken commitments. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the critical value of public transparency in holding institutions accountable for their stewardship of public resources. By making internal engineering reports accessible, the media was able to expose the discrepancy between official statements and factual assessments, proving that open data serves as an essential check against political expediency. It illustrates how releasing sensitive technical information empowers citizens to demand truth and efficiency in government spending.
