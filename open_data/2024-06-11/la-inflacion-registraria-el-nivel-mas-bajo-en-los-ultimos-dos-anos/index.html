La inflación registraría el nivel más bajo en los últimos dos años

Argentina’s upcoming consumer price index reveals a significant deceleration in inflation, projecting the lowest monthly rate in over two years. This downward trend marks the fifth consecutive month of cooling prices, suggesting that government policies have effectively constrained cost increases. The data indicates a substantial shift from previous highs, reflecting a stabilization in the broader economic environment. Crucially, core inflation has dropped to levels unseen since the pandemic, driven largely by the postponement of utility hikes for electricity, gas, and fuel. While regulated prices remained steady, the broader market expects annual inflation to decrease significantly from prior forecasts. This structural slowdown highlights the impact of administrative controls on specific sectors, which has played a decisive role in moderating overall price volatility for consumers. This information is vital for open-data advocates, as it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, timely, and accessible statistical releases from national institutes such as INDEC. Reliable public data allows for independent verification by private entities and empowers citizens to assess economic policies accurately. Ensuring these datasets are freely available fosters accountability, enables rigorous third-party analysis, and supports informed public debate regarding the effectiveness of fiscal and monetary policies.
