Vermont Data Privacy Act, Most Robust Consumer Protections Since CCPA

Vermont’s new Data Privacy Act establishes robust consumer rights, extending beyond standard models by mandating strict data minimization and granting private rights of action for sensitive data misuse. This legislative move highlights the ongoing fragmentation in US privacy regulation, as states act independently while federal comprehensive reform remains stalled. The law’s specific protections for marginalized groups and its stringent collection limits offer a higher bar for data handling than existing frameworks. These provisions demonstrate a growing regional commitment to enhanced privacy standards, challenging businesses to adapt to more demanding compliance requirements across different jurisdictions. For open data advocates, this shift underscores the tension between transparency and privacy. As states like Vermont enforce stricter controls on personal information collection, the scope for releasing detailed datasets may narrow. This necessitates careful evaluation of how open data initiatives can coexist with emerging, stringent privacy laws that prioritize individual consent over broad data availability.
