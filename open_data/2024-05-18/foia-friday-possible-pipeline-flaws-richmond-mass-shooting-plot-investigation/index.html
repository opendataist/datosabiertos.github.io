FOIA Friday: Possible pipeline flaws, Richmond mass shooting plot investigation

The article underscores a persistent gap between Virginia’s legal presumption of public access and the actual practice of officials keeping records private. Despite the Virginia Freedom of Information Act mandating transparency, many leaders default to secrecy, requiring citizens to fight for basic information. This systemic resistance highlights a critical challenge for open data advocates: the need to actively enforce existing transparency laws rather than assuming they are self-executing. Revealing specific failures through FOIA requests demonstrates the tangible stakes of this lack of openness. In one instance, obtained reports exposed significant structural flaws in a major pipeline, challenging official narratives about its safety. In another, records revealed that a local building department operated without a certified official, jeopardizing public safety for years without detection. These examples show that hidden data can mask serious infrastructure and administrative risks that remain unaddressed as long as they are obscured from public view. Furthermore, the release of police investigation files regarding a purported mass shooting plot exposed significant procedural errors and a lack of corroborating evidence. The case illustrates how undisclosed information can lead to wrongful accusations and erode public trust in law enforcement. For the open data community, these cases reinforce the vital role of transparency in holding government bodies accountable, ensuring that decisions and operations are subject to public scrutiny and that errors or abuses can be identified and corrected.
