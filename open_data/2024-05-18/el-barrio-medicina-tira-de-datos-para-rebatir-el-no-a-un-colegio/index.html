El barrio Medicina 'tira' de datos para rebatir el no a un colegio

El barrio Medicina 'tira' de datos para rebatir el no a un colegio

The Neighborhood Association of the University District is celebrating its tenth anniversary with a series of extensive community events, reinforcing its role as a highly active civic organization. While the festivities emphasize social cohesion through sports, music, and family activities, the association simultaneously prioritizes its advocacy function regarding local infrastructure needs. The central conflict revolves around the urgent demand for a new school, which local authorities have indicated will not be built in the short or medium term. In response, the association used public records to argue that the University neighborhood has a significantly higher population of young children compared to the traditional University district. This data-driven approach highlights the demographic reality and the inadequacy of current educational facilities to serve the area's growing youth population. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how citizens can utilize publicly available municipal statistics, such as population registers, to validate their social claims and hold institutions accountable. By transforming raw demographic data into compelling arguments for public services, the association exemplifies the power of open data in fostering transparency and enabling evidence-based civic engagement to drive policy changes.
