Kogi, Bauchi, Oyo top list of most expensive states – NBS

Nigeria’s recent Consumer Price Index data reveals that Kogi, Bauchi, and Oyo states are currently the most expensive places to live, driven by severe year-on-year inflation. While Lagos saw the highest month-on-month increases, the most critical concern is food inflation, which remains exceptionally high in states like Kogi and Kwara. This trend indicates a systemic crisis where access to basic necessities is becoming increasingly precarious for large segments of the population, signaling deepening economic instability across the region. The surge in prices is primarily attributed to climate change, insecurity, and global economic factors such as currency depreciation and geopolitical conflicts affecting imports. Small-holder farmers face significant disruptions due to erratic weather patterns, while supply chain bottlenecks and inadequate infrastructure exacerbate post-harvest losses and transport costs. These compounding issues have severely reduced food availability and increased costs, highlighting the vulnerability of local agricultural systems to both environmental shocks and broader market volatilities. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the critical need for transparent, granular, and accessible statistical information. High-quality open data allows researchers, policymakers, and citizens to identify regional disparities, understand the specific drivers of inflation, and hold institutions accountable. By making detailed indices publicly available, stakeholders can better advocate for targeted interventions in infrastructure and agricultural support, ultimately fostering greater equity and resilience in the food supply chain.
