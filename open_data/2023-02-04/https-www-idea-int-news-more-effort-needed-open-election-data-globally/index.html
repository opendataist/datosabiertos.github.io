Open Data
ES EN

Many Electoral Management Bodies still fail to fully embrace open data principles, resulting in significant gaps in accessibility and reliability. This lack of transparency persists despite decades of advocacy, with many agencies not publishing voter turnout statistics online, relying instead on temporary press conferences or inaccessible printed materials. Consequently, crucial election data remains missing, partial, or delayed, hindering comprehensive democratic analysis and public accountability. The absence of standardized, machine-readable formats further complicates efforts to build robust datasets for research and campaigning. Without consistent historical data or granular details on voter registration and campaign finance, stakeholders cannot effectively track trends or ensure financial transparency. These inconsistencies often stem from technical limitations or institutional cultures that prioritize control over openness, leading to unreliable figures that may obscure the true nature of electoral participation. This article highlights the critical need for Electoral Management Bodies to adopt comprehensive open data policies. By ensuring that election-related information is complete, timely, and permanently available online, institutions can foster greater trust and enable more informed political engagement. Addressing these data deficiencies is not merely a technical requirement but a fundamental step toward strengthening democratic integrity and empowering all electoral stakeholders with the accurate information they require.
