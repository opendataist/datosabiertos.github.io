Percepción de inseguridad en México bajó en 2023, su menor nivel en 10 años

The decline in Mexico’s perceived insecurity to its lowest level in a decade signals a positive shift in public sentiment, corroborated by concurrent reductions in homicide rates. This statistical improvement suggests that recent security policies and enforcement efforts are beginning to yield tangible results for citizens, marking a significant departure from the violence peaks experienced in recent years. Furthermore, the data reveals a persistent and concerning gender gap, with women reporting significantly higher levels of fear than men. This disparity highlights ongoing structural inequalities in personal safety and underscores the need for targeted interventions that address the specific vulnerabilities faced by women, ensuring that security improvements are equitable across all demographics. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible public statistics empower citizens and policymakers to monitor societal trends accurately. By making these metrics available, institutions like INEGI foster accountability and informed decision-making, proving that reliable data is essential for evaluating government performance and guiding future public safety strategies.
