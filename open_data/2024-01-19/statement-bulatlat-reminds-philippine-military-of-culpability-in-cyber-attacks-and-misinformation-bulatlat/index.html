STATEMENT | Bulatlat reminds Philippine military of culpability in cyber-attacks and misinformation - Bulatlat

The article critiques the Philippine military’s new cyber security initiatives as hypocritical, highlighting their alleged involvement in cyber-attacks against independent media. It argues that state actors are using "anti-misinformation" rhetoric to justify surveillance and suppress freedom of the press. This dynamic reveals a dangerous conflation of legitimate dissent with security threats, undermining democratic accountability and press independence. For open data enthusiasts, this case underscores the critical need for transparency in government digital operations. When state agencies operate opaque cyber units without public oversight, they risk weaponizing data collection to silence critics rather than protect infrastructure. Trust in open data ecosystems requires verifiable accountability from those managing digital systems, ensuring tools serve public interest rather than political suppression. Ultimately, the situation illustrates how cybersecurity narratives can be manipulated to erode digital rights. It calls for rigorous monitoring of government IT activities and robust legal protections for digital dissent. Without such safeguards, open data initiatives may inadvertently facilitate authoritarian control, making civic engagement and independent journalism vulnerable to state-sponsored digital harassment.
