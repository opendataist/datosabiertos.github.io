CIC asks defence ministry to revisit refusal to disclose records on 'Agnipath' scheme

The Central Information Commission ordered the Defence Ministry to reconsider its refusal to disclose records regarding the Agnipath scheme, rejecting the blanket classification of files as "secret." The Commission emphasized that invoking security exemptions requires specific justification, not merely labeling documents as confidential, thereby reinforcing the principle that administrative opacity cannot override statutory transparency requirements. This decision highlights the primacy of the Right to Information Act over other laws, such as the Official Secrets Act, when conflicting claims regarding information disclosure arise. By mandating a detailed explanation for any exemptions, the ruling ensures that public authorities must rigorously demonstrate how withholding information genuinely harms national interests, rather than using secrecy as a default procedural shield. This case is highly relevant to open_data advocates as it establishes a critical precedent for challenging arbitrary data withholding by government bodies. It affirms that citizens have the right to access non-sensitive policy deliberations, ensuring that major public initiatives remain subject to scrutiny. Such accountability mechanisms are essential for fostering trust in government operations and promoting genuine transparency in public administration.
