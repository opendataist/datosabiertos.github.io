The article reveals a significant decline in the perception of insecurity across Mexican cities, marking the lowest levels in a decade. This positive trend suggests that citizens are increasingly feeling safer in their daily lives compared to previous years. Furthermore, a notable gender gap persists, as women consistently report higher levels of perceived insecurity than men. This disparity highlights specific vulnerabilities and suggests that safety improvements have not yet been uniform across all demographic groups. This data is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates the value of transparent, public statistics in holding authorities accountable. Accessible datasets allow researchers and citizens to verify government claims, fostering informed public debate and ensuring that policy decisions are grounded in verified, objective evidence rather than political rhetoric.
