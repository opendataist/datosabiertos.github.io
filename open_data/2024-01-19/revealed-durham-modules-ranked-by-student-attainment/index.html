Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

This article highlights how freedom of information requests can unlock institutional data, revealing significant disparities in student attainment across various university modules and departments. By analyzing grade distributions, the report identifies physics and English as top-performing fields, while foundation years and certain foreign language studies rank lowest. Such transparency allows students and faculty to understand academic trends, grade boundaries, and the variability of assessment difficulty across different subjects. The findings demonstrate that performance varies drastically not just by department, but within specific courses across different year levels. For instance, some first-year design modules saw zero top grades, whereas business analytics modules saw nearly all students achieve first-class honors. Similarly, final-year dissertations showed a wide gap between disciplines, with modern languages and computer science students far outperforming their sports science peers. This level of granular detail exposes the inherent inequalities and structural factors influencing academic success, which are often hidden from public view. This content is highly relevant to the open_data movement because it illustrates the transformative power of releasing raw educational metrics. When universities make internal assessment data publicly accessible, it fosters accountability and empowers students to make informed decisions about their studies. It also encourages ongoing dialogue about educational quality and equity, proving that open data is not just about transparency for its own sake, but about creating tangible value for the community by demystifying complex academic outcomes.
