La preocupación en China por el acelerado descenso de población por segundo año consecutivo | Puranoticia.cl

China has entered a new era of demographic stagnation or decline, marked by fertility rates among the lowest in the world. This trend, accelerated by the pandemic, is primarily driven by structural economic problems such as the real estate crisis, youth unemployment, and financial uncertainty. Couples are postponing parenthood due to high living and education costs, demonstrating that economic security is a fundamental prerequisite for forming families. The slowdown in population growth is driving profound changes in the country’s labor and social structure. As the workforce ages, the state faces increasing pressure on its health care and pension systems. However, experts note that China is transitioning toward a service-based model, similar to other deindustrialized nations, where the population becomes more educated and skilled, adapting to new types of employment rather than traditional manufacturing. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how macroeconomic and demographic indicators must be analyzed in an integrated manner to understand social dynamics. Transparency in data on employment, inflation, and birth rates is crucial for researchers and citizens to correlate public policy with real quality of life. Moreover, it compares official sources with citizen perceptions on social media, highlighting the importance of validating and contextualizing open datasets with diverse voices to obtain a comprehensive view of structural trends.
