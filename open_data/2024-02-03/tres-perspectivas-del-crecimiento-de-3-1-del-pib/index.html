Tres perspectivas del crecimiento de 3.1% del PIB

Mexico’s recent leading GDP indicator confirms positive economic growth that, although it fell short of official targets, significantly exceeded prior expectations and the global average. This result reflects the country’s resilience in the face of previous contractions, driven primarily by domestic consumption and exports. However, analysts warn that this rebound may be more closely linked to post-pandemic recovery than to a sustainable structural transformation, raising doubts about the ability to maintain this pace in the long term without excessively increasing public debt. The significance of this figure diminishes when examining GDP per capita, which has declined in recent years, indicating that macroeconomic growth has not translated into greater individual wealth or improved quality of life. This disconnect suggests that the distribution of prosperity is stagnant and that initial commitments to generate real income gains for the population have not been fulfilled, undermining political efforts to replace traditional economic measures with subjective well-being indicators. Finally, the growth appears as a deeply unbalanced phenomenon, geographically concentrated and associated with a state-led economic restructuring that prioritizes specific projects over market efficiency. This regional inequality generates serious consequences, such as migration and increased insecurity in marginalized areas, demonstrating that development benefits only specific segments of the economy. This analysis is relevant to open data because it underscores the need to disaggregate national aggregates using disaggregated indicators, such as GDP per capita and detailed regional statistics. Only through the transparency of specific data can hidden disparities be revealed and it be truly assessed whether economic growth is inclusive or simply consolidates existing inequalities.
