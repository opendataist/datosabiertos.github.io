PEC lawsuit confirms abuse of FOIA by Orange County for the benefit of Amazon

The Piedmont Environmental Council successfully secured requested documents from Orange County through a Virginia Freedom of Information Act lawsuit, resolving the dispute before it reached court. This outcome demonstrates that local officials cannot legally obscure significant land-use decisions behind secrecy or non-disclosure agreements. The forced release of records exposed that Amazon Web Services was behind a major development proposal and highlighted how county officials restricted access to key stakeholders, including planning commissioners, thereby undermining transparent governance. The revealed documents confirmed suspicions that economic development partners facilitated secretive negotiations, while officials failed to adequately assess the substantial power and water requirements of the proposed data centers. This case serves as a critical lesson for local governments across the Commonwealth, illustrating that transparency laws are enforceable and that withholding public information violates the democratic principle that government affairs should remain open to scrutiny. This incident highlights a troubling statewide pattern where data center developers and local municipalities use secrecy to bypass public oversight, particularly regarding environmental impacts like pollution and noise. The relevance to open data lies in the urgent need for accountability mechanisms that ensure citizens have access to essential information about land use and infrastructure. By holding officials legally accountable for transparency violations, such legal actions preserve the integrity of the democratic process and empower communities to understand the long-term effects of local development decisions.
