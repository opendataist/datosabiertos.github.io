Ataque a servidores de Digitel evidencia falta de preocupación sobre protección de datos personales

The Digitel cybersecurity incident highlights a critical failure in corporate transparency and data protection. The company’s delayed notification to customers after blocking servers underscores a broader disregard for user privacy and information security. This lack of immediate communication demonstrates that companies prioritize internal operations over their ethical obligation to warn individuals of potential risks, thereby exacerbating vulnerability without allowing users to take preventive measures. The article argues that this behavior reflects a systemic absence of responsibility among both public and private institutions in Venezuela regarding personal data management. Experts warn that such negligence can constitute a violation of human rights and potentially criminal conduct, especially when the state fails to enforce adequate protections. The reference to previous breaches, such as those at Banco de Venezuela, illustrates a recurring pattern where sensitive data is exposed and subsequently leaked, further eroding public trust and individual safety. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the severe consequences of opaque data governance. While open data advocates for transparency in public administration, this incident shows that without strict legal frameworks and proactive security measures, the mere availability or storage of data can lead to exploitation. It emphasizes the urgent need for robust legal guarantees that protect privacy, ensuring that transparency does not come at the cost of exposing individuals to harm, but rather holds entities accountable for how they safeguard the information they hold.
