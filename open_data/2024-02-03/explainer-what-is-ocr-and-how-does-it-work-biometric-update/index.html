Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical Character Recognition transforms uneditable image files into machine-readable text, solving critical data management challenges in an increasingly digital world. By converting print media and scanned identity documents into searchable data, organizations eliminate manual intervention and enable seamless integration with business software for analytics and process automation. This technology underpins modern open data initiatives by making vast amounts of static, image-based information accessible, searchable, and usable for public and private analysis. The shift from unstructured visual data to structured text allows for broader accessibility and interoperability, which are foundational principles of the open data movement. Consequently, barriers to information retrieval are lowered, facilitating more transparent and efficient data utilization across sectors. Furthermore, the combination of OCR with biometric capabilities enhances digital identity verification, a growing area of interest within open data ecosystems. As businesses leverage these tools to streamline operations and automate form completion, the resulting standardized data formats contribute to richer, more comprehensive datasets. This evolution supports the creation of open, standardized repositories that can be freely accessed and analyzed, driving innovation and operational efficiency through shared information resources.
