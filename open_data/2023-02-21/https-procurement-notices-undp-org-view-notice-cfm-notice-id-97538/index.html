The Solomon Islands is actively advancing its anti-corruption agenda through the TAP Project, a strategic initiative building upon previous UN-supported frameworks. The project reinforces national institutions and independent bodies to combat systemic corruption, which is widely recognized as a barrier to economic growth and good governance. By aligning with existing national laws and strategies, the initiative seeks to strengthen the rule of law and improve public service delivery across the country. A critical component of this effort is the implementation of Freedom of Information (FOI) legislation, managed by the Office of the Ombudsman. This legal framework is essential for enhancing government accountability and citizen engagement. The process involves extensive consultations with parliament, civil society, and other stakeholders to draft effective regulations, ensuring that transparency mechanisms are robust and accessible. This article is highly relevant to open data because FOI legislation is the legal backbone that mandates the release of government information. By establishing clear rights to access information, the project creates the necessary conditions for open data ecosystems to thrive. This legal clarity empowers journalists, researchers, and the public to scrutinize government actions, thereby fostering greater transparency and reducing opportunities for corrupt practices through increased societal oversight.
