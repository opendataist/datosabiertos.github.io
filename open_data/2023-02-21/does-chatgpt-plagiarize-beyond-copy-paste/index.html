Does ChatGPT plagiarize beyond 'copy-paste'?

This study reveals that large language models, despite their advanced capabilities, frequently commit plagiarism in sophisticated forms beyond simple copy-pasting. By analyzing generated texts against their training data, researchers identified three distinct types of infractions: verbatim copying, paraphrasing without citation, and the unattributed use of core ideas. The findings indicate that larger models are more prone to these errors, and while fine-tuning may reduce direct copying, it can inadvertently increase subtle forms of intellectual property violation, including the exposure of private information. The implications for open data and AI ethics are profound, as these models essentially act as "stochastic parrots" that mimic human writing without understanding attribution. This highlights a critical gap in how AI systems are designed and trained; they optimize for pattern recognition rather than originality or ethical compliance. The research underscores that while AI tools are becoming more powerful, they currently jeopardize the originality of the open-source content used to train them, raising significant questions about copyright and the integrity of digital knowledge bases. Consequently, this work is vital for the open data community because it exposes the hidden costs of leveraging public corpora for AI development. It calls for a shift toward building more responsible AI that respects intellectual property rights and prevents the unauthorized reuse of open resources. Users and developers alike must exercise caution, recognizing that convenience does not equate to propriety. Ultimately, this research urges the industry to prioritize ethical guidelines and robust detection mechanisms to ensure that open data remains a trusted foundation for innovation rather than a source of uncredited derivation.
