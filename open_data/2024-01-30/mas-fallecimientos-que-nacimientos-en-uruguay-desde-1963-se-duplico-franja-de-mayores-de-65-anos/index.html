Más fallecimientos que nacimientos en Uruguay; desde 1963 se duplicó franja de mayores de 65 años

Uruguay is experiencing significant demographic stagnation, characterized by a virtually zero growth rate and an accelerated aging of its population. Preliminary census data reveal that the number of deaths has exceeded the number of births since 2021, signaling the onset of a phase of population decline. This trend, driven by low birth rates and mortality, contrasts with the sustained growth of the past, suggesting a future in which the population will shrink unless there are drastic changes in migration patterns. Structural aging is the most visible consequence of this phenomenon, with the proportion of older adults doubling over the past few decades. While the elderly population has grown substantially, the young population has declined notably. This demographic imbalance is not unique to Uruguay but is part of a broader regional trend; however, the country appears to be anticipating international projections, posing structural challenges to the long-term sustainability of its social and economic systems. This article is crucial for open data because it illustrates the importance of transparency in public statistics for anticipating social crises. By making the components of demographic change—births, deaths, and migration—accessible, governments enable civil society, academia, and the private sector to analyze and plan responses to population aging. The availability of accurate and timely data is essential for validating trends and fostering evidence-based decision-making in the face of demographic decline scenarios.
