Preocupa a la SIP la filtración de datos de periodistas que asisten a "mañaneras" de AMLO

The Inter-American Press Society (SIP) has condemned the unauthorized leak of personal data belonging to numerous journalists covering the Mexican presidency. This breach, which exposed sensitive identifiers such as addresses and tax codes, was described as a severe violation of privacy that places reporters at significant risk. The organization is demanding an immediate and thorough investigation to identify those responsible and ensure the safety of the affected individuals. This incident highlights the precarious environment for press freedom in Mexico, identified as one of the most dangerous countries in the hemisphere for journalists. The SIP emphasized that protecting data privacy is a fundamental right essential for secure journalistic work. In a context marked by violence and intimidation, such cybercrimes exacerbate existing threats, potentially leading to displacement or exile, thereby undermining democratic transparency and the ability of media professionals to report freely without fear of physical or digital harm. The relevance to open data lies in the critical balance between transparency and the right to privacy. While open data initiatives promote government accountability, this case illustrates the dangers of mishandling sensitive datasets associated with public officials and the press. It underscores the necessity for rigorous data governance, secure handling protocols, and strict legal safeguards to prevent abuse. Ensuring that openness does not compromise individual safety remains a paramount ethical and technical challenge for data stewards worldwide.
