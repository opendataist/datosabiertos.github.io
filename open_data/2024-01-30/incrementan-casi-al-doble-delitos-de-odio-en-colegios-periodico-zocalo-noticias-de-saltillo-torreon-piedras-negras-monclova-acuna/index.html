Incrementan casi al doble delitos de odio en colegios | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The FBI report reveals a significant increase in hate crimes in U.S. schools, driven by the politicization of the education system and reactions to recent social movements. The most affected victims include Black individuals, the LGBTQ community, and Jewish people, with bullying being the most common form of abuse, followed by vandalism and physical assaults. This phenomenon reflects broader social tensions, where the political context directly influences school safety. This issue is crucial for open data because it highlights the importance of transparency in the collection of official statistics on hate crimes. The report shows that actual figures may be much higher due to underreporting by victims and the failure of some local police departments to report incidents to the FBI. The accessibility of this data enables civil society and researchers to identify patterns, vulnerabilities, and areas requiring urgent intervention. The relevance of open data lies in its ability to inform public policy and raise social awareness about the protection of vulnerable groups. By making these indicators public, it facilitates public debate and holds educational and governmental institutions accountable. Open data not only documents reality but also serves as an essential tool for promoting structural changes and ensuring safety and equity in school communities.
