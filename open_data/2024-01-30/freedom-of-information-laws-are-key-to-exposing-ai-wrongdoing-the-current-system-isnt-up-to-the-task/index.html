Freedom of information laws are key to exposing AI wrongdoing. The current system isn’t up to the task

The rapid integration of artificial intelligence into government systems necessitates a fundamental modernization of freedom of information laws to ensure continued accountability and transparency. Without updated legal frameworks, opaque automation can lead to severe miscarriages of justice, as illustrated by international scandals where lack of access to technical data allowed flawed systems to operate unchecked for years. This highlights the critical link between open data rights and the ability to detect and correct errors in automated decision-making processes. Current transparency regimes, such as Australia’s, remain constrained by outdated provisions like broad cabinet exemptions and lengthy disclosure timeframes. These restrictions hinder public scrutiny, allowing potentially harmful algorithmic practices to persist without sufficient oversight. Reform proposals emphasize introducing public interest tests for withheld documents and significantly reducing the period before government records become accessible, thereby aligning legal standards with contemporary expectations for government openness and responsiveness. This article is vital to the open_data community because it demonstrates that static data release policies are insufficient for the AI era. To mitigate risks associated with automated governance, governments must proactively disclose details about the technologies they employ and reduce trade secret exemptions. Modernizing freedom of information laws ensures that open data initiatives effectively serve as a check on power, enabling citizens and advocates to investigate algorithmic bias and systemic failures in a timely manner.
