Submarinos nucleares en Gibraltar: Exteriores debe informar

Submarinos nucleares en Gibraltar: Exteriores debe informar

The Transparency Council has ordered the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to adequately justify its denial of access to documents concerning the presence of nuclear submarines in Gibraltar. The body has sanctioned the Ministry’s failure to provide detailed explanations, noting that it had invoked national security in a generic manner to withhold the information. This ruling reinforces the principle that restrictions on the right of access must be strict, proportionate, and clearly justified, preventing national security from being used as a vague excuse to maintain secrecy. The relevance of this case for the principles of open data lies in its direct impact on government transparency and public accountability. By demanding a concrete justification, the decision sets an important precedent for combating opacity in matters of defense and international relations. It promotes the idea that citizens and civil society have the right to oversee how risks associated with critical infrastructure are managed, even when such matters involve state security considerations. This fight for transparency is vital for public safety, as it has exposed the absence of a specific nuclear emergency plan in Spain for the Gibraltar area. The inability to access historical documentation hinders independent risk analysis and adequate preparedness for potential incidents, such as collisions or radioactive leaks. Therefore, free access to information is not merely an administrative right, but an essential tool for assessing and mitigating real dangers that affect the population and the environment.
