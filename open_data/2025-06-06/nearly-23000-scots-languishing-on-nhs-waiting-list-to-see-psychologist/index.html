Nearly 23,000 Scots languishing on NHS waiting list to see psychologist

Nearly 23,000 Scots languishing on NHS waiting list to see psychologist

Recent data reveals that nearly 23,000 individuals in Scotland are facing significant delays in accessing psychological treatment, with waiting lists reaching critical lengths across various health boards. The shortage is attributed to an unprecedented surge in demand coupled with severe gaps in the mental health workforce. Experts warn that current figures likely underestimate the true scale of the crisis, as official statistics frequently fail to account for the rising number of people seeking help for neurodevelopmental conditions like autism and ADHD. The situation poses a severe risk to the sustainability of mental health services, with professionals highlighting a "postcode lottery" in care quality and urgent calls for ring-fenced funding. Beyond recruitment, there is a pressing need for strategies to retain experienced staff who are leaving the profession due to excessive workloads and burnout. Without substantial resource allocation at the local level, waiting times are expected to worsen, undermining the government’s own budget commitments and leaving patients in dire straits. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it underscores the limitations of official statistics and the vital role of Freedom of Information requests in exposing systemic failures. By leveraging transparent data, advocates and researchers can challenge institutional narratives, reveal hidden burdens such as unrecorded neurodevelopmental cases, and hold policymakers accountable. This case demonstrates how accessible, granular data is essential for identifying the true scope of public service crises and driving necessary political and structural reforms.
