More than £30 million of Wiltshire’s public pensions fund fossil fuels

The Wiltshire Pension Fund has been identified as a significant contributor to the climate crisis, investing millions into controversial fossil fuel sources such as fracking, tar sands, and arctic drilling. This substantial financial backing highlights a critical disconnect between local councils’ public declarations of climate emergencies and their actual investment behaviors. By channeling public savings into these industries, pension funds are inadvertently profiting polluters, raising serious ethical questions about the alignment of public sector wealth with environmental sustainability goals. This finding is particularly relevant to the open data movement, as it demonstrates the power of transparency in holding institutions accountable. The analysis was made possible through the publication and scrutiny of asset data, allowing civil society organizations to expose the scale of fossil fuel investments across local government schemes. When data is open and accessible, it empowers activists and researchers to quantify the environmental impact of financial decisions, turning abstract budgetary figures into clear evidence of systemic issues. Without such data visibility, the true extent of climate risk embedded in public pension portfolios would remain hidden from public scrutiny. In response to these findings, Wiltshire Council has acknowledged the need to divest from fossil fuels by 2030, aiming to protect long-term investments from climate-related risks. However, the broader implication is that many local funds still lag behind in transitioning their portfolios, with billions still funding the climate crisis. This situation underscores the urgent need for standardized, open reporting of investment holdings across all pension funds. By making this data consistently available, stakeholders can better track progress, enforce commitments to carbon neutrality, and ensure that public funds are actively supporting, rather than hindering, the transition to renewable energy.
