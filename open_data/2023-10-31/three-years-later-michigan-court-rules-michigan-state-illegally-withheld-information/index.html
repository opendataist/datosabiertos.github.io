Three years later, Michigan court rules Michigan State illegally withheld information

This article highlights a critical tension between transparency and privacy in public records requests, specifically focusing on how Michigan schools exploit FERPA exemptions to withhold relevant public information. The core issue is the misuse of federal privacy protections to shield institutional decision-making from public scrutiny, thereby undermining the fundamental purpose of the state’s Freedom of Information Act, which is to ensure government accountability. In a significant legal victory, the Michigan Court of Appeals ruled that records involving students participating in public debates, such as petitioning administrators, are not exempt from disclosure simply because the individuals are enrolled. The court determined that these communications directly concerned the administrative situation in question rather than individual academic or financial records. This precedent clarifies that FERPA should not serve as a blanket shield for university actions merely because students are involved in the correspondence. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how legal loopholes can stifle the free flow of information necessary for civic engagement. It underscores the urgent need for legislative reform to close gaps that allow public entities to operate in secrecy. Ensuring clear definitions for what constitutes private versus public data is essential for maintaining trust in public institutions and enabling effective oversight of those in power.
