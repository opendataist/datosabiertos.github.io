《TAIPEI TIMES》 Name syllable duplication data available for first time - 焦點 - 自由時報電子報

《TAIPEI TIMES》 Name syllable duplication data available for first time - 焦點 - 自由時報電子報

Taiwan’s Ministry of the Interior recently released updated statistics on common personal names and surnames, highlighting shifting cultural trends in nomenclature. For the first time, the data captured names with duplicated syllables, revealing a strong gender disparity where such names are predominantly used by women. Meanwhile, traditional names like Jia-hao and Shu-fen remain the most popular for men and women respectively, indicating a persistence of classic naming conventions over decades. Beyond individual names, the report underscores significant demographic shifts through the aging index of top surnames. The concentration of the population among the ten most common surnames affects more than half the populace, suggesting that identity data and public services must account for this high degree of name overlap. This saturation impacts administrative efficiency, requiring robust systems to distinguish individuals who share identical or highly similar identifiers within official records. This information is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it illustrates the challenges of personal data management in regions with low naming diversity. It emphasizes the need for precise data handling and privacy protection when publishing demographic datasets. Furthermore, these statistics serve as valuable inputs for sociological research and urban planning, demonstrating how open government data can reveal deep-seated societal structures like aging populations and cultural heritage.
