¿Dónde está el pueblo más pobre de España?

The INE’s income distribution atlas reveals stark geographical wealth gaps in Spain, highlighting that the municipalities with the highest average incomes are concentrated in Madrid, Catalonia, and the Basque Country, while those with the lowest reside primarily in Andalusia. This statistical approach, which adjusts household income per inhabitant rather than just per taxpayer, offers a more nuanced view of economic well-being across different regions than traditional tax data alone. This distinction is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates how methodological choices in statistical aggregation significantly impact the narrative of social inequality. By making granular, household-level income data publicly available, open data platforms enable researchers and citizens to better understand localized poverty and prosperity trends beyond aggregate national figures. Ultimately, this transparency empowers communities to analyze their specific economic contexts using standardized, accessible datasets. It underscores the value of open data in fostering informed public debate on regional disparities, allowing for more targeted policy discussions and empowering citizens with clear insights into the financial realities of their immediate surroundings.
