Inflación: se conoce esta semana el dato de agosto, que saltaría a dos dígitos | Análisis

Inflación: se conoce esta semana el dato de agosto, que saltaría a dos dígitos | Análisis

The imminent release of August’s inflation data by the National Institute of Statistics and Census (INDEC) reveals a historic acceleration in prices, with estimates placing the monthly increase in double digits, surpassing levels recorded since 1991. This sharp rebound is primarily attributed to the devaluation of the official exchange rate and to increases in regulated prices, such as those for fuel and transportation. Private consulting firms agree that the exchange rate volatility following the primary elections acted as a key trigger, leading to an immediate pass-through to final prices and widening rather than narrowing the exchange rate gaps. The relevance of this report for the field of open data lies in the critical need for transparent, timely, and methodologically robust official statistics. In contexts of high inflation and divergence between official figures and private measurements, the quality of public data becomes a fundamental pillar for accountability and informed decision-making. The observed divergence among different sources underscores the risks of statistical opacity and the urgency of independent verification mechanisms that enable citizens and markets to trust the available economic information. Furthermore, forecasts indicate that this inflationary shock will set a high baseline for the following month, due to the carryover effect of prices from the second half of August and the persistence of economic uncertainty. This outlook suggests an unstable and sustained price dynamic, intensifying the demand for up-to-date data to monitor the evolution of repressed inflation. Transparency in the publication of these figures is not only vital for understanding the immediate situation but also for evaluating the effectiveness of public policies implemented amid volatility.
