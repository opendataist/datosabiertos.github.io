Attendance at the Fifth Global Meeting of the Open Government Partnership (OGP) revealed a clear conclusion: the fight for transparency, participation, and collaboration is not a solitary endeavor, but a far-reaching collective movement. Far from feeling isolated in their efforts to strengthen democracies, stakeholders discovered that there are thousands of voices worldwide committed to similar ideals. This sense of community dispels the illusion that citizens disregard these values, demonstrating that the call for pluralistic governance resonates significantly. The exchange of experiences among governments, civil society, and academics from multiple continents enabled the identification of common patterns and, crucially, the recognition of recurring errors and obstacles in these processes. By sharing both successes and failures, participants not only validated their own struggles but also generated invaluable collective learning. This shared vulnerability fosters a deeper and more realistic understanding of the structural challenges facing the implementation of open government in diverse contexts. This gathering is relevant to the field of open data because it situates technology within a broader human and political ecosystem. Data are not an end in themselves, but tools to enable citizen participation and accountability. The strength of the open government movement lies in this global network of actors who, by connecting their efforts, can transform abstract principles into tangible realities, ensuring that open innovation effectively addresses social problems and improves people’s lives.
