El impacto de la inteligencia artificial en el arte

The article examines the profound ethical and legal debates ignited by Jason Allen’s AI-generated artwork winning a state fair prize, which sparked controversies over authorship and intellectual property. The central issue is whether creations produced by algorithms trained on vast internet datasets can be considered original human work or if they constitute a collective, open-source effort derived from the original artists whose data was used without explicit consent. Legally, this remains unresolved, as current rulings in the U.S. deny copyright protection to AI-generated pieces for lacking human authorship. This rejection highlights a significant gap in existing frameworks, forcing the art world to reconsider traditional concepts of ownership and creativity. The tension lies in balancing the democratization of art through accessible technology against the potential exploitation of original creators’ works used in training these models. This topic is crucial for open data because it challenges the assumptions behind data usage rights and attribution. As AI systems increasingly rely on publicly available datasets to generate new content, the debate underscores the need for transparent data provenance and equitable credit systems. Understanding who owns the output of systems trained on open data is essential for establishing sustainable and fair models for future creative and technological developments.
