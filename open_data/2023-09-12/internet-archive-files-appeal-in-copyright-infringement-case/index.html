Internet Archive Files Appeal in Copyright Infringement Case

The Internet Archive has formally appealed the recent ruling in Hachette v. Internet Archive, challenging the lower court’s decision that deemed its Controlled Digital Lending program a copyright infringement. This legal battle is pivotal for the open_data community, as it tests whether digital preservation and equitable access to knowledge can coexist with traditional copyright protections. The outcome will significantly impact how institutions manage and share digital information without infringing on intellectual property rights. The core of the dispute involves the Internet Archive’s argument that owning a physical book grants the right to scan and lend a digital copy, provided the original is withheld. The initial ruling rejected this premise, asserting that no legal basis supports creating unauthorized copies to replace physical items. This distinction is critical for open data initiatives that rely on reproducing and distributing information, as it clarifies the boundaries of fair use in digital environments. As the case moves to the Second Circuit, it highlights the tension between preserving cultural heritage and protecting commercial publishing interests. For open data advocates, this appeal represents a crucial precedent that could either expand or restrict the ability to digitize and share public domain and copyrighted materials. The result will define the future of digital libraries and the sustainability of open access models in the modern information landscape.
