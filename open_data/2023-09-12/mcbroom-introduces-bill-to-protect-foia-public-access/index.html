McBroom introduces bill to protect FOIA public access

A Michigan senator has introduced legislation to strengthen public access to government communications, addressing concerns that officials might evade Freedom of Information Act requirements through obfuscation tactics. The proposal follows an incident where Greek characters in an email appeared to conceal sensitive discussions about the state’s water crisis response. By prohibiting the use of coded language, symbols, or non-standard characters to obscure records, the bill aims to close loopholes that allow for selective disclosure or hidden communication. The legislation focuses on ensuring that government transparency mechanisms function effectively without relying solely on the honesty of officials. It clarifies legal intent and imposes stricter penalties for non-compliance, thereby reinforcing accountability. This approach seeks to prevent future attempts to manipulate digital records, emphasizing that any ambiguity regarding disclosure should be resolved in favor of public access. The measure underscores the critical need for robust legal frameworks that adapt to modern communication technologies and prevent administrative evasion. This development is highly relevant to the open_data community as it highlights ongoing challenges in making government information truly accessible and machine-readable. It demonstrates how technical glitches or intentional obfuscation can compromise data integrity and public trust. Strengthening legal standards against such practices ensures that open data initiatives remain effective tools for civic engagement and accountability, protecting the public’s right to scrutinize government actions without technical barriers hindering transparency.
