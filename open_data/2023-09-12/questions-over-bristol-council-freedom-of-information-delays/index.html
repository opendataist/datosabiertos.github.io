Questions over Bristol council freedom of information delays

Bristol City Council faces ongoing scrutiny for its consistently poor performance in handling freedom of information requests, often failing to meet the legal requirement to provide data within four weeks. This systemic delay has drawn stern warnings from the Information Commissioner, highlighting a culture of secrecy that undermines public transparency. The situation is critical because open data serves as a fundamental mechanism for holding public authorities accountable, yet the council’s frequent non-compliance suggests a structural resistance to openness rather than mere administrative inefficiency. The upcoming transition from a directly elected mayor to a committee-based governance model was intended to improve transparency, but councillors warn that these FOI issues will likely persist regardless of the administrative structure. There is concern that the new system will immediately encounter conflicts regarding what information should or must be published, potentially perpetuating the existing barriers to accessing public records. This continuity suggests that the root cause lies deeper than the governance format, involving ingrained practices that may survive leadership changes. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the real-world challenges in ensuring that public information is accessible, timely, and usable. The delays and exemptions hinder the ability of journalists and citizens to engage with critical issues, such as infrastructure safety and planning controversies, which are often revealed through data requests. Ultimately, the case underscores that technical availability of data is insufficient without a cultural and procedural commitment to proactive transparency, a key principle for effective open data ecosystems.
