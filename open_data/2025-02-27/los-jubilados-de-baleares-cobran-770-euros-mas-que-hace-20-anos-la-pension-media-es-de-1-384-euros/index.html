Los jubilados de Baleares cobran 770 euros más que hace 20 años: la pensión media es de 1.384 euros

Los jubilados de Baleares cobran 770 euros más que hace 20 años: la pensión media es de 1.384 euros

Retirees in the Balearic Islands have seen their average pensions more than double over two decades, significantly outpacing inflation and increasing their purchasing power. This revaluation, driven by consumer price index adjustments and structural changes in the labor market that increased contributions, suggests that pensions have gained real value despite rising costs. However, financial security remains precarious due to skyrocketing housing prices and higher local living expenses. These economic pressures force many retirees to return to mainland Spain, highlighting a critical disconnect between nominal income growth and the actual cost of maintaining a dignified life in the region. The situation underscores the tension between statistical gains in pension value and the immediate, severe affordability crisis facing the elderly population. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the necessity of transparent, granular statistics to evaluate social policy effectiveness. It demonstrates that aggregated national indicators often mask local disparities, requiring accessible, localized datasets to accurately reflect regional economic realities. Open data enables researchers and citizens to scrutinize how inflation, housing markets, and wage policies interact locally, ensuring that transparency leads to informed public debate and targeted administrative improvements rather than relying solely on broad, potentially misleading national averages.
