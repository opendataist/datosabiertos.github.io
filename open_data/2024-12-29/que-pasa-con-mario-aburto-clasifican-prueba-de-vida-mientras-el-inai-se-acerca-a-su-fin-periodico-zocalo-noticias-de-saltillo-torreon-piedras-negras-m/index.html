¿Qué pasa con Mario Aburto? Clasifican prueba de vida mientras el INAI se acerca a su fin | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The article highlights a critical juncture for transparency in Mexico, where the imminent dismantling of the National Institute of Transparency (INAI) coincides with government authorities withholding vital information about the physical condition of Mario Aburto Martínez, who is imprisoned for the assassination of Luis Donaldo Colosio. This juxtaposition reveals a concerning trend: as the primary institution guaranteeing the right to access public information is being eliminated, state agencies such as Social Prevention and Readaptation are actively withholding documents related to high-profile cases, thereby obstructing citizen oversight during a transitional period of institutional uncertainty. A central implication of this situation is the erosion of accountability mechanisms for historical crimes. By refusing to provide immediate proof of life or health status for Aburto, and instead delaying access until after the current administration ends, the state is effectively prioritizing secrecy over the public’s right to know. This action undermines decades of progress in transparency, suggesting that without robust independent institutions, even the most significant national cases remain shielded from public scrutiny, leaving victims' families and society in a state of prolonged ignorance regarding justice and truth. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how institutional fragility can lead to the deliberate non-release or delayed release of public records, even when legally requested. It underscores the necessity of strong, autonomous bodies to enforce data transparency and protect civil rights. The scenario serves as a stark warning that without these safeguards, the principle of open government is vulnerable to executive discretion, potentially allowing critical historical and legal information to remain inaccessible indefinitely, which fundamentally compromises democratic transparency and the rule of law.
