Buscar estas 7 palabras en Google pone en riesgo tus datos personales

Cybercriminals are employing a technique known as “SEO poisoning” to compromise user security, demonstrating that even completely innocuous searches can lead to serious attacks. By strategically manipulating search engine ranking algorithms, attackers manage to get malicious sites to appear in the top search results, exploiting the inherent trust that users place in the authority of search platforms. This method enables the theft of personal information, such as banking data, and the infection of devices with malware, all without the victim having any suspicion. Unlike traditional attacks that require suspicious interactions, this tactic exploits legitimate and common searches, significantly increasing the likelihood of success and exposing a much broader audience to privacy and digital security risks. This situation is particularly relevant to the open data community because it highlights how the open infrastructure of the web can be used as an attack vector. It underscores the critical need to develop mechanisms for verifying the integrity and transparency of data and digital results. Understanding these vulnerabilities helps promote more robust standards to protect the accessibility and security of public information against malicious algorithmic manipulation.
