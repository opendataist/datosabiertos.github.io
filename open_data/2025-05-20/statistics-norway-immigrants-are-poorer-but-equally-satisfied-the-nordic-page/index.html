Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions compared to the general population, including lower incomes, worse housing, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Despite these material disadvantages and social challenges, the data reveals a paradoxical resilience: immigrants report levels of life satisfaction equal to those of the native-born population. This suggests that subjective well-being is not solely dependent on objective economic or social status, highlighting the importance of non-material factors in quality of life assessments. A crucial finding is the notably high confidence immigrants have in Norwegian institutions, such as the political system, judiciary, and police. While trust in other individuals remains lower than in the general population, their strong institutional trust and affiliation with Norway indicate successful integration into the civic fabric. This distinction challenges narratives that equate material disparity with social alienation, showing that immigrants can feel politically integrated and secure even when facing socioeconomic hurdles. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistical evidence can reveal complex, counter-intuitive social realities. By making detailed survey results publicly available, authorities enable a nuanced understanding of integration that goes beyond simple metrics. Such open data fosters informed public debate and policy-making, ensuring that efforts to improve living conditions are targeted effectively without ignoring the strong social cohesion already present.
