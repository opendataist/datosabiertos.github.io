En Bolivia la fecundidad cae a 2,1 hijos por mujer, un avance en los derechos sexuales y reproductivos

The article highlights a pivotal demographic shift in Bolivia, where the global fertility rate has dropped to 2.1 children per woman. This reduction signifies a transition towards lower population growth and reflects deeper socioeconomic and cultural changes, particularly regarding sexual and reproductive rights. It marks a significant departure from historical trends, aligning Bolivia with other Andean nations that have achieved similar demographic milestones. This decline is strongly linked to increased access to contraceptive methods and greater female participation in the labor market. The data underscores that improved economic autonomy allows women to make informed decisions about family planning. While this trend represents progress in gender equity and individual freedom, it also presents new challenges for policymakers to ensure that social and economic structures evolve to support a society with a changing population structure. The relevance of this article to open data lies in its demonstration of how rigorous, publicly available statistical surveys drive evidence-based policy. The 2023 Demography and Health Survey illustrates the importance of transparent, high-quality data collection in monitoring public health and social development. By making such detailed insights accessible, governments and researchers can better analyze trends like nutrition and health, ensuring that demographic changes lead to sustainable and equitable societal growth rather than emerging issues.
