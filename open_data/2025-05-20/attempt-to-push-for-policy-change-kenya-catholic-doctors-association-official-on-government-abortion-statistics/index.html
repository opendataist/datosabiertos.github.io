Attempt “to push for policy change”: Kenya Catholic Doctors’ Association Official on Government Abortion Statistics

The article highlights a critical tension between data-driven health policies and ethical frameworks, which is central to open data discourse. It demonstrates how statistical evidence regarding unsafe abortions can be interpreted in conflicting ways, shaping legislative outcomes based on underlying values rather than mere facts. The primary conclusion argues that simply legalizing safe procedures does not address the root causes of unsafe practices. Instead, the perspective presented suggests that promoting abortion contradicts medical ethics and the sanctity of life. This view posits that relying on high-volume statistics may lead governments to adopt policies that undermine traditional family structures and moral standards. This content is relevant to open data as it illustrates the danger of decontextualized metrics. It underscores the necessity of transparent data governance that acknowledges ethical implications. By revealing how specific narratives can manipulate public perception through selective interpretation, it emphasizes the need for critical data literacy to prevent policy decisions driven solely by aggregated numbers without broader societal consideration.
