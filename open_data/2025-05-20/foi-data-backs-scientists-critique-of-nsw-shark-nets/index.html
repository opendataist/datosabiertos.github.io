FOI Data Backs Scientists' Critique of NSW Shark Nets

FOI Data Backs Scientists' Critique of NSW Shark Nets

The recent release of Freedom of Information data reveals that New South Wales’ shark net program is both ecologically devastating and ineffective at ensuring public safety. The overwhelming majority of marine life caught in these nets consists of non-target species, including critically endangered grey nurse sharks and threatened turtles, resulting in high mortality rates for protected wildlife. This indiscriminate killing underscores a significant failure in conservation efforts, as the program harms biodiversity rather than protecting it. Furthermore, official scientific advice confirms that the nets provide no measurable reduction in shark attacks compared to non-netted beaches. Despite this lack of safety benefit, the government continues to maintain an antiquated system that fails to address modern conservation needs. The disconnect between the program’s original objective and its actual outcomes highlights a critical governance issue, where established but flawed policies persist despite clear evidence of their ineffectiveness and harmful side effects. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the power of transparent information in driving policy change. By making detailed catch statistics and independent scientific reports publicly accessible, citizens and advocates can hold authorities accountable and challenge ineffective practices. The case illustrates how accessible data empowers communities to demand evidence-based solutions, such as superior technological alternatives, replacing outdated methods with modern, humane, and effective strategies for coastal management.
