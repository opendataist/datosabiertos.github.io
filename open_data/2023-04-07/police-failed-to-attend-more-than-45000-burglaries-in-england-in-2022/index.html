Police failed to attend more than 45,000 burglaries in England in 2022

The article highlights a significant failure in the UK policing system, revealing that thousands of burglaries go entirely unattended by police, leaving victims without justice and offenders with impunity. This systemic breakdown underscores the urgent need for accountability and reliable law enforcement, as reported incidents often result in no officer visit and high closure rates without suspect identification. Critics argue this crisis stems from broken political promises and under-resourced forces, leading to a "postcode lottery" where safety varies drastically by location. In response, there are calls for statutory guarantees ensuring police attendance at burglary scenes and a restoration of neighborhood policing to rebuild public trust and community safety standards. This issue is highly relevant to open_data because the evidence of these failures was uncovered solely through Freedom of Information requests. It demonstrates how transparent access to government and police datasets empowers opposition parties and citizens to scrutinize official narratives, expose service gaps, and demand legislative action based on empirical reality rather than political rhetoric.
