La Semana Santa como forma de resistencia a la globalización: una mirada desde la antropología

Seville’s Holy Week transcends its religious origins to operate as a complex social and symbolic phenomenon that reinforces local identity in the face of globalization’s homogenizing forces. Anthropologists characterize it as a “total social fact,” wherein the celebration of spring and the sense of community belonging often take precedence over strict doctrinal adherence. This cultural resilience enables individuals to reconnect with their neighborhoods and family histories, providing a sense of rootedness that modern, individualistic society frequently fails to offer. The event functions as a significant form of social resistance, allowing diverse groups—including those with secular or left-leaning political views—to participate in a shared tradition. By portraying religious figures as familiar, approachable characters, the rituals foster a horizontal relationship between the people and the divine, distinct from orthodox hierarchical structures. This accessibility democratizes the experience, enabling it to endure even among those who might otherwise reject institutional religious authority, thereby preserving its heterodox and inclusive character. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how cultural phenomena cannot be reduced to simple quantitative metrics or single categorical labels. Understanding such multifaceted events requires qualitative insights that complement statistical data. It underscores the importance of preserving context and nuance within information systems, illustrating how open access to diverse perspectives helps capture the full complexity of social behaviors that standardized data models might otherwise obscure or misinterpret.
