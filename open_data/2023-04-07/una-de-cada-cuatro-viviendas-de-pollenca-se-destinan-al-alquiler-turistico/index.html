Una de cada cuatro viviendas de Pollença se destinan al alquiler turístico

Representatives of Mallorca’s short-term rental sector argue that the high concentration of vacation homes does not hinder access to primary housing. They maintain that these properties are typically isolated, located outside urban centers, and lack the infrastructure and services required for permanent residence. Moreover, the high prices of short-term rentals make them financially unaffordable as primary residences, thereby dismissing the notion that they replace everyday housing. Municipal management addresses this reality by scaling public services, such as waste collection and cleaning, to handle the seasonal influx of a floating population. Rather than viewing tourism as a negative form of saturation, it is presented as a key economic driver that complements the hotel offering and facilitates wealth distribution. The administration emphasizes that a tourist license does not necessarily imply active use, as a significant portion of licensed properties remain vacant or are used by their owners’ families, contributing to the available housing stock without necessarily encouraging massive speculation. This debate is relevant to open data because it highlights the need for transparent information systems regarding the housing stock. To effectively assess the impact of tourism on the housing market and urban planning, it is crucial to have open data that distinguish between licensed properties and those actually active in the tourist market. Without this level of granularity, the analysis of public policies and the understanding of market dynamics are limited by aggregate figures that obscure the functional reality of the properties.
