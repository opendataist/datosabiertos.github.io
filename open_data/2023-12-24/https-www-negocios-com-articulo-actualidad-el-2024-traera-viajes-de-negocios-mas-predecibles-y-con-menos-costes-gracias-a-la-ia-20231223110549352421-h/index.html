Business tourism in Spain has experienced significant growth, consolidating itself as a key economic driver that has surpassed pre-pandemic levels. This recovery is fueled by higher spending per traveler and a strong willingness to travel, although companies must navigate global uncertainty and the need to maintain work-life balance. The sector faces structural challenges such as cost management and the demand for equal opportunities, which compel organizations to redefine their corporate strategies in response to an increasingly demanding and diverse market. The implementation of artificial intelligence emerges as a transformative element, enabling optimized bookings and more accurate risk prediction. At the same time, sustainability and new technological standards for air distribution, such as NDC (New Distribution Capability), are being integrated into the core of travel programs. These trends reflect an evolution toward more efficient and personalized management, where technology not only reduces costs but also facilitates adaptation to new corporate expectations regarding environmental and operational responsibility. The relevance of this article to open data lies in how transparency and access to information drive strategic decision-making. Effective management of the "duty of care" and the guarantee of equality require precise and accessible data on employee location and well-being, especially in remote work environments. The openness of this data allows organizations to monitor climate and geopolitical risks in real time, ensuring that inclusion and safety policies are based on concrete evidence rather than assumptions, thus promoting fairer and more resilient governance.
