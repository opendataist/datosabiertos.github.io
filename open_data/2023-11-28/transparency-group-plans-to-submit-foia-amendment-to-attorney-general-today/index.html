Transparency group plans to submit FOIA amendment to attorney general today

Arkansas Citizens for Transparency is advancing a bipartisan effort to enshrine government openness into the state constitution. This initiative responds to recent political attempts to weaken existing freedom of information laws, aiming to establish transparency as a fundamental constitutional right. By requiring that any legislative reduction in public access be referred to voters and waiving sovereign immunity in record-request disputes, the measure seeks to create a robust, legally binding framework that protects citizens' ability to access government proceedings and documents. The proposal is notable for its broad coalition of lawmakers, journalists, and legal experts who prioritize transparency above partisan differences. This diverse support base underscores a widespread public demand for accountability, driven by organized consultations that have already refined the measure’s language. The group views this constitutional change not merely as a procedural update, but as a necessary structural reform to ensure that government operations remain visible and responsive to the electorate, thereby strengthening democratic engagement across the state. This effort is critically relevant to the open data movement because it elevates data accessibility from a statutory privilege to a constitutional entitlement. By legally mandating that exceptions to transparency be narrowly construed and establishing a dedicated commission to oversee compliance, the amendment creates a stable environment for data publishers and journalists. It ensures that the flow of public information cannot be easily restricted by shifting political winds, providing the legal certainty needed for long-term open data initiatives and promoting greater trust in public institutions.
