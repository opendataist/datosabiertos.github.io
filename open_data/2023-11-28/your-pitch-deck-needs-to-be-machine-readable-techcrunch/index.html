Your pitch deck needs to be machine-readable | TechCrunch

The rise of AI-driven screening tools in venture capital has created an urgent need for startups to make their pitch decks machine-readable. As investors increasingly rely on algorithms to sift through vast quantities of applications, traditional design-focused decks often fail, leading to misinterpretations that can cost founders critical funding opportunities. This shift implies that accessibility to automated systems is now as important as visual appeal, mirroring how digital content must be optimized for both human readers and search engines. To bridge this gap, founders must prioritize clear, structured text over purely creative typography or image-based layouts. AI tools struggle to extract information from embedded images or ambiguous slide titles, which can result in erroneous categorization and unfair rejection. By using explicit labels, standardizing terminology, and ensuring text is selectable rather than graphic-based, startups can ensure their core message is accurately parsed. This simple adjustment prevents technical failures from obscuring the quality of the business proposition. For the open data community, this trend highlights the broader necessity of structured, accessible data formats in an AI-centric world. Just as pitch decks require machine-readable text to convey meaning effectively, open data initiatives must ensure that information is presented in ways that algorithms can reliably interpret. This not only improves the efficiency of automated analysis but also democratizes access to information, ensuring that valuable insights are not lost due to technical barriers or poor formatting.
