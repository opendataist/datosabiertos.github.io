Los jubilados de Balears cobran 105 euros mensuales menos que la media nacional y 414 euros menos que los vascos

Residents of the Balearic Islands confront a stark economic paradox: despite having one of the highest costs of living in Spain, retirees receive some of the lowest average pensions. This disparity forces many elderly residents to struggle financially, often spending nearly all their income on rent while coping with rising food prices, resulting in a precarious existence despite the region’s overall wealth. The article highlights how inflation exacerbates these hardships, with essential goods becoming significantly more expensive in recent years. As a result, many pensioners are forced to make difficult trade-offs to survive, revealing a systemic gap between regional economic indicators and the actual purchasing power of the elderly population in high-cost areas. This data is crucial for open-data advocates because it underscores the need for transparent, granular socioeconomic statistics. By making detailed pension and cost-of-living metrics publicly accessible, researchers and policymakers can better analyze inequality, advocate for targeted social reforms, and ensure that demographic realities inform effective public policy decisions.
