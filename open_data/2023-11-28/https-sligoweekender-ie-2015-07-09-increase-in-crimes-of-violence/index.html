Recent crime statistics from the Sligo-Leitrim garda division reveal a troubling rise in violent offenses, including sex offences, assaults, and robberies, alongside increases in property damage and drink driving. These figures highlight significant shifts in local safety dynamics, suggesting that certain serious crimes are becoming more prevalent despite some declines in thefts and drug-related incidents. However, the reliability of these specific data points is complicated by recent administrative reviews. The Central Statistics Office resumed publishing crime figures only after addressing irregularities in how gardai recorded incidents on the PULSE system. Issues such as unrecorded crimes, misclassifications, and the improper categorization of non-crime incidents indicate that historical data may have been inconsistent, affecting the accuracy of trend analysis. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the critical importance of data quality and transparency in public datasets. For open data to be useful for accountability and policy-making, underlying recording systems must be robust and consistently verified. Without reliable metadata and quality assurance, even published statistics can mislead stakeholders, emphasizing that clean, trustworthy data is just as vital as data availability itself.
