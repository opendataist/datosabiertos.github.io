Ecosistema de frontera domínico-haitiana capta más de 260 mil toneladas de carbono al año

The report reveals that border ecosystems significantly contribute to climate change mitigation by sequestering substantial amounts of carbon annually. This highlights the critical role of natural habitats in regulating greenhouse gases, positioning environmental conservation as a vital strategy for global climate stability rather than merely a local concern. Furthermore, the analysis underscores that these border regions hold a disproportionate share of the nation’s most valuable ecological assets, including mangroves, coral reefs, and seagrass. This concentration of biodiversity demonstrates that protecting these specific territories is essential for maintaining national ecological balance, as they serve as primary reservoirs for carbon storage and ecosystem regulation services. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it provides transparent, evidence-based metrics that empower policymakers and citizens to advocate for conservation. By making ecosystem valuation metrics publicly accessible, open data fosters accountability and supports informed decision-making regarding sustainable land use and environmental protection policies.
