Twitter's Terms of Service now bans AI data scraping but does that protect you?

X’s recent Terms of Service updates explicitly prohibit third-party crawling and scraping without consent, ostensibly to protect user data and system integrity. However, this restriction does not extend to X itself, which retains the right to collect and utilize user information for internal purposes. This distinction reveals a selective application of privacy controls, where external AI developers are blocked, but the platform continues to harvest data freely. The core implication is that user data will still be used for artificial intelligence training, specifically for Elon Musk’s venture, xAI. Despite public rhetoric against data pillaging, the privacy policy remains unchanged, allowing X to feed its platforms’ content into machine learning models. This indicates that the primary goal of the new rules is not user protection, but rather monopolizing data access for the owner’s specific business interests. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocates because it highlights the growing tension between platform control and data accessibility. By restricting external scraping while maintaining internal access, X creates an artificial scarcity of data, hindering independent research and public oversight. It demonstrates how corporations can leverage privacy policies to consolidate power, effectively privatizing public discourse data for proprietary AI development while denying the broader community equal access to these digital resources.
