La inflación de Uruguay alcanzó el más bajo índice

Uruguay has reached its lowest year-on-year inflation rate since 2005, placing it within the Central Bank’s target range. This decline, the result of months of moderate inflationary pressures, indicates a more robust economic stabilization than analysts had anticipated. The downward trend suggests that monetary policies and external conditions are helping to control prices, providing significant relief to citizens’ purchasing power and reinforcing the credibility of financial institutions in managing the economy. The main explanation for this improvement lies in the drop in prices for fruits and vegetables, driven by the depreciation of the U.S. dollar and the reduction in international raw material costs. Although non-tradable goods continue to exert slightly higher pressure, all indicators point to sustained moderation. Projections suggest that inflation will continue to decline in the coming months, moving even closer to the center of the Central Bank’s target, reflecting a direct correlation between the exchange rate and the stability of consumer prices. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the value of official statistical indices and private consulting firms’ forecasts for decision-making. Transparency in the release of data such as the Consumer Price Index (CPI) enables civil society, academics, and markets to interpret economic realities in real time. By making these data accessible and comparable, it fosters accountability among authorities and facilitates critical analysis, which is essential for an informed democracy and for the development of technology-based solutions grounded in verifiable socioeconomic realities.
