El precio de la vivienda roza los niveles de la burbuja inmobiliaria

The article highlights the rapid recovery of real estate prices in Spain, with indices dangerously approaching the peaks recorded during the previous speculative bubble. This trend reflects a market dominated by an insufficient supply of new construction, which has led to a sharp increase in prices, particularly in high-demand areas, exacerbating the housing shortage and historically disrupting the sector's balance. Market dynamics are influenced by a significant rise in the cost of new housing and a moderate rebound in the secondary market, while overall demand shows signs of cooling due to higher mortgage costs. However, the persistence of high prices is partially sustained by an increased number of cash purchases, which temporarily mitigates the impact of rising interest rates on transaction volumes. This analysis is crucial for the open data community because it illustrates how the integration of public data, such as those from the National Statistics Institute or the General Council of Notaries, enables the detection of critical macroeconomic trends. Transparency in publishing these indicators facilitates citizen oversight and academic research on inequality and access to housing, demonstrating the value of open data for understanding and predicting structural crises in real time.
