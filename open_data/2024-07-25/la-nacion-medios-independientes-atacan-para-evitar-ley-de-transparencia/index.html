La Nación / Medios “independientes” atacan para evitar ley de transparencia

La Nación / Medios “independientes” atacan para evitar ley de transparencia

The article argues that self-proclaimed independent media outlets and NGOs are hypocritically opposing transparency laws for non-governmental organizations while simultaneously receiving significant foreign funding. By attacking politicians who support these transparency measures, these entities mask their own lack of financial accountability, revealing that their advocacy is driven by a desire to protect their own funding streams rather than uphold genuine journalistic or civil society standards. Specifically, the publication "El Surtidor," linked to Memetic Media, is shown to be financed by prominent international foundations such as the Open Society Foundations and the National Endowment for Democracy. Similarly, the NGO Tedic shares an address with Memetic and receives substantial funds from major tech corporations and global funders. The narrative highlights that these organizations benefit from the very opacity the proposed law aims to correct, creating a conflict of interest where they oppose regulations that would expose their own financial dependencies. This piece is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent public registries regarding financial flows and organizational affiliations. It illustrates how closed funding networks can influence public discourse and legislative processes, thereby obscuring the true interests behind seemingly independent civil society actions. The case serves as a practical example of why open data initiatives regarding NGO financing are essential for maintaining democratic integrity and preventing undisclosed foreign influence from shaping public opinion and policy.
