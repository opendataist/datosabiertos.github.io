Decimoquinto año descenso de la población japonesa - Mundo - ABC Color

Japan’s population growth has reached its lowest rate since records began in 1968, underscoring a critical demographic decline driven by record-low birth rates and rising mortality. This shrinking native population stands in stark contrast to a significant surge in the foreign resident population, which has surpassed three million, signaling an increasing reliance on international migration to sustain society. The country faces profound structural challenges, with nearly one-third of its citizens aged sixty-five or older and a shrinking active workforce. These shifts carry severe implications for social security and economic stability, making demographic management a central issue in recent local elections and policy debates. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how accessible, standardized demographic statistics empower public scrutiny. By transparently revealing these alarming trends, open data enables researchers, journalists, and policymakers to analyze societal impacts and advocate for effective responses to the challenges of aging and migration.
