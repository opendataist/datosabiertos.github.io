Three areas in Ireland had more deaths than births in 2021, experimental CSO research finds

This report highlights significant demographic disparities across Ireland’s Local Electoral Areas, revealing that Belmullet, Glenties, and Bantry-West Cork were the only regions where deaths outnumbered births in 2021. These areas exhibited the highest crude death rates and lowest birth rates, indicating a concerning population decline driven solely by natural increase. In contrast, urban hubs surrounding Dublin recorded the highest birth volumes and positive natural increases, underscoring a sharp divide between rural depopulation and urban growth. The findings demonstrate how granular, localized data can expose underlying social and health inequalities that national averages often obscure. By distinguishing between general fertility rates and crude death rates, the analysis shows that urban centers have lower fertility among women of childbearing age, while specific rural areas face disproportionately high mortality from external causes and neoplasms. This level of detail allows for more targeted public health interventions and resource allocation based on specific community needs rather than broad regional generalizations. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it illustrates the power of releasing granular, experimental statistics to inform public discourse. The CSO’s use of the IPEADS platform, despite its evolving nature, promotes transparency and encourages critical engagement with emerging methodologies. Open access to such detailed vital statistics empowers researchers, policymakers, and citizens to identify localized trends, fostering a more evidence-based approach to understanding demographic shifts and addressing regional inequalities.
