MP hides behind national security clause on Project Cheetah

MP hides behind national security clause on Project Cheetah

The Madhya Pradesh forest department has refused to disclose management records regarding the reintroduction of cheetahs and their local offspring, citing national security and foreign relations under Section 8 of the RTI Act. This decision marks a significant departure from previous transparency, as it is the first time information on the Cheetah Project has been withheld since its inception. By invoking broad exemptions, the authorities have closed a critical avenue for public scrutiny, prioritizing state interests over the accountability mechanisms intended to ensure proper conservation practices. This opacity is particularly concerning given the project’s complex history, which has included significant challenges such as the mortality of several adult animals despite recent successes in cub births. The refusal to provide correspondence raises doubts about whether scientific management and restorative measures are being effectively implemented or adequately monitored. Without access to detailed management data, stakeholders cannot verify if the growing population is being handled according to established ecological standards or if there are underlying issues in the operational strategy that warrant public debate. This incident is highly relevant to the open_data movement as it illustrates the tension between government transparency and bureaucratic secrecy in environmental governance. It highlights how sensitive labels like "security" can be used to suppress access to vital information about public initiatives, even when those initiatives involve ecological restoration and public funds. For open data advocates, this case underscores the urgent need to challenge broad exemptions and ensure that conservation projects remain subject to rigorous, accessible public oversight to maintain trust and effectiveness.
