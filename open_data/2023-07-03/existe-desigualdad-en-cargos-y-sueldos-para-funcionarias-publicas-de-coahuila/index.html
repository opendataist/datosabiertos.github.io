Existe desigualdad en cargos y sueldos para funcionarias públicas de Coahuila

The article reveals significant gender disparities in Coahuila’s public sector, where men consistently earn higher salaries than women across major income brackets. This wage gap highlights systemic inequalities in remuneration within state and federal administrations, indicating that women are concentrated in lower-paying roles despite performing similar duties. Furthermore, structural differences in employment contracts exacerbate this imbalance. Men are more likely to hold “trust” positions that often involve greater responsibility and directional authority, whereas women are disproportionately represented in “eventual” or temporary contracts. This contractual disparity suggests fewer opportunities for women to access stable, high-responsibility careers within the government. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it provides empirical evidence of labor inequality. By making such registries transparent, stakeholders can analyze systemic biases, advocate for policy reforms, and ensure equal opportunities. Accessible data empowers society to monitor compliance with pay equity laws and drives accountability in public administration.
