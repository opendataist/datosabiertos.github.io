Recent crime statistics for the Sligo-Leitrim region reveal a complex picture of public safety. While violent offenses, sex crimes, and burglary rates have increased significantly over the past year, certain non-violent categories like fraud, public order offenses, and drug possession have seen notable declines. This divergence suggests shifting patterns in criminal behavior rather than a uniform rise in all types of illicit activity. However, the reliability of these figures is complicated by previous administrative issues within the Garda system. After a pause in data publication due to recording irregularities, authorities resumed sharing statistics following a comprehensive review. Although the overall data volume is back, the review highlighted that a significant portion of reported crimes were either not recorded, misclassified, or incorrectly categorized as non-crime incidents in earlier years. This article is crucial for open data initiatives because it highlights the critical importance of data quality and integrity in public statistics. It demonstrates that raw numbers alone are insufficient; understanding the metadata, recording processes, and historical corrections is essential for accurate analysis. For open data advocates, this serves as a reminder that transparency includes explaining data limitations and quality assurance processes to ensure trustworthy public information.
