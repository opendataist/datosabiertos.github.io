CSO intensifies call for domestication of FOI in Akwa Ibom

Civil Society Organisation CODE advocates for domesticating the Freedom of Information Act in Akwa Ibom State, arguing that transparency yields significant economic and governance benefits. The campaign counters fears that the legislation facilitates witch-hunts against public officials, instead positioning it as a critical tool for establishing an open government framework. This shift is presented as essential for unlocking international funding, such as World Bank grants, which require public budget disclosure, and for attracting foreign direct investment by demonstrating financial accountability to potential partners. Implementing the FOI Act is highlighted as a mechanism to combat corruption and enhance civic oversight. By mandating public access to contract details and project specifications, the law empowers citizens and civil society groups to monitor government expenditures effectively. This increased scrutiny ensures that contractors adhere to agreed standards and that budgetary resources are utilized efficiently, thereby restoring public trust and improving service delivery through accountable administrative practices. This article is relevant to open_data because it underscores the practical necessity of publicly accessible government information for economic development and anti-corruption efforts. It illustrates how structured data transparency serves as a prerequisite for accessing global financial resources and fostering investor confidence. Furthermore, it emphasizes the role of civil society in leveraging open data to hold institutions accountable, reinforcing the broader open_data movement’s goal of using information as a catalyst for democratic engagement and fiscal integrity.
