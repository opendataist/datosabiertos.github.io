Descienden servicios de alojamiento y medios de comunicación en Quintana Roo - Noticaribe

The latest monthly service survey reveals a contraction in the private non-financial sector, highlighting declines in total real revenues, employed personnel, and expenses. This downturn suggests weakening economic momentum in key service areas, particularly within the hospitality and information media industries. These negative trends indicate potential challenges for consumer confidence and business stability in the latter part of the year, signaling a shift from previous growth patterns. Over time, the statistical framework governing these metrics has evolved significantly to improve data granularity and national coverage. Starting with a limited set of activities, the program now encompasses numerous economic domains, incorporating both probabilistic and non-probabilistic sampling designs. This continuous reformulation ensures that the statistics reflect current economic realities, moving from broad national indicators to more detailed sub-sector analyses available for specific federal entities. This evolution is crucial for open data because it demonstrates how public institutions can enhance transparency and utility by expanding dataset accessibility and methodological rigor. By providing detailed, multi-dimensional statistics on a national scale, the government supports evidence-based decision-making for researchers, businesses, and policymakers. The availability of such structured, long-term data allows for better analysis of economic trends, fostering a more informed and responsive public sector through the transparent release of high-quality statistical information.
