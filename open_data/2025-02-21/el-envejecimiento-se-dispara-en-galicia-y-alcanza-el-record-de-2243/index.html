El envejecimiento se dispara en Galicia y alcanza el récord de 224,3%

El envejecimiento se dispara en Galicia y alcanza el récord de 224,3%

Galicia has reached a record aging index, positioning itself as the second most elderly region in Spain. This demographic shift, driven by historically low birth rates and increasing life expectancy, signifies a structural change where the elderly population vastly outweighs the youth. This trend highlights an urgent challenge for the region's social and economic sustainability, as the shrinking proportion of young people threatens the traditional model of generational replacement in the workforce. In response to this reality, experts emphasize the critical need to integrate senior talent into the labor market. The article argues that relying on experienced older workers is no longer optional but essential for maintaining competitiveness and supporting the welfare state. To achieve this, companies must actively dismantle hiring biases against older professionals and invest in their continuous skill development, ensuring they can adapt to evolving industry demands rather than facing premature exclusion. This article is relevant to open data because it relies on statistical indicators, such as the aging index, to drive social and policy decisions. Open data initiatives are vital for tracking these demographic trends transparently and analyzing their impact on employment. By making such granular data accessible, stakeholders can better understand regional disparities, advocate for inclusive labor policies, and measure the effectiveness of strategies aimed at supporting an aging society.
