Calls for Botox crackdown to be put on hold as regulator admits 'evidence gap'

Calls for Botox crackdown to be put on hold as regulator admits 'evidence gap'

The article highlights a critical disconnect between the Scottish Government’s legislative ambitions for regulating non-surgical cosmetic procedures and the practical readiness of the regulator, Healthcare Improvement Scotland (HIS). Despite the law passing with the stated goal of eliminating unsafe practices, internal communications reveal that HIS lacked adequate funding, staffing, and data to implement the new requirements. The regulator warned that projected fees from new clinic registrations would not cover the actual costs of oversight, creating a significant financial deficit and indicating that the government significantly underestimated the resource burden. This lack of preparatory planning poses substantial risks to the open data ecosystem and regulatory transparency. The regulator admitted to having "intelligence and evidence gaps," unable to estimate the number of businesses needing registration or the resulting workload. This opacity prevents accurate public reporting on industry compliance and safety metrics. Without reliable data collection and clear cost modeling, the regulatory framework fails to provide the transparency necessary for effective governance, potentially leaving both the public and small business owners without accurate information on safety standards and operational requirements. GMB Scotland argues that this rushed implementation threatens the viability of hundreds of small clinics, which face high compliance costs and administrative delays. The union contends that ministers either misunderstood these risks or ignored them, forcing compliant businesses to bear the burden of legislative mismanagement. For open data advocates, this case serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of enacting regulations without first establishing robust, transparent, and well-resourced data infrastructures. It underscores that effective regulation requires not just legal authority, but the accurate, accessible, and financially sustainable data systems needed to enforce it fairly.
