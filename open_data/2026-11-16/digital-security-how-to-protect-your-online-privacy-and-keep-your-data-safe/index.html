Digital security: how to protect your online privacy (and keep your data safe)

Digital security: how to protect your online privacy (and keep your data safe)

The article emphasizes that digital privacy is not merely a technical concern, but a fundamental pillar of everyday life in a hyperconnected world. In the face of rising cyberattacks and sophisticated scams, protecting personal information has become an urgent priority, requiring the adoption of basic measures such as strong passwords, two-factor authentication, and the use of virtual private networks to mitigate risks on public connections. The narrative stresses that security largely depends on user habits, from moderating the sharing of data on social media to maintaining constant vigilance against phishing attempts and keeping technology up to date. It underscores that common sense and continuous education are as vital as technology itself, since skepticism toward suspicious offers and prudent management of private information significantly reduce exposure to threats. This reflection is relevant to the realm of open data because it illustrates the other side of the coin: while open data fosters transparency and innovation, individuals and organizations must remain alert to how their own sensitive information may be compromised. Balancing the sharing of open resources with the preservation of personal privacy integrity is essential for building a secure digital society, demonstrating that the protection of individual data is the foundation upon which trust in public information ecosystems is built.
