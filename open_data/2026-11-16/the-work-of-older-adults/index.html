The work of older adults

The work of older adults

The significant increase in the labor reintegration of older adults reflects a growing economic need that exceeds the capacity of existing support programs. Given that a considerable proportion of this population remains productive, it is imperative to adapt the legal framework to protect their labor rights. This trend underscores the urgent need for policies that recognize their ongoing contributions and prevent age discrimination in the current labor market. Legislators are proposing legal reforms to establish a reduced thirty-five-hour workweek without loss of salary or benefits for individuals over the age of sixty. The objective is to ensure that these workers retain their social security coverage, seniority, and income levels, thereby dignifying their economic participation. This measure seeks to balance the health and well-being of older adults with their desire to remain active and independent, ensuring fair and age-appropriate working conditions. This issue is relevant to open data because official figures from the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) demonstrate the scaling of this phenomenon, providing the necessary empirical basis to drive legislative changes. Transparency in these indicators allows for monitoring the effectiveness of future policies and evaluating the impact of the proposed reforms. By visualizing the demographic and labor reality through accessible data, accountability is facilitated and the argument for adapting institutions to an aging yet economically vital population is strengthened, ensuring that public information promotes social justice.
