Rising rents and the dream of homeownership growing ever more distant in the Region | El Dia

Rising rents and the dream of homeownership growing ever more distant in the Region | El Dia

Access to homeownership in La Plata and the surrounding Buenos Aires region is in sustained decline, consolidating renting as a permanent condition for a growing majority of households. This structural trend not only redefines the life plans of younger generations but also hinders youth emancipation and deepens family dependence, signaling a housing crisis that transcends temporary circumstances. Precarity in the rental market is evident through the unequal labor and financial burden placed on tenants, who work more hours, rely more frequently on debt, and live in smaller spaces compared to homeowners. This gap reflects an underestimation of socioeconomic vulnerability by traditional official statistics, which, by ignoring housing expenses, conceal the true magnitude of poverty affecting these families. This phenomenon is relevant to open data because it illustrates how public microdata, when cross-referenced and critically analyzed, reveal inequalities invisible in conventional metrics. The case demonstrates the need to transform raw data into actionable information that makes visible the dynamics of exclusion and wealth concentration, enabling a fairer and deeper understanding of social rights and urban quality of life.
