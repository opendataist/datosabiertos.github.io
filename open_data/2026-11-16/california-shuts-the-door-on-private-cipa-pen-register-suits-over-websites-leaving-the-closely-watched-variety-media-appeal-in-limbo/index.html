California Shuts the Door on Private CIPA Pen Register Suits Over Websites, Leaving the Closely Watched Variety Media Appeal in Limbo

California Shuts the Door on Private CIPA Pen Register Suits Over Websites, Leaving the Closely Watched Variety Media Appeal in Limbo

Senate Bill 690 fundamentally alters the landscape of California privacy litigation by stripping private individuals of the right to sue for website and app tracking violations under the state’s invasion of privacy act. Effective January 1, 2027, and applying retroactively to pending cases filed after early 2025, this legislation restricts enforcement of "pen register" claims exclusively to the state Attorney General. This legislative shift effectively dismantles a prolific wave of private lawsuits that had previously leveraged outdated telecommunications statutes to target digital analytics and cookies, offering businesses significant relief from statutory damages and settlement pressures. The change highlights the growing friction between legacy data protection laws and modern digital tracking technologies. By redefining who can enforce these provisions, the state acknowledges that private litigation strategies have stretched traditional privacy concepts beyond their original intent. However, this is not a total immunity; the Attorney General retains enforcement power, and plaintiffs are expected to pivot toward other legal theories, such as wiretapping claims. The upcoming ruling in *Variety Media v. Superior Court* remains critical, as it may still define the scope of what constitutes a "pen register" in the digital age, even if private rights of action are now removed. This development is highly relevant to open data practices because it signals a move toward more centralized, government-led regulation rather than fragmented private enforcement. For organizations managing public or open datasets, this reduces the risk of frivolous litigation related to standard web analytics while emphasizing the need for robust compliance with state-level oversight. The bill encourages a broader legislative review to balance privacy protection with fair business practices, urging stakeholders to anticipate further regulatory refinements that will likely reshape how open data is collected and processed in California.
