UK: Criminalisation of homelessness escalates under Conservative and Labour law-and-order agenda

The article highlights a resurgence in the criminalization of poverty in England and Wales, where authorities are increasingly using both an archaic Vagrancy Act and modern "anti-social behavior" powers to penalize homeless individuals. This approach prioritizes punishment over support, resulting in arrests, fines, and criminal records for people experiencing homelessness, while major political parties across the spectrum compete to appear tough on issue rather than addressing the root causes of destitution. The relevance to open_data lies in the critical role that transparency plays in exposing the scale and failure of these policies. The investigation relies on Freedom of Information responses to quantify arrests and reveal disparities between police forces, demonstrating how accessible public records can uncover systemic injustices. Furthermore, data regarding evictions, temporary accommodation numbers, and charity funding gaps provides essential evidence of the housing crisis, proving that legal crackdowns are disconnected from the escalating lack of affordable housing and support services. Ultimately, the piece argues that these repressive measures are ineffective and exacerbate the marginalization of vulnerable populations. By using data to show that homelessness is rising alongside service cuts, it underscores the necessity of open data for advocacy and policy reform. Accessible, verifiable information is vital for holding governments accountable and shifting the narrative from criminalization to solving the underlying social and economic drivers of homelessness.
