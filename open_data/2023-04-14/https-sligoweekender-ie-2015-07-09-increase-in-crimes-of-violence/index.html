The article highlights a notable rise in violent crimes within the Sligo-Leitrim region, encompassing offenses such as sexual assaults, robberies, and property damage. This uptick contrasts with declining rates in thefts, fraud, and public order offenses, indicating a complex and shifting criminal landscape. The data underscores the importance of accurate, granular reporting to understand specific security trends rather than relying on generalized statistics. A critical aspect of these findings is the Central Statistics Office’s recent decision to resume publishing crime figures after a prolonged suspension. This pause followed a significant audit that revealed substantial irregularities in how police recorded data on their digital system. The review identified issues ranging from unrecorded reports to incorrect classifications, which had previously compromised the reliability of the published statistics. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the necessity of verifying data quality before public dissemination. For open data to be trustworthy, transparent methodologies and rigorous cleaning processes are essential. The incident serves as a reminder that raw data must undergo comprehensive review to ensure accuracy, preventing the spread of misinformation and maintaining public confidence in statistical transparency.
