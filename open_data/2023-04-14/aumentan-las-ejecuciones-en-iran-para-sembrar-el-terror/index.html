Aumentan las ejecuciones en Irán para sembrar el terror

Aumentan las ejecuciones en Irán para sembrar el terror

Human rights organizations report a significant surge in executions in Iran, highlighting a government strategy to instill terror following nationwide protests. This sharp increase underscores a deliberate policy of repression rather than isolated judicial actions, signaling that the state is utilizing capital punishment as a primary tool for political control and social intimidation. The data reveals that the state disproportionately targets marginalized ethnic and religious minorities, particularly in regions such as Baluchistan, Kurdistan, and among Arab communities. This demographic skew demonstrates that the death penalty functions as an instrument of systemic discrimination, where vulnerable populations face harsher justice compared to the majority, reinforcing structural inequalities and suppressing dissent among specific groups. This case is critical for open data advocates as it illustrates how transparent, verifiable statistics can expose systemic human rights violations that governments often obscure. Reliable data enables civil society to hold authorities accountable and push for international policy changes, such as conditioning diplomatic cooperation on the abolition of the death penalty for drug offenses, proving that open information is essential for effective advocacy.
