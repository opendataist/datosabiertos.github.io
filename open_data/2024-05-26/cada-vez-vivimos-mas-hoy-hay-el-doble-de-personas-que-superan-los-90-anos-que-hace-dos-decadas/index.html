Cada vez vivimos más: hoy hay el doble de personas que superan los 90 años que hace dos décadas

The article highlights a significant demographic shift in Spain, where increased longevity has led to a substantial rise in deaths among individuals over ninety. This trend underscores the country’s exceptionally high life expectancy within the European Union, resulting in an increasingly aged population structure that directly challenges the financial sustainability of the Social Security system. Concurrently, persistently low birth rates have exacerbated this aging dynamic, creating a dependency ratio that places a heavier burden on the working-age population. The disparity in these trends across different autonomous regions further illustrates the uneven geographic distribution of demographic pressure, demanding nuanced policy responses tailored to local realities rather than a one-size-fits-all national strategy. This report is relevant to open data because it relies on granular, time-series statistical evidence from official sources like the National Statistics Institute to reveal long-term structural changes. It demonstrates how transparent, accessible demographic datasets are crucial for policymakers to identify emerging social pressures and plan for future economic impacts, showcasing the vital role of open data in informed public administration and societal planning.
