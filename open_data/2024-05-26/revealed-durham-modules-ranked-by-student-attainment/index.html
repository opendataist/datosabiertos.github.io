Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

This article demonstrates how Freedom of Information requests can unlock granular academic performance data, highlighting a critical opportunity for transparency in higher education. By analyzing departmental and individual module statistics, the report exposes significant disparities in grading standards across different disciplines. This level of detail allows stakeholders to move beyond superficial institutional rankings and scrutinize the actual educational experiences students encounter. The findings reveal stark contrasts in attainment, with certain subjects achieving remarkably high first-class rates while others lag significantly. Such data suggests that grading practices may vary widely between departments, potentially influenced by factors like module structure or student demographics. These inconsistencies underscore the need for standardized assessment frameworks to ensure fairness and equity for all students, regardless of their chosen field of study. Relevance to open_data lies in the power of public access to drive accountability. When institutions release detailed performance metrics, it fosters informed decision-making for prospective students and encourages departments to justify their grading standards. Ultimately, this transparency promotes a culture of continuous improvement, ensuring that academic outcomes are measured consistently and that resources are allocated to support areas with historically lower performance.
