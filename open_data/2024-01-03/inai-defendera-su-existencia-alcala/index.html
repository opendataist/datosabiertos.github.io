The article asserts that the right to access information and the protection of personal data must remain neutral, resisting partisan or ideological interference. These rights form a vital foundation for transparency and the protection of human rights, serving as essential tools to combat corruption rather than being ineffective. This perspective highlights how access to information enables citizens to document cases of corruption and secure other fundamental rights, such as justice and state oversight. Consequently, these mechanisms are not merely bureaucratic procedures but critical instruments for democratic accountability and societal integrity. Understanding this link is crucial for the open data community, as it underscores that making public data accessible is a prerequisite for the enforcement of human rights. Without robust open data frameworks, the ability to expose corruption and ensure government transparency diminishes, thereby weakening democratic institutions.
