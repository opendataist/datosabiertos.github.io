The article highlights critical failures in public safety and healthcare within Oaxaca de Juárez, revealing a disconnect between official recognition of crises and effective action. Authorities acknowledge severe insecurity, supported by citizen data showing high levels of fear, yet resource constraints and a lack of strategic implementation leave the police force under-equipped and ineffective. This negligence not only endangers residents but also threatens the local economy by damaging the region’s tourism appeal, illustrating how poor governance directly impacts social stability and economic vitality. Simultaneously, the healthcare sector suffers from long-term institutional neglect, characterized by dilapidated facilities, insufficient medical supplies, and a reliance on out-of-pocket expenses for essential medicines. Despite budgetary allocations and rhetorical commitments to improve living conditions for vulnerable populations, bureaucratic inertia prevents tangible improvements. The persistence of these deficiencies underscores a systemic issue where political promises consistently fail to translate into accessible, dignified medical services for the community. This report is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, accessible statistics to validate citizen concerns and drive policy change. By citing INEGI data and contrasting it with official inaction, the article illustrates how open information can expose governance gaps. It emphasizes that for open data to be truly effective, it must be integrated into public discourse to hold authorities accountable, ensuring that statistical evidence leads to concrete improvements in public safety and health infrastructure rather than remaining as mere acknowledgments of failure.
