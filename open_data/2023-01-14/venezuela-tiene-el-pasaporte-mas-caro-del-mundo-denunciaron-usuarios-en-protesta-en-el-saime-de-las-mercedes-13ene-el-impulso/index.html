▷ Venezuela tiene el pasaporte más caro del mundo denunciaron usuarios en protesta en el Saime de Las Mercedes #13Ene - El Impulso

Venezuelan civil society highlights the severe barriers citizens face in obtaining passports, citing exorbitant fees, systemic corruption, and administrative failures. The exorbitant cost, which dwarfs regional averages, acts as a de facto restriction on freedom of movement, forcing vulnerable populations into dangerous migration routes or preventing them from accessing basic rights abroad. This situation underscores the critical link between administrative transparency and human rights. When public institutions lack transparency and are perceived as corrupt, the fundamental right to identity and information is compromised. The arbitrary pricing and hidden costs violate constitutional guarantees, demonstrating how opaque governance directly obstructs civic mobility and social integration. This case is highly relevant to open_data as it illustrates the consequences of non-transparent public management. It highlights the urgent need for open, accessible data regarding public service pricing and processing times. Without transparent data, citizens cannot hold institutions accountable, and the state fails to ensure equitable access to essential documents, reinforcing exclusion rather than facilitating rights.
