New analysis reveals that numerous tax reliefs in the UK are expensive and rarely scrutinized for their value, with total costs rising significantly. Five specific reliefs stand out as particularly unjustified, costing billions annually while benefiting only a tiny fraction of the population. These breaks provide substantial advantages to wealthy individuals without clear evidence of encouraging entrepreneurship or addressing genuine need, suggesting they are inefficient uses of public resources. The article highlights that these specific tax breaks, primarily affecting capital gains and inheritance, are used by a minuscule percentage of taxpayers who receive enormous average benefits. Despite their high cost, there is little proof that these measures achieve their intended economic goals. The lack of efficacy, combined with the potential for public distrust regarding wealth inequality, marks these policies as prime candidates for reform or elimination by the government. This finding is crucial for open data advocates as it demonstrates how transparent tax statistics can expose policy inefficiencies. By publishing detailed data on who benefits and at what cost, regulators can hold the government accountable for fiscal decisions. This transparency empowers civil society and researchers to identify wasteful spending, advocating for a tax system that prioritizes fairness and economic effectiveness over obscure, costly exemptions.
