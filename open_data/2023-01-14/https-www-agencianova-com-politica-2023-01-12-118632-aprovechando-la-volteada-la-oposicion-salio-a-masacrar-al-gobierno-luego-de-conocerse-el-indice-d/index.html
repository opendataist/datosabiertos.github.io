The article highlights the intense political backlash in Argentina against the ruling party’s economic management, triggered by official statistics revealing an annual inflation rate of nearly 95%. This figure represents the highest increase in three decades, severely impacting citizens' purchasing power and pushing poverty rates to alarming levels. The opposition leveraged this data to criticize the government's failure to control prices, framing the crisis as a result of neglected priorities, lack of a coherent economic plan, and excessive fiscal deficits. Critics from across the political spectrum, including prominent opposition leaders, argued that the administration failed to honor its initial promises to fight inflation. They emphasized that these statistics are not merely abstract numbers but reflect the real suffering of families unable to make ends meet. The political discourse focused on the need for immediate structural changes, such as reducing state bureaucracy, ensuring legal certainty, and implementing serious investment plans to restore economic stability. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how high-quality, publicly accessible statistical data serves as a critical tool for political accountability. The publication of official inflation figures by the national statistics institute allowed for independent verification and public debate, enabling the opposition to hold the government accountable for its performance. It illustrates the vital role of transparent data in democratic societies, where accurate information empowers citizens and political actors to assess governance effectiveness and demand evidence-based policy decisions.
