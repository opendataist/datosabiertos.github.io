Provincia de Bío Bío: sólo el 8% de imputados por tráfico de drogas quedó en prisión preventiva

The article highlights significant transparency efforts by Radio Bío Bío, which successfully used the Law of Transparency to obtain detailed judicial data regarding drug trafficking prosecutions. This outcome underscores the critical role of open data initiatives in ensuring accountability within the justice system, allowing citizens and the media to scrutinize how legal authorities manage case burdens and individual rights. Analysis reveals that the vast majority of individuals accused of drug trafficking in the Biobío region were not subjected to pre-trial detention. This statistical reality challenges assumptions about punitive trends, suggesting that the legal system frequently releases defendants without restrictive measures. It emphasizes a gap between public perception of strict enforcement and the actual procedural outcomes observed in regional courts. Judicial explanations clarify that pre-trial detention is reserved for serious crimes involving specific risks, such as flight or societal danger. Consequently, the low imprisonment rates for drug offenses reflect legal standards rather than negligence. This distinction is vital for understanding open data, as it provides empirical evidence that contextualizes legal decisions beyond mere arrest numbers, promoting a more nuanced public discourse on justice and transparency.
