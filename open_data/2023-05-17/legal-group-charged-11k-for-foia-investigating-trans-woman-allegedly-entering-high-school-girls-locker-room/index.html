Legal group charged $11K for FOIA, investigating trans woman allegedly entering high school girls locker room

The incident highlights a significant friction point between transparency mandates and privacy protections within educational institutions. A legal group sought extensive records regarding a locker room privacy breach, arguing that the school district failed to address the violation adequately. However, the district maintained that the matter involved confidential student data, protected by strict state statutes. This conflict underscores the complex challenge public entities face when balancing the public’s right to know with the legal obligation to safeguard individual student privacy, particularly in sensitive situations involving minors. The financial implications of such requests serve as a notable barrier to accessing information. The school district estimated that fulfilling the broad records request would cost over eleven thousand dollars, citing the need for significant administrative and IT resources. This substantial fee reflects the reality that open data requests are not merely symbolic gestures but involve tangible operational costs. Consequently, high charges can act as a deterrent, effectively limiting who can afford to exercise their right to access public records, regardless of the request’s merit or urgency. This story is relevant to open data discussions because it illustrates how cost and privacy laws can restrict the flow of public information. It demonstrates that the principle of openness is often mediated by economic feasibility and legal constraints, rather than absolute availability. Understanding these barriers is crucial for advocates seeking to ensure that transparency initiatives do not inadvertently exclude citizens or organizations due to prohibitive expenses or conflicting privacy regulations.
