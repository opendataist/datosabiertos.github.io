Irish renters turn to anti-depressants and sleeping tablets over eviction ban

Irish renters turn to anti-depressants and sleeping tablets over eviction ban

The lifting of the eviction ban has created a severe humanitarian crisis for vulnerable tenants, forcing many into mental health decline due to the immediate threat of homelessness. Without the protective pause, renters facing eviction are experiencing extreme distress, with some resorting to medication to cope with the sudden loss of housing security. This highlights a critical gap in social safety nets for older and lower-income individuals who cannot access council housing or private rentals, revealing that the transition from emergency measures to normal market conditions leaves the most precarious populations exposed to sudden displacement and psychological trauma. Conversely, the rental market is simultaneously contracting as small and accidental landlords exit the sector, driven by unsustainable tax burdens and strict rent controls. Many property owners report that regulatory pressures make it financially unviable to maintain rentals, prompting them to sell to councils or withdraw from the market entirely. This exodus reduces the overall supply of available housing, creating a paradox where tenants are terrified of eviction while the number of available properties shrinks, suggesting that current fiscal policies are inadvertently worsening the housing shortage rather than stabilizing the market for long-term tenants. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it exposes the urgent need for transparent, accessible datasets to understand the true scale of the housing crisis. By analyzing public records on evictions, landlord exits, and tenant demographics, researchers and policymakers can identify systemic failures and demographic vulnerabilities that anecdotal evidence alone cannot fully capture. Open data initiatives can empower communities to hold governments accountable, ensuring that policy decisions are based on comprehensive, real-time evidence of housing market dynamics and human impact rather than political expediency.
