China: la tasa de desempleo juvenil alcanza su nivel más alto

China is confronting unprecedented levels of youth unemployment, signaling a severe challenge in the post-pandemic economic recovery. This crisis highlights a growing disconnect between the outcomes of higher education and a shrinking job market, particularly in sectors that were previously booming, such as technology and real estate. The situation reveals deep structural issues, where intense societal competition and regulatory shifts leave young graduates struggling to find meaningful employment, prompting a cultural shift toward disengagement. The struggles of young workers illustrate the human cost of these macroeconomic trends, as many face prolonged joblessness despite submitting numerous job applications. This reality has fueled the "tangping" (lying flat) phenomenon, in which young people choose to withdraw entirely from the competitive labor market. The lack of opportunities and recognition for new entrants creates a sense of hopelessness, demonstrating how policy failures in aligning vocational training with market needs and in regulating key sectors directly impact individual life trajectories and social stability. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent, granular, and reliable statistics to track such socio-economic phenomena. Accurate data on youth unemployment enables better policy evaluation and public accountability. Without open access to high-quality datasets, stakeholders cannot fully grasp the scale of the crisis or design effective interventions, making data transparency essential for addressing systemic labor market failures and ensuring informed public discourse.
