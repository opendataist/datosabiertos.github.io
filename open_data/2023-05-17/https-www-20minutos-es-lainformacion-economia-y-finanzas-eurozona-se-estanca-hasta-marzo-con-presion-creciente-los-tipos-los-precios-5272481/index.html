The Eurozone remains stagnant due to persistent inflation and the rising cost of credit, constrained by the European Central Bank’s restrictive monetary policy. The increase in prices, particularly driven by food costs, generates structural pressures that necessitate maintaining high interest rates, thereby hindering economic recovery and prolonging uncertainty in the financial environment. The European Commission’s forecasts suggest a slower-than-expected moderation in inflation, which reduces medium-term growth prospects. Although the containment of energy prices allows for a slight improvement in estimates, the tightening of financing conditions will continue to limit commercial activity and regional GDP expansion, consolidating a macroeconomic scenario characterized by caution. This analysis is relevant to open data because it underscores the need to monitor economic indicators in real time to understand the social impact of monetary policies. Transparent dissemination of data on inflation, country-specific growth, and access to credit enables citizens and developers to create tools that visualize these regional disparities. Furthermore, it facilitates the production of independent analyses that can critique or validate institutional decisions, promoting more responsible, evidence-based governance that is accessible to all.
