Con internet sólo 3 de cada 10 hogares pobres

Con internet sólo 3 de cada 10 hogares pobres

The article reveals a persistent digital divide in Mexico, where internet access remains disproportionately scarce among low-income households compared to high-income groups. This disparity highlights a critical socioeconomic barrier, suggesting that technological adoption is not merely a matter of availability but is deeply rooted in existing wealth inequalities. For open data advocates, this underscores the urgent need to track and analyze income-based gaps in connectivity to ensure inclusive digital development policies. Despite overall growth in internet usage and higher engagement among women, the data shows a stark urban-rural split in access. Smartphones are the dominant device for connection, indicating a shift toward mobile-first internet consumption across the population. These trends imply that future infrastructure planning must prioritize mobile accessibility and rural coverage to bridge the geographic and technological gaps identified in national statistics. Furthermore, the technology sector workforce is heavily male-dominated, with low average wages that often hover near minimum income levels. This gender and pay inequality within the ICT industry reflects broader structural issues that open data initiatives aim to expose. By making this employment data transparent, stakeholders can better understand the human side of the digital economy and advocate for equitable professional opportunities in the tech sector.
