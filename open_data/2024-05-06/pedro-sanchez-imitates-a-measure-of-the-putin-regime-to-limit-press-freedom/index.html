Pedro Sánchez imitates a measure of the Putin regime to limit press freedom

The article argues that recent proposals by Spanish political leadership to mandate "collegiate journalism" constitute a severe threat to freedom of information and expression. By suggesting mandatory professional registration to control media intrusion and combat disinformation, the measures effectively bypass individual rights, creating a system where state-approved bodies dictate journalistic legitimacy. This approach undermines the fundamental right to seek, receive, and impart ideas without interference, violating international human rights standards. Historical parallels are drawn between these contemporary proposals and the oppressive press controls established during Spain’s dictatorship, as well as authoritarian registries in Russia. The author contends that such mechanisms are not designed to protect democracy but to silence dissent and centralize control over public discourse. Linking these tactics to regimes known for suppressing free speech highlights the dangerous precedent of using national security narratives to justify censorship, thereby eroding democratic institutions and judicial independence. This issue is critically relevant to open data because it illustrates how regulatory frameworks can be weaponized to restrict access to information and limit who is permitted to share it. When the state controls the definition of legitimate journalism, it often correlates with the suppression of transparent data practices and independent scrutiny of government actions. Protecting open data requires safeguarding the journalists and platforms that disseminate this information, ensuring that access remains unrestricted and free from political interference or mandatory professional gatekeeping.
