La deforestación mundial se mantiene alta, aunque con mejoras en Brasil y Colombia

The global loss of primary tropical forest in 2023 remained stubbornly high, with areas equivalent to hundreds of football fields disappearing every minute. Although a significant decline in deforestation rates compared to the previous year was achieved through enhanced protection efforts in Brazil and Colombia, these gains were largely offset by increased destruction in nations like Bolivia, Laos, and Nicaragua. Consequently, the overall trajectory remains insufficient to meet international climate targets, indicating that the world is not on the path to halting forest loss by 2030. This environmental degradation resulted in the release of massive amounts of carbon dioxide, undermining global efforts to mitigate climate change and protect vital biodiversity. While political leadership successfully reduced forest clearing in key Amazonian nations, the persistence of high loss rates elsewhere highlights the fragility of regional successes. The data reveals that without immediate, widespread action across all affected regions, the planet will continue to lose one of its most effective tools for absorbing carbon and sustaining ecosystems, despite temporary improvements in specific countries. This article is highly relevant to open_data because its findings rely entirely on the transparency, accessibility, and interoperability of geospatial datasets. The World Resources Institute’s ability to quantify these losses and compare trends across borders depends on the continuous sharing of satellite imagery and verification methods. Open data standards enable independent monitoring, foster accountability among governments, and allow civil society to track progress toward environmental commitments, proving that accessible information is a prerequisite for effective global environmental governance.
