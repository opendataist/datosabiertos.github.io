Disproportionate? —

The Philippines experienced a rise in unemployment to a three-year high, despite achieving robust economic growth. This paradox is largely attributed to seasonal agricultural delays caused by weather and a surge of fresh graduates entering the labor market. The data reveals that while overall employment numbers shifted slightly, the quality of jobs improved significantly, with more full-time positions and fewer part-time roles, suggesting a structural shift toward more stable income sources for workers. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how accessible official statistics can foster public debate and policy analysis when presented clearly. The labor force survey provides transparent metrics that allow experts to critique the disconnect between GDP growth and job creation. By making these indicators available, the government enables independent scholars and civil society to hold officials accountable, ensuring that economic narratives are grounded in empirical evidence rather than political rhetoric. However, the report also highlights the challenges of interpreting open data without adequate context or complementary qualitative analysis. Experts warn that official figures alone do not capture underlying issues like power costs or smuggling, necessitating broader investigations. The availability of this data serves as a starting point for advocacy, urging policymakers to prioritize education and agricultural diversification. Ultimately, open employment statistics empower citizens to demand more inclusive growth strategies that translate national wealth into tangible opportunities for the workforce.
