Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022 - Palatinate

Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022 - Palatinate

This article highlights how accessible public data, obtained through Freedom of Information requests, can reveal granular insights into academic performance that are otherwise opaque to the general public. By leveraging FOI mechanisms, independent outlets can extract specific educational metrics from institutions, demonstrating the practical power of open government data in areas beyond simple fiscal transparency. This example underscores how statutory information rights can be utilized to enhance accountability and provide a deeper understanding of institutional operations and outcomes. The findings illustrate significant disparities in student achievement across different departments and specific modules, suggesting that attainment levels vary widely regardless of subject matter or academic year. These variations point to potential inconsistencies in assessment standards or the influence of specific module structures on student success. Such data allows for a more nuanced discussion about what drives high or low performance, moving beyond general rankings to examine the specific components of university education that lead to different outcomes for students. For the open data community, this case is relevant because it showcases the tangible value of releasing granular, dataset-level information to the public. It demonstrates that when universities share detailed performance metrics, it empowers stakeholders to analyze trends, question anomalies, and engage in informed debates about educational quality. This approach transforms static institutional reports into dynamic resources for scrutiny, encouraging a culture where data availability directly contributes to transparency and continuous improvement in higher education systems.
