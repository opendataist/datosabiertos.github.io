Canasta básica: en junio, una familia tipo necesitó casi $1.130.000 para no ser pobre

Canasta básica: en junio, una familia tipo necesitó casi $1.130.000 para no ser pobre

The article reveals that Argentina’s inflation surged in June, driven by rising costs for education and housing, which directly impact household budgets. This economic pressure is critical because it determines whether families can meet their basic needs, highlighting the urgent necessity for accurate, timely statistical data to understand these living standards. Open data is essential here, as precise indices like the Basic Food Basket and the Total Basic Basket allow researchers and policymakers to quantify poverty and indigency thresholds. Without transparent, freely accessible datasets from national institutes, it would be impossible to track these economic shifts or assess the true scale of financial vulnerability across different regions. The core implication is that open data empowers society to hold institutions accountable and design effective social policies. By making inflation and poverty metrics publicly available, stakeholders can advocate for interventions that protect vulnerable populations. Therefore, maintaining robust open data infrastructures is not just a technical requirement but a fundamental tool for social justice and economic stability in times of crisis.
