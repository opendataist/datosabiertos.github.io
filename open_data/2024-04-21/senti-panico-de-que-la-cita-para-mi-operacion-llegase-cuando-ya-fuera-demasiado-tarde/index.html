«Sentí pánico de que la cita para mi operación llegase cuando ya fuera demasiado tarde»

The healthcare system is facing a structural crisis marked by surgical and specialized care waiting lists that have reached historic levels. This deterioration is not merely a matter of statistical delays; it causes real human suffering, with patients enduring intense pain, psychological decline, and loss of autonomy for months or even years. The system’s inefficiency turns medical care into a source of constant uncertainty, forcing people to postpone vital life plans and rely on emergency services for unresolved pain, thereby revealing a serious failure in guaranteeing basic health rights. The opacity in managing response times and the lack of transparency regarding delays exacerbated by system saturation are critical issues that require monitoring and correction. Referring patients among specialists to reduce official waiting lists, rather than facilitating genuine access to necessary surgery, distorts the quality of care and increases the risk of irreversible complications. This underscores the need for mechanisms that enable citizens to verify the actual status of their treatment and hold authorities accountable when legal healthcare response deadlines are violated. This case is relevant to open data because it highlights the urgent need for open, updated, and disaggregated health data. Transparency in publishing real waiting lists, referral times, and complaint outcomes would allow civil society and the press to monitor the system’s effectiveness. Moreover, it would foster accountability among public administrations, ensuring that health policies are based on concrete evidence rather than mere promises, thereby improving public health through shared informational responsibility.
