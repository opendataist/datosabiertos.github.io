The article highlights a significant shift in Baja California’s demographic and labor landscape, noting that nearly half of working mothers are employed in the industrial sector. At the same time, the region reports one of the nation’s lowest fertility rates, reflecting a broader national trend toward smaller families or childlessness. These insights are crucial for open data initiatives, as they demonstrate how public statistical agencies make critical socioeconomic trends accessible to the public. Transparent data on gender-based employment and demographic changes enables researchers, policymakers, and citizens to analyze structural shifts and advocate for evidence-based social policies. Ultimately, the publication of such detailed regional statistics underscores the importance of open government data in fostering accountability. By providing clear metrics on labor participation and birth rates, these open datasets empower communities to understand and respond effectively to evolving social dynamics.
