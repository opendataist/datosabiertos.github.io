Comments on AI taking away jobs non-sense: MoS IT Rajeev Chandrasekhar - ET Telecom

The Minister of State for Information Technology dismissed fears that artificial intelligence will eliminate jobs in India, labeling such concerns as baseless. He argued that current AI technologies are task-focused and designed to enhance efficiency by mimicking human behavior, rather than replacing workers entirely. This perspective challenges the widespread anxiety regarding automation and suggests that technological advancements should be viewed as tools for productivity improvement rather than existential threats to employment. To support domestic innovation, the government is launching a curated data sets program, providing anonymized government data to Indian researchers and start-ups. This initiative aims to foster a robust ecosystem for AI development, ensuring that local entities have access to the necessary resources to build sophisticated models. By facilitating data access, the government seeks to accelerate the growth of India’s AI sector and encourage private investment in next-generation computing technologies. This policy is directly relevant to the open data movement, as it demonstrates how public sector data can be responsibly shared to drive technological progress. By anonymizing and curating government data for research, the initiative highlights the potential of open data to stimulate innovation while maintaining privacy. It underscores a strategic approach to utilizing public information assets to build a competitive, world-class semiconductor and AI ecosystem, proving that transparent data access can catalyze economic growth and technical expertise.
