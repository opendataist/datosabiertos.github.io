Las personas de 65 años o más que viven solas en Santa Cruz ya superan las 8.000

The aging of the population and the rise in single-person households among those aged 65 and over have solidified unwanted loneliness as a critical priority for public administration. Demographic data reveal a clear trend toward the feminization of this situation, with older women constituting the absolute majority of those living alone, thereby necessitating the adaptation of social policies to address a specific and growing vulnerability. This vulnerability has been exacerbated by the pandemic, which significantly increased feelings of isolation even among individuals who had not previously experienced them. As a result, more older adults at risk have been identified, compelling social services to intensify their interventions through teleassistance and programs focused on leisure and social cohesion. Coordination between health and social services has become essential to provide effective supervision and support, addressing not only care needs but also emotional well-being. This article is relevant to the open data community because it illustrates how the use of detailed census statistics can transform abstract demographic data into tangible evidence for public action. Transparency in data on household composition, age, and gender facilitates the early detection of social trends, enabling local governments to design precise interventions and measure the impact of their policies against loneliness, thereby demonstrating the value of open data in governance aimed at achieving social outcomes.
