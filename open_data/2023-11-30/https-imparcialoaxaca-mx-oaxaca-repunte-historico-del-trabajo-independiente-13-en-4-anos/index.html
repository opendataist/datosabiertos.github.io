Oaxaca leads the nation in independent employment, which accounts for nearly half of its workforce. This significant prevalence highlights a labor market structure in which the majority of workers operate without traditional employer protections, relying on self-direction rather than formal contracts. The sustained growth in this sector over recent years indicates a deepening shift toward non-standard employment arrangements across the region. The broader national context reveals that independent workers constitute a substantial portion of the overall labor force, growing steadily since the onset of the pandemic. This trend suggests an economic transition in which individuals increasingly pursue autonomous work or engage in necessity-driven employment outside traditional job arrangements. Consequently, the lack of social security and job stability for these workers poses ongoing challenges for social protection systems and economic equity nationwide. This article is crucial for open data because it demonstrates how government statistics, specifically those from INEGI, enable the monitoring of labor market trends and social vulnerability. By making such granular employment data publicly available, researchers and policymakers can identify regional disparities and advocate for improved regulatory frameworks. Open access to this information empowers civil society to analyze structural inequalities and push for data-driven reforms that support informal and independent workers.
