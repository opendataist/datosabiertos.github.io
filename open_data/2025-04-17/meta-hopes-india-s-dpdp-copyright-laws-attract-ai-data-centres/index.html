Meta hopes India's DPDP & copyright laws attract AI data centres

Meta hopes India's DPDP & copyright laws attract AI data centres

Meta aims to position India as a premier jurisdiction for AI model training by leveraging exemptions in the Digital Personal Data Protection Act for publicly available personal data. The company hopes that combining these privacy exemptions with copyright provisions allows for efficient data scraping and storage. This legal landscape suggests that if regulatory frameworks permit verification of public data status, India could become highly attractive for establishing data centers and training artificial intelligence models. However, significant legal ambiguities remain regarding copyright infringement during the AI training process. While some experts argue that incidental storage for transformative model training may be exempt, others contend that India lacks a specific text and data mining exemption. The ongoing court proceedings highlight the tension between protecting intellectual property rights and enabling data processing for AI, creating uncertainty for companies seeking to rely on current statutory interpretations for their data ingestion strategies. This debate is critically relevant to open data principles, as it exposes conflicts within India’s own regulatory framework. Requirements for data deletion by social platforms clash with the need to preserve copyrighted user-generated content for algorithmic analysis. These internal contradictions illustrate the challenges of balancing individual privacy and intellectual property rights with the open access to data necessary for technological innovation, underscoring the need for clearer, harmonized regulations that support both rights and open data ecosystems.
