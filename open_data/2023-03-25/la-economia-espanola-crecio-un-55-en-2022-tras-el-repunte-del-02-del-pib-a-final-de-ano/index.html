La economía española creció un 5,5% en 2022 tras el repunte del 0,2% del PIB a final de año

The article confirms that Spain’s economy experienced a robust recovery in 2022, with GDP growth outperforming government projections and marking the strongest expansion since 1973. This surge represents a significant rebound from the pandemic-induced recession, driven primarily by a shift toward external demand, as domestic consumption and investment contributed less to growth compared to the previous year. Despite this momentum, the fourth quarter showed signs of economic cooling, with notable decelerations in private investment and export rates, indicating that the post-pandemic boom is normalizing. Relevant to the open data movement, this report underscores the critical importance of transparent, timely, and granular statistical access. By publishing detailed breakdowns of GDP components, sectoral performance, income distribution, and employment metrics, national statistical institutes empower researchers and citizens to verify economic narratives independently. Open access to such rigorous data fosters accountability, allowing for a deeper understanding of how macroeconomic shifts impact specific industries and social groups, rather than relying solely on aggregated political summaries. Furthermore, the availability of comprehensive datasets enables the analysis of complex interdependencies between production, income, and labor markets. This transparency supports evidence-based decision-making for businesses, policymakers, and academics who need to assess structural changes, such as the varying growth rates across services, construction, and primary sectors. Ultimately, open data transforms raw statistics into actionable knowledge, ensuring that economic evaluations are grounded in verified facts rather than partial interpretations.
