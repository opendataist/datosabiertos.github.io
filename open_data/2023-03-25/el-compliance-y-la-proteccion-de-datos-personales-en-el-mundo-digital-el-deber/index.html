El “compliance” y la protección de datos personales en el mundo digital | El Deber

El “compliance” y la protección de datos personales en el mundo digital | El Deber

The core challenge lies in the absence of specific national regulations, which leaves data protection subject to corporate discretion rather than robust legal frameworks. This regulatory gap forces organizations to navigate complex compliance requirements independently, often relying on self-regulation instead of clear state mandates. Globally, compliance principles have expanded from anti-corruption efforts to include personal data protection through extraterritorial laws like the GDPR. These international standards compel companies worldwide to adapt their practices, yet many regions struggle to incorporate these norms effectively, risking non-compliance and associated penalties in an increasingly digital economy. This dynamic is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the tension between public transparency and private control. When regulation relies on private entities or self-imposed rules, the consistency and reliability of open data ecosystems are compromised, making it difficult to ensure equitable access and accountability across borders.
