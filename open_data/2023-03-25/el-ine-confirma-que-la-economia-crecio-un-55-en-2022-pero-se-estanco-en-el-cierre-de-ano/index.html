El INE confirma que la economía creció un 5,5% en 2022 pero se estancó en el cierre de año

Spain’s economy expanded significantly in 2022, leading the eurozone in growth rates and surpassing international expectations. However, this headline performance masks a pronounced slowdown in the second half of the year, driven by weakening household consumption and persistent economic inertia. Consequently, Spain remains far behind its pre-pandemic output levels, lagging behind other major European economies that had already fully recovered their gross domestic product capacity. The outlook for 2023 reflects continued caution, with projections indicating a marked deceleration as the economy struggles to regain momentum. Consumer spending remains fragile due to eroded purchasing power, exacerbated by historically high inflation, particularly in essential food prices. Compounding these domestic challenges are tighter monetary policies from the European Central Bank, which restrict credit availability, alongside global uncertainties stemming from recent banking sector instability in the United States and Europe. This article is relevant to open_data because it highlights the critical role of transparent, timely statistical indicators from institutions like the National Statistics Institute and the Bank of Spain. Reliable public data is essential for stakeholders to interpret complex economic realities, such as the divergence between annual growth figures and quarterly stagnation. Furthermore, it underscores the need for accessible data to monitor inflation trends and recovery trajectories, enabling informed decision-making amidst geopolitical and monetary volatility.
