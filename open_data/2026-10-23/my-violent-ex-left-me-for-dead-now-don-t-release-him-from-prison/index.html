'My violent ex left me for dead — now don't release him from prison'

This article highlights a stark conflict between individual victim safety and broader data transparency reforms. A survivor of a severe domestic abuse attack is campaigning against the potential release of her stalker, who has breached parole conditions. Her personal struggle underscores the urgent need for robust tracking systems for violent offenders, as current mechanisms fail to adequately protect victims from repeat harms despite judicial reviews and extensive evidence of continued danger. The survivor’s efforts have directly influenced systemic changes regarding the monitoring of perpetrators. Following her advocacy, the government agreed to update the VISOR register to include more domestic abuse cases. However, she continues to push for proactive, comprehensive tracking of serial stalkers, arguing that existing data sharing between agencies is insufficient to prevent recidivism and ensure public safety. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical intersection of personal privacy, public safety, and data accessibility. While the victim’s traumatic details remain private, the systemic failures in offender tracking reveal gaps in how data is collected, shared, and utilized by justice systems. The campaign demonstrates how individual narratives can drive policy changes, emphasizing the need for more transparent and effective data structures to manage high-risk individuals without compromising the necessary legal protections surrounding parole decisions.
