Los Pajaritos snatches the top spot for poverty in Spain from Polígono Sur

Los Pajaritos snatches the top spot for poverty in Spain from Polígono Sur

The Urban Audit study reveals that Seville accounts for six of Spain’s fifteen poorest neighborhoods, maintaining this disadvantaged position since 2020. Although average income in these areas has experienced real growth in recent years, this increase has not been sufficient to improve their relative ranking or to surpass minimum thresholds of socioeconomic well-being, highlighting a stagnation in social mobility among the most disadvantaged neighborhoods. The inequality gap widens when these figures are contrasted with those of the highest-income areas, where Madrid and Barcelona dominate nearly the entire top of the rankings. The disparity between the richest and poorest neighborhoods exceeds €40,000 annually, with the income of privileged areas more than six times that of marginalized ones. This comparison illustrates profound territorial fragmentation and the existence of two nearly incompatible national realities within the same country. This report is relevant to the field of open data because it demonstrates how the standardized and periodic publication of socioeconomic indicators at the neighborhood level enables the identification of structural trends and disparities that would otherwise remain hidden. Transparency in these data facilitates critical analysis by civil society and academia, prompting administrations to respond more effectively to evident urban inequalities and contributing to evidence-based accountability.
