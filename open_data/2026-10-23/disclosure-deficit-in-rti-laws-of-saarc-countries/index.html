Disclosure Deficit in RTI Laws of SAARC Countries

Disclosure Deficit in RTI Laws of SAARC Countries

This analysis reveals that while Freedom of Information laws are widely adopted in South Asia, their effectiveness is severely compromised by restrictive exemption clauses. The study highlights a significant disclosure deficit across the region, driven by laws that prioritize secrecy over transparency. These legislative frameworks often lack the precise, limited exclusions required by international norms, thereby undermining the fundamental human right to access public information. The core of the problem lies in how these laws define what information can be withheld. Many SAARC nations employ broad, ambiguous exemptions that allow government bodies to easily deny requests without justification. Crucially, these laws frequently fail to include necessary safeguards, such as public interest overrides or clear time limits on withholding data. This structural weakness enables authorities to utilize secrecy laws to block access, creating a hostile environment for citizens seeking accountability. This article is vital to the open data movement because it demonstrates that legal recognition of access rights is insufficient without robust implementation standards. The disparity in legal frameworks shows that without strict limitations on exemptions and strong provisions for pro-active disclosure, open data initiatives remain theoretical. For true transparency, open data policies must be underpinned by legislation that minimizes secrecy and maximizes public access, ensuring that data is truly open rather than merely available in restricted forms.
