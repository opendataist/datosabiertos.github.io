Library and Archives Canada cracking down on AI books amid copyright threats - National | Globalnews.ca

Library and Archives Canada cracking down on AI books amid copyright threats - National | Globalnews.ca

Library and Archives Canada has revised its ISBN policy to prioritize human authorship, requiring publishers to declare that books are written by humans rather than artificial intelligence. This regulatory shift aims to preserve the integrity of Canada’s creative sector by limiting registrations for content primarily generated by machines. By enforcing a self-declaration of human involvement, the policy seeks to distinguish authentic literary work from automated output, ensuring that cultural identity remains shaped by human creativity. The update addresses growing concerns about market saturation with low-quality, AI-generated titles that obscure legitimate publications and risk spreading disinformation. Industry groups, including the Canadian Authors Association and the Association of Canadian Publishers, welcome this move as a crucial step against fraud and consumer confusion. They view the policy as a necessary defense against "AI-slop" that threatens to crowd out human-authored works, thereby protecting both authors’ rights and public access to reliable information. This development is significant for open data initiatives as it establishes a precedent for metadata standards that reflect provenance and authenticity in digital publishing. As AI tools become more prevalent, clear labeling and registration protocols become essential for maintaining trust in information ecosystems. The policy highlights the need for robust frameworks that integrate human-centric values into digital infrastructure, ensuring that open access to knowledge does not come at the cost of misleading or synthetic content.
