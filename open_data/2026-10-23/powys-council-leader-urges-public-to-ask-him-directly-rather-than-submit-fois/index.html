Powys council leader urges public to ask him directly rather than submit FOIs

Powys council leader urges public to ask him directly rather than submit FOIs

The article highlights a recurring tension between proactive open data practices and reactive information governance in local government. Powys County Council reported a rise in Freedom of Information (FOI), Environmental Information Regulations (EIR), and Subject Access Request (SAR) volumes, with performance against statutory deadlines slipping below targets. This data underscores the growing administrative burden and resource strain associated with handling statutory information requests, particularly as backlogs accumulate in specific categories. Council leadership suggested that direct engagement might be more efficient than formal requests, reflecting a broader debate about how citizens interact with public sector transparency mechanisms. However, the underlying data reveals that reliance on FOI and SAR regimes is intensifying, driven by complex information needs that internal processes struggle to meet efficiently. This highlights the critical need for better data management systems to support compliance without overwhelming staff resources. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the pressure points in public sector information handling. When statutory request volumes rise and processing times lengthen, it signals gaps in proactive data publication and user-friendly access portals. Strengthening open data initiatives can help alleviate these bottlenecks by providing citizens with immediate access to non-sensitive information, thereby reducing the reliance on formal legal requests and improving overall government efficiency.
