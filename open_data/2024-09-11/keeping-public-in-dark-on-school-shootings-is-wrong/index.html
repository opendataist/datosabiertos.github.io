Keeping public in dark on school shootings is wrong

The article highlights the conflict between public interest and legal secrecy following the Perry High School tragedy, where state investigators completed their report but the county attorney refused to release it. By citing exemptions for peace officers’ investigative documents, the official withheld findings that could answer critical questions about the shooter’s motives, access to weapons, and school security failures. This decision leaves the grieving community in the dark, fueling rumors rather than providing authoritative clarity on how such a preventable disaster occurred. This situation underscores the vital importance of open data in maintaining public trust during crises. When governments withhold detailed investigative records under the guise of confidentiality, they undermine the democratic right to know and hinder collective efforts to prevent future violence. Transparency is not merely an administrative preference but a necessary component of safety and accountability, allowing communities to understand risks and advocate for effective policy changes based on facts rather than speculation. The relevance to open data lies in the urgent need to balance law enforcement privacy with the public’s right to access information about significant societal events. The article calls for legislative reform to clarify when investigative reports should be disclosed, emphasizing that openness leads to better-informed citizens and safer communities. By advocating for greater transparency, the piece illustrates how access to data empowers citizens to hold officials accountable and drive meaningful conversations about school safety and government responsibility.
