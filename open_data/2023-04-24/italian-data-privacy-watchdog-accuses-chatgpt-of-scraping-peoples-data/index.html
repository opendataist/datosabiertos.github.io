Italian data privacy watchdog accuses ChatGPT of scraping people’s data

The Italian data protection agency has accused ChatGPT of scraping personal data without consent, violating strict European privacy laws. This ruling serves as a critical warning to AI developers that transparency and user control are non-negotiable requirements for operating in regulated markets. The agency mandates specific compliance measures, including age verification, clear data usage notices, and mechanisms for users to correct or delete their information, setting a high bar for ethical data handling in artificial intelligence applications. This legal challenge in Italy is likely to trigger a broader regulatory domino effect across the European Union. Because the EU’s General Data Protection Regulation is among the world’s most stringent, a failure to comply could lead to bans or heavy fines in multiple member states, including France, Germany, and Ireland. Consequently, OpenAI faces the risk of a continent-wide prohibition if it cannot demonstrate legitimate interest and lawful processing of user data, highlighting the power of regional privacy authorities to shape global tech standards. This situation is profoundly relevant to open data discussions because it illustrates the tension between the openness of public information and the rights of individuals regarding their personal data. While open data promotes accessibility, this case emphasizes that AI systems must respect consent and privacy boundaries when processing information. It underscores the necessity for developers to balance data utility with strict legal compliance, ensuring that open innovation does not come at the cost of fundamental digital rights.
