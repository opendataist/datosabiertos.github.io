The INAI is essential for citizens to enforce their rights to access information and protect personal privacy. Without this autonomous body, society lacks the vital tool necessary to challenge government opacity and safeguard data against potential violations. It serves not as an elite privilege, but as a fundamental mechanism for any individual seeking transparency or defending their privacy rights against public entities. However, the institute currently faces a critical operational crisis due to the inability to hold sessions or resolve complaints stemming from a lack of quorum. This legal paralysis prevents the resolution of citizen grievances regarding information requests and data protection, highlighting an urgent need for new commissioners to restore functionality. The situation underscores the fragility of transparency institutions and the significant risk to democratic accountability when such bodies cannot function legally. This article is highly relevant to open data because the INAI’s paralysis directly threatens the enforcement of transparency obligations and the accessibility of government information. For open data advocates, the institute’s inability to operate means fewer mechanisms to ensure that public data is accurate, complete, and accessible. The struggle to maintain the INAI’s strength reflects the broader challenge of sustaining the legal and institutional frameworks that make open data meaningful and enforceable for society.
