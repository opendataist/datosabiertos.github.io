Colombia, segundo país del mundo con más datos públicos sobre biodiversidad

Colombia has emerged as the second country worldwide with the largest volume of public biodiversity data, solidifying its Biodiversity Information System (SIB) as an indispensable strategic tool. This achievement, endorsed by international recognition from the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF), demonstrates that open information availability is not merely a technical exercise, but a critical foundation for policy decision-making, fulfillment of climate commitments, and sustainable territorial planning. The significance of this advancement for open science lies in the democratization of environmental knowledge. By making millions of biological records accessible and verifiable, the system enables researchers, NGOs, and citizens to use these data to assess ecosystem trends and identify threats. This transparency fosters inter-institutional collaboration and ensures that biodiversity management is based on robust evidence, facilitating species conservation and protection of natural resources in a country with exceptional biological richness. Finally, the success of the Colombian model highlights the importance of public-private partnerships and citizen participation in generating open data. By integrating diverse organizations and strengthening the technical capacities of local stakeholders, the country has created a resilient network that prioritizes the quality and accessibility of information. This approach serves as a global reference for how nations can leverage open data to enhance their natural heritage, ensuring that knowledge about biodiversity becomes a common good used for the benefit of society and science as a whole.
