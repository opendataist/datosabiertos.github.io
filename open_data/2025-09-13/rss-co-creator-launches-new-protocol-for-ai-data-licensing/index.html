RSS co-creator launches new protocol for AI data licensing

RSS co-creator launches new protocol for AI data licensing

The AI industry faces mounting legal pressure as copyright lawsuits accumulate, prompting the launch of Real Simple Licensing (RSL) to solve the training data problem. This new system provides a scalable, machine-readable framework that allows web publishers to define and communicate licensing terms directly within their website infrastructure. By integrating these terms into standard protocols, RSL creates a clear technical and legal foundation for data usage, aiming to prevent an avalanche of litigation that could severely hinder industry growth. RSL operates through a collective licensing model similar to music rights organizations, offering publishers a unified point of contact for negotiations and royalty collection. This approach empowers both large platforms with existing deals and smaller entities lacking bargaining power to secure compensation for their content. The system accommodates various licensing structures, including blanket fees and per-inference payments, though it acknowledges the technical challenge of verifying exactly which data contributed to specific AI model outputs. This initiative is critical to open data ecosystems because it shifts the paradigm from unchecked scraping to structured, rights-respecting data exchange. It demonstrates a viable path for harmonizing open access with creator compensation, ensuring that valuable human-generated content remains a sustainable resource for AI development. By establishing standardized protocols, RSL encourages transparency and fairness, fostering a more equitable environment where data providers are acknowledged and compensated, ultimately supporting the long-term health and legality of open information sharing.
