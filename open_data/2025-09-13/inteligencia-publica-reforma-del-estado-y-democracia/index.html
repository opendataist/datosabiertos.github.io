Inteligencia pública, reforma del Estado y democracia

Inteligencia pública, reforma del Estado y democracia

The article argues that Dominican democracy suffers from a critical deficit in deliberation and a fragile state bureaucracy, creating a cycle in which political discretion erodes public trust and weakens the public sphere. This structural imbalance prevents the translation of collective debate into legitimate decisions, reducing politics to arbitrary administration. Consequently, the state lacks the institutional credibility necessary to support informed citizen engagement, leaving democracy vulnerable to populism and disinformation. In this context, the concept of "public intelligence" emerges as a vital tool for modernizing governance through digital infrastructure. By leveraging artificial intelligence not as a private commodity but as a public good, the state can professionalize its bureaucracy and enhance transparency. This approach aims to create interoperable systems that reduce arbitrariness, anticipate social needs, and empower citizens, thereby strengthening the link between technical implementation and political decision-making. This proposal is highly relevant to open data because it champions the use of interoperable, public digital platforms to democratize information and reduce asymmetries between the state and citizens. Open data serves as the foundational infrastructure for this "public intelligence," enabling accountability and efficiency. By treating data as a public good, the state can foster a more informed public sphere, ensuring that technological advancements reinforce, rather than undermine, democratic deliberation and institutional trust.
