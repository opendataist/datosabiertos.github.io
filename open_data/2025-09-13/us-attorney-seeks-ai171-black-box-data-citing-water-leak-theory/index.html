US Attorney Seeks AI171 Black Box Data Citing Water Leak Theory

US Attorney Seeks AI171 Black Box Data Citing Water Leak Theory

The article highlights a critical legal and technical dispute regarding the Air India AI171 crash, where US attorney Mike Andrews is leveraging Freedom of Information Act requests to challenge the prevailing narrative of pilot error. By citing FAA airworthiness directives for Boeing 787 models, Andrews argues that the disaster was likely caused by a design or manufacturing flaw. Specifically, he posits that water leakage from improperly installed couplings led to electrical shorts in the electronics bays, resulting in the automatic shutdown of fuel controls and subsequent engine failure. This case underscores the vital role of transparency in aviation safety investigations. The reliance on public records and official regulatory data demonstrates how open access to administrative documents can empower legal representatives and families to scrutinize official conclusions. It illustrates that open data is not merely about public statistics but involves accessing specific technical reports and directives that may contradict initial accident assumptions, ensuring a more rigorous examination of potential systemic failures. Ultimately, this situation reflects the broader relevance of open data in holding corporations and regulators accountable. When technical directives and flight recorder data are accessible, it facilitates independent verification of safety hazards. This transparency is essential for preventing future tragedies, as it allows stakeholders to identify and address latent design defects, such as moisture intrusion in critical systems, rather than attributing catastrophic failures solely to human error.
