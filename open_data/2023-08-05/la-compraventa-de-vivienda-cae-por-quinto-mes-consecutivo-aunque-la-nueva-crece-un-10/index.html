La compraventa de vivienda cae por quinto mes consecutivo aunque la nueva crece un 10%

The article highlights a significant divergence in the Spanish real estate market, where new home sales are rising while second-hand transactions, which constitute the majority of the sector, continue to decline. This contrast indicates a stabilization phase rather than a crisis, as the cooling effect of higher interest rates is primarily impacting the used housing stock. Experts suggest this moderation reflects a return to normalcy compared to the pandemic boom, with demand remaining resilient despite economic headwinds. The relevance to open data lies in the critical need for transparent, disaggregated statistics to interpret these nuanced market dynamics accurately. Official data from the INE allows for a granular analysis that distinguishes between property types and regions, preventing a monolithic interpretation of "market cooling." By making such detailed information publicly accessible, stakeholders can move beyond simplistic narratives of boom or bust and understand specific structural shifts, such as the sustained demand for new developments versus the contraction in the secondary market. Furthermore, open data facilitates the validation of expert opinions and long-term trend analysis. Comparing current figures against historical baselines, such as pre-pandemic levels, provides essential context for policymakers and citizens. This transparency ensures that decisions regarding housing policy and investment are based on comprehensive evidence rather than fragmented indicators, fostering a more informed public debate on the implications of interest rate changes and housing accessibility.
