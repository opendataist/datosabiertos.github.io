Google admitirá solicitudes para eliminar cualquier imagen personal explícita de los resultados del Buscador

Google has announced significant updates focused on privacy and data control, highlighting the global expansion of SafeSearch as the default option for filtering harmful content. This measure aims to protect users, especially minors, by automatically blurring explicit images, while also offering the possibility of disabling the filter according to administrative settings. Additionally, the mechanisms for users to request the removal of personal sexually explicit images published without their consent have been simplified. Although Google clarifies that these actions do not delete the content from the web in general, they do reduce its visibility in search results, empowering individuals to manage their digital footprint and protect their private information from unwanted exposure. The relevance of this article to open data lies in the constant tension between information transparency and the protection of individual identity. These changes illustrate how major technology platforms are redefining the limits of public access to personal data. By implementing tracking and removal tools, Google sets a crucial precedent regarding the responsibility of digital intermediaries in managing the right to be forgotten, balancing the availability of information with user security and autonomy in the face of sensitive data.
