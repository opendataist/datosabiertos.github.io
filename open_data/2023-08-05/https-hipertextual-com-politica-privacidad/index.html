The article outlines Hipertextual S.L.'s legal framework for collecting and processing personal data, emphasizing transparency regarding user rights and data security measures. It establishes that while some data collection is mandatory for service delivery, users retain significant control, including the right to access, correct, delete, or port their data. Crucially, the policy highlights that users within the EU are entitled to higher protection standards, particularly concerning consent withdrawal and objection to processing for commercial purposes or legitimate interests. A central finding is the extensive use of third-party tools, such as Google Analytics and Ad Manager, which collect usage data and cookies, often involving transfers to countries like the United States. The policy acknowledges these international transfers while asserting that appropriate safeguards are implemented to protect user information. It details how data is used not only for core services but also for analytics, advertising, and legal defense, ensuring users understand the full scope of how their digital footprint is utilized across different platforms and jurisdictions. This document is highly relevant to open data discussions because it exemplifies the critical intersection between public transparency and private data governance. By explicitly detailing the legal bases, data handlers, and user rights, it demonstrates how organizations must balance operational transparency with individual privacy protections. Understanding such policies is essential for open data advocates who strive to promote accountability, ensuring that while data collection practices are open and auditable, they strictly adhere to regulatory standards that protect citizen autonomy and prevent unauthorized data exploitation.
