The Punjab Information Commission staff are striking due to severe understaffing and inadequate service structures. Despite being legally mandated to ensure public access to information, the body has operated with minimal personnel for years, leading to overwhelming workloads and employee frustration. This highlights the critical need for proper institutional support to fulfill transparency obligations effectively. The commission’s inability to recruit approved staff reflects systemic administrative failures in enforcing right-to-information laws. Even when recruitment was eventually permitted, only a fraction of vacancies were filled, and many refused joining due to poor facilities. Consequently, the commission remains significantly under-resourced, undermining its capacity to handle public information requests and serve citizens adequately. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocacy, as it demonstrates how institutional neglect and resource constraints can cripple transparency mechanisms. Without sufficient staffing and structural support, even legally robust frameworks fail to deliver accessible information. Ensuring proper resources for such bodies is essential for maintaining public trust and achieving genuine governmental openness and accountability.
