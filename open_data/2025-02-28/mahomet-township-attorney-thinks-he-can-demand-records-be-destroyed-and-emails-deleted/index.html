Mahomet Township Attorney Thinks He Can Demand Records Be Destroyed And Emails Deleted –

This article highlights a public body’s attempt to retroactively restrict access to records already released under FOIA, demanding their destruction and prohibiting further sharing. It clarifies that such "clawback" demands are legally invalid without a court order, emphasizing that public entities bear the responsibility for their own disclosure errors rather than the requester. This is crucial for open data because it reinforces the principle that once information is released to the public, it remains freely accessible, ensuring transparency cannot be arbitrarily revoked by agencies.
