Consumo: 2024 cerró con caídas en las ventas de supermercados y mayoristas - EL DEBATE

Consumo: 2024 cerró con caídas en las ventas de supermercados y mayoristas - EL DEBATE

The article reveals that despite significant nominal increases in sales driven by inflation, the real volume of transactions in the retail sector experienced a notable contraction. For supermarkets, constant-price sales declined year-on-year, indicating a reduction in actual consumer demand rather than merely price adjustments. This disparity highlights a crucial insight for open data initiatives: relying solely on nominal monetary figures can mask underlying economic weaknesses, necessitating the use of deflated metrics to understand true market dynamics. Furthermore, the wholesale sector faced even steeper declines in real sales volume, suggesting a more severe shock to business-to-business transactions. The data shows that while gross revenues grew substantially, the physical quantity of goods sold dropped significantly. This emphasizes the importance of accessible, high-quality statistical data that distinguishes between price effects and volume changes, allowing analysts to accurately assess supply chain pressures and commercial health without the distortion of inflation. This information is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical need for transparent, standardized economic indicators that are easily interpretable. By providing detailed breakdowns by category, payment method, and region, such datasets empower researchers and developers to build tools that track real economic trends. Open access to this granular, adjusted data fosters greater accountability and enables better decision-making by distinguishing superficial monetary growth from genuine commercial activity.
