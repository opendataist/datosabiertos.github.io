A recent 11% wage increase for domestic workers across all categories underscores the ongoing struggle for equitable labor compensation in the informal economy. This adjustment reflects a broader push for formalization, ensuring that hourly rates accurately reflect the value of care and domestic labor while accounting for work conditions such as overnight stays or regional location bonuses. The structured categorization by job function highlights the diverse responsibilities within household work, from supervision to personal care and general cleaning. By recognizing these distinctions, the update aims to provide clearer, standardized protections that validate the professional nature of these roles, moving beyond vague definitions toward specific labor rights. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as transparent salary data enables better monitoring of economic trends and labor policy effectiveness. Openly accessible wage information supports researchers and advocates in identifying disparities, assessing the impact of minimum wage adjustments, and promoting greater accountability in the domestic work sector.
