Feds Worried About Anarchists Gluing the Locks to a Government Facility

This article highlights a significant tension in open data transparency between national security concerns and public accountability. It details how the FBI redacted a 2015 intelligence bulletin warning of anarchist activity on May Day, specifically omitting lines that admitted there was no credible threat and downplaying the motivation behind such actions. The removal of this context, despite the existence of unredacted versions in leaked databases, illustrates how critical data can be selectively altered before public release, obscuring the reality of the information landscape. The incident serves as a stark reminder of the limitations imposed by exemptions like "law enforcement techniques" in Freedom of Information Act requests. By censoring specific tactical advice while simultaneously issuing vague, fear-inducing warnings, authorities create a narrative that may not align with factual assessments. This selective disclosure prevents researchers and the public from fully understanding the basis of government surveillance strategies, thereby undermining the core principles of open data which demand clarity, completeness, and truthfulness in published records. Ultimately, this case underscores the vital role of independent journalists and open-source intelligence communities in verifying official narratives. The ability to cross-reference FOIA-denied documents with leaked archives demonstrates that open data ecosystems can expose inconsistencies in government reporting. This relevance to open data lies in its potential to hold power accountable; when raw data is accessible and comparable across multiple sources, it becomes easier to identify censorship and demand greater transparency, ensuring that public records truly reflect the information they are intended to convey.
