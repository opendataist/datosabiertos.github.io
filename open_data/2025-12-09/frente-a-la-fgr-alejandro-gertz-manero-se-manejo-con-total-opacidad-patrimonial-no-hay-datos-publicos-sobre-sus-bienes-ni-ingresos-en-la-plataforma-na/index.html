Frente a la FGR, Alejandro Gertz Manero se manejó con total “opacidad” patrimonial: no hay datos públicos sobre sus bienes ni ingresos en la Plataforma Nacional de Transparencia y Declaranet, según El Universal

The article reveals a significant lack of transparency within the Mexican Public Ministry (FGR), where the Attorney General and several high-ranking officials withheld their financial disclosures for years. By citing security risks or personal consent as justification, these leaders prevented public access to their asset and income declarations. This practice effectively shields officials from scrutiny, undermining the fundamental principle of open government by obscuring potential conflicts of interest or unexplained wealth accumulation among those in powerful positions. The implications for accountability are severe, as the inability to verify financial data hampers efforts to detect corruption or irregularities. When key figures in sensitive areas like organized crime and anti-corruption units operate with financial opacity, it erodes public trust and limits civil society’s capacity to monitor the integrity of state institutions. This systemic secrecy suggests that transparency laws are being manipulated to protect individuals rather than ensure institutional integrity, creating a dangerous precedent where privacy concerns override the public’s right to know. This case is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical gap between the publication of information and its actual accessibility and usability. Even when declarations exist, classifying them as "reserved" renders them useless for data analysis and civic oversight. It underscores the necessity of advocating for default transparency and robust data standards, ensuring that information about public officials is not only published but also accessible, machine-readable, and free from unjustified restrictions that impede democratic accountability.
