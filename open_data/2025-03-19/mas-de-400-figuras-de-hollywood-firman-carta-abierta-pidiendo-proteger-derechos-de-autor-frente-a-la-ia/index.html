Más de 400 figuras de Hollywood firman carta abierta pidiendo proteger derechos de autor frente a la IA

Over 420 prominent entertainment figures have urged the U.S. government to reject tech giants’ calls for relaxed copyright regulations regarding artificial intelligence. They argue that allowing AI companies to train models on protected works without consent or compensation threatens the economic viability of the creative industry. This collective action highlights the tension between rapid technological innovation and the legal rights of creators, emphasizing that intellectual property protections must remain intact to sustain millions of jobs and billions in revenue. The concern extends beyond immediate financial losses to the fundamental integrity of creative professions. Artists warn that unrestricted data mining for AI development undermines the licensing frameworks that currently support sectors like film, music, and literature. By bypassing negotiated agreements, tech firms risk devaluing human authorship, potentially leading to a landscape where derivative AI-generated content floods the market without acknowledging or rewarding original creators. This article is highly relevant to open_data because it addresses the critical ethical and legal boundaries of data usage. It challenges the assumption that public or commercial data can be freely ingested by AI systems without regard for ownership or attribution. The debate underscores the need for transparent data sourcing policies and reinforces the importance of respecting intellectual property rights when curating and utilizing datasets for machine learning, ensuring that the pursuit of open data does not infringe upon the rights of individuals and industries.
