El gasto turístico consolida la recuperación del sector con un aumento en septiembre del 17% sobre el récord de 2019

The article highlights a robust recovery and evolution in Spain’s tourism sector, characterized by significant increases in both visitor numbers and expenditures. Crucially, the data reveals a shift toward higher-quality tourism, evidenced by rising average spending per traveler and longer stays compared to pre-pandemic levels. This trend indicates that the industry is not just rebounding in volume but is enhancing its economic impact per visitor, aligning with government strategies focused on sustainable growth rather than mere quantity. Relevant to open data, these statistics demonstrate the value of transparent, granular datasets in tracking complex socio-economic shifts. By breaking down metrics by origin country and destination region, policymakers can identify specific market trends, such as the surge in long-haul travelers from the US. This level of detailed, publicly accessible information allows for evidence-based decision-making, enabling authorities to allocate resources efficiently and tailor marketing strategies to the most profitable and sustainable tourist segments. Ultimately, the report underscores the importance of monitoring diverse indicators beyond simple headcounts, such as overnight stays and environmental impact. This holistic approach supports the development of balanced policies that benefit local territories while maintaining economic momentum. The availability of such comprehensive data is essential for stakeholders to understand the true state of the tourism industry, facilitating a transition toward a model that prioritizes sustainability, regional equity, and long-term economic resilience over short-term volume gains.
