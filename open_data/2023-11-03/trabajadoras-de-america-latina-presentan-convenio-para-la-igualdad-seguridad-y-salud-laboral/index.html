Trabajadoras de América Latina presentan convenio para la igualdad, seguridad y salud laboral

Working women in Latin America’s digital platform sector have unveiled a framework demanding an international convention with a gender perspective. They argue that the current non-recognition of labor relations violates decent work standards, disproportionately harming female workers by denying them basic protections and rights. The proposal emphasizes that platforms impose their own rules regardless of national regulations, creating a power imbalance that requires strict oversight. By focusing on privacy, data protection, and equality, the initiative seeks to address systemic discrimination and ensure that technological advancements do not further marginalize women in the workforce. This article is vital for open data advocates as it highlights the intersection of labor rights and algorithmic governance. It underscores the need for transparent data practices and equitable policy-making, demonstrating how open information can empower marginalized workers to demand accountability from tech giants and shape fairer digital economies.
