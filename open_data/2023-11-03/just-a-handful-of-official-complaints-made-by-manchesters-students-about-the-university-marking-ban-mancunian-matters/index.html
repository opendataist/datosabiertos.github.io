Just a handful of official complaints made by Manchester’s students about the university marking ban - Mancunian Matters

Just a handful of official complaints made by Manchester’s students about the university marking ban - Mancunian Matters

Freedom of Information requests reveal that student engagement with the marking and assessment boycott at major Manchester universities was surprisingly low. Despite the severe impact on graduations, formal complaints and refund requests remained minimal, suggesting that many affected students either accepted the outcome or lacked the agency to navigate institutional appeals processes. This data highlights a significant gap between academic staff industrial action and student mobilization, especially when compared to the high participation in previous rent strikes. The disparity indicates that while economic grievances regarding living costs sparked robust collective action, disputes over degree classifications failed to generate similar levels of organized student resistance. This finding is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency mechanisms can expose hidden realities about community responses to institutional policies. By analyzing FOI responses, stakeholders can better understand the actual impact of strikes on learners and identify where advocacy efforts might be most effective, moving beyond assumed narratives to evidence-based insights.
