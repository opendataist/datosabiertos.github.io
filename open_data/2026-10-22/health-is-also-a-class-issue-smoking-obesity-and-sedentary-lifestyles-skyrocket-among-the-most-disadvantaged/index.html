Health is also a class issue: smoking, obesity, and sedentary lifestyles skyrocket among the most disadvantaged

Social class profoundly shapes health, influencing everything from perceptions of well-being to access to basic services. Data show that the most disadvantaged socioeconomic groups experience a progressive decline in their health indicators, revealing a structural gap in which the level of occupational qualification is directly correlated with poorer health habits and physical conditions. This inequality manifests in risky behaviors and barriers to healthcare access, notably sedentary lifestyles, smoking, and dental problems. Lower social strata suffer higher prevalence rates of obesity and depression, and have less financial capacity to meet dental care needs, leading to lasting consequences. The lack of resources not only limits self-care but also concentrates a disproportionate burden of informal caregiving among individuals with weaker social support networks, exacerbating stress and personal burnout. This report is relevant for data transparency because it illustrates how socioeconomic variables must be systematically cross-referenced with public health indicators. To design effective and equitable policies, it is crucial that both citizens and public officials have access to disaggregated statistics that reveal these hidden disparities. Only through the clear visualization of these intersections can we promote genuine social justice within the healthcare system and understand the social determinants that perpetuate inequity.
