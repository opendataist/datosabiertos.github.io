Child labor declines marginally over a decade; 3.5 million minors work in Mexico

Child labor declines marginally over a decade; 3.5 million minors work in Mexico

The report reveals that, although there was a slight absolute reduction in the number of children in situations of child labor in Mexico over the last decade, overall rates have remained stable or even increased slightly. This apparent stability masks a worrying reality in which the phenomenon persists strongly, particularly in prohibited activities and in inappropriate domestic work, indicating that previous efforts have not succeeded in significantly breaking the structural cycle of child labor exploitation. A critical point highlighted is the close relationship between school dropout and child labor, as well as marked territorial inequality. Children outside the education system have alarmingly high labor participation rates, while rural areas and the southern regions of the country show the highest incidences. These data suggest that poverty and lack of access to education are determining factors, and that solutions must address regional gaps and family sustainability to be effective. This analysis is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how the transparent and detailed publication of official statistics enables the identification of complex patterns and hidden disparities in social reality. By accessing these data disaggregated by age, gender, and location, researchers and policymakers can go beyond aggregate figures, understanding root causes such as family economic vulnerability and school dropout. This facilitates the design of precise, evidence-based interventions, promoting accountability and continuous improvement of social policies from a human rights and government transparency perspective.
