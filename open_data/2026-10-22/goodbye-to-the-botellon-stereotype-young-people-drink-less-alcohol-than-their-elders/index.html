Goodbye to the botellón stereotype: young people drink less alcohol than their elders

Goodbye to the botellón stereotype: young people drink less alcohol than their elders

The new 2025 Spanish Health Survey reveals a significant shift in alcohol consumption patterns, challenging the traditional perception that associates alcohol use exclusively with youth and weekends. The data show that, although young people drink less frequently, habitual consumption is more prevalent among adults, indicating that the reality is more complex than what simple participation statistics suggest. This structural change underscores the importance of measuring consumption frequency to properly understand population habits. By distinguishing between regular and sporadic drinking, it becomes evident that adults maintain a more constant presence of alcohol in their daily lives, while young people concentrate their consumption in specific moments. This requires a more nuanced analysis that goes beyond simply knowing whether someone has drunk alcohol in the past year. However, risk has not decreased among young people, who continue to exhibit high rates of binge drinking—a phenomenon that has also increased in the general population. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the need for detailed, disaggregated indicators to avoid erroneous conclusions, enabling data transparency to support more precise and effective public policies addressing harmful alcohol consumption.
