How Recycled Minerals from GM EV Batteries Are Helping Power New Ones - CleanTechnica

How Recycled Minerals from GM EV Batteries Are Helping Power New Ones - CleanTechnica

General Motors has successfully completed a pilot program demonstrating a closed-loop recycling pathway for electric vehicle batteries. By recovering critical minerals from end-of-life packs and refining them into cathode active material, the company produced new battery cells containing 100% recycled nickel, cobalt, and manganese. These materials met stringent safety and performance standards, allowing them to be integrated into new EVs, including the Cadillac Lyriq and Chevrolet Silverado EV. This achievement validates that recovered materials can effectively replace virgin resources in high-quality battery production. This milestone is crucial for strengthening supply chain resilience and reducing reliance on newly extracted raw materials, which are significant cost drivers in battery manufacturing. By reusing these critical minerals, the industry can potentially lower battery costs and enhance sustainability. The pilot underscores the importance of collaborative engineering and quality assurance across the recycling, processing, and manufacturing sectors to ensure that recycled components perform identically to new ones, thereby establishing a viable model for circular economy practices in the automotive industry. The article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the necessity of transparent, shareable lifecycle data to foster circular economies. Scaling this model requires robust data sharing regarding battery composition, recycling efficiency, and material tracking across multiple corporate partners. Open data initiatives can facilitate this transparency, enabling better coordination among recyclers, manufacturers, and regulators. By making such lifecycle information accessible, the industry can accelerate innovation, improve resource recovery rates, and create a more resilient, sustainable supply chain for electric vehicles globally.
