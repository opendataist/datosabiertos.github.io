AI will eat a fifth of tech budgets in 2027, but don't expect it to pay for itself

AI will eat a fifth of tech budgets in 2027, but don't expect it to pay for itself

Organizations struggle to align AI investments with necessary structural changes, leading many leaders to miss return on investment targets. The report emphasizes that technology adoption alone is insufficient; measurable, firmwide results require significant shifts in organizational processes and workflows. Consequently, most success will remain confined to isolated use cases rather than driving broad enterprise value, highlighting a critical gap between spending and operational readiness. To manage rising costs and complexity, enterprises are increasingly adopting open-weight generative AI models. These cost-effective solutions handle high-volume workloads like document processing and customer support, representing a major portion of token usage despite minimal budget share. By reserving expensive proprietary models for critical, high-stakes tasks, companies can optimize their technology spend while maintaining performance standards for routine operations. This strategy is vital for open_data advocates, as open-weight models democratize access to advanced AI capabilities. By reducing reliance on closed proprietary systems, organizations can foster greater transparency, flexibility, and innovation. This shift supports the open_data principle of accessible, reusable technology, enabling broader participation in AI development and ensuring that data-driven insights are not restricted by vendor lock-in or prohibitive costs.
