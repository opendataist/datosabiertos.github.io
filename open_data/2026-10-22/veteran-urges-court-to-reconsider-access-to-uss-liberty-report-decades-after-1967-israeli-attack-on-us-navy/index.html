Veteran Urges Court to Reconsider Access to USS Liberty Report Decades After 1967 Israeli Attack on US Navy

Veteran Urges Court to Reconsider Access to USS Liberty Report Decades After 1967 Israeli Attack on US Navy

A researcher and veteran is petitioning the Ninth Circuit to reconsider a ruling that denied access to a congressional report on the 1967 USS Liberty attack, arguing that decades of NSA possession transformed the document into an agency record subject to the Freedom of Information Act. The petitioner contends that the agency’s active use and circulation of the report demonstrate control under federal precedent, thereby overriding claims that Congress retained exclusive authority via a restrictive banner. This legal challenge highlights the tension between statutory definitions of agency records and legislative attempts to maintain secrecy over historical materials. Transparency advocacy groups support the petition, warning that the current interpretation allows agencies to shield information from public scrutiny with minimal evidentiary justification. They argue that treating a generic restriction notice as definitive proof of congressional control undermines the purpose of open data laws, which require the government to actively justify withholding specific exemptions rather than relying on blanket secrecy labels. The briefs emphasize that this approach risks reviving outdated standards where classification alone barred judicial review. This case is significant for open data because it tests the boundaries of accountability when historical records intersect with national security agencies. If the court reaffirms the initial decision, it could set a precedent allowing legislative bodies to permanently exempt transferred documents from FOIA, limiting public access to critical historical accounts. Conversely, a reversal would reinforce the principle that agency custody and usage can subject sensitive documents to transparency requirements, ensuring that important government records remain accessible for public scrutiny and historical analysis.
