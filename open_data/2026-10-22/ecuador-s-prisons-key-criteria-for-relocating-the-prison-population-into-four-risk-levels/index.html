Ecuador's Prisons: Key Criteria for Relocating the Prison Population into Four Risk Levels

Ecuador's Prisons: Key Criteria for Relocating the Prison Population into Four Risk Levels

The beginning of the classification of inmates in Ecuador represents a critical step toward greater transparency and evidence-based management within the prison system. By establishing clear technical criteria to separate the inmate population according to risk levels, the State aims to reduce the opacity that has traditionally facilitated the control of organized crime in prisons. This measure not only enhances security but also legitimizes the use of public resources by aligning prison conditions with international standards and current regulations. The implementation of a four-tier risk classification system—ranging from ordinary to a special maximum-security regime—enables more efficient and secure allocation of resources. This differentiated approach requires that information on each inmate’s profile be rigorous and accessible to ensure compliance with established rules and to prevent arbitrary decisions. By restricting in-person visits for the most dangerous cases and prioritizing telematic monitoring, the system seeks to disrupt criminal command structures while maintaining a documented record of authorized interactions, which is vital for future audits. For the open data movement, this process is fundamental because it establishes the need to publish standardized data on the inmate population and their classification. The relevance lies in the fact that civil society and oversight bodies must be able to access the classification methodology and aggregated results to monitor the alleged democratization of the system. Without open, verifiable data on how these risks are determined and which individuals fall into each category, the reform loses part of its transformative potential, and accountability is compromised.
