ELECTION NUMBERS: 766,000 Charedi Voters Among 7.3 Million Israelis Eligible To Vote

The Central Bureau of Statistics has released new voter demographics for Israel’s upcoming Knesset elections, revealing a significant shift in electoral weight. A key finding is the substantial influence of the Charedi community, which constitutes over ten percent of the total electorate despite comprising only thirteen percent of the overall population. This disparity arises primarily because the Charedi population is notably younger, with a large proportion of residents currently too young to vote. Age distribution and citizenship status heavily influence regional voting eligibility. Cities with dense Charedi populations show lower percentages of eligible voters due to their youthful demographics, whereas northern towns exhibit much higher rates of eligible adults. These variations highlight how demographic structures, including the number of children and non-citizen residents, directly impact the practical size of the voting base in different municipalities. This data is relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the critical importance of transparent, accessible, and timely demographic statistics for understanding political realities. By making such detailed breakdowns public, authorities enable better analysis of electoral trends and community representation. Furthermore, this information supports civic engagement and academic research, allowing stakeholders to visualize the gap between population size and actual voting power.
