With more than 2,700 entries, Morelia prepares for the Catrinas and Catrines Parade

The Catrinas and Catrines Parade in Morelia highlights the duality between the utility of mandatory registration for logistical management and the universal accessibility of the event. Authorities use participant registration to obtain accurate data on expected attendance, enabling better resource planning and security arrangements. However, it is emphasized that participation is open to everyone, regardless of whether they have registered, ensuring civic inclusion. Collecting these statistics is essential for measuring crowd flow capacity and organizing institutional contingents. This approach demonstrates how personal or group data serve as a key tool for efficient public administration at large cultural events. Planning includes security measures and barriers, ensuring that the knowledge derived from registration translates into a safe and orderly experience for all attendees. This initiative is relevant to the open data sector because it illustrates the value of real-time collected information for improving urban governance and social participation. It shows that, although the data are used internally for organizational purposes, their structure reflects a commitment to transparency and evidence-based planning. The event exemplifies how public administration can balance the need for operational data with the right to access culture, promoting a more responsive and citizen-centered management model.
