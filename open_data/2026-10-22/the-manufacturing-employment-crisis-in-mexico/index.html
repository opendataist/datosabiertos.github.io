The manufacturing employment crisis in Mexico

The manufacturing employment crisis in Mexico

The Mexican manufacturing industry is experiencing a widespread contraction in employment that affects most of its subsectors and regions, despite slight apparent improvements in the nominal value of production. This negative trend is not limited to vulnerable traditional activities; it also severely impacts key pillars such as transportation and electronics, revealing that growth in monetary value does not offset the real erosion driven by inflation or the loss of jobs. A detailed analysis shows that the increase in high-technology exports does not proportionally translate into national job creation, suggesting a disconnect between external demand and local productive capacity. Furthermore, the geographic dispersion of unemployment confirms that the most industrialized and export-oriented states are not immune, indicating a structural and systemic problem rather than a cyclical shock in isolated sectors. This case is fundamental to the discourse on open data, as it illustrates the need to go beyond superficial metrics. Transparency and critical analysis of official statistics are vital to demonstrate that growth in nominal value does not equate to labor well-being or physical productivity. Only through the integration and rigorous scrutiny of these multiple data sources can the true health of the industry be understood and policies that prioritize the creation of quality jobs over misleading monetary indicators be demanded.
