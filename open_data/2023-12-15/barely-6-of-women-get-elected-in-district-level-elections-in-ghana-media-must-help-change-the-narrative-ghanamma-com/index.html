Barely 6% of Women Get Elected in District-level Elections in Ghana: Media must help change the narrative - Ghanamma.com

Women constitute more than half of Ghana’s population yet occupy only a small fraction of district-level elected offices, highlighting a severe gender disparity in local governance. Although women have historically been vastly outnumbered by men in these positions, their representation has declined further in recent years, falling significantly short of international recommendations for equitable decision-making. This underrepresentation persists despite legal frameworks and advocacy efforts, with proposed affirmative action measures facing political delays. The root causes of this exclusion include societal barriers, economic challenges, and the disproportionate domestic burdens placed on women, which hinder their political participation. This lack of female leadership undermines the potential for inclusive resource allocation and accountability, ultimately jeopardizing the nation's ability to achieve broader sustainable development goals. Without diverse leadership, the promise of decentralized governance to effectively address community needs remains unfulfilled, as women’s specific perspectives on social services and domestic impacts are marginalized. This case is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency initiatives, such as obtaining election records through right-to-information requests, are essential for exposing systemic inequalities. By making hidden statistical realities visible, open data empowers advocates and civil society to hold institutions accountable and drive policy reform. Ultimately, accessible data serves as a catalyst for change, enabling targeted interventions to dismantle barriers and ensure that women’s voices are not only heard but represented in local governance structures.
