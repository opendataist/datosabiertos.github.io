Public raise concerns over RTI payments

The article highlights significant accessibility barriers in Ghana’s Right to Information implementation, where high fees deter citizens, particularly those with disabilities or limited means, from exercising their constitutional rights. This inequality underscores a critical challenge in open data governance: transparency mechanisms must be financially accessible to ensure equitable public engagement. Without addressing these economic hurdles, the promise of open government risks excluding vulnerable populations, undermining the fundamental goal of inclusive information sharing. Institutional culture and infrastructure further impede progress, as secrecy and poor record-keeping hinder the free flow of information. The delay in regulatory frameworks and the slow adoption of digital platforms exacerbate these issues, preventing efficient and transparent data handling. These systemic failures suggest that legislative presence alone is insufficient; robust operational strategies and a shift toward digital openness are essential to overcome bureaucratic inertia and foster a culture of accountability. This narrative is relevant to open data because it illustrates the real-world friction between legal frameworks and practical implementation. It emphasizes that true openness requires not just legislation, but active investment in digital infrastructure, public education, and equitable access policies. For open data advocates, this serves as a reminder that technical solutions must be paired with social and financial inclusivity to transform legal rights into tangible transparency.
