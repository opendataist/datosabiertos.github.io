The latest census data reveals a significant demographic contraction in Ferrol, positioning it among the Spanish cities with the sharpest population declines. This trend is largely mirrored across the broader region, indicating a sustained depopulation crisis that is already altering local governance structures. The shrinking resident base has forced specific municipalities to reduce their number of councilors, demonstrating how demographic shifts directly impact administrative representation and political resources. This insight is crucial for open data because it highlights the impact of the INE’s methodological shift to annual, administrative registry-based reporting. By moving away from decennial household surveys, the new system provides high-frequency, granular insights that were previously unavailable. This allows researchers and citizens to track demographic fluctuations in near real-time, fostering a deeper understanding of local dynamics and enabling more responsive policy analysis. Despite the overall negative trajectory, the data offers nuanced exceptions, such as slight increases in a few neighboring towns and marginal natality improvements in specific areas. These variations suggest that while the regional trend is downward, localized factors can create pockets of stability. Understanding these subtleties through accessible, updated datasets is essential for identifying effective local interventions and comprehensively mapping the changing social fabric of the area.
