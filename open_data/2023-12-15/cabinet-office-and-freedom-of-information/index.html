Cabinet Office and Freedom of Information

The UK Cabinet Office serves as the central authority for Freedom of Information policy, ensuring consistent compliance across government departments while balancing transparency with the protection of sensitive national interests. By managing complex referrals and coordinating responses to multi-departmental queries, the office aims to standardize procedures and prevent contradictory disclosures. This structural oversight is critical for maintaining public trust, as it ensures that the release of information adheres strictly to legal frameworks without compromising security or collective cabinet responsibilities. A significant evolution in this role is the transition of the FOI Clearing House from an operational function to a dedicated advisory model. Following a strategic review, the focus has shifted toward setting high standards and promoting best practices rather than handling individual cases directly. This change emphasizes proactive guidance on cross-cutting themes, such as data protection and the role of special advisers, empowering departments to handle requests more effectively and in alignment with contemporary privacy laws. This article is highly relevant to open data because it delineates the institutional mechanisms that govern how government information is made accessible to the public. Understanding these compliance structures and the shift toward advisory support helps data practitioners navigate the legal boundaries of disclosure. It highlights the importance of aligning open data initiatives with broader government transparency policies, ensuring that data releases are both legally robust and strategically consistent across all public authorities.
