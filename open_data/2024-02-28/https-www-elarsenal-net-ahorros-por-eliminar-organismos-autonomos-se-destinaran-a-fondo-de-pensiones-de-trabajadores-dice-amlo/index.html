The Mexican government has proposed eliminating seven key autonomous bodies, arguing that their existence generates inefficiency, duplication of functions, and unnecessary bureaucratic costs. The initiative aims to centralize these competencies within the corresponding ministerial departments, justifying this measure with specific cases of alleged lack of transparency in bodies such as the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), or regulatory inefficacy in sectors like telecommunications and energy. By reintegrating these roles into the executive branch, the government intends to strengthen state leadership and reduce administrative complexity, which it claims has weakened public policy planning. The primary financial objective of this consolidation is to channel the resulting savings toward the Pension Fund for Welfare. This mechanism seeks to guarantee full pensions for workers affiliated with the Mexican Social Security Institute (IMSS) and the State Workers’ Social Security and Social Services Institute (ISSSTE), directly linking governmental efficiency with social welfare. The proposal also includes the dissolution of other decentralized agencies, noting that recent autonomy has often led to bloated structures that do not necessarily contribute to better technical decisions but instead hinder state coordination. This reform is particularly relevant to the field of open data because it directly affects entities fundamental to information governance, such as the INAI. The elimination of this institute could weaken independent oversight mechanisms regarding transparency and access to public information, transferring those responsibilities to the very departments that should be subject to audit. This poses a potential risk to accountability, personal data protection, and the public availability of government information—elements essential for an informed society and a transparent administration.
