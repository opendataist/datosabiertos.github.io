INAI authorities emphasized the urgent need to prioritize the protection of personal data and privacy in the use of emerging technologies, such as mobile phones and artificial intelligence. This call to action came in response to recent incidents that violate people’s dignity and autonomy, underscoring that respect for these rights is fundamental to safeguarding individual integrity in an increasingly complex digital environment. It is argued that technology regulation must be grounded in ethics, positive law, and international cooperation. To this end, it is crucial to establish ongoing dialogues among regulators, governments, and the private sector, harmonizing policies and global standards. Collaboration, coordination, and cooperation among stakeholders enable the creation of robust legal frameworks that ensure security, privacy, and equity in the face of the cross-cutting challenges posed by digital transformation. This article is relevant to open data because it establishes that transparency and access to information must not compromise privacy. Responsible data management involves clearly defining what information is shared, with whom, and for what purposes. By promoting a balance between technological innovation and data protection, it ensures that open data initiatives are ethical, sustainable, and respectful of fundamental rights, thereby strengthening public trust in the responsible use of information.
