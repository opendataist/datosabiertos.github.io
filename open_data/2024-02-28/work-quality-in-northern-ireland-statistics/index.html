Work Quality in Northern Ireland Statistics

The latest Work Quality statistical publication reveals a generally positive trend in Northern Ireland’s employment landscape, with six out of eight key indicators showing significant improvement since 2020. Job security remains universally high, while advancements in career progression, decision-making involvement, and flexible working opportunities mark substantial gains over the last four years. These shifts indicate that the quality of work is evolving, offering employees broader prospects for growth and better work-life balance, even though job satisfaction and the alignment of skills with roles have seen minimal change. However, this data highlights persistent inequalities among different demographic cohorts, demonstrating that progress is not evenly distributed. Significant gaps exist between genders regarding flexible working access, with females more likely to utilize these arrangements than males. Economic and social disparities are also evident, as employees in less deprived areas and those in high-skilled roles consistently report better wages, career development opportunities, and flexible work options compared to their counterparts in deprived areas or low-skilled positions. Notably, while the wage gap between high and low-skilled workers has narrowed, structural disadvantages in career mobility remain for lower-income and lower-skill groups. This analysis is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the importance of disaggregated statistical indicators in revealing nuanced social truths. By making detailed metrics on bullying, harassment, and managerial support publicly available, such datasets empower researchers and policymakers to identify specific friction points in the labor market. Open access to this granular information fosters transparency and allows for targeted interventions, ensuring that future policy decisions are informed by comprehensive evidence regarding both overall improvements and lingering structural inequities in the workforce.
