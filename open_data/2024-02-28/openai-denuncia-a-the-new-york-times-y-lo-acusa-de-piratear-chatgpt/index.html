OpenAI denuncia a ‘The New York Times’ y lo acusa de piratear ChatGPT

OpenAI has filed a legal motion to dismiss the copyright claims brought by The New York Times, arguing that the newspaper employed improper methods—such as exploiting system vulnerabilities and violating terms of use—to gather evidence. The company contends that the newspaper’s practices do not reflect normal use of the tool and seeks to exclude this evidence from the litigation. This dispute highlights the fundamental tension between the artificial intelligence industry and content creators over the use of copyrighted materials to train generative models. OpenAI maintains that developing innovative products based on publicly available data is both legal and essential, while media outlets warn that without adequate protection, the economic viability and quality of traditional journalism would be severely jeopardized. This case is crucial for open data, as it sets a precedent regarding the legal limits of training AI on public domain or copyrighted data. The outcome will determine whether technology companies can freely access and process information to generate new content, or whether rights holders will be able to restrict such access, directly affecting the availability and flow of data necessary for research and the development of open-source technologies.
