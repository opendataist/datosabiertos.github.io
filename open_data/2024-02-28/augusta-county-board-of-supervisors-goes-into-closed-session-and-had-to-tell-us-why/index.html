Augusta County Board of Supervisors goes into closed session, and had to tell us why

The Augusta County Board of Supervisors recently demonstrated significant improvement in adhering to transparency laws by providing highly specific justifications for closed sessions. This shift follows a legal defeat where a court ruled that vague references to meeting topics were insufficient under Virginia’s Freedom of Information Act. The board’s new motion explicitly detailed exemptions related to personnel, economic development, and active litigation, showing a clear effort to comply with judicial mandates for greater specificity. The root of this change lies in a prior incident where the board illegally held a closed session to discuss a supervisor’s resignation, only to be criticized for cryptic meeting descriptions. The ruling emphasized that general references do not meet legal standards, requiring effective identification of the subject matter. Consequently, the board’s current detailed approach highlights a corrective response to these strict judicial interpretations, ensuring that any closure of public meetings is grounded in precise, statutory language rather than ambiguity. This case is relevant to the open data community as it illustrates the ongoing tension between government secrecy and public access. It underscores that legal victories often require sustained pressure and that procedural transparency is just as critical as the content of public records. The board’s adherence to strict disclosure standards sets a precedent for how local governments must handle sensitive information, reinforcing the necessity of robust open data frameworks to hold public bodies accountable.
