This investigation reveals that Michigan wildlife officials misled the public by fabricating stories of aggressive wolf attacks on humans to justify killing protected gray wolves. The agency falsely claimed the wolves posed a direct threat to people, a narrative used to bypass federal laws prohibiting the killing of endangered species for livestock protection alone. In reality, the wolves were eliminated primarily because they were killing valuable cattle, creating significant financial burdens for the state through taxpayer-funded reimbursements to ranchers. The discrepancy between public statements and internal records highlights a systemic failure in transparency and accountability within the Department of Natural Resources. Internal emails demonstrate that officials were aware the wolves did not exhibit fearful behavior toward humans yet continued to promote exaggerated anecdotes, including entirely fictional incidents involving children at daycares. This deliberate obfuscation suggests that economic interests, rather than scientific assessment or public safety, drove the decision to delist and kill the wolves, undermining trust in the agency’s management practices. This case is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how government secrecy can facilitate misinformation and corruption. By withholding or delaying public records through legal loopholes, officials prevented journalists and citizens from verifying the facts behind wildlife management decisions. The incident underscores the essential role of transparent data access in holding public institutions accountable, ensuring that policy debates regarding endangered species are based on verified evidence rather than manipulated narratives.
