Violent crimes in the Sligo-Leitrim division have risen significantly, while property and fraud-related offenses have decreased. This divergent trend highlights shifting local safety dynamics, where aggressive offenses like sexual violence and assaults are increasing alongside a spike in drink-driving incidents. Understanding these specific shifts is crucial for community awareness and resource allocation in policing efforts. However, the reliability of these statistics is tempered by historical data recording issues. The Central Statistics Office recently resumed publishing figures after verifying the integrity of police data systems, addressing previous irregularities in how crimes were classified and recorded. This context is vital for open data, as it underscores the necessity of validating source accuracy before drawing definitive conclusions from administrative datasets. The article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the tension between transparency and data quality. While releasing detailed crime statistics supports public accountability, it also exposes the complexities of maintaining clean, standardized datasets in public sector records. It reminds stakeholders that open data initiatives must account for historical inconsistencies and classification errors to ensure the information remains trustworthy and actionable for researchers and the public alike.
