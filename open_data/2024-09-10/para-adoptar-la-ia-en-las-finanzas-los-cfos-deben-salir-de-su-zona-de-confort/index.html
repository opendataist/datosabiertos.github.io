Para adoptar la IA en las finanzas, los CFOs deben salir de su zona de confort

The adoption of artificial intelligence in the financial sector requires proactive leadership that goes beyond mere technological investment, focusing on closing the trust gap between executives and operational teams. Chief financial officers have the critical responsibility of fostering a culture in which technology is perceived as a value-enhancing tool rather than a threat, ensuring that the workforce feels comfortable and equipped to leverage these new capabilities. To achieve effective integration, it is essential to prioritize transparency, auditability, and openness in data management. By applying the same rigorous standards of clarity and understanding required for traditional financial statement reporting, leaders can build the necessary credibility. This data traceability enables algorithm-driven decisions to be verified, which is essential for maintaining institutional trust and ensuring integrity in automated processes. The relevance of this article to open data lies in its emphasis on the need for clear, accessible, and well-documented data as the foundation for algorithmic transparency. Trust in AI systems depends directly on the quality and openness of the information that feeds them; without open and comprehensible data, it is impossible to ensure the required auditability. Therefore, promoting open and understandable standards in financial data is an indispensable prerequisite for an ethical and sustainable digital transformation.
