‘The system has fallen apart’: A child dies every 3 days under Ontario’s care network | Globalnews.ca

‘The system has fallen apart’: A child dies every 3 days under Ontario’s care network | Globalnews.ca

The article reveals that between 2020 and 2022, 354 children associated with Ontario’s child welfare system died, averaging one every three days. Most of these fatalities involved children with open case files, indicating that being under state supervision did not guarantee safety. This high mortality rate among monitored children suggests a systemic failure to protect the most vulnerable, contradicting the fundamental duty of care entrusted to the government. The lack of comprehensive investigations into these deaths leaves many circumstances unclear, with a significant portion classified as undetermined. Advocates and former officials criticize the absence of mandatory public inquests for child welfare-related deaths, arguing that this opacity prevents accountability and obscures the specific ways the system fails. Without rigorous scrutiny, it becomes difficult to identify structural flaws or implement effective preventative measures. This report is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how freedom of information requests can expose critical gaps in government transparency and child safety. By obtaining previously aggregated or inaccessible data, journalists highlighted a severe public welfare crisis that official channels had not adequately communicated. The incident underscores the importance of accessible, granular data in holding institutions accountable and driving necessary policy reforms to protect vulnerable populations.
