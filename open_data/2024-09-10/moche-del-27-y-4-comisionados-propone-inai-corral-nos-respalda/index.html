Moche del 27% y 4 comisionados, propone INAI; Corral nos respalda

The proposed reform to abolish the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) has encountered strong resistance, with alternative proposals favoring structural downsizing over outright elimination. This underscores the vulnerability of institutions tasked with guaranteeing access to information when political pressures prioritize budget cuts over transparency rights. The article emphasizes the critical role of independent bodies like the INAI in safeguarding open data principles against administrative dismantling. It illustrates how legislative attacks on such entities directly threaten the availability and reliability of public information. Ultimately, this situation reveals the fragility of Mexico’s open data infrastructure. It serves as a warning that, without robust institutional protection, access to government information remains susceptible to political whims and fiscal austerity measures.
