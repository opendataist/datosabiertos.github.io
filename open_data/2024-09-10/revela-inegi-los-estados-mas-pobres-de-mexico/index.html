Revela INEGI los estados más pobres de México

The National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) released preliminary data on the State Gross Domestic Product (GDP) for 2022, identifying Zacatecas, Baja California Sur, Nayarit, Colima, and Tlaxcala as the states with the lowest economic output. This report highlights the structural dependence of these regions, ranging from mining and tourism to agriculture and port logistics, revealing how specific sectoral vulnerabilities limit broader economic growth despite their unique productive profiles. However, the analysis emphasizes that GDP is insufficient for accurately measuring poverty or quality of life. While it serves as a primary indicator of economic activity, relying solely on aggregate production figures obscures the complex reality of inequality and individual well-being. A comprehensive understanding of regional welfare requires integrating additional metrics, such as per capita income and social indicators, to avoid conflating low macroeconomic output with low individual prosperity. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the necessity of transparency and multidimensional data availability. By advocating for a broader set of indicators beyond GDP, it supports the open data movement’s goal of providing users with holistic tools for decision-making. Accessible, diverse datasets enable stakeholders to look past simple economic aggregates and address underlying social challenges more effectively, fostering informed public discourse and policy development.
