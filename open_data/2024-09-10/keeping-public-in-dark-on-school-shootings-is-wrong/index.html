Keeping public in dark on school shootings is wrong

Keeping public in dark on school shootings is wrong

The article highlights a critical failure in transparency following the Perry High School shooting, where the public remains uninformed about official investigative findings. Despite an eight-month wait for answers regarding the shooter’s motives and safety failures, the county attorney has withheld the detailed report by citing exemptions for police investigative documents. This decision prioritizes legal technicalities over the community’s urgent need for accurate information, leaving families and educators without closure or understanding of the tragic events. This case is directly relevant to open data principles as it illustrates the tension between statutory secrecy and the public’s right to access authoritative information. The author argues that legal custodians possess the discretion to release records, even when exemptions exist, and that withholding such data undermines public trust and safety. The inability to access this digital and documentary record prevents the community from addressing systemic vulnerabilities, demonstrating how opaque data management can hinder societal learning and prevention efforts. Ultimately, the article calls for legislative reform to clarify the balance between investigative confidentiality and public access. It urges officials to justify their secrecy and suggests that the law should be updated to ensure that critical data regarding public safety incidents is available when needed. By keeping the public in the dark, authorities deny citizens the ability to analyze causes and implement effective safeguards, reinforcing the need for greater accountability and openness in government-held records.
