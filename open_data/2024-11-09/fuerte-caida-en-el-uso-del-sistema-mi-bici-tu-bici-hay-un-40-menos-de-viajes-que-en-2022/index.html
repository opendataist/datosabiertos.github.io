Fuerte caída en el uso del sistema Mi bici tu bici: hay un 40% menos de viajes que en 2022

Rosario’s public bicycle system has experienced a sustained decline in usage for two years, marking a significant setback after reaching historic highs in 2022. This downward trend suggests that accessibility barriers, rather than simple seasonal weather variations, are now the primary drivers reducing rider engagement. The correlation between the introduction of the Sube card and the subsequent difficulties in reloading the old Movi payment method indicates that payment infrastructure challenges directly impact public transport adoption. The shift away from the Movi card created a practically insurmountable hurdle for users, effectively deterring casual riders and contributing to a sharp drop in monthly trips. While climate continues to influence usage patterns, the baseline number of rentals has fallen drastically since 2023, demonstrating that convenience is critical for maintaining public service viability. The inability to easily reload payment methods rendered the system obsolete in the eyes of many citizens, leading to a steady erosion of its user base over time. This case highlights the critical importance of open data in identifying systemic failures and validating hypotheses regarding public service efficiency. By analyzing official municipal records, stakeholders can pinpoint the exact moment payment policy changes correlated with usage drops, proving that transparent data is essential for diagnosing operational issues. Ultimately, the article underscores how accessible information enables evidence-based decision-making, which is crucial for modernizing infrastructure and ensuring that open data practices lead to tangible improvements in urban mobility services.
