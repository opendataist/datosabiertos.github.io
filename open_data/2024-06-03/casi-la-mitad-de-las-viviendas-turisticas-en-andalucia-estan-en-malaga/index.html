Casi la mitad de las viviendas turísticas en Andalucía están en Málaga

Andalusia emerges as the undisputed leader in Spain’s tourist housing sector, accounting for nearly a quarter of the national total and significantly outpacing other regions in absolute numbers. This dominance highlights a structural shift in the regional real estate market, where short-term rental units have become a pervasive feature of the residential landscape, far exceeding the national average density. The geographic distribution reveals a strong concentration along coastal areas and major tourist hubs, with provinces like Málaga driving the bulk of this growth. However, the data also indicates that certain inland municipalities are experiencing notable saturation, suggesting that the trend of converting residential properties into tourist accommodations is no longer limited to traditional seaside destinations but is spreading throughout diverse localities. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent, accessible, and up-to-date public statistics to monitor urban dynamics. As tourism impacts housing availability and community structures, open access to granular data on property registration and occupancy allows researchers, policymakers, and citizens to analyze trends, identify potential imbalances, and advocate for sustainable urban planning based on empirical evidence rather than anecdotal observation.
