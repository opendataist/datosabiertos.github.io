How do wages in Topeka and Manhattan compare to the nation?

How do wages in Topeka and Manhattan compare to the nation?

The Bureau of Labor Statistics report highlights that major Kansas cities like Topeka and Manhattan lag significantly behind the national average for hourly wages. This disparity underscores regional economic challenges, with management and healthcare sectors leading pay while food preparation roles remain the lowest compensated. Shawnee County emerged as the strongest performer among Kansas’s largest counties, showing notable growth in both wages and employment during the final quarter of 2024. These localized insights reveal how economic opportunities and income levels vary widely across different communities within the state. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the value of accessible, granular labor statistics. By making such detailed wage and employment information publicly available, researchers and policymakers can better analyze regional disparities, inform workforce development strategies, and advocate for equitable economic growth.
