Asistentes virtuales privados: ¿existen opciones que no espían tus datos?

Asistentes virtuales privados: ¿existen opciones que no espían tus datos?

The article highlights the fundamental tension between the convenience offered by mainstream virtual assistants and the protection of privacy. While services like Siri or Alexa provide immediate functionality, they process data in the cloud, exposing users to risks of corporate surveillance and security breaches. This centralization of data calls into question the true confidentiality of user interactions, despite the incremental privacy controls implemented by manufacturers. As an alternative, open-source solutions and local tools are presented that prioritize on-device information processing, eliminating dependence on external servers. Projects such as Mycroft and Almond ensure a high level of privacy through fully local voice processing, although they require greater technical expertise and sacrifice seamless integration with popular services. Similarly, private search tools like BraveLeo offer contextual assistance without tracking, demonstrating that artificial intelligence can coexist with strict privacy standards. This discussion is relevant to the open data movement, as it underscores the importance of transparency, data sovereignty, and the accessibility of ethical technological alternatives. The shift toward local and open-source models reflects a critical demand for systems that do not exploit user information for commercial purposes. This paradigm shift drives the need for open standards that enable citizens to regain control over their digital footprint, balancing innovation with fundamental rights.
