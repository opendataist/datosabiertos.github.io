The Peshawar High Court emphasized the necessity of utilizing the Right to Information framework rather than bypassing it through judicial petitions. By directing a petitioner to the provincial RTI Commission for details on pending cases, the bench reinforced that statutory bodies are the primary channels for accessing public records. This ruling underscores the principle that citizens should leverage designated transparency mechanisms established by law before seeking judicial intervention for information retrieval. This decision highlights the operational hierarchy in open data governance, where specialized commissions hold the authority to mandate disclosure from public bodies. It serves as a critical reminder that legal rights to information exist independently of the courts, provided the proper administrative procedures are followed. Consequently, individuals are encouraged to engage directly with oversight institutions to ensure accountability and transparency in law enforcement records, thereby reducing unnecessary litigation and strengthening institutional compliance. The relevance to open data lies in the affirmation of statutory rights to access information held by public authorities. This case validates the RTI Act’s power to compel government agencies to release records, promoting a culture of transparency. By directing citizens to these commissions, the judiciary supports the ecosystem of open data, ensuring that information flows freely from public institutions to the public. This approach strengthens democratic oversight and ensures that data availability is governed by clear, accessible legal frameworks rather than ad-hoc judicial remedies.
