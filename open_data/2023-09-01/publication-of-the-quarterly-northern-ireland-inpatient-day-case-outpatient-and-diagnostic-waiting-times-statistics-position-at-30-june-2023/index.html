Publication of the Quarterly Northern Ireland Inpatient, Day Case, Outpatient and Diagnostic Waiting Times Statistics - position at 30 June 2023

This report highlights significant challenges within Northern Ireland’s healthcare system, as evidenced by the Department of Health’s latest quarterly statistics. The data reveals that a substantial majority of patients are waiting far beyond established draft targets for first outpatient appointments, inpatient treatments, and diagnostic services. For instance, nearly half of outpatient patients wait over a year, while more than half of those needing inpatient care exceed the 52-week limit. These figures indicate a systemic backlog that continues to strain resources and patient wellbeing, with waiting times showing slight increases or stagnation compared to previous periods. The implications of these extended wait times are severe, extending beyond simple delays to include dangerously long periods for critical care. With median waiting times for outpatient and inpatient treatments nearing or exceeding a year, and some cases reaching several years, the system is failing to meet its own benchmarks. This suggests that capacity issues are deep-seated, affecting not just the volume of patients but the efficiency of service delivery across diagnostics and treatment pathways. The inability to adhere to short-term targets for urgent diagnostic reporting further underscores the operational pressures faced by healthcare providers. This information is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the value of transparent, standardized government statistics in holding public institutions accountable. By publishing granular details on waiting lists and performance against targets, the Department enables researchers, journalists, and citizens to scrutinize healthcare efficacy. Open access to such metrics fosters informed public debate and policy advocacy, ensuring that systemic inefficiencies are visible and actionable. It reinforces the principle that data transparency is crucial for driving improvements in public service delivery and maintaining trust in health infrastructure.
