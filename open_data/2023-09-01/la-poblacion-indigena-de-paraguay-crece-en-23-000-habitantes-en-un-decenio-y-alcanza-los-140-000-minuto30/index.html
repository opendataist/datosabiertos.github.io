La población indígena de Paraguay crece en 23.000 habitantes en un decenio y alcanza los 140.000 | Minuto30

Paraguay’s latest census reveals significant growth in its indigenous population, highlighting the successful integration of cultural respect and community-led data collection methods. This approach underscores a vital open data principle: involving marginalized groups in their own representation ensures greater accuracy, trust, and inclusivity in national statistics. The findings expose stark linguistic and demographic disparities among nineteen native communities, with Guaraní languages dominating while smaller groups face marginalization. By making this granular data publicly available, the government supports transparency and enables researchers to analyze social inequities, fostering policies that address specific cultural needs rather than relying on generalized assumptions. Ultimately, this report demonstrates how open, participatory data practices can empower indigenous voices while correcting national population trends influenced by migration and health crises. Sharing these insights promotes accountability and helps civil society monitor the effective implementation of rights, proving that accessible data is essential for equitable governance and social justice in diverse societies.
