CCOO lamenta que el registro de accidentes laborales con baja deja el mes de noviembre con más siniestralidad desde 2009 - El Faradio | Periodismo que cuenta

CCOO lamenta que el registro de accidentes laborales con baja deja el mes de noviembre con más siniestralidad desde 2009 - El Faradio | Periodismo que cuenta

The recent report highlights a worrying surge in work-related accidents in Cantabria, marking the worst November in over a decade. This increase points to a systemic failure by companies to comply with safety regulations, leading to greater suffering for workers and revealing a critical shortage of preventive resources and investment in occupational health. These figures underscore the urgent need for strong safety policies and adequate training to protect employees. By emphasizing that statistics represent a human cost rather than mere numbers, the article argues that decisive action from employers is essential to reduce accident rates and ensure genuinely safe working environments. For open data initiatives, these statistics demonstrate the vital importance of transparent and accessible workplace safety data. Publicly available, disaggregated data enables effective monitoring of industry compliance, allowing stakeholders to hold organizations accountable and drive necessary improvements in labor conditions through informed public scrutiny and policy advocacy.
