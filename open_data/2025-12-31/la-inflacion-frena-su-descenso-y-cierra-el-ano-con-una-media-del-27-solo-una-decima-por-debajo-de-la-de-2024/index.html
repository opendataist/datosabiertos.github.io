La inflación frena su descenso y cierra el año con una media del 2,7%, sólo una décima por debajo de la de 2024

La inflación frena su descenso y cierra el año con una media del 2,7%, sólo una décima por debajo de la de 2024

Spain’s inflation remains stubbornly above the European Central Bank’s target, reflecting a broader economic challenge where price stability has proven elusive despite recent moderation. The persistence of inflation above the two percent threshold for five consecutive years indicates structural pressures that prevent a swift return to normalcy, creating uncertainty about whether the economy can achieve its monetary policy goals in the near future. The data reveals that volatile sectors, specifically energy and fresh foods, are the primary drivers of the aggregate consumer price index, while underlying inflation moves closer to the desired range. This divergence suggests that while core price pressures are easing, external shocks in essential commodities continue to distort overall metrics. Consequently, the headline figures remain elevated not due to sustained demand-pull inflation, but rather through temporary supply-side fluctuations in critical input costs. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it highlights the necessity for granular, high-frequency statistical visibility to diagnose economic health accurately. Transparent access to detailed indices allows researchers and citizens to distinguish between transient price spikes and entrenched inflationary trends. Such clarity is essential for fostering informed public debate and ensuring that policymakers can craft effective, evidence-based responses to persistent cost-of-living pressures.
