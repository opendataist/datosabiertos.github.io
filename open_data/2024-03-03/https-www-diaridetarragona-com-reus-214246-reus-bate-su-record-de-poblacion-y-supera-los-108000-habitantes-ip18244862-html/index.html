The article highlights that Reus has surpassed its previous population record, reaching over 108,000 residents in 2023. This milestone confirms the city's consolidation as a municipality with more than 100,000 inhabitants. The primary driver of this demographic growth is positive net migration, as natural population growth remains negative due to structural factors such as higher death rates than births. Consequently, the influx of new residents is essential for sustaining the city's population levels and reversing previous declines. Local authorities view this growth as an opportunity to reinforce sustainable urban development. The focus has shifted toward investing in services that ensure quality of life and implementing sound urban planning to accommodate the increasing demand. There is a strong emphasis on balancing economic promotion with social welfare, ensuring that living in Reus remains an attractive and viable option for both current and future residents amidst rising housing pressures. This case is particularly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how public statistical records, such as those from the INE, are crucial for understanding complex demographic dynamics. By analyzing transparent data on migration balances versus natural growth, policymakers and citizens can identify the true causes behind urban expansion. This transparency allows for evidence-based decision-making in areas like housing and public services, ensuring that city planning responds effectively to the specific needs of a growing, migrant-influenced population.
