Un total de 55.756 personas teletrabajaron en C-LM en 2024, un 6,3% "a la cola en teletrabajo en España" según UGT

The article reveals that teleworking remains marginal in Castilla-La Mancha, affecting only a small fraction of the workforce compared to national averages. This disparity highlights a significant gap in labor market evolution, where regional adoption lags behind global trends and other Spanish territories, suggesting that structural and cultural barriers prevent the broader implementation of remote work models in the area. Beyond adoption rates, the text emphasizes the critical need for legal protections and digital rights. It warns that the lack of regulation exposes workers to risks such as blurred work-life boundaries and difficulties with digital disconnection. The union argues that without clear frameworks, remote work can lead to negative health and social consequences, underscoring the urgency of establishing robust standards that ensure employee well-being alongside flexibility. This content is highly relevant to open data because it relies on official statistical records from the National Institute of Statistics to substantiate claims about labor trends. By publicly disseminating these specific metrics, the data enables civil society and unions to monitor policy effectiveness, advocate for legislative changes, and hold institutions accountable for improving working conditions based on transparent, accessible evidence.
