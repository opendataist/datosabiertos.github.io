Imágenes IA al estilo Ghibli: ¿Por qué ChatGPT enfrentaría problemas por derechos de autor?

Imágenes IA al estilo Ghibli: ¿Por qué ChatGPT enfrentaría problemas por derechos de autor?

The viral spread of “Ghibli-ification” via ChatGPT exposes a fundamental ethical tension between technological innovation and the protection of intellectual property. This tool generates images inspired by Hayao Miyazaki’s style without obtaining a license, raising serious questions about whether artificial intelligence models have legal permission to train on copyrighted works. This use directly challenges the legitimacy of training AI on the work of living artists and its impact on the professional livelihoods of human creators. Despite technical safeguards announced by OpenAI to block individual artists’ styles, the trend continues to proliferate, sparking outrage over the potential commercial exploitation of Miyazaki’s legacy. Artists and legal experts argue that while “style” is generally not protected by copyright, the replication of specific and distinctive elements from famous works may constitute infringement. Miyazaki himself firmly rejects this technology, viewing it as an insult to life and human creativity—a stance that contrasts sharply with the enthusiasm of the tech industry. This case is crucial for the open-data community, as it illustrates the urgency of regulating how artistic datasets are collected, processed, and used. The lack of transparency regarding AI training licenses underscores the need for clear ethical standards in the development of data-driven models. The dispute highlights that democratizing access to generative tools must not override the moral and economic rights of original creators, setting a significant precedent for debates on algorithmic justice and data governance.
