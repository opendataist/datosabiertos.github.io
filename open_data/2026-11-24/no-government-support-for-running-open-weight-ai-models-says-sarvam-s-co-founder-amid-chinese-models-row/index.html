No government support for running open-weight AI models, says Sarvam's co-founder amid Chinese models row

No government support for running open-weight AI models, says Sarvam's co-founder amid Chinese models row

Sarvam AI co-founder Pratyush Kumar has clarified that government subsidies under the IndiaAI Mission were exclusively used to develop the company’s indigenous foundation models, not its commercial inference services. By distinguishing between state-supported training infrastructure and privately funded commercial operations, the startup addresses concerns that public funds were utilized to host foreign-developed models, thereby attempting to align its business practices with national goals of technological self-reliance. The controversy highlights the complex tension between sovereign AI ambitions and the practical reality of global model availability. While the government aims to reduce dependence on foreign technology, Sarvam’s platform offers access to open-weight models from Chinese developers on domestic hardware. This distinction raises broader questions about whether true AI sovereignty requires exclusive domestic model development or if hosting international open-source models on local infrastructure also contributes to national digital independence. This issue is highly relevant to open data and open source communities, as Sarvam makes its proprietary model available as an open-weight model while leveraging domestic infrastructure to serve other open-weight variants. It underscores the growing need for transparency in how public resources interact with open ecosystems and emphasizes the importance of distinguishing between training foundational knowledge and serving inference queries. Such clarity is essential for fostering trust in public-private partnerships within the open data landscape.
