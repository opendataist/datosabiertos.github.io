Mexico's economy surprises: GDP grows 3.3% in 2026, reaching its largest advance in three years

Mexico's economy surprises: GDP grows 3.3% in 2026, reaching its largest advance in three years

Mexico’s economy experienced a significant acceleration in its last quarter, marking its best annual performance in over three years. This broad-based rebound, driven primarily by services, construction, and trade, indicates that national economic activity maintained a positive and robust trajectory, surpassing expectations of stabilization and suggesting renewed dynamism in the domestic market. The tertiary sector consolidated itself as the main engine of this growth, with services having a disproportionate impact on GDP. Meanwhile, industry and construction showed solid gains, with the latter registering particularly vigorous expansion. This divergence among sectors reveals an economy in transition, where demand for services and real estate activity are leading the recovery, while manufacturing grows at a more moderate pace. This report is crucial to the open data movement, as it underscores the critical importance of having timely and transparent access to official statistical information. The ability to analyze these figures in real time enables researchers, citizens, and businesses to make informed decisions about the country’s economic health. Furthermore, it highlights the need to standardize and democratize these macroeconomic data to promote transparency and accountability in public management.
