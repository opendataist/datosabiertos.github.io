Madrid hosts regional and local governments and civil society from Ibero-America to jointly address the challenges of open government

The Ibero-American Meeting on Open Local Government, organized by Madrid and the Open Government Alliance, brings together numerous institutions to redefine public service as a way of understanding governance, going beyond merely being a set of tools. This collaborative space seeks to strengthen ties and share knowledge on transparency, participation, and accountability, emphasizing that institutional trust is rebuilt through evaluable, practical, and effective reforms that respond to citizens’ needs. The relevance to open data lies in the focus on using high-quality data and clear language as pillars to improve communication with citizens and facilitate access to public information. By promoting digital governance and artificial intelligence, the meeting underscores that transparency is not an end in itself, but a means to democratize knowledge of administrative activities. This implies that open data must be accessible and understandable to expand opportunities for citizen involvement in decision-making, transforming the relationship between the State and society. Finally, the local level is positioned as crucial for developing participatory policies, given that proximity to citizens allows for adapting solutions and transferring best practices across different contexts. Collaboration among cities and the creation of Ibero-American networks enable addressing common challenges such as corruption and polarization, using social innovation and evidence-based management. Ultimately, this event consolidates the idea that modern public management must integrate the strategic use of open information to build institutions that are more accountable, closer to citizens, and effective, ensuring that technology and transparency truly serve to improve people’s lives.
