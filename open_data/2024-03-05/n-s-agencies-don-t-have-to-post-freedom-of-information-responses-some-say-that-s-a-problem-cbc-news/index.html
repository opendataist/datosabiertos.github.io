N.S. agencies don't have to post freedom of information responses. Some say that's a problem | CBC News

The article highlights a critical transparency gap in Nova Scotia, where the lack of mandatory requirements for public bodies to publish completed access to information requests hinders public accountability. Because responses are often kept private by individual agencies, citizens and journalists are denied insight into the decision-making processes that impact the environment and community welfare. This opacity prevents a full understanding of government interactions with industry, leaving the public reliant on the limited disclosures made by the central provincial portal. The implications for open data are significant, as the current system allows for selective sharing that can obscure uncomfortable truths or restrict public awareness to only those who actively file requests. Experts argue that this "wiggle room" undermines democratic health by preventing the broader dissemination of information that could benefit entire communities. Without standardized, open publication of these records, the potential for collaborative scrutiny and informed civic engagement is severely diminished, as valuable data remains siloed rather than contributing to a shared public resource. Relevance to open data lies in the urgent need for systemic reform to ensure that government records are not just accessible to individuals, but openly available to all. The ongoing legislative review represents a pivotal moment to mandate the publication of these records, thereby transforming isolated data points into a comprehensive, searchable knowledge base. Achieving true open data in this context requires moving beyond voluntary measures to enforce transparency, ensuring that information serves the public interest rather than remaining hidden behind bureaucratic discretion.
