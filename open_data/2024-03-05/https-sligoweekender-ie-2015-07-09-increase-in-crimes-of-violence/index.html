Recent crime statistics for the Sligo-Leitrim division reveal a significant rise in violent offenses, including sex offenses, assaults, and robberies, alongside increased property damage. Conversely, non-violent crimes such as theft, fraud, and public order offenses have declined. These shifts highlight evolving patterns in local criminal activity over the past year. A critical context for interpreting these figures is the Central Statistics Office’s recent suspension and subsequent resumption of data publication. This pause followed a major report exposing irregularities in how Gardaí recorded crimes on the PULSE system. The halt allowed for a comprehensive quality review to address systemic issues that previously compromised the accuracy of official crime records. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the vital importance of data governance and integrity in public sector reporting. It illustrates how administrative flaws in data collection systems, such as incorrect classification or missing records, can distort statistical insights. For open data initiatives, this case emphasizes the necessity of rigorous data validation and transparency to ensure that publicly available statistics accurately reflect reality and support informed decision-making.
