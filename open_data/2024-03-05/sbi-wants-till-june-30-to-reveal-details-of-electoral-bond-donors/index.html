SBI wants till June 30 to reveal details of electoral bond donors

The State Bank of India is seeking a delay in disclosing voter-relevant data on political donations, arguing that the technical complexity of matching donor identities to contributions is hindering compliance with the Supreme Court’s order. The bank contends that the Electoral Bond scheme was deliberately designed with fragmented, non-centralized data silos to protect anonymity, making the reconciliation of purchase and redemption records a time-consuming manual exercise. This procedural claim suggests that the system’s architecture was optimized to obscure information, creating significant barriers to transparency. The Supreme Court previously struck down the scheme as unconstitutional, ruling that anonymous political funding violated citizens' right to information regarding potential corruption and undue influence. By delaying the disclosure of these records, the implementation of this landmark judgment is postponed until after the upcoming general elections. This delay effectively allows the current political landscape to remain unaffected by the immediate exposure of funding sources, prioritizing procedural convenience over the prompt restoration of electoral transparency. This situation highlights a critical challenge in open data governance: the interplay between legal mandates and technical implementation. It demonstrates how systemic design choices, intended to ensure privacy, can be exploited to delay public accountability and data accessibility. For open data advocates, this case underscores the importance of ensuring that data structures required for public oversight are centralized, digital, and readily accessible, preventing institutions from citing logistical complexity to withhold information that is essential for democratic integrity.
