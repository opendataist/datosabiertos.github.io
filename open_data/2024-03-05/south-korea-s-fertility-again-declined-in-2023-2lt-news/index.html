South Korea's fertility again declined in 2023 - 2LT News

South Korea’s record-low fertility rate highlights how economic and social barriers suppress childbirth, threatening national stability despite significant government spending. The crisis is exacerbated by severe gender inequality, particularly the largest pay gap in the OECD, which prevents women from balancing career progression with caregiving responsibilities. This data illustrates the critical need for open data to expose systemic inequities and inform effective policy interventions beyond mere financial incentives. The situation reflects a broader East Asian demographic collapse, with Japan and China also reporting historic lows. Without transparent, accessible data, policymakers may continue to misdiagnose the root causes of declining birth rates. Open data enables rigorous analysis of how housing costs and workplace structures interact with family planning decisions across different regions. This case underscores that demographic challenges cannot be solved with funding alone. By making socioeconomic datasets publicly available, researchers and citizens can advocate for structural reforms. Ultimately, open data serves as a vital tool for understanding the complex interplay between gender equality, economic pressure, and population sustainability in modern societies.
