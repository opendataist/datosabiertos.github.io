Elon Musk's X targeted with nine privacy complaints after grabbing EU users' data for training Grok | TechCrunch

Elon Musk's X targeted with nine privacy complaints after grabbing EU users' data for training Grok | TechCrunch

The core issue involves X utilizing the personal data of European Union users to train its Grok AI model without obtaining explicit consent, thereby potentially violating the General Data Protection Regulation. Multiple privacy complaints have been filed across several EU nations, arguing that relying on "legitimate interest" as a legal basis is insufficient for this type of processing. Privacy advocates emphasize that such direct interactions require clear user permission, similar to standard privacy prompts used for other features. Regulatory bodies, particularly the Irish Data Protection Commission, have faced scrutiny for their enforcement approach, with critics alleging insufficient action to protect user rights. While X introduced an opt-out mechanism, it failed to notify users beforehand or allow them to prevent their data from being ingested during the initial period. This delay highlights a significant gap in transparency, as users remained unaware that their posts were being used for AI development, undermining the fundamental principle that individuals should control how their information is utilized. This situation is critical to the open data movement because it underscores the tension between innovation in artificial intelligence and the fundamental right to data privacy. It reinforces the necessity for robust legal frameworks that prioritize user consent over corporate efficiency, ensuring that data scraping for AI does not circumvent established privacy protections. The outcome of these complaints may set a precedent for how tech companies must handle personal information in the age of generative AI, impacting global standards for ethical data usage and regulatory compliance.
