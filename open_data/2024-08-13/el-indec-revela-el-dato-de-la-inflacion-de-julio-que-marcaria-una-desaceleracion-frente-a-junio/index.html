El Indec revela el dato de la inflación de julio que marcaría una desaceleración frente a junio

The National Institute of Statistics and Censuses (INDEC) is about to release the Consumer Price Index for July, consolidating a trend of inflationary deceleration following the rebound recorded in June. This return to a downward trajectory suggests that economic policy has succeeded in moderating the pace of price increases, although private estimates indicate a figure slightly higher than the government’s ambitious expectations. The divergence between official and private forecasts highlights the complexity of measuring economic reality in an environment characterized by high volatility. The relevance of this article to open data lies in the critical importance of transparency and comparability between official figures and private estimates. In a context where multiple sources generate different data, access to clear methodologies and open data from INDEC enables citizens and analysts to understand structural differences, such as the impact of seasonal or regulated components. The timely and accessible publication of these indicators is essential for maintaining public trust and facilitating an informed debate on the effectiveness of public policies. Furthermore, the contrast between the national data and that of the City of Buenos Aires illustrates how regional variations can distort macroeconomic interpretation if not properly contextualized. This scenario underscores the need for open data to include detailed disaggregations that explain the underlying causes of fluctuations, such as the influence of exchange rates or climatic factors on food prices. Only through the availability of granular and verifiable information can the true behavior of inflation and its impact on people’s daily lives be accurately assessed.
