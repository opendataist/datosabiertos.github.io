Electoral bonds case: Transparency triumphs over donors’ right to privacy

Electoral bonds case: Transparency triumphs over donors’ right to privacy

The Supreme Court declared the Electoral Bonds Scheme unconstitutional, ruling that it disproportionately infringed upon voters' fundamental right to know about political funding sources. By prioritizing donor anonymity over transparency, the scheme obstructed the electorate's ability to make informed choices, which is essential for maintaining free and fair elections. The court determined that shielding the identity of donors, particularly corporations, created an opaque environment that facilitated potential quid pro quo arrangements, thereby undermining the integrity of the democratic process. This decision highlights the critical link between open data and democratic accountability. Without accessible, clear data on political contributions, citizens cannot effectively scrutinize the influence of wealth on policy formation. The ruling underscores that transparency is not merely an administrative preference but a constitutional necessity, ensuring that information flows freely from institutions to the public to prevent corruption and undue influence in governance. Ultimately, the judgment establishes that while privacy rights are significant, they cannot override the public’s right to information regarding political financing. By invalidating the scheme, the court reinforces the principle that political funding must be visible to ensure a level playing field. This precedes a broader expectation for governments and institutions to adopt open data practices that enhance civic engagement and safeguard democratic values against secrecy and opacity.
