Stimulus effects of a large public employment programme

A South African public employment program significantly boosts participant spending, particularly on non-essential goods, with lasting economic effects even after the program ends. The study extrapolates these spending increases to demonstrate substantial growth in domestic factor incomes, highlighting positive impacts on both national and local wage bills. This evidence supports the broader relevance of open data by showing how transparent, individual-level spending records can accurately quantify the multiplier effects of government initiatives on wider economic stability.
