SNP minister for transparency deletes his WhatsApps messages after 24 hours

Scottish Minister George Adam faced scrutiny after his accidental admission that he uses disappearing messages on WhatsApp, highlighting the tension between informal digital communication and statutory records. This incident occurred shortly after the Scottish Information Commissioner launched an intervention into the government’s retention of informal messages, citing risks to transparency and accountability revealed during the UK Covid Inquiry. Although Adam insists decisions are not made on the platform and claims such messages are merely logistical reminders that do not replace official records, the automatic deletion mechanism raises serious questions about record-keeping integrity. The core issue centers on whether critical government business is inadvertently escaping public scrutiny through temporary messaging apps. Adam argues that official channels are exclusively used for substantive work, asserting his commitment to openness as the minister for freedom of information. However, the presence of a default timer that erases content after twenty-four hours suggests a potential gap between stated policy and technical reality, challenging the government’s narrative that informal communications do not constitute decision-making processes or require formal retention. This revelation is crucial for open data advocates because it underscores the fragility of democratic transparency when political actors rely on private, ephemeral tools for official duties. The inability to preserve these communications impedes public accountability and violates the principles of open data, which demand accessible and complete government records. As political parties face criticism for allegedly covering tracks, the episode serves as a stark reminder that technology choices directly impact the public’s right to know, urging stricter controls on digital governance to ensure all state business remains permanently accessible.
