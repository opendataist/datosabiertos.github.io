MEC registró 14.344 títulos médicos en los últimos 10 años - Política - ABC Color

The article reveals a massive surge in medical degree registrations in Paraguay, driven primarily by private universities, particularly Uninter, which has issued hundreds of degrees in a single year. This concentration highlights a significant disparity with public institutions, which have graduated far fewer students over the same decade. The data suggests a systemic shift in which private entities dominate the output of medical education, raising concerns about the quality and accessibility of healthcare training across the country. Complicating this landscape are serious allegations of irregularities and potentially fraudulent degrees, prompting legislative efforts to temporarily suspend the accreditation of medical programs. The Ministry of Education admits it lacks the capacity to verify the authenticity of these degrees, relying solely on institutional declarations. Consequently, investigators are probing alleged corruption schemes involving state officials and private universities, underscoring a crisis of trust and regulatory failure within the higher education system. This report is crucial for open data advocates, as it demonstrates how public government records can expose significant governance failures when aggregated and analyzed. The availability of these registries allows civil society to detect anomalies, such as disproportionate graduation rates and potential fraud rings, that would otherwise remain hidden. It exemplifies the power of transparency in holding public institutions accountable and drives the demand for stricter data verification protocols and public oversight mechanisms.
