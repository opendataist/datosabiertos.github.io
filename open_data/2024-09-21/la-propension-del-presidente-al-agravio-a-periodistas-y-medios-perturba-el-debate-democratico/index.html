"La propensión del presidente al agravio a periodistas y medios perturba el debate democrático"

The report concludes that the President’s aggressive rhetoric against the media significantly harms democratic debate, fostering a hostile environment that encourages online harassment and self-censorship among journalists. By disproportionately targeting the press, state leadership exacerbates social polarization and undermines the necessary climate for civic dialogue, illustrating how political language directly impacts the safety and functioning of the press. Furthermore, the document highlights critical threats to open access to government information, arguing that recent decrees narrow citizens' right to know by contradicting international transparency standards. It also underscores the urgent need for robust copyright protections against technology platforms and artificial intelligence, emphasizing that sustainable journalism requires fair compensation and the legal defense of intellectual property to prevent the erosion of responsible content creation. This article is relevant to open_data because it explicitly links government transparency policies with journalistic viability and public trust. It argues that restricting access to public information not only violates democratic principles but also weakens the media’s capacity to function as an essential watchdog, thereby impacting the flow and accessibility of data crucial for an informed society.
