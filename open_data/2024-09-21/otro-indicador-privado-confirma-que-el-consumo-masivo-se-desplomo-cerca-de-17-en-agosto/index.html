Otro indicador privado confirma que el consumo masivo se desplomó cerca de 17% en agosto

The article highlights a severe contraction in mass consumption and the broader economy, driven by a significant loss in purchasing power. Reports from major consultancies confirm sharp declines in sales across all product categories, including food, beverages, and personal care, which are indicative of broader economic distress. This drop in consumer activity is not isolated to large retailers but extends to small and medium enterprises, underscoring a widespread reduction in economic vitality and demand. The root cause of this downturn is identified as the erosion of real wages, which have fallen to historic lows relative to their peak values. Even though inflationary pressures have slowed and wage negotiations have occurred, average purchasing power remains below previous levels. This sustained decline in household income has directly suppressed consumers’ ability to maintain their spending habits, leading to the observed reductions in retail sectors and contributing significantly to the contraction of gross domestic product in the first half of the year. This report is highly relevant to open_data because it demonstrates how independently verified, granular datasets from various sources can converge to tell a coherent story about economic health. By aggregating information from consumer goods monitoring firms, labor institutes, and official statistical bodies, we can move beyond isolated metrics to understand systemic trends. This multidimensional approach is essential for policymakers and researchers to identify the specific mechanisms—such as wage erosion versus inflation—that drive macroeconomic indicators, thereby improving the transparency and utility of public economic data.
