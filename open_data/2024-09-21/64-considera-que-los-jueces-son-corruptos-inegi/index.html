64% considera que los jueces son corruptos: INEGI

64% considera que los jueces son corruptos: INEGI

Data from the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) reveals a mixed public perception of judges, showing improved trust and effectiveness ratings alongside persistent concerns about corruption. While confidence has risen slightly, judges remain the second most corrupt institution, indicating a critical trust deficit. This matters for open data because transparency tools can help clarify the roles of the judiciary and reduce misinformation. By making these insights accessible, open data fosters accountability, allowing citizens to distinguish between perceived and actual performance, thereby rebuilding institutional credibility through informed public discourse.
