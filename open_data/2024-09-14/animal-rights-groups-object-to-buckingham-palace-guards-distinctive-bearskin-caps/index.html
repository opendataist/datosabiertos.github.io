The article documents the rapid disappearance of India’s single-screen cinemas, a cultural phenomenon once central to communal film-going experiences. As these historic theaters are demolished or replaced by multiplexes, a unique interactive social ecosystem is vanishing. The shift represents more than a change in venue; it signifies the loss of a collective, energetic engagement with cinema that defined generations of viewers and local economies. This transition highlights the tension between modern commercial efficiency and traditional cultural preservation. While multiplexes offer comfort and convenience, critics argue they provide a sterile, generic experience that lacks the emotional resonance of older halls. The decline underscores how economic pressures and changing consumer habits are eroding the architectural and social landscapes that once fostered a deeper, more passionate connection between audiences and films. This subject is highly relevant to open data initiatives because the physical decay of these venues creates an urgent need for digital preservation. Archiving photographs, oral histories, and location data of these disappearing sites allows for the creation of accessible cultural records. Such open datasets enable researchers and the public to study and retain knowledge of this vanishing heritage, ensuring that the stories and significance of these spaces remain available even after the buildings themselves are gone.
