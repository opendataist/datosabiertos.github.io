Office of Minister for Equality Roderic O’Gorman criticised for breaking Freedom of Information rules

The Department of Equality, led by Minister Roderic O’Gorman, has been formally reprimanded for failing to adhere to Freedom of Information regulations. An independent ruling determined that the department’s refusal to process a request for files regarding recent failed constitutional referendums was unjustified. By ignoring statutory time limits and offering no substantive response, the office violated established transparency protocols, prompting the Information Commissioner to annul their initial decision and compel a fresh review of the application. This failure to disclose information is particularly significant because other departmental records had already contradicted the government’s public claims about the referendum amendments. While ministers asserted the proposals carried no legal or financial risks, internal warnings from other government bodies highlighted substantial implications for immigration law, taxation, and public expenditure. The secrecy surrounding these documents prevented public scrutiny of the discrepancies between official campaign narratives and the internal expert assessments that were later revealed through separate FOI requests. For the open data community, this case underscores the critical importance of enforceable transparency mechanisms in government. It demonstrates how administrative delays and opaque decision-making can hinder accountability, especially when dealing with high-stakes political issues. The incident highlights the necessity of independent oversight to ensure public bodies process requests efficiently and fairly. Ultimately, it reinforces the principle that true openness requires more than passive availability of data; it demands proactive compliance with information rights to maintain public trust and enable informed democratic participation.
