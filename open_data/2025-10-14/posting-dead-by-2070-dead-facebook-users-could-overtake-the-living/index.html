Posting Dead. By 2070 Dead Facebook Users Could Overtake the Living

Posting Dead. By 2070 Dead Facebook Users Could Overtake the Living

Research from Oxford University predicts that by the end of the century, deceased Facebook accounts will outnumber active ones, creating an unprecedented digital heritage. This demographic shift forces society to confront the long-term implications of social media usage, specifically regarding who controls this data and how it serves the interests of grieving families versus future historians. The sheer volume of digital remains challenges current frameworks for privacy and archival management. Relevant to open data, this phenomenon highlights the urgent need for ethical curation of vast, accumulated datasets generated by users. The study argues that society has barely begun to address these issues, emphasizing that digital legacy management must be sustainable for decades rather than just years. This requires collaboration between tech platforms, archivists, and ethicists to ensure these digital footprints are preserved responsibly, transforming personal data into a structured resource for understanding the past. While Facebook offers legacy contacts to memorialize profiles, this current mechanism is insufficient for managing the global scale of future digital archives. The implication is that open data practices must evolve to handle the "digital dead" systematically, ensuring accessibility and security without compromising privacy. As these profiles become part of our global heritage, the open data community must lead discussions on preserving this collective memory while respecting the rights of the deceased and their loved ones.
