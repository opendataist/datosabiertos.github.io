La siniestralidad laboral se reduce mínimamente: 489 personas fallecieron en su trabajo, nueve menos hasta agosto

La siniestralidad laboral se reduce mínimamente: 489 personas fallecieron en su trabajo, nueve menos hasta agosto

The article highlights a persistent crisis in workplace safety, where accident-related fatalities remain at record levels despite a slight annual decrease. The construction sector is identified as the most dangerous industry, carrying a disproportionately high risk compared to the national average. This disparity underscores the urgent need for targeted interventions in high-risk environments, particularly within small and medium enterprises where oversight is often insufficient. Central to the discussion is the call by major trade unions for a comprehensive reform of labor risk prevention laws, which have not been updated in decades. These organizations argue that current regulations fail to address modern challenges, such as psychosocial risks and the impacts of climate change. They demand stricter enforcement, increased resources for labor inspection, and specific roles to monitor small businesses, aiming to create a robust state pact to significantly reduce accidental deaths and injuries. This issue is critically relevant to open data because transparent, accessible statistics on workplace accidents are essential for driving policy change and ensuring accountability. Public access to granular data allows civil society and unions to hold employers and the government responsible for safety standards. Without this transparency, it remains difficult to analyze trends, allocate resources effectively, or push for the legislative reforms necessary to protect workers’ lives and rights.
