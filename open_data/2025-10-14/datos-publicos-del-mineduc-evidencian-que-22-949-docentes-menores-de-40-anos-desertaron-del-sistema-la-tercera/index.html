Datos públicos del Mineduc evidencian que 22.949 docentes menores de 40 años desertaron del sistema - La Tercera

Datos públicos del Mineduc evidencian que 22.949 docentes menores de 40 años desertaron del sistema - La Tercera

Chile has postponed the increase in the minimum score required to enter teacher training programs, prioritizing the urgent need to address the crisis of teacher shortages. This regulatory change prevents thousands of young people from losing the opportunity to study at a critical time, although experts warn that lowering requirements alone does not guarantee educational quality. The proposed true solution does not lie in admissions, but in retaining already-trained talent, highlighting the need for a systemic approach that strengthens the teaching profession. The analysis reveals an alarming dropout rate of nearly 23,000 teachers under the age of 40, many of whom achieved high scores on selection tests but left the profession early. This shows that current working conditions, such as rigidity in career progression and lack of autonomy, drive away the most capable professionals. Instead of attracting new entrants, the system should recover these former teachers, who demonstrate that there are available skills that are not being utilized due to an unattractive work environment. The relevance of this article to open data lies in how the study used public information from the Ministry of Education to identify invisible structural gaps. By applying clear dropout criteria to historical datasets, it was possible to quantify the impact of past policies and generate concrete evidence for public debate. This demonstrates that data transparency is essential for transforming political narratives into evidence-based solutions, enabling the design of specific retention policies and measuring their long-term effectiveness.
