The National Institute of Statistics and Censuses has introduced the "Childcare Basket," a pioneering metric that quantifies the economic cost of raising children from infancy through adolescence. This tool reveals that childcare expenses often consume nearly half of a family’s basic basket, significantly outpacing general inflation. By assigning monetary value to care tasks, the report highlights the substantial financial burden on households and exposes the economic weight of unpaid domestic labor. A primary focus of this initiative is addressing the gender gap, as care responsibilities disproportionately affect women. By calculating the theoretical hours of care required for each age group and valuing them based on professional caregiving wages, the index makes visible the hidden costs of social reproduction. This approach shifts the narrative from invisible domestic duties to measurable economic contributions, aiming to inform policy and recognize the critical role of caregiving in society. The relevance to open data lies in the democratization of social indicators. By publishing transparent, standardized methodologies alongside the data, the government enables researchers, civil society, and developers to analyze inequality and economic stress with greater precision. This openness fosters accountability and allows for the creation of new tools that can better support families and advocate for policies that reduce the gendered economic disparities inherent in traditional cost-of-living metrics.
