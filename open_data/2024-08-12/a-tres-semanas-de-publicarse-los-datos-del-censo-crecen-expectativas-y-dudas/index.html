A tres semanas de publicarse los datos del Censo, crecen expectativas y dudas

The article highlights the critical intersection between the upcoming release of Bolivia’s 2024 Census data and the nation’s political stability, particularly regarding the redistribution of parliamentary seats. The main conclusion is that while the National Statistics Institute (INE) has committed to providing preliminary demographic results by late August, the political handling of this data has sparked significant controversy. The transparency and granularity of the census data are essential not just for statistical accuracy, but for establishing equitable political representation based on current population distributions. Relevance to open data is profound, as experts emphasize that disaggregated, accessible data is necessary to validate the legal allocation of seats and to address rapid urbanization trends. Without open, detailed datasets that allow for comparison with historical records and granular administrative codes, the risk of political manipulation increases. The demand for clear, comparable data underscores how open information serves as a check against arbitrary political decisions, ensuring that territorial organization reflects actual demographic realities rather than political expediency. The proposal to hold a referendum on increasing the number of parliamentary seats illustrates the tension between technical governance and political maneuvering. Analysts argue that seat distribution should be determined strictly by census results and existing laws, not by populist consultations. This situation demonstrates the vital role of open data in preventing the distortion of democratic processes, proving that reliable, transparent statistics are foundational for legitimate resource allocation and conflict prevention in a society undergoing rapid structural change.
