Between 2010 and 2023, a significant proportion of prison escapes occurred in open sections, highlighting vulnerabilities in less secure detention environments. While escape attempts inside prisons were frequent, the majority of successful evasions took place outside traditional prison walls, particularly during hospital visits, transfers, or court appearances. This pattern reveals that risk is not confined to correctional facilities but extends to external interactions where oversight is fragmented and security protocols may be insufficient. The high number of escape attempts underscores systemic challenges in managing inmate custody across various judicial and medical contexts. Security associations have responded by demanding updated protocols, improved surveillance infrastructure, and dedicated secure zones in hospitals and courthouses. These measures aim to mitigate risks during transportation and external movements, addressing gaps that current outdated guidelines and resource limitations leave open. This data is relevant to open data because it stems from transparency laws, demonstrating how public access to government statistics drives accountability and policy reform. By making institutional failures visible, open data empowers civil society to advocate for necessary security improvements. It illustrates the critical role of transparent, accessible datasets in identifying trends, evaluating institutional performance, and fostering evidence-based discussions on public safety and administrative effectiveness.
