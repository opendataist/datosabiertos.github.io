Las carreras mejor pagadas no son las de mayor demanda

The article reveals a significant gap between students’ academic preferences and the actual demands of the labor market in Mexico. While young people prioritize traditionally prestigious fields such as Law and Accounting, the market requires profiles in engineering, economics, and education. This disconnect poses considerable economic risk, as graduates from saturated or technically declining fields face fewer employment opportunities and stagnant wages, demonstrating that social prestige does not always translate into financial stability or professional relevance. The relevance of this text to the field of open data lies in its reliance on official data, such as that from the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI), to validate claims about income and employment. Open data democratize information, enabling institutions and citizens to compare educational offerings with real market demand. Without transparent access to these macroeconomic and labor indicators, career guidance is based on misconceptions or historical biases, perpetuating inefficiencies in human resource allocation and hindering informed decision-making for human capital development. It is concluded that there is an urgent need to reform career guidance systems through the objective use of available data. The automation of traditional roles in law and accounting amplifies the need to align with emerging fields such as data science and software development. Transparency in employability and salary statistics is essential to correct this mismatch, ensuring that university education responds to current and future economic needs rather than remaining anchored to outdated social status markers.
