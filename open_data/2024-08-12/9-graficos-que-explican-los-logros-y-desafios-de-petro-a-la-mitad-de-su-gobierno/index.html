9 gráficos que explican los logros y desafíos de Petro a la mitad de su Gobierno

The administration of President Gustavo Petro confronts a complex legacy marked by contrasting socioeconomic outcomes and enduring security challenges. While his government has achieved historic reductions in deforestation and significantly lowered extreme poverty rates, these gains are overshadowed by record-high coca cultivation and stalled peace negotiations with remaining guerrilla groups. Public approval remains deeply polarized, reflecting a population divided between appreciation for social welfare improvements and frustration over the lack of comprehensive security strategies and political stagnation. A critical tension exists between the administration’s social achievements and its economic performance. Although millions have escaped poverty, the broader economy has stagnated, and fiscal deficits are growing. The government’s attempt to stimulate growth through targeted sector investments conflicts with monetary policy constraints, as central bank independence limits efforts to aggressively lower interest rates despite inflationary pressures. This dissonance highlights the difficulty of balancing progressive social agendas with macroeconomic stability in a fragile post-conflict context. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how diverse, high-quality datasets—ranging from satellite imagery on forest cover and illicit crops to national census figures on poverty and crime—are essential for objective governance evaluation. It underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible data to verify policy impacts, hold leaders accountable, and inform public debate on complex issues like migration and peace processes, rather than relying solely on political narratives.
