The core revelation is that Elon Musk actively sought absolute control over OpenAI, including majority equity and CEO status, prior to his departure. This directly contradicts his current legal claims that the organization betrayed its non-profit mission by pursuing a for-profit structure. The evidence demonstrates that the leadership team refused Musk’s terms precisely because granting one individual such power would violate the foundational ethical guidelines required for safe and beneficial AI development, highlighting a fundamental misalignment in vision between Musk and the original founders. Furthermore, the release of historical correspondence clarifies that Musk understood the mission did not mandate open-sourcing artificial general intelligence. Internal discussions confirm that both parties accepted the necessity of restricting access to advanced AI models to ensure responsible deployment. This undermines the assertion that the company acted deceptively by partnering with Microsoft, showing that the shift toward a closed-source model was a deliberate, agreed-upon strategic decision rather than a clandestine betrayal of early promises to the public. This incident is highly relevant to open data discourse because it illustrates the tension between the ideal of universal information access and the practical requirements of managing transformative technology. It underscores that "open" does not always mean "fully available," as security and governance concerns can necessitate restricted data flows. Understanding these historical negotiations provides context for current debates regarding transparency, corporate responsibility, and the ethical boundaries of data sharing in the rapid evolution of artificial intelligence.
