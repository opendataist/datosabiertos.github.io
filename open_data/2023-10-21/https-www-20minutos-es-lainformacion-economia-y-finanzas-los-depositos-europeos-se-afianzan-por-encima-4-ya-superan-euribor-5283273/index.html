Bank deposits are no longer frozen and have begun to exhibit notable volatility, driven by the European Central Bank’s restrictive monetary policy. This trend reflects a significant recovery in conservative household savings among Spanish families, although the total volume remains well below the levels recorded a decade ago. The main implication is that customers now face a dynamic market where yields are frequently adjusted, requiring greater attention and flexibility in managing their liquid assets. Competition among financial institutions is pushing interest rates higher, with offers exceeding the Euribor and competing with higher-yielding European products. However, structural differences persist between local and European banks, with the latter typically offering better returns, albeit often accompanied by restrictions such as strict fixed-term commitments and penalties for early withdrawal. This distinction underscores the current dilemma between seeking maximum profitability and maintaining liquidity, revealing that higher benefits come with reduced operational flexibility for savers. This article is relevant to the field of open data because it exemplifies the need for transparency and access to comparable financial information for citizens. Users’ ability to make informed decisions depends directly on the availability of accurate, up-to-date, and standardized data on financial products. By highlighting rapid variations and cross-border differences, it demonstrates how open data can empower users by facilitating objective comparisons of costs and benefits, thereby promoting a fairer and more efficient market.
