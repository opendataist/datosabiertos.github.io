Refrenda presidenta del INAI compromiso para mantener al organismo de pie

The article underscores the critical role of the INAI in safeguarding citizens' rights to access public information and protect personal data. It highlights a severe institutional crisis, as the body currently lacks the legal quorum to function or adjudicate complaints. This paralysis threatens the fundamental democratic principle that transparency is a universal right, not an elitist privilege, leaving society without the necessary tool to hold the government accountable or defend its privacy against violations. The relevance to open data lies in the urgent need for strengthened governmental transparency and the openness of public datasets. The narrative suggests that the current institutional instability directly impedes the operation of essential mechanisms for data access, such as the National Transparency Platform. Without a functional INAI, the capacity to review and enforce transparency obligations is compromised, which can potentially exacerbate corruption and weaken the democratic fabric by limiting citizen oversight of government actions. Furthermore, the text emphasizes that a fully operational INAI is vital for addressing complex societal issues, from digital divides to security concerns, through collaboration with civil society. The consensus among leaders is that restoring the institute’s functionality is not merely an administrative matter but a prerequisite for maintaining an open government. The failure to appoint new commissioners represents a significant challenge to the country’s democratic health, as it undermines the infrastructure required for a transparent, accountable, and participatory democracy.
