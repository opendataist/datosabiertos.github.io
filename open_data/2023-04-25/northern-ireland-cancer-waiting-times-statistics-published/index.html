Northern Ireland cancer waiting times statistics published

The report reveals a stark disparity in cancer service performance within Northern Ireland, highlighting significant challenges in meeting statutory waiting time targets for urgent care. While the initial treatment timeline after a diagnosis remains largely compliant with standards, the system struggles severely with urgent referrals from general practitioners, where only a small fraction of patients receive first definitive treatment within the required 62 days. This bottleneck suggests systemic delays in the early diagnostic pathway, contrasting sharply with the more efficient processing of patients already enrolled in treatment programs. Regional performance varies considerably, with the Western Health and Social Care Trust demonstrating notably higher compliance rates compared to the broader Northern Ireland average. This trust consistently exceeds national benchmarks for both initial treatment timelines and urgent breast cancer assessments, indicating that localized management strategies or resource allocations can significantly improve patient access and timeliness. The data underscores that while some institutions excel, the overall regional performance masks underlying inefficiencies that disproportionately affect certain patient groups and specialties. This article is pertinent to open data advocates because it exemplifies the critical need for granular, accessible health data to identify systemic inequities and hold public services accountable. By publishing detailed metrics at both the regional and trust levels, stakeholders can scrutinize performance gaps that aggregate national statistics might obscure. Transparent reporting of these specific waiting times empowers researchers, policymakers, and the public to demand targeted interventions, ensuring that data-driven insights translate into tangible improvements in healthcare delivery and patient outcomes.
