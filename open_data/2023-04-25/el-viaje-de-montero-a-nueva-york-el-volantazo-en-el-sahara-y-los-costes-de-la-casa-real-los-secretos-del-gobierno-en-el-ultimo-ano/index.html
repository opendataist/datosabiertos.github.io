El viaje de Montero a Nueva York, el volantazo en el Sáhara y los costes de la Casa Real: los secretos del Gobierno en el último año

The article reveals how the Spanish government uses the 2013 Transparency Law to withhold significant public information, citing national security, defense, and foreign relations as primary justifications for secrecy. Rather than fostering openness, the data indicates that state secrecy is frequently applied to restrict access to financial details regarding high-level political activities and sensitive diplomatic maneuvers, suggesting a systemic preference for opacity over accountability in matters involving senior officials and international relations. Key examples include the refusal to disclose the specific costs of security and operational logistics for the Minister of Equality’s trip to New York and the Prime Minister’s travels, as well as the complete denial of copies of diplomatic letters concerning the recognition of Moroccan sovereignty over Western Sahara. Additionally, the Ministry of the Interior blocked information regarding security costs for the Royal Family and sensitive border incidents, while the Ministry of Defense withheld details on military aid to Ukraine and the status of Ukrainian refugees. These patterns highlight how broad legal exceptions are used to shield politically sensitive expenditures and diplomatic strategies from public scrutiny. This case is critically relevant to open data advocates as it demonstrates the practical limitations of transparency legislation when political convenience overrides public interest. It underscores the necessity for stricter definitions of what constitutes genuine security risks versus administrative secrecy. For the open data movement, these findings illustrate the urgent need for robust oversight mechanisms and clearer legal standards to prevent the misuse of exemption clauses, ensuring that access to public information remains meaningful and that government accountability is not eroded by vague interpretations of national security.
