Para el Gobierno la inflación seguirá bajando: podría reducir el ritmo de devaluación del dólar

The article highlights the slowdown in inflation in Argentina, which the Milei-Caputo government interprets as a triumph of its economic policy. The reduction in the rate of price increases, now below the 2.9% threshold, allows authorities to project a more gradual adjustment in the planned devaluation of the dollar. This scenario is seen as a sign of confidence in the effectiveness of the stabilization plan, marking a turning point in the trajectory of prices toward historically low levels. The observed convergence between the parallel and official exchange rates suggests that the foreign exchange controls (cepo cambiario) could be definitively lifted in 2025. Authorities believe that once inflation is under control, maintaining the pre-established devaluation schedule is no longer functional and could generate inefficiencies. Therefore, the strategic objective shifts toward free currency competition, encouraging the use of the dollar for everyday transactions, except for tax payments, which would imply a structural liberalization of the foreign exchange market. This news is relevant to open data because the success of these policy measures critically depends on the transparency and accessibility of public statistics. The credibility of INDEC’s report not only validates the government’s narrative but also enables analysts, markets, and citizens to objectively assess the country’s economic health. Moreover, the promise to liberalize the foreign exchange market requires reliable and publicly available indicators to ensure legal certainty and the proper allocation of resources in a multi-currency environment.
