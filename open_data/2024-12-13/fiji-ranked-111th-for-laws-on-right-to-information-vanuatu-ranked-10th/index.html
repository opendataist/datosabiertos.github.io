Fiji ranked 111th for laws on right to information, Vanuatu ranked 10th

Fiji ranked 111th for laws on right to information, Vanuatu ranked 10th

Fiji’s low global ranking for right-to-information laws highlights significant gaps between constitutional guarantees and actual transparency practices. This poor standing underscores the urgent need for systemic reform, as the existing Information Act remains unimplemented due to the failure to appoint a necessary oversight commission. Without operationalizing these frameworks, the nation struggles to fulfill its democratic obligations and ensure genuine accountability. The article emphasizes the critical role of institutional commitment and cultural shift within the civil service. Transitioning from a mindset of secrecy to one of openness is essential for effective freedom of information. By appointing oversight bodies and actively engaging with civil society, the government aims to align public service values with legal requirements, thereby fostering greater trust and responsiveness toward citizens. This case is highly relevant to open_data because robust legal frameworks are the foundation for public access to government information. Without enforceable rights and transparent institutions, data remains inaccessible or withheld. Strengthening these legal structures enables better data governance, ensuring that citizens can hold authorities accountable through informed scrutiny of public records and decisions.
