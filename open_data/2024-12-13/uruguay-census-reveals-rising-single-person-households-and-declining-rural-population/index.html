Uruguay census reveals rising single-person households and declining rural population

Uruguay census reveals rising single-person households and declining rural population

Uruguay’s 2023 census reveals a society undergoing profound demographic transformation. The nation faces negative natural growth as deaths surpass births, driven by a rapidly aging population and significantly lower birth rates. This shift highlights an urgent need for policy adjustments to support elderly citizens and address the resulting economic and social challenges. Concurrently, urbanization continues to reshape the country’s structure. Rural populations are shrinking, while cities like Canelones and Maldonado grow, leaving Montevideo and other regions in decline. This rural-to-urban migration creates uneven development pressures, requiring targeted infrastructure and service planning in expanding urban centers versus contracting rural areas. Finally, household dynamics have fundamentally changed, with single-person homes becoming far more common than large families. These trends are critical for open data, as they demonstrate how granular demographic statistics inform equitable public policy. Understanding these shifts allows governments and researchers to better model resource allocation, social services, and long-term societal resilience in a changing landscape.
