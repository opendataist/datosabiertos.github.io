The 2023 Uruguayan Census reveals that pets are deeply embedded in the nation’s social fabric, with a vast majority of households keeping dogs or cats. This widespread prevalence highlights a significant cultural shift in which animals are viewed as integral family members rather than mere commodities. The data illustrate a strong societal commitment to animal companionship, reflecting broader trends in urban and rural life that shape how these relationships are maintained across different regions. Geographic disparities further enrich this narrative, showing that while the interior of the country favors dogs due to available space, the capital city sees higher cat ownership. These regional nuances provide critical context for understanding lifestyle patterns and housing dynamics. Recognizing these differences is essential for developing targeted public policies and community initiatives that respect local customs while promoting responsible pet ownership throughout the country’s diverse landscape. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how national statistical institutes can aggregate granular demographic information to drive social awareness and policy-making. By making such detailed census data publicly accessible, citizens and policymakers can better address public health challenges, such as zoonotic disease control and animal overpopulation. Transparent data empowers communities to advocate for welfare improvements and ensures that government interventions are grounded in accurate, representative evidence of societal needs.
