NatureServe Launches Conservation Data Portal for the Western U.S. with Enhanced Map of Biodiversity Importance

NatureServe has launched an enhanced Conservation Data Portal for the Western U.S., centered on an upgraded Map of Biodiversity Importance (MoBI). This update significantly improves spatial resolution and incorporates new data on at-risk species, including solitary bees, while highlighting critical hotspots like the Rio Grande and desert southwest. The portal serves as a strategic resource for prioritizing conservation efforts, integrating with federal agencies and corporate evaluations to guide science-based decisions. The initiative demonstrates the power of combining decades of localized field data with advanced machine learning and predictive modeling. By collaborating with state heritage programs and technology partners like Esri, NatureServe ensures that conservation planners can access precise, up-to-date information on ecosystem vulnerability and protection status. This synergy between scientific rigor and technological innovation allows for more effective targeting of resources to protect biodiversity amidst increasing environmental threats. This release is highly relevant to open data because it provides publicly accessible, standardized biodiversity datasets through open portals and platforms like Esri’s Living Atlas. By removing barriers to critical conservation information, the project empowers a wide range of stakeholders—from researchers to policymakers—to utilize high-quality data for decision-making. This transparency fosters collaborative, evidence-based approaches to environmental stewardship and supports global goals such as conserving significant portions of natural habitats.
