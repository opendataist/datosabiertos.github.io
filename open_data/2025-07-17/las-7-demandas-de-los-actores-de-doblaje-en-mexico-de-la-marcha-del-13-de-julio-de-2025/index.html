Las 7 demandas de los actores de doblaje en México de la marcha del 13 de julio de 2025

Las 7 demandas de los actores de doblaje en México de la marcha del 13 de julio de 2025

Mexican voice actors and audio professionals recently protested against the unregulated use of artificial intelligence, highlighting significant threats to their livelihoods and artistic integrity. The demonstration emphasized that current technological practices allow for the cloning and commercialization of voices without consent, credit, or fair compensation, thereby undermining copyright protections and risking the preservation of artists' legacies. A primary conclusion is the urgent need for robust legal frameworks that classify voice data as sensitive biometric information. Stakeholders demanded explicit consent mechanisms and transparent contracts for AI training, ensuring that human talent remains central to localization industries while safeguarding the economic rights of both living and deceased artists. This event is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the ethical implications of harvesting biometric datasets for AI development. It calls for transparency in data sourcing and ownership, reinforcing that open data initiatives must respect human rights and intellectual property to prevent exploitation and ensure equitable benefits for creators whose data fuels technological progress.
