Carlos Adrianzén sobre el INEI: 1.6 millones de limeños no cubren media canasta

The article highlights severe labor precarity in Lima, where millions work without earning enough to meet basic family needs, a situation that has worsened since 2019. This stagnation is driven by low productivity, minimal private investment, and structural economic flaws, revealing that recent apparent recoveries are partial and insufficient to improve citizens' real incomes. The lack of reforms in public services, particularly education, and paralyzed state institutions further hinder meaningful economic progress. Political instability and ineffective governance exacerbate this crisis by deterring investors and disrupting public order. Without security and the rule of law, formal employment cannot expand, trapping workers in low-wage conditions despite controlled inflation. The analysis emphasizes that superficial growth rates fail to address these root causes, leaving the majority of the population in economic stress while the state remains corrupt and inefficient. This report is relevant to open data because it utilizes official statistics from the National Institute of Statistics and Informatics to expose hidden socioeconomic realities. It demonstrates how accessible, transparent data is crucial for validating expert analyses of structural crises. By scrutinizing these metrics, society can demand accountability and effective policies, proving that open data is not merely a technical resource but a vital tool for democratic oversight and economic justice.
