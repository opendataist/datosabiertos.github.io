¿Vulneración de datos?: El intenso debate legal abierto por "certificado laboral" ofrecido por startup

¿Vulneración de datos?: El intenso debate legal abierto por "certificado laboral" ofrecido por startup

The controversy surrounding Cualiffy highlights critical tensions between technological innovation and data protection in the labor market. While the startup aims to verify employment history through a centralized platform to increase transparency in hiring, users and legal experts argue that the mechanism violates privacy rights. The core issue lies in the method of data aggregation, where experts contend that obtaining access to sensitive personal information via a user’s Clave Única exceeds the necessary scope, effectively turning a consented request into unauthorized surveillance of private life. From an open data perspective, this case underscores the importance of explicit, informed consent and the principle of data minimization. Critics emphasize that aggregating disparate official records—ranging from tax data to judicial histories—without clear, specific authorization for each data source creates significant legal and ethical risks. This incident serves as a cautionary tale for developers and organizations handling public or semi-public data, demonstrating how convenience cannot override the fundamental requirements for user autonomy and the secure handling of personal information in digital ecosystems. Ultimately, the debate reveals that open data initiatives must prioritize user trust and legal compliance over efficiency. The startup’s defense, which claims the system only facilitates user-initiated access without storing sensitive keys, contrasts sharply with the perception of identity substitution raised by users. This conflict illustrates that for open data technologies to be sustainable, they must align strictly with privacy legislation and human rights standards, ensuring that transparency does not come at the cost of individual dignity or security.
