¿Qué pasará con el fallo sobre inteligencia artificial de la Suprema Corte?

¿Qué pasará con el fallo sobre inteligencia artificial de la Suprema Corte?

The Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation reactivated the debate on intellectual property in the digital age by initially withdrawing a proposal that classified works generated exclusively by artificial intelligence as public domain. Although the court clarified that this statement would not be part of the final ruling, the controversy underscores the urgency of establishing clear criteria to distinguish between human creativity and automation, thereby protecting authors without hindering technological development. The current legal uncertainty poses significant risks for creators and developers, as the lack of automatic protection could facilitate the free use of artificial content, blurring the line between hybrid works and purely automated ones. Experts warn that an imprecise judicial precedent could invalidate rights over combined content, forcing parties to seek alternative forms of protection, such as trademark registration, to safeguard intellectual property in an environment increasingly dominated by generative tools. This case is relevant to the open data movement because it illustrates the tensions between the freedom of access to information and the need to regulate authorship in the digital economy. Transparency in judicial processes and regulatory clarity are essential to define what knowledge can be freely used and what requires protection, thus balancing the interests of the technology sector with the rights of creators within a framework of open and accessible data.
