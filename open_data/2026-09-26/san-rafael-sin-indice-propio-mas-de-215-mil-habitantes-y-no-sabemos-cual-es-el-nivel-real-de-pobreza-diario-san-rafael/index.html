San Rafael, sin índice propio: más de 215 mil habitantes y no sabemos cuál es el nivel real de pobreza - Diario San Rafael

The article highlights a critical data gap in Argentine social statistics, where official poverty rates are calculated exclusively for 31 major urban agglomerates, thereby excluding significant populations in other regions. In San Rafael, a department with over 215,000 inhabitants, there is no specific official measurement of poverty, leaving its economic reality in a statistical gray area despite projections indicating a substantial population. This lack of localized data is problematic because San Rafael’s economy and social structure differ markedly from those of the provincial capital, Gran Mendoza. Extrapolating poverty rates from the capital to this distant, agriculture- and tourism-driven region is inaccurate. As a result, policymakers cannot determine the precise scale of indigence, monitor local trends, or identify the most affected sectors, making it difficult to assess whether social conditions are improving or deteriorating. This case underscores a major challenge in open data initiatives: the need for granular, localized datasets to enable effective governance. Accurate, region-specific information is essential for designing targeted public policies, allocating budgets efficiently, and evaluating the impact of social programs. Without expanding statistical methodologies to cover all territories, significant portions of the population remain invisible to decision-makers, hindering evidence-based policymaking and equitable resource distribution.
