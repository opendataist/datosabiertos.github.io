Judge dismisses President Donald Trump’s 2024 Iowa Poll lawsuit

An Iowa court dismissed President Trump’s lawsuit against the Des Moines Register and pollster J. Ann Selzer, ruling that publishing pre-election polling data constitutes protected speech. The judge determined that treating accurate polling results as election interference would unlawfully stretch consumer fraud laws and violate First Amendment protections. This decision prevents future litigation on these grounds and affirms that statistical surveys are social science, not deliberate political manipulation or fraud. The ruling highlights the legal boundary between reported data and actionable misconduct, rejecting claims that unfavorable poll results constitute "fake news" or illegal interference. It underscores the principle that pollsters cannot be held liable for results that differ from final election outcomes, as polling inherently reflects a snapshot in time rather than a guaranteed prediction. This precedent protects the fundamental freedom to pursue and publish data without fear of political reprisal. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates because it safeguards the transparency and accessibility of electoral information. By preventing the criminalization of public polling through litigation, the ruling ensures that citizens and media can access unbiased statistical insights without censorship or legal threats from powerful figures. It reinforces the importance of maintaining an open informational ecosystem where data integrity and free speech remain immune to political pressure.
