Tesla Claims Its Full Self Driving (FSD) Saves Lives, But Wants You To Pay $99/Month To Get It - CleanTechnica

The article explores the historical evolution of automotive safety, contrasting the widely accepted, life-saving mandate of seatbelts with the current controversial status of autonomous driving systems. It highlights how innovations often shift from novelty to moral obligation, using the universal adoption of seatbelts as a benchmark for how society should eventually treat fully autonomous vehicles. This progression suggests that as technology proves its superiority over human error, regulatory and ethical frameworks may soon require widespread implementation rather than optional subscription services. Central to the discussion is the tension between Tesla’s proprietary Full Self-Driving (FSD) technology and the need for transparent, open validation. While the author argues that FSD could significantly reduce traffic fatalities by eliminating human error, the lack of public data on accident rates and the reliance on a paid subscription model raise ethical concerns. The narrative questions whether it is morally defensible to profit from a technology that has the potential to save lives, drawing parallels to Nils Bohlin’s decision to make the three-point seatbelt patent open-source for the greater good. This debate is crucial for the open_data movement because the credibility of autonomous safety claims depends entirely on transparent, publicly available data. Without open access to comprehensive performance metrics and crash statistics, regulators and the public cannot accurately assess the true safety impact of these systems. The article implies that for autonomous driving to become a universally adopted standard akin to seatbelts, manufacturers must embrace data openness, allowing for independent verification and collective improvement of safety algorithms.
