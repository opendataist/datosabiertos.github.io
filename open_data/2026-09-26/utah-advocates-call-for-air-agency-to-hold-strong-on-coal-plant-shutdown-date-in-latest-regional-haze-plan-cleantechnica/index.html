Utah Advocates Call for Air Agency to Hold Strong on Coal Plant Shutdown Date in Latest Regional Haze Plan - CleanTechnica

Utah Advocates Call for Air Agency to Hold Strong on Coal Plant Shutdown Date in Latest Regional Haze Plan - CleanTechnica

The Utah Division of Air Quality is proposing a revision to its regional haze plan that removes mandatory shutdown dates for coal units at the Intermountain Power Plant, effectively prolonging operations of one of the nation’s worst pollution sources. This move contradicts previous EPA disapprovals and ignores original closure timelines, despite the plant no longer having primary power contracts. Instead, the state appears poised to keep the facility running, potentially driven by interest from data centers and political efforts to maintain energy supply, rather than public health or environmental obligations. This regulatory rollback exacerbates haze pollution that severely degrades air quality and scenic visibility in Utah’s national parks and distant destinations like Yosemite. Advocates argue that allowing an aging, uncontrolled coal facility to operate undermines the Regional Haze Rule’s intent to restore natural visibility and protect ecosystem health. The continued emissions threaten not only the visitor experience and ecological integrity of these treasured public lands but also the respiratory health of nearby communities and park staff, representing a significant failure in environmental stewardship. This case highlights the critical importance of open data in holding government agencies accountable for environmental compliance. Transparent access to regulatory documents, legislative records, and emissions data allows advocates and citizens to identify discrepancies between state actions and federal mandates. By making such information accessible, open data empowers the public to challenge decisions that prioritize short-term economic interests over long-term public health and environmental preservation, ensuring that policy revisions are subject to rigorous scrutiny and democratic oversight.
