La pobreza en Argentina sube al 32,3% y corta dos años de descenso

Argentina’s poverty rates have risen again, interrupting a previous downward trend and highlighting the fragility of economic recovery. This reversal is primarily driven by high inflation and a sluggish labor market, which erode household purchasing power despite recent decelerations in price growth. The data underscores that measuring poverty through income alone makes the indicator highly volatile and sensitive to inflationary pressures, rather than reflecting solely structural improvements in welfare. The implications for open data are significant, as this case illustrates the critical need for transparent, accessible, and consistently calculated statistical indicators. Reliable open data allows for independent verification of government claims and facilitates accurate analysis of socioeconomic disparities across different demographics and regions. Without clear, openly available methodologies, stakeholders cannot fully assess the true impact of economic policies or identify vulnerable populations effectively. Ultimately, this report serves as a reminder that open data is not just about publishing numbers, but about enabling accountability and informed public debate. It empowers citizens and researchers to challenge official narratives, understand the real drivers behind statistical changes, and demand policies grounded in empirical evidence. Ensuring data accessibility is essential for democratic oversight and for holding institutions accountable to the most affected communities.
