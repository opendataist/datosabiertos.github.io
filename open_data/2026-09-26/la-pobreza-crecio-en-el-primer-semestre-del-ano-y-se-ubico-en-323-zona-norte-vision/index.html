La pobreza creció en el primer semestre del año y se ubicó en 32,3% | Zona Norte Visión

The article reveals that official census data shows a reversal in the previous downward trend of poverty and extreme poverty. This resurgence is primarily attributed to sharp increases in the costs of essential goods, such as meat and fuel, alongside real hikes in utility tariffs. These economic pressures disproportionately impact lower-income households, pushing a significant number of people below the poverty line and eroding previous gains in economic stability. The implications highlight how inflation and currency depreciation severely diminish the purchasing power of salaries, particularly affecting children and young adults. Despite earlier declines, the latest figures indicate a renewed struggle for families to cover basic food and total basket costs. This data underscores the vulnerability of specific demographic and regional groups, revealing that economic recovery remains fragile and uneven across different sectors of society. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical importance of transparent, timely, and accessible statistical information from national institutes. Accurate census data allows economists, researchers, and policymakers to track real-time economic impacts and validate independent projections. Without open access to such rigorous metrics, public debate lacks empirical grounding, hindering effective responses to social welfare challenges and reducing accountability in government economic management.
