Centre likely to commence population census in 2025 after four-year delay

Centre likely to commence population census in 2025 after four-year delay

India plans to restart its decennial census and update the National Population Register in early 2025, with results expected by 2026. This delay has shifted the future census cycle, moving the next count to 2035 and altering the traditional decade-long schedule. Crucially, the government has not yet decided whether to include a caste census, despite political demands for data on the Other Backward Classes (OBCs). This uncertainty highlights the tension between administrative timelines and socio-political pressures for granular demographic breakdowns. The timing of the 2026 data release creates significant legal challenges regarding electoral delimitation. Constitutional provisions currently freeze seat allocations based on the 1971 census until data from the first post-2026 census is published. Consequently, even if the new census concludes in 2025, using this data to redraw parliamentary constituencies immediately would require amending Article 82 of the Constitution. This regulatory hurdle underscores the complex intersection of demographic data and political representation. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it reveals how government data collection cycles directly impact civic rights and political equity. The potential exclusion of caste data limits the granularity of open datasets, affecting research on social equity. Furthermore, the impending debate over delimitation demonstrates how population statistics are not merely administrative records but powerful tools that determine resource distribution and political voice, emphasizing the need for transparent, accessible, and comprehensive public data.
