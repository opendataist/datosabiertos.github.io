SFP da carpetazo a 92% de las denuncias contra superdelegados

SFP da carpetazo a 92% de las denuncias contra superdelegados

The article reveals that, during López Obrador’s administration, the Secretariat of Public Function archived the vast majority of investigations into irregularities committed by super-delegates. Despite hundreds of complaints accumulating—alleging nepotism, abuse of authority, misuse of resources for electoral purposes, and conflicts of interest—the disciplinary body determined that there was insufficient evidence to substantiate administrative offenses. This conclusion raises a fundamental critique regarding the effectiveness of the accountability system and the perceived impunity in recent public management. The complaints, driven by both citizen denunciations and disgruntled public servants, highlighted systematic practices such as conditioning access to social programs and instrumentalizing the government structure for partisan political benefit. The analysis details specific cases in several states where investigations were dismissed, even when internal testimonies emerged regarding coercion and misuse of resources. This narrative exposes a disconnect between serious allegations and official conclusions, suggesting a tendency to downplay grave irregularities as mere labor or subjective matters. The relevance to the field of open data lies in the utility of the Transparency Law in making visible what institutions seek to keep hidden. Access to official databases enabled the reconstruction of a reality contrary to the official narrative, demonstrating how the release of public information is crucial for auditing power. Without access to these records, complaints would remain anonymous and unaddressed, underscoring that transparency is the only effective tool for demanding accountability and combating structural corruption.
