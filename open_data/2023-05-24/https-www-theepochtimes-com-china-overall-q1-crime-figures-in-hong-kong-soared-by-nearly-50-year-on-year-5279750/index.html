The article highlights a significant resurgence in Hong Kong’s crime rates in early 2023, driven primarily by a surge in deception and theft. While police attribute this partly to the normalization of social activities after pandemic restrictions, historical data reveals that crime levels have not merely returned to pre-pandemic norms but have actually exceeded those historical baselines. This discrepancy suggests that factors beyond simple post-lockout rebound, such as economic stagnation, are contributing to the deteriorating public safety landscape. Of particular concern is the dramatic rise in online and phone-based scams, which account for the majority of the increase. Experts and commentators argue that the government’s focus on the "return to normal" narrative minimizes the severity of these specific crimes. There are growing calls for greater transparency regarding the types and origins of these scams, raising questions about whether changing social or political atmospheres are making residents more susceptible to fraudulent activities. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the critical need for granular, disaggregated statistics. The police’s high-level figures obscure important details, such as the specific modalities of scams or demographic breakdowns of offenders. To understand true public safety trends and policy effectiveness, stakeholders require detailed, accessible data that allows for deeper analysis rather than relying on aggregated metrics that may mask underlying issues.
