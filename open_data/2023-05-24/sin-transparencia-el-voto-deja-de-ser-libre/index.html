Sin transparencia, el voto deja de ser libre

The president of the INAI argues that genuine democracy requires autonomous bodies to guarantee transparency, ensuring that voters have access to the information needed to make free and authentic choices. She opposes absorbing the INAI into other branches of government, emphasizing that independence prevents authorities from judging their own actions and upholds the rule of law through accountability. This perspective highlights that substantive democracy extends beyond elections, relying on informational freedoms and transparent decision-making processes. It asserts that transparency is an inalienable component of democracy, necessary to verify that governance adheres to legal standards rather than arbitrary power. This article is relevant to open data as it frames data access not merely as a technical procedure, but as a fundamental democratic right. It underscores the critical role of independent institutions in protecting open data infrastructure, ensuring that information transparency serves as a check on power and enables informed citizen participation in governance.
