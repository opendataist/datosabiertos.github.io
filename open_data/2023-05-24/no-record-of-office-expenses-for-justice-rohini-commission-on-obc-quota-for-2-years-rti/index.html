No record of office expenses for Justice Rohini Commission on OBC quota for 2 years: RTI

No record of office expenses for Justice Rohini Commission on OBC quota for 2 years: RTI

The Justice G. Rohini Commission, tasked with sub-categorizing Other Backward Classes to ensure equitable reservation benefits, has operated for years with significant financial opacity. Recent information requests reveal that the government lacks records of office expenditures incurred since September 2021. This gap in financial accountability raises serious concerns about transparency and administrative oversight, as the ministry claims no data exists for a substantial period, hindering public scrutiny of how public funds are managed during prolonged extensions. Despite multiple delays and a history of incomplete documentation, the Commission has reportedly completed its substantive analysis, identifying a few dominant groups that monopolize quota benefits while marginalizing numerous smaller communities. The proposed solution involves dividing OBCs into four distinct categories, ensuring that historically excluded groups receive a larger share of reservations. This structural reform aims to correct systemic inequities, moving beyond the current one-size-fits-all approach to address the specific socio-economic realities of diverse caste groups. This development is critically relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the tension between necessary policy reforms and the absence of accessible, verified administrative records. The lack of expenditure logs undermines the integrity of the process, demonstrating that even when technical work is finished, the failure to maintain and publish financial data erodes public trust. Sustainable open government requires not just policy decisions, but also the proactive release of accurate, granular data on costs and progress to ensure accountability and facilitate independent verification of governmental actions.
