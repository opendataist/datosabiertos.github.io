Anxiety among women: INEGI reveals it affects one in four in Mexico

Anxiety among women: INEGI reveals it affects one in four in Mexico

The article emphasizes that anxiety among Mexican women is not merely an individual health issue, but a direct consequence of deep-seated structural inequalities. Factors such as the income gap and the disproportionate burden of unpaid domestic work create conditions of vulnerability that significantly affect women’s emotional well-being. This perspective integrates mental health within the framework of social justice, demonstrating how socioeconomic conditions shape public health. The normalization of anxiety symptoms hinders their timely identification, allowing suffering to remain private and without the necessary attention. By mistaking these manifestations for passing concerns, the seriousness of the problem is obscured, perpetuating a cycle of institutional and social neglect. Recognizing these signs as indicators of crisis, rather than as trivial emotional states, is crucial to breaking down stigma and facilitating access to appropriate resources. This content is relevant to open data because it underscores the urgency of generating, sharing, and using gender-disaggregated data on mental health and socioeconomic conditions. Transparency in information makes it possible to reveal the correlations between structural inequality and health indicators, thereby promoting evidence-based public policies. Moreover, it fosters accountability by showing how the lack of open and accessible data contributes to the invisibility of women’s specific vulnerabilities, which is essential for designing effective and equitable interventions.
