Gasoline tax cut extended until November after diesel prices surge 34%

Gasoline tax cut extended until November after diesel prices surge 34%

The INE report confirms that inflation in Spain reached 4.9% in September, driven primarily by the sustained increase in fuel prices. This rise, stemming from the geopolitical situation, has significantly raised the costs of diesel and gasoline, far exceeding previous sub-rates and contributing to the rebound in both the overall index and underlying inflation, indicating widespread price pressures beyond the energy sector. To mitigate this impact on purchasing power, the government has maintained a tax reduction of 20 cents per liter for November, activating a safeguard mechanism due to the sharp increase in fuel prices. Although this measure aims to protect households and productive sectors, the aid package comes at a high cost to public finances and is pending parliamentary approval, while other energy sources like electricity do not receive the same immediate fiscal support. This article is relevant to the open data sector because it illustrates how time series of consumer price indices and international market prices can be directly correlated with political and economic decisions. The analysis of these open data allows researchers and citizens to understand the transparency in justifying public aid, monitor the effectiveness of anti-inflation measures, and visualize the real impact of global crises on the domestic economy through verifiable indicators.
