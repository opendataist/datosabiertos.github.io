TDs should get back to work to tackle eviction crisis – Tóibín | Westmeath Examiner

Peadar Tóibín argues that the Dáil must be recalled immediately to address the escalating homelessness crisis, criticizing the political class for remaining inactive during a period of severe public distress. He emphasizes that elected officials are disconnected from ordinary citizens who, despite having jobs and savings, face eviction due to a lack of rental properties. This disconnect highlights a failure in governance to respond urgently to housing shortages and the resulting human suffering. The article underscores the critical role of official statistics in driving political accountability and emergency response. By citing Residential Tenancies Board data, Tóibín illustrates how accessible and transparent data serves as a catalyst for demanding legislative action. This aligns with open data principles, where empirical evidence is used to reveal societal issues that might otherwise remain obscured by political inaction or bureaucratic delay. This situation demonstrates how open data empowers civil society and opposition figures to hold governments accountable. When statistical information is published and widely understood, it creates pressure on decision-makers to address urgent national problems. The case shows that open data is not just a technical asset but a vital tool for democratic engagement, forcing transparency and prompting timely interventions in crises like housing emergencies.
