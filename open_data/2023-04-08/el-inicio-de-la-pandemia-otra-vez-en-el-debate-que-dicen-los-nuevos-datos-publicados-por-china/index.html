El inicio de la pandemia otra vez en el debate: ¿qué dicen los nuevos datos publicados por China?

Three years into the pandemic, a peer-reviewed study has confirmed the presence of SARS-CoV-2 genetic material in environmental samples collected from the Huanan market in Wuhan. The findings indicate that the virus was circulating at the site shortly after its initial detection, suggesting that the market served as a significant amplifier for transmission due to high foot traffic. This evidence supports the hypothesis that the market facilitated the spread, potentially involving an animal intermediate host, although the data remain inconclusive regarding the specific animal source. Crucially, the researchers concluded that the origin of the virus cannot be definitively determined from current analyses. While the study rules out certain domestic animals and notes that wild animals were present, it does not prove that any specific creature was infected. Furthermore, the possibility of human-to-animal transmission or introduction via the cold chain remains open. This ambiguity highlights the limitations of retrospective environmental sampling, as the absence of infected animals in tested specimens does not entirely exclude natural spillover events. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical importance of sharing underlying data and biological samples. The international scientific community had long demanded access to these raw datasets to conduct independent verification and broader analysis. The publication represents a step toward transparency, yet it also underscores that without comprehensive, timely, and freely accessible data—including early historical records—understanding complex global health crises remains challenging and subject to ongoing debate.
