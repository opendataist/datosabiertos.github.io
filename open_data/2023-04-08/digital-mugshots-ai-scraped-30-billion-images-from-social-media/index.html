Digital mugshots: AI scraped 30 billion images from social media...

Clearview AI has agreed to restrict the use of its massive facial recognition database to settle allegations regarding the non-consensual scraping of billions of images from social media. This settlement highlights a critical tension in open data ethics: while the company claims its methods are lawful, similar to search engine indexing, privacy advocates argue that harvesting biometric data without consent violates fundamental rights. The resolution underscores the urgent need for transparent data governance and clearer legal boundaries when private entities aggregate publicly available information for surveillance purposes. The technology raises profound concerns about privacy erosion and the creation of a permanent, involuntary biometric record for citizens. Experts warn that such databases enable potential retroactive prosecution, as individuals cannot predict future legal changes or avoid being identified in incidental photos shared by others. This situation demonstrates how open data practices, when applied to biometrics, can bypass individual control and security measures, affecting everyone regardless of their own digital hygiene or intent to share information. This case is highly relevant to open data discussions because it challenges the assumption that publicly accessible data is free for unrestricted commercial and governmental use. It emphasizes that without robust oversight, open data ecosystems can facilitate widespread surveillance and civil liberties violations. The incident calls for stricter regulations on data scraping and mandatory accountability for organizations using open web data for identity tracking, ensuring that openness does not come at the cost of fundamental human privacy and consent.
