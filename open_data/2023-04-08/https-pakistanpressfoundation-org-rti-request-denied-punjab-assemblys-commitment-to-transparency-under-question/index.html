The article highlights a significant tension between legislative transparency and bureaucratic obstruction within the Punjab Assembly. Lawmakers often advocate for open governance, yet the Assembly’s Public Information Officer initially refused to disclose details regarding privilege motions and standing committee questions. This refusal relied on technicalities about application structure, illustrating how administrative hurdles can undermine the very right-to-information laws representatives help create. The Punjab Information Commission intervened, acknowledging that while separate applications might be procedurally cleaner, the assembly’s blanket rejection was unjustified. By ordering the piecemeal release of the data, the commission affirmed that citizens possess a fundamental statutory right to access such public records. This ruling emphasizes that procedural technicalities cannot override the core principle of accountability, ensuring that essential governmental information remains accessible to the public despite internal resistance. This case is crucial for open data advocates because it establishes a precedent for forcing legislative bodies to comply with transparency mandates. It contrasts the Punjab Assembly’s initial resistance with a similar previous victory at the National Assembly, demonstrating that consistent legal pressure can succeed. The narrative underscores that open data is not merely a technical requirement but a vital democratic tool, and that institutional inertia must be actively challenged to ensure genuine accountability in governance.
