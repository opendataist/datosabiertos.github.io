Modi degree: HC quashes order asking Gujarat University to provide details, fines Arvind Kejriwal

The Gujarat High Court recently quashed a directive requiring Gujarat University to disclose Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s educational credentials to Delhi Chief Minister Arvind Kejriwal, imposing a significant fine for what the judge described as an abuse of the Right to Information Act. The court ruled that Kejriwal’s insistence on obtaining information already available in the public domain demonstrated a lack of bona fide intent, transforming a legitimate inquiry into a political controversy that fell outside the Act’s purpose. This decision underscores the legal boundary between genuine public interest and politically motivated inquiries, establishing that electoral politics cannot override the principles governing information requests. Crucially, the judgment affirms that academic qualifications are personal information protected by fiduciary obligations, rather than public data, unless a larger public interest warrants disclosure. The court emphasized that the character and welfare devotion of elected officials matter more than their educational background, rejecting the notion that political officeholders forfeit privacy regarding personal academic records. By clarifying that "public interest" is a strict legal standard distinct from mere public curiosity, the ruling protects institutions from being compelled to release sensitive personal data solely to satisfy political adversaries or media sensationalism. This case is highly relevant to open_data discussions because it defines the limits of transparency when balancing privacy rights against the public's right to know. It challenges the assumption that all information about public figures must be freely accessible, reinforcing that data holders have valid grounds to withhold personal details when no direct public interest exists. For open_data advocates, this highlights the importance of precise legal frameworks in data governance, ensuring that transparency initiatives do not erode individual privacy rights or enable the misuse of information requests for political leverage.
