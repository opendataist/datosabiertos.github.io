'Ensure Public Authorities Follow Mandate Of Section 4 RTI Act' : Supreme Court Directs Central/State Information Commissioners

The Supreme Court has mandated that Information Commissions must rigorously monitor how public authorities fulfill their proactive disclosure obligations under the Right to Information Act. By emphasizing the symbiotic relationship between citizen rights and state duties, the bench clarified that the core value of transparency relies entirely on authorities systematically cataloging and publishing essential data, such as organizational structures, budgets, and decision-making processes, without waiting for specific requests. This ruling reinforces the principle that accountability is the cornerstone of the RTI framework, governing the dynamic between right holders and duty bearers. The court highlighted that Information Commissions hold a statutory responsibility to ensure this balance is maintained, moving beyond reactive dispute resolution to actively overseeing the systemic adherence to transparency norms. This shifts the focus from mere information retrieval to institutionalizing open data practices across all government bodies. This decision is highly relevant to open_data initiatives because it legally enshrines the proactive dissemination of public records as a mandatory duty rather than an optional practice. It establishes a robust mechanism for ensuring that critical government data is not only available but also accessible and understandable to citizens. Consequently, it drives the standardization and regularity of open government data, ensuring that transparency is embedded in the administrative workflow rather than treated as an afterthought.
