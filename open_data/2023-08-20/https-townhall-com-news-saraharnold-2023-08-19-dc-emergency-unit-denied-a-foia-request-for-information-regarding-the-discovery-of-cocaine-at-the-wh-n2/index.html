The article highlights how national security and privacy exemptions can severely limit public access to government records, a critical issue for open data advocates. By withholding documents related to a high-profile security incident, agencies demonstrate the tension between transparency and security protocols, often leaving citizens in the dark about accountability. This refusal underscores the importance of scrutinizing the specific legal bases used to deny information requests. When entities cite potential terrorism mitigation or investigative technique protection, it raises concerns about whether such claims are used to obscure operational failures rather than genuinely protect public safety. For the open data community, this case serves as a reminder that data liberation is not guaranteed even for significant public events. It emphasizes the need for robust FOIA advocacy and the continuous push to clarify where legitimate security concerns end and unnecessary secrecy begins, ensuring that government actions remain verifiable and transparent.
