An Ontario university student got scammed out of $100K — here's how you can avoid becoming a victim | CBC News

Recent security reports highlight a surge in sophisticated fraud schemes targeting university students, particularly those new to the region or studying abroad. A primary concern is the emergence of "fake police" scams, where perpetrators impersonate law enforcement and customs officials to intimidate victims into paying fabricated fines. These incidents demonstrate how criminals exploit the fear of legal consequences and the isolation of international students to extract large sums of money through false threats of arrest and investigation. Another prevalent tactic involves fraudulent tuition discount services that trick students into paying upfront for services that never materialize. By leveraging social media platforms, scammers gain access to student accounts and mimic legitimate payment confirmations, causing victims to lose their money while still owing their actual tuition fees. This pattern reveals how easily personal credentials can be compromised and how financial institutions can be manipulated, leaving victims in a precarious financial position with no recourse for the lost funds. These incidents are critically relevant to open_data because they underscore the necessity of transparent, publicly accessible fraud databases and real-time information sharing. When universities, police, and anti-fraud centers openly disseminate data on emerging scam trends, they empower communities to recognize red flags and prevent victimization. Public access to this information transforms individual awareness into collective defense, ensuring that lessons from one case are immediately available to protect others from similar exploitation.
