Gobierno abierto: cómo mejorar la transparencia y participación en la gestión pública

The article argues that digitalization is an indispensable prerequisite for achieving an effective Open Government, as it enables the transformation of physical documents into traceable data. This technical infrastructure not only optimizes state processes and reduces corruption but also democratizes access to critical information such as public spending and procurement, facilitating citizen oversight and accountability. A central challenge identified is the gap between the state and citizens, driven by a narrative that creates distance and makes it difficult to understand public management. To bridge this gap, the article emphasizes the need to design simple and user-friendly technological interfaces, similar to commercial consumer experiences, that actively engage society. This allows citizens to track their local projects and budgets, turning transparency into a tool for genuine participation rather than mere information dissemination. The relevance of this text to the field of open data lies in its practical demonstration of how publishing structured data drives civic innovation. The example of Ukraine illustrates how access to real-time open information on reconstruction and funding enables precise planning and rigorous social auditing. This confirms that digital transparency is essential for building trust, improving state efficiency, and empowering civil society to oversee complex public resources.
