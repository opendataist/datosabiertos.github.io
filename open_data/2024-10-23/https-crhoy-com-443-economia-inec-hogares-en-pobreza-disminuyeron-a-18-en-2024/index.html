The study reveals a significant decline in household poverty across both rural and urban areas, alongside a notable rise in average household and per capita incomes. This dual trend indicates improving economic conditions for Costa Rican families, as higher earnings contribute to lifting more households above the poverty and extreme poverty thresholds defined in the survey. These shifts are unevenly distributed regionally, with the Central region maintaining the lowest poverty rates while Brunca and Huetar Caribe remain the most affected. The data underscores the importance of granular geographic analysis in understanding economic disparities, highlighting specific areas that may require targeted policy interventions to sustain development progress. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how publicly available statistical surveys can drive evidence-based policy decisions. By making detailed socioeconomic metrics accessible, researchers and policymakers can monitor inequality trends effectively, ensuring transparency and accountability in national economic planning.
