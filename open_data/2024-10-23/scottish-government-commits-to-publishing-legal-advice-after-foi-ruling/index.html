Scottish Government commits to publishing legal advice after FOI ruling

The article highlights a significant tension between traditional government secrecy and the principles of open data. When the Scottish Government attempted to withhold evidence and legal advice related to the Nicola Sturgeon ministerial code inquiry, data protection authorities mandated its disclosure. This outcome underscores how open data frameworks can pierce through established conventions of confidentiality, forcing transparency even in highly sensitive political contexts. The ruling emphasizes that public interest in understanding government decision-making processes outweighs the desire for legal privilege. By ordering the release of information often kept secret, the authorities reinforced the idea that citizens have a right to scrutinize whether officials followed their own legal counsel. This prioritizes accountability over the convenience of internal government operations, aligning with core open data values of accessibility and scrutiny. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the mechanism by which opaque systems are made transparent. It proves that legal barriers, such as ministerial codes, are not absolute shields against public inquiry. For those advocating for open data, this incident serves as a critical precedent showing that regulatory bodies can effectively enforce the release of restricted information when the public interest is substantial.
