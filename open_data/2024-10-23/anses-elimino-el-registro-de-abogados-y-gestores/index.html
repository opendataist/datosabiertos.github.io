ANSES eliminó el registro de abogados y gestores

The government abolished the mandatory registry for lawyers and administrative managers at ANSES, framing the move as essential deregulation. By removing this barrier, officials aim to simplify social security procedures and allow citizens the freedom to choose their representatives without bureaucratic obstacles, reducing both administrative complexity and potential costs for beneficiaries. This decision highlights the redundancy of special registers when professional regulations already govern legal representation. It reinforces that hiring intermediaries is optional, empowering individuals to manage their own affairs directly if they prefer, thereby aligning administrative processes with broader legal frameworks for private mandates. This initiative is relevant to open data principles because it prioritizes transparency and user autonomy over centralized gatekeeping. By reducing unnecessary regulatory layers, it encourages a more open access model where information and services are directly available to the public, minimizing institutional friction and promoting equitable access to essential social security systems.
