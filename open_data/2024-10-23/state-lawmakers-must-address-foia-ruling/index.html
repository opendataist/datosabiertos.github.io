State lawmakers must address FOIA ruling

A recent Michigan court ruling excludes public employees from Freedom of Information Act requirements, allowing individual workers to withhold records that governing bodies cannot. This legal gap threatens to obscure taxpayer-funded activities, enabling potential cover-ups of misconduct by keeping documents within private employee hands rather than making them publicly accessible. This decision undermines the core principle of open data by severing the link between policy creation and its practical execution by staff. Without transparency into employee actions, citizens cannot effectively scrutinize government operations, fundamentally weakening public accountability and trust in democratic institutions. The article urges legislative amendments to clarify that FOIA applies to all public employees, not just governing bodies. Restoring this transparency is essential to prevent malfeasance and ensure that open data laws truly empower the people to understand how their government functions, fulfilling the original intent of freedom of information statutes.
