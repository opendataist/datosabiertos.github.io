RDF exports up 6% in 2023 - letsrecycle.com

England’s Refuse Derived Fuel (RDF) exports experienced a notable resurgence in 2023, reversing previous declines and reaching levels not seen since 2017. This recovery was primarily driven by a return to post-pandemic normalcy and easing global tensions, with Sweden emerging as the dominant destination for English waste resources. The strong commercial ties between England and Sweden highlight how cross-border data flows in waste management facilitate efficient energy recovery, showcasing the international interdependence of circular economy supply chains. However, industry analysts caution that this growth may not represent a long-term sustained surge, as it largely offsets previously depressed volumes. The sector faces ongoing challenges, including CO2 taxation and logistical disruptions, which create a landscape of wary stability rather than robust expansion. Despite these uncertainties, the continued export of RDF to highly efficient combined heat and power facilities in Europe underscores the economic and environmental rationale for importing waste-to-energy resources, reinforcing the practical value of transparent trade data in optimizing energy grids. This article is relevant to open data because it relies on transparent, publicly sourced figures from the Environment Agency to track international waste movements. Such data is crucial for monitoring the effectiveness of recycling policies, assessing environmental impacts across borders, and ensuring accountability in the global trade of secondary raw materials. Open access to this information allows stakeholders to analyze trends, verify sustainability claims, and make informed decisions that support the transition toward a more resilient and resource-efficient circular economy.
