Explained: Why the Supreme Court struck down the electoral bonds scheme

The Supreme Court has declared the electoral bonds scheme unconstitutional, ruling that it disproportionately infringes upon voters' fundamental right to information. By allowing anonymous donations, the scheme obstructed the public’s ability to scrutinize potential quid pro quos between political parties and their funders. Since informed voting requires transparency regarding who supports political entities, the Court determined that the opacity introduced by the bonds undermined the very foundation of democratic participation and informed choice. The judgment emphasizes that while individuals possess a right to informational privacy, financial contributions intended to influence public policy must remain in the public domain. The Court found that the government’s justification for restricting this transparency was not a reasonable limitation under the Constitution, as it did not fall within legally valid grounds for curbing free speech. Furthermore, the anonymity provided was deemed selective and arbitrary, failing to serve its stated purpose of combating black money when less restrictive alternatives, such as standard electronic transfers, were available to maintain integrity. This decision is pivotal for open data initiatives as it mandates the Election Commission to publish comprehensive details of all electoral bond transactions, including purchaser identities and amounts encashed. By legally compelling the disclosure of previously hidden funding flows, the ruling transforms opaque corporate political financing into accessible public information. This shift ensures that citizens and researchers can analyze financial influences on governance, promoting accountability and reinforcing the principle that transparency in political funding is essential for a healthy democracy.
