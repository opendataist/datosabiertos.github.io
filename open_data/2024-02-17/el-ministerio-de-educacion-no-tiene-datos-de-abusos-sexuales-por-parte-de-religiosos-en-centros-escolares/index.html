El Ministerio de Educación no tiene datos de abusos sexuales por parte de religiosos en centros escolares

The Ministry of Education states that it has no records of sexual abuse committed by religious personnel in schools, arguing that this responsibility falls exclusively under other public administrations and law enforcement agencies. In response to a transparency request, the education ministry maintains that, while it acknowledges the seriousness of the issue, it lacks its own data and considers that the management of such incidents falls under the jurisdiction of the Ministries of the Interior and Justice. This stance has sparked controversy, as the requester argues that the Ministry of Education has high-level inspection functions and should take a more active role, rather than "keeping a low profile" in the face of a problem that the Ombudsman frequently identifies within the school environment. The relevance of this case for open data lies in the transparency and accessibility of public information regarding fundamental rights and the protection of minors. It demonstrates how institutions use legal transparency frameworks to define the limits of their responsibility, even when there are contradictory reports or prior statistical data, such as those from the Ombudsman. For citizens and civil society, this exchange highlights the importance of requesting access to information to verify whether records truly exist or if they have simply not been collected, thereby revealing potential gaps in public data collection systems concerning rights violations. Finally, the case underscores the tension between the autonomy of public administrations and the need for an integrated data perspective. While the Ministry of Education limits itself to the information it formally holds, other actors, such as the Church, are initiating reparations processes based on moral convictions rather than official statistics. This highlights the urgency of improving interoperability and data sharing among different state entities to prevent systemic cases from being overlooked due to institutional ignorance, thus ensuring that public information effectively serves the protection and collective well-being of society.
