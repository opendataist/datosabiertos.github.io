SC Strikes Down Electoral Bonds as Unconstitutional

The Supreme Court declared the electoral bond scheme unconstitutional, ruling that it violated voters' right to information by ensuring contributor anonymity. This decision dismantles a system that allowed selective opacity for wealthy donors, effectively prioritizing their privacy over the public’s democratic right to know who funds political parties. By striking down associated amendments to key laws, the Court affirmed that secrecy in political financing undermines transparency and democratic integrity. Consequently, immediate steps have been taken to reverse the scheme’s impact. The State Bank of India is mandated to halt all issuance of electoral bonds and disclose complete details of transactions since 2019 to the Election Commission. This data, including purchaser identities and amounts, must be made public shortly thereafter, ensuring that past anonymous contributions are now subject to full public scrutiny. Any unencashed bonds within their validity period are also being returned and refunded. This ruling is highly relevant to open_data as it establishes a legal precedent for transparency in political funding. It emphasizes that data related to political contributions should be accessible to the public rather than shielded by corporate or state secrets. By forcing the disclosure of previously hidden financial flows, the judgment reinforces the principle that open data is essential for holding power accountable and preventing undue influence in the democratic process.
