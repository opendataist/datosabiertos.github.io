Unlocking transparency: Why Bangladesh Bank must share lending data with researchers

Unlocking transparency: Why Bangladesh Bank must share lending data with researchers

Bangladesh’s banking sector is plagued by systemic corruption and political interference, leading to massive fraud and eroded public trust. Recurring scandals reveal that unrestricted access to lending data by independent researchers could serve as a critical early warning system. By analyzing granular loan details, experts could detect irregularities like unusual credit patterns or evergreening before they escalate into insolvency, allowing for proactive regulatory intervention rather than reactive bailouts. Transparent lending data enables the identification of systemic risks, such as sectoral over-concentration and geopolitical disparities in credit allocation. This visibility promotes accountability by facilitating cross-institutional benchmarking and independent audits, which are essential for exposing collusion and reinforcing enforcement mechanisms. Such measures not only stabilize financial institutions but also ensure that resources reach intended beneficiaries, fostering a more equitable economic environment. This article is highly relevant to open data as it advocates for the responsible release of sensitive financial datasets to drive public interest analysis. It highlights how anonymized, secure data sharing can balance privacy with transparency, preventing future crises. By adopting global best practices, regulators can leverage open data to enhance governance, restore confidence, and create a resilient financial ecosystem, proving that accessibility is key to accountability.
