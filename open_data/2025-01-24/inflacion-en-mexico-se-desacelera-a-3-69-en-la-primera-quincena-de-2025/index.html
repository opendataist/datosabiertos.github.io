Inflación en México se desacelera a 3.69% en la primera quincena de 2025

Inflación en México se desacelera a 3.69% en la primera quincena de 2025

Mexico’s inflation continues its downward trend in early 2025, falling into the Bank of Mexico’s target range for the first time since 2021. This stabilization suggests that monetary authorities have strong justification to maintain or even accelerate interest rate cuts, aiming to support economic growth while ensuring that price stability remains anchored within expected parameters. However, underlying dynamics reveal a divergence: while headline inflation decelerates due to volatile components such as fruits, vegetables, and energy, core inflation excluding these items actually accelerated. This indicates persistent pressure on essential goods like food and merchandise, highlighting that the broad cooling of prices masks ongoing structural challenges in specific sectors that continue to drive costs upward for consumers. This data is highly relevant to open data advocates because it underscores the necessity of accessible, granular statistical releases from institutions like INEGI. Transparent, timely, and detailed public datasets allow economists and citizens to distinguish between temporary fluctuations and structural inflationary pressures, fostering informed public debate and ensuring accountability in monetary policy decisions.
