The recent economic census conducted by INEGI reveals a significant slowdown in business growth and employment in Quintana Roo during the current presidential administration. By explicitly excluding informal enterprises, the data highlights a stark contrast with the previous period, showing that both the number of economic units and jobs have stagnated or declined. This divergence suggests that the economic engine driving earlier expansion has lost momentum, necessitating a critical re-evaluation of how the local and federal economies are performing relative to historical trends. The impact of this deceleration is unevenly distributed across different business sizes, with large enterprises experiencing the sharpest contraction in both establishments and workforce. Micro and small businesses also saw their growth rates plummet compared to previous six-year cycles, indicating a broad-based cooling effect rather than an isolated issue. These figures imply that policy environments may have shifted in ways that discourage expansion or job creation, particularly affecting larger organizations that traditionally drive substantial employment. This report is crucial for the open data community because it demonstrates how transparent, granular statistical datasets can serve as neutral evidence for policy analysis. Rather than offering political interpretations, the release of raw census data empowers independent researchers and economists to identify root causes of economic shifts. It underscores the value of accessible, high-quality government statistics in fostering objective, data-driven debates about public management and regional development strategies.
