REVELA INEGI ALTO PORCENTAJE DE CORRUPCIÓN EN LA POLICÍA DE CANCÚN: En su Encuesta Nacional de Seguridad Pública Urbana, la confianza en la Seguridad Municipal está por debajo del 50% - Noticaribe

The article highlights a subtle increase in the perception of insecurity in Cancún alongside a decrease in Chetumal, while exposing significant discrepancies in public trust between federal and municipal police forces. It further details high rates of police corruption in Cancún, which starkly contrast with lower figures in the state capital, revealing how local governance impacts citizen safety and institutional credibility. These findings are critically relevant to open data initiatives, as they demonstrate the necessity of transparent, granular statistical reporting to identify specific areas of concern and hold authorities accountable. By making such detailed survey data publicly accessible, researchers and citizens can analyze trends in corruption and perceived safety, fostering informed dialogue and enabling evidence-based policy interventions that address the root causes of crime rather than superficial symptoms. Ultimately, the disparity in perceived police effectiveness underscores the value of open data in benchmarking institutional performance. When communities can access and compare metrics across different jurisdictions and agencies, it empowers civil society to demand higher standards of integrity and efficiency, transforming raw statistics into powerful tools for social accountability and democratic governance.
