Labour Market Statistics - September 2025

Labour Market Statistics - September 2025

The Northern Ireland labour market is experiencing a noticeable deceleration, characterized by divergent trends across key economic indicators. While payroll numbers show slight growth and earnings have risen, broader measures from the Labour Force Survey indicate a simultaneous rise in unemployment and economic inactivity, alongside a decline in employment rates. This mixed picture suggests that despite nominal job increases in certain sectors, the overall health of the labour market is weakening, with workforce participation facing headwinds. Business-level data reveals that employee jobs reached record highs, driven primarily by gains in manufacturing and services, yet these gains are juxtaposed with rising redundancies and stagnant working hours. The stability in the claimant count, combined with annual redundancy figures mirroring pre-pandemic levels, points to a sector-specific recovery rather than a uniform economic rebound. The lack of statistically significant annual changes in headline labour metrics underscores the fragility of the current employment landscape, where growth is uneven and not yet translating into broad-based labour market expansion. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical importance of accessing and cross-referencing multiple, granular datasets to form an accurate economic narrative. Relying on a single metric can be misleading; for instance, high job counts mask rising inactivity and unemployment. Open access to these diverse sources—payroll data, surveys, and redundancy records—enables analysts, researchers, and the public to uncover these underlying complexities, fostering a more nuanced understanding of economic realities and improving the transparency of government statistical reporting.
