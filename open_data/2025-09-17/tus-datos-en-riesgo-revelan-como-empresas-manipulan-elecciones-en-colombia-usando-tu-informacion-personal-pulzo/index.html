Tus datos en riesgo: revelan cómo empresas manipulan elecciones en Colombia usando tu información personal - Pulzo

Tus datos en riesgo: revelan cómo empresas manipulan elecciones en Colombia usando tu información personal - Pulzo

Recent investigative reports have revealed how political campaigns in Colombia increasingly collect citizens' personal data through social media platforms and online forms. This practice enables sophisticated voter segmentation based on preferences and behaviors, allowing for the delivery of highly targeted political advertising. The core issue is that this data collection often occurs without the explicit knowledge or consent of the individuals involved, raising serious concerns about the manipulation of democratic processes through digital means. The analysis highlights significant gaps in the current regulatory framework, noting that existing data protection laws lack clear norms and effective sanctions to deter these practices. Experts warn that while a legal framework nominally exists, loopholes allow companies to profile citizens with minimal oversight. This regulatory vacuum creates an environment where digital election surveillance becomes increasingly difficult to detect, leaving citizens vulnerable to invisible influence operations that bypass traditional transparency standards. This story is critically relevant to open data discussions because it illustrates the dangers of unchecked data aggregation and the lack of transparency in how personal information is repurposed for political ends. It underscores the urgent need for stronger institutional controls, improved digital literacy among citizens, and robust accountability mechanisms. Ultimately, it serves as a cautionary tale about how the open availability of digital footprints can be exploited to undermine privacy and democratic integrity when proper safeguards are absent.
