What tax data teach us about firms, trade and jobs

What tax data teach us about firms, trade and jobs

Tunisia’s experience reveals that corporate tax exemptions often fail to stimulate new economic activity, as growth remains concentrated among large incumbents. This challenges the assumption that fiscal incentives alone drive broad-based business creation and highlights the need for more nuanced policy approaches to job generation. Tax data offers a high-resolution view of firm behavior, trade, and market structures, addressing the common opacity in observing how private sector dynamics shape economic outcomes. By leveraging this existing data, policymakers can better understand the intricate links between geography, liquidity, and employment. This approach is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how publicly accessible, safeguarded fiscal records can guide smarter, inclusive job policies. Openly sharing these insights fosters transparency and enables evidence-based decisions that promote equitable economic development without relying on ineffective tax breaks.
