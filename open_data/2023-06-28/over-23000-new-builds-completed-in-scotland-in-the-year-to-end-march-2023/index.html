Over 23,000 new builds completed in Scotland in the year to end March 2023

Scottish housing statistics reveal a critical divergence in the current market: while home completions have reached their highest annual level since 2008, new construction starts have fallen significantly below pre-pandemic levels. This trend highlights a looming supply risk, as recent outputs are likely to be unsustainable without an immediate turnaround in development activity. The data indicates that despite increased completion rates in the private and housing association sectors, overall momentum is slowing due to persistent barriers facing builders and developers. The data transparency improvements are equally notable, with the elimination of reporting time lags between private-led and social housing sectors. This synchronization allows for a more accurate and timely comparison of different housing delivery methods, enhancing the reliability of official records. By presenting all quarterly figures simultaneously, the publication provides a clearer, unified view of the housing landscape, reducing confusion and enabling stakeholders to assess sector performance on a consistent timeline. This report is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates how timely, synchronized, and granular public statistics can support evidence-based policymaking and sector accountability. The detailed breakdown of completions, starts, and approvals against government targets illustrates the necessity of accessible, high-quality data for monitoring progress toward long-term social goals. Furthermore, it underscores the role of open data in identifying economic challenges and guiding regulatory reforms to address housing shortages effectively.
