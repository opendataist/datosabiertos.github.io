C-LM recibió 2,2 millones de viajes de residentes en España en el primer trimestre, que gastaron 51 euros diarios

The article analyzes national tourism trends, highlighting a significant recovery in domestic travel volume and a sharp increase in expenditure compared to the previous year. A key finding is the divergent spending patterns between internal and international trips, with cross-border journeys showing substantially higher daily costs and growth rates than local vacations. This indicates a strong rebound in consumer willingness to spend on travel, particularly for experiences abroad, while domestic tourism remains steady with modest gains. Another major insight involves the evolution of travel infrastructure and preferences, such as the surge in hotel usage for international trips and the continued dominance of private vehicles for internal journeys. The data reveals shifting motivations for travel, with leisure and family visits remaining the primary drivers, reflecting broader social and economic behaviors. These shifts in mobility and accommodation choices provide valuable context for understanding how travelers allocate resources and plan their trips in a post-pandemic environment. This information is crucial for open data initiatives because it offers granular, standardized metrics on human mobility and economic activity. Such datasets enable researchers and policymakers to monitor regional disparities, assess the impact of tourism on local economies, and develop targeted strategies for sustainable development. By making this statistical information freely accessible, open data fosters transparency and supports evidence-based decision-making in areas like urban planning, transportation, and tourism management.
