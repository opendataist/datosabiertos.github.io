Crimen toma control de Los Altos de Jalisco

The article highlights how organized crime in Jalisco’s Los Altos region has triggered massive forced displacement, transforming voluntary migration into a desperate exodus for survival. Criminal groups, primarily the CJNG and their rivals, control key economic assets such as livestock and fuel extraction routes, effectively replacing state authority in these territories. This power vacuum forces local producers to pay extortion fees and live in constant fear, severely disrupting legal economic activities and destabilizing the region’s social fabric. The central conclusion is that the crisis is driven by the criminal monopolization of profitable sectors, turning rural livelihoods into targets for extortion and violence. The state’s inability to enforce the rule of law allows these groups to dictate terms, including sales channels and production volumes, while simultaneously engaging in brutal conflicts over drug trafficking corridors and fuel theft infrastructure. Consequently, residents flee not for economic opportunity, as in the past, but to escape imminent threats, leading to a tripling of outward migration in just a few years. This case is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent, accessible information to track human rights violations and the economic impacts of organized crime. Reliable, geospatial data on displacement rates, extortion practices, and violent incidents is essential for civil society and researchers to expose the true scale of state failure and criminal influence. Without open access to such data, the systematic erosion of democratic institutions and the humanitarian crisis in regions like Los Altos remain obscured, hindering effective policy responses and accountability efforts.
