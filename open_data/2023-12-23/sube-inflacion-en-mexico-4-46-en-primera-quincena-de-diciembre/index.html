Sube inflación en México 4.46 % en primera quincena de diciembre

Mexican inflation rose to 4.46% year-on-year in early December, surpassing market expectations and continuing a recent upward trend. Although significantly lower than at the start of the year, this increase remains above the central bank’s target, complicating efforts to stabilize prices and keeping interest rates at restrictive levels. The data reveal distinct pressures, with core inflation showing persistent growth in goods and services, while volatile food and energy costs also contributed to the overall rise. This divergence highlights the complexity of the current economic environment, where underlying trends suggest that price stability has not yet been fully achieved, despite some moderation compared to previous peaks. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how granular, high-frequency statistical indicators provide essential transparency for economic analysis. By making detailed inflation metrics publicly available, institutions enable researchers, analysts, and the public to track real-time economic health, fostering informed debate and accountability in monetary policy decisions.
