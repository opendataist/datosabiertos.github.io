The article highlights a significant backlog in Jersey’s coroner system, driven by a surge in deaths and major tragedies. This overwhelming caseload, compounded by chronic staffing shortages, illustrates how opaque or inaccessible public data can hinder accountability and public trust. When citizens cannot easily monitor the efficiency of critical government functions, it undermines transparency and the ability to advocate for resource allocation. Delays in concluding inquests arise from complex legal dependencies and logistical hurdles, such as awaiting criminal proceedings or international evidence. These systemic bottlenecks affect bereaved families and witnesses, creating emotional distress and procedural uncertainty. Understanding these timelines is essential for evaluating the performance of public services and identifying where structural reforms are needed to improve responsiveness. Relevance to open data lies in the need for granular, timely statistics on case durations and staffing levels. By making this information publicly available in machine-readable formats, authorities can enable better scrutiny of administrative efficiency. Open data fosters informed debate on resource needs and ensures that the justice system operates with the necessary speed and clarity, ultimately supporting the rights of those affected by tragic events.
