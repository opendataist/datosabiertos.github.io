88% of workers self employed, says NBS report

Nigeria’s labor market is overwhelmingly driven by self-employment and informal sectors, with only a tiny fraction of workers in formal wage positions. This structural reality highlights a significant gap between official statistical definitions and the lived economic experiences of the majority, as most individuals survive through own-account work or farming rather than traditional employment. The data reveals stark disparities across demographics and geography, showing higher unemployment rates among youth, women, and urban residents. These trends underscore the vulnerability of specific groups within the broader informal economy, suggesting that standard metrics may obscure the precarious nature of livelihoods that do not fit conventional employment categories. This article is crucial for open data advocates because it illustrates the challenges of measuring modern economies where formal job titles are rare. It emphasizes the need for transparent methodologies and community-informed metrics to capture the true scope of economic participation, ensuring that open data initiatives reflect the informal realities that define much of the global workforce.
