The article highlights a worrying increase in poverty in Costa Rica between 2010 and 2023, alongside stark regional disparities. The Central region remains the only area with poverty rates below the national average, while other regions, particularly Huetar Norte, have experienced significant deterioration. This data is crucial for open data initiatives, as it underscores the need for accessible, granular socioeconomic indicators to identify vulnerable territories and drive effective policy. Beyond mere income insufficiency, this poverty is manifested in higher levels of violence, crime, and political apathy. Experts urge public policies to target areas lacking educational and employment opportunities. By making such detailed regional data openly available, researchers and officials can better analyze the structural causes of inequality and design precise interventions. Ultimately, the economic college emphasizes the importance of contributing to accurate policy design. Open access to these detailed poverty indicators enables a deeper understanding of the human impact behind the statistics, fostering transparency and empowering stakeholders to implement solutions that genuinely improve living conditions for affected populations.
