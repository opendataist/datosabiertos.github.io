Kerala HC Directs 'Indian Kanoon' To Anonymise Identity Of A Rape Accused, Says Details Of Victim & Accused Can't Be Published Sans Court's Permission

This ruling clarifies that while the press has a legitimate right to report on court proceedings to ensure transparency, this freedom is strictly limited by statutory requirements to protect victim and accused anonymity in sexual offense cases. The court emphasized that publishing details without prior judicial permission, which violates Sections 327 and 373 of the CrPC, is unlawful, regardless of the public's interest in knowing the specifics of the trial. However, the judgment distinguishes between news reporting and the digital archiving of judicial records. It determined that publishing anonymized judgments on legal databases does not constitute a privacy violation, as these serve as vital public records. Consequently, while news media must mask identities and seek permission, legal data repositories are permitted to host such documents provided they ensure the confidentiality of personal details, balancing the open justice principle with privacy rights. For the open data community, this decision is pivotal as it defines the boundaries of permissible data release regarding sensitive legal information. It establishes that while raw court data can be publicly accessible for transparency, it must be rigorously processed to remove identifiable information before distribution. This highlights the necessity for open data providers to implement strict anonymization protocols when dealing with criminal justice data to remain compliant with privacy laws and ethical standards.
