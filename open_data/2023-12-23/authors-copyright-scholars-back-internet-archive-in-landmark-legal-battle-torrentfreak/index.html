Authors & Copyright Scholars Back 'Internet Archive' in Landmark Legal Battle * TorrentFreak

The Internet Archive’s Controlled Digital Lending initiative faces a critical legal challenge that could redefine digital access and preservation. While a lower court ruled against the Archive, citing copyright infringement, the organization is appealing, arguing that its model supports the public interest. This case highlights the tension between traditional copyright enforcement and the evolving needs of digital libraries in preserving history and providing equitable access to knowledge. Supporters argue that the publishers’ lawsuit exacerbates an existing market imbalance by prioritizing corporate control over public benefit. The Authors Alliance and various scholars contend that limiting libraries’ ability to lend digital copies concentrates power among major publishers, reducing accessibility for readers and authors alike. They emphasize that fair use should protect library practices that function similarly to physical lending, ensuring that digital materials remain available without compromising the rights of creators or the public’s ability to learn and research. This dispute is vital to open data and open access movements because it determines whether digital resources can be freely shared and preserved or locked behind restrictive licensing. A ruling against the Archive could set a precedent that prevents libraries from owning or lending digitized works, effectively ending the concept of digital ownership in libraries. Upholding fair use ensures that information remains a public good, fostering transparency, education, and cultural preservation in an increasingly digital world.
