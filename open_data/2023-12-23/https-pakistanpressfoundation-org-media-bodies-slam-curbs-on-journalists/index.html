The article highlights how government restrictions on media coverage directly undermine public access to information. This suppression prevents citizens from understanding critical developments, creating a systemic barrier to transparency. Such actions violate the fundamental right to be informed about public affairs and justice. Journalists face illegal censorship and pressure from state institutions, silencing vital reporting. This environment fosters fear, limiting the press’s ability to hold power accountable. Without freedom to report, democratic oversight erodes, leaving citizens uninformed and vulnerable to unchecked authority. This is relevant to open data because media freedom ensures the flow of public information. When journalists are censored, the dissemination of open datasets and public records is stifled. Open data initiatives rely on a free press to interpret and broadcast findings. Without this, open data remains inaccessible, failing to empower society with the knowledge needed for civic engagement and accountability.
