85 grupos de verificadores de datos de todo el mundo escriben una carta abierta de protesta a YouTube

The open letter from fact-checkers underscores the inadequacy of YouTube’s current policies in combating misinformation. Rather than implementing effective measures, the platform allows conspiratorial groups to manipulate users and raise funds, spreading false cures and hate speech on a global scale. This inaction reveals a systemic failure in which profitability derived from user engagement outweighs ethical responsibility, enabling harmful content to thrive while the company avoids constructive dialogue with verification organizations. The problem extends beyond YouTube, affecting other social media platforms that face an inherent conflict between their commercial objectives and public health. Their algorithms prioritize polarizing and easily consumable content, incentivizing user retention regardless of the accuracy of the information. This incentive structure creates an environment where misinformation is more profitable than truth, and platforms retain controversial accounts due to their high traffic, raising questions about the consistency of their rules when applied to public figures or established media outlets. This case is crucial for the field of open data, as it highlights the need for algorithmic transparency and access to moderation tools. Collaboration with fact-checkers should be formalized through access to structured data that enables swift and prioritized verification. Without open data mechanisms that facilitate auditing and technical collaboration, platforms will continue to operate in opacity, perpetuating a digital ecosystem in which false information takes precedence over the common good and democracy.
