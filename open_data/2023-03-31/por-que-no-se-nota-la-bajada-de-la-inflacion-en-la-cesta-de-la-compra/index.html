¿Por qué no se nota la bajada de la inflación en la cesta de la compra?

¿Por qué no se nota la bajada de la inflación en la cesta de la compra?

The article highlights a significant discrepancy between the reported inflation rate and the lived economic reality for consumers. While official statistics show a decline in overall inflation due to lower energy and fuel costs, underlying inflation remains high. This creates a situation where macroeconomic data fails to reflect the persistent price pressures felt in everyday shopping, particularly for essential goods. This divergence is crucial for open data initiatives because it exposes the limitations of aggregated statistics in capturing granular consumer experiences. It underscores the necessity of analyzing sub-categories and underlying trends rather than relying solely on headline figures. Open data can help bridge this gap by providing detailed, accessible breakdowns of price changes across different product sectors. Ultimately, the relevance to open data lies in its ability to support nuanced economic analysis. By making granular data publicly available, stakeholders can better understand the specific drivers of cost-of-living changes. This transparency allows for more accurate policy assessments and helps citizens navigate the complex relationship between statistical indicators and their actual purchasing power.
