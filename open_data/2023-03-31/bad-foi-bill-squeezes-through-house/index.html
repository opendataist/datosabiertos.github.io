Bad FOI bill squeezes through House

Arkansas lawmakers narrowly advanced legislation that significantly weakens the state’s Freedom of Information Act by redefining quorum rules. This change permits governing board members to hold private discussions without press notification, provided fewer than one-third of the body participates. Although civil liberty groups and a dedicated task force strongly opposed the measure, the bill passed with minimal dissent, signaling a legislative shift toward reduced governmental transparency. The sponsor argued that the change would allow officials casual interaction without the burden of strict public meeting statutes, framing strict oversight as an impediment to effective governance. Critics countered that the proposal threatens public accountability by enabling officials to coordinate decisions away from public scrutiny. This tension highlights the ongoing struggle to balance administrative efficiency with the fundamental right of citizens to monitor how their representatives operate. This development is critical for open data advocates because it erodes the legal framework ensuring government transparency. Weakening FOIA provisions makes it harder to access records and understand decision-making processes, directly undermining efforts to maintain open, accessible government data. As the bill moves to the Senate, it represents a significant risk to the integrity of public information systems and the trust between citizens and their elected officials.
