La construcción no frena su siniestralidad laboral en Jaén

Official statistics reveal a significant decline in workplace accidents across most sectors and severity levels in Jaén, underscoring the effectiveness of current prevention strategies. While fatal and severe injuries have decreased markedly, the construction sector remains a critical exception, experiencing an increase in incidents. This divergence highlights that although a broad safety culture is taking root, specific high-risk industries require targeted interventions to avoid lagging behind in overall progress. A crucial trend emerges when analyzing commuting accidents, or *in itinere* incidents, which have risen significantly alongside a decline in workplace injuries. This shift suggests that while risk mitigation within physical work environments has been successful, safety measures must be expanded to cover travel routes. Consequently, the definition of workplace safety is evolving, requiring policymakers to address hazards that occur outside the immediate premises of employment in order to achieve truly comprehensive protection for workers. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it demonstrates the power of transparent, granular public information to drive policy and education. By making these statistics accessible, the administration is enabling the integration of preventive education into vocational training for future workers. This approach exemplifies how open data can facilitate the transition from reactive statistics to proactive cultural change, ensuring that safety knowledge is instilled early in the workforce to sustain long-term reductions in accident rates.
