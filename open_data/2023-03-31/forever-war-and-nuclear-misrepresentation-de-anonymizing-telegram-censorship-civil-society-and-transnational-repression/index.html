Forever war and nuclear misrepresentation. De-anonymizing Telegram? Censorship, civil society, and transnational repression.

Russia is pivoting its war rhetoric toward a "forever war" narrative, acknowledging that victory will require indefinite sacrifice rather than a quick resolution. This shift signals a deepening commitment to prolonged conflict, where national survival is framed as dependent on enduring significant human and social costs. The implication is a society mobilized for sustained hardship, with official media reinforcing the idea that long-term combat is now an accepted reality for citizens. Simultaneously, Moscow is leveraging nuclear posturing and advanced digital surveillance to consolidate control and deter Western intervention. By framing nuclear deployments as defensive responses to Western aid, Russia seeks to justify escalation while maintaining diplomatic ambiguity. More critically, the development of tools to de-anonymize Telegram users represents a severe escalation in domestic surveillance, aiming to pierce the privacy of dissenters and align public discourse strictly with state interests through technological coercion. These domestic crackdowns are part of a broader strategy of transnational repression that threatens global democratic values. By targeting civil society groups and human rights defenders through cyber means, the Kremlin exemplifies the authoritarian tactics that international coalitions are now explicitly condemning. This is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the urgent need for transparent, accessible information to expose censorship mechanisms and protect digital rights, ensuring that civil society can counteract state-sponsored disinformation and surveillance.
