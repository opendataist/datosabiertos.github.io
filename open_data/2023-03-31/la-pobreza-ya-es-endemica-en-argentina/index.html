La pobreza ya es endémica en Argentina

Argentina is confronting a critical socioeconomic emergency characterized by endemic poverty, which disproportionately affects minors and the working class. High inflation and economic contraction have eroded purchasing power, meaning that employment no longer guarantees freedom from poverty. This structural shift has pushed the middle class into destitution and created a cycle of intergenerational poverty, indicating that traditional labor markets alone are insufficient to reverse these trends. The crisis extends beyond income deficits, encompassing widespread violations of basic rights and deepening social exclusion. Significant disparities in education, access to healthcare, and quality of life highlight a broader failure in social equity. With millions of children experiencing multiple forms of deprivation, the issue transcends simple economic metrics, revealing a societal fracture that requires immediate and comprehensive attention to prevent further marginalization of vulnerable populations. This article underscores the vital role of open data in diagnosing complex social realities. Transparent, accessible statistical information is essential for holding institutions accountable and informing evidence-based policy interventions. By making these indicators publicly available, stakeholders can drive the consensus needed for effective, long-term economic strategies, ensuring that government actions are grounded in accurate, real-time realities rather than outdated estimates.
