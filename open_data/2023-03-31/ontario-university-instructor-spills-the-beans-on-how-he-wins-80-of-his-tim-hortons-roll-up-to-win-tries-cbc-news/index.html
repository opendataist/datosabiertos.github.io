Ontario university instructor spills the beans on how he wins 80% of his Tim Hortons Roll Up to Win tries | CBC News

Professor Michael Wallace utilized publicly available data to determine optimal timing for winning Tim Hortons’ contest, proving that strategic analysis can significantly improve odds. His findings suggest that participating early in the morning offers the highest probability of success, demonstrating how accessible data can be leveraged for practical decision-making. Tim Hortons acknowledges and accepts this user-driven optimization, viewing enthusiastic players as engaged fans rather than threats. This interaction highlights how modern contests incorporate digital platforms, allowing companies to monitor usage patterns while consumers apply advanced statistical methods to enhance their experience. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparently published datasets can empower individuals to derive meaningful insights. It serves as an educational tool, showing that statistics are not abstract concepts but dynamic tools for analyzing real-world information, thereby encouraging broader public engagement with data-driven thinking and transparency.
