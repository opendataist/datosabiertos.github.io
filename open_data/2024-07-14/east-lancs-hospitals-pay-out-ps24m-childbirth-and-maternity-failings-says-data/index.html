East Lancs hospitals pay out £24m childbirth and maternity failings, says data

The analysis reveals that NHS trusts in Lancashire paid nearly £100 million in compensation for childbirth and maternity negligence between 2019 and 2023. This substantial financial burden highlights systemic failures in care, with top claims stemming from delayed diagnoses and inadequate responses to fetal distress. The high volume of claims across major hospitals indicates widespread issues rather than isolated incidents, underscoring the urgent need for improved standards and accountability within the healthcare system. This data aligns with findings from the UK’s first parliamentary inquiry into birth trauma, which gathered testimony from over 1,300 women describing harrowing experiences of medical negligence. The inquiry concluded that poor care is frequently tolerated as normal, leading to recommendations for a new maternity commissioner and guaranteed safe staffing levels. These insights emphasize the human cost of systemic flaws and the critical requirement for structural reforms to prevent life-changing injuries and fatalities for mothers and babies. This information is vital for open_data advocates as it demonstrates the public value of Freedom of Information requests in exposing hidden truths about institutional performance. By making legal and financial records transparent, citizens and researchers can hold health authorities accountable, driving evidence-based policy changes. Open data serves as a crucial mechanism for revealing disparities in care quality, ensuring that statistical evidence supports demands for safer maternity services and greater transparency in how taxpayer funds are allocated for negligence settlements.
