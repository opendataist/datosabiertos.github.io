Juana Escudero, especialista en derechos de autor: “A la IA solo se le puede pedir que te haga zumo del pasado”

Juana Escudero, especialista en derechos de autor: “A la IA solo se le puede pedir que te haga zumo del pasado”

Juana Escudero, the new Director of Projects at the Autor Institute, emphasizes that local culture and copyright are fundamental to democracy and social development. She regards public libraries as essential spaces for socialization and the democratization of culture, arguing that local culture is the true backbone of society. This perspective highlights the importance of open access to cultural resources and the need for equitable management of cultural assets at the municipal level, which aligns with open data principles by advocating for accessible, community-driven infrastructure rather than centralized, elite-controlled narratives. The article underscores that creative works are human rights, protected by international frameworks that balance creators’ rights with public domain access. Escudero stresses that while intellectual property rights eventually expire, recognizing creators’ rights is crucial for sustaining innovation. This reinforces the open data ethos of transparency and ethical use, ensuring that knowledge and cultural outputs are respected and properly attributed, fostering an environment where creativity can thrive without exploitation. Addressing contemporary challenges, Escudero warns against tech companies exploiting creative works for artificial intelligence without proper licensing. She argues that humans must regulate AI to prevent the commodification of creativity, advocating for collective licenses that benefit authors. This is vital for open data, as it promotes ethical AI development, ensuring that data used to train algorithms respects creators’ rights and contributes to a fair, sustainable ecosystem for both technology and cultural production.
