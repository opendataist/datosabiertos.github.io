¿Cuáles son las ciudades donde la gente se siente insegura en México, según INEGI?

¿Cuáles son las ciudades donde la gente se siente insegura en México, según INEGI?

The INEGI’s latest survey reveals a growing perception of insecurity among Mexicans, with more than half of adults considering their cities unsafe. This trend highlights a significant shift in public sentiment, indicating that citizens increasingly feel vulnerable in everyday environments. The data underscores a widespread concern that transcends mere statistics, reflecting a deep-seated anxiety about personal safety in urban areas. Specific locations such as ATMs, public transport, and public streets are identified as major sources of fear. These findings suggest that daily routines are increasingly perceived as hazardous, eroding trust in public spaces. The rise in reported insecurity at these key sites signals a deterioration in the quality of life for many residents, demanding urgent attention from local authorities and urban planners. This report is vital to open data because it democratizes access to critical social metrics. By providing quarterly insights into public perception, the ENSU enables researchers, journalists, and policymakers to analyze safety trends transparently. Such accessible data fosters evidence-based decision-making, allowing communities to advocate for targeted security improvements and hold governments accountable for their public safety strategies.
