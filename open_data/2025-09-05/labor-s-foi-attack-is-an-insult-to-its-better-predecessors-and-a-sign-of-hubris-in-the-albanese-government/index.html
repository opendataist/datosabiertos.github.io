Labor's FOI attack is an insult to its better predecessors — and a sign of hubris in the Albanese government

Labor's FOI attack is an insult to its better predecessors — and a sign of hubris in the Albanese government

The proposed 2025 Freedom of Information Amendment Bill represents a significant regression in government transparency, effectively nullifying previous reforms designed to expand public access. By reintroducing application fees and expanding cabinet exemption categories, the legislation creates substantial financial and procedural barriers. These changes contradict earlier expert reviews that advocated for fee removal and narrower exemptions, suggesting the primary goal is to restrict citizen oversight rather than improve administrative efficiency. Crucially, the bill introduces a subjective public interest test that allows ministers to withhold documents if disclosure might impede internal government deliberations. This provision legitimizes the notion that officials require absolute secrecy to provide frank advice, directly opposing historical consensus that transparency actually strengthens bureaucratic integrity. By prioritizing political convenience over accountability, the changes erode the principle that open governance is essential for a healthy democracy. This legislative shift is vital for open data advocates as it demonstrates how political stability can be exploited to dismantle accountability mechanisms. The move signals a systemic attempt to insulate the government from scrutiny, challenging the core tenets of open data that rely on mandatory disclosure. Understanding this trend is critical for citizens and organizations committed to maintaining accessible, transparent public information systems against increasingly opaque regulatory frameworks.
