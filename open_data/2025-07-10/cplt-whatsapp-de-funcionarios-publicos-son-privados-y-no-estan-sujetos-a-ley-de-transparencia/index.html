CPLT: WhatsApp de funcionarios públicos son privados y no están sujetos a Ley de Transparencia

CPLT: WhatsApp de funcionarios públicos son privados y no están sujetos a Ley de Transparencia

The Transparency Council ruled that WhatsApp messages sent by public officials are private and exempt from transparency laws, prioritizing constitutional privacy rights over immediate public access. This decision sets a significant precedent, shielding digital communications used in municipal administration from standard information requests, thereby limiting the scope of traditional accountability mechanisms for real-time administrative actions. However, the ruling highlights a critical tension between privacy protections and the necessity of formal governance. While acknowledging the legal status of these messages as confidential, the Council emphasized that official instructions must also be recorded through formal, traceable channels. This distinction underscores the importance of maintaining accessible records for citizen oversight, even when private platforms are utilized for informal communication. This case is vital for open data advocates because it exposes the risks of governance shifting to unarchived, private digital tools. It raises urgent questions about how open data frameworks can ensure transparency when significant administrative activities occur off-the-record. The decision challenges policymakers to clarify data retention protocols, ensuring that the digital shift in public service does not create new avenues for opacity and hinder the right to information.
