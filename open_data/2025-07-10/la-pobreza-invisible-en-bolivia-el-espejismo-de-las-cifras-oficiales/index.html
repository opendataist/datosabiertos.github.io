La pobreza invisible en Bolivia: el espejismo de las cifras oficiales

La pobreza invisible en Bolivia: el espejismo de las cifras oficiales

The article argues that official poverty statistics in Bolivia significantly underreport the reality due to outdated measurement lines that fail to account for high inflation. This discrepancy creates a misleading narrative of stability, as many families who have lost their ability to afford basic necessities remain statistically invisible. The core implication is that current data does not reflect the true scale of deprivation, preventing an accurate assessment of the social crisis and undermining the effectiveness of public policy. Beyond income, poverty is revealed as a structural issue rooted in rural and indigenous demographics, high labor informality, and educational deficits. These factors trap populations in cycles of vulnerability where work no longer guarantees escape from poverty. The erosion of the middle class further demonstrates that economic growth has not translated into sustainable security, highlighting that poverty is multidimensional, involving lack of access to health, housing, and social cohesion. This text is crucial for open data because it exposes the limitations of relying solely on aggregate, static metrics. It advocates for the development of dynamic, multidimensional indicators that incorporate real-time cost-of-living adjustments and qualitative social factors. By shifting the focus from mere income thresholds to comprehensive well-being, open data can better capture structural inequalities and support more equitable, evidence-based decision-making.
