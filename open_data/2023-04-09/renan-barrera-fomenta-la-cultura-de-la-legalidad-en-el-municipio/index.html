Renán Barrera fomenta la cultura de la legalidad en el Municipio

Renán Barrera fomenta la cultura de la legalidad en el Municipio

The article highlights Mérida’s commitment to open government by integrating institutional oversight with active citizen participation. The Municipal Council for Anti-Corruption recently reviewed compliance with audit recommendations, emphasizing the critical role of social control committees. These groups, formed by beneficiaries of public works, monitor the use of resources during construction projects, ensuring that public funds are used correctly and that community concerns are addressed before issues arise. A significant advancement in this transparency framework is the establishment of the first National Secretariat for Citizen Participation. This office supports a "Public Consultation" tool designed to gather citizen input on social problems, reflecting a governance model that prioritizes collective problem-solving. By institutionalizing channels for direct community feedback, the administration aims to align municipal actions with the actual needs and priorities of the population, thereby strengthening democratic legitimacy and accountability. This initiative is relevant to open data as it demonstrates the operationalization of transparency portals and participatory digital tools. The public accessibility of project information through geoportals and online surveys illustrates how open data principles can be leveraged to facilitate real-time citizen oversight. It underscores the importance of making government processes visible and accessible, allowing individuals to actively engage in the monitoring of public services and contributing to a culture of integrity and shared responsibility in resource management.
