The "Mi Casa Ya" program continues its operations under a new approach adopted by the current administration, maintaining the evaluation through the Sisbén IV survey to classify households according to their level of vulnerability. This classification determines the amount of the subsidy, prioritizing the most needy sectors by offering higher benefits for lower-income groups. The bureaucratic process requires certification of mortgage credit approval by financial entities to transition from the status of interested party to that of applicant, at which point the state's prioritization criteria are applied for the final allocation. A substantial change in this phase involves calculating subsidies in terms of the current legal minimum wage instead of the Tax Value Unit, providing greater clarity and stability in the receipt of benefits. Additionally, the possibility of combining subsidies has been restored, allowing households to complement state support with resources from other entities, such as compensation funds or local governments. This flexibility aims to maximize housing purchasing capacity for the most vulnerable families, especially those in rural areas or small municipalities that historically had less access to these policies. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the importance of transparency in classification and prioritization based on public data such as Sisbén IV. The publication of the first allocation cuts and vulnerability criteria demonstrates how the availability of structured data enables auditing the equity in the distribution of public resources. Understanding these business rules is essential for citizens and civil society to monitor whether the technical criteria truly benefit the target groups and to promote accountability in the management of social policies.
