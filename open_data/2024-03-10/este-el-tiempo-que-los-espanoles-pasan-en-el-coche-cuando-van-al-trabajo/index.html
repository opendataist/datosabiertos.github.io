Este el tiempo que los españoles pasan en el coche cuando van al trabajo

The analysis of Spanish commuting habits reveals a strong preference for private vehicles over public transport or active mobility options. This trend is primarily driven by geographic distance and household income levels, creating a mobility landscape in which car usage is disproportionately high among wealthier families and those who live farther from their workplaces. As a result, reliance on public transportation or walking is largely confined to lower-income groups, highlighting a structural disparity in access to sustainable and affordable commuting options. Time efficiency and employment status further reinforce these patterns, as full-time workers significantly favor cars despite the longer travel times associated with public transit. The data indicate that personal vehicles offer a perceived advantage in convenience and schedule flexibility, which strongly appeals to the core working-age population. This preference for individual transport modes contributes to increased congestion and fewer opportunities to integrate active lifestyles into daily routines, suggesting that current infrastructure does not adequately support alternative commuting behaviors for the majority of the workforce. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how large-scale national surveys can uncover critical social inequalities and urban planning challenges. By making detailed demographic and mobility statistics publicly accessible, researchers and policymakers can better understand the intersection of economics, geography, and transportation. This transparency encourages evidence-based decision-making aimed at reducing carbon footprints and improving equitable access to mobility resources, demonstrating that open data is essential for diagnosing and addressing complex societal issues.
