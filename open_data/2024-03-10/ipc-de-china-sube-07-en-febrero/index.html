IPC de China sube 0,7% en febrero

China recorded a deceleration in inflation in February, with the Consumer Price Index (CPI) posting a slight rebound after January’s contraction. This shift is primarily attributed to the seasonal surge in demand during the Spring Festival holidays, which drove up prices for food and tourism services. Despite the increase, overall inflation remains at very low levels, reflecting a fragile economic recovery and the persistent lack of strong domestic demand outside of holiday periods. The price structure reveals a significant divergence between food and non-food items. While basic food baskets and leisure activities experienced notable increases, the underlying CPI, which excludes these volatile components, showed minimal growth. This discrepancy suggests that inflationary pressure is superficial and seasonal, without indicating a broad-based trend of rising prices in the real economy, thereby maintaining caution in medium-term consumption expectations. The relevance of this article to the field of open data lies in the critical importance of transparency in official statistics. Public and timely access to macroeconomic indicators such as the CPI and the Producer Price Index (PPI) is essential for researchers, analysts, and citizens to verify economic health, evaluate public policies, and make informed decisions. The publication of these data by the National Bureau of Statistics facilitates accountability and enables the early detection of economic imbalances through independent analysis.
