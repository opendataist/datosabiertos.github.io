It cost £10.6m to run Conwy Council's controversial HQ over five years

Conwy County Council’s headquarters incurred significant running costs, raising concerns about financial efficiency during a period of severe budget cuts. The revelation highlights a critical contradiction: while the council claims to operate "paperless," it still spent substantial amounts on stationary. This expenditure occurred alongside nearly a ten percent council tax increase and significant reductions in services and school funding, causing public distress and forcing institutions to seek loans or make redundancies. The council defends these costs by explaining that the new facility replaced aging, costly-to-repair buildings inherited from previous reorganizations. They argue that leasing the state-of-the-art complex was more economical than repairing old structures or finding alternative space. With the option to buy the building for a nominal fee at the end of the lease, the council maintains that this arrangement was a necessary financial decision to consolidate staff and avoid higher repair or leasing expenses. This article is relevant to open data because it was uncovered through a Freedom of Information request, demonstrating the power of transparency in holding public bodies accountable. It underscores the importance of accessible government spending data, allowing citizens to scrutinize budgetary decisions. By making such detailed financial records available, authorities enable the public to question resource allocation and demand better value for money, especially when austerity measures impact essential services.
