Kidnappers, Bandits, Boko Haram Terrorists, 'Yahoo Boys' May Occupy Government Offices In Nigeria One Day, Says Coalition

A civil society coalition is pursuing legal action against Nigeria’s Code of Conduct Bureau to enforce the public disclosure of assets declared by elected officials. By highlighting the bureau’s consistent disregard for the Freedom of Information Act, the group aims to expose a system lacking adequate checks and balances that allows politicians to engage in corrupt practices undetected. The initiative addresses the alarming trend of bogus asset declarations, where officials list assets they do not yet possess, intending to acquire them with public funds once in office. Without transparency, citizens remain unaware of the true wealth and potential conflicts of interest of their leaders, enabling systemic graft that undermines democratic integrity and national security. This matter is critical to open data advocacy, as it demonstrates how inaccessible public records facilitate corruption. Making asset declarations freely available promotes accountability and allows society to monitor public figures effectively. Consequently, this campaign underscores the vital role of transparent data in fostering good governance and ensuring that information remains a tool for public oversight rather than political privilege.
