Baleares continúa atrapada en el ciclo de la siniestralidad laboral sin fin

The Balearic Islands frequently maintain national leadership in workplace accident rates, a situation that has spurred the creation of specialized institutes and local regulatory frameworks to improve the detection and prevention of risks, particularly in intensive sectors such as hospitality. Although accident rates have declined in recent years, there remains critical concern about the rise in serious accidents alongside a decrease in minor ones, suggesting that the problem has not been comprehensively resolved but rather has evolved in nature. The causes of this high incidence are primarily attributed to the peculiarities of the local economy: temporary employment, a shortage of skilled workers, and work overload stemming from tourism seasonality. These conditions exacerbate physical and psychosocial risks, creating an environment where companies, pressured by demand, often prioritize operational speed over investment in safety, resulting in precarious working conditions and older, more vulnerable workforces. The relevance of this article to open data lies in the need for transparency and public access to workplace accident data. The debate between employer associations and trade unions regarding the effectiveness of preventive measures and the insufficiency of inspections underscores the importance of having open, reliable, and disaggregated data. This enables civil society, researchers, and government authorities to critically audit regulatory compliance, assess the real impact of public policies, and demand accountability based on verifiable evidence, thereby fostering a safer and more informed culture grounded in public information.
