Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022 - Palatinate

Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022 - Palatinate

This article demonstrates the significant value of Freedom of Information requests in promoting transparency within higher education institutions. By securing detailed academic data previously unavailable to the public, student publications can hold universities accountable for grading consistency. This process highlights how open data mechanisms empower students to access granular performance metrics, moving beyond simple rankings to understand the structural factors influencing academic outcomes across different disciplines and year levels. The analysis reveals stark disparities in attainment rates between departments, with physics and English showing high achievement while foundation years and specific language modules lagged significantly. Furthermore, individual module performance varied widely, ranging from courses where no first-class grades were awarded to others with near-universal success. These findings underscore the complexity of academic assessment, suggesting that factors such as assessment type and cohort size heavily impact results, thereby challenging simplistic interpretations of university quality or teaching effectiveness based solely on aggregated grades. Understanding these variations is crucial for open data initiatives in education, as it exposes systemic inequalities and operational inconsistencies that might otherwise remain hidden. When such data is made accessible and interpretable, it fosters a more informed dialogue between students, faculty, and administration. Ultimately, this transparency supports data-driven reforms in curriculum design and assessment strategies, ensuring that educational standards are equitable and that students are fully aware of the factors contributing to their academic performance.
