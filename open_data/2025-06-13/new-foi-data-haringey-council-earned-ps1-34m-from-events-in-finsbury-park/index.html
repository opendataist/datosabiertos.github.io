The article argues that municipal authorities increasingly prioritize commercial revenue over public welfare, effectively treating parks as disposable assets rather than communal trusts. Such exploitation highlights a critical governance failure, suggesting that local councils cannot be trusted to act as responsible custodians. The text implies that protecting public land requires external intervention and potential legislative changes to remove these spaces from municipal control, ensuring they remain accessible and preserved for community use rather than corporate profit. This narrative is vital to open data discussions because it exposes the hidden costs of privatized management and lack of transparency. By highlighting the disparity between official "events season" narratives and the reality of neglected infrastructure, it underscores the need for open access to municipal maintenance records. Such data would enable citizens to hold authorities accountable and advocate for policies that prioritize public trust over commercial interests.
