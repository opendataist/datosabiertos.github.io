Uno de cada cuatro jóvenes europeos vive en riesgo de pobreza o exclusión social - Mundo - ABC Color

Uno de cada cuatro jóvenes europeos vive en riesgo de pobreza o exclusión social - Mundo - ABC Color

The article reveals that youth poverty and social exclusion in Spain have barely decreased, remaining critically high compared to the rest of the European Union. This persistent deprivation highlights a structural failure where young people face significant material hardship, indicating that recent economic conditions have not sufficiently alleviated the vulnerabilities experienced by this demographic over the last five years. Crucially, data shows a stark disconnect between employment status and economic security, as young individuals are ten times more likely to live in poverty despite being employed. This finding underscores that low wages and unpaid internships are driving inequality, effectively preventing social mobility. The narrative emphasizes that working hard no longer guarantees freedom from poverty, challenging the assumption that labor market participation alone resolves economic insecurity for younger generations. This analysis is vital for open data because it demonstrates how granular, publicly available datasets can expose hidden social inequities that aggregate statistics often obscure. By making employment and poverty metrics accessible, researchers and citizens can hold policymakers accountable for regulatory gaps. Transparent data enables the validation of union claims, fostering informed public debate and advocating for legislative reforms that ensure fair compensation and genuine opportunity for all young workers.
