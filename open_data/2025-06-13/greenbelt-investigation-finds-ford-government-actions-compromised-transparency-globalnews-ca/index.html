Greenbelt investigation finds Ford government actions ‘compromised’ transparency | Globalnews.ca

Greenbelt investigation finds Ford government actions ‘compromised’ transparency | Globalnews.ca

The Ontario Information and Privacy Commissioner’s report highlights a significant failure in government transparency during the controversial Greenbelt land removal. By utilizing personal emails and opaque codewords to conduct official business, the Ford government violated legal record-keeping obligations. This systematic obfuscation weakened public accountability and demonstrated a lack of commitment to proper records management, ultimately eroding trust in the integrity of government decision-making processes. The consequences of these poor practices include the permanent loss of critical decision records, leaving fundamental questions about the rationale behind the policy and its potential financial benefits to developers unanswered. The deliberate use of personal devices to bypass access-to-information requests suggests a culture that prioritized hiding actions over maintaining public records. Such behavior not only violates statutory requirements but also impedes the public’s ability to understand how major environmental and policy decisions were made. This case is highly relevant to open data principles because it underscores the necessity of preserving government records in accessible, durable formats. When officials circumvent standard communication channels, they create data voids that hinder transparency and accountability. The report serves as a cautionary tale, emphasizing that robust open data frameworks and strict prohibitions against using private channels for public business are essential for maintaining public trust and ensuring that government actions remain visible and verifiable.
