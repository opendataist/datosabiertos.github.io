Disney y Universal demandan a la empresa de inteligencia artificial Midjourney por violación de derechos de autor

The lawsuit filed by Disney and Universal against Midjourney marks a turning point in the regulation of intellectual property in the digital age. By accusing the artificial intelligence company of generating unauthorized copies of their iconic characters, the studios assert that technology does not exempt one from copyright infringement. This legal action seeks to protect the economic value of human-created content and to affirm that the automatic generation of derivative works without a license constitutes an act of piracy, regardless of the underlying technology. The dispute highlights the tension between the fair use doctrine invoked by AI developers and the rights of original creators. While Midjourney argues that its systems operate similarly to human inspiration, creative industries insist that the scale and automation of AI pose an existential threat to professional creativity. This case, along with parallel litigation, underscores the lack of consensus on how to adapt current laws to the learning capabilities of machines, forcing technology companies to reconsider their data acquisition and licensing strategies. This confrontation is crucial for the open data movement, as it questions the foundations of training models using publicly available information without explicit consent. If intellectual property rights prevail over free access to training data, the availability of high-quality datasets for the development of open-source AI could be restricted. The resolution of this conflict will determine whether future algorithms can rely on shared, accessible cultural knowledge or whether they will be confined to controlled, licensed environments, directly impacting the democratization and transparency of generative technologies.
