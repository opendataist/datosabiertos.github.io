Un futuro sostenible necesita transparencia y datos confiables

Un futuro sostenible necesita transparencia y datos confiables

The article argues that the discrepancy between official data and government discourse erodes public trust and hinders the development of effective public policies. By contrasting official claims with verifiable statistics, it becomes evident that the nation’s socioeconomic reality presents more complex challenges than those communicated, particularly in critical areas such as education and the economy. This lack of accuracy is not a minor detail but a direct obstacle to building tangible solutions that address the population’s actual needs. In the social and economic sphere, the analysis reveals that, despite certain positive macroeconomic indicators, purchasing power has declined and poverty disproportionately affects women. Structural problems persist, including high levels of labor informality and a deterioration in social progress that is not clearly addressed. Transparency in these data is essential to understanding the true magnitude of existing gaps and to designing strategies that prioritize inclusion and equity, rather than masking urgent problems with optimistic but unfounded narratives. The relevance for open data advocates lies in the urgent need to access truthful and accessible official information to exercise rigorous democratic oversight. Without accurate and comparable data, civil society cannot act as an effective watchdog or demand accountability. Therefore, ensuring the transparency and accuracy of public statistics is a fundamental pillar of governance, enabling state decisions to be based on real evidence rather than statistical fiction, which is vital for an inclusive future.
