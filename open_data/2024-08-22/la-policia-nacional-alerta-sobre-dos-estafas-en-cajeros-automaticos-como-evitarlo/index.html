La Policía Nacional alerta sobre dos estafas en cajeros automáticos: cómo evitarlo

La Policía Nacional alerta sobre dos estafas en cajeros automáticos: cómo evitarlo

Fraudsters frequently exploit automated teller machines (ATMs) using techniques such as skimming and the “ruler scam” to steal banking data and block cash withdrawals. By installing covert devices to copy card information and PINs, or by physically obstructing bill dispensers, criminals compromise user security. This article highlights recent police alerts in Spain, emphasizing that these illicit methods remain a persistent threat to financial safety in public spaces. The narrative focuses on the critical need for vigilance when using ATMs outside bank premises. Users are advised to prefer secure in-branch machines, inspect equipment for signs of tampering, and protect their PINs from observation. These precautions are essential to prevent unauthorized access to personal funds and data, illustrating the broader challenge of maintaining integrity in digital financial interactions amidst evolving criminal tactics. This topic is relevant to open data because it underscores the vulnerability of sensitive personal information collected during routine transactions. Understanding how data is intercepted helps users appreciate the importance of data protection principles and secure infrastructure. As banking systems increasingly rely on digital channels, ensuring that data remains confidential and intact against physical and digital intrusions is fundamental to preserving trust in open and accessible financial services.
