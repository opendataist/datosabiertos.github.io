Argentina: 2,3 millones de jóvenes de entre 25 y 35 años no pueden acceder a una vivienda

The persistent difficulty for Argentine youth to achieve independence, which has remained high for two decades, reveals a structural housing crisis. This stagnation highlights an unmet latent demand for housing, as low incomes prevent young people from living independently. Consequently, the construction sector fails to recognize this demographic as viable consumers, perpetuating the shortage and limiting the market’s response to actual societal needs. Regional disparities further illustrate the economic divides affecting independence. Northern provinces suffer from significantly lower emancipation rates due to income constraints, whereas Buenos Aires sees higher rates due to better wages and a developed rental market. These contrasts demonstrate how local economic contexts directly influence housing accessibility and youth autonomy across the country. This article is relevant to open data because it relies on public statistical sources, such as the EPH (Permanent Household Survey), to uncover social inequalities. By analyzing accessible government data, civil society can identify systemic issues in housing and labor markets. This transparency empowers policymakers and researchers to track trends over time, fostering evidence-based discussions on how to address the widespread inability of young people to achieve independent living.
