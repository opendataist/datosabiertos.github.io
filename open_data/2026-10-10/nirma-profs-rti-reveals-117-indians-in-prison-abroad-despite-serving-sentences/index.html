Nirma prof’s RTI reveals 117 Indians in prison abroad despite serving sentences

Nirma prof’s RTI reveals 117 Indians in prison abroad despite serving sentences

A Right to Information request exposed a significant human rights crisis wherein numerous Indian nationals remain imprisoned in foreign jurisdictions long after serving their legal sentences. The Ministry of External Affairs confirmed that a substantial number of individuals are languishing in countries like Bangladesh and Saudi Arabia due to administrative delays or other issues, despite having completed their respective terms. This situation highlights systemic failures in diplomatic consular services and international legal coordination. The investigation underscores the urgent need for the government to intervene and resolve these cases, preventing further hardship for citizens who have already faced punishment. Activists argue that this continued incarceration violates fundamental human rights and international conventions, emphasizing that the state must ensure humane treatment and timely repatriation. The lack of immediate resolution threatens the safety and dignity of Indians abroad, prompting calls for higher-level legal action and policy reform. This case is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency initiatives can uncover critical governance failures. By accessing specific government records, civil society actors can identify gaps in public service delivery and hold authorities accountable. It illustrates the power of data-driven advocacy to bring hidden injustices to light, encouraging better governance and ensuring that citizens' rights are protected through accessible information and rigorous oversight.
