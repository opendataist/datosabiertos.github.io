La inflación subió en enero dos décimas, hasta el 5,9%, ¿a qué se debe?

Spanish inflation rose to 5.9 percent in January, halting a five-month decline driven primarily by higher fuel costs. Although general prices decreased, underlying inflation reached its highest level since late 1986, highlighting persistent pressures despite government measures reducing electricity and transport expenses. The divergence between headline and underlying inflation indicates that moderation in energy and food prices has not fully stabilized the broader economy. While specific sectors such as clothing and fuel saw price increases, subsidies successfully lowered costs for essential utilities, suggesting a complex economic landscape where temporary policy interventions mask deeper structural price trends. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it exemplifies the importance of transparent, high-frequency economic indicators. Accurate public statistics allow researchers and citizens to verify government claims, understand real purchasing power, and analyze the effectiveness of fiscal policies through accessible, standardized datasets.
