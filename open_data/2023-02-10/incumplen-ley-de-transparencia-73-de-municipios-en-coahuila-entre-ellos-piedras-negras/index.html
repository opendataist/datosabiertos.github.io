Incumplen Ley de Transparencia 73% de municipios en Coahuila, entre ellos Piedras Negras

Over seventy percent of municipalities in Coahuila were publicly cited in the state legislature for failing to publish their financial management reports, highlighting systemic non-compliance with transparency obligations. The legislative body urged these local governments to immediately fulfill their legal duty to disclose fiscal progress, emphasizing that adherence to reporting timelines is mandatory rather than optional. This situation underscores the critical importance of enforceable accountability mechanisms within local governance structures. By publicly identifying non-compliant entities, the legislature aims to reinforce institutional integrity and ensure that public funds are managed transparently, setting a precedent for future oversight and reducing the risk of unauthorized or unmonitored expenditures. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how public disclosure mandates serve as the foundation for accessible government information. It illustrates the gap between legal requirements and actual practice, reinforcing the need for robust digital platforms and automated reporting systems to guarantee that financial data is not only legally mandated but also readily available and verifiable by citizens and researchers.
