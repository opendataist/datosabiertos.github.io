The Venezuelan Electoral Council confirmed Nicolás Maduro’s victory, attributing delays in releasing detailed vote tallies to alleged massive cyberattacks. This official narrative contrasts sharply with opposition claims that Edmundo González Urrutia won by a wide margin, supported by published voting records and international recognition. The government rejects these allegations, framing the unrest as an attempted coup rather than a legitimate democratic dispute. This dispute highlights critical vulnerabilities in open data governance during electoral processes. When official institutions withhold granular datasets such as individual voting records, it undermines transparency and prevents independent verification. The reliance on partial summaries fosters deep public distrust and facilitates conflicting narratives, demonstrating how opacity can exacerbate political polarization and conflict. The article underscores that open data is essential for electoral legitimacy. Without accessible, verifiable, and complete datasets, citizens and international observers cannot validate outcomes, leading to contested results and social instability. Ensuring full data availability is not merely a technical requirement but a fundamental prerequisite for democratic accountability and conflict prevention in polarized environments.
