AI companies to music labels: scraping copyrighted tracks on the internet to train algorithms is "fair use"

Major record labels have sued AI startups Udio and Suno for copyright infringement, accusing them of illegally scraping copyrighted music to train their generative models. While the companies admit to using this data, they argue their actions constitute fair use. They contend that training algorithms to recognize genres and styles is a transformative process that creates new, original content rather than reproducing protected expressions, thereby avoiding direct competition with existing recordings. The music industry strongly rejects this defense, asserting that industrial-scale scraping infringes on artists' rights and constitutes unfair competition. Labels argue that consent is required before using creative works, emphasizing that open availability on the internet does not negate copyright protection. This legal conflict highlights a critical tension between rapid technological innovation and established intellectual property laws, as industry leaders seek legislative safeguards to regulate how copyrighted materials are utilized in AI development. This dispute is vital to the open data community because it challenges the assumption that publicly accessible online content is free for unrestricted computational use. The outcome will determine whether "open" data implies permission for commercial AI training or if it remains bound by traditional copyright frameworks. Establishing clear boundaries here will define the legal landscape for future open data projects, potentially restricting access to valuable datasets if courts rule that AI training does not qualify as fair use.
