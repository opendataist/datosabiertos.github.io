El PS 63: cero derechos de acceso a la información pública - Claridad

El PS 63: cero derechos de acceso a la información pública - Claridad

The legislative approval of Senate Project 63 in Puerto Rico represents a critical setback for government transparency, effectively weakening constitutional rights to public information. By extending response times for agencies and introducing complex notification requirements, the measure significantly hinders citizens' ability to access data. This legislative shift suggests a systemic effort to obscure governmental activities rather than facilitate openness, raising serious concerns about accountability and the potential for hiding corruption behind bureaucratic delays. The speed and manner in which the bill was passed, despite widespread opposition from journalism groups, legal associations, and civil rights organizations, highlight a disregard for democratic deliberation. Stakeholders argue that the rushed process and limited public input were strategic moves to prevent meaningful scrutiny of the bill’s restrictive clauses. The consensus among critics is that the proposed limitations fundamentally violate the spirit of open governance, making it practically difficult for individuals to exercise their right to information. This article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how legal frameworks can actively restrict data accessibility and interoperability through legislative manipulation. For open data advocates, PS 63 serves as a cautionary example of how opaque processes can undermine trust and limit the utility of public information. Understanding such legislative threats is essential for protecting the integrity of open data ecosystems and ensuring that digital transparency tools are not eroded by political decisions aimed at reducing government accountability.
