EE UU aprueba desclasificar los papeles de Epstein: ¿qué contiene la ley y qué incógnitas abre?

EE UU aprueba desclasificar los papeles de Epstein: ¿qué contiene la ley y qué incógnitas abre?

The unanimous approval of the Epstein Files Transparency Act by the U.S. Congress marks a milestone in the fight against impunity, legally requiring the Department of Justice to declassify documents linked to the billionaire’s sex exploitation network. This step responds to intense social and political pressure, overcoming historical administrative resistance and reflecting a moral obligation to victims that institutions can no longer ignore. However, the central challenge lies in the implementation of the law, not merely its legislative approval. There is a significant risk that the Department of Justice will use procedural arguments or ongoing investigations to delay or selectively filter the information, particularly under the political influence of the current administration. The key question is whether the declassification will be comprehensive or limited to protect influential elites, which will determine whether true justice is achieved or institutional cover-ups persist. This case is crucial for the open data movement because it illustrates the power of access to public information as a tool for accountability. It demonstrates how legal transparency can force the disclosure of state secrets and corrupt practices, although its effectiveness depends on genuine political will to share complete data. For the open data community, the Epstein case serves as a study in the friction between the right to know and the powerful interests that conceal the truth, underscoring the need for robust mechanisms to ensure the full and accessible disclosure of sensitive documents.
