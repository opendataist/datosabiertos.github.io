The capital city of Chihuahua has launched Mexico’s first Local Action Plan for Open Government, co-created using an internationally adapted methodology. This milestone institutionalizes the participation of multiple stakeholders, including citizens and academia, in the design of public policies. The core commitments address security, support for entrepreneurs, and the rural sector, setting a new standard for municipalities nationwide by prioritizing inclusion in decision-making. The relevance of this article to open data lies in its focus on transparency and accountability as fundamental pillars. By ensuring that government is accessible and inclusive, it creates an environment where public information must be clear, verifiable, and usable for civil society. The co-creation of the plan implies that data and decisions are not exclusive to the state, but rather the result of an open dialogue that requires access to information to be effective. Finally, the plan is integrated into the global network of the Open Government Partnership, enabling the exchange of experiences and lessons learned with other local governments. This continuous monitoring ensures the real implementation of commitments, transforming the promise of openness into tangible results. Chihuahua thus positions itself as a vital case study, demonstrating how open data structures and intersectoral collaboration can enhance competitiveness and address complex public problems innovatively.
