En 7 departamentos la economía creció, menos en Tarija y Potosí

The article highlights a widespread economic slowdown in Bolivia, where GDP growth has cooled significantly following a post-pandemic rebound. Experts warn that this trend is critical, particularly in departments such as Tarija and Potosí, which are experiencing deep economic contractions. The stark disparity between national and local performance suggests that the purported economic recovery has not been uniform, leaving several regions in a persistent recessionary process. The decline in Tarija is closely linked to a drop in hydrocarbon activity and deterioration in other key sectors such as agriculture and construction. This historical correlation between the decline in gas production and regional GDP exposes the vulnerability of departmental economies dependent on natural resources. The analysis indicates that previous positive figures were the result of temporary public spending policies rather than genuine structural improvement, explaining the rapid return to contraction. This information is relevant to open data because it underscores the need for transparency and access to disaggregated statistical data. By visualizing how national figures mask local crises, the importance of analyzing open data at the subnational level to detect regional inequalities is demonstrated. Access to accurate information enables citizens and experts to question broad economic narratives and demand public policies better tailored to the specific realities of each department, fostering evidence-based governance grounded in detailed data.
