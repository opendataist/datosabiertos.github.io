when a wall isn't a wall.......

when a wall isn't a wall.......

The Australian government has refused to disclose the rationale behind its rejection of reports labeling Israel as an apartheid state, citing that processing such requests would unreasonably divert departmental resources. This refusal, following a Freedom of Information application, suggests a systematic lack of transparency regarding how foreign policy decisions on human rights are justified. By withholding the basis for this significant diplomatic stance, the state denies citizens the evidence needed to evaluate their government's adherence to international legal standards and moral obligations. This case highlights the fragility of freedom of information laws when balanced against administrative convenience rather than public interest. The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade’s repeated reliance on resource constraints to avoid scrutiny indicates a potential misuse of FOI provisions to obscure politically sensitive matters. Such practices undermine democratic accountability, as they prevent civil society and the media from challenging official narratives with factual documentation, thereby weakening public discourse on critical geopolitical issues. The article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how bureaucratic opacity can actively suppress transparency in areas of high public concern. It demonstrates that without robust enforcement mechanisms for information access, governments can indefinitely delay or deny the publication of data that holds them accountable. This incident serves as a cautionary example for open data advocates, showing that legal frameworks alone are insufficient without rigorous oversight to ensure that the right to information is not arbitrarily overridden by administrative excuses.
