Open Data
ES EN

Panama has launched its twelfth national census and eighth housing census, marking a significant leap in digital data collection by employing mobile devices for ninety percent of the coverage. This modernization effort aims to replace traditional paper methods with real-time GPS tracking and digital questionnaires, demonstrating a commitment to technological innovation that has attracted international observers from neighboring countries. The primary implication is a shift toward more efficient, accurate, and timely demographic analysis, with preliminary results expected mid-year and final data by September. Transparency and public accountability are central pillars of this initiative, with authorities dedicating specific platforms to track every dollar spent on the project. Citizens are empowered to verify the identity of census takers and monitor budget execution, fostering trust in the statistical process. This openness ensures that public funds are used effectively and that the resulting data serves the community rather than just bureaucratic interests, reinforcing the link between government spending and civic oversight. This article is highly relevant to open data because it exemplifies how robust data infrastructure must be paired with transparency mechanisms to maintain public trust. By making budget details accessible and allowing citizen verification of field agents, Panama sets a precedent for how large-scale data collection projects can operate with integrity. The initiative highlights that true open data involves not just the release of statistics, but also the openness of the processes and financial transactions that generate them, ensuring that demographic insights can be used to improve public policies in health and transport.
