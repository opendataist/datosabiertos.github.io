Saltillo; se encarecen garrafones de agua, frituras y galletas

Recent data from Mexico’s statistical institute highlight that the prices of basic basket items have surged by up to 53 percent over the past two years. This trend is driven by rising raw material and logistics costs, prompting businesses to adjust prices at the start of the year. Local observations in cities such as Saltillo confirm these national shifts, with essential goods like water, canned foods, and cereals becoming significantly more expensive for consumers. Residents report that wages have not kept pace with inflation, making it increasingly difficult to afford basic necessities. The shift toward selling produce by the piece further burdens household budgets, as convenience stores capitalize on the higher costs associated with preparing food at home. This economic pressure forces families to rethink their purchasing habits, often opting for cheaper, pre-packaged alternatives despite their long-term unhealthiness or lower quality. This article is relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the critical need for accessible, high-quality statistical data to monitor inflation and cost-of-living changes. By providing transparent metrics on price fluctuations, open datasets empower citizens, researchers, and policymakers to verify official reports, understand local economic realities, and advocate for informed policy decisions that address systemic inflation and support vulnerable populations.
