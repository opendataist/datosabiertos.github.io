Necesario hacer pública la estrategia de ciberdefensa

The National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) ordered the Mexican Navy to publish a public version of its joint cyberdefense strategy, rejecting the initial five-year confidentiality reservation justified on national security grounds. This decision underscores the tension between state secrecy and the principle of transparency, demonstrating that not all military strategic documents warrant complete opacity. The authority determined that fundamental sections, specifically the introduction and legal framework, contain no sensitive operational data that could threaten security if disclosed. This distinction allows citizens to understand the strategic context and legal basis of cyberdefense efforts without exposing classified tactics or vulnerabilities to potential threats. This case is highly relevant to open data, as it establishes a precedent for partial transparency in government security strategies. It demonstrates that institutional frameworks can be shared openly to foster public trust and accountability, provided that truly sensitive operational details remain protected, thereby balancing security needs with the right to access public information.
