Restricting Right To Information Only To Citizens Contrary To Constitution, RTI Act; No Absolute Bar On Disclosure To Foreigners: Delhi High Court

The Delhi High Court has clarified that the Right to Information Act does not strictly limit information access to Indian citizens. By interpreting Section 3 as a positive recognition of rights for citizens rather than a prohibition against non-citizens, the court emphasized that the spirit of transparency in public governance applies broadly. This ruling ensures that individuals seeking information related to their life, liberty, or civil status can access public records, provided it aligns with constitutional guarantees. This judgment is crucial for open data advocacy because it dismantles artificial barriers to information, promoting greater accountability and transparency in public authorities. It establishes that excluding non-citizens from accessing data relevant to their rights would contradict constitutional principles. By allowing authorities discretion while mandating disclosure where rights are implicated, the decision reinforces the idea that open data should serve all individuals interacting with the state, thereby enhancing the integrity and inclusivity of government information systems. Ultimately, the ruling serves as a vital precedent for ensuring that open data initiatives remain universally accessible. It protects against discriminatory practices that might arbitrarily withhold information from specific groups, including foreigners or stateless persons. This broad interpretation supports the fundamental premise that transparency is a public good, essential for holding power accountable, regardless of the applicant's citizenship status, thus strengthening the legal framework for information access.
