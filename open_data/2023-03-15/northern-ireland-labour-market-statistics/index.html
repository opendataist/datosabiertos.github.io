Northern Ireland Labour Market Statistics

Northern Ireland’s labour market demonstrates significant recovery, characterized by record-high employee numbers and returning stability in unemployment rates. Employment metrics have improved substantially over the past year, with the unemployment rate reverting to pre-pandemic levels and the employment rate seeing statistically significant growth. Concurrently, economic inactivity has decreased notably, while total hours worked have rebounded, indicating a broad-based strengthening of the workforce participation and overall labour health. Beyond employment volume, financial indicators reflect positive momentum, with both payrolled employee numbers and earnings increasing over the year. This growth suggests rising confidence among businesses and improved compensation structures for workers. The Quarterly Employment Survey highlights that these gains are largely driven by the services sector, although construction, manufacturing, and other industries also contributed to the upward trend in job creation, signaling a diversified and robust economic expansion. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it exemplifies the value of transparent, multi-source statistical reporting. By integrating administrative records like payroll data with survey-based estimates, authorities provide a comprehensive, real-time view of economic health. Such openness allows researchers, policymakers, and the public to verify trends, understand regional economic shifts, and make informed decisions based on reliable, publicly accessible government statistics.
