La inflación de febrero fue del 6,6 % y llegó a 102,5 % en un año

Year-over-year inflation reached a historic 102.5%, the highest level in more than three decades, significantly surpassing both official and private estimates. This figure breaks the government’s expectation of an early slowdown, demonstrating that monetary control targets are far from being achieved in the short term. The persistence of inflationary inertia suggests that the adjustment of lagged prices and the lack of expectation anchors are generating a dynamic that runs counter to economic stabilization objectives. The core component accelerated notably, indicating that price pressures are not limited to seasonal or specific cyclical factors. The cost structure reflects a widespread correction, with key sectors such as food, energy, and services experiencing significant price increases that erode purchasing power. This situation complicates the viability of official projections, as the observed variations require considerably lower monthly inflation rates to meet annual commitments, which appears unlikely given the current trajectory. This report is relevant to open data because it underscores the importance of transparency and accessibility in official statistical information. When official data diverge from market expectations or perceived reality, rapid, complete, and verifiable access to economic indicators enables civil society, academics, and the media to conduct real-time critical analyses. The quality and clarity of data published by institutions such as INDEC are fundamental to fostering accountability, facilitating public understanding of economic crises, and enabling the generation of independent knowledge that complements or challenges the government’s narrative.
