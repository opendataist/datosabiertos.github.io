CEGAIP debe sancionar a Ayuntamientos opacos

The article highlights a critical gap in local transparency, as most municipalities fail to comply with information laws, often citing technical limitations. It concludes that the oversight body must strictly sanction non-compliant town councils while simultaneously providing the necessary technical support and training to help them overcome these barriers. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the gap between legal frameworks and actual data accessibility. It emphasizes that sustainable open data ecosystems require not just enforcement but also capacity building for local governments. Without adequate resources and accountability, the fundamental right to access public spending information remains unfulfilled for citizens. The proposed reforms aim to enforce stricter response times, ensuring that data availability becomes a tangible reality rather than a legal formality. This narrative stresses that effective open data governance relies on balancing regulatory pressure with institutional support. Ultimately, it calls for a systemic approach to guarantee that public information is not only published but also accessible, understandable, and regularly updated for public scrutiny.
