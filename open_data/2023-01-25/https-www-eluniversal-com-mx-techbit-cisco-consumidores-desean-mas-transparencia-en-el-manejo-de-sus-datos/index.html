Open Data
ES EN

Data privacy has evolved from being merely a legal requirement to becoming a strategic imperative and a profitable investment for organizations. In the post-pandemic era, companies recognize that protecting personal information builds trust, loyalty, and operational efficiency, delivering significant returns. However, a critical gap persists between corporate priorities, which focus on regulatory compliance, and consumer expectations, which demand genuine transparency regarding how their data is used. This misalignment is exacerbated by the advancement of technologies such as artificial intelligence, where users demand control over how their personal data influences decision-making. Consumers value the ability to choose and understand how their information is handled more than the mere geographic location of servers, even though many still prefer local providers due to perceived security. The true need is to build trust through transparency, enabling users to actively decide what happens with their data. This article is fundamental to the open data movement because it redefines the relationship with citizens: data openness is not valid without user protection and clear consent. Transparency is not an obstacle to innovation but its foundation. For open data to be truly useful and ethical, institutions must prioritize security and clarity regarding the use of information, aligning their processes with the genuine demands for data sovereignty and privacy.
