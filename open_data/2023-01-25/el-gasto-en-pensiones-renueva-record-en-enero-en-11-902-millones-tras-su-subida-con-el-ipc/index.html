El gasto en pensiones renueva récord en enero en 11.902 millones tras su subida con el IPC

The article highlights a historic milestone in Spain’s social security system, where pension expenditures have surpassed 11,000 million euros for the first time, driven by an 8.5% revaluation aligned with inflation. This increase ensures that pensioners maintain their purchasing power, resulting in a higher average monthly pension. While total spending has risen, it remains a lower percentage of GDP compared to previous years, suggesting a stabilization of fiscal pressure despite the higher nominal amounts paid to beneficiaries. A significant focus of the new data is the implementation of gender equality measures within the pension framework. Specifically, nearly 410,000 pensions now receive a gender gap complement, which primarily benefits women to correct disparities in pension amounts. This mechanism provides a fixed monthly amount per child, aiming to mitigate the long-term economic impact of caregiving responsibilities that often reduce women’s lifetime earnings and, consequently, their retirement benefits. The data illustrates how policy adjustments are directly influencing individual household income structures. This information is crucial for open data because it demonstrates the transparency of government spending and the effectiveness of social policies. By publishing detailed, granular datasets on pension types, amounts, and specific complements, the government enables independent verification of fiscal health and social equity metrics. This accessibility allows researchers and citizens to analyze the real-world impact of legislative reforms, fostering accountability and informed public debate regarding the sustainability of social welfare.
