Envejecer, un reto que se afronta en soledad; Se desarrolla Saltillo… Pero adultos quedan atrás | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

Saltillo is confronting a rapidly aging population, yet its infrastructure and social services remain largely unprepared to meet their needs. The city lacks accessible transportation, safe pedestrian pathways, and inclusive design, while family structures are fragmenting due to industrialization and migration. This shift threatens social cohesion, as older adults increasingly face isolation and inadequate support systems within a changing urban landscape. A significant portion of seniors lacks pensions, forcing many into precarious informal labor without social security. Experts warn that improvisation is no longer viable; instead, systemic organization is required to ensure dignity in later life. The current neglect of this demographic exacerbates risks such as abandonment and economic vulnerability, highlighting the urgent need for structural reforms that address both immediate survival and long-term quality of life. This article is relevant to open data because transparent, granular datasets on demographics, urban accessibility, and pension coverage are essential for evidence-based policy. Publicly available information can drive the development of age-friendly urban planning and targeted social programs. Without accessible data, governments cannot accurately measure the extent of exclusion or effectively allocate resources to create inclusive, sustainable communities for aging populations.
