No poner en duda el derecho de acceso a la información pública

No poner en duda el derecho de acceso a la información pública

The article highlights a concerning pattern of opacity within the Santiago Peña administration, specifically regarding the delayed release of an intervention report for the Asunción Municipal Government. Initially, the President refused to transmit the document to the Chamber of Deputies as legally mandated, revealing a deliberate intent to withhold information. This resistance was only overcome after intense pressure from civil society, the media, and political opponents, underscoring a weak commitment to institutional transparency and raising fears of a pact of impunity for the former mayor. The core findings of the report reveal severe administrative mismanagement, noting that the vast majority of infrastructure funds were misused for current expenses rather than public works. The investigation concludes that the municipality has drifted dangerously close to "anomia," lacking both governance standards and legal compliance. These details illustrate the tangible consequences of poor management and the urgent need for accountability, as the lack of transparency prevents citizens from fully understanding the extent of the fiscal and administrative failure. This incident is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how obstructing access to public information directly undermines democratic health and the rule of law. It validates the importance of laws guaranteeing citizen access to government data, showing that when institutions fail to proactively share information, they erode public trust. True transparency is not merely a legal formality but a fundamental prerequisite for reconstructing faith in public institutions and ensuring that governance remains accountable to the people.
