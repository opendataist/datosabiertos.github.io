Diario HOY | Argentina divulga datos provisionales del Censo 2022

The United States has established a new international deportation architecture through financial agreements with developing countries, allocating hundreds of millions of dollars to accept non-deportable migrants in their countries of origin. This strategy shifts the responsibility for migration management to nations in Africa, Latin America, and the Pacific, where individuals are often held in a legal limbo, lacking prior ties or clear rights, exposing them to precarious conditions and a lack of due process. The implementation of this system has been facilitated by a specialized office within the State Department that coordinates direct payments to foreign governments and UN agencies. These funds, intended to facilitate return and strengthen local asylum systems, have drawn significant criticism from international organizations and human rights advocates, who question the ethics of commercializing the acceptance of deportees and the potential exploitation of vulnerabilities in countries with fragile economies. This case is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical need for transparency in government financial flows and complex bilateral agreements. The exposure of these mechanisms through investigative journalism demonstrates how access to declassified data, contracts, and expenditure figures enables civil society and academics to audit controversial public policies, ensuring that migration decision-making is verifiable, accessible, and subject to public scrutiny.
