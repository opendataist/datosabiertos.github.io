Ourol pierde dos concejales al morir un vecino el 1 de enero, último día oficial de cómputo, y bajar a 999 habitantes

The article illustrates how precise population data directly dictates the political and financial structure of Spanish municipalities. It highlights two specific cases, Ourol and O Valadouro, which are set to lose council seats because their registered populations dropped below critical thresholds of 1,000 and 2,000 residents, respectively. These reductions are not merely administrative adjustments but result from significant demographic shifts, including depopulation in rural Galicia and specific individual census changes that pushed these towns below the required limits. The implications of these population declines extend far beyond a smaller legislative body. Since the size of the municipal council influences state funding allocation, losing representatives leads to a substantial decrease in financial resources for these local governments. This scenario exemplifies the severe challenges faced by rural areas undergoing depopulation, where administrative capacity and economic stability are compromised simultaneously, creating a difficult cycle for communities already struggling to maintain their services and infrastructure. This case is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the tangible, real-world consequences of public statistics. Official population registers, once published by national institutes, serve as the definitive basis for electoral laws, budget distribution, and governance structures. The story underscores the importance of data accuracy and transparency, showing how public datasets directly shape political representation and resource allocation, thereby validating the critical role of open data in ensuring equitable and informed public administration.
