Aumenta el desempleo femenino en Perú y perjudica sobre todo a las mujeres más preparadas

Aumenta el desempleo femenino en Perú y perjudica sobre todo a las mujeres más preparadas

Peru is experiencing growing labor market inequality, as female unemployment rates have risen significantly while male rates have declined. This widening gap indicates that women, particularly those with university degrees and younger demographics, face increasing barriers to employment despite their qualifications. The trend highlights a systemic failure to integrate educated women into the workforce, suggesting structural issues that go beyond general economic cycles. The data reveal a paradox in which higher education does not guarantee employment for women, contrasting with improvements observed for other educational groups. This disparity underscores the urgent need to address specific labor market barriers affecting female professionals. Understanding these nuanced trends is essential for developing targeted policies that promote equitable economic participation and reduce gender-based disparities in employment opportunities. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical value of granular, disaggregated statistics in exposing hidden social inequalities. By analyzing data by gender, age, and education level, policymakers can identify specific vulnerable groups rather than relying on aggregate national averages. Open access to such detailed datasets empowers researchers and civil society to advocate for evidence-based reforms, ensuring that labor policies effectively address the root causes of gender inequality in the workforce.
