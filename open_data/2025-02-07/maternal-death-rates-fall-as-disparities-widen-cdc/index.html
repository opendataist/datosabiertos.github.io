Maternal death rates fall as disparities widen: CDC

Recent CDC data reveals a national decline in maternal mortality, driven by significant improvements for white and Hispanic women. However, this progress starkly contrasts with a rise in deaths among Black mothers, highlighting persistent racial disparities in healthcare outcomes. This divergence underscores how systemic racism and unequal access to care continue to disproportionately endanger Black women. The data suggests that structural inequities remain a critical barrier to equitable health results, despite overall improvements in the general population. For open data advocates, these statistics illustrate the vital importance of disaggregated data analysis. Breaking down aggregated health metrics by race is essential to exposing hidden inequalities and holding systems accountable, ensuring that policy interventions target the specific communities most at risk rather than masking disparities behind average improvements.
