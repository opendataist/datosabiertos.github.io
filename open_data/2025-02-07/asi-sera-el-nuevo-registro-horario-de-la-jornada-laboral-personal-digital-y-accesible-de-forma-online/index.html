Así será el nuevo registro horario de la jornada laboral: personal, digital y accesible de forma online

Así será el nuevo registro horario de la jornada laboral: personal, digital y accesible de forma online

The Spanish government’s decision to reduce the standard workweek to 37.5 hours represents a significant shift in labor rights, aiming to resolve longstanding tensions between the economic and labor ministries. This move not only shortens working time but also introduces stricter regulations on how working hours are tracked, addressing the critical issue of unpaid overtime and ensuring greater transparency in employment conditions. Central to this reform is the mandatory digital and objective recording of daily work hours, including breaks, which must be verified by the employee to ensure authenticity. This system requires high interoperability, allowing immediate remote access for workers, unions, and labor inspectors while imposing strict documentation duties on employers. By standardizing these records across all contract types, the law aims to eliminate ambiguities and guarantee that working time is accurately accounted for in real time. This legislation is highly relevant to open data principles, as it mandates that labor records be kept in accessible, machine-readable formats compatible with various systems. By ensuring these data points are immediately available to authorities and employees, the policy promotes transparency and accountability. It transforms private employment data into a tool for public oversight, setting a precedent for how sensitive workplace metrics can be structured to support legal compliance and social equity without compromising individual traceability.
