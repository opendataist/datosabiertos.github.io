«Estamos viendo niñas de 9 y 10 años que debutan con trastornos alimentarios por el TikTok»

The article highlights a critical public health concern in the Balearic Islands regarding the early introduction of smartphones to children, which has led to a surge in severe mental health issues such as eating disorders and hypersexualization. In response, the movement 'Adolescència sense mòbils' has successfully pressured local government bodies to create a pilot program for mobile-free school zones and an official registry of school policies. This initiative underscores the urgent need for coordinated regulatory action to protect minors from the detrimental psychological impacts of unchecked digital exposure. Experts warn that continuous exposure to short-form video platforms like TikTok is distorting body image and accelerating developmental issues at increasingly younger ages. The constant visual stimulation disrupts attention spans, contributing to academic difficulties, while premature access to inappropriate content normalizes hypersexual behavior. These findings suggest that the current digital environment is fundamentally altering cognitive and social development, necessitating a cultural shift where mobile ownership becomes the exception rather than the norm. This situation is highly relevant to the open data movement because the current lack of a centralized, updated database on smartphone usage policies in schools creates a significant transparency gap. Without standardized, publicly accessible data, it is impossible to effectively monitor trends, compare interventions, or hold institutions accountable. Establishing open data standards for these registries would enable researchers, parents, and policymakers to analyze the correlation between device access and student well-being, driving evidence-based solutions and ensuring that communities have the information needed to implement protective measures effectively.
