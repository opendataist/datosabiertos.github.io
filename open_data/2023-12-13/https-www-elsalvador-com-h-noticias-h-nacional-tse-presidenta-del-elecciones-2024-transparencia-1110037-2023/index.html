The article highlights a significant credibility gap between the Electoral Supreme Tribunal’s assertions of transparency and experts’ observations of systemic opacity. Specialists point to the non-public disclosure of meeting minutes, agendas, and hiring processes since 2021, arguing that these actions contradict the institution’s claims of democratic participation. This lack of accessible information undermines public trust and prevents meaningful external verification of electoral integrity. Furthermore, legal experts question the Tribunal’s independence, particularly regarding its controversial validation of candidacies despite constitutional prohibitions. Critics argue that such decisions reveal a bias favoring specific political figures rather than an impartial application of the law. These concerns suggest that the Tribunal prioritizes political alignment over strict adherence to legal frameworks, raising serious doubts about its role as a neutral arbiter. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how withholding essential public records erodes accountability in democratic processes. If electoral bodies do not proactively release data on decisions and administrative actions, citizens cannot effectively monitor governance. True transparency requires not just claims of openness, but the actual availability of verifiable, granular data that allows for independent scrutiny and fosters genuine public confidence.
