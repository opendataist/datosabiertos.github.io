Tras una intensa investigación de un comité secreto, Israel declaró muertos a un número de rehenes en Gaza

The article reveals the creation of a confidential medical committee in Israel tasked with determining the fate of hostages kidnapped by Hamas in Gaza. In the absence of bodies or physical evidence, these experts have developed protocols based on the analysis of videos and testimonies to infer death. This determination has profound legal and social implications, enabling families to achieve the closure they need and allowing widows to remarry, thereby avoiding the religious status of "agunah" (a woman "chained" under Jewish law). The relevance of this case for open data management lies in the use of heterogeneous and unstructured sources to generate operational conclusions in contexts of high uncertainty. The process illustrates how triangulating multimedia and testimonial information can compensate for the lack of traditionally verifiable data. This raises ethical and technical challenges regarding transparency and data validation when privacy and emotional sensitivity take precedence over the public availability of detailed information. Furthermore, the case highlights the importance of interconnecting demographic data, oral testimonies, and indirect forensic analysis to close information gaps in humanitarian crises. The need to confirm the vital status of the missing directly impacts administrative processes and civil rights, demonstrating that the quality of available data, even if indirect, is crucial for institutional decision-making and social well-being in situations of prolonged conflict.
