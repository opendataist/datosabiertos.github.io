Labour Market Statistics

Northern Ireland’s labor market demonstrates a complex recovery, characterized by significant improvements in employment metrics alongside concerning financial and structural shifts. Employment rates, unemployment figures, and economic inactivity levels have notably improved, returning to pre-pandemic baselines over the last year. This positive trend is further supported by a substantial increase in the total number of hours worked and a rise in the overall count of employee jobs across key sectors like manufacturing and services. However, these gains are offset by widening economic disparities and increased workforce instability. Median earnings in the region have fallen behind the UK average, creating the largest recorded gap in pay. Simultaneously, there has been a dramatic surge in redundancies, with both confirmed and proposed job losses tripling compared to the previous year. This spike suggests underlying fragility in the local economy, despite the broader headline improvements in employment statistics. This report is critical for open data enthusiasts as it illustrates the necessity of triangulating multiple datasets to understand true economic health. Relying solely on headline employment rates masks critical issues such as wage stagnation and rising job insecurity. For analysts, this highlights the importance of integrating payroll, survey, and administrative data to reveal the nuanced realities of labor markets, ensuring that positive trends do not obscure negative structural indicators.
