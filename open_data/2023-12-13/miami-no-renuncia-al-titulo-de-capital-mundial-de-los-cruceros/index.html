Miami no renuncia al título de capital mundial de los cruceros

PortMiami has set a new all-time record for passenger numbers during the last fiscal year, doubling the figures from the pre-pandemic period. This explosive recovery not only surpasses 2019 levels but also reflects an insatiable demand for long-haul and luxury travel, driven by a population seeking to make up for lost time. This resurgence is accompanied by significant infrastructure expansion, including the opening of new terminals and the arrival of mega-ships, consolidating the port as a vital economic engine for South Florida. While other tourism sectors show signs of deceleration, the cruise market maintains exceptional strength, attracting travelers willing to spend considerably on exclusive experiences. This local resurgence aligns with a global trend of continuous growth, with projections indicating that the industry will far exceed historical volumes in the coming years. The ability to fill voyages scheduled months and even years in advance suggests that the sector has found a new high-value normal, where the onboard experience and the scale of the journey take priority over the frequency of travel. This article is relevant to the open data community because it illustrates the critical importance of transparency and access to government data for strategic decision-making. Rigorous tracking of metrics such as passenger volume or economic impact enables civil society, investors, and policymakers to assess the true health of key industries and the effectiveness of post-pandemic public policies. Furthermore, it highlights how the timely availability of official data facilitates the verification of macroeconomic trends and fosters an informed public debate on the sustainable development of tourism.
