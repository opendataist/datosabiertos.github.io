Master Open Data: Licenses, APIs, FOIA, and Analysis

Open data gives users quick access to public information. But this access has both pros and cons. Supporters say open data builds trust. It holds leaders accountable. For example, Hawaii got a perfect score for its open data policies. This shows that good rules help government work better. It also proves that working together helps everyone see what is happening.

However, rules can be confusing. Users need to know their legal rights. Our guide on [legal] What licence open data carries, and what you may do with it -> /guides/open-data-licences/ explains these rules.

In Africa, sites like Kachwanya.com help close information gaps. They share news on tech and innovation. This includes AI, fintech, and cybersecurity. This open reporting makes important news easy to find. It helps build a connected community. But, technical hurdles remain. Many people struggle to get data from modern portals. They often lack the right skills. Our guide on [access] How to use an open-data portal’s API: CKAN, DCAT and OpenDataSoft -> /guides/open-data-portal-api/ shows you how to handle this.

Direct access empowers citizens. But sometimes, institutions hide data. You may need to ask for it actively. You must know your right to request records. If an institution stays silent, do not give up. Our guide on [access] How to file a FOIA request — and what to do when it is denied -> /guides/how-to-file-a-foia-request/ walks you through this process.

Also, you must read data carefully. This stops you from spreading false info. Check the context, sample size, and bias before sharing stats. Our guide on [analysis] How to read a statistic before you quote it -> /guides/read-a-statistic/ teaches you to look past the headline. Learn the true story behind the numbers.
