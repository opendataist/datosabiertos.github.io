Canada, Australia, Belgium, pledge to support NEITI on open data policy

The Embassies of Belgium, Australia, and Canada have formally pledged support for Nigeria’s Open Data Policy within the extractive industry, following an inspection of the newly established NEITI Data Center in Abuja. This strategic alliance underscores a collective commitment to enhancing transparency and accountability in the sector. By providing skills development, training, and technical assistance, these diplomatic missions aim to bolster the infrastructure necessary for effective data management and usage. The Data Center serves as a comprehensive repository for information regarding oil, gas, and solid minerals, facilitating easy public access for civil society, media, and government bodies. It aligns with international standards for systematic disclosure, transforming raw data into actionable insights. This centralized hub is crucial for fostering public understanding and enabling evidence-based decision-making, which is essential for sustaining robust governance and combating corruption in resource-rich environments. This initiative highlights the critical role of open data in navigating the global energy transition and addressing revenue dependencies on hydrocarbons. International cooperation is vital for sharing knowledge and technical support, ensuring that developing nations can effectively manage their natural resources. By prioritizing transparency and collaborative partnerships, stakeholders can better address environmental and gender issues, ultimately promoting sustainable development and equitable benefit distribution across the extractive sector.
