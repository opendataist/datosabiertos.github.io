Brasil va al balotaje: las claves que explican la elección entre Lula y Flávio Bolsonaro

The first round of Brazil’s 2026 presidential election failed to produce a winner, necessitating a runoff between Flávio Bolsonaro and Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. This outcome underscores the vital importance of transparency and public access to electoral data, as the legitimacy of the democratic process depends directly on the independent verification of votes through official open sources, such as those published by the Superior Electoral Court (TSE). Brazil’s electoral system requires an absolute majority to win in the first round, a threshold neither candidate achieved, with the vote margin being extremely narrow. This situation illustrates how data reflect the rules of the democratic game in real time, enabling citizens to understand the statistical and procedural implications that determine advancement to the second round, thereby preventing speculation and ensuring institutional clarity. The significance of this event for the culture of open data lies in the fact that the vast electorate transforms the election into a massive exercise in public data collection and analysis. By making these data accessible, society is empowered to monitor the integrity of the process, recognizing that the final outcome in the runoff will be based solely on the will expressed at the polls, thereby validating confidence in the integrity of digital and statistical electoral systems.
