Consuman Diputados reforma que extingue al Inai

Consuman Diputados reforma que extingue al Inai

The Mexican Congress approved a significant reform that dismantles the autonomous National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI), transferring its functions and resources to the Secretariat of Anticorruption and Good Government. This shift centralizes authority over public information under the executive branch, creating a new entity called “Transparency of the People.” The move fundamentally alters the institutional framework governing access to public records, raising serious concerns about the independence and effectiveness of oversight mechanisms that have historically protected citizens' rights to know. Critics argue this change effectively eliminates true transparency by allowing the government to both generate and control access to information, potentially classifying vast amounts of data as confidential under vague criteria like national interest or social peace. Opponents warn this constitutes a desecration of democratic principles, turning access to information from a fundamental right into a government concession. The reform threatens to entrench political control over data, undermining accountability and preventing the public from scrutinizing actions taken by public servants, which is essential for a functioning democracy. This development is critical to the open data community because it challenges the core tenets of openness, autonomy, and free flow of information. By consolidating control over data repositories within the executive branch, the reform risks creating a system where open data is selective and subject to political discretion rather than universal availability. It highlights the vulnerability of data ecosystems to legislative changes that prioritize administrative efficiency over citizen rights, signaling a potential regression in global efforts to maintain transparent, accessible, and unbiased public information systems.
