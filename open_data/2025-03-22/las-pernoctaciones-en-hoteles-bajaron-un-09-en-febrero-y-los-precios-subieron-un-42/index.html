Las pernoctaciones en hoteles bajaron un 0,9% en febrero y los precios subieron un 4,2%

Las pernoctaciones en hoteles bajaron un 0,9% en febrero y los precios subieron un 4,2%

The article reports a modest growth in Spanish hotel occupancy and a notable increase in average daily rates for February 2025. This data highlights a divergent trend where domestic demand weakened slightly while international tourism remained resilient, particularly from key markets like the UK and Germany. The overall revenue metrics improved significantly, driven by rising prices rather than a surge in volume, indicating that hotels are prioritizing yield management to maximize profitability per available room. Canary Islands emerged as the dominant destination for both volume and occupancy, followed by mainland hubs like Madrid and Barcelona. The concentration of foreign tourists in specific regions underscores the importance of coastal and island tourism infrastructure in sustaining the national hospitality sector. Meanwhile, inland areas and lower-category establishments experienced varied price dynamics, with some seeing significant inflation while others faced slight declines, reflecting localized economic pressures and competitive strategies across different hotel tiers. This information is crucial for open data initiatives because it illustrates how aggregated statistical indicators reveal nuanced market behaviors that affect policy-making and business intelligence. By making granular tourism data accessible, stakeholders can better analyze regional disparities, forecast demand shifts, and understand the correlation between occupancy rates and pricing strategies. Such transparency supports evidence-based decision-making for local governments aiming to optimize tourism management and for researchers studying the economic resilience of the hospitality industry in an evolving global travel landscape.
