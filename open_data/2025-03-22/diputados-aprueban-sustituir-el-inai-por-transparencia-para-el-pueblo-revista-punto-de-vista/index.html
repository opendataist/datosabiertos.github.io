Diputados aprueban sustituir el INAI por Transparencia para el Pueblo | Revista Punto de Vista

Diputados aprueban sustituir el INAI por Transparencia para el Pueblo | Revista Punto de Vista

The recent legislative overhaul replaces Mexico’s independent National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI) with a new body, Transparencia para el Pueblo, which is subordinate to the Anticorruption Secretariat. By centralizing control under the Executive branch and appointing its leader directly through presidential decree, the reform dismantles the previous collegiate structure. This shift fundamentally alters the balance of power, transforming the transparency mechanism from an autonomous oversight entity into a government-dependent apparatus. Consequently, the independence crucial for checking state abuses is significantly weakened, raising concerns about potential political interference in information management. A critical implication involves the expanded ability to classify information as secret for extended periods, potentially up to a decade, based on vague criteria such as national security or social peace. This provision allows the government to withhold sensitive data regarding major public projects and state expenditures with minimal judicial oversight, as security and justice institutions remain exempt from the new transparency committees. The removal of anonymous request options further endangers critics and journalists, creating an environment where accessing public records becomes riskier and less effective for holding power accountable. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates because it threatens the very foundation of data transparency. When the state controls both the generation and the release of public information, the quality and availability of open data for civic innovation, academic research, and public scrutiny diminish. The erosion of independent oversight mechanisms undermines trust in digital governance and hinders the open data ecosystem, which relies on accurate, accessible, and unbiased information to function effectively in a democratic society.
