GASTAT: Saudi Arabia Tops G20 Countries in Safety Index 2023

Rising sea levels caused by climate change are creating new flooded coastal habitats that facilitate the spread of invasive marine species. This environmental shift threatens local biodiversity and economic activities by exposing previously unaffected ecosystems to non-native animals, effectively linking the climate and biodiversity crises into a single compounding threat. Estuaries at mid-latitudes are identified as high-risk zones, serving as critical stepping stones for invaders moving further inland as waters advance. Major human hubs, such as those in the eastern United States and the Gulf of Mexico, face significant vulnerability as these areas provide ideal conditions for species like the Pacific oyster and Atlantic blue crab to thrive and expand their ranges. This research is vital for open data initiatives because it underscores the urgent need for accessible, integrated datasets that track both climate projections and biological invasion patterns. Policymakers require transparent, shared information to mitigate billions in economic losses and protect ecosystems, demonstrating how open data enables proactive responses to interconnected global environmental challenges.
