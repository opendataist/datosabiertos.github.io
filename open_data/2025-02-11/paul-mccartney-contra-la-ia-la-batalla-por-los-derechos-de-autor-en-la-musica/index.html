Paul McCartney contra la IA: la batalla por los derechos de autor en la música

Paul McCartney contra la IA: la batalla por los derechos de autor en la música

Paul McCartney warns that the use of artificial intelligence in music creation threatens artists’ rights, arguing that current digital platforms often divert revenue to tech giants rather than to the actual creators. He emphasizes that young composers are losing ownership and economic benefits from their work, as AI facilitates the copying of songs without proper attribution or compensation to the original authors. The article highlights ongoing regulatory efforts, particularly in the United Kingdom, where a proposed bill suggests an “opt-out” system allowing technology companies to use copyrighted works for training AI unless creators explicitly refuse. This approach aims to foster investment in AI but contrasts with the concerns of artists who feel their intellectual property is being exploited without consent or fair payment mechanisms in place. This discussion is crucial for open data because it underscores the urgent need for transparent, fair data governance in AI development. When music datasets are derived from copyrighted works, the lack of clear attribution and benefit-sharing mechanisms mirrors broader issues in open data access. It calls for ethical standards that respect creators’ rights while enabling innovation, ensuring that data usage does not disenfranchise the communities and individuals who generated the original content.
