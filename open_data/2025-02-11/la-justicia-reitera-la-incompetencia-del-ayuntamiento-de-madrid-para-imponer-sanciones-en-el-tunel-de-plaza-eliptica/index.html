La Justicia reitera la incompetencia del Ayuntamiento de Madrid para imponer sanciones en el túnel de Plaza Elíptica

La Justicia reitera la incompetencia del Ayuntamiento de Madrid para imponer sanciones en el túnel de Plaza Elíptica

A recent court ruling has called into question the legality of fines issued by the Madrid City Council within Low Emission Zones (LEZ), specifically in the Plaza Elíptica tunnel. The judge determined that the municipality lacks jurisdiction over this stretch of the A-42 highway, which remains under state ownership and has not been officially transferred to local authorities. Consequently, the penalty was deemed null and void, as it applied a general provision that does not align with current legal frameworks regarding administrative competencies. This decision reinforces a growing judicial trend in Madrid, where courts are increasingly invalidating LEZ sanctions for exceeding municipal authority. The ruling emphasizes that conduct cannot be punished if it occurs under a regulation that is legally unsound. This development provides significant leverage for drivers challenged in these zones, reinforcing the argument that local governments must strictly adhere to established boundaries of power when enforcing environmental restrictions on federal infrastructure. This case is highly relevant to open data transparency and accountability, as it demonstrates how public information can expose administrative overreach. By analyzing published records and legal documents, civil society organizations can identify jurisdictional inconsistencies and challenge invalid state actions. It highlights the critical role of accessible data in safeguarding citizens' rights against potentially unlawful municipal decisions, ensuring that environmental policies are implemented within strict legal confines and respecting the separation of powers.
