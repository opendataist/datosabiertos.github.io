Bolivia registró en enero su inflación más alta de los últimos 17 años

Bolivia is experiencing its highest inflation rates in nearly two decades, primarily driven by a severe shortage of US dollars and the resulting parallel currency market. This macroeconomic instability has caused prices for essential goods, particularly food and transportation, to surge dramatically, surpassing government annual targets early in the year. The situation is exacerbated by lingering effects from recent supply chain disruptions and political blockades, creating a complex environment where official statistics fail to capture the full extent of the crisis felt by citizens. The relevance of this article to open data lies in the critical need for transparent, accessible, and reliable statistical information to understand such economic volatility. When official indicators like inflation rates diverge significantly from lived experiences or independent assessments, it highlights the importance of verifying data sources and understanding the methodologies behind national accounts. Open data initiatives can empower economists and citizens to cross-reference government reports with local market realities, fostering accountability and better-informed decision-making during periods of severe economic distress. Furthermore, this case underscores the necessity of granular data disaggregation to identify specific drivers of price increases, such as particular commodities or transport sectors. By making detailed datasets publicly available, stakeholders can analyze the precise impact of policy interventions, like export bans, on local markets. Ultimately, robust open data frameworks are essential not only for monitoring economic health but also for diagnosing the root causes of crises, enabling more effective responses to issues like currency scarcity and supply chain failures.
