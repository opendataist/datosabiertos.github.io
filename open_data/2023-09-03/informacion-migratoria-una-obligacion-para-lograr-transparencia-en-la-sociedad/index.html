Información migratoria: una obligación para lograr transparencia en la sociedad

Información migratoria: una obligación para lograr transparencia en la sociedad

The article highlights that, despite institutional efforts, Colombia lacks timely, granular, and accessible statistical data on migration, which undermines effective public policy planning. Although entities such as the DANE (National Administrative Department of Statistics) and the DNP (National Planning Department) have initiated studies, their outputs are often delayed, incomplete, or insufficiently specific, while other agencies have ceased publishing or sharing critical information. This gap hinders an accurate diagnosis of migratory flows and complicates the design of integration strategies for both local governments and national actors. The core issue lies in the insufficiency and instability of current data sources. While surveys such as the GEIH (Continuous Household Survey) and Pulso de la Migración (Migration Pulse) provide a baseline, they require greater territorial specificity and long-term consistency to be truly useful. The article emphasizes that transparency and rigorous, up-to-date statistics are fundamental obligations for a democratic society and are essential for coordinating responses to complex migratory phenomena, particularly concerning Venezuelan residents. This issue is highly relevant to open data, as it demonstrates how the absence of accessible, standardized, and regularly updated datasets directly impacts social equity and administrative efficiency. By calling for specialized, territorially detailed, and consistently available migration data, the article advocates for robust open data frameworks that empower civil society, researchers, and policymakers to make informed, evidence-based decisions rather than relying on estimates.
