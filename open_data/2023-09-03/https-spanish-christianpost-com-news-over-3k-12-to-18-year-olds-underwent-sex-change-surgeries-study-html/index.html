The study reveals a significant increase in gender-affirming surgeries among adolescents in the United States, raising a crucial debate about the accessibility of these medical procedures for minors. The data indicate that a notable proportion of young people underwent irreversible surgical interventions, underscoring the urgent need to evaluate informed consent protocols and the protection of vulnerable groups within the healthcare system. This situation exposes ethical and healthcare tensions between the reported benefits of improved quality of life and concerns about long-term effects, including regret and physical complications. The existence of legal lawsuits and opposition from individuals who have transitioned away from gender identification challenge the prevailing narrative, highlighting the complexity and potential risks associated with genital or breast surgery in developing bodies. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how the publication of large medical datasets facilitates public scrutiny and independent analysis of critical healthcare trends. By making information on treatment patterns and demographics accessible, it empowers civil society, legislators, and researchers to question established policies, fostering the transparency necessary in health data governance that directly impacts the rights and well-being of the young population.
