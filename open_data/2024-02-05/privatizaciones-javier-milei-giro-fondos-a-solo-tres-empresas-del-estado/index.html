Privatizaciones: Javier Milei giró fondos a solo tres empresas del Estado

The government of Javier Milei has significantly reduced state assistance to public enterprises, transferring funds to only three companies so far this year. This abrupt measure contrasts with the multibillion-dollar expenditures of the previous year and reflects a clear strategy of financial dismantling in preparation for the partial or total privatization of various entities. The opposition managed to keep strategic companies under state ownership, but the government continues to exert pressure to reduce their budgetary dependence. The legislative process has been subject to intense negotiations, resulting in a list of companies slated for privatization that was reduced and modified following political pressure. Although a regulatory framework has been established that mandates transparency through public reports prior to any transfer, the concrete implementation remains uncertain. This balance between privatization ambitions and parliamentary oversight defines the immediate future of critical infrastructure such as railways, aviation, and public services. This issue is relevant to open data because the requirement to publish comprehensive reports on the assets and financial status of state-owned enterprises establishes a precedent for mandatory transparency. For citizens and civil society to effectively monitor these privatizations, access to detailed, standardized, and open public data on the economic and operational management of these organizations is essential. The quality and availability of these data will determine the actual degree of accountability in the public sector reform.
