Hey, Fauci's staff: You can't just 'make your emails disappear'

The article highlights a serious breach of transparency by NIH scientist David Morens, who collaborated with FOIA officers to conceal official emails during the coronavirus origins investigation. This incident underscores the fundamental open_data principle that publicly funded information belongs to the citizenry. When officials actively obscure records, they undermine the trust essential for democratic accountability and the free flow of information. Morens’ tactics, including deleting messages and using personal accounts to evade searches, illustrate how inadequate training and hostile institutional cultures can compromise data integrity. The attempt to treat emails as non-records reveals a dangerous disconnect between legal obligations and internal practices. Such actions suggest a coordinated effort to restrict public access to critical health data, rather than a simple misunderstanding of records management protocols. For the open_data community, this serves as a stark warning that transparency cannot be assumed; it must be rigorously protected against intentional obfuscation. The "Streisand effect" observed here demonstrates that suppression efforts often amplify scrutiny, causing greater reputational damage than the original information. Ultimately, the case reinforces that all government-generated data, regardless of format, is public property, and attempts to hide it only highlight the urgent need for robust, unalterable archival standards.
