The post-pandemic era has seen a significant surge in labor complaints, largely driven by unjustified dismissals that dominate the dispute landscape. While initial spikes occurred, recent trends show a notable decline, indicating that the immediate shock of the labor market transition is subsiding as conflicts are increasingly processed through established channels. Crucially, the mandatory conciliation mechanism proves highly effective, resolving the vast majority of these cases before they reach judicial tribunals. This shift not only accelerates resolution times but also facilitates reinstatement for many workers, demonstrating that alternative dispute resolution is the primary tool for restoring labor rights rather than prolonged litigation. This dynamic is vital for open data because it highlights the importance of transparently publishing conciliation outcomes and rejection rates. By making data on procedural compliance and settlement successes publicly available, society can assess the true efficacy of labor reforms and ensure accountability for employers who fail to participate, thereby strengthening the rule of law.
