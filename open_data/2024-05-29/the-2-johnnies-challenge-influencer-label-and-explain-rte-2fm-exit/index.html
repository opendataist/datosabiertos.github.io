The 2 Johnnies challenge 'influencer' label and explain RTE 2FM exit

The 2 Johnnies challenge 'influencer' label and explain RTE 2FM exit

The departure of prominent radio hosts highlights the evolving definition of media professionals in the digital age. Rather than being categorized as traditional influencers, the creators argue for the title of content creators, emphasizing a shift in how media consumption and production intersect. This distinction is crucial for open data discussions, as it challenges existing taxonomies that may fail to capture the multifaceted nature of modern digital contributors. The industry is moving away from single-stream employment toward multi-platform monetization, creating a complex landscape where income sources are diverse and precarious. This reality necessitates transparent frameworks for disclosing interests, as traditional broadcast models no longer apply. For open data initiatives, this implies that registries must be dynamic and comprehensive, accurately reflecting the interconnected nature of today’s media economy to maintain public trust and accountability. Ultimately, the stability of media institutions depends on adapting to these structural changes rather than resisting them. By recognizing the new realities of creator economies, stakeholders can better understand the motivations behind industry shifts and the need for robust data governance. This perspective underscores the importance of updating data standards to reflect contemporary media practices, ensuring that transparency mechanisms are relevant to the current digital ecosystem.
