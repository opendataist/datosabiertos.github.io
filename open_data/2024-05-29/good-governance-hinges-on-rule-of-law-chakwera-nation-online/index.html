Good governance hinges on rule of law—Chakwera | Nation Online

President Lazarus Chakwera emphasized that sustainable governance relies on robust systems empowering citizens to hold officials accountable, rather than just competent leadership. By launching Open Government Partnership Week, the administration highlighted its commitment to the rule of law and combating systemic corruption. This initiative underscores the critical role of transparency in strengthening democratic institutions and fostering a responsive government that collaborates with citizens and the private sector. A key implication for open data is the strategic expansion of access to information, exemplified by the appointment of a Registrar of Political Parties to oversee financing. Furthermore, the government’s adoption of open contracting and the disclosure of beneficial ownership represent significant advancements in transparency. These measures ensure that public resources are tracked effectively, allowing stakeholders to monitor government actions and reduce opportunities for corruption through verifiable data. However, implementing these reforms requires shifting from short-term, two-year plans to longer, resource-backed timelines aligned with national budgets. The operationalization of digital public services and whistleblower protections further supports an environment where data can drive accountability. This approach is vital for open data because it establishes the legal and infrastructural frameworks necessary for continuous, meaningful public scrutiny and sustainable development.
