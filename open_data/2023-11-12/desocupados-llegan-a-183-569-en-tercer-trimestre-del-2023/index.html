Desocupados llegan a 183.569 en tercer trimestre del 2023

The latest labor data reveals a slight increase in unemployment during the third quarter of 2023, marking a notable shift from previous trends. This rise highlights a growing gender disparity, as the surge in joblessness is predominantly driven by women, whereas the male population experienced a relative improvement in employment status compared to the prior year. These fluctuations underscore the volatility within the current labor market and the uneven impact of economic conditions across different demographic groups. Despite the rise in unemployment, the overall labor force expanded, and the number of underemployed individuals decreased significantly. This suggests that while securing full-time positions remains challenging for many, those who are employed are increasingly finding stable hours, contrasting with the period where a substantial portion of the workforce struggled to find sufficient hours. The reduction in underemployment indicates a subtle strengthening in labor quality for those currently working, even as entry-level access presents hurdles for others. This article is crucial for open data initiatives as it exemplifies the necessity of transparent, high-frequency household surveys to monitor socioeconomic health. The publication of detailed breakdowns by gender and region allows researchers and policymakers to analyze micro-level trends accurately. By making such granular statistical data accessible, institutions foster accountability and enable civil society to engage with evidence-based discussions on employment policies, ensuring that public data serves as a foundational tool for informed democratic oversight.
