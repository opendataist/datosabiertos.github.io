La esperanza de vida de los baleares aumenta 10 años desde 1975

Life expectancy in Spain has increased significantly, driven by improvements in quality of life, economic well-being, and advances in healthcare. This trend reflects a gradual aging of the population, although notable gaps persist between autonomous communities and between genders. Women outlive men, and the Madrid region leads in longevity indices, while Ceuta reports lower figures. Although the pandemic caused slight, temporary setbacks, Professor Salvà maintains that the overall trend remains positive, mitigating the short-term impact of the health crisis. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how demographic indicators, structured within public databases, reveal territorial and gender inequalities. Access to these time series enables researchers and policymakers to analyze the impact of social and healthcare policies, facilitating evidence-based decision-making grounded in transparent and verifiable data on social well-being.
