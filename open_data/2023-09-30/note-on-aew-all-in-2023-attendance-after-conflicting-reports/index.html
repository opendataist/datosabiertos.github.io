Note On AEW All In 2023 Attendance After Conflicting Reports

This article highlights the discrepancy between ticket sales and actual venue entry at AEW All In, emphasizing how raw data like turnstile counts must be contextualized. It distinguishes between distributed tickets, paid attendance, and scanned entries, revealing that a significant portion of sold tickets remained unused. This gap underscores the complexity of deriving accurate metrics from promotional claims versus logistical records. Understanding these nuances is crucial for open_data advocates who scrutinize public or semi-public datasets. The confusion surrounding varying figures demonstrates the importance of transparent data sourcing and clear definitions within public records. Without such clarity, consumers may misinterpret statistics, leading to inaccurate conclusions about event success or audience size. Relevant to open_data, this case illustrates why standardized metadata and accessible primary sources are essential. It warns against treating aggregated numbers as absolute truth without understanding their collection methodology. By analyzing these variations, we learn that true openness requires not just data availability, but also comprehensive documentation of how those numbers are generated and verified by authorities.
