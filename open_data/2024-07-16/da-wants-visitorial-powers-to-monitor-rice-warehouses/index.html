DA wants visitorial powers to monitor rice warehouses

DA wants visitorial powers to monitor rice warehouses

The Philippine Department of Agriculture advocates for restoring its authority to register, monitor, and inspect rice warehouses and retailers. This proposed amendment to the rice tariffication law aims to address the significant regulatory blind spots created after recent reforms stripped the National Food Authority of these oversight duties. By seeking direct visitorial powers, the department intends to ensure compliance with food safety standards and effectively track the movement of rice supplies across the industry. This shift in regulatory power is critical for enhancing transparency and combating agricultural smuggling, hoarding, and profiteering. With current data on rice mills and retailers largely missing, the government struggles to understand the true scale of the market. Empowering the Agriculture Department with comprehensive tracking capabilities would allow for precise analysis of local production and imports, thereby strengthening enforcement under stricter anti-sabotage legislation that imposes severe penalties for illicit trade activities. For the open data community, this article highlights a pivotal opportunity to improve public sector transparency and accountability. If enacted, the mandatory registration and inspection protocols would generate structured, granular datasets on the rice supply chain. Accessing such data would enable researchers and citizens to monitor food security metrics, verify inventory levels, and analyze market trends, fostering a more informed public discourse on agricultural policy and economic stability in the Philippines.
