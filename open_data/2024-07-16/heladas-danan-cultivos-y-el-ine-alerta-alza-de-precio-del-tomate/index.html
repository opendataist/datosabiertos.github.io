Heladas dañan cultivos y el INE alerta alza de precio del tomate

Heladas dañan cultivos y el INE alerta alza de precio del tomate

Recent frosts have severely damaged agricultural production in key Bolivian regions, causing significant losses in vegetable, fruit, and tuber crops. The National Statistics Institute warns that these supply disruptions will inevitably drive up market prices, particularly for tomatoes, reflecting the direct economic impact on consumers. This situation highlights the critical importance of open data in public policy and crisis management. Transparent, timely statistical information enables authorities and citizens to monitor resource allocation, accurately assess damage, and understand market dynamics. Without accessible data, coordinating effective responses to climate-induced shocks becomes nearly impossible. Ultimately, this incident underscores how open data serves as a vital tool for transparency during emergencies. It allows stakeholders to track inflation trends and food security risks in real time. By making such information freely available, governments can foster accountability and ensure that aid reaches affected communities more efficiently, thereby strengthening resilience against future climate-related disasters.
