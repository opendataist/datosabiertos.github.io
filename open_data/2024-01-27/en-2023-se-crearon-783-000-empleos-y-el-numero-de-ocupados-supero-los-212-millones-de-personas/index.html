En 2023 se crearon 783.000 empleos y el número de ocupados superó los 21,2 millones de personas

The latest data from Spain’s National Statistics Institute reveals a transformative shift in the labor market, characterized by historic growth in employment and a significant structural improvement in job quality. The primary conclusion is that recent economic reforms have successfully enhanced stability, drastically reducing unemployment and temporary contracts to levels comparable with the European average. This demonstrates not only the resilience of the Spanish economy amidst complex international conditions but also a profound shift toward secure, long-term workforce engagement. A critical aspect of this transformation is the dominance of the private sector in job creation and the marked improvement in employment stability. The surge in permanent positions and full-time roles indicates a qualitative upgrade in labor conditions, moving away from the precariousness that historically defined much of the workforce. This trend suggests that economic dynamism is now supported by a more sustainable and reliable job market structure, fostering greater confidence among workers and highlighting the effectiveness of policy interventions aimed at labor market reform. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the importance of transparent, high-frequency statistical indicators for public policy evaluation. By making granular data on employment sectors, regions, and contract types publicly available, governments can enable rigorous analysis of reform impacts and economic health. Open access to such detailed labor metrics allows researchers, journalists, and citizens to verify official narratives, fostering accountability and ensuring that data-driven decisions truly reflect the evolving realities of the workforce.
