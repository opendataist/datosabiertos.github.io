La inflación PCE de EEUU se mantiene en el 2,6% y la subyacente baja al 2,9%

The recent decline in core inflation in the United States, coming in below consensus expectations, signals a persistent slowdown in price increases. This data suggests that the Federal Reserve is approaching its 2% inflation target, generating expectations of future gradual interest rate cuts. The current strength of the economy allows regulators to address inflation without compromising growth, marking a turning point in monetary policy. The evolution of price components, such as the decline in services and rents, reinforces the downward trend in inflation. Analysts anticipate that these corrections will continue, giving the Fed the confidence needed to begin lowering interest rates in the coming months. This outlook implies that official inflation forecasts will continue to be revised downward, aligning more closely with the observed economic reality. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparency and timely access to official statistics, such as those published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics, are essential for economic analysis. The availability of these data enables independent analysts and markets to validate trends, fostering informed decision-making based on public, verifiable evidence—the core principle of the open data movement.
