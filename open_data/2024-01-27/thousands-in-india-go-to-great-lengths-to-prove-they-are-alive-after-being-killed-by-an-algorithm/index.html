Thousands in India go to great lengths to prove they are alive after being ‘killed’ by an algorithm

Automated government databases used for welfare verification can cause severe real-world harm by erroneously deeming citizens deceased. This technical failure strips individuals of essential financial support, forcing them into extreme measures to prove their existence. The case highlights the dangerous gap between algorithmic efficiency and human-centric accountability in public service delivery. For open data advocates, this incident underscores the critical need for accurate, auditable, and transparent data systems. When digital identifiers dictate access to life-sustaining resources, data integrity becomes a matter of civil rights rather than just administrative efficiency. Systems must include robust error-correction mechanisms that prioritize individual dignity over bureaucratic convenience. Ultimately, the article demonstrates that open data initiatives must be designed with strong safeguards against automated bias and administrative rigidity. Without proper oversight and avenues for redress, well-intentioned digitalization can inadvertently disenfranchise vulnerable populations, turning data management into a tool of exclusion rather than inclusion.
