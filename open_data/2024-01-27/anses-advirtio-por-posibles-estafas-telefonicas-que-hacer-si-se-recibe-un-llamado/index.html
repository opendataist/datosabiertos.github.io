ANSES advirtió por posibles estafas telefónicas: ¿qué hacer si se recibe un llamado?

The article highlights that ANSES strictly prohibits soliciting personal data, passwords, or banking information through unofficial channels such as phone calls, emails, or social media. It emphasizes that legitimate interactions occur only via their secure official website, which can be accessed exclusively using a personal CUIL (Unique Identification Code for Workers) and Social Security password, ensuring that no third-party intermediaries are ever required for any official procedures or consultations. This guidance is critically relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of verifying official sources to maintain trust in publicly shared information systems. By warning against phishing attempts and fake forms, the notice reinforces how users must distinguish between genuine open government services and fraudulent actors who exploit the accessibility of public sector data. The implications extend beyond individual safety to the integrity of the entire digital ecosystem. When citizens understand that official services are free and direct, they are better equipped to resist social engineering tactics. This protection ensures that open data initiatives remain secure, fostering a reliable environment where citizens can confidently engage with government resources without fear of unauthorized data extraction or financial loss through impersonation.
