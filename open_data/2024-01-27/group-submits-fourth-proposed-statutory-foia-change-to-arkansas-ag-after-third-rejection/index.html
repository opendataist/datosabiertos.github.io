Group submits fourth proposed statutory FOIA change to Arkansas AG after third rejection

Arkansas Citizens for Transparency is advancing initiatives to expand public access to government records and meetings, challenging recent legislative efforts to shield certain security records from view. After the Attorney General rejected the latest draft of the proposed Freedom of Information Act for misleading "preemptive repeal" language, the group plans to resubmit with minor adjustments while simultaneously launching a signature drive for a constitutional amendment. This amendment seeks to enshrine government transparency as a fundamental right, thereby limiting the Legislature's power to restrict public access. The proposals aim to redefine key terms like "public meeting" and "cybersecurity" while establishing a Government Transparency Commission to help citizens enforce their rights. Key provisions include protecting the right to appeal FOIA decisions and recovering attorney fees, imposing stricter penalties for violations, and mandating that records regarding security services for elected officials become public after three months. These measures are designed to close loopholes that allow officials to hold closed-door meetings or withhold information under vague grounds. This effort is crucial for open data advocates because it addresses the tension between government secrecy and public accountability through direct democracy. By attempting to constitutionally guarantee transparency and codify clear definitions for open meetings, the initiative highlights how legislative changes can inadvertently restrict data access. The ongoing legal and political battle underscores the importance of precise legal language in preserving the public's right to obtain and utilize government information.
