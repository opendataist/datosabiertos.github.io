Attorney general approves proposed amendment regarding FOIA

Arkansas Attorney General Tim Griffin recently approved a proposed constitutional amendment related to the Freedom of Information Act, allowing advocates to begin collecting signatures for the November ballot. This approval follows a lawsuit filed by transparency advocates who challenged his previous refusal to certify their initiated act language, arguing that the AG exceeded his legal authority by demanding text rewrites rather than adhering strictly to statutory guidelines. The dispute highlights the tension between the AG’s limited statutory role and practical enforcement of ballot certification rules. While the Attorney General claims to rely solely on the law, critics contend his actions demonstrate personal bias or overreach, undermining the transparent process essential for civic engagement and legal clarity in democratic procedures. This conflict is crucial to open data advocacy because it directly impacts public access to government information. Successful certification of the amendment would strengthen legal frameworks for transparency, ensuring citizens can effectively request and obtain official records. Ultimately, the outcome sets a precedent for how state officials interpret and enforce freedom of information laws, affecting broader data accessibility and accountability.
