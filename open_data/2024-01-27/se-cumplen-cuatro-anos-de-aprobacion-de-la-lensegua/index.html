Se cumplen cuatro años de aprobación de la Lensegua

Guatemala recognized Sign Language as an official national language through Decree 3-2020, addressing historical communication barriers for approximately 360,000 deaf citizens. This legislative milestone fulfills international human rights commitments, ensuring equitable access to essential services such as education, justice, and healthcare. By legally validating Sign Language, the state mandates coordinated action across sectors to guarantee effective participation and eliminate systemic exclusion for the deaf community. To implement this law effectively, the government has prioritized certifying sign language interpreters in collaboration with educational institutions and disability organizations. This professionalization is crucial for facilitating communication in hospitals, public events, and conferences. The initiative underscores a shift toward institutional responsibility, requiring public entities to allocate financial resources for specialized staff, thereby transforming legal recognition into tangible, accessible services for deaf individuals. This article is vital for open data because it highlights the necessity of inclusive data collection and representation. The 2018 census provided the baseline figures that informed this policy, demonstrating how accurate, disaggregated statistics drive legislative change. Furthermore, the push for interpreters in media and public institutions suggests a broader need for accessible digital platforms and transparent data dissemination. Open data initiatives must ensure that information regarding such policies is not only published but also accessible to marginalized groups, using formats such as video interpretation to bridge the digital divide and guarantee true informational equity.
