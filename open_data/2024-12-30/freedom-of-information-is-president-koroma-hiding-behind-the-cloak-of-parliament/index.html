Freedom of Information: Is president Koroma hiding behind the cloak of parliament?

The urgent passage of the Freedom of Information Bill in Sierra Leone is critical for establishing government transparency and accountability. By granting the public access to previously secret information, such as government contracts, the legislation serves as a vital mechanism to combat corruption and prevent the recurrence of human rights abuses that fueled the nation’s past conflict. The prolonged delay in enacting this law highlights a disconnect between political rhetoric and action, despite strong public statements from leadership supporting transparency. Civil society organizations emphasize that moving beyond partisan blame is necessary to fulfill democratic principles and ensure that governance standards align with international human rights obligations and the recommendations of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission. This issue is directly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of legal frameworks that mandate the release of public information. Without such legislation, the potential for open data initiatives remains stifled by secrecy, limiting citizens' ability to monitor state actions, hold officials accountable, and realize their broader human rights through access to knowledge.
