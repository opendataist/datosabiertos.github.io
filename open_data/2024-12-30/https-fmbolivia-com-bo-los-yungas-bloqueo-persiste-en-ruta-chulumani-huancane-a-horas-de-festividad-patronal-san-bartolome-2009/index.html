The community of Cuchumpaya has intensified a road blockade on the route connecting Chulumani and Huancané, demanding the expulsion of Fausto Reynal and improved public security. This sustained protest has severely disrupted passenger and commercial mobility, forcing travelers to walk significant distances to find alternative transport. The measures coincide with the upcoming festival of San Bartolomé, creating a complex dynamic between the need for civic pressure and the necessity of maintaining access for celebratory events. The conflict has drawn in various local stakeholders, including transport unions and motorcycle associations, who are seeking solutions that allow both the lifting of the blockade and the continuation of scheduled sporting competitions. Negotiations among local leaders aim to balance these competing interests, highlighting the tension between civic demands and community logistics during peak cultural periods. This situation is relevant to open data because effective crisis management and transparent governance rely heavily on accessible, real-time information. Open data frameworks can facilitate better communication between authorities and citizens, ensuring that mobility disruptions and security concerns are monitored and addressed publicly. By making logistical and security data available, communities can foster trust, streamline negotiations, and reduce the social friction caused by opaque decision-making processes during regional conflicts.
