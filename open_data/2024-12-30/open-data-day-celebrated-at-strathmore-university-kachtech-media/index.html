Open Data Day celebrated at Strathmore University - KachTech Media

The recent celebration of International Open Data Day in Kenya highlighted a critical paradox: while substantial data exists, it remains largely inaccessible and underutilized for effective policy-making and research. Stakeholders emphasized that the core challenge lies not in data scarcity, but in the lack of contextualization and user-friendly packaging. Without these improvements, the potential for data-driven decision-making is stifled, preventing citizens and institutions from leveraging available information to address complex social issues effectively. Relevance to open data is deeply rooted in the necessity of bridging the gap between raw information and actionable insights. The event underscored that open data initiatives must prioritize ease of access and public sensitization to empower citizens to exercise their constitutional right to information. By transforming data into a tool for accountability, open data platforms enable the public to fulfill their watchdog roles, ensuring that government entities are held responsible through transparent and informed civic engagement. Furthermore, sustainable open data ecosystems require robust collaboration among government bodies, civil society, and technical partners to safeguard data privacy and enforce regulatory frameworks. As the global community marks a decade of open data advocacy, the focus has shifted from mere persuasion to active community building and solution creation. This holistic approach ensures that open data becomes a shared responsibility, fostering an environment where diverse stakeholders can co-create solutions that enhance societal welfare and drive sustainable development.
