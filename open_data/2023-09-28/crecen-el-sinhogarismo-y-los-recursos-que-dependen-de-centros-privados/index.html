Crecen el sinhogarismo y los recursos que dependen de centros privados

The article reveals that poverty and homelessness in Spain are increasingly managed by the private third sector rather than the state, with the majority of shelters and support centers operated by nonprofits relying heavily on volunteers and private donations. This structural shift highlights a systemic gap in public policy, where social welfare functions as a charitable necessity rather than a guaranteed right, placing the burden of maintaining basic dignity on altruistic entities and the working poor who must cover rising social needs. Beyond housing, the data indicates a significant rise in food insecurity and social exclusion, exacerbated by immigration challenges and economic instability. The high volume of requests for basic sustenance and the limited capacity of public resources demonstrate that traditional income metrics fail to capture the true extent of vulnerability. Many individuals face a "risk of poverty" index that reflects social exclusion and inability to afford basic dignities, suggesting that employment no longer guarantees protection against destitution or marginalization. This situation is critically relevant to open data initiatives because it exposes the limitations of relying solely on aggregated statistics to drive social policy. While indices like Arope provide a macro view of exclusion, they often obscure the qualitative realities of invisibility and the specific needs of marginalized groups. True open data advocacy requires not just transparency in these numbers, but also access to granular, disaggregated datasets that can identify gaps in service coverage, ensuring that technological solutions and public accountability address the root causes of structural inequality rather than just its symptoms.
