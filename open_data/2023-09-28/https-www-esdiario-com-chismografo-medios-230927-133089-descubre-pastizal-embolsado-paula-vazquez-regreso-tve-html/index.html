The article highlights a significant discovery regarding the financial transparency of Spanish public broadcasting. Through a request to RTVE’s transparency portal, it was revealed that presenter Paula Vázquez earned substantial fees for her work on the television program *El puente de las mentiras*. This disclosure underscores the critical role of public transparency tools in monitoring how public funds are allocated within state media institutions. The financial details show that Vázquez received a fixed salary for the season, which constituted a specific portion of the program's total budget. By accessing official data portals, citizens and journalists can verify compensation packages for public figures. This example demonstrates that open data initiatives provide a tangible mechanism for accountability, allowing the public to scrutinize expenditures and ensure that resources are managed responsibly. This situation is relevant to the open data community because it validates the practical utility of government transparency portals. It proves that structured public data can be leveraged to answer specific questions about public spending, fostering greater trust and engagement. Such cases encourage the continued development and usage of open data standards, reinforcing the principle that public information should be accessible, understandable, and useful for holding institutions accountable.
