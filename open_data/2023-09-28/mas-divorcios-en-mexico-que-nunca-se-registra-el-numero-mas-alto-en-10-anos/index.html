Más divorcios en México que nunca, se registra el número más alto en 10 años

Mexico recorded its highest divorce rate in a decade in 2022, signaling significant shifts in family structures and legal procedures. The predominance of judicial over administrative resolutions indicates that the justice system remains the primary channel for marital dissolution, underscoring the complexity of the legal frameworks governing changes in personal status. This trend reflects broader societal transformations in which individual autonomy increasingly influences the stability of traditional partnerships. At the same time, marriage rates have risen to their highest levels in five years, accompanied by a notable increase in the average age of spouses. This dual trend points to a maturing demographic landscape in which individuals delay commitment yet still pursue formal unions, often embracing diverse family models such as same-sex marriages. The data suggest that while social norms evolve, the institution of marriage retains cultural significance, albeit with changing temporal and structural characteristics. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, standardized statistical collection enables the tracking of complex social dynamics over time. By making this information publicly available, government agencies allow researchers, policymakers, and citizens to analyze trends in social welfare, legal efficiency, and demographic behavior. Such transparency fosters evidence-based discussions on family support systems, legal reforms, and public health strategies, empowering society to respond effectively to evolving human relationships through accessible, high-quality data.
