Recent INDEC statistics reveal a significant deterioration in living conditions, with poverty and extreme poverty rates rising notably by mid-2023. The data indicates that economic pressures are intensifying, particularly affecting younger demographics and vulnerable regions. Although this information offers a crucial baseline, it is rapidly becoming outdated due to subsequent inflationary spikes and currency devaluations, suggesting that the current reality is even more severe than reported. The report highlights stark geographic disparities, with the northwest and specific urban centers experiencing disproportionately high hardship. Conversely, metropolitan Buenos Aires and Patagonia show comparatively better indicators, though they are not immune to broader national trends. These localized insights are essential for understanding how economic shocks impact different communities unevenly, revealing the spatial concentration of vulnerability across the country. This article is relevant to open data as it exemplifies the critical need for timely, accessible, and granular public statistics to monitor social welfare. High-quality open datasets allow researchers, journalists, and citizens to verify official narratives and track real-time economic impacts. By making such detailed indicators transparent, society can better hold institutions accountable and drive informed policy decisions aimed at reducing inequality and addressing poverty effectively.
