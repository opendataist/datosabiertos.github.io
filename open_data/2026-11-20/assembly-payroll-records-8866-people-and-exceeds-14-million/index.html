Assembly payroll records 8,866 people and exceeds $14 million

Assembly payroll records 8,866 people and exceeds $14 million

The analysis reveals significant growth in the National Assembly’s workforce, highlighting a predominance of temporary hires over permanent staff and a strong concentration of resources in administrative and support positions. This structure raises questions about the actual proportion of personnel directly supporting deputies versus the broader bureaucratic apparatus, suggesting potential inefficiencies in the allocation of human resources within the legislative body. The relevance to open data lies in the need to make public financial and labor information transparent and contextualized. Although records of costs and personnel exist, the lack of detail regarding distribution across departments and the absence of administrative justification hinder accountability. Citizens and legislators demand not only access to the figures but also a detailed breakdown to determine whether the increase in staff responds to genuine operational needs or to opaque hiring processes. In conclusion, this case illustrates how the publication of open data serves as a fundamental tool for auditing public spending and questioning institutional decisions. For transparency to be effective, it is imperative that data go beyond aggregate totals, providing granular information on job functions, employment modalities, and the geographic or hierarchical distribution of personnel, thereby enabling rigorous social scrutiny of the size and efficiency of the state structure.
