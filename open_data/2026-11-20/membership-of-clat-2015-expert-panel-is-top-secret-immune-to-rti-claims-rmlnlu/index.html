Membership of CLAT 2015 ‘expert panel’ is top-secret, immune to RTI, claims RMLNLU

Membership of CLAT 2015 ‘expert panel’ is top-secret, immune to RTI, claims RMLNLU

The article highlights significant concerns regarding transparency and accountability within the CLAT 2015 admission process, directly impacting open data principles. By refusing to disclose the identities of the expert panels reviewing alleged errors, the organizing body obstructed public scrutiny and hindered the availability of crucial information. This secrecy prevents stakeholders from verifying the integrity of the evaluation process, undermining trust in the system’s fairness. Furthermore, the handling of the second expert panel’s findings demonstrated a lack of procedural openness. Despite a court order, the convenor failed to publicly notify the panel’s formation or results, even as the admission timeline expired. This deliberate omission of critical procedural data suggests an intentional effort to limit public access to information about how grievances were addressed, raising questions about the organization's commitment to honest and accessible record-keeping. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how institutional opacity can mask potential inaccuracies in large-scale standardized testing. When organizations withhold data about their internal evaluations and the composition of decision-making bodies, they violate the core tenets of transparency. Ultimately, the incident serves as a cautionary example of how restricted access to administrative data can erode public confidence and obscure the true state of institutional accountability.
