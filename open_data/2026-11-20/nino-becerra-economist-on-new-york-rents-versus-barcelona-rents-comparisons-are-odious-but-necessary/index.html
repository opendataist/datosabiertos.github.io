Niño Becerra, economist, on New York rents versus Barcelona rents: "Comparisons are odious, but necessary

Economist Santiago Niño Becerra presents a detailed comparison of commercial rents on Passeig de Gràcia in Barcelona and Fifth Avenue in New York. While initially highlighting a substantial nominal difference in price per square meter, the analysis deepens by incorporating local economic variables such as per capita income and land-use composition, offering a more nuanced perspective on the commercial competitiveness of both locations. The relevance of this study lies in its methodological approach to open data, as it demonstrates the importance of normalizing international comparisons. By measuring rental costs in relation to the local population’s economic capacity, the gap between the two streets is shown to narrow considerably. This method avoids biases stemming from exchange rate fluctuations and contextualizes prices within the real purchasing power of each area, providing a more robust analytical tool for global economic assessments. The main conclusion underscores that, although New York remains significantly more expensive, the gap is much smaller than raw figures suggest. The analysis confirms that factors such as the scarcity of residential supply in Manhattan and the high demand from international brands influence rents, but when adjusted for disposable income, Barcelona emerges as a more balanced option. This implies caution when interpreting isolated nominal data, always prioritizing local socioeconomic context to understand the true scale of real estate markets.
