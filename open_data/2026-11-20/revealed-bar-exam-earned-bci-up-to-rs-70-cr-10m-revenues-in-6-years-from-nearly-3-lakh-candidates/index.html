Revealed: Bar exam earned BCI up to Rs 70 cr ($10m) revenues in 6 years from nearly 3 lakh candidates

Revealed: Bar exam earned BCI up to Rs 70 cr ($10m) revenues in 6 years from nearly 3 lakh candidates

An RTI disclosure reveals that the Bar Council of India has generated substantial annual revenue from law graduates through the All India Bar Examination. Since its inception, the council has collected millions in fees from nearly three hundred thousand candidates. This financial data highlights a significant disparity between the growing number of examinees and the escalating costs they must bear, illustrating the lucrative nature of mandatory professional licensing for entry into the legal profession in India. The investigation underscores a clear trend of increasing fees and decreasing intervals between exams, which directly impacts candidates' access to the profession. The BCI has repeatedly hiked registration costs while simultaneously reducing the frequency of concessions for repeaters. These structural changes suggest a prioritization of revenue generation over facilitating efficient legal practice entry, raising questions about the proportionality of such financial barriers for aspiring lawyers who are already managing the educational expenses of their degrees. This information is critically relevant to the open data movement as it demonstrates the power of transparency in holding regulatory bodies accountable. By leveraging public information requests to uncover financial practices that were previously opaque, advocates can expose potential exploitation and advocate for policy reforms. The case serves as a strong example of how accessible data can drive public discourse on fairness, affordability, and the ethical conduct of professional councils, ensuring that public interest remains central to regulatory governance.
