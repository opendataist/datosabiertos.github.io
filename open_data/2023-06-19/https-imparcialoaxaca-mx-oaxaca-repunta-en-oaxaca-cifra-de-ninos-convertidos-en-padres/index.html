Oaxaca has experienced a notable demographic shift in early parenthood, with an increase in the number of children under fifteen becoming parents, while the figure among adolescents aged fifteen to nineteen has declined. This trend, supported by official data, underscores the urgent need for targeted interventions in sexual health, given that young people in the state begin their sexual lives at an extremely early age. The response from health authorities involves implementing comprehensive strategies that go beyond prevention, focusing on access to specialized services such as counseling and contraceptive methods. By aligning these actions with national standards that ensure patient autonomy, the aim is to eliminate administrative barriers and protect the sexual rights of youth, facilitating their access to medical care without requiring parental authorization. This approach is relevant to open data because it highlights the importance of transparency in public health information. The availability and analysis of detailed statistical records enable the evaluation of public policy effectiveness and the detection of subtle changes in social behaviors. Moreover, it promotes accountability by demonstrating how data management directly influences the creation of legal and health frameworks that prioritize the well-being and rights of vulnerable populations.
