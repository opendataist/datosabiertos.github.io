El 47 % de los hombres se identifica como padres

The article highlights a critical data gap in open data ecosystems, revealing that statistical efforts regarding sexual and reproductive health disproportionately focus on women. This scarcity of gender-disaggregated metrics obscures the male role in contraception and family planning, perpetuating a cultural narrative that assigns reproductive responsibility exclusively to females. By lacking comprehensive, accessible datasets on men’s engagement in these areas, researchers and policymakers are denied the evidence needed to address health disparities or design inclusive public health interventions. Demographic analysis exposes how the traditional concept of the patriarch shapes current social structures. Most identified fathers are household heads and primary earners, reinforcing an economic model where male identity is tied to provision rather than caregiving. This data underscores that while men are numerically present as parents, their involvement often remains defined by financial support and authority, rather than shared domestic or reproductive duties, limiting our understanding of evolving family dynamics. Reproductive behavior varies significantly across regions, indicating that local cultural factors heavily influence male participation in contraception. The stark contrast in contraceptive prevalence between states suggests that health outcomes are deeply rooted in specific sociocultural contexts. This geographic disparity is crucial for open data applications, as it demonstrates that a one-size-fits-all approach to family planning fails to account for regional nuances. Effective policy requires granular, open-access statistics that allow for targeted strategies addressing these localized challenges.
