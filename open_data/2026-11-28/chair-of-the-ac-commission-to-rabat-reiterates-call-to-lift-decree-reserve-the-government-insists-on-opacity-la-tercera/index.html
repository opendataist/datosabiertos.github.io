Chair of the AC Commission to Rabat reiterates call to lift decree reserve: “The government insists on opacity” - La Tercera

Chair of the AC Commission to Rabat reiterates call to lift decree reserve: “The government insists on opacity” - La Tercera

The legislative debate highlights the tension between transparency and government control over sensitive administrative information. The opposition demands that the secrecy surrounding sentence-reduction decrees be lifted, arguing that citizens and commissioners have the right to know the legal basis for these decisions. The Executive’s reluctance is questioned, with suggestions that the secrecy may be concealing inconsistencies or legal gaps in the administration of justice. The relevance of this case to the field of open data lies in the struggle for the accessibility and reuse of public information. Data are not merely administrative records but essential tools for accountability and democratic scrutiny. By anonymizing and restricting access to these documents, the government limits civil society’s ability to audit the efficiency and legality of its prison policies. The insistence on maintaining this opacity generates a profound critique of the culture of institutional secrecy. To promote open data, it is crucial to establish precedents in which the protection of personal data is not used as an excuse to hide errors or procedural deficiencies. Radical transparency, ensuring that the legal foundations are made public, strengthens trust in institutions and enables effective oversight of state power.
