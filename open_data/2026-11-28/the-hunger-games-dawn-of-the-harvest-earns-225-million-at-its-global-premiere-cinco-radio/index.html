‘The Hunger Games: Dawn of the Harvest’ earns $225 million at its global premiere - Cinco Radio

The severe impact of extreme rains linked to El Niño in Ecuador has caused a significant number of fatalities and affected thousands of people, highlighting the vulnerability of the coastal region. Authorities have declared a national emergency due to the overflowing of multiple rivers and the destruction of homes, forcing residents to face overcrowded conditions, loss of belongings, and health risks such as disease and lack of access to clean drinking water. This event is considered one of the most intense ever recorded, comparable in magnitude and human consequences to past crises. The scale of the damage underscores the urgent need to improve resilience against recurring climate phenomena, requiring effective coordination in risk management and humanitarian assistance to restore normalcy and protect the public health of displaced populations. The relevance of this case for open data lies in the critical importance of transparency and access to geospatial and statistical information during disasters. Continuous monitoring of hydrological variables, public recording of affected individuals, and dissemination of early warnings are essential for evidence-based decision-making. Furthermore, facilitating access to these data supports academic research and the optimization of public policies, promoting a more prepared society capable of responding to future global climate crises.
