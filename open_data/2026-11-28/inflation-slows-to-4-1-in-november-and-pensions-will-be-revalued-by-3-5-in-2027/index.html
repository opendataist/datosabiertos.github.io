Inflation slows to 4.1% in November, and pensions will be revalued by 3.5% in 2027

Inflation slows to 4.1% in November, and pensions will be revalued by 3.5% in 2027

The leading indicator of the Consumer Price Index (CPI) shows a significant moderation, driven by the decline in fuel and lubricant prices, thanks to government fiscal policies aimed at cushioning the impact of the conflict in the Middle East. This slowdown suggests that energy inflation has not spread to the rest of the consumption basket, reflecting relative stabilization in sectors such as food and clothing, which provides a positive signal regarding the containment of basic prices. It is crucial to highlight that this statistical information has direct implications for everyday life, as it determines the amount of pension adjustments for the following year. Since the adjustment is based on the average inflation rate over the previous twelve months, these advance data ensure that beneficiaries receive the corresponding update in their January paychecks, affecting millions of people and ensuring that purchasing power is maintained in the face of price developments. This case is relevant to the field of open data because it illustrates how transparency and the early publication of macroeconomic indicators enable society to anticipate specific financial and legal consequences. The availability of these data not only facilitates individual planning for pensioners but also demonstrates the importance of real-time public data for informed decision-making, directly linking official statistics with social protection policies and the understanding of the real impact of inflation.
