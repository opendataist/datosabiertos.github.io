$10M OVERSEAS TRAVEL BILL FOR TTPS

$10M OVERSEAS TRAVEL BILL FOR TTPS

Freedom of Information disclosures reveal that Trinidad and Tobago’s police service spent millions on overseas travel, primarily to provide security for senior officials during private vacations. This expenditure includes substantial costs for accompanying officers assigned to protect the President and former Prime Minister on personal trips abroad, highlighting a significant allocation of public funds for protective details during leisure travel rather than official state duties. A critical accountability gap exists within these financial records, as police officers are not required to submit reports or receipts upon returning from overseas engagements. Consequently, the agency cannot provide detailed explanations for certain high-cost trips, such as a nearly seven-hundred-thousand-dollar mission to the United Kingdom with no stated purpose. The absence of mandatory post-travel reporting means there is no formal mechanism to verify whether these large expenditures were necessary or appropriately utilized. This situation is highly relevant to open data principles because it illustrates the limitations of current transparency measures. While FOIA requests can unlock initial financial data, the lack of granular, mandatory reporting requirements prevents a complete understanding of how public money is spent. True accountability requires not just the release of figures, but the structural obligation to document and justify every aspect of government expenditure to ensure public trust and efficient resource management.
