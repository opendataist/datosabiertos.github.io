France contracts more than expected in the third quarter: GDP falls 0.2% and nears recession

France’s economy is technically in recession following a contraction in Gross Domestic Product (GDP) in the third quarter, exceeding expectations for decline and extending a negative pattern that began earlier this year. The data reveal structural weakness: household consumption managed only a slight increase, while fixed investment, particularly in construction and manufactured goods, fell sharply. This lack of dynamism in domestic investment, combined with a negative contribution from foreign trade, confirms overall stagnation and the current inability to drive sustained growth. Economic prospects for the coming years are extremely bleak, with estimates of annual growth nearly zero for the current year and minimal rates for the next. Declining business and consumer confidence, coupled with a labor market that generates uncertainty, keep economic agents cautious. The lack of visibility and weak domestic demand are expected to hinder any significant recovery in the short term, leaving France well below the eurozone’s growth expectations and facing predominantly downside risks for its future outlook. This report is relevant to the open data community because it illustrates how transparency in official macroeconomic figures, such as those published by INSEE, enables immediate and granular analysis of economic health. The timely availability of revised and sector-disaggregated data allows experts and analysts to detect critical trends, such as the specific slowdown in construction or manufacturing investment. This level of detail empowers civil society and researchers to independently verify official claims, promoting accountability and enabling an objective assessment of public policies and their real impact on society.
