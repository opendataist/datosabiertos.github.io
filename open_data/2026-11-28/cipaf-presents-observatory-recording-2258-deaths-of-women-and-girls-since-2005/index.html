CIPAF presents observatory recording 2,258 deaths of women and girls since 2005

CIPAF presents observatory recording 2,258 deaths of women and girls since 2005

The Center for Research on Women’s Action launched a digital observatory that highlights the severity of violence against women in the Dominican Republic, revealing that the official data system is insufficient and fragmented. The study underscores that a large majority of key indicators lack open public statistics, which hinders transparent and effective management of protection policies. This transparency gap prevents adequate assessment of the impact of institutional interventions and leaves victims without comprehensive follow-up. The collected data show that violence typically occurs in domestic settings and is perpetrated by current or former partners, with alarming figures of femicides and attempted femicides that leave hundreds of children orphaned. Additionally, a high rate of teenage pregnancies and reports of sexual abuse are documented, underscoring the vulnerability of girls and adolescents. The decline in formal complaints contrasts with the rise in emergency calls, suggesting that the true extent of violence may be underreported in traditional government records. This report is crucial for the open data movement because it demonstrates how the lack of access to public information obstructs accountability and the improvement of social services. By providing a free platform with verified information, the organization seeks to push for legislative updates and the implementation of a single, inter-institutional registry. In this way, it fosters shared responsibility among the government, the press, and civil society in making gender-based violence visible and eradicating it through radical data transparency.
