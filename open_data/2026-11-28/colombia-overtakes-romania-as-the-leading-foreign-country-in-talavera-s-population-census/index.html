Colombia overtakes Romania as the leading foreign country in Talavera's population census

The article highlights a significant demographic shift in Talavera de la Reina, where the Colombian community has surpassed the Romanian community as the largest foreign-born group. This explosive growth reflects a recent migratory dynamic that is reshaping the city’s multicultural composition, which is particularly relevant for open data analysis, as it demonstrates how census databases enable the visualization and quantification of these complex social changes in near real time. In addition to this restructuring of nationalities, there is a progressive aging of the overall population and a decline in younger age groups. These demographic patterns are crucial for urban and social planning, as open data facilitates the identification of long-term trends. Transparency in the management of these statistics allows citizens and policymakers to better understand the structural evolution of the community beyond isolated data points. The relevance of this text to open data lies in its practical demonstration of how publicly available statistics serve as a tool for social diagnosis. By making detailed information on origin, age, and distribution accessible, it fosters accountability and enables deeper research into integration and sustainability. Understanding these migratory and demographic flows is essential for designing informed and effective public policies.
