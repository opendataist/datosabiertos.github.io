The article attributes Bolivia’s high inflation primarily to political blockades and currency issues, highlighting how civil unrest directly distorts economic indicators. This underscores the critical importance of open data in identifying external shocks, allowing analysts to distinguish between structural economic problems and temporary political disruptions. By separating these factors, transparent statistical reporting enables a more accurate understanding of underlying price trends. This distinction is vital for policymakers and researchers who rely on clean, contextualized data to make informed decisions and assess true economic health without political noise. Ultimately, the piece illustrates how open data initiatives must account for socio-political context to be truly useful. Understanding the root causes of statistical anomalies ensures that open datasets reflect reality accurately, fostering trust and better decision-making in complex environments.
