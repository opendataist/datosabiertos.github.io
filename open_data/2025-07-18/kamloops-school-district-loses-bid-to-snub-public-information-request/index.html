Kamloops school district loses bid to snub public information request

Kamloops school district loses bid to snub public information request

The Kamloops-Thompson School District attempted to dismiss a Freedom of Information request by labeling the applicant’s actions as malicious harassment stemming from a lost construction contract. The district argued the company was weaponizing transparency laws, claiming the repeated inquiries were vexatious and intended to disrupt operations. This defensive stance highlights the tension between public institutions’ desire for operational ease and citizens’ right to accountability in public procurement. However, the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner rejected these assertions, ruling that the applicant had a legitimate motive to understand the tendering process. The adjudicator found insufficient evidence to support claims of bad faith or excessive burden on staff. This decision underscores that seeking clarity on public spending decisions is a valid exercise of transparency rights, rather than an abuse of the system, protecting the integrity of open data access against institutional pushback. This case is crucial for open data advocates as it establishes that procedural friction cannot be used to deny information access based on subjective interpretations of intent. It reinforces the principle that FOI requests must be evaluated on their factual basis rather than the petitioner’s relationship with the institution. By validating the applicant’s right to information despite the district’s hostility, the ruling sets a precedent that safeguards transparency mechanisms from being undermined by administrative resistance or personal grievances.
