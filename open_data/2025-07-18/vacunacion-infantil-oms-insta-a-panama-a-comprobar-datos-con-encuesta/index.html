Vacunación infantil: OMS insta a Panamá a comprobar datos con encuesta

Panama faces significant uncertainty in its child vaccination data, marked by a reported decline in the target population that the WHO and UNICEF consider insufficiently validated. In light of this discrepancy, it is strongly recommended to conduct high-quality surveys to distinguish between methodological errors, genuine demographic changes, or a true reduction in coverage. This process is vital for unraveling the root causes, which include previous supply shortages and difficulties in completing full vaccination schedules, although some vaccines, such as the measles vaccine, show notable improvements. The relevance of this article to open data lies in demonstrating that the availability of statistics does not guarantee their reliability. Without transparent verification mechanisms and access to the underlying methodology, public health authorities make decisions based on potentially flawed information. Transparency in how these indicators are generated and validated is essential to maintain public trust and ensure that resources are allocated where they are truly needed, avoiding stagnation in critical indicators. At the regional level, the situation illustrates the persistent risk of vaccine exclusion, with millions of children receiving no doses at all. This underscores the urgency of robust monitoring systems and accurate data that enable coordinated and equitable action. The openness and quality of data are not merely technical metrics but fundamental tools for protecting vulnerable children, ensuring equitable access to health services, and preventing the resurgence of preventable diseases through evidence-based interventions.
