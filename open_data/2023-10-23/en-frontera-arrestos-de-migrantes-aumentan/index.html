En frontera, arrestos de migrantes aumentan

The United States reports a sustained increase in migrant apprehensions at its southern border, cementing 2023 as one of the years with the highest volume of arrests since 1925. This rise reflects a historic migration trend across the continent, significantly driven by the influx of Venezuelans and other Central American nationalities, indicating that recently implemented restrictions have not succeeded in curbing the movement of people. In response, the border agency has increased resources and enhanced international cooperation, while resuming selective deportations to deter further flows. Despite these restrictive measures, the use of digital tools such as the CBP One app shows that many migrants are opting for formal asylum processes through scheduled appointments, highlighting the complexity and scale of the current humanitarian crisis. This article is relevant to the field of open data because it illustrates how real-time government statistics, such as those on apprehensions and asylum applications, are essential for analyzing migration trends. Transparency in these data enables civil society, academics, and international organizations to monitor public policies, assess their effectiveness, and understand the impact of government decisions on global humanitarian crises, thereby fostering evidence-based accountability.
