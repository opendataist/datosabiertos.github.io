Billions in local government pensions invested in fossil fuels – analysis

Analysis reveals that UK local government pension funds have allocated billions of pounds to fossil fuel investments, a practice that directly conflicts with municipal net zero goals. By allowing public savings to finance oil, gas, and coal projects, these funds undermine local climate action efforts. The findings highlight a critical disconnect where the financial assets of public sector workers are inadvertently supporting the very industries that exacerbate the climate crisis, thereby hindering the transition to a carbon-free future. This issue is particularly significant for open data advocates because it demonstrates the power of Freedom of Information requests to expose hidden financial flows and hold institutions accountable. The ability to scrutinize asset allocations through transparent data collection allows campaigners and researchers to identify systemic risks and advocate for policy changes. The study shows how structured data release can reveal gaps in climate risk reporting, prompting public debate on whether fiduciary duties should include ethical environmental considerations. Relevance to open data lies in its potential to drive governance reform. By making investment data accessible, stakeholders can pressure local authorities to divest from high-risk assets and align portfolios with sustainability targets. The disparity in investment strategies across regions further illustrates how data transparency can encourage best practices and peer learning among different local government pension schemes, ultimately fostering a more transparent and accountable approach to public fund management.
