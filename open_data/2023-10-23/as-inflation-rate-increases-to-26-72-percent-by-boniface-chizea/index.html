As inflation rate increases to 26.72 percent, By Boniface Chizea

As inflation rate increases to 26.72 percent, By Boniface Chizea

Nigeria is experiencing its highest inflation rate in decades, driven by structural economic failures rather than transient external shocks. The author argues that immediate policy decisions, specifically the abrupt removal of fuel subsidies and the ill-advised unification of exchange rates, have exacerbated a cost-push spiral. Rather than expecting rapid deceleration, the focus must shift to moderating the rate of increase, acknowledging that these issues stem from deep-seated inefficiencies in production and foreign exchange management rather than simple market fluctuations. The article emphasizes that sustainable relief requires addressing fundamental supply-side constraints, such as boosting domestic refining capacity through the Dangote Refinery to reduce dependency on costly fuel imports. Furthermore, it critiques the government’s approach to foreign exchange, suggesting that managing multiple rates and restricting access to essential goods for political expediency ignores the reality of a shortage of inflows. True stability would come from drastically reducing import dependence for critical commodities and ensuring the Central Bank adheres to its developmental mandate by supporting productive sectors like agriculture and manufacturing, rather than engaging in superficial market adjustments. This analysis is critically relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the urgency of transparent, accessible economic indicators to inform policy corrections. When inflation is structural and driven by complex factors like subsidy deregulation and exchange rate mismanagement, the public and policymakers require accurate, real-time data to distinguish between temporary symptoms and systemic causes. Without robust open data infrastructure, it becomes impossible to monitor the effectiveness of interventions, hold institutions accountable for fiscal mismanagement, or understand the true impact on poverty and social stability, making data transparency a prerequisite for economic recovery.
