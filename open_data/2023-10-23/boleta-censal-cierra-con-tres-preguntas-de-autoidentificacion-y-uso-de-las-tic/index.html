Boleta censal cierra con tres preguntas de autoidentificación y uso de las TIC

The debate surrounding Bolivia’s 2024 Census highlights the tension between the need for accurate data and the perception of a restrictive methodology in ethnic self-identification. Analysts criticize the reduction of identity categories, arguing that this simplification does not reflect the country’s true diversity nor adequately engage with civil society, potentially producing biased results that obscure real social changes. This critique underscores the importance of census instruments capturing complex nuances such as mestizaje (mixed heritage) and specific indigenous peoples. At the same time, the inclusion of new indicators related to access to information and communication technologies, as well as poverty measures, is viewed positively. These improvements align the census with the Sustainable Development Goals, enabling a more comprehensive assessment of well-being and the digital divide. By including questions on connectivity and economic conditions, the census aims to provide a fuller picture of inequality, although methodological challenges remain in directly measuring income. The relevance of this census for open data lies in its potential to inform the redistribution of public resources and the redefinition of political representation. The results will directly impact the electoral roll and the allocation of tax revenues to municipalities, making the quality and transparency of these data crucial for state planning and social justice. Ultimately, this information will allow for a precise identification of actual population dynamics, surpassing previous estimates and facilitating more accurate, evidence-based management grounded in verifiable empirical data.
