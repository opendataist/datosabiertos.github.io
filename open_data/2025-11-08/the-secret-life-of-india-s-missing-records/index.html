The Secret Life Of India's 'Missing' Records

The Secret Life Of India's 'Missing' Records

The frequent use of "file not traceable" by public authorities to deny information requests is identified as a deliberate breach of statutory duties, effectively undermining the Right to Information Act. Legal precedents from courts and the Central Information Commission establish that missing files do not justify non-disclosure; instead, they constitute proof of information denial. Authorities are legally obligated to proactively locate, reconstruct, or formally attest to the status of records, rather than using absence as an excuse to withhold transparency. This recurring pattern suggests intentional concealment, particularly in sensitive cases involving land allotments or disciplinary proceedings, rather than accidental misplacement. Judicial interpretations emphasize that public information officers must fulfill their solemn responsibility to provide accurate information in a truthful manner. The failure to maintain or produce records triggers penal actions, reinforcing that citizens are entitled to specific documents and cannot be driven away by bureaucratic obstruction or filibustering tactics. For open data initiatives, this highlights the critical necessity of digitizing public records to ensure permanent, accessible, and tamper-proof availability. Implementing robust cybersecurity, cloud backups, and mandatory online disclosure of RTI applications can eliminate the excuse of missing files. This shift not only streamlines governance but also restores public trust by ensuring that data integrity is preserved, thereby strengthening accountability and making the right to information truly enforceable in the digital era.
