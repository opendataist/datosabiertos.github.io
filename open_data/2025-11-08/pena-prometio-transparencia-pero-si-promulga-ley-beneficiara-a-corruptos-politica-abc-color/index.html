Peña prometió transparencia pero si promulga ley beneficiará a corruptos - Política - ABC Color

Peña prometió transparencia pero si promulga ley beneficiará a corruptos - Política - ABC Color

The article highlights a critical contradiction between President Peña’s past public commitments to transparency and the current legislative threat posed by Paraguay’s new data protection law. While he previously championed open government as essential for democracy, the enacted law contains a specific provision that could severely undermine these ideals by allowing officials to restrict access to their personal information, including details about their qualifications, travel, and employment status. This discrepancy creates a significant test for executive accountability, as the law effectively empowers state organs to mediate public access to data that should be freely available. The potential inclusion of this restrictive clause threatens to nullify existing freedom of information protections, creating a barrier that could hide conflicts of interest and prevent citizens from verifying the integrity of public servants. For the open data community, this situation is vital because it underscores the fragility of transparency frameworks when not backed by coherent political will. It demonstrates how seemingly neutral privacy laws can be manipulated to obscure public interest information. The outcome depends on whether the President exercises his veto power to remove the harmful article, proving if his earlier rhetoric was genuine or merely performative in the face of legislative pressure.
