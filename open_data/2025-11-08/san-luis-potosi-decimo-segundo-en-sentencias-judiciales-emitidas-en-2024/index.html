San Luis Potosí exhibits significant judicial inefficiency, evidenced by a high volume of unresolved cases. This backlog highlights systemic delays that impede timely justice, affecting both state and federal proceedings. Such transparency is vital for open data initiatives, as it allows citizens to monitor government performance and demand accountability. The entity balances this challenge with substantial financial resources and a relatively small administrative structure. Despite having fewer operational units than many peers, the budget allocation suggests a focus on maintaining infrastructure rather than efficiency. This disparity invites analysis of resource management within the public sector, a core interest of open data advocacy. Gender representation in judicial roles remains skewed, with men dominating the lower judiciary while women lead in higher magistracies. These demographic insights, derived from publicly released statistics, underscore the importance of accessible data. By revealing these inequalities, open data fosters informed public debate on diversity and equity in the legal system.
