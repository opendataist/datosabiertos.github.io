Kenya wins Bid to host the International Open Data Conference

The International Open Data Conference is shifting its focus to the Global South for the first time, with Nairobi, Kenya, selected to host the 2020 event. This pivotal move signals a strategic rebalancing of global open data leadership, recognizing Africa’s emerging capacity to drive innovation and address developmental challenges. By hosting this premier bi-annual gathering, Africa asserts its readiness to move from being a passive recipient of data initiatives to an active leader in shaping data policies and technologies. Kenya’s selection highlights the critical role of high-level political commitment and cross-sector collaboration in fostering an inclusive data ecosystem. The decision underscores that open data is no longer merely a technical issue but a rational economic and political choice essential for sustainable development. This shift encourages South-South collaboration, allowing nations to share lessons and build practical partnerships that leverage data for agriculture, health, and fiscal transparency, thereby accelerating impact across diverse workstreams. This event is highly relevant to open data as it emphasizes the necessity of bridging diverse communities—government, civil society, academia, and the private sector—to maximize data utility. With a theme focused on connecting these stakeholders, the conference aims to enhance interoperability and accessibility of data in support of global frameworks like the Sustainable Development Goals. Ultimately, it demonstrates how decentralized, inclusive data governance can empower economies and ensure that technological benefits reach all populations, leaving no one behind.
