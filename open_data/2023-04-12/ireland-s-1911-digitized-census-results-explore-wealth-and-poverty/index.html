Ireland's 1911 digitized census results explore wealth and poverty

The digitization of the 1911 Irish Census by the Central Statistics Office reveals stark historical economic disparities, identifying Meath as the wealthiest county and Donegal as the poorest. By analyzing valuation per person, the data illustrates a significant wealth concentration in Leinster alongside acute poverty in the western seaboard. This release highlights how historical statistical recovery initiatives can transform raw census records into accessible insights about societal structures and regional inequality over a century ago. Relevance to open data lies in the CSO’s commitment to making historical statistics publicly available through their PxStat database. This initiative, driven by volunteer efforts, preserves sensitive demographic information while empowering researchers and the public to explore complex narratives hidden within the data. The availability of these tables allows for granular analysis of population density, construction levels, and other factors, demonstrating the power of open data to uncover deep historical contexts beyond simple counts. Furthermore, this resource supports genealogical research and personalized data exploration, allowing individuals to investigate specific household stories from the era. The detailed breakdowns by area, housing, and population provide a comprehensive view of life in 1911, from large landed estates to modest thatched homes. By democratizing access to this historical data, the CSO enables a broader understanding of Ireland’s past, fostering transparency and encouraging further inquiry into the socioeconomic forces that shaped the nation.
