Una buena y una mala

Una buena y una mala

Mexico currently faces a paradoxical economic landscape in which inflation has cooled significantly, enabling the central bank to move closer to its stabilization targets. However, this progress masks a deeper stagnation in economic growth, driven by weak domestic demand and high borrowing costs that suppress consumer spending. The potential imposition of U.S. tariffs under the Trump administration poses an immediate threat, risking a contraction that could derail recovery efforts and deepen national uncertainty. The relevance to open data lies in the critical need for transparent, real-time economic indicators to monitor these vulnerabilities. Reliable public data on inflation, employment, and investment flows allow citizens and analysts to assess the true health of the economy beyond official forecasts. Without accessible and granular datasets, it becomes difficult to detect early warning signs of stagnation or to hold institutions accountable for fiscal management and policy outcomes, leaving society blind to structural risks. Looking ahead, long-term prosperity depends on reversing a historical trend of low growth by attracting sustained investment through nearshoring. This strategy requires a stable regulatory environment and political predictability, particularly regarding trade relations with the United States. The government must balance deficit reduction with growth-enabling policies to avoid a vicious cycle of unemployment and reduced competitiveness. Ultimately, leveraging high-quality open data is essential to design effective policies and ensure that economic decisions are grounded in accurate, verifiable evidence rather than political speculation.
