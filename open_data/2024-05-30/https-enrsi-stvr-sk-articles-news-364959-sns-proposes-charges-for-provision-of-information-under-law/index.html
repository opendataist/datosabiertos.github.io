The Slovak National Party proposes charging fees for Freedom of Information requests to offset administrative costs from excessive inquiries. While the government argues this improves efficiency, critics view it as an arbitrary barrier that undermines constitutional rights to transparency. Opposition lawmakers and environmental NGOs strongly condemn the amendment, warning it erodes public access to essential state data. They argue the lack of appeal mechanisms creates unpredictability, effectively punishing citizens seeking accountability for government spending and operations. This development is critical for open data advocates as it threatens the fundamental principle of free public access to non-secret information. It highlights the tension between administrative efficiency and democratic transparency, showing how financial barriers can restrict the flow of public sector data and hinder civic oversight.
