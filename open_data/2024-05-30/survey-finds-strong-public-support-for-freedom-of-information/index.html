Survey finds strong public support for freedom of information

Survey finds strong public support for freedom of information

The survey reveals overwhelming public support for Freedom of Information (FOI) as a cornerstone of democratic accountability. Respondents strongly believe that access to public information is crucial for preventing misconduct and ensuring transparency, dismissing concerns about cost as negligible. This consensus highlights the vital role FOI plays in maintaining trust between citizens and government institutions, reinforcing the idea that openness is essential for healthy democratic engagement. There is a significant push to expand FOI laws to cover newly emerging service providers, such as publicly funded care homes and contractors managing infrastructure projects. The public views these extensions as necessary to close loopholes where outsourced services might currently evade scrutiny. Strengthening these legal frameworks ensures that all entities delivering public services remain subject to the same transparency standards, thereby protecting the public interest across a broader spectrum of government activities. Furthermore, the research underscores a demand for stricter enforcement mechanisms, including criminal penalties for those attempting to subvert FOI legislation. This relevance to open data lies in the potential for Scotland to lead globally by adopting more robust information rights. By aligning legal reforms with public sentiment, policymakers can enhance data accessibility and integrity, setting a benchmark for how open data initiatives can be strengthened through legislative rigor and public empowerment.
