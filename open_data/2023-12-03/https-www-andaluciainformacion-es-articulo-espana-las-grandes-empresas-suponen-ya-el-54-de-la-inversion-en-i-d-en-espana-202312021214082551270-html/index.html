In 2022, Spain’s internal R&D expenditure reached a significant milestone, driven primarily by corporate investment, which has now become the main engine of national innovation growth. Despite this progress, the sector continues to underperform relative to global peers, underscoring a persistent gap in meeting strategic European targets. The heavy reliance on large enterprises highlights a structural trend in which major corporations, rather than public administrations or universities, are leading the country’s technological advancement and economic competitiveness. This corporate dominance has fostered an emerging ecosystem of innovation hubs, particularly in the telecommunications and security sectors. Major firms are establishing physical centers dedicated to open innovation and co-creation, focusing on advanced technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), the Internet of Things (IoT), and cybersecurity. These initiatives represent a strategic shift toward integrating research with business development, aiming to create sustainable solutions and enhance customer satisfaction through cutting-edge experimentation and the attraction of specialized talent. The relevance of open data lies in the need for transparency and interoperability within these new innovation frameworks. As companies centralize their R&D activities and adopt open innovation models, the generation, sharing, and analysis of data become critical assets. This trend calls for robust open data practices to facilitate collaboration among the private sector, public institutions, and academia, ensuring that the valuable data produced in these hubs contributes to broader societal benefits and accelerates technological progress across the economy.
