It contends that the right to life is unalienable and endowed by a Creator, meaning individuals do not possess absolute ownership over their existence. This perspective frames suicide not merely as a mental health issue, but as a failure of the political community to uphold its founding principles of preserving human life and dignity. Beyond immediate factors like drug abuse or pandemic aftermath, the author identifies a deeper societal cause: the breakdown of community structures and a decline in religious belief. This spiritual void creates a meaning crisis that material pursuits and secular frameworks cannot adequately address. The text suggests that ignoring the soulful and transcendent aspects of human personhood leaves individuals vulnerable to despair, contributing to the erosion of family and social bonds that traditionally support well-being. Relevance to open_data lies in the need for comprehensive, multi-dimensional data collection that captures social and spiritual health alongside traditional medical metrics. Current datasets often focus on clinical diagnoses and socioeconomic indicators, missing the nuanced context of community cohesion and meaning. To effectively address such complex societal challenges, researchers and policymakers must advocate for open data practices that integrate broader qualitative and contextual variables, enabling a more holistic understanding of the drivers behind public health crises.
