Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical character recognition transforms static image data into searchable, machine-readable text, solving fundamental limitations in managing digital information. This capability is crucial for modern businesses that must process print media, such as identity documents, without manual intervention. By converting visual data into structured text, organizations can seamlessly integrate physical records into digital workflows, enabling advanced analytics and operational automation. The technology relies on sophisticated software algorithms that analyze scanned bitmaps by distinguishing characters from background noise. Through pattern matching or feature extraction, systems identify letters and numbers even when standard fonts are not available. This technical precision allows for the accurate conversion of diverse document types into editable files, supporting tasks like form automation and biometric data binding for enhanced security. This article highlights open_data relevance by demonstrating how OCR unlocks hidden value in unstructured visual information. It bridges the gap between physical archives and digital ecosystems, making non-textual assets accessible for public and private analysis. By converting images into raw text, OCR facilitates transparency and efficient data utilization, proving essential for any initiative aiming to digitize and democratize access to printed records.
