The article highlights a severe educational crisis in Costa Rica, driven primarily by budget cuts and economic pressures that force vulnerable youth to abandon their studies. The absence of resources creates a multifactorial exclusion cycle: families lack funds for tuition and supplies, while schools suffer from poor infrastructure and technology deficits. Consequently, many students drop out to support their households through informal labor or caregiving, trapping them in poverty and limiting their access to dignified employment or higher technical training. This systemic failure undermines social mobility and equity, as incomplete education prevents young people from meeting basic requirements for vocational institutes or university entry. The burden falls disproportionately on the State, which is responsible for ensuring accessible educational opportunities. Without adequate financial support, scholarships, and infrastructure maintenance, the system perpetuates inequality, particularly affecting women who often balance education with domestic responsibilities, and men who are frequently pushed into early labor markets. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistics on enrollment, dropout rates, and socioeconomic barriers are essential for diagnosing policy failures. Open data allows citizens and researchers to track the impact of budget decisions on vulnerable populations, fostering accountability and enabling evidence-based advocacy for educational rights. By making these indicators public, society can better understand the structural causes of exclusion and demand effective interventions to break the cycle of poverty.
