Un nutricionista desvela el alimento más peligroso para los niños: «Es causante de asfixias cada año»

Un nutricionista desvela el alimento más peligroso para los niños: «Es causante de asfixias cada año»

The article highlights that cutting sausages into coin-shaped slices significantly increases the risk of choking for young children, as the size and shape can easily obstruct their airways. This common presentation is often mistakenly viewed as safe due to tradition, leading to a normalization of dangerous practices among caregivers and even some healthcare professionals. The core message urges families to change this cutting method, emphasizing that established habits do not justify ignoring life-threatening risks. The narrative addresses the prevalence of misconceptions in infant nutrition, noting that people often worry excessively about nuts while overlooking equally hazardous foods like sliced sausages, grapes, and raw carrots. By challenging the argument that "it has always been done this way," the text asserts that any food posing a risk comparable to a marble must be handled with extreme caution. The author stresses that proper cutting techniques are simple, evidence-based interventions that should be universally adopted to prevent annual incidents of asphyxiation during complementary feeding. This information is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for accessible, transparent, and widely distributed public health information. When reliable data regarding child safety is democratized through open channels, it empowers families and institutions to make informed decisions, breaking down false beliefs and harmful norms. Making such life-saving nutritional guidelines easily available ensures that prevention strategies reach all demographics, reducing accidents driven by misinformation or outdated traditions rather than scientific evidence.
