Preocupa el aumento de la violencia doméstica: en un año las denuncias crecieron un 8%

Preocupa el aumento de la violencia doméstica: en un año las denuncias crecieron un 8%

The article highlights a troubling upward trend in domestic violence, particularly affecting women and children, which persists regardless of political leadership. Official data reveals a significant increase in cases handled by judicial offices, indicating that the severity and frequency of these incidents are not decreasing but accelerating. This surge suggests a systemic failure to contain or reduce gender-based aggression, signaling a worsening crisis that demands immediate and robust societal attention. A critical implication is the simultaneous withdrawal of state support and resources, creating a dangerous gap in victim protection. Specialized organizations report a reduction in essential measures such as electronic monitoring and police assistance, compounded by budget cuts from both government entities and the private sector. This erosion of safety nets forces NGOs to rely on emergency fundraising, exposing how institutional retrenchment directly endangers vulnerable populations who lack adequate state-backed interventions. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the necessity of transparent, accessible statistics to hold institutions accountable and drive policy changes. By making data on domestic violence public, civil society can expose trends of neglect and advocate for effective resource allocation. Open data thus serves as a vital tool to challenge political narratives, validate the experiences of victims, and pressure authorities to fulfill their protective obligations in the face of rising violence.
