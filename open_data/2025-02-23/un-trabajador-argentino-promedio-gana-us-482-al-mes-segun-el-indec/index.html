Un trabajador argentino promedio gana US$ 482 al mes, según el Indec

The article highlights severe economic disparities in Argentina, revealing that poverty persists despite inflation controls. Average income has failed to keep pace with rising living costs, causing the Gini coefficient to increase and signaling deeper social inequality. A significant gender wage gap further exacerbates these financial pressures, particularly affecting household stability and widening the divide between different economic groups. Unemployment remains a critical challenge, with millions actively seeking work and youth disproportionately impacted. Regional variations show high joblessness in several provinces, contrasting with lower rates in others. The data indicates that a substantial portion of the population is either underemployed or working multiple jobs to survive, reflecting a fragile labor market where economic security is elusive for many citizens. This report is vital for open data advocates as it demonstrates how transparent statistical information from public institutions like Indec exposes systemic social issues. By making such detailed socioeconomic metrics accessible, open data enables critical analysis of government performance and public policy efficacy. It empowers researchers and citizens to verify official narratives against empirical evidence, fostering accountability and informed public debate regarding economic management and social welfare in democratic societies.
