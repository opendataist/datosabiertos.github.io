De los afiliados en Magallanes a la Administradora de Fondos de Cesantía sólo un 42% cotiza

De los afiliados en Magallanes a la Administradora de Fondos de Cesantía sólo un 42% cotiza

The article highlights a significant disparity in the Magallanes region between unemployment insurance affiliates and active contributors, revealing that a large portion of the workforce remains uncovered by the system. This gap underscores critical challenges in labor market inclusion and social protection coverage, suggesting that many workers are vulnerable to economic shocks without a safety net. The slow growth in active contributors despite a stable affiliate base points to structural issues in employment formalization that persist locally. Furthermore, the data exposes deep gender inequalities within the labor market, particularly evident in the wage gap and the predominance of women in informal sectors like domestic work, which often have different contribution requirements. The declining number of beneficiaries indicates a shift in unemployment trends, yet the underlying asymmetries between men and women in contract types and income levels remain stark. These disparities are crucial for understanding the real impact of labor policies and the effectiveness of social security mechanisms across different demographics. This report is relevant to open data because it illustrates the importance of accessible, disaggregated statistics in diagnosing social issues. By making detailed information about contributions, demographics, and benefits publicly available, researchers and policymakers can identify inequities and measure the effectiveness of labor reforms. Transparent data enables evidence-based decisions that can improve coverage, reduce gender gaps, and ensure that social security systems truly protect the most vulnerable workers.
