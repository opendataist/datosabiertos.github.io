Tribunales de inmigración en EEUU tienen pendientes más de 3,5 millones de casos de asilo

The collapse of the U.S. asylum system reveals a structural crisis in which judicial capacity fails to keep pace with migration, resulting in a monumental backlog of cases. This imbalance not only slows down proceedings but also leaves millions of people in uncertainty, underscoring the urgency of addressing root causes and improving administrative management. The lack of legal representation disproportionately affects migrants during deportation decisions, compromising the fairness of due process. This disparity highlights the need for mechanisms that guarantee fundamental rights, demonstrating how transparency in judicial data is essential to identify and correct systemic inequities in immigration justice. New government initiatives combine visa restrictions with regional humanitarian support to discourage irregular migration and combat human trafficking. This multifaceted approach is relevant to open data because it requires the integration and analysis of cross-border and sectoral datasets, enabling the evaluation of public policy effectiveness and fostering international collaboration based on verifiable and accessible evidence.
