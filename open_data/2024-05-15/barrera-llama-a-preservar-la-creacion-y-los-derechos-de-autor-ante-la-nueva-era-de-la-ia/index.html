Barrera llama a preservar la creación y los derechos de autor ante "la nueva era" de la IA

The article highlights a critical intersection between cultural policy and open data principles through the lens of Artificial Intelligence regulation. By advocating for strict transparency in AI development, officials emphasize the necessity of revealing how algorithms are trained and applied. This demand for verifiable information directly supports open data initiatives, which serve as the foundation for public accountability and trust in digital systems. Without accessible, clear data regarding AI operations, society cannot effectively monitor or understand the technologies shaping their reality. A central concern is the protection of human creativity and copyright against automated generation. The discussion underscores that preserving the rights of artists requires clearly distinguishing between human-made and machine-generated content. This distinction relies on accurate data labeling and provenance tracking, key components of high-quality open data. When data regarding the origin of digital assets is open and standardized, it empowers creators to maintain control over their work and ensures that economic value remains with human talent rather than opaque corporate algorithms. Ultimately, the push for transparency is framed as a constitutional duty to prevent discrimination and ensure an informed citizenry. The relevance to open data lies in the requirement that governments and tech companies make their processes visible. Open data is not just about releasing statistics; it is about enabling democratic oversight of emerging technologies. By making AI usage knowable, society can uphold ethical standards and protect cultural integrity, demonstrating that transparent data practices are essential for responsible innovation.
