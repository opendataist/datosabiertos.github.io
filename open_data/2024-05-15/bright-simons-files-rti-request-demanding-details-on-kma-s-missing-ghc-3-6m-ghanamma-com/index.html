Bright Simons files RTI request demanding details on KMA's 'missing' GH¢3.6m - Ghanamma.com

The article highlights a significant test of access to information laws in Ghana, where lawyers for a public official are using Right to Information legislation to demand transparency from a commercial bank. By classifying the financial institution as a relevant private body due its handling of public funds, the applicants argue that commercial secrecy cannot override the public’s right to know how taxpayer resources are managed. This legal strategy directly challenges the boundary between private banking operations and public accountability. This case is highly relevant to open data because it establishes a precedent for demanding the disclosure of financial records held by non-governmental entities when they interface with state assets. It demonstrates how open data principles can extend beyond government agencies to include any organization processing public money, ensuring that transparency mechanisms are robust enough to cover quasi-public financial interactions. Ultimately, the implications suggest that open data frameworks must evolve to encompass the entire ecosystem of public fund utilization, not just direct government outputs. Ensuring comprehensive accountability requires treating data from private partners as public data when it pertains to public interest, thereby strengthening democratic oversight and preventing opacity in large-scale infrastructure projects funded by the state.
