Jefe del INEI confirma que postergación del informe de pobreza fue por orden expresa de la PCM

Jefe del INEI confirma que postergación del informe de pobreza fue por orden expresa de la PCM

The article reveals that the Peruvian government explicitly ordered the National Institute of Statistics and Informatics (INEI) to delay the release of the 2023 poverty report, aiming to censor the rise in poverty rates during President Boluarte’s first year. Despite this political pressure, the statistical agency published the data as scheduled, confirming that monetary poverty increased to affect twenty-nine percent of the population and extreme poverty rose significantly. This incident highlights the tension between technical transparency and political sensitivity in public administration. The attempt to manipulate the timing of data dissemination underscores the challenges faced by independent statistical bodies when their findings contradict government narratives. It demonstrates how executive branches may exert influence over official statistics to manage public perception during periods of socioeconomic deterioration. This story is crucial for open data advocates as it illustrates the vulnerability of public datasets to political interference. It serves as a warning that accessibility does not guarantee non-interference, emphasizing the need for robust legal protections for statistical independence. Ensuring that data release mechanisms are insulated from executive orders is vital for maintaining public trust in official statistics and the integrity of open government initiatives.
