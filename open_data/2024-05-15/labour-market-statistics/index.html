Labour Market Statistics

Northern Ireland’s labor market demonstrates a resilient recovery, characterized by rising employment rates and improved earnings despite monthly fluctuations in workforce size. The overall trend indicates that more people are working, and those employed are earning significantly more compared to the previous year, signaling a strengthening economic foundation. This positive trajectory suggests that the region has largely stabilized its workforce dynamics, with key indicators moving in favorable directions even as certain monthly metrics show minor contractions. However, this progress is counterbalanced by a notable surge in job losses, with both proposed and confirmed redundancies doubling compared to the prior year. This spike in layoffs raises concerns about underlying stability, although the volume of job cuts remains comparable to pre-pandemic levels. The disparity between high employment and increasing redundancies highlights a transitional phase where job creation is outpacing job destruction, yet the rapid pace of exits warrants close monitoring to prevent future volatility. This data is highly relevant to open_data initiatives because it illustrates the critical need for transparent, accessible, and timely statistical reporting to inform policy and public discourse. By making these diverse indicators—from payroll figures to redundancy counts—available, governments enable stakeholders to assess economic health accurately. Open access to such granular labor metrics fosters accountability, allows for better resource allocation, and empowers researchers to identify emerging trends, ensuring that societal decisions are grounded in comprehensive, real-world evidence rather than isolated snapshots.
