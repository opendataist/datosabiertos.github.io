Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

This article highlights the critical role of transparency in higher education by utilizing Freedom of Information requests to reveal granular academic performance data. By accessing and publishing detailed attainment metrics, such as average marks and grade distributions across specific modules, the report demonstrates how open data can demystify institutional outcomes. This approach shifts assessment from opaque institutional decisions to publicly verifiable facts, empowering stakeholders to evaluate the effectiveness of different academic programs. The analysis reveals significant disparities in student achievement across various disciplines and year groups. Certain departments, like Physics and English, demonstrate high attainment rates, while others, including foundation years and specific language courses, show notably lower performance. These variations suggest that factors beyond student ability, such as module design or assessment structures, heavily influence results. Understanding these patterns is essential for identifying strengths and weaknesses within the curriculum, fostering a more equitable educational environment. The relevance to open data lies in its potential to drive accountability and informed decision-making. When universities share comprehensive grading statistics, it enables students, parents, and policymakers to make evidence-based choices regarding course selection and institutional quality. Furthermore, this transparency encourages departments to reflect on their pedagogical methods, potentially leading to improvements in teaching standards. Ultimately, treating academic data as a public resource promotes a culture of continuous improvement and trust within the university sector.
