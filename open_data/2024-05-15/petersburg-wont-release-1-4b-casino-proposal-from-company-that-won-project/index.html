Petersburg won’t release $1.4B casino proposal from company that won project

Petersburg officials have refused to release the winning proposal from Cordish Companies for a major casino development, citing that the competitive bidding process was canceled and no formal contract was awarded. This decision has sparked significant controversy and criticism from open government advocates, who argue that using procedural technicalities to withhold public records undermines transparency laws. By treating the file as closed, the city is avoiding disclosure of detailed financial projections, timelines, and community impact assessments that would allow citizens to evaluate the project’s viability and fairness. The lack of public scrutiny is compounded by allegations that the selection process was influenced by external political pressures rather than independent local governance. City attorneys have suggested the bid was a forced condition for state approval, while disputes over a prior letter of intent hint at behind-the-scenes maneuvering. These concerns suggest a departure from standard procurement practices, raising questions about whether the chosen developer was selected based on merit or political convenience, further eroding trust in local decision-making. This situation is critically relevant to open data principles because it highlights how governments can exploit legal ambiguities to obscure high-stakes economic decisions. When public records are withheld, citizens lose the ability to access the data necessary for informed democratic participation and accountability. Ensuring that procurement documents remain accessible, regardless of procedural shortcuts, is essential for maintaining integrity in public spending and ensuring that community benefits are not negotiated in secret.
