The FOI head wants to improve public access to government information. How?

The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner’s first comprehensive survey of Freedom of Information (FOI) practitioners highlights systemic gaps in how government agencies manage public records. A primary finding is the reliance on outdated, static tools like basic spreadsheets rather than integrated digital systems. This technological deficit hinders efficient document retrieval and reporting, suggesting that investing in modern records management infrastructure is essential for simplifying compliance and enabling proactive information release. Furthermore, the report reveals that staff training remains largely informal and inconsistent across agencies. While most learning occurs through on-the-job experience or internal resources, there is a clear opportunity to formalize and expand educational programs. By standardizing training approaches and leveraging external guidance, agencies can ensure all employees are adequately equipped to handle FOI obligations, thereby reducing errors and fostering a culture of transparency rather than mere legal compliance. Ultimately, FOI is often siloed within legal departments rather than being viewed as a whole-of-agency responsibility. Elevating this function requires strong leadership to ensure all public servants act as stewards of government-held information. This insight is crucial for the open data movement because it underscores that true transparency depends not just on releasing data, but on embedding robust information management practices and accountability into the daily operations of all government bodies.
