Siete de cada diez quejas ciudadanas tienen que ver con la falta de respuesta a información que se solicita

Siete de cada diez quejas ciudadanas tienen que ver con la falta de respuesta a información que se solicita

The Transparency Commission of Castilla y León underscores the critical role of independent oversight in enforcing citizens’ right to public information. Data show that local administrations are the primary source of disputes, with a significant majority of complaints arising from a lack of response or unjustified denials by municipalities. This pattern highlights the need for accessible judicial mechanisms to counter administrative opacity and ensure that local government entities remain accountable to the public they serve. The results demonstrate that formal complaints effectively compel transparency, as many cases are resolved favorably or through administrative compliance even before a final ruling is issued. However, certain denials persist, often justified by data protection concerns or procedural arguments such as repetitive requests. Striking a balance between privacy rights and public access remains a complex challenge, requiring careful legal interpretation to maintain trust while protecting individual rights. This article is relevant to open data because it validates the practical utility of open data frameworks as tools for accountability. It illustrates that, without robust enforcement mechanisms, the mere existence of open data policies may not guarantee actual access. The high rate of successful resolutions shows that structured complaint processes are essential to transform theoretical openness into tangible transparency, encouraging other regions to adopt similar robust oversight structures.
