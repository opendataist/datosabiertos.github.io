HMRC simple assessments soar 74% in a year as stealth tax hits pensioners - IFA Magazine

HMRC simple assessments soar 74% in a year as stealth tax hits pensioners - IFA Magazine

The article reveals a dramatic surge in HMRC’s use of "simple assessments," a process where tax bills are issued automatically without requiring the taxpayer to file a return. This method has become increasingly prevalent as government policies, including frozen income tax thresholds and rising state pensions, push more individuals into the tax bracket. Consequently, many people, particularly pensioners, are facing unexpected liabilities due to fiscal drag, often unaware they owe money until the bill arrives. The relevance to open data lies in the critical role Freedom of Information requests played in exposing these trends. By obtaining official HMRC statistics through FOI, researchers and consumers gained visibility into government collection practices that were previously opaque. This transparency highlights how open data initiatives can uncover systemic shifts in public administration, allowing the public to scrutinize policy impacts that might otherwise remain hidden within bureaucratic processes. Ultimately, this case underscores the importance of accessible government data in empowering citizens to understand their financial obligations. The availability of such statistics enables experts to identify widespread issues like stealth taxation, fostering public awareness and prompting individuals to seek clarification or assistance. It demonstrates that open data is not just about transparency for its own sake, but a vital tool for holding institutions accountable and protecting individuals from unforeseen administrative burdens.
