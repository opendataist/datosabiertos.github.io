Meta entrenó su IA con textos pirateados: Lo que se esconde tras el saqueo digital más sofisticado del momento

Meta entrenó su IA con textos pirateados: Lo que se esconde tras el saqueo digital más sofisticado del momento

Meta’s deliberate use of pirated academic content from sources like LibGen to train its AI models reveals a troubling extractive logic inherent to big tech’s economic model. This action highlights a paradox where a corporation enforcing strict copyright norms against others exploits illegal repositories for its own commercial gain, prioritizing profit over educational value. The case underscores how major tech entities appropriate shared cultural resources without proper recognition or compensation for the original creators, perpetuating exploitative digital capitalism practices. This incident demonstrates the tension between traditional copyright structures and the demand for accessible knowledge, showing how tools created to resist restrictive publishing systems can be co-opted for private technological advancement. The true concern lies in the neutralization of academic effort, transforming collective scholarly work into free raw material for proprietary AI development. It illustrates a shift from mere copyright violation to the systemic appropriation of knowledge culture, stripping creators of agency and context in the process. This situation is critically relevant to open data because it challenges the foundational principles of openness, transparency, and equitable access that define the open data movement. If the most powerful technology corporations bypass open data ecosystems by illegally harvesting data, it erodes trust and undermines efforts to create fair, sustainable knowledge sharing. The article argues that solving this issue requires rejecting both rigid copyright monopolies and predatory extraction, advocating instead for ethical frameworks that ensure knowledge remains a shared resource rather than a privatized commodity.
