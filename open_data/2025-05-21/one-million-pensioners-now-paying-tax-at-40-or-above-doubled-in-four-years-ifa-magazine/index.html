One million pensioners now paying tax at 40% or above - doubled in four years - IFA Magazine

One million pensioners now paying tax at 40% or above - doubled in four years - IFA Magazine

The number of pensioners paying income tax at higher or additional rates has doubled in four years, surpassing one million. This surge is driven by frozen tax thresholds combined with significant, inflation-linked increases in state pensions. Consequently, a larger portion of the retired population is falling into higher tax brackets, shifting the financial landscape for millions of retirees who previously relied on tax-free allowances. Crucially, crossing the higher rate threshold by a minimal amount triggers disproportionate tax increases on savings and capital gains. Pensioners lose their Personal Savings Allowance, causing interest income to become taxable, and face higher Capital Gains Tax rates. This creates a severe "cliff-edge" effect where a small increase in overall income results in a disproportionate loss of net wealth, punishing those who just barely exceed the threshold with what can be described as a triple tax penalty. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights how transparency mechanisms, such as Freedom of Information requests, can expose significant societal impacts hidden within complex government statistics. Without accessing granular, specific data on pensioner tax rates, the scale of this issue and its regressive nature would likely remain obscured. It demonstrates that open data is essential for identifying structural inequalities and ensuring policymakers are held accountable for decisions that disproportionately affect vulnerable demographics.
