EEUU reporta 80 mil muertes por sobredosis de drogas en 2024 - Noticias Prensa Latina

EEUU reporta 80 mil muertes por sobredosis de drogas en 2024 - Noticias Prensa Latina

Recent data indicates that overdose deaths have decreased compared to the previous year, yet remain a leading cause of death for young adults and exceed pre-pandemic levels. This trend underscores the ongoing public health crisis, emphasizing that sustained efforts are essential to preserve any recent progress. Experts warn that current gains are fragile and could be reversed by changes in policy or funding priorities. The relevance to open data is critical, as the study quantifying the impact on millions of children relies on national surveys. However, the team responsible for this essential data collection was disbanded amid administrative restructuring. This dismantling of data-gathering capabilities threatens the transparency and availability of vital statistics, undermining the evidence-based approach necessary to effectively address addiction and its societal consequences. Furthermore, drastic cuts to federal funding for treatment and research programs raise serious concerns about the long-term effectiveness of public health strategies. The reduction of resources for addiction research risks halting momentum in reducing overdose mortality. Maintaining access to accurate, timely, and comprehensive data is crucial for holding authorities accountable and ensuring that policy decisions are informed by empirical evidence rather than political agendas.
