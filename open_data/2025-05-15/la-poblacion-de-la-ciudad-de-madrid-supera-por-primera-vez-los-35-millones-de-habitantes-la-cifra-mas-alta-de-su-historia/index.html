La población de la ciudad de Madrid supera por primera vez los 3,5 millones de habitantes, la cifra más alta de su historia

La población de la ciudad de Madrid supera por primera vez los 3,5 millones de habitantes, la cifra más alta de su historia

Madrid has reached a historic milestone by surpassing 3.5 million registered residents, marking the first time in the series’ history that the capital has exceeded this demographic threshold. This significant population growth is primarily driven by international migration, particularly from South American countries, rather than natural increase. The balance between births and deaths has become virtually neutral, highlighting that migratory flows, both external and internal, are the decisive factors shaping the city’s current demographic structure and expansion. The city’s population is becoming increasingly diverse, with one-fifth of residents being foreign nationals and nearly a third born abroad. While internal migration shows a net outflow to other Spanish municipalities, international immigration strongly compensates for this loss, solidifying Madrid as a preferred residential destination. This trend reflects a broader shift in the capital’s social fabric, where external mobility dynamics outweigh local residential exchanges, thereby sustaining the upward population trajectory observed throughout the decade. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because the Municipal Register (Padrón Municipal) serves as a critical, granular source for analyzing urban dynamics. It provides essential insights into neighborhood-level trends, such as the specific growth patterns in peripheral districts versus the center. For researchers and policymakers, this publicly available statistical record is vital for understanding social integration, planning public services, and addressing housing and infrastructure needs based on accurate, real-time demographic evidence.
