The NAEOTOM Alpha scanner provides high-resolution imaging with significantly reduced radiation doses, enabling faster, less invasive diagnoses and more informed therapeutic decisions. This technology lowers operational costs for hospitals while improving patient outcomes, marking a major advancement in medical diagnostics and care efficiency. Despite its potential to reduce mortality rates from malignant tumors, Mexico and Latin America face regulatory and economic barriers to adopting this technology. Closing this gap is essential for public health, as access to such resources could directly influence survival rates in regions with high cancer mortality, underscoring a critical healthcare equity issue. This case highlights the importance of open data in health technology by emphasizing the need for transparent and accessible information on medical innovations. Sharing performance metrics, cost analyses, and outcome studies can inform policymakers and stakeholders, promoting equitable adoption of advanced diagnostic tools and driving evidence-based improvements in healthcare systems.
