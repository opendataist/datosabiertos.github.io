Lasting peace in Ukraine not possible without accountability: Council of Europe

European leaders affirmed that lasting peace in Ukraine requires accountability, establishing a damage register as a foundation for international compensation. This mechanism ensures victims receive legal redress, emphasizing that without justice for aggression, enduring stability remains impossible. It highlights the critical role of transparent, accessible data in documenting war crimes and facilitating future reparations, which aligns with open data principles by creating a verifiable, public record of violations. Additionally, the summit adopted principles strengthening democratic standards, including freedom of expression and judicial independence. By prioritizing these values, leaders reinforce the necessity of transparent governance and civil society participation. These commitments support open data initiatives that promote government accountability and allow citizens to scrutinize decision-making processes, thereby enhancing trust in democratic institutions. The assembly also addressed human rights within the environmental crisis, recognizing the right to a clean environment. This intersection of environmental and legal frameworks suggests that open data on ecological impacts can serve as evidence for rights enforcement. Ultimately, these decisions underscore how accessible information and legal accountability are intertwined, providing a model for using data to uphold international law and protect vulnerable populations.
