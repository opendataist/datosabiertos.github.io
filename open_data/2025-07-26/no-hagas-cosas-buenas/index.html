No hagas cosas buenas...

No hagas cosas buenas...

The latest National Survey on Urban Public Security reveals a national trend of rising insecurity, driven largely by cartel conflicts and shifting political strategies. However, regional data highlight a distinct contrast, showing that states such as Coahuila and Durango generally maintain safer perceptions than the national average. This divergence underscores how local governance and specific security policies significantly influence citizen confidence, suggesting that effective local management can mitigate broader national instability. At the city level, performance varies drastically: places like Saltillo and Piedras Negras demonstrate stability or improvement, while Torreón faces concerning increases in perceived risk. These disparities indicate that security is not uniform even within neighboring regions, emphasizing the critical role of municipal leadership. The negative trend in Torreón, potentially linked to extortion in the agricultural sector, serves as a warning that complacency can quickly erode hard-won stability, requiring constant vigilance and targeted interventions. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the value of granular, publicly available statistical metrics in diagnosing regional security conditions. By disaggregating national averages into city-specific insights, open data enables stakeholders to identify emerging threats, such as sector-specific crimes, and evaluate the efficacy of local policies. This transparency fosters accountability and allows communities to leverage evidence-based information to advocate for resources and strategies that protect economic prosperity and social well-being.
