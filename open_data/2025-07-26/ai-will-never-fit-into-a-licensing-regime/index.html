AI Will Never Fit Into A Licensing Regime

AI Will Never Fit Into A Licensing Regime

The article argues that traditional licensing models for AI training data are fundamentally flawed and unlikely to benefit artists. Because AI training involves ingesting vast datasets to learn abstract concepts rather than reproducing specific works, individual contributions become statistically insignificant. Consequently, any potential revenue share would be so diluted that it offers no meaningful compensation, rendering the current approach to licensing artists’ work in this context ineffective. Furthermore, establishing financial rights based on stylistic influence or model impact is legally and technically impractical. Current copyright frameworks do not recognize rights over non-infringing stylistic similarities, and quantifying an artist’s specific contribution to a "black box" model is impossible due to how neural networks process information. Attempting to solve these issues through complex royalty structures ignores the core nature of AI learning and fails to address the reality that outputs are generally not direct copies of training data. The piece suggests that the most effective strategy for artists is adaptation rather than litigation or licensing. Artists are already integrating AI as a tool to enhance their workflows and create higher-quality work, ensuring their relevance in the creative industry. Additionally, denying copyright protection to low-effort AI-generated content may serve as a safeguard, preventing large corporations from exploiting AI to replace human creators. This perspective is vital to open data discussions as it challenges the assumption that data usage must be monetized through traditional copyright frameworks and highlights the need for new legal and economic models that reflect the unique mechanics of machine learning.
