This article highlights breast cancer as Mexico’s most frequent malignancy and a leading cause of death, emphasizing the urgency of awareness campaigns during October. It details clinical symptoms and notes that triple-negative metastatic cases pose a significant therapeutic challenge for health systems due to their resistance to hormonal treatments. The report reveals regional disparities, such as Chiapas reporting notably lower incidence rates compared to national averages. This geographic variation underscores the importance of localized epidemiological data in understanding disease distribution and resource allocation across different Mexican states. This content is relevant to open data as it relies on official statistics from INEGI to map disease prevalence and mortality. Transparent access to such health metrics enables researchers and policymakers to identify vulnerable populations, tailor public health interventions, and evaluate the effectiveness of screening programs based on empirical, publicly available evidence.
