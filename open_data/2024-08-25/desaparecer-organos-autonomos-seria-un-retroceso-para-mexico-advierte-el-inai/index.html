Desaparecer órganos autónomos sería un retroceso para México, advierte el INAI

The National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI) strongly opposes proposals to eliminate autonomous bodies in Mexico, arguing that their removal would significantly weaken the country’s democratic structure and fundamental rights. The Institute warns that these entities serve as essential checks on power, ensuring accountability and transparency. Rather than dismantling them, the INAI advocates for an open parliamentary dialogue to modernize and strengthen the National Transparency System, adapting it to contemporary challenges in technology and human rights governance. This stance is critical for open data because autonomous institutions guarantee the legal and operational framework necessary for public accountability. By preserving these bodies, Mexico ensures that mechanisms for accessing information and protecting personal data remain robust, preventing a regression in citizen liberties. The INAI emphasizes that only through fortified transparency mechanisms can the nation advance toward a more equitable society where government power is exercised with integrity and respect for civil rights. The active leadership of the current commissioners further underscores the ongoing commitment to defending transparency and data protection. Their expertise and recent appointments highlight the vital role these individuals play in shaping policies that safeguard open data practices. Ultimately, the article serves as a rallying cry for collaborative action to preserve institutional independence, ensuring that open data frameworks continue to function as pillars of a healthy democracy and effective governance.
