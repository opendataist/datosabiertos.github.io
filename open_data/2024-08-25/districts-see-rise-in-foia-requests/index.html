Districts see rise in FOIA requests

Districts see rise in FOIA requests

FOIA Buddy, a for-profit service that simplifies public record requests, has flooded several Pennsylvania school districts with bulk submissions, prompting significant administrative strain. District officials describe the influx as a nuisance, noting that fulfilling these demands consumes substantial staff time and legal resources, thereby disrupting daily operations and increasing costs for taxpayers who must already operate under tight budgets. While the state’s Office of Open Records confirms that using third-party tools or charging fees for such services is legal and not a scam, school leaders argue the practice exploits loopholes. They contend that FOIA Buddy engages in data mining to profit from publicly available information, effectively bypassing the intent of transparency laws by aggregating data rather than seeking specific, legitimate public interest disclosures. This tension highlights a gap in legislation regarding commercial aggregation of public records. This situation is crucial to the open data community as it illustrates the real-world friction between automated data access and public institution capacity. It raises urgent questions about the sustainability of open data ecosystems when commercial entities can scale request volumes beyond what public servants can reasonably manage. The conflict underscores the need for clearer regulatory frameworks that balance the right to information with the operational realities of government transparency.
