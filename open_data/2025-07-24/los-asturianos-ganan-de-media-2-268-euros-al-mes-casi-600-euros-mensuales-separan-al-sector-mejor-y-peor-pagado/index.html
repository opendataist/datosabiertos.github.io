Los asturianos ganan de media 2.268 euros al mes: casi 600 euros mensuales separan al sector mejor y peor pagado

Los asturianos ganan de media 2.268 euros al mes: casi 600 euros mensuales separan al sector mejor y peor pagado

Asturias has consolidated its position as the fifth autonomous community with the highest average salary, registering a sustained annual increase that places the region behind Madrid, the Basque Country, Catalonia, and Navarre. This rise, driven by the industrial sector, reflects a continuous trend of labor market growth since 2021, although a notable wage gap persists between the best- and worst-paid sectors. The data reveal a regional economy that, despite these increases, still lags behind the areas with the highest national remuneration. However, the economic analysis reveals a critical distortion between nominal figures and real well-being. Despite salary increases, inflation has completely eroded these gains, leading to a significant decline in purchasing power in recent years. This loss of buying capacity makes the region one of the most affected nationally, demonstrating that superficial wage growth does not necessarily translate into improved living conditions for the working population. This contrast between nominal wages and purchasing power is fundamental to the field of open data, as it highlights the need to interconnect different statistical sources. To obtain a realistic view of the economy, it is crucial to cross-reference labor cost data from the National Statistics Institute (INE) with inflation and purchasing power indices, such as those from the Adecco Monitor. Only through this multi-source integration can the gap between what people earn and what they can actually afford be accurately identified, facilitating more precise analyses of inequality and social well-being.
