Secretive Albanese government goes backward on transparency

Secretive Albanese government goes backward on transparency

Analysis reveals that the current Labor government has become significantly more secretive than its predecessor, despite campaigning on a platform of integrity. The proportion of Freedom of Information requests released in full has plummeted, while the rate of outright refusals has spiked. This shift indicates a regression in government transparency, suggesting that the administration is prioritizing information control over the open disclosure it previously promised to uphold. The reliability of these secrecy claims is questionable, as official reviews found that more than half of the refusals to release documents lacked legitimate grounds. Furthermore, the government has been far less responsive to Senate requests for policy details compared to the previous era. This pattern, coupled with an increased reliance on non-disclosure agreements to silence stakeholders during policy consultations, points to a deliberate effort to avoid public scrutiny and undermine constitutional accountability mechanisms. This situation is critically relevant to open data because it highlights the fragility of data accessibility when political will declines. When governments systematically obscure decision-making processes and limit public access to information, the potential for data-driven accountability evaporates. The findings serve as a stark warning that technical access to data is insufficient without robust legal frameworks and political commitment to transparency, which are essential for maintaining public trust in bureaucratic institutions.
