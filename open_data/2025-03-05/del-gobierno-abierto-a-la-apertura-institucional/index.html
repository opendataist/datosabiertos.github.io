Del gobierno abierto a la apertura institucional

Del gobierno abierto a la apertura institucional

The imminent extinction of transparency bodies in Mexico signifies the formal end of the tripartite open government model, historically reliant on alliances between civil society, the state, and oversight agencies. Although this collaborative framework once fostered innovation and inclusion, its viability is now compromised by the absence of a key pillar and a prior decline in active partnerships. This structural shift represents a critical juncture, moving beyond simple administrative changes to redefine how democratic participation and institutional accountability are sustained in the country. In response, a new institutional design emphasizes proactive transparency and effective citizen participation as the foundational elements of an open state. This approach requires public institutions to publish relevant information proactively, rather than merely responding to requests, ensuring data is accessible and useful for decision-making. Simultaneously, it mandates the creation of mechanisms that allow genuine societal involvement in policy formulation and evaluation. This transition seeks to strengthen trust and ensure that government actions are more closely aligned with the observable needs and opinions of the population. This evolution is vital for open data because it shifts the focus from procedural compliance to substantive utility and engagement. By reconfiguring the agenda around active disclosure and meaningful interaction, the new model aims to create a more robust and participatory democracy. The relevance lies in the opportunity to build a sustainable, innovative strategy for state openness that adapts to current challenges, ensuring that data serves as a true tool for civic empowerment rather than just a bureaucratic obligation.
