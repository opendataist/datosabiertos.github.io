Fauci adviser says he helped Fauci evade FOIA: 'He is too smart' to get caught

The article highlights alarming evidence that senior public health officials intentionally circumvented transparency laws during the pandemic. New disclosures reveal that advisors helped senior figures evade the Freedom of Information Act by utilizing private email accounts or in-person document transfers, effectively removing critical records from public scrutiny. This suggests a deliberate systemic effort to shield decision-making processes from external accountability and journalistic oversight. This behavior underscores the fragility of current government record-keeping practices when confronted with intense political pressure. The use of informal channels to bypass official documentation protocols indicates that important data regarding public health decisions may be permanently obscured. Such actions erode public trust and demonstrate how institutional safeguards can be manipulated to prevent meaningful historical review or legislative investigation into executive actions. This story is directly relevant to the open data movement, as it illustrates the tangible consequences of non-transparent government operations. It reinforces the critical need for robust digital preservation standards that mandate the use of official, auditable channels for all substantive government communications. Without strict adherence to open data principles, vital information regarding public policy remains hidden, preventing citizens and researchers from accessing the complete truth necessary for informed democracy.
