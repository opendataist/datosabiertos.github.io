Víctima de la 4T, Yolli García revela la transa de Cuitláhuac con 160 patrullas

Víctima de la 4T, Yolli García revela la transa de Cuitláhuac con 160 patrullas

The testimony of Yolli García Álvarez reveals a clear hostility on the part of the Veracruz state government toward autonomous transparency bodies, as illustrated by her arbitrary detention after leaving office. This case serves as an alarming example of how the executive branch can exploit the criminal justice system to silence and punish those who demand accountability, marking the beginning of a protracted legal battle lacking solid evidentiary support. The conflict arose specifically over key decisions made by the former commissioner to protect minors’ privacy and to demand details about the acquisition of police vehicles and possible nepotistic ties within the administration. By denying access to this information under the guise of deliberative security, questionable practices such as inflated vehicle rental prices were concealed, demonstrating the state’s resistance to genuinely applying the principles of transparency and anti-corruption. This article is crucial for the field of open data because it underscores that merely publishing data does not guarantee its accessibility or accuracy if there is political will to resist it. It reaffirms that effective transparency requires robust institutional safeguards for autonomous bodies, protecting them from legal persecution and ensuring that the disclosure of public information is not penalized but rather protected as a fundamental right.
