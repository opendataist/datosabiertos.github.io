The article highlights how data on media coverage reveals a stark polarization, with specific outlets generating predominantly negative headlines about the government while state-controlled RTVC maintains a flawless positive record. This disparity suggests that the measurement tool functions less as an objective metric and more as a political instrument to legitimize narratives of media warfare against the administration. Understanding this dataset is crucial for open data advocates because it demonstrates the risks of using opaque, proprietary analytics to define truth. The reliance on unverified Comscore metrics allows political leaders to cherry-pick data that supports their ideological stance, potentially misleading the public and undermining trust in transparent, independent information sources. Furthermore, the study exposes how data visualization can be manipulated to reinforce existing power structures. By ignoring context and focusing solely on headline sentiment, the analysis ignores editorial methodology and source diversity. This reinforces the need for open, auditable data standards that prevent the misuse of statistical narratives to justify censorship or attack independent journalism.
