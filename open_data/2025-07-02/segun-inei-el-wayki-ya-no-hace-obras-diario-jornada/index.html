Según INEI: El Wayki ya no hace obras | Diario Jornada

Según INEI: El Wayki ya no hace obras | Diario Jornada

The construction sector in Ayacucho experienced a significant decline in early 2025, marking it as one of the regions with the least physical progress in public works. While national construction activity grew, Ayacucho’s downturn highlights a stark regional disparity, positioning the department near the bottom of national performance metrics. This negative trend contrasts sharply with other regions that saw substantial growth, underscoring a localized crisis in infrastructure development rather than a nationwide issue. The stagnation is attributed to specific administrative failures by both the Regional and National governments, including halted hospital and road projects and neglected bridge maintenance. Audits have revealed serious management irregularities, such as poor inventory controls and delayed material tracking, which jeopardize the execution of vital infrastructure. These inefficiencies not only delay completion but also threaten to increase transportation costs and isolate communities by impairing interdepartmental connectivity. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how public statistical indicators, like the Departmental Productive Activity Indicator, can hold authorities accountable when their findings are made accessible and analyzed. The intersection of INEI data, audit reports, and governmental statements illustrates the necessity of transparent, disaggregated data in identifying governance failures. It emphasizes that for open data to drive development, it must be actively used by citizens and media to question prioritization strategies and demand remediation for irregular public spending.
