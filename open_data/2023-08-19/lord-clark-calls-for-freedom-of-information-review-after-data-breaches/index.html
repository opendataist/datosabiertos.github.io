Lord Clark calls for freedom of information review after data breaches

Lord Clark calls for freedom of information review after data breaches

The UK’s Freedom of Information Act is facing a crisis of credibility, driven by significant data breaches and a systemic culture of resistance within government departments. Prominent architects of the legislation argue that the system is currently weakened by civil servants who view transparency as an inconvenience rather than a governance tool. This internal resistance has led to a "culture of concealment," where legitimate requests are obstructed or mishandled, undermining public trust and highlighting a dangerous disconnect between the law’s intent and its practical application. The lack of meaningful enforcement has emboldened non-compliance, as public bodies realize there are rarely consequences for withholding information. Recent high-profile data leaks by police forces demonstrate the severe risks of treating freedom of information requests as a low priority or an administrative afterthought. These incidents reveal that insufficient resources and poor handling of sensitive data pose significant threats to individual privacy and operational security, suggesting that the current framework fails to adequately protect the public interest while seeking transparency. This situation is critically relevant to open data advocates, as it illustrates how weak governance and inadequate enforcement erode the foundations of information sharing. The call for a comprehensive parliamentary review and stronger penal powers for the information commissioner underscores the need for structural reforms. Without these changes, the balance between openness and confidentiality will remain skewed, jeopardizing the integrity of open data initiatives and preventing the government from fulfilling its duty to provide citizens with accessible, reliable information.
