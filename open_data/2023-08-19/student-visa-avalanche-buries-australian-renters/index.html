Student visa avalanche buries Australian renters

Australia experienced record-high net overseas migration, largely driven by an unprecedented surge in international student visas. This influx has created significant pressure on the housing market, with students absorbing new supply in major cities and exacerbating affordability crises. The article argues that government policies, including uncapped work hours and extended post-study rights, have inadvertently turned student visas into pathways for long-term residency and labor migration. Consequently, the resulting population growth has outpaced infrastructure development, leading to severe housing shortages. This data is relevant to open_data as it highlights the importance of accessible, high-quality demographic statistics for public discourse. Transparent migration metrics allow stakeholders to analyze the causal links between immigration policies and socioeconomic outcomes, such as housing availability, fostering evidence-based debates on future regulatory adjustments.
