En julio, una familia necesitó $248.962 para no ser pobre – Diario Necochea

The article highlights a critical divergence between the rising cost of essential goods and official inflation rates, revealing that both the basic food basket and the total cost of living increased more than the general inflation index in July. This trend indicates that household purchasing power is eroding faster than general economic indicators suggest, forcing families to spend significantly more to maintain their previous standard of living and avoid poverty. This disparity underscores the urgency of accurate, granular data in monitoring social welfare. When official inflation metrics fail to capture the actual price dynamics of necessities, policy responses may be misaligned with the lived realities of vulnerable populations. The data demonstrates that without precise tracking of basic consumption costs, the severity of economic distress is underestimated, leading to ineffective social safety nets. This case is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it validates the need for transparent, accessible, and detailed statistical methods. Open data allows for independent verification of government figures and facilitates the creation of better indices that reflect true household needs. By making such detailed cost structures public, societies can demand greater accountability and design more equitable policies based on real economic pressures rather than aggregated averages.
