Ingreso y consumo: binomio asimétrico | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The article highlights profound disparities in income and expenditure in Mexico, where a small fraction of the population consumes the majority of economic resources. This inequality is rooted in Keynesian theory, which explains that lower-income households spend a much larger proportion of their earnings on basic necessities, whereas wealthier families allocate less to essentials and more to savings or luxury goods. Consequently, the poor face a precarious existence, while the rich maintain financial flexibility. The data reveals that the highest income decile captures over thirty percent of the nation’s economic flow, while the lowest decile receives only a tiny fraction. Despite the paradoxical absolute spending difference, the relative burden is significantly heavier on the poor. This concentration of wealth demonstrates that market mechanisms alone cannot rectify distribution imbalances, as economic growth does not automatically lead to an equitable sharing of resources among all social strata. This analysis is crucial for open data because it demonstrates how accessible statistical information can expose structural social inequities. By making national household expenditure data public, platforms empower citizens and policymakers to verify claims about wealth distribution and understand the real impact of economic policies. Transparency in such data is essential for driving informed public debate and demanding government intervention to correct systemic injustices and reduce the gap between the wealthiest and most vulnerable sectors of society.
