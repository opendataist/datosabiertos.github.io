Por qué para combatir la desinformación educar el pensamiento crítico es más efectivo que la censura

Disinformation poses a critical threat in the digital age, distinguished by its malicious intent to deceive and cause harm, unlike simple accidental misinformation. Recent studies indicate that combating this phenomenon through censorship or content bans is less effective than promoting early and continuous education. This strategy aims to develop critical thinking skills among citizens, teaching them to evaluate sources and remain open to reconsidering their opinions in light of new evidence. Modeled research demonstrates that public education is the most significant tool for halting the spread of fake news. Rather than attempting to correct already entrenched beliefs, the educational approach acts preventively, helping individuals recognize their own cognitive biases. This is crucial in an environment where technology enables mass manipulation, affecting democratic processes and public health, and where the human tendency to believe ideas that confirm existing prejudices facilitates the viral spread of falsehoods. This analysis is relevant to open data because it underscores the need to promote transparency and digital literacy as evidence-based public policies. Open data and verification tools can empower citizens to distinguish between verified information and manipulation. By prioritizing education over restriction, a more critical and resilient civil society is fostered, capable of using available information ethically and effectively, thereby strengthening trust in institutions and the digital information ecosystem.
