SC seeks govt response on plea against YouTube channel '4PM' ban

SC seeks govt response on plea against YouTube channel '4PM' ban

The Supreme Court has agreed to examine a petition challenging the opaque blocking of the '4PM' YouTube channel, highlighting critical tensions between state security protocols and digital free speech. The petitioners argue that the suspension, enacted without prior notice or transparent reasoning, constitutes a severe violation of journalistic independence and the public’s right to access information. By allowing the government to block content under vague national security grounds without scrutiny, the executive threatens to undermine democratic accountability and constitutional safeguards. This case exposes the inadequacies of current digital governance frameworks, specifically targeting rules that mandate strict confidentiality in blocking requests. The legal challenge asserts that these provisions enable arbitrary censorship by bypassing fundamental due process rights, such as the opportunity to be heard before content removal. It contends that "national security" cannot serve as an unfettered justification for suppressing dissent or silencing media platforms, emphasizing that statutory powers must remain subject to judicial review to prevent abuse. For open data advocates, this development underscores the urgent need for transparency in algorithmic moderation and state-imposed digital restrictions. The reliance on secret directives to enforce internet censorship sets a dangerous precedent for data governance, where accountability mechanisms are weakened by secrecy. Ensuring that digital enforcement actions are open to public scrutiny and legal challenge is essential for maintaining a free and responsible information ecosystem, reinforcing the principle that access to information should never be arbitrarily withheld behind closed doors.
