AI Systems Rely on English Few Globally Speak

AI Systems Rely on English Few Globally Speak

Current generative AI systems are heavily skewed toward mainstream American English, creating a monolithic digital representation that erases linguistic diversity. This bias stems from historical technological dominance by US-based corporations and data scraping practices that prioritize Western internet content. Consequently, the technology systematically ignores or “corrects” non-standard varieties, reinforcing unequal power dynamics and marginalizing minoritized and regional voices. The implications of this linguistic homogeneity are profound for global users who face exclusion from essential services like education, employment, and housing. When AI tools fail to understand or value diverse Englishes, such as Indian or Australian Indigenous varieties, it results in tangible discrimination and the loss of cultural knowledge. The prevailing notion that only one version of English is “correct” is a myth; however, AI development currently treats linguistic variation as noise, leading to systems that are multilingual in theory but monolingual in practice. True progress requires shifting toward linguistic justice by designing inclusive AI that respects and accommodates varied linguistic structures. This involves community-led documentation and interdisciplinary collaboration to ensure technology adapts to human language rather than forcing speakers to conform to algorithmic standards. For the open data community, this highlights the critical need to diversify training datasets and annotation practices. Without intentional inclusion of underrepresented language varieties, open data initiatives risk perpetuating systemic biases and failing to serve global populations equitably.
