Several CT bills would limit public access to records through FOIA

Several CT bills would limit public access to records through FOIA

Connecticut lawmakers are advancing numerous bills that would significantly restrict public access to government records under the state’s Freedom of Information Act. These proposals aim to exempt various categories of information from disclosure, including higher education records, lottery winners’ identities, police misconduct complaints, and the home addresses of public employees. While proponents argue these measures protect individuals from harassment and safety risks, transparency advocates warn this represents a systematic erosion of the law’s core principles, potentially shielding misconduct and reducing government accountability. The legislation also seeks to alter how public meetings are conducted, with some measures allowing school superintendents to participate in executive sessions, thereby moving more government business behind closed doors. Critics describe this as a "death by a thousand paper cuts," where incremental exemptions cumulatively undermine the utility of open records laws. Although some bills focus on improving access efficiency, the majority prioritize narrowing the scope of transparency, raising concerns about the long-term impact on democratic oversight and public trust in state institutions. This situation is highly relevant to open_data as it highlights the tension between privacy concerns and the public’s right to know. The potential reduction in available data creates significant barriers for researchers, journalists, and citizens seeking to analyze government performance and integrity. Ensuring that data remains accessible is crucial for maintaining accountability, detecting corruption, and fostering an informed citizenry, making the outcome of these legislative efforts a critical test for the future of open government.
