FOI reveals statistics from 2022/23 examinations

Freedom of Information requests have shed light on undergraduate academic outcomes, revealing that a significant number of students progressed to their final year despite failing previous modules. This practice highlights the university's progression policies, where students carrying failures may only be eligible for an Ordinary degree rather than a full honors qualification. Understanding these outcomes is crucial for open data initiatives, as it exposes the mechanisms of student retention and academic support that are often opaque to external observers. The data indicates a general upward trend in student achievement across year levels, with senior students consistently outperforming juniors. While average scores fluctuated slightly compared to previous years, the overall distribution of degree classifications remained stable, with a majority of graduates receiving upper-second-class honors. This stability suggests that despite minor variations, the institution maintains consistent standards, providing valuable benchmark data for comparative institutional analysis. Departmental performance varied, with Physics and Biology achieving the highest marks, while languages faced lower averages, though most departments stayed within the upper-secondary range. These findings emphasize the importance of transparent data release in education, allowing stakeholders to assess equity and excellence across disciplines. By making such detailed statistics accessible, institutions enable a deeper understanding of educational efficacy and student support structures, fostering accountability and informed policy discussions within the higher education sector.
