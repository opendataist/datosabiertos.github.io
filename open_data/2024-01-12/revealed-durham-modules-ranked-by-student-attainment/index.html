Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

This article demonstrates the practical value of open data by utilizing Freedom of Information requests to expose institutional performance metrics. The main conclusion is that significant disparities exist in academic attainment across different disciplines, revealing how publicly accessible data can drive transparency and accountability within higher education. The analysis highlights profound variations in success rates, with physics and English leading in high-grade awards, while foundation years and certain language modules lagged behind. These findings suggest that factors beyond individual student effort, such as curriculum structure or assessment methods, heavily influence outcomes. By making this data available, the university allows for a more nuanced understanding of educational quality that raw grades alone cannot provide. Relevance to open_data lies in how this transparency empowers stakeholders. Students can make informed decisions about their course selections, while educators and administrators can identify systemic inequities or teaching inconsistencies. This case illustrates that when data is open and accessible, it fosters a culture of continuous improvement and enables the academic community to critically engage with institutional performance rather than accepting opaque reporting.
