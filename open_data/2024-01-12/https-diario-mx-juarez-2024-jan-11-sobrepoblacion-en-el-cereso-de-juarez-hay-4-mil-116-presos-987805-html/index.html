The excessive inmate population in Mexico’s penitentiary system highlights severe infrastructure deficits, with Ciudad Juárez setting new overcrowding records. This situation reflects a critical gap between available capacity and the actual number of detainees, exposing systemic failures in social reintegration and judicial efficiency. Persistent violence within these facilities exacerbates human rights violations, leading to numerous injuries and deaths. The high incidence of internal conflicts and external attacks suggests that current containment strategies are ineffective, fostering environments where safety cannot be guaranteed for either staff or inmates. This report is vital for open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, real-time monitoring of correctional systems. Public access to such granular statistics enables civil society to demand accountability, identify resource allocation failures, and advocate for policy reforms that prioritize humane conditions over mere detention metrics.
