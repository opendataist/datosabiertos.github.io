A federal court has ordered the Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs to release withheld EEO-1 workforce reports from nearly five thousand contractors who objected to disclosure. The ruling rejected the agency’s claim that demographic data constitutes confidential commercial information, determining that broad job categories do provide specific insights into daily business operations or commercial success. Consequently, these reports are not exempt from public disclosure under freedom of information laws. This decision challenges long-standing assumptions about the privacy of diversity metrics collected by the government. By clarifying that employee demographic data lacks a direct link to a company’s specific commercial advantages, the court set a significant precedent for how such statistical information is classified. The ruling implies that general workforce composition data, much like basic employee identifiers, does not inherently qualify for trade secret protection when submitted to federal agencies. For the open data community, this case underscores the importance of rigorous legal advocacy in securing access to government-held datasets. It highlights how judicial intervention can override administrative reluctance to share data, promoting greater transparency in diversity and inclusion efforts. The outcome encourages researchers and journalists to pursue FOIA requests for similar datasets, reinforcing the principle that public accountability often outweighs corporate claims of confidentiality.
