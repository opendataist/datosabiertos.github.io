The article highlights the INAI’s strict enforcement of data protection laws, imposing significant financial penalties on individuals and companies for unauthorized data handling. This underscores the legal risks organizations face when failing to secure explicit consent or properly disclose privacy policies, demonstrating that regulatory bodies are actively holding the private sector accountable for privacy violations. Furthermore, the report reveals high volumes of complaints and exercises of rights related to access, correction, and deletion of personal data. The media and financial sectors are identified as the primary sources of grievances, indicating that consumer scrutiny is particularly intense in industries that manage vast amounts of sensitive information and maintain high public visibility. This data is relevant to open data initiatives because it establishes the critical boundary between transparency and privacy. It reminds stakeholders that while open data promotes accountability, it must strictly adhere to legal frameworks protecting individuals’ rights. Balancing public access with rigorous data protection ensures that openness does not compromise personal security or legal compliance.
