Número de defunciones en el país sigue alto: Inegi

Mexico recorded a significant rise in mortality during the first nine months of 2022, reaching levels comparable to those observed since 1990. Although these figures are lower than those during the peak pandemic years, the data reveal that cardiovascular diseases have surpassed COVID-19 as the leading cause of death. This shift highlights a critical transition in public health challenges, indicating that while the immediate viral crisis has subsided, underlying chronic health conditions are driving a substantial excess mortality rate, particularly among men and older adults. The analysis emphasizes persistent gender and geographic disparities in death rates. Men experienced a notably higher percentage of excess mortality compared to women, and specific states exhibited significantly higher death rates per inhabitant than others. These variations suggest that local health infrastructure and socioeconomic factors play crucial roles in determining mortality outcomes. Understanding these localized trends is essential for policymakers aiming to address regional health inequities and allocate resources more effectively to the areas most affected by premature deaths. This report is highly relevant to the open data community because it demonstrates the value of transparent, comprehensive statistical registries for tracking long-term public health trends. By making granular data on causes of death, demographics, and geographic distributions publicly available, institutions such as INEGI enable researchers and citizens to independently verify health narratives. This openness fosters data-driven policymaking, allowing for a better assessment of healthcare system performance and ensuring that societal responses to non-communicable diseases are based on accurate, accessible, and verifiable evidence rather than speculative assumptions.
