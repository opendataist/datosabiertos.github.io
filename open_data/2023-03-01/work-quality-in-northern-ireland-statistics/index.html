Work quality in Northern Ireland statistics

Northern Ireland’s latest work quality analysis reveals a nuanced landscape of employment conditions, highlighting distinct trends in job satisfaction, compensation, and workplace autonomy. The data indicates that while foundational aspects of employment such as security, meaningfulness, and satisfaction remain stable and high, economic rewards and scheduling flexibility have seen significant improvements. Specifically, the proportion of employees earning above the Real Living Wage has grown substantially, signaling a positive shift in financial well-being. This progress is complemented by a rise in flexible working arrangements, suggesting that employers are increasingly adapting to workforce needs beyond basic job retention. Demographic disparities, however, expose ongoing inequalities in career development and workplace control. Younger workers demonstrate greater opportunities for career progression compared to their older counterparts, indicating that seniority does not necessarily correlate with upward mobility in the current market. Gender differences are also pronounced, particularly regarding flexible work and perceived meaningfulness. Women report higher levels of flexible arrangements, largely driven by part-time employment patterns, and find more meaning in their roles. Conversely, men hold a consistent advantage in career advancement, pointing to persistent structural barriers that may limit women’s long-term professional growth despite short-term gains in flexibility. This article is highly relevant to open data advocates as it exemplifies the power of transparent, standardized statistical indicators to reveal complex social realities. By making diverse metrics—from subjective job satisfaction to objective wage thresholds—publicly available, agencies like NISRA enable stakeholders to identify specific areas of inequity. This transparency allows policymakers, researchers, and civil society to move beyond aggregate averages and address targeted disparities in work quality. Open data, therefore, serves as a critical tool for holding institutions accountable and driving evidence-based interventions that promote fairer and higher-quality employment for all demographic groups.
