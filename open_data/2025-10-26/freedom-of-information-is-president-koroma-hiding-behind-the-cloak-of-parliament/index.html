Freedom of Information: Is president Koroma hiding behind the cloak of parliament?

Freedom of Information: Is president Koroma hiding behind the cloak of parliament?

Civil society organizations and human rights groups urge the Sierra Leonean government to urgently enact the Freedom of Information Bill, emphasizing that public access to government data is fundamental to transparency and accountability. The legislation, which has languished in Parliament for over a year despite passing preliminary stages, would empower citizens to inspect previously inaccessible records, including sensitive contracts with multinational corporations. This transparency is viewed as a critical mechanism for dismantling the entrenched corruption and governance failures that historically fueled the nation’s brutal civil conflict. The article highlights the dangerous consequences of secrecy, arguing that when public officials operate without oversight, the potential for abuse of power increases significantly. By withholding information, the government undermines the rule of law and hinders effective service delivery to its citizens. International human rights instruments recognize the right to information as an essential component of freedom of expression, making its legal protection not just a domestic priority but a fulfillment of global human rights obligations. Without this legal framework, citizens remain unable to fully realize their other rights or hold leaders accountable for their duties. This case is highly relevant to the open data movement as it illustrates the legal and structural prerequisites for a functional open government ecosystem. Data alone is insufficient if citizens lack the legal right to access it; therefore, robust Freedom of Information legislation is the foundational layer that enables true open data initiatives. For open data advocates, this situation serves as a stark reminder that technical openness must be supported by strong political will and legal mandates to prevent data hoarding and ensure that information serves the public interest rather than remaining an exclusive tool of the state.
