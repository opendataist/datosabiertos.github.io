More than 1700 cops quit Police Scotland in two years

More than 1700 cops quit Police Scotland in two years

Police Scotland is experiencing a severe retention crisis, losing over 1,700 officers in two years due to burnout, low pay, and high workloads. This exodus, driven by early retirements and resignations, has left the force significantly understaffed and stretched thin. The resulting gap undermines community safety and highlights a systemic failure to support officers facing increasing operational pressures and assaults. This article is highly relevant to open data because the scale and causes of this crisis were only revealed through Freedom of Information requests. It demonstrates how transparent access to administrative and staffing data allows unions and the public to hold government bodies accountable. Without this data transparency, the true extent of the workforce attrition and the resulting impact on policing capacity would remain obscured from scrutiny. Political parties and police leadership are now debating the urgent need for increased funding and better working conditions to reverse this trend. While the government cites record investment, critics argue that structural issues like pension rules and inadequate staffing levels require more immediate intervention. The situation underscores the critical role of data in identifying public service failures and driving necessary policy reforms to stabilize essential services.
