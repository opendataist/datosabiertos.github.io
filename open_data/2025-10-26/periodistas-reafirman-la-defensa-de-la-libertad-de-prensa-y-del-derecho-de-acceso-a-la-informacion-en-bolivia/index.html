Periodistas reafirman la defensa de la libertad de prensa y del derecho de acceso a la información en Bolivia

Periodistas reafirman la defensa de la libertad de prensa y del derecho de acceso a la información en Bolivia

Bolivian journalists are urgently calling for the approval of a Public Information Access Law and a specific Protection Law for journalists. These legislative measures are seen as fundamental pillars for achieving genuine transparency and enabling the responsible exercise of journalism. Without these legal frameworks, the profession cannot effectively fulfill its democratic role or secure the necessary safeguards against systemic threats and criminalization. The collective statement highlights a severe crisis characterized by widespread violence, threats, and labor precarity aimed at inducing self-censorship. By documenting extensive violations of press freedom, the sector emphasizes that the current environment undermines the public’s right to information. This systematic pressure not only endangers individual reporters but also erodes the foundational principles of democratic accountability and free expression. This article is critically relevant to open data because unrestricted access to public information is a prerequisite for transparency in governance. The demand for a Public Access Law directly supports the principles of open data, ensuring that citizens and journalists can obtain government information without barriers. Furthermore, the call for auditing state advertising spending underscores the need for open, transparent fiscal data to prevent political manipulation, thereby linking press freedom directly to the availability of open governmental data.
