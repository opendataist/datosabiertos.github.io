El nuevo frente de la inteligencia pasa de los secretos a los datos

El nuevo frente de la inteligencia pasa de los secretos a los datos

The article argues that modern security no longer relies on secrecy but on the strategic use of open data to anticipate threats. This shift from analog, reactive intelligence to digital, predictive analysis represents a fundamental change in how nations and institutions maintain safety. The core conclusion is that power in the 21st century belongs to those who can process visible information faster and more accurately, transforming public data into a strategic asset for prevention rather than just documentation. A significant driver of this transformation is the private sector, which often surpasses state capabilities in analytical precision. Companies are leveraging artificial intelligence and open datasets to detect patterns in crime, environmental damage, and cyber threats, demonstrating that advanced intelligence is accessible beyond government agencies. This evolution suggests a new policy framework where collaboration between the state, academia, and the private sector becomes essential, provided it operates within clear ethical guidelines that protect privacy and prevent discriminatory biases. This development is highly relevant to open data because it validates the premise that publicly available information is not merely administrative but a critical component of global security and decision-making. By illustrating how open datasets can be integrated to create early warning systems and prevent criminal activities, the text underscores the necessity of transparent, interoperable, and well-governed data ecosystems. It highlights that for open data to fulfill its potential, it must be accessible, standardized, and ethically managed to empower proactive societal protection.
