Descenso en las tasas de vacunación infantil en EE. UU. en medio de un brote de sarampión

Descenso en las tasas de vacunación infantil en EE. UU. en medio de un brote de sarampión

The significant decline in childhood vaccination rates in the United States is directly correlated with the resurgence of measles outbreaks, dangerously approaching levels that undermine herd immunity. This phenomenon, accelerated by an increase in religious or medical exemptions, highlights a critical vulnerability in national public health that demands immediate and coordinated attention. The relevance to open data lies in the need for transparency and public access to this health information. The availability of detailed federal data by state and disease enables citizens, researchers, and the media to identify trends and at-risk regions, fostering evidence-based social awareness. Furthermore, access to these data facilitates accountability and the development of more effective public policies. By making visible the correlation between low vaccination rates and outbreaks, open data becomes an essential tool for promoting vaccine confidence and mitigating the consequences of misinformation, ensuring that community protection remains above safety thresholds.
