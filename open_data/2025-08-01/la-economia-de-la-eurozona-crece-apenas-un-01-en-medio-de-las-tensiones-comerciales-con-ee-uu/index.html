La economía de la eurozona crece apenas un 0,1% en medio de las tensiones comerciales con EE. UU.

La economía de la eurozona crece apenas un 0,1% en medio de las tensiones comerciales con EE. UU.

The eurozone economy is experiencing stagnant growth, well below expectations, driven by global uncertainty and specifically affected by U.S. trade policies. Tensions arising from tariffs on European imports have had a significant negative impact on exports and market confidence. This scenario contrasts with the internal dynamism of some countries, such as Spain, while others, like Germany and Ireland, face economic contractions that reflect the region’s vulnerability to these external shocks. The relevance of this article to open data lies in statistical transparency and access to public economic information. The publication of GDP data by the European Union’s statistical office and the detailed country-by-country analysis are vital examples of how open data enable citizens, researchers, and policymakers to monitor the true state of the economy. The timely availability of disaggregated indicators is essential for accurately assessing the impact of trade policies and understanding divergences among member states. Despite current challenges, there are signs of moderate recovery and contained inflation that support the European Central Bank’s long-term optimism. Forecasts indicate gradual growth in the coming years, supported by fiscal policies and improved competitiveness. However, the persistence of external trade obstacles remains a key risk factor that could hinder economic resilience, underscoring the need to critically analyze data to anticipate future scenarios and enhance evidence-based governance.
