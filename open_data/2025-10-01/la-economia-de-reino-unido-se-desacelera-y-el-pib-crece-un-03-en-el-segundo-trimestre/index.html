La economía de Reino Unido se desacelera y el PIB crece un 0,3% en el segundo trimestre

The UK’s economic performance in the second quarter reveals a modest recovery driven primarily by public spending and construction, contrasting with a contraction in the productive sector. While services showed positive growth, the overall picture suggests a sluggish economy heavily reliant on government expenditure rather than robust private demand. This dependency highlights structural weaknesses that may persist in the coming quarters, indicating that the recovery remains fragile and unevenly distributed across different industries. Trade dynamics further complicate the landscape, as a decline in goods exports was not fully offset by improvements in services, resulting in a persistent trade deficit. Experts warn that this underlying weakness, combined with high inflation and uncertain interest rates, limits the central bank’s ability to stimulate growth effectively. The data implies that upcoming fiscal adjustments and tax increases are likely, reflecting the broader challenge of stabilizing an economy that has struggled since the pandemic.
