Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions, including lower income, employment struggles, and housing issues, compared to the general population. Despite these material disadvantages and higher rates of mental health problems and loneliness, they report equal levels of life satisfaction. This paradox suggests that subjective well-being is not solely determined by objective economic or social standing. Furthermore, immigrants demonstrate surprisingly high confidence in Norwegian institutions, such as the political system, judiciary, and police, often exceeding the trust levels found in the native population. This strong institutional affiliation and connection to Norway persist even when personal social connections are strained. These findings highlight the complex interplay between structural disadvantage and social integration, indicating that trust can remain robust despite personal hardships. This article is relevant to open data because it exemplifies how comprehensive statistical surveys can reveal nuanced societal trends that simple metrics might miss. By making such detailed data publicly available, governments enable researchers and policymakers to move beyond surface-level analysis. Open data facilitates a deeper understanding of the lived experiences of diverse populations, supporting evidence-based decisions that address both material conditions and social cohesion.
