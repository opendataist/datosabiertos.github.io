Policía justifica monitoreo en redes sociales por la marcha de la Generación Z

Policía justifica monitoreo en redes sociales por la marcha de la Generación Z

The National Police defended its social media monitoring during the "Generation Z" protests in Asunción, clarifying that its actions were strictly based on publicly available information and citizen reports rather than covert surveillance. By analyzing open posts on platforms like TikTok and screenshots shared by users regarding potential criminal content, the institution fulfilled its legal obligation to report suspected illegal acts to the Public Ministry. This approach emphasizes that monitoring public digital spaces does not equate to the interception of private communications, a distinction crucial for maintaining the boundary between lawful information gathering and privacy violations. This case is highly relevant to open data as it illustrates the practical application of accessing publicly shared digital information for civic oversight and law enforcement purposes. It highlights the importance of distinguishing between open data sources and protected private data, a core principle in data ethics. Understanding how authorities utilize publicly available information helps define the limits of digital freedom and accountability, ensuring that transparency in public discourse does not inadvertently justify intrusive surveillance practices. Despite the police's defense, the incident sparked criticism from civil society and legislators, who raised concerns about the potential criminalization of social protests through digital monitoring. The resulting preliminary investigation by the Public Ministry aims to determine if the shared messages constituted incitement to violence or criminal apology. This tension underscores the ongoing challenge of balancing public safety with civil liberties in the digital age, showing how open data usage can become a contentious issue when perceived as overreach by authorities.
