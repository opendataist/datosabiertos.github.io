Los privilegiados “hijos de…” en la función pública con salarios de hasta G. 18 millones

Los privilegiados “hijos de…” en la función pública con salarios de hasta G. 18 millones

The article reveals that several children of prominent Paraguayan politicians secured high-paying government positions without merit-based competitions, often holding only high school diplomas. These appointments, facilitated by nepotistic networks within the legislative and judicial branches, highlight significant irregularities in public hiring practices. The lack of transparency in these selections undermines institutional integrity and suggests that political influence, rather than professional qualifications, drives administrative staffing. This disclosure was made possible specifically by the legal framework for Access to Public Information, which mandates institutional transparency. By relying on open data published by state bodies, journalists were able to trace salaries, job titles, and hiring dates, effectively exposing the extent of clientelism. The case demonstrates how open data initiatives serve as essential tools for civic oversight, allowing citizens to verify whether public resources are managed in accordance with established legal standards. The relevance to open data lies in its power to democratize accountability. When information is publicly accessible, it empowers the media and civil society to challenge corruption and demand reforms in public administration. This narrative underscores that transparency is not merely a bureaucratic formality but a critical mechanism for safeguarding democratic values and ensuring that public institutions serve the population rather than political elites.
