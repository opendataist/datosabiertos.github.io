The New York Times has sued OpenAI and Microsoft for copyright infringement, arguing that their AI chatbots unlawfully reproduce journalistic content to compete directly with the publisher. This unauthorized use deprives the media outlet of revenue from subscriptions and advertising, while also threatening its credibility through the dissemination of inaccurate information linked to their reporting. This legal action highlights the critical tension between generative artificial intelligence and traditional media rights. It underscores the necessity for transparent data sourcing and fair compensation mechanisms within the AI development lifecycle, ensuring that intellectual property owners are not exploited without consent or remuneration. The case is highly relevant to open data as it challenges the assumption that publicly available information can be freely used to train commercial models without restriction. It forces a reevaluation of how datasets are collected, emphasizing the importance of ethical data governance, legal compliance, and respecting creator rights in the evolving landscape of open and accessible digital information.
