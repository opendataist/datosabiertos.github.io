The New York Times demanda a Microsoft y OpenAI por los derechos de autor

The New York Times is suing Microsoft and OpenAI, alleging that they illegally used millions of its copyrighted articles to train their artificial intelligence models. This legal action aims to protect the integrity of independent journalism against technology products that directly compete with the original source of reliable information. The lawsuit highlights that the mass copying of content, particularly from The Times’ work, threatens media outlets’ ability to sustain their investment in quality journalism. By creating digital substitutes without permission or payment, these companies are improperly leveraging editorial effort, which could hollow out the market for verified news. This situation is crucial for the open data community, as it illustrates the tension between the open availability of data or texts for algorithmic training and the need to protect intellectual property. The case sets a precedent for how language models should handle copyrighted content, questioning whether unlimited access for AI implies an erosion of creators’ rights.
