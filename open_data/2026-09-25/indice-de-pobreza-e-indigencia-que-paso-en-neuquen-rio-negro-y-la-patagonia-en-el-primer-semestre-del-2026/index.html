Índice de pobreza e indigencia: qué pasó en Neuquén, Río Negro y la Patagonia en el primer semestre del 2026

The official statistical report highlights a general deterioration of social vulnerability in the Patagonian region, characterized by rising poverty and indigence levels compared to the previous period. While the regional average indicates a worsening trend, the data reveals stark contrasts between provinces and urban centers. Notably, Neuquén defies the regional pattern by showing a significant decline in poverty rates, whereas Río Negro and Chubut experience severe spikes, identifying specific geographic and economic disparities within the territory. These divergent outcomes underscore the heterogeneity of the region’s socio-economic realities. In areas like Comodoro Rivadavia and Rawson, poverty rates have reached critical highs, affecting tens of thousands of residents who lack the resources to cover basic needs. Conversely, the improvement in Neuquén suggests that local economic dynamics or policy impacts can successfully mitigate vulnerability, even when broader regional indicators suggest instability. This variation is crucial for understanding how different productive and administrative poles respond to economic pressures. This article is particularly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the power of granular, publicly accessible government statistics to drive nuanced policy analysis. By making detailed, localized data available, institutions like INDEC enable researchers and civil society to move beyond aggregate national figures and identify precise areas for intervention. This transparency fosters accountability and allows for evidence-based decision-making that addresses the specific needs of vulnerable communities rather than applying uniform solutions to complex regional challenges.
