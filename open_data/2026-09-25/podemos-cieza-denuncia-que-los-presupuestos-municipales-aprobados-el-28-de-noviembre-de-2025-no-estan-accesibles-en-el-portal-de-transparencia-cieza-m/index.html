Podemos Cieza denuncia que los presupuestos municipales aprobados el 28 de noviembre de 2025 no están accesibles en el portal de transparencia - Cieza - murcia.com

The article highlights a critical failure by the Cieza municipal government to comply with Spain’s Transparency Law, specifically regarding the non-publication of updated budget information on the official transparency portal. This omission violates the legal obligation to provide citizens with clear, accessible data on public fund management. By keeping the 2025 budget details hidden despite their existence, the administration demonstrates a lack of transparency, denying the public their right to scrutinize how their money is spent and undermining democratic accountability. The core issue stems from prolonged reliance on prorogued budgets, a situation that has persisted for years under the current administration. This chronic instability prevents the execution of new projects, restricts access to available liquidity, and forces continuous credit modifications to cover basic expenses. The political criticism emphasizes that this pattern is not an isolated incident but a systemic negligence, where the government has operated with outdated financial frameworks for nearly four years, effectively paralyzing normal municipal operations and strategic planning. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the gap between mere legal compliance and genuine data accessibility. Simply having budgets approved is insufficient; if the data is not actively published and kept current in an open, machine-readable format, it fails to serve the public interest. It serves as a reminder that effective open government requires proactive, accurate, and timely data sharing to ensure that citizens can truly monitor local governance and hold officials accountable for their financial decisions.
