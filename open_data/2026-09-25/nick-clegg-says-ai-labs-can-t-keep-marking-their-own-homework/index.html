Nick Clegg says AI labs can't keep marking their own homework

Nick Clegg argues that the era of self-regulation by AI labs has ended, as public backlash over issues like cancelled data centers proves voluntary measures are insufficient. He contends that without mandatory baseline standards for transparency and accountability, society faces a "pitchfork rebellion" against the technology. This shift implies that open data and oversight must move from optional corporate responsibility to enforceable regulatory frameworks to maintain public trust and prevent further resistance. The current regulatory landscape is fragmented and ill-suited for the speed of generative AI, creating a chaotic environment that risks putting European companies at a disadvantage. Clegg critiques the EU’s approach as a retrofitted "dog’s dinner" that struggles with overlapping data protection laws and inconsistent enforcement across member states. This highlights the urgent need for coherent, unified open data policies that can effectively manage AI risks without stifling innovation through disjointed national regulations. Looking forward, Clegg advises focusing on immediate, known risks rather than apocalyptic scenarios, suggesting practical steps like incident hotlines to improve accountability. He also notes that Europe should leverage its talent by focusing on application layers and vertical deployments rather than trying to replicate US infrastructure dominance. For open data advocates, this underscores the importance of targeted, practical governance and capital investment in specific sectors to foster a competitive and responsible AI ecosystem.
