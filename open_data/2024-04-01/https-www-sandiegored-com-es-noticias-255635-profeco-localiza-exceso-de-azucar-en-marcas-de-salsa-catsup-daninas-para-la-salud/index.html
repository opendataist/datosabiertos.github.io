The investigation reveals that many ketchup brands in Mexico mislead consumers by underreporting sugar content and using high-fructose corn syrup, which poses significant health risks. This discrepancy between labeled information and actual composition constitutes a false representation of nutritional value, directly impacting consumer trust and safety. These findings highlight a systemic failure in product labeling compliance, where major brands fail to disclose excess sugar or the presence of harmful ingredients. The inconsistency suggests that current regulatory oversight is insufficient to guarantee transparency, leaving buyers vulnerable to health issues like cardiovascular diseases due to unchecked consumption of deceptive products. This case is critical for open data advocates because it demonstrates how accessible, verified information can expose corporate malpractice. By publishing detailed, factual data, authorities empower citizens to make informed choices, underscoring the vital role of transparent, open datasets in holding corporations accountable and protecting public health.
