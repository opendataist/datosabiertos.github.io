SERAP gives Sani, Wike, others ultimatum to publish loan agreements, account for N5.9t, $4.6b loans |

A civil society organization is demanding full transparency regarding the massive domestic and external debts incurred by Nigerian state governments and the Federal Capital Territory. The group insists that loan agreements and detailed spending records be publicly disclosed, arguing that citizens have a fundamental right to know how public funds are managed. This push for openness aims to dismantle the opacity that currently shields government finances from public scrutiny and enables potential financial misconduct. The initiative is driven by serious concerns that borrowed funds are being misused for lavish political lifestyles rather than essential public services, leaving many states unable to pay salaries or provide quality healthcare and education. By making these financial details accessible, the organization seeks to empower citizens to hold their leaders accountable. This visibility is presented as a critical mechanism to detect corruption, ensure loans are utilized for developmental purposes, and restore public trust in democratic institutions. This demand is highly relevant to open data principles as it highlights the necessity of proactive data publication for democratic accountability. It underscores that access to information is not merely a bureaucratic formality but a legal and moral obligation under various international and constitutional frameworks. When governments treat financial data as a public good rather than a state secret, it enables meaningful civic engagement and serves as a robust check against the abuse of public trust, reinforcing the rule of law through transparency.
