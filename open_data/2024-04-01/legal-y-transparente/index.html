¿Legal y transparente?

The article exposes the genuine lack of transparency in the Government of Navarre, where the publication of audit reports is inconsistent and is often used to legitimize administrative irregularities. Rather than correcting flaws identified by auditors, the executive branch prioritizes streamlining procedures over legal compliance, systematically ignoring legal objections and issuing suspensive reservations that validate questionable practices. Specific cases of non-compliance with the Contracts Law are identified, such as the artificial splitting of works to avoid public tendering or illegal contract modifications that alter essential conditions. These decisions, often driven by politically appointed officials, persist despite warnings from legal counsel, creating a recurring pattern of illegality that goes unpunished by government resolutions, suggesting a system designed to bypass internal controls. This situation is relevant to open data because it reveals that publishing data does not guarantee its accessibility or its critical utility. Opacity does not stem solely from the absence of information, but from the manipulation of accountability processes. The example demonstrates how formal transparency can mask a lack of integrity, underscoring the need for mechanisms that ensure audited information is independently reviewed and that anomalies are sanctioned rather than ignored.
