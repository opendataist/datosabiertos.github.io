Denmark’s 'non-Western' immigrants triple parents’ earnings

Denmark’s 'non-Western' immigrants triple parents’ earnings

Statistics Denmark reveals that third-generation immigrants from non-Western countries experience significant income growth compared to their fathers, often exceeding the generational gains seen in native Danish families. This suggests that while starting economic conditions were historically lower, the second generation is rising rapidly, narrowing the gap through substantial upward mobility relative to their parents' baseline. However, native-born Danes still maintain higher absolute median incomes than their immigrant peers. The study highlights that previous economic disadvantages are being actively overcome, yet the cumulative effect of historical employment gaps means the final income levels have not yet fully converged with those of the native population. This distinction between relative improvement and absolute equality is crucial for understanding long-term integration trends. This data is vital for open_data initiatives as it demonstrates how granular, longitudinal statistics can reveal nuanced societal shifts that broad averages might obscure. By making such detailed breakdowns accessible, researchers and policymakers can better evaluate the effectiveness of integration policies and address structural inequalities, fostering a more inclusive and transparent public discourse on economic equity.
