Elige Inai a su nuevo presidente

The recent appointment of Adrián Alcalá Méndez as the new president of Mexico’s National Institute of Transparency reveals deep internal fractures within the institution. His election, marked by allegations of opacity and ethical concerns, underscores a critical challenge for democratic oversight: the struggle to maintain institutional integrity while addressing accusations of harassment and lack of accountability. This leadership transition occurs against a backdrop of prolonged dysfunction, during which the institute was previously unable to process citizen requests due to a shortage of commissioners. The political tension highlights how administrative paralysis directly hinders the public’s ability to demand accountability from government bodies, creating a vacuum that undermines the very mechanisms designed to protect transparency and data privacy. This development is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the fragility of the institutions that enforce data rights. When the body responsible for guaranteeing access to information operates under internal conflict or external political pressure, the practical utility of open data diminishes. Ensuring that these oversight bodies are both ethically sound and functionally independent is essential for transforming data openness from a legal principle into a tangible tool for civic engagement.
