Arkansas AG rejects proposed constitutional amendment to enshrine state FOIA laws

Arkansas Attorney General Tim Griffin rejected a ballot initiative aiming to constitutionally enshrine the state’s Freedom of Information Act. The proposal was dismissed primarily due to undefined key terms and ambiguous language, which risked misleading voters and creating administrative confusion. Griffin argued that the amendment’s use of persuasive terminology obscured its actual legal impact, potentially favoring partisan interests over genuine public understanding. Consequently, the measure was returned for redesign rather than proceeding to a public vote. This case highlights the critical importance of precise legal definitions in open data and transparency legislation. It demonstrates how vague framing can undermine public accountability efforts, emphasizing that clear, unambiguous language is essential for successful civic engagement and effective government information access.
