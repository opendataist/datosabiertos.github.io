Revealed: How the UK tech secretary uses ChatGPT for policy advice

Revealed: How the UK tech secretary uses ChatGPT for policy advice

The disclosure of UK Technology Secretary Peter Kyle’s use of ChatGPT establishes a significant precedent regarding public accountability in the digital age. By confirming that ministerial interactions with commercial AI tools are subject to Freedom of Information requests, the case highlights the necessity of transparency when politicians utilize third-party technologies for official duties. This development forces government bodies to reconsider how they manage digital records and engage with emerging technologies, ensuring that public interest in understanding decision-making processes is not obscured by private corporate platforms. Furthermore, the specific content of these interactions reveals the practical challenges and perceptions surrounding AI adoption within the business community. The Secretary sought to understand barriers such as regulatory concerns and limited awareness among small businesses, indicating that even high-level officials rely on AI to navigate complex policy landscapes. This underscores the growing integration of generative AI into governance not just as a labor-saving tool, but as an instrument for strategic insight and education, reflecting a broader shift in how government entities approach innovation and efficiency. This case is critically relevant to open data because it blurs the traditional boundaries of what constitutes public record. Unlike standard search queries, AI interactions generate new information based on user input, raising complex legal questions about data ownership and accessibility. As governments increasingly adopt similar tools, this incident serves as a warning that reliance on proprietary systems may expose officials to greater scrutiny, potentially driving a future preference for open-source or state-provided AI solutions to maintain both security and compliance with transparency laws.
