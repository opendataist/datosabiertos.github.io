Diputados aprueban en comisión reformas para que Seguridad accese a datos personales; busca reforzar investigación de delitos | El Universal

Diputados aprueban en comisión reformas para que Seguridad accese a datos personales; busca reforzar investigación de delitos | El Universal

The approval of two significant laws by the Chamber of Deputies marks a pivotal shift in Mexico’s public security framework, granting the Secretariat of Security and Citizen Protection expansive powers to collect, process, and utilize real-time data. These initiatives aim to create a unified national system for intelligence and investigation, enabling deep integration between various government entities and the private sector to effectively combat organized crime and enhance investigative efficacy. The proposed legislation mandates the establishment of a national registry for mobile telephone users and authorizes the collection of diverse sensitive data, including biometric, financial, and vehicular records. By facilitating the flow of information across federal, state, and private institutions, the system seeks to provide authorities with comprehensive evidence trails, thereby strengthening their capacity to neutralize criminal networks through enhanced connectivity and data sharing. This development is critically relevant to open data discourse as it raises profound concerns regarding privacy, human rights, and data governance. The breadth of data access granted to the state challenges existing norms on transparency and accountability, highlighting the tension between security objectives and individual liberties. The debate underscores the necessity for robust legal safeguards and ethical frameworks to prevent misuse, ensuring that expanded surveillance capabilities do not compromise civil rights or lead to unauthorized data exploitation.
