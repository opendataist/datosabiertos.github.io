Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Norway’s latest living conditions report reveals a complex reality for immigrants, who face significant socioeconomic disadvantages yet maintain high levels of life satisfaction. Despite lower incomes and poorer housing, immigrants report confidence in national institutions and a strong affiliation with Norway. This paradox highlights a resilient social fabric where material disparities do not necessarily diminish subjective well-being or civic engagement. However, these individuals experience higher rates of mental health challenges and loneliness compared to the general population. While employment and health standards lag, the data underscores the need for targeted social and psychological support systems. The findings suggest that addressing mental health and social isolation is crucial for improving the holistic living experience of immigrant communities, independent of their reported satisfaction with daily life. This research is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the power of transparent, comprehensive surveys in uncovering nuanced social dynamics. By making such detailed insights publicly available, policymakers can move beyond aggregate statistics to understand specific vulnerabilities. Open access to this data facilitates evidence-based interventions, ensuring that resources are directed effectively to support integration and well-being for marginalized groups.
