Tumores, infartos o suicidios: ¿de qué se mueren los gallegos?

The article highlights how mortality statistics in Galicia reflect a deeply aging population, with dementia emerging as the leading cause of death. This shift underscores the demographic challenge posed by increased life expectancy and the growing burden of chronic conditions. The prevalence of these diseases reflects broader societal changes, emphasizing the need for healthcare systems to adapt to long-term care requirements rather than focusing solely on acute interventions. Significant gender disparities exist in cause-specific mortality: men are more affected by tumors, while women are more affected by dementia. Additionally, accidents have recently surpassed suicides as the primary external cause of death. These patterns reveal distinct behavioral and biological risk factors across sexes. Understanding these differences is crucial for developing targeted public health strategies that address the specific vulnerabilities of different demographic groups, particularly in preventing accidental injuries and supporting mental health. This data is vital for open data initiatives because it transforms raw statistics into actionable insights for policy-making and resource allocation. By making mortality trends transparent, open data enables researchers and policymakers to track the effectiveness of health interventions and identify emerging crises, such as the rise in respiratory diseases or the decline in infectious disease mortality. Such transparency fosters evidence-based decisions that improve public health outcomes and ensure equitable support for an aging society.
