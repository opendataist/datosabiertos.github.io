There's only 1 explanation for failure to expand open records law: Cowardice | Opinion

The article highlights a significant transparency deficit in Michigan’s Freedom of Information Act, which uniquely exempts the governor and the state Legislature from public records requirements. While the law grants citizens access to documents from other government entities, this legislative and executive exclusion prevents full accountability, allowing officials to operate without public scrutiny regarding their official conduct and expenditures. Passing pending expansion legislation would subject these branches to FOIA by 2027, providing a buffer period for agencies to upgrade their record-keeping systems. However, the delayed implementation and lingering exemptions, such as shielding communications with constituents, have drawn criticism for potentially allowing current officials to avoid immediate oversight. Despite these flaws, proponents argue that establishing a legal framework for transparency is a crucial foundational step toward democratic integrity, even if the details require future refinement. This issue is central to open data discussions because it underscores that true accessibility extends beyond merely publishing data; it requires equitable legal obligations for all branches of government. Without closing these loopholes, the potential of open data to prevent corruption and inform the public remains unrealized for the highest levels of state power. Ensuring that the architects of policy are held to the same transparency standards as the agencies they oversee is essential for building genuine trust in public institutions.
