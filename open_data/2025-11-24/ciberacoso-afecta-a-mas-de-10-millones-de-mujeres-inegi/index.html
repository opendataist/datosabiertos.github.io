Ciberacoso afecta a más de 10 millones de mujeres: Inegi

Ciberacoso afecta a más de 10 millones de mujeres: Inegi

The report highlights that cyberbullying in Mexico severely violates fundamental rights such as privacy, emotional integrity, and safety. This widespread digital violence affects millions, disproportionately impacting women through sexual harassment and identity theft. The prevalence of these abuses underscores the urgent need for robust legal and social frameworks to protect citizens from digital threats and uphold their human rights in the online sphere. Aggressors are frequently strangers, indicating that anonymous online environments often facilitate unchecked abuse. Victims experience significant psychological harm, including fear and distrust, yet many only resort to blocking accounts rather than formal reporting. This gap between experiencing harm and seeking institutional justice reveals critical shortcomings in current support systems and enforcement mechanisms, leaving many survivors without adequate recourse or protection against further victimization. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it provides essential metrics on digital violence trends, demographic disparities, and reporting gaps. Transparent access to such statistics enables researchers, policymakers, and civil society to analyze root causes, design targeted interventions, and advocate for better technological safeguards. Ultimately, sharing this information fosters accountability and supports evidence-based strategies to mitigate cyberbullying and empower vulnerable populations.
