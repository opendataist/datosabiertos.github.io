Posting Dead. By 2070 Dead Facebook Users Could Overtake the Living

Posting Dead. By 2070 Dead Facebook Users Could Overtake the Living

Research from the Oxford Internet Institute predicts that by 2070, deceased Facebook accounts will outnumber active ones, signaling a massive shift in how society manages digital remains. This projection highlights the urgent need to address who owns personal data after death and how it should be handled for the benefit of grieving families. It forces a reevaluation of social media platforms as long-term repositories of human history rather than transient communication tools. The study emphasizes that these aggregated profiles constitute a significant portion of our global digital heritage. Consequently, it is crucial for tech companies to collaborate with historians, archivists, and ethicists to curate this data responsibly. This approach ensures that future generations can access and understand the past through these digital traces, while simultaneously protecting the privacy and dignity of the deceased and their loved ones. For open data, this situation underscores the complexity of preserving historical records when proprietary platforms hold the keys. It raises critical questions about data accessibility, legacy rights, and the sustainability of digital archives. As users increasingly leave behind vast amounts of personal information, there is a growing imperative for transparent policies that balance corporate control with public interest, ensuring that digital memories remain meaningful and ethically managed long after the platform itself may change.
