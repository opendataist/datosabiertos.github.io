Naciste en los años 70 y eres de la Generación X: este es el nombre más común en Baleares

Between 1960 and 1979, naming trends in the Balearic Islands reflected the cultural identity of Generation X. While traditional religious names like Antonio and Catalina remained dominant, their relative frequency declined significantly by the end of the decade. This shift signaled an incipient expansion in name diversity, marking the beginning of social changes that would become more pronounced during Spain's political transition. The persistence of these classic names demonstrates how deeply rooted cultural and religious values influenced personal identity during the dictatorship era. However, the gradual decrease in the share of these top names indicates a move away from strict uniformity. By the late 1970s, families began exploring a wider array of appellatives, suggesting that societal openness was starting to reshape individual expression and family choices. This analysis is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it illustrates how public statistics capture nuanced socio-cultural transformations. By making historical naming data accessible, researchers can study the intersection of policy, culture, and individual identity over time. It highlights the value of longitudinal datasets in understanding how broader historical events, such as democratic transitions, manifest in everyday personal decisions and demographic patterns.
