Estas fueron las causas de muerte en México en 2023

Mexico’s 2023 mortality data reveal a complex public health landscape in which the immediate impact of the pandemic has receded, yet death rates remain elevated compared to pre-pandemic levels. Although the total number of deaths decreased significantly from 2022, the persistent gap above 2019 figures indicates that the crisis left lasting structural effects on the population’s well-being, challenging assumptions about a full recovery. Cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, and malignancies have firmly re-established themselves as the leading causes of death, replacing pandemic-related mortality that dominated previous years. This shift underscores a transition toward chronic health management, highlighting the need for sustained medical resources. Meanwhile, external causes, including homicides, have seen minor fluctuations, suggesting that violence remains a persistent, albeit secondary, factor in national mortality statistics. This information is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates the value of transparent, definitive statistical records in tracking long-term societal trends. Accessible, high-quality datasets enable researchers and policymakers to move beyond immediate crisis metrics, facilitating better analysis of health systems’ resilience and the effectiveness of public policies over time.
