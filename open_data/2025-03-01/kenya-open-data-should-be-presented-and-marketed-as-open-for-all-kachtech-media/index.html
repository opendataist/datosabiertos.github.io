Kenya Open data Should be Presented and Marketed as Open for all - KachTech Media

Kenya Open data Should be Presented and Marketed as Open for all - KachTech Media

The article argues that the prevailing perception of open data as a niche tool for technologists and developers in Kenya is fundamentally flawed and counterproductive. By marketing government data primarily as a resource for creating innovative applications, the initiative alienates the general public who do identify with the tech sector. This narrow framing causes data to remain underutilized by the local population, as ordinary citizens feel disconnected from the concept of "innovation," leading to a significant gap between data availability and actual societal impact. To realize the true potential of open data, it must be presented as a universal asset involving all sectors of society, including farmers, teachers, health workers, and bloggers, rather than just software engineers. The current approach creates confusion and barriers to entry, resulting in a paradox where local traffic is high but meaningful local usage is low, while international actors reap more benefits. The focus must shift from demanding high-tech innovations to encouraging broad, accessible engagement that allows non-technical users to interpret and apply data to their daily lives and community challenges. This article is highly relevant to open_data because it highlights a critical implementation failure: data transparency does not equal data utility if the audience is misidentified. It serves as a cautionary tale that open data initiatives must prioritize inclusive design and diverse stakeholder engagement to drive domestic adoption. Effective open data strategies require bridging the gap between technical availability and everyday user needs, ensuring that information serves the entire populace and fosters genuine civic engagement rather than remaining an idle repository for external developers.
