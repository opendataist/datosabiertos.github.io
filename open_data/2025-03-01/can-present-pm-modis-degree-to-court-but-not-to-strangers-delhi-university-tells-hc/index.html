Can present PM Modi’s degree to court, but not to strangers, Delhi University tells HC

Can present PM Modi’s degree to court, but not to strangers, Delhi University tells HC

This case highlights the critical tension between public accountability and individual privacy rights within the open data framework. Delhi University’s stance that student records are private, held in a fiduciary capacity, challenges the principle that information generated by public bodies should generally be accessible. The legal debate centers on whether educational records constitute personal data that requires protection or public documents that serve the citizen’s right to know. The resolution of this dispute significantly impacts how open data policies are implemented for historical institutional records. If courts uphold the university’s position, it sets a precedent that educational metadata might be exempt from disclosure, potentially limiting transparency. Conversely, affirming the Central Information Commission’s earlier ruling reinforces the notion that public institutions cannot withhold data simply because it involves specific individuals, ensuring that open data initiatives remain robust against claims of privacy overreach. Ultimately, this litigation underscores the necessity for clear guidelines on releasing non-sensitive educational data. It matters for open data because it determines whether citizens can verify claims about public figures’ qualifications using official records. A favorable outcome for transparency promotes trust in public institutions, while a restrictive outcome could hinder efforts to make historical academic data available for public scrutiny and verification.
