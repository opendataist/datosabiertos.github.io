Padece violencia 66 por ciento de mujeres en México: Inegi - Plaza de Armas | Querétaro

The ENDIREH 2016 report reveals that the majority of Mexican women have experienced some form of violence or discrimination, highlighting a pervasive crisis that transcends private domestic spheres. This data underscores that abuse is not isolated to intimate partners but systematically affects women in public, educational, and professional environments. Violence manifests extensively across different life stages, with high prevalence in schools and workplaces. Discrimination, emotional aggression, and sexual violence are particularly common in these contexts, indicating that societal structures often fail to protect women from harassment and abuse regardless of their social or economic status. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how standardized, publicly available statistics are essential for diagnosing social inequalities. By making such comprehensive data accessible, governments and researchers can track trends, hold institutions accountable, and design evidence-based policies to combat gender-based violence effectively.
