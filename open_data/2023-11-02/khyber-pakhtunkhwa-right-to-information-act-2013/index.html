Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Right to Information Act 2013

The Right to Information transforms citizens from passive subjects into active participants by establishing that information belongs to the people, with the government serving merely as a custodian. This legal framework ensures that those utilizing public funds remain accountable for their duties and decisions. By granting individuals the power to question how public money is spent and to scrutinize major decisions, the law shifts the balance of power, ensuring that transparency is maintained and that governmental actions are open to public examination. In regions like Khyber Pakhtystan, this legislation serves as a critical anti-corruption tool and a mechanism for democratizing knowledge. It prevents the hoarding of vital information by political elites, thereby empowering ordinary citizens regardless of their educational or professional background. The ability to access data regarding essential services like health, education, and infrastructure allows communities to hold authorities responsible, fostering a more equitable and transparent political system that respects the dignity of every citizen. This article is fundamentally relevant to open data because it provides the legal and ethical foundation for data accessibility and civic engagement. Open data initiatives cannot thrive without the underlying principle that public information is a public right, not a state secret. By highlighting the mechanisms for requesting data and the penalties for withholding it, the text underscores the necessity of robust legal frameworks to ensure that open data initiatives effectively serve the public interest and combat corruption.
