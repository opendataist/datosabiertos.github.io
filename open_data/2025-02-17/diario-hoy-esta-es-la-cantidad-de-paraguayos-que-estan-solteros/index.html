The article reveals a profound transformation in Paraguayan family structures, marked by a significant shift away from traditional marriage toward free unions and single-person households. This evolution reflects broader social changes, including delayed marriage and reduced fertility rates, indicating that modern family units are becoming smaller and more diverse. Educational attainment among women now exceeds that of men, yet the data highlights a persistent gender income gap, with women requiring more qualifications to earn equivalent salaries. This disparity underscores systemic inequities in the labor market despite increased investment in female education. This data is vital for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how accessible, high-quality statistical surveys can expose critical social dynamics and inequalities. By making such detailed demographic and economic information transparent, societies can better inform policy-making and address structural issues such as gender wage gaps and evolving family needs.
