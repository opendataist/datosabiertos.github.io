La informalidad laboral alcanza a casi 6 de cada 10 jóvenes menores de 29 años | Análisis

La informalidad laboral alcanza a casi 6 de cada 10 jóvenes menores de 29 años | Análisis

The report highlights a significant rise in labor informality, revealing that nearly half of all workers lack formal registration. This precariousness disproportionately affects the youngest demographic and specific sectors like domestic service and construction, where the majority operate outside legal protections. Consequently, these individuals are denied essential social benefits, such as healthcare and pensions, underscoring a deepening structural vulnerability in the workforce. Beyond informal status, the landscape shows a troubling shift toward more fragile employment models. Registered private jobs have declined sharply, partially offset by a growth in autonomous work and monotributo schemes. This transition indicates a substitution of stable, rights-bearing positions with less secure alternatives, reflecting a systemic erosion of job quality and long-term economic security for many citizens. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates how public statistics can expose hidden socioeconomic crises. By making detailed metrics on informal employment and job precarity accessible, researchers and policymakers can drive evidence-based reforms. Open data transforms raw numbers into powerful tools for accountability, enabling society to understand and address the gaps in labor rights and social protection systems.
