Panamá con el reto de generar más y mejor empleo formal en el sector privado

Panamá con el reto de generar más y mejor empleo formal en el sector privado

Panama faces a critical economic challenge driven by a surge in informal employment and heavy public debt. The state can no longer sustain job creation through borrowed funds, necessitating a strategic shift toward fostering robust private sector investment. This transition is essential not only to reduce unemployment but also to ensure the financial sustainability of the social security system. The urgency of this structural change is highlighted by the fact that recent job growth has been predominantly informal, undermining efforts to provide dignified pensions. Official data reveals a significant loss of formal private jobs, contradicting union narratives that link pension protests solely to benefit reductions. Instead, the core issue lies in the lack of quality formal employment that could naturally support adequate social security contributions without excessive state intervention or debt. This situation is relevant to open data because transparent, accessible statistics are vital for distinguishing factual economic trends from political rhetoric. By making high-quality labor market data publicly available, society can better understand the structural causes of inequality and inform policy decisions. Open data empowers citizens to verify claims about employment quality and social security funding, facilitating a more informed public debate on necessary economic reforms.
