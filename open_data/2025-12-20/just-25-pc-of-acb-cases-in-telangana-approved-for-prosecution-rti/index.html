Just 25 pc of ACB cases in Telangana approved for prosecution: RTI

Just 25 pc of ACB cases in Telangana approved for prosecution: RTI

An investigation into the Telangana Anti-Corruption Bureau reveals a systemic inefficiency where only a small fraction of registered corruption cases secure prosecution approval. This bottleneck stems from excessive delays in obtaining governmental consent, often taking years, which allows accused officials to avoid immediate accountability. Consequently, many high-profile corruption cases remain pending for over a decade, severely undermining the deterrent effect of anti-corruption measures and suggesting that disciplinary actions within departments often replace necessary legal prosecution. The relevance to open data lies in how transparent access to bureaucratic records exposes these critical governance failures. By utilizing Right to Information petitions to uncover case statistics and timelines, civil society organizations can highlight disparities between enforcement actions and judicial outcomes. This case demonstrates that open data is not merely about publishing numbers but serves as a vital tool for holding institutions accountable. It forces public scrutiny on the gap between registering a crime and achieving justice, proving that data transparency is essential for identifying structural weaknesses in the rule of law. To address these issues, advocates urge the implementation of strict timelines for prosecution approvals and the suspension of officials during investigations. These recommendations aim to prevent the reinstatement of corrupt officers and ensure faster disposal of cases. The recent arrest of a university engineer for bribery illustrates the ongoing need for efficient enforcement mechanisms. Ultimately, streamlining the legal process and maintaining public access to case statuses are crucial for restoring trust in the system and ensuring that corruption is punished without undue delay.
