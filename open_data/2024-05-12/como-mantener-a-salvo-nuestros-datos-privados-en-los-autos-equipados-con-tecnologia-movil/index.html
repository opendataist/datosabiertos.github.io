¿Cómo mantener a salvo nuestros datos privados en los autos equipados con tecnología móvil?

The growing proliferation of connected vehicles poses serious risks to privacy and cybersecurity, as manufacturers collect and share an excessive amount of personal and sensitive driver data, including detailed behavioral patterns. As this technology becomes integrated into daily life, users’ lack of control over their information creates a power asymmetry that can lead to misuse or sale to third parties, exposing individuals to significant ethical and legal vulnerabilities. This phenomenon is crucial to the realm of open data, as the indiscriminate extraction of private information contradicts the principles of transparency and consent that underpin data culture. The automated exchange of data without adequate safeguards hinders the creation of reliable information ecosystems, in which users should have agency over how their data are used, shared, or deleted, thereby questioning the legitimacy of current vehicle data management models. To address these challenges, it is imperative to establish frameworks that prioritize privacy by default and by design, ensuring that technological innovation does not overshadow individual rights. The relevance of this debate lies in the need to balance the benefits of connectivity with the protection of privacy, promoting ethical standards that guarantee user control and comprehensive security against cyber threats, thus laying the foundation for responsible data management in future mobility.
