Escritores contra la inteligencia artificial: reclaman que respete y pague derechos de autor

The article describes an intense ethical and legal dispute between the artificial intelligence industry and content creators, in which writers demand compensation and consent for the use of their works as training data. The main conclusion is that generative technology has illegally exploited human intellectual property, threatening the economic viability of the literary profession by flooding the market with derivative content without crediting or compensating original authors. This contentious relationship underscores the urgency of updating regulatory frameworks to ensure that AI systems respect copyright and provide fair compensation. Authors argue that the quality and originality of language stem exclusively from human experience, and that the unregulated use of their texts not only constitutes an economic injustice but also devalues creativity by allowing machines to reproduce styles and narratives without ethical oversight. The relevance to the field of open data is critical, as it illustrates the inherent risks of mass data collection without clear consent. This case serves as a warning about the need to balance technological innovation with the protection of intellectual property, emphasizing that transparency and fairness in access to information are fundamental to maintaining public trust and the sustainability of open knowledge ecosystems.
