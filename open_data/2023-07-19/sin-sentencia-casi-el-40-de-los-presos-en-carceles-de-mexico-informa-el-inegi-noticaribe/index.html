Sin sentencia casi el 40% de los presos en cárceles de México, informa el Inegi - Noticaribe

The recent census reveals a critical justice issue in Mexico: nearly forty percent of the prison population lacks a final sentence, indicating that the system detains individuals long before judicial conclusions are reached. This high proportion of pretrial detention suggests systemic inefficiencies and highlights the gap between legal theory and the reality of incarceration, where many await trial indefinitely. Data shows significant gender disparities, with women far more likely to be held without sentencing than men. This finding underscores how preventative justice measures disproportionately affect vulnerable groups, particularly in states where preventative detention is applied universally to those without convictions. These trends point to deeper structural biases within the penal framework. This information is vital for open data initiatives because it exposes the limitations of amnesty laws and demonstrates the need for transparent, real-time monitoring of judicial processes. By making such granular data accessible, researchers and policymakers can advocate for reforms that reduce overcrowding and protect human rights, ensuring that the justice system prioritizes liberty until proven otherwise.
