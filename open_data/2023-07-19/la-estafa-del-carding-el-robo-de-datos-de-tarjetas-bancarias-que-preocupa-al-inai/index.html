La estafa del "carding", el robo de datos de tarjetas bancarias que preocupa al Inai

The INAI has issued a critical alert regarding the surge in credit and debit card theft through carding, shoulder surfing, and fake online stores. These fraudsters steal sensitive numerical data and verification codes to make small, recurrent purchases on streaming and gaming platforms, a tactic designed to evade detection by avoiding large, suspicious transactions. This highlights the urgent need for heightened digital literacy and vigilance regarding personal financial information in an increasingly connected economy. This situation underscores the vital importance of open data initiatives focused on consumer protection and transparency. By making security protocols, fraud patterns, and best practices openly accessible, authorities and institutions can empower citizens to identify risks effectively. Open data acts as a foundational tool for educating the public about emerging threats, ensuring that warning signs and prevention strategies are not siloed but widely disseminated to enhance collective security. To mitigate these risks, experts recommend verifying website security, avoiding public networks, and utilizing features like virtual cards and transaction alerts. Destroying expired cards and disabling NFC when unnecessary further secures personal data. Ultimately, this article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how accessible, clear, and comprehensive information empowers individuals to defend their privacy and financial integrity against sophisticated cybercriminal techniques.
