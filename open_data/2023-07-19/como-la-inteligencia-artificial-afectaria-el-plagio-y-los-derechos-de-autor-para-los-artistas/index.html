Cómo la inteligencia artificial afectaría el plagio y los derechos de autor para los artistas

Artists from various disciplines acknowledge that artificial intelligence is profoundly transforming creativity, optimizing processes while also generating uncertainty about the value of personal signature and the human essence in art. Although technological tools enable the rapid production of content, creators argue that AI cannot replace the curation, aesthetic intention, and emotional connection that humans bring, establishing a clear distinction between automated production and genuine artistic creation. The impact goes beyond mere technical competition, posing serious ethical and labor challenges related to the possible dehumanization of cultural industries and the displacement of workers. There is an urgent need for global regulations that protect copyright, manage the training of algorithms, and prevent neoliberal exploitation, ensuring that technology serves to enhance human talent rather than becoming a threat that renders professionals obsolete or ignores them entirely. This debate is fundamental to the field of open data, as the training of these artificial intelligence models heavily depends on access to and use of large cultural datasets, often without the consent or adequate compensation of original creators. Transparency in how these data are collected, licensed, and used is crucial for establishing fair legal frameworks, ensuring digital sovereignty, and promoting a technological ecosystem that respects intellectual property and cultural diversity rather than extracting resources without control.
