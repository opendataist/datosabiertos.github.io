North Lanarkshire Council spends £4m on consultant fees ahead of £1.8b deal

North Lanarkshire Council has faced intense scrutiny after a Freedom of Information request revealed that £4 million in external consultant fees were incurred while preparing for a £1.8 billion housing and infrastructure contract with Mears LLP. Critics argue this expenditure is indefensible given the region’s severe financial constraints and the necessity for council members to propose budgets involving significant service cuts. The revelation highlights a tension between ensuring rigorous procurement processes and demonstrating fiscal responsibility to constituents facing economic hardship. Further complicating the situation are allegations from a whistleblower who claims Mears manipulated data to falsely report timely completion of repairs. Since the council holds a third ownership stake in the firm, there are concerns about potential conflicts of interest and the integrity of performance metrics. The whistleblower’s claims, which suggest systematic alteration of records to hide delays, underscore the critical importance of independent oversight and transparency in public-private partnerships to prevent data manipulation that masks service failures. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the consequences of opaque decision-making and restricted information access. The council’s alleged refusal to engage with questioning councillors and the handling of whistleblowing concerns demonstrate how limited transparency can erode public trust and hinder effective democratic scrutiny. Ensuring that procurement data, contract performance metrics, and fee structures are openly accessible allows citizens and auditors to verify value for money and hold officials accountable, thereby preventing misconduct and ensuring that public funds serve their intended community purposes.
