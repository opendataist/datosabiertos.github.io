Today in Austria: A roundup of the latest news on Thursday

Austria has finally enacted a Freedom of Information Act, fulfilling a decades-long push for transparency that began in the late 1980s. This landmark legislation marks a significant shift away from official secrecy, empowering citizens and journalists to access government information. While political parties generally welcome the compromise, debates continue regarding exemptions for small municipalities and the lack of automatic responses to rejected requests, which critics argue may create a two-tier society based on legal literacy. The passage of this law is critically relevant to open data because it establishes the legal framework necessary for public institutions to make non-sensitive information available to the public. By mandating greater transparency and protecting investigative journalism, the act encourages the systematic publication of government data. This legislative step transforms open data from a voluntary initiative into a regulated right, ensuring that citizens can hold authorities accountable through accessible information. Beyond governance, the article highlights broader societal trends in Austria, including energy resilience through innovative heat exchange between hospitals and data centers, rising road safety concerns for senior citizens, and political tensions involving far-right movements. However, the core narrative remains centered on the structural improvement of civic access to information, setting a precedent for future transparency efforts across the country.
