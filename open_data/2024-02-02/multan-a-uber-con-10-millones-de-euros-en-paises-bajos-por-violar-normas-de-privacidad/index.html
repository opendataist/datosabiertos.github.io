Multan a Uber con 10 millones de euros en Países Bajos por violar normas de privacidad

Uber has been hit with a substantial penalty for failing to ensure transparency concerning the retention and international transfer of driver data. This case underscores that privacy laws require clarity: individuals cannot exercise their rights or grasp potential unfairness if they lack access to information about how their personal data is managed and shared globally. The regulator stresses that accessibility is crucial; merely providing digital tools is insufficient if they are difficult to locate or use. Companies are legally obligated to facilitate the process for individuals to view or obtain their data. Making such requests unnecessarily complex violates the principle that data subjects must be able to easily verify and control their personal information without encountering artificial barriers. This enforcement action is relevant to open data, as it highlights that transparency and accessibility are foundational, not optional. Without clear, user-friendly access to data practices, the potential for open and accountable data ecosystems remains unrealized. It serves as a reminder that respecting individual rights through simplicity and honesty is essential for maintaining trust in digital services and data handling.
