Los datos no publicados de las votaciones del Balón de Oro: "No hubo competencia, Dembélé ganó por mucho"

Los datos no publicados de las votaciones del Balón de Oro: "No hubo competencia, Dembélé ganó por mucho"

Ousmane Dembélé has been unanimously crowned the winner of the 2025 Ballon d’Or, succeeding Rodri as the world’s best footballer. The editor-in-chief of France Football emphasized that the victory was decisive and unequivocal, noting that Dembélé secured broad support from judges across all continents. This outcome highlights a consensus among international media that Dembélé’s individual performance and contributions made him the clear choice, overshadowing other strong contenders in a field that lacked genuine competition. Despite the significant success of his teammates at Paris Saint-Germain, their presence did not fragment the voting pool in favor of other club members. While players like Vitinha and Achraf Hakimi received some first-place votes, the distribution remained heavily skewed toward Dembélé. This suggests that judges prioritized individual excellence over team affiliation, reinforcing the award’s focus on personal merit rather than collective club achievements during the evaluation period. This announcement is relevant to open data as it illustrates the transparency and global reach of voting mechanisms in sports journalism. The detailed breakdown of votes across different continents and the clear ranking of finalists demonstrate how public datasets can reveal patterns in global opinion and media influence. By making such granular voting results accessible, organizations like France Football contribute to the ecosystem of open sports data, allowing analysts and fans to study trends in recognition and valuation within the football community.
