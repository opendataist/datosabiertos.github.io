Proposed Generative AI Disclosure Act Seeks Transparency and Fairness in AI Training Datasets

U.S. Representative Adam Schiff has introduced the Generative AI Disclosure Act to address the contentious use of copyrighted material in training artificial intelligence models. This legislation mandates that AI developers provide transparency by submitting detailed notices to the Copyright Office before launching new systems. These disclosures must include comprehensive lists of copyrighted works and dataset URLs, aiming to balance technological innovation with fairness for content creators. The bill establishes a publicly accessible database for these disclosures, allowing creators to verify if their works were used without authorization. By enforcing substantial civil penalties for non-compliance, the proposed law seeks to ensure accountability in an industry that currently lacks specific regulatory guidance. Supporters argue this measure protects creator rights and fosters responsible development, serving as a critical first step toward clearer legal frameworks for AI-generated outputs. This legislative effort is highly relevant to open data discussions because it highlights the tension between open accessibility and intellectual property rights. While open data promotes transparency and innovation, this act suggests that unchecked access to training data can infringe on creators' rights. It underscores the necessity for clear governance standards that define how data can be ethically sourced and used, impacting future policies on data openness, attribution, and the ethical deployment of machine learning technologies.
