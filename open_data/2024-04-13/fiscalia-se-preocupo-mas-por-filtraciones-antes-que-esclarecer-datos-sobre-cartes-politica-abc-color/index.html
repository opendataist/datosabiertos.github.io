Fiscalía se preocupó más por filtraciones antes que esclarecer datos sobre Cartes - Política - ABC Color

Fiscalía se preocupó más por filtraciones antes que esclarecer datos sobre Cartes - Política - ABC Color

The article highlights a critical failure in the Paraguayan justice system, where prosecutors Aldo Cantero and Giovanni Grisetti focused more on suppressing sensitive intelligence leaks than on verifying the substantial evidence against former president Horacio Cartes. Despite Seprelad’s detailed financial reports alleging illicit activities, including complex networks for tobacco trafficking and unexplained offshore transfers, the Ministry of Public Prosecution did not disprove these findings. Instead, they prioritized protecting reputations and questioning the data collection methods, revealing a disturbing lack of judicial rigor in addressing serious allegations of money laundering. This negligence extends to international cooperation, as prosecutors criticized the unauthorized publication of confidential requests from Panama without attempting to verify the underlying claims regarding Cartes’s undeclared offshore entities. Furthermore, the failure to scrutinize significant financial flows involving high-ranking officials, such as current president Santiago Peña and former Seprelad head Óscar Boidanich, undermines the integrity of anti-money laundering efforts. By ignoring the implications of these transactions, the authorities effectively allowed potentially illegal financial practices to remain uninvestigated, eroding public trust in institutional accountability. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the dangers of transparency being misinterpreted as a security threat rather than a civic right. It demonstrates how sensitive public interest data, when leaked or made accessible, can expose systemic failures in governance and law enforcement. The incident serves as a cautionary tale for open data advocates, emphasizing that without robust legal frameworks to protect whistleblowers and journalists, valuable information regarding corruption may be suppressed by powerful entities, hindering democratic oversight and the fight against financial crimes.
