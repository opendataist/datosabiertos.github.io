CIC pulls up EC for not replying to RTI query on EVMs

CIC pulls up EC for not replying to RTI query on EVMs

The Central Information Commission recently censured the Election Commission for failing to respond to a Right to Information request regarding concerns about voting machine credibility. This delay was deemed a serious breach of legal obligations, highlighting the critical importance of accountability in public institutions. The ruling underscores that transparency is not optional but a mandatory requirement for democratic bodies. This incident reveals significant gaps in how electoral authorities handle citizen inquiries, particularly those involving technical scrutiny of election integrity. When public authorities ignore statutory timelines, it erodes trust in the system and denies citizens their right to know. The Commission’s directive for a written explanation signals a strict stance against administrative silence and impunity in matters of public interest. This case is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the necessity of enforcing access to information laws to ensure government data is accessible and responsive. Without robust enforcement mechanisms, transparency initiatives remain ineffective, and public scrutiny of critical infrastructure like voting systems is hindered. Ultimately, this ruling reinforces that open data practices must be backed by legal accountability to maintain democratic credibility and public trust in electoral processes.
