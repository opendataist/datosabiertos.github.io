Bucks Council moving archives to High Wycombe Tesco as it struggles for space

Buckinghamshire Council is addressing a critical infrastructure crisis by relocating its county archives from cramped, substandard facilities to a newly refurbished space above a downsized Tesco superstore. The current Aylesbury offices have reached full capacity, threatening the loss of national accreditation due to inadequate climate control and storage conditions. This move ensures compliance with preservation standards and safeguards centuries of historical records, including maps and electoral registers, from permanent loss. The decision is driven by the urgent need to meet professional archival standards and enable future collection growth. Current limitations restrict the council’s ability to acquire materials reflecting the diverse heritage of Buckinghamshire’s residents. By securing dedicated, climate-controlled strong rooms, the authority can properly preserve documents ranging from Victorian workhouse records to 800-year-old artifacts, ensuring these cultural assets remain accessible and secure for the long term. This relocation is highly relevant to open data as it establishes the physical and digital infrastructure necessary for preserving public records. Without proper archival conditions, the integrity and accessibility of the data degrade, hindering transparency and public engagement. Furthermore, the plan integrates archaeological findings from major infrastructure projects like HS2, ensuring that data generated by development is systematically cataloged. This consolidation supports the open data principle by creating a stable, standardized repository where citizens can reliably access historical and archaeological information, fostering greater accountability and community knowledge.
