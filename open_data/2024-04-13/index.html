Open Data’s Double Edge: Accountability vs. Privacy and AI Risks

Open data lets people see public information directly. This transparency has good sides and bad sides.

On the positive side, open data helps hold organizations accountable. For example, in the UK, people used freedom of information requests to find out that museums were losing items. This forced the museums to improve their security and care for their collections. Open data also helps public health. In the UK, people could see detailed reports on food safety. These reports showed problems like rat infestations in restaurants. This made businesses fix safety issues quickly.

Open data also helps people make better economic decisions. Users can check if government policies are working. For instance, Argentina had an 11% inflation rate. This high number showed that strict money policies were needed. In Spain, data showed that the island of Menorca was falling behind other islands economically.

However, there are serious downsides. Open data can violate privacy and create legal problems. Recently, the Delhi High Court in India ruled that travel records are private. This means they are protected by law. As a result, platforms must hide sensitive information to protect individuals’ rights.

New technology adds more complexity. “Synthetic data” is made by computers rather than real people. Current privacy laws, like the GDPR in Europe, focus on real personal data. They do not clearly cover synthetic data. This creates a gray area. It risks revealing real identities or showing bias.

Also, open data can hurt the rights of creators. In the U.S., Representative Adam Schiff proposed a new law. This law would require AI companies to reveal the data they used to train their systems. This aims to protect copyrights while allowing innovation.

Finally, there is a tension between sharing data and governing it ethically. While some groups, like Kakao, are joining alliances to set standards, the lack of strong rules for synthetic data is worrying. Without clear labels and rules, people may lose trust in these systems.
