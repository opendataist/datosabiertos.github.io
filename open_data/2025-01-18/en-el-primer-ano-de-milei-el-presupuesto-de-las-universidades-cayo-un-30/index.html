En el primer año de Milei, el presupuesto de las universidades cayó un 30%

En el primer año de Milei, el presupuesto de las universidades cayó un 30%

The article highlights a deep conflict between the Argentine national government and the public university sector, centered on a drastic 30% reduction in university budgets and corresponding cuts to faculty salaries. This financial squeeze, exacerbated by the presidential veto of the University Funding Law, has triggered massive nationwide protests involving hundreds of thousands of students, academics, and union members. The government frames these measures as necessary corrections against alleged mismanagement, while the academic community argues that the austerity is arbitrary and ignores the actual operational needs of the institutions. A significant part of the dispute involves accusations of opacity and the fabrication of student numbers to justify funding. The executive branch claims universities resist audits and harbor "ghost students," allegations that rectors and university officials firmly deny. They point to existing public auditing mechanisms and transparent digital registration systems as proof of accountability. The government’s introduction of new inspections is viewed by the university sector as a political tool to delegitimize their autonomy and demand for resources, rather than a genuine effort to improve financial transparency or efficiency. This situation is critically relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and verifiable public information to hold power accountable. The conflict demonstrates how governments can manipulate narratives around public spending when data is not universally accessible or easily auditable by civil society. By highlighting the discrepancy between claimed inefficiencies and the reality of transparent, public auditing systems, the article illustrates that open data is essential for validating institutional integrity and ensuring that public resources are allocated based on evidence rather than political bias.
