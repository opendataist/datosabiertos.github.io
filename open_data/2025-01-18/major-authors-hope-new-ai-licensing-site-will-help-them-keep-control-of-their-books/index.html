Major authors hope new AI licensing site will help them keep control of their books

The article highlights a pivotal shift in the relationship between creators and artificial intelligence, moving from adversarial litigation toward collaborative licensing frameworks. By introducing "Created by Humans," a marketplace partnered with The Authors Guild, authors can now voluntarily grant AI companies permission to use their work in exchange for compensation and transparency. This approach addresses the growing indignation among writers who feel their intellectual property is being exploited without consent, offering a structured alternative to the current wave of copyright lawsuits. For the open data community, this development underscores the critical importance of consent and ethical data sourcing in machine learning. It demonstrates a practical mechanism where data producers, specifically literary authors, can exercise agency over how their content is ingested by AI models. By establishing clear terms and maintaining non-exclusive rights, the platform suggests that open data principles can be extended to commercial creative works, ensuring that the training data ecosystem respects individual ownership rather than relying on ambiguous fair use interpretations. Ultimately, this initiative seeks to bridge the gap between the AI industry and the creative community by simplifying the complex landscape of licensing. While the financial and legal protections are still emerging, the model offers a clearer set of principles for using human-generated content. This relevance to open data lies in its potential to set precedents for how large-scale data collection should be conducted: with prior permission, adequate remuneration, and full disclosure, fostering a more equitable and sustainable relationship between technology developers and content creators.
