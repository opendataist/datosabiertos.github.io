Fraudsters exploit compromised official databases to impersonate Social Security agents, gaining victims’ trust by using precise personal data before extorting money or carrying out financial scams. This highlights a critical vulnerability in how sensitive government information is managed and shared, demonstrating the severe risks that arise when open data lacks adequate privacy controls and access restrictions. The ease with which criminals access such detailed personal records underscores the urgent need for robust data governance frameworks that protect individual privacy without stifling transparency. Without stricter safeguards, the availability of certain public-sector information becomes a tool for exploitation rather than civic empowerment. This case is highly relevant to open-data advocates, as it illustrates the fine line between transparency and security. It emphasizes that open-data initiatives must prioritize data minimization and strict access protocols to prevent malicious actors from leveraging public information for criminal activities, ensuring that openness does not compromise citizen safety.
