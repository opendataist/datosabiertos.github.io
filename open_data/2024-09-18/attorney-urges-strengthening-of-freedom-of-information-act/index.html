Attorney urges strengthening of Freedom of Information Act

Legal expert E Ann Henry advocates for a comprehensive overhaul of the Freedom of Information Act, arguing that current enforcement mechanisms are too weak to ensure compliance. She highlights that the burden of proving wilful obstruction and the high costs of litigation deter citizens from seeking information, effectively blocking access to justice. Strengthening these provisions is essential to prevent transparency laws from becoming merely symbolic gestures that lack real-world impact. The article emphasizes the critical role of the newly appointed Information Commissioner in bridging this gap. With the position vacant for years, government accountability has suffered, as evidenced by legislators' inability to obtain records regarding public spending. The Commissioner’s mandate extends beyond handling individual requests to ensuring government departments are adequately trained and resourced, making this leadership vacancy a significant barrier to effective open data governance. This discussion is highly relevant to open_data because it exposes the structural and legal barriers that hinder data accessibility and usability. True open data requires more than just publication; it demands enforceable rights, clear governance standards, and accessible dispute resolution mechanisms. Without these safeguards, public data remains inaccessible in practice, undermining the potential for transparency and civic engagement in democratic societies.
