Crecen escolaridad e ingresos, pero persisten brechas laborales por género y ocupación - Economía - ABC Color

Crecen escolaridad e ingresos, pero persisten brechas laborales por género y ocupación - Economía - ABC Color

The article demonstrates that, although improvements in educational attainment and labor income represent a general national trend, they do not guarantee equity. A positive correlation between education and wages is confirmed, with the public sector standing out for its high standards in both areas, while domestic workers, who have lower levels of schooling, face limited wage growth. This underscores that academic training is fundamental but insufficient on its own to close structural gaps in the labor market. Furthermore, a persistent gender wage gap is identified, despite women, on average, having higher levels of education than men. This disparity persists across all segments, including both the public and private sectors, demonstrating that inequalities are not solely due to differences in qualifications. The data reveal that women continue to earn significantly less, indicating the existence of systemic biases that go beyond individual human capital. This information is relevant to open data because it illustrates how public datasets can debunk simplistic narratives about educational merit and expose deep-seated social inequalities. By making these discrepancies visible, open data facilitate public scrutiny and accountability, playing an essential role in designing evidence-based public policies that address gender equity and the valuation of work, thereby promoting more inclusive development.
