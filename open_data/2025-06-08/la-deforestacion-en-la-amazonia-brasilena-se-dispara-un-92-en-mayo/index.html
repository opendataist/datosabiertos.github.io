La deforestación en la Amazonía brasileña se dispara un 92% en mayo

The alarming surge in Amazon deforestation in Brazil signals a significant setback to national environmental goals, undermining the downward trend established since the current administration took office. This sharp increase jeopardizes the presidential commitment to eliminate illegal logging by 2030, indicating that previous progress is fragile and potentially reversible without immediate, intensified intervention and policy adjustments. Consequently, Brazil’s ability to meet its international climate obligations under the Paris Agreement is severely compromised, as forest loss drives a substantial portion of its greenhouse gas emissions. The rising deforestation rates not only threaten biodiversity and indigenous communities but also diminish the region’s critical capacity to store carbon, posing a direct challenge to global efforts to mitigate climate change during upcoming UN negotiations. This situation is highly relevant to open data because transparent, timely, and accessible monitoring systems are essential for holding governments accountable and informing global environmental policy. Reliable public data allows civil society and international bodies to verify claims, identify trends early, and demand evidence-based actions, making the availability of accurate deforestation metrics a cornerstone of effective environmental governance and global trust.
