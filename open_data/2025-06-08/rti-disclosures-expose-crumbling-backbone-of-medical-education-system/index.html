RTI disclosures expose crumbling backbone of medical education system

RTI disclosures expose crumbling backbone of medical education system

RTI disclosures reveal a systemic crisis in India’s medical education, where postgraduate residents face extreme workloads, including shifts up to 36 hours, despite regulatory limits. This institutionalized overwork, often justified by patient load, leads to severe physical and psychological exhaustion among young doctors. The data indicates a widespread normalization of unsafe working conditions across elite institutes, highlighting a stark contrast between official policies and ground realities. The human cost is evident in rising dropout rates and tragic incidents involving student suicides driven by academic and professional stress. These outcomes reflect a failure to implement the Uniform Residency Scheme, which mandates reasonable duty hours and rest periods. Without a central monitoring mechanism to enforce these norms, residents lack protection from excessive work, leading to burnout and a significant attrition of talent from the healthcare system. This situation is critical for open data advocates as it demonstrates how transparency mechanisms like RTI can expose institutional failures when official records are fragmented or ignored. The lack of centralized, accessible data on duty breaches and grievances undermines accountability and hinders evidence-based policy reform. Bridging this data gap is essential to ensuring resident safety, improving healthcare quality, and holding regulatory bodies accountable for their oversight responsibilities.
