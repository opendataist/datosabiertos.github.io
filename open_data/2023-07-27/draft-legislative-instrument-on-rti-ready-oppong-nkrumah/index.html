Draft Legislative Instrument on RTI ready – Oppong Nkrumah

The Ghanaian government has finalized the drafting of the Legislative Instrument supporting the Right to Information law, marking a critical step toward institutionalizing transparency. With stakeholder consultations concluding soon, this regulatory framework aims to solidify the legal infrastructure necessary for effective information access. This development is vital for open data as it establishes the mandatory legal baseline that empowers citizens to claim their rights to public information, ensuring that data disclosure is not merely discretionary but enforceable by law. Parallel to legal advancements, the RTI Commission has launched a five-year strategic plan to expand its operational reach and enhance its quasi-judicial functions. By opening new regional offices and implementing an online records management system, the commission seeks to democratize access to information across diverse demographics and geographic areas. This systemic upgrade is significant for open data ecosystems because it reduces bureaucratic friction, allowing for more efficient data requests and responses while promoting the digital infrastructure required for modern, accessible government transparency. Successful implementation of these initiatives relies heavily on multi-stakeholder partnerships and external funding to sustain operations beyond government treasury constraints. The emphasis on aligning demand, supply, and regulation creates a holistic environment where transparency is actively cultivated rather than passively managed. For the open data community, this highlights the necessity of collaborative governance models; sustainable transparency requires continuous engagement from civil society and development partners to maintain accountability and ensure that information flows freely to the public.
