Emails reveal more information about ‘communication protocol’ in Davenport City Hall

Two Davenport City Council members were placed under an unofficial communication protocol by the City Attorney, requiring them to route all interactions with city staff through legal channels. This restriction effectively isolates elected officials from the employees who possess the information necessary to address constituent concerns. The aldermen argue this arrangement obstructs their official duties and prevents them from effectively serving their communities, creating significant administrative roadblocks in municipal governance. The controversy highlights a severe lack of transparency and due process, as the policy exists only in verbal directives rather than written city handbooks. One council member was targeted for asking about potential environmental violations and staff retirements, while the other faced restrictions after admitting to inappropriate comments. The absence of a codified policy raises serious questions about the arbitrary application of power and the right of elected officials to investigate city operations without undue interference or punishment for legitimate scrutiny. This situation is critical to the open_data community because it demonstrates how bureaucratic opacity can actively suppress the flow of public information. When communication pathways are legally restricted or socially enforced without clear rules, citizens lose the ability to hold their government accountable. It underscores the necessity for transparent, accessible data channels that operate independently of personnel disputes, ensuring that public servants can share essential information without fear of retribution or procedural obstruction.
