State authorities are actively investigating illegal roadside checkpoints in Sonora, coordinating with federal agencies to identify individuals involved in their operation. The core conclusion is that any traffic stop lacking official identification, established protocols, and visible QR codes is strictly illegal. This investigation underscores that security operations must never be conducted unilaterally or clandestinely, but rather through transparent, supervised mechanisms that prevent abuse of power and ensure public safety through accountability. Relevant to open data, this case highlights the critical role of transparency in governance. The requirement for visible QR codes and clear identification serves as a public interface for accountability, allowing citizens to verify the legitimacy of authority actions. By making the rationale for checks accessible and reportable, the state promotes a system where data about administrative conduct is open and verifiable. This approach empowers the public to monitor government behavior directly, fostering trust through digital transparency rather than opaque enforcement. Ultimately, the emphasis on mandatory multi-agency presence and standardized procedures reinforces the need for open, auditable processes in public security. The call for citizens to report suspicious activity via direct channels to federal or state bodies illustrates a commitment to civic oversight. This framework demonstrates how open principles can be integrated into law enforcement, ensuring that power is exercised within defined legal boundaries and that all actions are subject to immediate public scrutiny and correction.
