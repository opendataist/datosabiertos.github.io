El Constitucional afianza el derecho al olvido en internet

El Constitucional afianza el derecho al olvido en internet

The Spanish Constitutional Court has reaffirmed the right to be forgotten on the internet, ruling that search engines must remove links to outdated personal information or data lacking public interest. In this case, a merchant was protected by dissociating his name from professional criticisms published on servers outside the European Union, establishing that privacy takes precedence over the historical accessibility of outdated data. This ruling clarifies the limits of the right to data erasure, emphasizing that search companies have the responsibility to comply with data protection regulations when content no longer justifies its retention. It weighs the conflict between individual privacy and freedom of expression, concluding that privacy prevails when information loses its timeliness and social relevance. This does not imply the removal of the original content, but rather its dissociation from the individual’s identifier. This judgment is fundamental to the field of open data and digital ethics, as it establishes a crucial legal precedent regarding the management of memory on the internet. It defines how transparency and information availability must be balanced with individuals’ fundamental rights, guiding technology platforms and developers in creating systems that respect personal dignity and privacy by default, even when data originates from open or global sources.
