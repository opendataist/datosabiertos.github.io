Major U.S. cities appear to have gained residents due to Census Bureau corrections rather than actual migration. This highlights the critical importance of data accuracy and transparency in government statistics, a core tenet of open data initiatives. These adjustments resulted from merging previously separate urban areas, proving that raw datasets often require rigorous validation and cleaning. Such errors demonstrate why open data ecosystems must prioritize quality control mechanisms to ensure reliable information. Ultimately, this situation underscores the need for public scrutiny and accessible data records. When datasets are open and understandable, citizens can identify discrepancies, fostering trust and encouraging continuous improvement in how public information is collected and shared.
