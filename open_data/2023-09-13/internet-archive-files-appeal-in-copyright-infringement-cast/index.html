Internet Archive Files Appeal In Copyright Infringement Cast

The Internet Archive has formally appealed the ruling in Hatchette v. Internet Archive, contesting a lower court’s decision that its scanning and digital lending program constituted copyright infringement. By challenging this judgment, the Archive seeks to overturn permanent injunctions that restrict its ability to lend unauthorized digital scans of commercially available books. This legal maneuver highlights a critical battleground for the future of digital preservation and library services, as the outcome will determine whether established library practices can adapt to the digital age or must submit to restrictive corporate licensing models. This appeal is vital to open data and open access communities because it directly threatens the concept of controlled digital lending. The initial ruling cast doubt on the legal theory allowing libraries to lend digitized versions of physically owned items, potentially dismantling a key mechanism for equitable information distribution. If the appeal fails, it sets a precedent that could severely limit non-commercial sharing of cultural heritage, forcing institutions to rely on expensive, proprietary platforms that often impose strict usage limits and hinder universal access. The case underscores the tension between copyright protection and the public interest in preserving knowledge. Libraries argue that their core functions of preservation and access are under attack from both legal pressures and budget cuts. The Internet Archive’s fight represents a broader struggle to maintain an open, accessible internet where information is not gated by commercial interests. For anyone committed to free knowledge, this appeal is a crucial test of whether legal frameworks will protect the public’s right to learn and explore without undue restriction.
