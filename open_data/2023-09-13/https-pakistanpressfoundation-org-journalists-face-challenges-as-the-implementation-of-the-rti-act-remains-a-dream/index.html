The Balochistan government’s failure to implement the Right to Information Act 2021, despite its legislative approval, highlights a critical disconnect between legal frameworks and administrative action. The core issue is the prolonged delay in establishing the Information Commission and approving necessary operational rules, which has rendered the law effectively dormant for years. This inaction prevents the enforcement mechanisms required to hold public officials accountable and ensures that information officers are properly designated and trained. For journalists and civil society, this gap severely hampers transparency and accountability. Without a functional commission, citizens cannot access data on public spending, government projects, or bureaucratic conduct, leaving them dependent on opaque internal processes. The lack of implementation not only violates constitutional entitlements but also erodes public trust, as officials frequently ignore requests or claim ignorance of the law due to the absence of clear procedural guidelines. This situation is pertinent to open data because statutory support is essential for the creation and maintenance of open data ecosystems. Without an enforceable right to information and an active oversight body, governments have little incentive to proactively release data or respond to access requests. Consequently, the stagnation of this act demonstrates how weak governance structures can obstruct the flow of public information, undermining efforts to promote transparency and civic engagement.
