Kenya wins Bid to host the International Open Data Conference

Kenya wins Bid to host the International Open Data Conference

The International Open Data Conference is moving to Africa for the first time, with Kenya selected to host the 2020 event in Nairobi. This decision highlights the continent’s growing maturity in data governance and its strategic shift toward leveraging information for development. Kenya secured the hosting rights due to sustained high-level political commitment and tangible initiatives that demonstrate a robust, inclusive data ecosystem involving government, private sector, and civil society. The conference aims to strengthen South-South collaboration and bridge diverse data communities globally. With a theme centered on connecting stakeholders, the event seeks to share best practices and accelerate the impact of open data across critical sectors like health, agriculture, and fiscal transparency. This gathering serves as a practical platform to enhance interoperability and accessibility, ensuring that data initiatives directly support broader development priorities and international commitments. This shift is highly relevant to open data as it democratizes access to global knowledge networks, reducing historical centralization in the Global North. By empowering African nations to lead these conversations, the conference fosters local innovation and ensures that data solutions are tailored to regional challenges. It signals a pivotal moment where developing regions are not just consumers of open data but active shapers of global policy, driving more equitable and effective use of information for societal benefit.
