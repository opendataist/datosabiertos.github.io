Can I get UFO records through the Freedom of Information Act? Ask the lawyer

Can I get UFO records through the Freedom of Information Act? Ask the lawyer

This article explains how citizens can use Freedom of Information Act and state Public Records Act requests to access government documents, emphasizing that these tools are essential for increasing transparency and accountability. FOIA allows access to federal executive branch records, while California’s Public Records Act covers state and local agencies. Both mechanisms empower the public to seek information, though exemptions exist to protect national security, privacy, and proprietary interests. Regarding UFOs, now termed unidentified anomalous phenomena, federal agencies are mandated to organize and disclose records by the end of the fiscal year, with archives stored at the National Archives. For local incidents, such as hit-and-run cases involving officials, individuals can file specific Public Records Act requests. While responses may take time and records may be redacted, these legal avenues provide a structured path to uncover truths that might otherwise remain hidden due to institutional silence or lack of public attention. These laws are highly relevant to open data because they establish the legal framework for public access to government-held information. Understanding how to navigate FOIA and state requests enables citizens and researchers to obtain critical datasets, holding officials accountable and ensuring that public records serve the community’s interest in transparency. This process supports the open data movement by converting closed bureaucratic information into accessible, actionable public knowledge, fostering trust and informed civic engagement.
