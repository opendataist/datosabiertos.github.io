Buscan que universidades privadas que reciban fondos estatales estén sometidas a Ley de Transparencia

A legislative proposal seeks to mandate transparency in private universities and institutes that receive state funding by requiring them to proactively disclose financial management data. This initiative responds to a high-profile controversy involving excessive compensation for former officials, highlighting a critical gap in accountability within publicly funded private educational institutions. The core obligation involves publishing detailed information such as organizational structures, income and expense statements, and specific salary data for high-level academic and administrative staff. By integrating these requirements into existing public access laws, the project ensures that financial dealings are visible to the public, thereby preventing the misuse of resources for personal enrichment or opaque political financing. This development is highly relevant to open data principles, as it expands the scope of mandatory transparency beyond traditional government bodies to include semi-private entities handling public money. It reinforces the necessity of accessible, granular financial data to ensure democratic oversight and trust, demonstrating how open data frameworks can effectively combat corruption and promote fiscal responsibility in the education sector.
