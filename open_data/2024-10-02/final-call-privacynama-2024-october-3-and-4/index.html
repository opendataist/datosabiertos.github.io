Final Call: PrivacyNama 2024, October 3 and 4

PrivacyNama 2024 convenes global experts to examine the critical intersection of Artificial Intelligence and privacy regulations. The central inquiry focuses on whether privacy-preserving AI is feasible amidst the proliferation of models trained on mixed datasets containing both personal and non-personal data. This dialogue is essential for defining how traditional principles like purpose limitation and consent apply to modern machine learning technologies. The discussion highlights tensions between regulatory frameworks, such as India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, and the open nature of open-source Large Language Models. Key concerns include the impact of publicly disclosed data exemptions on privacy rights and how data sovereignty restrictions might influence cross-border data flows. These debates are vital for establishing clear guidelines on data trust and the ethical sourcing of non-personal data to mitigate bias in AI systems. This event is highly relevant to the open_data community as it directly addresses the availability of non-personal data for training. By exploring mechanisms to share voice, image, and video datasets while ensuring privacy, the conference offers insights into balancing transparency with protection. Understanding these regulatory and technical challenges helps open_data advocates navigate the complexities of deploying inclusive, region-specific AI solutions without compromising individual rights.
