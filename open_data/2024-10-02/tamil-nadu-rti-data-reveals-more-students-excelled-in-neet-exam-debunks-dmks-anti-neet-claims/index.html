Tamil Nadu: RTI data reveals more students excelled in NEET exam, debunks DMK’s anti-NEET claims

Data obtained through a Right to Information request significantly challenges the political narrative propagated by the Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam regarding the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test. The party has long argued that the exam disproportionately harms rural and government school students by necessitating expensive coaching and causing undue mental distress. However, the newly revealed statistics demonstrate a marked improvement in the performance of disadvantaged students, directly contradicting claims that the system is inherently biased against them or detrimental to rural healthcare initiatives. The evidence indicates that government school students are achieving substantial success without relying on the reserved quotas or private tutoring, suggesting they are competitive in a national framework. A significant portion of admitted medical students did not attend coaching classes, and many secured seats purely on merit. This undermines the assertion that private education is a prerequisite for success, highlighting instead that systemic barriers may have been overstated by political opponents seeking to abolish the standardized testing mechanism. This revelation is highly relevant to the open data community because it illustrates how transparent access to public records can debunk politically motivated narratives with empirical evidence. It reinforces the value of RTI mechanisms in holding governments accountable and fostering informed public discourse. By making performance metrics visible, open data enables citizens to evaluate policy effectiveness based on reality rather than rhetoric, ensuring that debates about educational equity are grounded in factual, verifiable outcomes rather than unsubstantiated claims.
