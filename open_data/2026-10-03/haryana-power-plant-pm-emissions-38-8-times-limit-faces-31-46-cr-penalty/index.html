Haryana power plant PM emissions 38.8 times limit, faces ₹31.46 cr penalty

Haryana power plant PM emissions 38.8 times limit, faces ₹31.46 cr penalty

A thermal power plant in Haryana has been found to emit particulate matter at levels drastically exceeding national safety standards, triggering significant environmental penalties. The Central Pollution Control Board determined that emissions were nearly forty times the prescribed limit for one unit, classifying the facility as highly polluting. Consequently, substantial interim compensation was assessed for the period of non-compliance, highlighting the strict financial liabilities associated with violating air quality norms. This case underscores the critical role of transparent monitoring and regulatory enforcement in the energy sector. The findings, revealed through a Right to Information request, demonstrate that data transparency empowers civil society to hold industrial operators accountable. By exposing the gap between actual emissions and legal limits, such disclosures reveal systemic operational failures, including inadequate dust control and defective monitoring systems, which are often hidden from public view without active scrutiny. The incident is particularly relevant to the open data movement as it validates the necessity of accessible, verifiable environmental data. It illustrates how open access to government records and inspection reports can drive compliance and protect public health. Ensuring that emission data is not only collected but also made publicly available and auditable is essential for maintaining trust in regulatory frameworks and encouraging industries to adopt cleaner practices.
