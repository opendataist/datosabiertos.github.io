Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

A recent report reveals a paradox where immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions, including lower income and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness, yet remain as satisfied with life as the native population. Despite these socioeconomic disparities, immigrants demonstrate strong social cohesion, evidenced by their high trust in key institutions and a deep affiliation with the country. This data highlights the complexity of integrating diverse populations, showing that objective hardship does not necessarily correlate with subjective dissatisfaction. The findings underscore the importance of examining both material standards and psychological well-being when assessing integration success, as traditional metrics often overlook the nuanced emotional resilience and institutional confidence held by immigrant communities. This article is relevant to open_data initiatives because it illustrates the power of accessible, comprehensive statistical surveys in uncovering societal contradictions. By making such detailed demographic and psychosocial data available, researchers and policymakers can better understand the full spectrum of integration challenges. Open access to this information facilitates more informed discussions on equity, mental health support, and the design of effective social policies for diverse communities.
