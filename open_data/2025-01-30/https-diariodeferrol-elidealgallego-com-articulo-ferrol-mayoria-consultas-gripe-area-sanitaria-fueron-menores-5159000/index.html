Galicia is experiencing stable, low-intensity flu activity, with consultation rates remaining the lowest in the region and hospital admissions decreasing significantly. Although pediatric cases have risen slightly, the overall trend suggests a manageable situation, with the virus’s behavior contrasting with typical early-year spikes. Crucially, the regional health authority has extended the vaccination campaign deadline to ensure broader public protection, urging citizens to take advantage of the remaining days to get vaccinated at health centers. The relevance to open data lies in the transparency and real-time availability of these public health metrics. By publishing detailed weekly reports on consultation rates, hospitalizations, and virus positivity, authorities enable researchers and developers to create tools that monitor disease spread. This accessible data supports evidence-based decision-making, allowing communities to track the effectiveness of interventions such as extended vaccination campaigns and to understand demographic impacts without relying on opaque systems. Furthermore, the clear communication of statistical trends, such as the decline in respiratory infections and vulnerabilities in specific age groups, empowers users to analyze public health patterns. Open access to this granular information fosters trust and enables the development of innovative applications that visualize health data, ultimately helping society better prepare for and respond to current and future respiratory health challenges through data-driven insights.
