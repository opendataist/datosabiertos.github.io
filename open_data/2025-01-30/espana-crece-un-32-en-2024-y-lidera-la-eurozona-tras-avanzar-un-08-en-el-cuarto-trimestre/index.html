España crece un 3,2% en 2024 y lidera la eurozona, tras avanzar un 0,8% en el cuarto trimestre

Spain’s economic growth in 2024, which exceeded both government expectations and international projections, stands out as the highest in the eurozone. This balanced performance, driven primarily by domestic demand and internal consumption, demonstrates notable resilience in the face of a weak European context and adverse events such as the floods in Valencia. The future sustainability of this model will depend on maintaining investment and improving productivity, as external demand remains constrained by the slowdown of key trading partners. In the realm of open data, this case underscores the critical importance of transparency and accessibility in macroeconomic data. The National Statistics Institute’s (INE) ability to quickly publish preliminary updates enables analysts, markets, and citizens to compare the actual economic situation with official projections in real time. This immediacy fosters institutional trust and enables accountability, as the scientific community and media can verify hypotheses about social well-being and the effectiveness of public policies based on verifiable evidence. The relevance to open data lies in how these structured data enable independent analyses that validate or challenge official narratives. By making detailed breakdowns by sector and region accessible, society is empowered to understand underlying dynamics, such as the differentiated impact of natural disasters or the specific weight of private investment. This transforms statistics from an administrative tool into a common good that facilitates informed democratic participation and strategic decision-making in both the public and private sectors.
