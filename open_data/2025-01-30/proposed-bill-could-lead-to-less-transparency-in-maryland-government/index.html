Proposed bill could lead to less transparency in Maryland government

Proposed bill could lead to less transparency in Maryland government

The proposed legislation in Maryland seeks to empower government agencies to ignore or block public records requests deemed frivolous, vexatious, or abusive. While proponents argue this measure is necessary to prevent harassment and improve governmental efficiency, critics warn that it fundamentally undermines the Maryland Public Information Act. By allowing custodians of records to suppress inquiries based on vague criteria, the bill risks turning a cornerstone of democratic accountability into a tool for political convenience. The primary concern lies in the lack of clear definitions for what constitutes an abusive request, creating a loophole that could be exploited to deny transparency for any reason. This ambiguity threatens the core principle that public access to information is vital for rooting out corruption, waste, and fraud within state agencies. Without strict safeguards, the process could become politicized, enabling officials to hide their activities rather than subject them to public scrutiny, thereby eroding trust in democratic institutions. This issue is highly relevant to open data because the ability to obtain, process, and share government records is the foundation of data-driven accountability. If barriers to accessing information are lowered under the guise of efficiency, the flow of open data necessary for independent analysis and oversight will be severely restricted. Ultimately, protecting the right to request information ensures that open data remains a transparent resource for citizens, rather than a controlled commodity withheld by those in power.
