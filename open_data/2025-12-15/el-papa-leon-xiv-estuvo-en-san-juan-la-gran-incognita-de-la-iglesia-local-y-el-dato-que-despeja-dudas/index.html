El papa León XIV, ¿estuvo en San Juan?: la gran incógnita de la Iglesia local y el dato que despeja dudas

The election of Robert Prevost as Pope Leo XIV highlights the critical role of accessible, transparent institutional records in verifying historical narratives. Although local Church sources in San Juan confirmed the absence of formal documentation regarding the Pope's previous visits, the lack of centralized, publicly searchable archives allows speculation to persist. This situation underscores a fundamental challenge for open data initiatives: without standardized, digitized, and open registries of institutional movements, verifying historical facts remains dependent on fragmented human memory rather than verifiable data, leaving gaps that fuel uncertainty. The contrast between the confirmed presence of the Pope in Mendoza and the unverified status in San Juan illustrates the inconsistency in data availability across regions. While one province possesses documented testimonies, the other relies on the absence of evidence due to a century-long institutional discontinuity. This disparity emphasizes that open data is not merely about publishing information, but ensuring its comprehensiveness and comparability. When historical data is siloed or missing, it hinders the ability to construct a complete and accurate regional history, impacting both scholarly research and public trust in institutional transparency. Ultimately, this article demonstrates that open data serves as an essential tool for truth-seeking within large, complex organizations like the Catholic Church. By advocating for the digitization and open access of ecclesiastical records, society can transform vague historical hypotheses into verified facts. The relevance to open data lies in its capacity to bridge the gap between oral tradition and empirical evidence, ensuring that historical legacies are preserved accurately and made accessible to all, rather than remaining obscured by administrative opacity or geographic fragmentation.
