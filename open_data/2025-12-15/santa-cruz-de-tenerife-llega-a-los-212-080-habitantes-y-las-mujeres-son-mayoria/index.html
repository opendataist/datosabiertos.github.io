Santa Cruz de Tenerife llega a los 212.080 habitantes y las mujeres son mayoría

Santa Cruz de Tenerife exhibits a significant demographic shift in which women constitute the majority of the population, a trend driven primarily by the higher life expectancy of older adults. This gender imbalance is consistent across most urban districts, although younger age groups show a slight male predominance. The data highlight a distinct generational divide, with the city experiencing noticeable population aging that contrasts with the relative youthfulness of specific residential zones. Municipal statistics reveal deep structural inequalities linked to education and geography. Women increasingly dominate higher education levels, particularly in central urban areas, while men are more represented in vocational training sectors. Concurrently, the city faces a challenge regarding its aging infrastructure, as the native population shrinks in its youth proportion. However, this demographic contraction is partially offset by an influx of foreign residents, who are predominantly of working age and help sustain the local labor market. This article is crucial for open data initiatives as it demonstrates how granular, sex-disaggregated demographic datasets enable precise urban planning and social policy formulation. By making such detailed statistical information publicly accessible, authorities can identify specific community needs, from elder care to educational resource allocation. It underscores the value of transparent, structured data in revealing hidden social dynamics and ensuring that public services evolve in response to actual population trends rather than general assumptions.
