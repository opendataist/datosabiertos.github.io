Gobernadora firma ley que endurece el acceso a la información pública - NotiCel - La verdad como es - Noticias de Puerto Rico - NOTICEL

Gobernadora firma ley que endurece el acceso a la información pública - NotiCel - La verdad como es - Noticias de Puerto Rico - NOTICEL

The recent enactment of Law 156-2025 in Puerto Rico significantly curtails citizens’ right to access public information, marking a pivotal shift in transparency governance. By extending agency response times and allowing officials to dictate the format in which information is delivered, the law creates substantial barriers to effective oversight. This legislative change undermines the efficiency of public data access, directly challenging the principles of open and timely government accountability. Critics argue that these modifications prioritize bureaucratic convenience over civil rights, effectively limiting investigative journalism and civic engagement. The removal of requirements to provide documents in the formats requested by citizens, along with the introduction of demands for personal data, imposes unnecessary hurdles for ordinary individuals. Consequently, the legal framework now favors institutional opacity, reducing the capacity for independent analysis and scrutiny of government actions by both the public and the media. This development is critical to open data discussions because it demonstrates how legislative adjustments can erode digital rights and transparency infrastructures. It highlights the ongoing tension between administrative efficiency and the fundamental right to know. The situation underscores the necessity of robust legal safeguards that ensure data remains accessible, timely, and usable for the public, rather than becoming an obstacle to democratic participation and informed civic discourse.
