Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

The publication of university assessment data through a Freedom of Information Request highlights the significant disparity in academic outcomes across different disciplines. This transparency allows stakeholders to examine how module selection and subject matter influence student performance, revealing that departments like physics and English boast notably higher attainment rates compared to others, such as foundation years or modern foreign languages, which lag behind in average grades and first-class distinctions. Furthermore, the data exposes extreme variations in specific modules, ranging from those where no first-class grades are awarded to others where the vast majority of students achieve top honors. These inconsistencies suggest that grading standards and module difficulty vary widely, raising questions about the fairness and consistency of assessment practices within the institution and encouraging a deeper analysis of how pedagogical approaches impact student success across different year groups. This information is vital to the open_data movement as it demonstrates the power of public sector transparency in holding educational institutions accountable. By making granular assessment data accessible, universities can foster informed decision-making for prospective students and educators, while also prompting necessary debates about equity in higher education. Such openness ensures that resource allocation and support systems are guided by empirical evidence rather than opaque practices, ultimately improving educational quality and student outcomes through data-driven insights.
