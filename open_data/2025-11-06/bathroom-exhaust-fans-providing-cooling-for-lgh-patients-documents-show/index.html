Bathroom exhaust fans providing cooling for LGH patients, documents show

Bathroom exhaust fans providing cooling for LGH patients, documents show

The article reveals that Launceston General Hospital faces critical heat stress issues, with outdated infrastructure forcing reliance on inadequate ventilation methods such as using bathroom exhaust fans to draw cool air from corridors. An engineering report confirms that patient rooms in several wards lack essential cooling provisions, with some air-handling units nearly fifty years old. This structural failure results in uncontrollable and excessively high temperatures, creating hazardous conditions for vulnerable patients and impeding staff performance. In response to public outcry and documented health risks, the government has commissioned a comprehensive upgrade plan estimated to cost millions of dollars. The strategy involves replacing obsolete systems with modern chilled pipework and fan coil units, rather than relying on temporary portable solutions. Although immediate relief measures like icy poles were provided during extreme heat, the long-term fix requires significant capital investment and careful logistical planning to relocate patients during the construction phases. This situation is highly relevant to open data because transparency was achieved through the release of confidential government documents via Right to Information laws. These disclosures exposed the severity of the infrastructure deficits and held authorities accountable, highlighting how accessible public records enable citizens and media to monitor service quality and demand better resource allocation in essential healthcare facilities.
