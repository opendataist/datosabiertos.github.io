Uganda: Tech Experts Urge Government to Keep Internet On During 2026 Elections

Uganda: Tech Experts Urge Government to Keep Internet On During 2026 Elections

Digital rights advocates are urging Uganda’s government to guarantee uninterrupted internet access during the 2026 general elections, warning that shutdowns threaten democracy and economic stability. By treating the internet as a fundamental utility rather than a luxury, these experts argue that restricting connectivity undermines citizens’ rights to education, livelihood, and verified information. Drawing parallels to severe disruptions in other African nations, they emphasize that cutting off digital channels creates vacuums easily filled by misinformation and fake news, thereby eroding public trust and democratic engagement. The advocacy extends beyond mere connectivity, focusing on the necessity of digital literacy and inclusive data education. With the rise of artificial intelligence, the ability to discern truth from falsehood is critical, requiring government investment in public awareness rather than restrictive policies. Simultaneously, initiatives like the "Data Museum" aim to demystify data concepts for communities lacking basic access. By using gamified learning and interactive exhibits, these programs strive to shift public perception of data from a simple commodity to a tool for innovation and empowerment, ensuring marginalized populations are not excluded from the digital conversation. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical intersection between data availability, accessibility, and democratic integrity. It underscores that open data initiatives must prioritize digital inclusion and literacy to be effective, ensuring that information flows freely and securely. By challenging the normalization of internet blackouts, the piece advocates for transparent, open information environments where citizens can actively participate in civic life. Ultimately, it reinforces the principle that open data is not just about providing information, but about safeguarding the open infrastructure necessary for a functioning, informed society.
