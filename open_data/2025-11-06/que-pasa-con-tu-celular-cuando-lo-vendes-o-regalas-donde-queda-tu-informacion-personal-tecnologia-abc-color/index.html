Qué pasa con tu celular cuando lo vendés o regalás: dónde queda tu información personal - Tecnología - ABC Color

The security of personal information on older mobile devices fundamentally depends on a comprehensive management approach that goes beyond simply erasing local data. The article demonstrates that a factory reset is effective due to modern native encryption, but only when combined with the explicit unlinking of cloud services, user accounts, and SIM or eSIM profiles. Ignoring these steps leaves access to banking, social media, and backups vulnerable, allowing digital identity to persist regardless of the hardware’s state. The relevance to open data lies in raising awareness about individual responsibility in managing the lifecycle of electronic devices as part of the global data infrastructure. By ensuring that users properly dissociate their accounts before recycling or selling equipment, the risk of sensitive information contaminating secondary markets or being accessed by unauthorized third parties is reduced. This reinforces trust in digital ecosystems and underscores the importance of secure disposal standards that protect the privacy of ordinary citizens. Consequently, effective protection against data recovery hinges on severing the digital threads between the physical device and remote services. The main conclusion is that privacy is not guaranteed solely through technical operations on the phone, but through a rigorous protocol that includes controlled backups, logging out of active sessions, and remote verification of account unlinking. Only this holistic approach ensures that personal information remains private after the device is transferred.
