Tinubu wants to stall release of FBI files, Atiku’s aide alleges |

Atiku Abubakar’s special assistant has condemned President Bola Tinubu’s legal maneuvers in the United States to suppress records related to his past criminal investigation. By filing motions to block the release of files held by the FBI and CIA through a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit, the President is allegedly attempting to conceal details regarding his identity and historical involvement in illicit activities. This action is described as an unbecoming effort by a head of state to hide a "sordid past" that raises serious questions about his integrity and qualifications to lead a nation. The critique emphasizes that these attempts to obscure the truth contradict previous political narratives, specifically the claim that earlier probes were merely tax-related matters. The speaker argues that if the allegations were baseless, there would be no reason to resist transparency. By maintaining silence on his educational background and familial origins, and by actively working to prevent judicial disclosure, Tinubu is portrayed as engaging in a systematic deception that denies citizens the fundamental right to know the character of their leader. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical intersection of legal transparency mechanisms, such as FOIA, with public accountability and democratic governance. The article underscores how data suppression can be used to conceal potential misconduct, reinforcing the argument that accessible, unredacted public records are essential for verifying official claims. Ultimately, it serves as a call for authorities to uphold the principle that open data is not just a technical process, but a vital safeguard against imposture and corruption in public office.
