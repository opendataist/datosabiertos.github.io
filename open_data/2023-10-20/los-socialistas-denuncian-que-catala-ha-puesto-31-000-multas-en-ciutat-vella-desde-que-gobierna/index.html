Los socialistas denuncian que Catalá ha puesto 31.000 multas en Ciutat Vella desde que gobierna

The article highlights significant discrepancies between the local government’s promises and operational realities in Valencia, particularly regarding mobility policies and public safety. A central issue is the claim that parking regulation changes would be cost-neutral, which has been contradicted by internal reports indicating substantial financial losses for the municipality. This divergence suggests a lack of accurate data management and transparency in how policy impacts are communicated to the public, undermining trust in official statistics. Furthermore, the text critiques the administration for maintaining restrictive parking schemes while promising their elimination, illustrating a pattern where announced decisions do not align with enacted measures. These inconsistencies extend to emergency services, where the opposition points to inadequate planning and restricted access to critical information sources like police and fire units. The refusal to share operational details prevents independent verification of safety protocols and resource allocation, creating an opaque environment that hinders effective oversight. This case is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for accessible, accurate, and verifiable government information. When authorities withhold data or present misleading figures, citizens cannot hold them accountable or make informed decisions. True transparency requires not just the publication of data, but its reliability and availability for public scrutiny, ensuring that policy claims can be validated against factual records.
