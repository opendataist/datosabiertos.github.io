The article highlights how scammers exploit personal data to carry out fraud via WhatsApp, emphasizing that the vulnerability often stems from previous online interactions. Data obtained through surveys, contests, or warranty cards is frequently sold to third parties, enabling criminals to create targeted and convincing traps. This mechanism illustrates the tangible risks associated with the commodification of personal information—a core concern in the open data ecosystem, where transparency must be balanced with privacy safeguards. The narrative describes how initially harmless messages evolve into elaborate schemes, such as fake job offers or prize notifications, designed to lure victims into sharing sensitive financial credentials. By leveraging trust and the allure of easy earnings, these operations compromise user security, resulting in significant financial losses. The story underscores the critical need for digital literacy and the importance of recognizing how seemingly innocuous data-sharing practices can be weaponized against individuals. This content is relevant to open data discussions because it reveals the dual nature of data accessibility: while open data can drive innovation, unrestricted data flows can also facilitate criminal activities. It serves as a stark reminder that without robust ethical frameworks and protective measures, the openness of data can inadvertently expose citizens to harm. Consequently, stakeholders must prioritize data governance and user consent mechanisms to ensure that information sharing does not become a vector for exploitation.
