Cabinet Office and Freedom of Information

The UK Cabinet Office acts as the central authority for Freedom of Information policy, balancing the public’s right to access information with the need to protect sensitive national interests. By managing complex requests and ensuring consistent application across departments, the Office mitigates risks associated with collective responsibility and national security, while guiding public bodies on legal compliance and data protection standards. A significant structural shift occurred when the FOI Clearing House was redesigned from an operational coordinator to a purely advisory role. This change, driven by a strategic review, aims to enhance the Office’s ability to set best practice standards and promote effective handling of requests. Rather than making final disclosure decisions, the Office now focuses on providing expert guidance to ensure uniformity and quality in how government transparency is implemented. This article is crucial for open data advocates because it outlines the institutional framework governing public sector transparency. It highlights the tension between openness and security, emphasizing that while the government strives for consistent standards, the ultimate responsibility for disclosure lies with individual departments. Understanding this hierarchy is essential for anyone navigating government data, as it reveals where policy guidance originates and how requests are filtered through national security and privacy lenses.
