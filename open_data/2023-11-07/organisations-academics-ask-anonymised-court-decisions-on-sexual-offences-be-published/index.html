Organisations, academics ask anonymised court decisions on sexual offences be published

Over sixty organizations urge Romania’s judiciary to publish anonymized rulings on sexual offenses to ensure transparency and accountability. They argue that hiding these decisions undermines public trust and allows biased judicial practices to persist, particularly those rooted in sexist or racist stereotypes that incorrectly question victim consent. Releasing this data enables society to monitor sentencing trends and the effectiveness of protective measures for victims. Without access to outcomes, it is impossible to verify if justice is truly served or if offenders receive appropriate penalties, leaving vulnerable populations without recourse or reassurance. This issue is critical for open data initiatives, as it highlights how selective withholding of public records obstructs democratic oversight. Ensuring access to anonymized judicial data empowers civil society to analyze systemic patterns, challenge institutional bias, and advocate for legislative reforms that prioritize victim rights and societal safety.
