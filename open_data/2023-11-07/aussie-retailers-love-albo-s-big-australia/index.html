Aussie retailers love Albo's Big Australia

Retail sales volume adjustments reveal a significant divergence between aggregate demand and individual consumer welfare. When accounting for rapid population growth, underlying retail activity actually declined, exposing a "burnout economy" where record net migration masks deeper individual consumption struggles driven by high interest rates. This dynamic highlights how demographic shifts artificially sustain macroeconomic aggregates, potentially forcing the central bank to maintain restrictive monetary policies despite household financial stress. The resulting inflationary pressure suggests that immigration levels are currently working against economic stabilization efforts. This data is crucial for open data analysis as it demonstrates the necessity of granular, adjusted metrics to interpret official statistics correctly. It underscores the importance of understanding how population variables influence economic indicators, enabling more accurate evaluations of policy impacts and true economic health beyond raw headline figures.
