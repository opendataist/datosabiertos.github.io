AFP handled seven sexual assault claims against federal parliamentarians following Brittany Higgins’ allegations, FoI shows

Following the high-profile allegations by Brittany Higgins, federal law enforcement bodies received seven additional briefs concerning sexual assault against parliamentarians within a sixteen-month period. Two cases proceeded with federal police investigation, while five were referred to state or territory authorities. This influx of claims highlights the urgent need for robust mechanisms to address misconduct, directly impacting open data by demonstrating how transparency initiatives are essential for uncovering the full scope of institutional failures that remain hidden from public scrutiny. The secrecy surrounding these investigations, with key details redacted from freedom of information responses, underscores significant barriers to accountability. The suppression of information prevents the public and researchers from understanding the true scale of harassment within legislative buildings. In the context of open data, this highlights the critical importance of mandating the release of non-sensitive operational metrics. Without accessible data on the frequency and handling of such complaints, it is impossible to assess whether reforms are effectively reducing harm or merely managing reputational risk. In response to growing concerns about parliamentary culture, new oversight bodies are being established, yet their implementation has faced delays. Critics argue that without genuine consequences for misconduct, victims will remain reluctant to report abuse, perpetuating a cycle of impunity. Open data serves as a vital tool here by enabling independent monitoring of these new commissions’ effectiveness. Publishing performance metrics and case outcomes would allow stakeholders to verify whether these bodies can enforce standards, ensuring that legislative environments become safer and more accountable for all workers.
