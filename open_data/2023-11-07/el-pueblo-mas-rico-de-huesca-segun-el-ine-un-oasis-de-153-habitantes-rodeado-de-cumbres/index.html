El pueblo más rico de Huesca, según el INE: un oasis de 153 habitantes rodeado de cumbres

El pueblo más rico de Huesca, según el INE: un oasis de 153 habitantes rodeado de cumbres

The article highlights a significant geographical and statistical divide within Aragon, where high-income rural areas contrast sharply with both the region's industrial hubs and poorer municipalities. By analyzing household income data from 2021, the INE reveals that several small towns in the province of Huesca possess higher average per capita rents than major urban centers. This finding challenges conventional assumptions that economic prosperity is exclusively linked to large metropolitan areas or industrial zones, illustrating a complex distribution of wealth that favors specific rural communities. The disparity is partly explained by methodological differences in how income is measured. While tax authority data relies on declared income from larger population centers, the INE’s atlas utilizes a more comprehensive model that estimates average income per person based on age and household size. This approach incorporates diverse revenue streams, including capital gains, pensions, and benefits, providing a broader view of economic well-being. Consequently, it captures the financial reality of smaller communities more accurately than traditional tax-centric metrics, which often overlook or underreport income in less densely populated areas. This study is particularly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, methodologically diverse datasets can reshape our understanding of regional inequality. It underscores the importance of accessible statistical models that go beyond simple tax records to reveal nuanced economic patterns. For researchers and policymakers, such open datasets offer critical insights into where wealth is actually concentrated, facilitating more targeted interventions and a deeper comprehension of the socio-economic fabric of rural versus urban Spain.
