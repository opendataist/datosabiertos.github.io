La canasta básica subió 8% en marzo: una familia tipo necesita más de $191.228 para no ser pobre - EL DEBATE

The article highlights a sharp rise in the cost of basic food and goods, revealing that over eighteen million people fall below the poverty line while millions face absolute destitution. This economic deterioration indicates that inflation is disproportionately affecting essential items like food, forcing families to spend a significantly larger share of their income just to survive and pushing vulnerable populations deeper into hardship. This situation underscores the critical role of open data in monitoring social welfare and economic health. Transparent, accessible statistics allow citizens, researchers, and policymakers to independently verify official figures and understand the real impact of inflation on daily life. Without this open data, the severity of social exclusion and the disparity between income and costs remain obscured, hindering informed public discourse. Ultimately, these figures demonstrate that open data is not merely technical information but a vital tool for accountability. By making such socioeconomic indicators publicly available and understandable, society can better assess government responses to rising living costs. This transparency empowers communities to demand effective policies that address the root causes of poverty and ensure that statistical reporting accurately reflects the struggles of the most affected citizens.
