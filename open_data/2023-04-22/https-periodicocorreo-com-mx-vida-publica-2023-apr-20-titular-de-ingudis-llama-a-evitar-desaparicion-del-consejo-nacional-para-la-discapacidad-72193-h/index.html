José Grimaldo Colmenero urged the preservation of Conadis, warning that its dissolution threatens the meaningful inclusion of people with disabilities in public policy. He emphasized that genuine advancement of human rights requires the active participation of this community, in alignment with international conventions and national recommendations. This position underscores the critical need for transparent, participatory governance structures that respect the voices of marginalized groups. Technology serves as a vital tool for independence and social participation, as demonstrated by breakthroughs that enable communication for individuals with severe disabilities. By leveraging scientific advancements, societies can remove barriers to autonomy, fostering a more inclusive environment in which assistive technologies empower users to express themselves and fully engage in societal roles. This article is relevant to open data because effective inclusion depends on transparent and accessible information systems. Open data principles ensure that the benefits of technology and policy decisions are visible and accountable. Moreover, accessible digital platforms enable the direct participation of people with disabilities in civic discourse, ensuring that their perspectives shape the policies that affect their lives, thereby democratizing access to both information and opportunity.
