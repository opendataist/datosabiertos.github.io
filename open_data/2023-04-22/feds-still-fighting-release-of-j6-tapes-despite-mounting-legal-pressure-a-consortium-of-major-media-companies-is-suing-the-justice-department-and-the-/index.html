Feds Still Fighting Release of J6 Tapes Despite Mounting Legal Pressure A consortium of major media companies is suing the Justice Department and the FBI for ignoring Freedom of Information Act requests to obtain the still-secret recordings of January 6. By Julie Kelly

The article highlights a significant legal and political clash regarding the transparency of evidence collected during the January 6 Capitol breach. U.S. Attorney Matthew Graves and the FBI are resisting requests from a media coalition and defendants to release the full archive of closed-circuit television footage, classifying it as "highly sensitive" to prevent potential security risks. This stance conflicts with the government’s previous actions of using clips as evidence in court and allowing limited public dissemination through other channels, prompting accusations of hypocrisy and selective prosecution. The core tension lies in the struggle for public access to government records versus the state’s desire to maintain control over the narrative surrounding the events. Advocates argue that the broad classification of the footage is unjustified given the enhanced security measures already in place and the precedent set by prior disclosures. The legal challenges suggest that the government’s refusal to declassify the material undermines the principles of open justice and suggests an attempt to withhold information that could provide a more comprehensive understanding of the day's events. This situation is highly relevant to open data discussions as it illustrates the critical importance of preserving and releasing digital evidence in the public interest. The conflict demonstrates how institutional secrecy can obstruct accountability and limit the ability of the media and citizens to independently verify historical records. It underscores the necessity of robust freedom of information mechanisms to ensure that significant governmental actions and historical moments are documented transparently, rather than being curated or suppressed by those in power.
