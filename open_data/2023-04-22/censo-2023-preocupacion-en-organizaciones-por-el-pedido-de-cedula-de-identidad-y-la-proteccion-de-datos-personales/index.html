Censo 2023: preocupación en organizaciones por el pedido de cédula de identidad y la “protección de datos personales”

Censo 2023: preocupación en organizaciones por el pedido de cédula de identidad y la “protección de datos personales”

The 2023 census introduces a dual digital and in-person modality, prompting the National Institute of Statistics to request citizens' ID cards to prevent duplicate registrations. This requirement has sparked significant concern among privacy advocates and data society laboratories, who argue that requesting unique identifiers undermines the fundamental expectation of anonymity inherent in statistical exercises. The core debate centers on whether such personal data is strictly necessary for the census’s primary objective or if it unnecessarily exposes individuals to potential security risks and misuse. Authorities defend the measure by emphasizing that providing the ID is optional and that data is rapidly transformed through encryption upon entry to separate identity from responses. Officials assert that rigorous security protocols, overseen by regulatory bodies and supported by telecommunications and technology agencies, ensure compliance with statistical secrecy laws. By isolating identifying information and relying on established legal frameworks for data protection, the institute aims to demonstrate that the technical and organizational measures are sufficient to safeguard personal privacy while maintaining data integrity. This case is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical tension between administrative efficiency and the ethical handling of personal information. It underscores the necessity of implementing privacy-by-design principles and conducting impact assessments before deploying data collection systems. Furthermore, it illustrates how transparency and clear communication regarding data usage are essential for maintaining public trust, which is a cornerstone of any successful open data initiative. Understanding these dynamics helps ensure that future data projects balance utility with individual rights.
