Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway experience significantly poorer living conditions compared to the general population, facing disadvantages in employment, housing, and health, including higher rates of mental distress and loneliness. Despite these material and psychological challenges, they report equal satisfaction with life in Norway and demonstrate strong social cohesion and confidence in national institutions. This paradox highlights a complex reality where structural inequality does not necessarily diminish subjective well-being or civic trust. Immigrants exhibit higher confidence in the political system, judiciary, and police than native Norwegians, suggesting that institutional trust can remain robust even when socioeconomic integration lags behind. This finding is highly relevant to open data initiatives, which often focus on quantifying disparities to drive policy changes. By revealing the disconnect between objective hardships and subjective satisfaction, such data challenges simplistic narratives of immigrant integration. It encourages policymakers to look beyond economic metrics, recognizing that social and institutional factors play crucial roles in community resilience and overall quality of life.
