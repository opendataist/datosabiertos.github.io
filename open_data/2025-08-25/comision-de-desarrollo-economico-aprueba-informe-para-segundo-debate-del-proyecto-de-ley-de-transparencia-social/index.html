Comisión de Desarrollo Económico aprueba informe para segundo debate del proyecto de Ley de Transparencia Social

Comisión de Desarrollo Económico aprueba informe para segundo debate del proyecto de Ley de Transparencia Social

The National Assembly has advanced legislation aimed at enforcing transparency within non-profit organizations, prioritizing the detection and disruption of illicit capital flows linked to criminal economies. This regulatory shift addresses critical vulnerabilities where illegal financial activities may infiltrate social sectors, thereby strengthening state oversight mechanisms to ensure accountability and integrity in the management of resources. The proposed framework introduces a risk-based classification system to tailor supervisory intensity, ensuring that controls are proportional to the volume and origin of funds managed by these entities. By aligning oversight with specific risk indicators, the government seeks to protect legitimate civil society operations while preventing the misuse of organizations as vehicles for financial crimes or political persecution. This development is highly relevant to open data as it establishes new transparency standards that will likely require public disclosure of organizational financial flows and risk assessments. Enhancing the availability of such structured data allows for greater societal scrutiny and independent analysis, empowering citizens and researchers to monitor the health of the nonprofit sector and identify potential anomalies in resource distribution.
