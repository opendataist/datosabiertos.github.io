Kenya wins Bid to host the International Open Data Conference

Kenya wins Bid to host the International Open Data Conference

The International Open Data Conference is set to host its first African edition in Nairobi in 2020, marking a significant geopolitical shift for the open data movement. This decision reflects a broader commitment by African leadership to integrate data ecosystems into national development strategies, moving beyond political will to treat data as a rational economic asset. By bringing this premier global gathering to the continent, the event underscores a growing recognition that sustainable development in Africa relies heavily on inclusive, cross-sector collaboration involving government, academia, and the private sector. The conference theme, "Bridging Data Communities," aims to connect diverse stakeholders to address critical challenges in health, agriculture, and fiscal transparency. This focus is particularly relevant to open data as it highlights the necessity of interoperability and accessibility in supporting the Sustainable Development Goals. The event serves as a catalyst for strengthening South-South cooperation, allowing nations to share lessons learned and leverage technology to solve complex social issues. By fostering these connections, the conference facilitates the exchange of best practices that can enhance policy-making and drive innovation across developing economies. For the open data community, this expansion signifies the maturation of the field from a niche technical interest to a central component of global development policy. It demonstrates how open data initiatives can empower regions to take leadership roles in shaping the future of information governance. The anticipated participation of thousands of delegates and major technology companies indicates a robust ecosystem ready to invest in Africa’s innovation spaces, reinforcing the idea that transparent data practices are essential for economic growth and leaving no one behind.
