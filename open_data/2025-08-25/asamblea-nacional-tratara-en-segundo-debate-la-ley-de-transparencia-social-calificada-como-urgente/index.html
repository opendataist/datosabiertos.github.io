Asamblea Nacional tratará en segundo debate la Ley de Transparencia Social, calificada como urgente

Asamblea Nacional tratará en segundo debate la Ley de Transparencia Social, calificada como urgente

The National Assembly is set to debate the Organic Law on Social Transparency, a measure aimed at preventing money laundering and ensuring financial integrity among non-profit organizations. By mandating greater accountability and transparency in financial flows, this legislation seeks to strengthen institutional cooperation against illicit activities. The law represents a significant step toward regulating the economic behavior of civil society entities, ensuring they adhere to strict fiscal and ethical standards. Parallel to this primary legislative push, various parliamentary commissions are addressing related governance issues. These include reforms to the judicial code to enhance ethical safeguards and proposals for transparency within trade unions. Additionally, committees are examining territorial organization and conducting political oversight through impeachment proceedings. These efforts collectively underscore a broader governmental commitment to reinforcing ethical structures across different public and private sectors. This development is highly relevant to open data because it institutionalizes the disclosure of financial and organizational information. By legally compelling greater transparency from social organizations and union leaders, the law expands the scope of accessible public information. This regulatory shift facilitates the creation of more comprehensive datasets, allowing for better analysis of funding sources and operational integrity, which are essential for robust civic oversight and data-driven governance.
