Justicia distributiva

Justicia distributiva

The article highlights a historic reduction in poverty in Mexico between 2018 and 2024, attributing this progress largely to increases in the minimum wage and targeted welfare programs. These policies successfully lifted millions out of economic hardship by boosting real incomes and encouraging labor formalization through a "beacon effect." Consequently, the nation demonstrated that strategic interventions can significantly lower poverty rates even amid challenging global conditions, affirming that economic development and poverty reduction are not strictly bound by rigid conditionalities. This achievement is highly relevant to open data because it showcases the critical importance of accessible, multidimensional statistics for evidence-based policymaking. By utilizing transparent metrics from official institutions, citizens and experts can verify government claims, ensuring accountability and fostering public trust. Open data facilitates this scrutiny, allowing society to understand how specific social indicators change over time and to hold leaders accountable for their commitments to equitable distribution and social justice. Furthermore, the reduction in poverty strengthens the domestic market and attracts investment, proving that social equity drives broader economic stability. The article argues that transparent, data-backed policies create certainty for both domestic and foreign investors. Thus, the availability of clear, open statistical evidence is essential not just for monitoring social progress, but for legitimizing economic strategies that promise sustainable development and improved quality of life for all citizens.
