Por qué es mejor pensar dos veces antes de publicar en Internet las fotos de los hijos

The phenomenon of “sharenting” poses a significant risk to the privacy and security of minors, as the constant exposure of their personal data on social media can facilitate identity theft, harassment, and undue surveillance. Beyond economic and physical threats, there is the danger that third parties, including technology companies, may use this information without consent to train algorithms or shape perceptions that could affect the child’s future opportunities in educational and professional spheres, thereby limiting their autonomy to construct their own narrative. This situation is crucial for the field of open data because it illustrates the ethical and legal limits of using personal information, especially when it involves vulnerable populations unable to provide full consent. The discussion underscores the need to balance transparency and data availability with the protection of individual privacy, challenging the assumption that all data published on public platforms is free from restrictions or security implications. The proposed solution involves a cultural shift toward digital consent, where parents prioritize the protection of sensitive data and educate their children about ownership of their digital identity. This demonstrates that responsible information management is not merely an individual practice but a fundamental principle for building data ecosystems that are safer and more respectful of human rights, thereby preventing the non-consensual commercial exploitation of private life.
