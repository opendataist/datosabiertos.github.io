NIH adviser David Morens can't recall if he deleted COVID records, laughs off Fauci FOIA evasions

The article details allegations that senior NIH adviser Dr. David Morens systematically circumvented transparency protocols regarding COVID-19 origins research. By utilizing private email accounts and coordinating with the NIH’s Freedom of Information Act office, Morens allegedly facilitated the deletion of critical records and the concealment of communications involving Dr. Anthony Fauci and EcoHealth Alliance. This conduct suggests an intentional effort to obstruct public accountability and hide the extent of federal funding for specific virology projects. These revelations highlight serious concerns about government opacity and the potential misuse of institutional power to shield controversial scientific activities from public scrutiny. The alleged coordination to suppress documents indicates a breakdown in standard archival practices, raising questions about the integrity of official records during the pandemic. Such actions undermine public trust in scientific leadership and suggest that key decision-makers may have prioritized protecting their organizations over maintaining transparent government operations. This incident is highly relevant to the open data movement as it exemplifies the critical need for robust, immutable digital record-keeping in government. It underscores the vulnerabilities in current systems that allow officials to bypass archiving requirements and manipulate information flows. For open data advocates, this case serves as a urgent argument for mandatory use of official, auditable communication channels and stricter enforcement of record preservation laws to prevent the loss of historical and scientific data.
