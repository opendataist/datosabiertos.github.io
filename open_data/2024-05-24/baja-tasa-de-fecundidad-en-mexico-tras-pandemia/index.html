Baja tasa de fecundidad en México tras pandemia

Mexico’s fertility rate has fallen below the replacement level, signaling a long-term population decline despite recent data from the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI). This shift, accelerated by the post-pandemic period, reflects profound demographic changes across both urban and rural areas, with significant variations among indigenous communities and declining adolescent birth rates. These trends highlight disparities in access to reproductive health services and the adoption of family planning, as evidenced by increased contraceptive use among teenagers. The widening gap between urban and rural fertility rates suggests divergent social dynamics and unequal distribution of resources, necessitating nuanced policy responses to address emerging inequalities in family structures. This article is crucial for open data initiatives because it underscores the need for transparent, accessible demographic statistics to inform public policy. Reliable, open government data enables researchers and citizens to monitor these critical societal shifts, ensuring that decisions regarding healthcare, education, and social security are based on accurate, real-time evidence of changing population needs.
