Former regulator reacts to memo found during FOX Carolina News investigation

Former regulator reacts to memo found during FOX Carolina News investigation

A 2021 internal memo from Duke Energy warned board directors that extreme cold could necessitate rolling blackouts, a prediction realized during the Christmas Eve 2022 storm. However, this critical document was classified as confidential and never presented to the Public Service Commission or included in the official regulatory report investigating the outages. This lack of transparency suggests that utility companies may withhold vital risk assessments from public oversight bodies, creating significant safety and accountability gaps. The situation highlights a stark failure in regulatory diligence, as commissioners claimed ignorance of the warning despite its existence. When such foundational data is excluded from official investigations, the resulting reports fail to capture the full scope of corporate preparedness and failure. This opacity prevents effective policy-making and leaves the public unaware of known vulnerabilities in critical infrastructure, undermining the trust necessary for effective utility governance. This case is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how corporate secrecy can obscure public safety risks. When essential documents are shielded behind "confidential" labels, citizen oversight is rendered ineffective. True open data practices require that all information pertinent to public welfare and utility reliability be accessible, ensuring that regulators and citizens alike can make informed decisions rather than reacting to preventable crises.
