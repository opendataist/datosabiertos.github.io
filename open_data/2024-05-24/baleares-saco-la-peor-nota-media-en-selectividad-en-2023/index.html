Baleares 'sacó' la peor nota media en Selectividad en 2023

The 2023 university access exam statistics reveal significant shifts in higher education participation, marking a stark decline for older applicants while reaching record highs for traditional candidates. Notably, enrollments for the over-25s pathway have plummeted by nearly half over the last seven years, suggesting changing demographic trends or alternative educational routes for mature students. Conversely, the general undergraduate pathway saw unprecedented engagement, with nearly ninety-five percent of registered candidates sitting for the exam and achieving the highest approval rates in nearly a decade. Performance disparities highlight systemic inequities between different educational sectors and regions. Students from private institutions consistently outperform their public school counterparts in both average scores and top-grade achievements, raising concerns about resource allocation and social mobility. Furthermore, significant geographical variations exist across autonomous communities, with some regions demonstrating markedly superior outcomes in both average grades and the proportion of students achieving top distinctions compared to others. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of accessible, transparent, and granular statistical reporting. The detailed breakdown of metrics by region, institution type, and applicant category allows researchers and policymakers to identify trends and inequalities effectively. By publishing such specific, localized data publicly, educational authorities enable evidence-based decision-making, fostering accountability and facilitating a deeper societal understanding of the factors influencing academic success and access.
