Misión espacial Euclid captura cinco nuevas imágenes sin precedentes del universo [FOTOS] | RPP Noticias

The European Space Agency’s Euclid mission has released its first set of scientific images, marking a pivotal moment in the creation of the largest three-dimensional map of the cosmos. Rather than serving merely as aesthetic achievements, these observations provide unprecedented views of galactic clusters and star-forming regions. The images demonstrate the telescope’s high sensitivity and wide field of view, proving its capability to capture detailed data on galaxies billions of light-years away, which will help unravel the nature of dark matter and dark energy over the mission's six-year lifespan. The core significance of this release lies in the application of gravitational lensing to map invisible mass. Since dark matter does not emit light, Euclid detects its presence by observing how it distorts the light from distant background galaxies. This technique allows scientists to trace the distribution of dark matter across vast cosmic structures, offering critical insights into how the universe clusters and evolves. By combining visible and infrared data, the mission is beginning to build the foundational catalog necessary to understand the fundamental forces shaping the universe’s expansion. This article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the power of publicly available scientific data to transform our understanding of the universe. Euclid’s approach ensures that these "treasures" of information are accessible for global research, enabling scientists worldwide to analyze gravitational lensing effects and star formation processes without proprietary barriers. The release underscores how open, high-quality datasets empower the scientific community to verify theories, discover unexpected phenomena, and collaborate on solving some of the most profound questions in cosmology.
