MRA Condemns Harassment Of Citizens By DSS For Exercising Right To Information | African Examiner

A civil society organization has condemned the harassment of a citizen activist by security agents for utilizing the Freedom of Information Act to demand transparency regarding a major land deal. The incident highlights a critical tension between state power and individual rights, demonstrating how legitimate legal inquiries can be met with intimidation rather than cooperation. This action undermines the core purpose of information access laws, which are designed to foster open governance and hold authorities accountable for public actions. The activist’s request sought clarification on the financial details of a significant government transaction, specifically concerning the allocation of land for a major industrial project. By pursuing this inquiry through official channels and subsequently filing a lawsuit due to non-response, the citizen acted within the legal framework established to ensure government transparency. The subsequent summons by security services for exercising this statutory right represents a direct violation of democratic principles and the rule of law. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the barriers to accessing public information even when legal mechanisms exist. It underscores the necessity of protecting citizens who seek to make government data visible and accountable. Without safeguards against retaliation, the potential for open data to improve governance is severely limited, as fear of persecution suppresses the public’s ability to scrutinize state activities and demand better record-keeping and disclosure practices.
