La inflación en EEUU sube menos de lo esperado y mantiene a la Fed en camino para recortar tipos

The US Consumer Price Index rose to 3.0% year-over-year in September, with core inflation also settling at 3.0%, both figures exceeding market expectations. Despite a slight monthly acceleration in gasoline prices driving the overall increase, the broader economic signal points toward a moderation in inflationary pressures. This outcome suggests that the Federal Reserve is likely to continue its monetary easing cycle, as the data supports the anticipation of additional interest rate cuts to stimulate economic stability. The release of these figures highlights the intricate relationship between domestic consumption patterns, energy costs, and external trade policies. While energy costs remained a primary driver of monthly volatility, the stabilization of core goods inflation indicates that recent tariff increases have begun to permeate specific consumer sectors, such as clothing, without causing a systemic spike in overall price levels. This nuanced performance provides central banks with clearer signals for future policy adjustments, balancing growth against price stability.
