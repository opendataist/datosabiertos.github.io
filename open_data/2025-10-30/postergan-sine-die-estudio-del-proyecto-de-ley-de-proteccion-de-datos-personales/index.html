Postergan sine die estudio del proyecto de ley de protección de datos personales

Postergan sine die estudio del proyecto de ley de protección de datos personales

The personal data bill in Paraguay has been indefinitely postponed due to significant disagreements, primarily centered on Article 24. Critics argue that this controversial provision creates unnecessary obstacles to accessing public information by requiring individual official consent, potentially undermining existing transparency laws and hindering the state’s obligation to provide timely data. Despite the delay, proponents emphasize that the legislation remains crucial for economic development and business opportunities. The decision was made to allow time for necessary corrections and studies, with a commitment to revisit the project in November, acknowledging that inaction also carries significant costs for national progress. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocates, as it highlights the delicate balance between protecting personal privacy and maintaining public transparency. The risk of legislative provisions that restrict data access serves as a cautionary example of how poorly designed regulations can stall digital governance and limit the societal benefits of an open data ecosystem.
