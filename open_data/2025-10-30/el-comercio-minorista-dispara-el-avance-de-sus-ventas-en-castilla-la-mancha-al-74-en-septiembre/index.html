Spain’s retail sector maintains a solid positive trend, marked by sustained growth in sales and employment throughout the year. Year-on-year figures reveal a broad-based increase in revenue, driven particularly by large chains and e-commerce, while employment continues to expand, albeit with a slowdown in the growth rate compared to previous months. This stability suggests resilience in domestic consumption and the market’s adaptation to new distribution dynamics. The relevance of this article for open data lies in the complexity and structure of the public data provided by statistical institutions. The information is disaggregated across multiple variables, such as distribution channel, product type, geographic region, and deseasonalized time series. This rich dimensionality enables analysts and citizens to cross-reference data sources to gain more nuanced perspectives on the real economy, facilitating transparency and scrutiny of economic health from multiple angles. However, the usefulness of these data also depends on their accessibility and clarity. For citizens and the technical community to fully leverage this information, it is crucial that the formats be interoperable and that the calculation methodologies, particularly regarding seasonal adjustments, be clearly documented. Greater openness of these data not only improves public understanding of economic trends but also fosters innovation in the development of services based on financial and commercial transparency.
