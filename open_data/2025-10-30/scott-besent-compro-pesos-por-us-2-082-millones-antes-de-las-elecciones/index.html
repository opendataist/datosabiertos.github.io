Scott Besent compró pesos por US$ 2.082 millones antes de las elecciones

The United States Treasury’s direct intervention in Argentina’s currency market served as a crucial geopolitical signal of support for Javier Milei’s administration. By purchasing pesos to stabilize the exchange rate before legislative elections, Washington helped prevent a chaotic depreciation. This action not only bolstered the elected government but also demonstrated a strategic alignment, reinforcing bilateral ties during a period of intense market volatility and capital flight. Financially, the maneuver proved highly advantageous for the U.S. Treasury. The funds were utilized to buy Argentine pesos at peak exchange rates and subsequently placed in domestic liquidity instruments. This structure allowed the U.S. entity to potentially generate profit through interest accrual and favorable exchange rate movements, turning a crisis-management operation into a sound financial investment. The approach highlights how foreign direct participation can simultaneously address macroeconomic stability while securing fiscal benefits for the intervening state. This case is significant for open data initiatives as it underscores the necessity of transparent financial reporting in cross-border economic interventions. The initial ambiguity regarding the transaction structure emphasizes the critical need for accessible, detailed public records to verify the terms, such as interest rates and maturity dates, of such operations. Enhanced data openness ensures accountability and allows for independent analysis of how international capital flows impact local monetary policies, fostering greater trust in economic governance.
