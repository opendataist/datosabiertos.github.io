El padrón municipal pierde de golpe 500 habitantes debido a las bajas de inmigrantes

A Coruña experiences significant fluctuations in its registered immigrant population due to administrative expirations and mobility. Many non-EU residents without long-term authorization do not renew their registration after two years, often moving to other municipalities or updating their identification status. This creates an apparent sharp decline in figures that reflects bureaucratic transitions rather than an actual exodus. The relevance to open data lies in the critical distinction between statistical records and demographic reality. Official datasets, such as municipal censuses, can misrepresent the true population size when they fail to account for these administrative cycles and high mobility. Understanding these nuances is essential for interpreting public statistics accurately and avoiding misleading conclusions about community composition. Despite temporary statistical drops, the foreign population continues to grow steadily, offsetting low natural population growth in the region. This trend highlights the importance of dynamic data analysis that considers migration patterns and residency status changes. Policymakers must recognize that nominal declines in registries do not equate to a shrinking diverse community, emphasizing the need for robust, context-aware data collection methods.
