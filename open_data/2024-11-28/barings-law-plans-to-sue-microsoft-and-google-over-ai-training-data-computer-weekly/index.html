Barings Law plans to sue Microsoft and Google over AI training data | Computer Weekly

Barings Law plans to sue Microsoft and Google over AI training data | Computer Weekly

Legal action is emerging against major tech firms like Microsoft and Google, alleging they unlawfully harvest personal data to train artificial intelligence models without user consent. This lawsuit highlights a critical gap in current privacy frameworks, where individuals remain unaware that their behavioral, demographic, and voice data fuels proprietary AI systems. The core issue is the lack of transparency and choice, suggesting that the convenience of AI services currently comes at the direct expense of fundamental user rights and privacy protections. The relevance to open data lies in the growing tension between the scalable data harvesting necessary for AI development and the ethical imperative of data sovereignty. As data becomes the primary economic commodity, these legal challenges force a re-evaluation of how information is collected, stored, and utilized. The cases underscore the urgent need for clearer regulatory standards that distinguish between public accessibility and legitimate usage, ensuring that the drive for technological advancement does not override individual ownership and control over personal information. Furthermore, these disputes illuminate the limitations of existing defenses like "fair use" when applied to large-scale data scraping for commercial AI training. By challenging the legality of using personal data without explicit permission, the lawsuits advocate for a more accountable data ecosystem. This shift is essential for open data initiatives, as it pushes for a model where data usage is transparent, consensual, and respect for individual rights is prioritized alongside innovation and technological progress.
