The article highlights the vast gap between official statistics and the reality of violence against women in Mexico and globally, pointing out that recorded figures represent only the tip of the iceberg. Most cases go unreported due to fear, cultural normalization, or lack of institutional trust, meaning that current public data do not reflect the true scale of this systematic violation of human rights. It is crucial to emphasize that significant gaps exist in data collection, particularly for marginalized groups such as indigenous women, migrants, and women with disabilities. The absence of disaggregated data by ethnicity, migration status, or health condition obscures structural inequalities and leaves these populations facing disproportionate risks. Without accurate records, it is impossible to design effective public policies that address the root causes and specificities of each context. This situation is critical for the open data community because it underscores the urgency of creating more inclusive, standardized, and accessible public datasets. To achieve genuine prevention and zero tolerance for gender-based violence, it is imperative that institutions publish granular and transparent information. Only through higher data quality can patterns be identified, resources be appropriately allocated, and the real impact of social interventions be evaluated, thereby transforming available evidence into concrete action.
