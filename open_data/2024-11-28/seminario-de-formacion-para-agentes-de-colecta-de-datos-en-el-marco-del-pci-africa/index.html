Seminario de formación para agentes de colecta de datos en el marco del PCI- África

Seminario de formación para agentes de colecta de datos en el marco del PCI- África

The article highlights Equatorial Guinea’s transition to self-financing the International Comparison Program after external support ceased. By assuming responsibility for data collection, the government ensured that the country remained part of global benchmarking efforts, allowing for the calculation of purchasing power parity and enabling accurate cross-national comparisons of living standards. This commitment underscores the vital role of national statistical institutes in maintaining rigorous data production even when external aid diminishes. The decision to continue routine data gathering reflects a strategic priority to sustain high-quality economic statistics, ensuring that the nation’s development metrics remain reliable, transparent, and internationally comparable despite financial constraints. This narrative is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the essential need for sustained, high-quality primary data infrastructure. Open data initiatives depend on accurate, standardized inputs to drive accountability and informed policy; without consistent government investment in collecting such fundamental economic indicators, the transparency and utility of open data ecosystems would significantly suffer.
