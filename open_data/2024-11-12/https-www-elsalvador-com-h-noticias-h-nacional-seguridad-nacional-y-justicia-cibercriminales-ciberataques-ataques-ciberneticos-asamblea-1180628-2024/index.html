Open Data
ES EN

The article highlights the approval of fundamental cybersecurity and personal data protection laws, which establish a regulatory framework to govern public information and create a State Security Agency. These initiatives aim to transform oversight into audits and ensure that measures are based on international standards, promoting technological non-discrimination. The central objective is to give citizens control over their personal data, allowing access, correction, and deletion of information, as well as the reversal of data leaks in search engines. The relevance for open data lies in the balance between necessary transparency and individual privacy. Although these laws protect personal data, their implementation directly affects how institutions manage and share open information. The creation of technical regulations and protocols requires regulated entities to coordinate closely with the authority, which can influence the criteria for publishing government data. This means that data openness must strictly align with the security and privacy standards defined by the new agency. Finally, the context of recent public data breaches underscores the urgency of these legal frameworks. The exposure of sensitive information from state and private institutions demonstrates the current vulnerability of information systems. Therefore, these laws not only protect citizens but also aim to safeguard the national digital infrastructure. For the open data ecosystem, this means that information availability must be managed under strict security controls, prioritizing integrity and confidentiality over indiscriminate accessibility.
