FOIA Turns Up Zilch On Authority Garland Said He Gave Weiss

The investigation reveals a critical absence of documentation regarding the authority Attorney General Merrick Garland allegedly granted U.S. Attorney David Weiss over the Hunter Biden probe. Despite repeated assertions to Congress that Weiss held ultimate decision-making power, the Department of Justice failed to produce any written records corroborating these claims during a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit. This lack of evidence suggests that the declared authority was never formally established, raising serious questions about the accuracy of official statements made to legislative bodies. The discrepancy between public assurances and internal records highlights a significant transparency failure within the Department of Justice. Legal experts argue that any substantive delegation of such high-stakes authority would necessarily be memorialized in writing, yet no such documents exist. This omission implies that Garland’s representations to lawmakers may have been misleading, as they suggested a level of autonomy for Weiss that was never formally codified, thereby undermining the integrity of the investigative process. This case is highly relevant to the open_data movement because it underscores the vital role of transparency in holding government institutions accountable. When critical administrative decisions lack publicly accessible or discoverable records, it obscures the chain of command and prevents meaningful public scrutiny. Ensuring that all significant governmental authorities are properly documented and accessible through freedom of information mechanisms is essential to prevent political manipulation and maintain trust in the justice system.
