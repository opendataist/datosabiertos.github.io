Isabella commissioners reject FOIA appeal

The Isabella County Commission has firmly rejected persistent Freedom of Information Act requests aimed at recovering data from electronic poll books, thereby dismissing allegations of election fraud. Officials emphasize that the state’s directive to erase this data protects private voter information and that all votes were counted accurately. By upholding the denial, commissioners aim to halt what they view as baseless conspiracy theories and stop the emotional distress caused to election workers and local officials. This decision highlights the tension between public transparency and the protection of non-public data mandated by state election authorities. The county asserts that the Michigan Bureau of Elections has adequately addressed all concerns, leaving no substantive evidence of wrongdoing by local authorities. The rejection serves as a clear boundary against frivolous inquiries, reinforcing the integrity of the local election process and the legal standing of state-directed data retention policies. The article is relevant to open_data discussions as it illustrates the complex interplay between FOIA requests and specific data privacy regulations in the electoral context. It demonstrates how claims of transparency can be leveraged to support unfounded conspiracy theories, prompting authorities to prioritize data security and statutory compliance over indefinite public access. This scenario underscores the challenges local governments face in balancing open information principles with the need to safeguard sensitive voter data and maintain public trust in election systems.
