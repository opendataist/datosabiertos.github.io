Crece población cárcelaria 2.6% durante 2022: Inegi

The 2022 prison census reveals a critical systemic imbalance in Mexico, where nearly forty percent of the incarcerated population remains without a final judgment. This surge in pre-trial detention highlights profound inefficiencies within the justice system, disproportionately affecting vulnerable groups and suggesting that the presumption of innocence is frequently undermined by prolonged legal delays rather than procedural safeguards. Gender disparities further exacerbate this crisis, as women face significantly higher rates of being held without sentencing compared to men. The reliance on preventive custody, particularly "mandatory preventive prison," indicates a punitive approach to bail and detention that prioritizes confinement over due process, revealing structural biases that hinder fair trial access and contribute to overcrowding in correctional facilities. These findings are vital for the open data community as they demonstrate how transparent, granular statistics can expose systemic human rights issues. By making such data accessible, researchers and activists can advocate for necessary judicial reforms, holding institutions accountable for detention practices and pushing for policies that prioritize justice over mere incarceration rates.
