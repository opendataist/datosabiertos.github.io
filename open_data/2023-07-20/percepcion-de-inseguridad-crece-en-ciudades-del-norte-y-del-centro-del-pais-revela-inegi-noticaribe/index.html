Percepción de inseguridad crece en ciudades del norte y del centro del país, revela INEGI - Noticaribe

A significant majority of Mexicans perceive their cities as unsafe, with specific regions exhibiting particularly high levels of insecurity. This widespread feeling of vulnerability highlights a critical gap in public safety and underscores the urgent need for transparent, accessible data to understand the spatial distribution of fear and its underlying causes across different urban centers. Public trust in security forces is heavily skewed toward federal institutions, while local police performance is viewed far less favorably. This divergence suggests that current governance strategies may not adequately address community-level safety needs, indicating a necessity for open data initiatives that track the comparative effectiveness of different governmental agencies to inform better resource allocation and policy adjustments. Furthermore, citizens express deep pessimism about future improvements in security and infrastructure, citing persistent issues such as poor road conditions and utility failures. This lack of hope emphasizes the importance of open data in fostering accountability, as detailed, publicly available information on government performance can empower communities to demand concrete solutions and monitor progress in addressing both safety and basic service deficiencies.
