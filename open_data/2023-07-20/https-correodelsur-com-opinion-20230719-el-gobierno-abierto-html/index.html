Carlos Borth proposes the creation of a new political party to overcome what he considers the current state’s failure in Bolivia. His model seeks to rebuild the nation by combating corruption and strengthening regional autonomies. Central to this proposal is the introduction of open government as a new way of managing public administration, marking a paradigmatic shift in how the relationship between the state and citizens is understood. Open government is defined by three fundamental pillars: transparency, accountability, and citizen collaboration. This approach demands the eradication of administrative secrecy to allow society access to state information, which is essential for holding leaders accountable. By guaranteeing access to information, citizens’ capacity to oversee public decisions is strengthened, fostering trust and improving the quality of government management through active participation. This article is relevant to open data because it directly links access to public information with democracy and the fight against corruption. Transparency is not merely a legal right but a structural requirement for efficient governance, supported by international conventions such as those of the United Nations and the Organization of American States. By promoting data openness and freedom of expression, the necessary ecosystem is created for citizens to use public information as a tool for oversight, demonstrating that open data are essential for accountability and institutional legitimacy.
