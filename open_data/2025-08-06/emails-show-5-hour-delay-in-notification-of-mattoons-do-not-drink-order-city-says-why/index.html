Emails show 5-hour delay in notification of Mattoon’s ‘do not drink’ order; city says why

Emails show 5-hour delay in notification of Mattoon’s ‘do not drink’ order; city says why

The article highlights a significant delay between receiving critical water safety test results and issuing a public health warning in Mattoon. Residents discovered via obtained records that there was a several-hour gap between the city identifying potential contamination and officially notifying the public. This lag has sparked local concern, as individuals argue that immediate transparency is essential for community safety, allowing time for residents to take protective actions before exposure occurs. City officials defended the timeline, explaining that the initial notification did not explicitly mandate an order, prompting a period of verification and consultation with state environmental agencies. The administration emphasized the need to confirm the validity of the data and coordinate with multiple departments before making a final decision during a scheduled council meeting. This process, while intended to ensure accuracy, resulted in a response time that frustrated citizens who felt the urgency of the situation warranted faster communication. This incident is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how proactive disclosure of internal communications and decision-making timelines can build public trust and drive systemic improvements. By making these records accessible, citizens were able to identify inefficiencies in emergency protocols, leading to discussions about better training for communication systems and the development of clearer emergency response plans. Ultimately, it underscores the value of transparency in holding local governments accountable and ensuring that data informs faster, more reliable public health responses.
