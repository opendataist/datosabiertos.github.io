La inflación acumulada por Bolivia en 2025 roza el 17% y duplica las previsiones del Gobierno

Bolivia’s inflation rate has significantly exceeded initial government projections, reflecting a severe economic contraction driven by chronic foreign exchange shortages and fuel scarcity. Although recent months have shown a slight deceleration in monthly price increases due to improved supply chains and stabilized parallel exchange rates, the accumulated annual inflation remains dangerously high. This disparity between official fixed rates and market realities creates a distorted economic environment where the prices of essential goods fluctuate based on access to informal currency rather than fundamental production costs. The disconnect between official statistics and citizens’ lived experiences highlights a critical issue of data transparency. When official indices fail to reflect the true cost of living, public trust in institutional metrics erodes, making it difficult to implement effective policy responses. The reliance on a rigid official exchange rate, while actual market rates vary widely, obscures the true magnitude of economic pressure, leading to social unrest and protests stemming from the inability to afford basic necessities such as transportation and food. This case is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the necessity of independent, accessible, and granular economic datasets. Without transparent, real-time information on parallel markets and actual consumer prices, policymakers and citizens cannot accurately assess economic health or hold institutions accountable. Open data initiatives can bridge this gap by providing alternative metrics that capture informal economic activities, ensuring that economic indicators are holistic, verifiable, and truly representative of societal welfare rather than just official accounting.
