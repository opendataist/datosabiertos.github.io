UK researchers develop new model to predict 10-year breast cancer risk

UK researchers develop new model to predict 10-year breast cancer risk

British researchers have developed a statistically robust model that accurately predicts a woman’s likelihood of developing and dying from breast cancer within ten years. This breakthrough leverages anonymised data to identify high-risk individuals with greater precision, paving the way for more targeted and effective screening protocols. The study highlights the potential for risk-based strategies to balance the benefits and harms of breast cancer screening. By enabling personalised information, this approach allows for earlier or more frequent screenings for those at highest risk, while sparing lower-risk women from unnecessary procedures. Such efficiency supports better decision-making and optimizes health service resources. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the power of utilizing large, diverse, and anonymised datasets to drive public health innovations. The rigorous validation of the model across different ethnic groups and regions underscores the importance of transparent, high-quality data sharing in developing equitable and generalizable AI solutions for medicine.
