Ojo AUH y jubilados ANSES: la advertencia de Fernanda Raverta a los beneficiarios, ¿qué dijo el organismo?

ANSES urgently warns beneficiaries, particularly pensioners and recipients of the Universal Child Allowance (AUH), to remain vigilant against sophisticated scams circulating via social media and email. The agency highlights a surge in fraudulent activities in which criminals impersonate official staff or create fake websites to steal sensitive personal and financial information under the guise of updating identification data. This warning coincides with the final payment days, emphasizing the critical need for cybersecurity among vulnerable populations who may inadvertently expose their identity. The article outlines specific indicators of legitimacy, noting that official ANSES communications never request banking or phone details, and that all legitimate transactions occur solely through verified digital platforms, the official app, or physical agencies. Beneficiaries are instructed to verify URLs, look for verified blue checks on social media, and rely exclusively on secure, established channels. By distinguishing official processes from deceptive attempts, individuals can protect their assets and personal data from identity theft and financial loss. This content is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it underscores the importance of data integrity and public trust in government information systems. As agencies increasingly digitize services, clear communication about data protection and official channels is essential to maintain transparency. Furthermore, understanding how fraudulent actors manipulate public data reinforces the need for robust digital literacy and secure data management practices, ensuring that open government initiatives do not inadvertently expose users to security risks.
