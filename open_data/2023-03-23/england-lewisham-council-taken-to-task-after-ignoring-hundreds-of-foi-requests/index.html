England: Lewisham Council taken to task after ignoring hundreds of FOI requests

The Information Commissioner’s Office has sanctioned the London Borough of Lewisham Council for ignoring hundreds of Freedom of Information requests, revealing a severe backlog that undermined public trust. By prioritizing new inquiries over resolving older, overdue cases, the local authority failed to maintain necessary transparency, keeping citizens uninformed about matters they had a legal right to access. This systemic neglect highlights how administrative negligence can directly erode confidence in democratic institutions and open government practices. The enforcement notice mandates the council to clear its accumulated backlog within six months and publish a concrete action plan to prevent future delays. This regulatory intervention underscores the ICO’s commitment to holding public authorities accountable when they repeatedly breach transparency laws. The threat of potential contempt of court serves as a stern warning that failure to comply with statutory information duties will not be tolerated, ensuring that local governments remain answerable to the public they serve. This case is critically relevant to open_data advocates as it demonstrates the real-world consequences of withholding accessible information. It illustrates that technical compliance with deadlines is insufficient if the underlying culture does not prioritize proactive transparency. For the open data community, it reinforces the necessity of legal enforcement mechanisms to ensure that data remains truly open and accessible, preventing bureaucratic inertia from blocking citizen engagement and accountability.
