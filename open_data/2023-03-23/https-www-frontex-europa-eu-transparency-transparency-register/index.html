Frontex’s Transparency Register implements strict data protection measures to manage personal information related to stakeholder interactions. By processing data solely to prevent conflicts of interest and ensure lobbying transparency, the agency aligns its operations with European Union privacy regulations. This approach demonstrates how public institutions can legally handle sensitive personal data while maintaining rigorous ethical standards in their governance structures. The core objective is to make the functions of individuals contacting agency representatives publicly accessible, thereby promoting accountability. Personal details are recorded only for these specific transparency and conflict-of-interest purposes, ensuring that data usage remains limited and justified. This selective processing highlights the importance of purpose limitation in open data initiatives, showing that transparency does not require indiscriminate data release but rather targeted, relevant disclosure. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the balance between public accountability and individual privacy rights. By maintaining a publicly available record of processing activities and offering clear avenues for data subjects to exercise their rights, Frontex sets a precedent for responsible data management. It emphasizes that open governance requires not just data availability, but also robust frameworks for privacy, consent, and oversight, which are essential for building public trust in institutional transparency.
