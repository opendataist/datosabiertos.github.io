The article argues that trust between law enforcement and communities is foundational to public safety, yet this trust is rapidly eroding due to opaque police practices. The central conclusion is that transparency must be prioritized over rigid policies to prevent the deterioration of community relations. When agencies withhold information or release it slowly, they create an environment where suspicion flourishes, ultimately undermining the cooperative dynamic necessary for effective policing and crime prevention. The author critiques specific failures in recent high-profile incidents, contrasting delayed and fragmented disclosures with the benefits of immediate, honest communication. Arbitrary timelines for releasing evidence, such as body camera footage, are presented as harmful flaws that suggest secrecy rather than procedural necessity. In contrast, instances where leadership provided rapid and comprehensive updates helped mitigate public outrage, demonstrating that accurate and expedient information sharing can stabilize volatile situations and preserve institutional credibility. This perspective is highly relevant to open data because it highlights how accessibility of information directly impacts social trust. The piece suggests that law enforcement agencies should treat public data release as a default standard, minimizing delays unless strictly necessary for active investigations. By adopting open data principles in crisis communications, police departments can reduce misinformation and foster accountability, proving that transparency is not just a legal obligation but a critical tool for maintaining social license and community cooperation.
