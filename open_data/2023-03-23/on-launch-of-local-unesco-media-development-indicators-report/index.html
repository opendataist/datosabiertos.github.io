On launch of local UNESCO media development indicators report!

The launch of The Gambia’s first media development indicators report highlights the critical need for transparency and legal frameworks like Freedom of Information laws. This initiative underscores the vital role of accessible information in empowering citizens to make informed decisions and engage in democratic debates, aligning with global open data principles that prioritize public access to government information. While the liberalization of the media sector has increased the number of private broadcasters, the report reveals persistent challenges in journalists' economic wellbeing and working conditions. These gaps hinder media independence and pluralism, demonstrating that mere structural expansion is insufficient without addressing the underlying socioeconomic factors that affect the quality and reliability of information dissemination. Relevant to open data, this report illustrates how systematic assessment of information ecosystems can drive policy reform and improve good governance. By identifying specific gaps in media development, stakeholders can advocate for better protections and resources, ensuring that the flow of information remains robust, independent, and beneficial for democratic transition processes.
