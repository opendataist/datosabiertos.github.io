Oaxaca is confronting a critical public health crisis, as evidenced by the fact that nearly 410,000 residents lack access to piped water. This deficiency ranks the state fourth nationally in terms of water scarcity incidence, significantly affecting community well-being, particularly in vulnerable hillside neighborhoods where health risks are heightened. The lack of adequate sanitation and safe drinking water is directly linked to rising rates of infectious diseases. The region reports substantial numbers of hepatitis A, paratyphoid, and intestinal infection cases, which disproportionately affect children and the elderly. These health burdens underscore the urgent need for infrastructure improvements to prevent waterborne illnesses. This data is crucial for open data initiatives, as it highlights the importance of transparent and accessible government statistics. By making such health and infrastructure metrics publicly available, citizens and organizations can advocate for policy changes, monitor service delivery, and hold authorities accountable for equitable resource distribution and public safety.
