Los edificios sostenibles fijan los cimientos de la transición energética

The Spanish real estate sector is emerging as a central player in the energy transition, given that its activities account for a significant share of national carbon emissions and energy consumption. To meet ambitious decarbonization targets, it is imperative that both commercial and residential buildings adopt comprehensive efficiency strategies. The key lies in integrating energy management with space comfort and usage, turning consumption reduction into the decisive lever for achieving the environmental sustainability required. The fundamental basis for any effective action is precise measurement and continuous monitoring of energy data. Without reliable information on emissions, it is impossible to implement real improvements or access financial benefits and subsidies. Moreover, there is growing demand from tenants and the market for efficiency certifications, which has made sustainability an indispensable requirement for the competitiveness and ongoing operation of real estate businesses, beyond being merely an ethical or regulatory issue. However, a marked gap persists within the sector between large companies, which have the resources to modernize, and the vast majority of the housing stock, where most homes are energy inefficient. This asymmetry poses a complex challenge that goes beyond technical investment in buildings, requiring awareness among users about their daily consumption and the management of urban mobility associated with spaces such as shopping centers. The transition demands not only technology but also a cultural shift in the behavior of tenants and operators. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for transparent, standardized, and accessible data on energy consumption. Market efficiency and trust depend on management systems that collect, certify, and share reliable information on emissions and energy performance, enabling informed decision-making and accountability in the fight against climate change.
