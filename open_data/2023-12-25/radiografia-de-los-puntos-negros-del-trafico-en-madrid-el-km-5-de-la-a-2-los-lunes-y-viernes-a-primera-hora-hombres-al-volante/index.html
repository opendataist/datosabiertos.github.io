Radiografía de los puntos negros del tráfico en Madrid: el Km. 5 de la A-2, los lunes y viernes a primera hora, hombres al volante...

An analysis of the open data published by the Madrid City Council reveals that road accidents are predominantly concentrated at strategic intersections and major arterial roads with high traffic volumes, especially during peak morning and evening commute hours. This correlation between high vehicle density and accident frequency underscores the importance of leveraging data transparency to identify risk zones and prioritize road safety at the most critical mobility nodes. Demographic and temporal patterns indicate that young drivers and men are the most vulnerable groups, while the prevalence of front-lateral collisions suggests frequent conflicts at complex intersections. The relevance of this article for the open data community lies in how access to these municipal records enables the transformation of raw information into actionable evidence. By making hidden trends visible, data-driven insights support fact-based decision-making, facilitating the evaluation of public policies and the prevention of casualties through targeted interventions addressing the root causes identified. In conclusion, the openness of these data not only documents the everyday reality of mobility in the capital but also serves as a fundamental tool for accountability and the continuous improvement of urban infrastructure. The ability to disaggregate information by district, vehicle type, and time of day empowers citizens and policymakers to engage in precise discussions about road safety, demonstrating that informational transparency is key to building safer and more resilient cities.
