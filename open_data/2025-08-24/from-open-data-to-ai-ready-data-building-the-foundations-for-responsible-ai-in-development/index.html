From Open Data to AI-Ready Data: Building the Foundations for Responsible AI in Development

From Open Data to AI-Ready Data: Building the Foundations for Responsible AI in Development

The article argues that development data must evolve into "AI-ready" formats to ensure that artificial intelligence systems access authoritative, accurate, and structured information. As AI becomes a primary interface for retrieving insights, current limitations often lead to unreliable responses derived from unverified sources. By prioritizing high-quality, well-documented data, stakeholders can prevent misinformation and ensure that AI tools rely on trusted institutional datasets rather than hallucinated or outdated web content. To achieve this readiness, three foundational pillars are required: robust data systems that facilitate discovery through interoperable standards, high-quality data paired with comprehensive metadata, and strong governance frameworks ensuring ethical use and transparency. These elements extend existing open data principles by making datasets not just accessible to humans, but also machine-readable and interpretable for AI applications. This structural shift ensures that data remains findable, usable, and trustworthy across diverse platforms and user expertise levels. This evolution is critical to open data because it bridges the gap between raw information and actionable policy insights, democratizing access to reliable development metrics. When data is optimized for AI, it accelerates decision-making and fosters innovation while maintaining public trust in statistical integrity. Collaborative efforts among governments, international organizations, and the private sector are essential to standardize these practices, ultimately ensuring that development data continues to serve the public good equitably in the Age of AI.
