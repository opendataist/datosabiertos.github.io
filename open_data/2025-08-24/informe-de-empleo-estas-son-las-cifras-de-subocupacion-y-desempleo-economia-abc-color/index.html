Informe de empleo: estas son las cifras de subocupación y desempleo - Economía - ABC Color

Informe de empleo: estas son las cifras de subocupación y desempleo - Economía - ABC Color

The labor market shows a slight improvement in employment rates, driven by favorable economic activity, particularly in the tertiary sector fueled by tourism and commerce. However, this positive headline figure masks underlying instability, as the gains are partially offset by a rise in time-related underemployment. This indicates that while more people are working, many are not securing sufficient hours, revealing a qualitative weakness in the quality of the new jobs being created despite the quantitative increase in employment coverage. Unemployment has decreased compared to the previous year, yet the combined pressure of joblessness and underemployment still affects a significant portion of the workforce. This persistent dual challenge highlights that formal employment statistics alone do not fully capture labor market distress. The data suggests that while access to work has expanded, the adequacy and stability of that work remain inconsistent, leaving a substantial number of individuals without the labor security they require for economic well-being. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the necessity of analyzing diverse labor indicators beyond simple employment rates. Relying solely on headline figures can obscure critical details about underemployment and sectoral shifts. Open data advocates emphasize the importance of accessing granular, disaggregated datasets to uncover these hidden trends. By making comprehensive survey results publicly available and transparent, stakeholders can better understand the true nature of economic dynamics and advocate for policies that address not just job creation, but job quality and sufficiency.
