La desigualdad en las tareas de cuidado persiste en Panamá

The article highlights a significant gender disparity in which women in Panama perform three times the amount of unpaid care work compared to men. This reality restricts their participation in the labor market and hinders economic development. This imbalance is not merely a domestic issue but a structural barrier that perpetuates inequality, affecting women’s career progression, particularly in fields such as science, where a lack of support forces professional interruptions. Recognizing care as a shared social and economic responsibility is essential to dismantling these systemic barriers and fostering equitable societies. This narrative is crucial for open data because it demonstrates the real-world impact of statistical visibility. Data from national institutes exposes these gaps, enabling evidence-based advocacy for policy reforms such as Panama’s National Care System. Open data initiatives are vital in this context, as they transform hidden informal labor into measurable economic value, allowing governments and civil society to track progress, allocate resources effectively, and hold institutions accountable for achieving gender equality and decent work goals. Ultimately, addressing this disparity requires a collective shift involving the state, the private sector, and civil society. The campaign “Caring is not just women’s business” uses data-driven insights to promote shared responsibility, aligning local actions with global sustainability objectives. By making care visible through robust data and public discourse, societies can implement supportive measures such as equal parental leave and accessible childcare, ensuring that care is valued as a fundamental pillar of social and economic well-being for all genders.
