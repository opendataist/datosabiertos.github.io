Exnovio me acosaba, entré a app de citas seguras y terminaron filtrando mi dirección personal

The Tea dating app, marketed to women as a safe space, has instead facilitated severe gender-based harassment through leaked data and parallel male-only groups. These platforms enabled the non-consensual sharing of intimate images, doxxing, and misogynistic commentary, revealing how digital spaces can be weaponized against women under the guise of community protection. This exploitation highlights a critical failure in platform governance and user verification, where claims of safety mask environments ripe for abuse and defamation. This incident underscores the urgent need for robust data protection and ethical standards in open data ecosystems. When personal information is aggregated and shared without consent, it amplifies existing power imbalances and harms vulnerable populations. The case demonstrates that open data practices, if implemented without strict privacy safeguards and consent mechanisms, can exacerbate online violence rather than mitigate it, challenging the narrative that transparency inherently promotes security. The fallout contributes to a growing "gender war" in online dating, eroding trust and pushing users away from digital romance due to fear and insecurity. As tensions rise, the relevance to open data lies in recognizing that unregulated information flows can deepen social divisions and compromise individual safety. Stakeholders must prioritize accountability and ethical design to ensure that data-driven tools support, rather than undermine, mutual respect and safety in digital interactions.
