Y entonces ¿la pobreza es un mito? / Rafael Cano Franco - Kioscomayor

Y entonces ¿la pobreza es un mito? / Rafael Cano Franco - Kioscomayor

The article critically examines official poverty reduction statistics released by Mexico’s INEGI, arguing that the data is potentially misleading due to methodological changes and the lack of independent verification. By eliminating specialized agencies like Coneval and reducing poverty indicators, the government has created a narrative of success that lacks scientific rigor. This raises concerns that the reported decrease in poverty may reflect a redefinition of metrics rather than a genuine improvement in living conditions, undermining the credibility of public policy evaluations. This issue is crucial for open data because it highlights how data accessibility does not guarantee transparency or trustworthiness. When statistical institutions become structurally aligned with the executive branch and lack diverse, independent analytical methods, the resulting data serves political narratives rather than objective reality. Open data initiatives must therefore prioritize methodological independence and multidimensional metrics—such as health, housing, and food security—beyond simple income levels, to ensure that statistical outputs truly reflect societal well-being. Ultimately, the text warns against accepting single-dimensional economic improvements as proof of poverty alleviation. Without comprehensive indicators and external validation, citizens cannot accurately assess their economic status. For open data advocates, this case demonstrates the necessity of preserving institutional autonomy and enforcing robust, multi-variable standards in public statistics to prevent the manipulation of data for political propaganda.
