Deputy Guillermo Zamacona Urquiza concluded his second activity report by highlighting the fulfillment of environmental and governance commitments that directly benefit citizens. His management achieved the closure of the San Miguel Mimiapan landfill, preventing ecological damage, and stopped the destruction of vast forested areas in Jilotzingo. He also consolidated the Espíritu Santo Water Sanctuary as a key tool for preserving water resources, demonstrating that legislative action can reverse local environmental crises through coordination with municipal authorities and the state congress. A central pillar of his management was the initiative to require municipalities to disclose construction permits. This measure aims to combat corruption and uncontrolled urban growth by ensuring that information about real estate developments is accessible and understandable to the community. By requiring town councils to publish this data, it fosters a culture of public accountability where territorial planning does not occur behind closed doors, but under the scrutiny and oversight of affected residents. This article is highly relevant to the open data community because it exemplifies the practical application of transparency in urban management. The requirement to publish construction permits constitutes an ideal case study on how government data should be accessible, standardized, and usable to empower citizens. By linking information openness with the prevention of poor planning and corruption, it reinforces the fundamental argument that open data are not merely a technical end, but an indispensable tool for democracy, environmental sustainability, and effective social oversight.
