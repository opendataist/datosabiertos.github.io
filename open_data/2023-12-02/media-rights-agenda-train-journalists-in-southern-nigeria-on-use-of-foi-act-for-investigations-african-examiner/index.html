Media Rights Agenda Train Journalists In Southern Nigeria On Use Of FOI Act For Investigations | African Examiner

A recent workshop for journalists emphasized the critical role of the Freedom of Information Act in enhancing investigative reporting. The primary conclusion is that media practitioners must significantly increase their usage of the Act to access public records, thereby improving the quantity and quality of stories that hold government institutions accountable. By mastering these legal tools, journalists can move beyond optimal potential to actively drive transparency and good governance at state and local levels. Beyond legal frameworks, the exercise highlighted the structural challenges facing investigative journalism, particularly the lack of funding for time-intensive projects. Participants acknowledged that while such reporting exposes corruption, it is often too expensive for individual news organizations. Consequently, there is a pressing need for established funding mechanisms, such as grants, to support these efforts. This financial support is essential to sustain the rigorous scrutiny required to evaluate the performance and effectiveness of public institutions. The article also underscores the urgent need for legal and physical protections for journalists, noting frequent misuse of laws like the Cybercrimes Act to harass media workers. There is a strong call for legislative safeguards that protect journalists’ sources and equipment, ensuring they can gather information without fear of reprisal. This is directly relevant to open data because effective open data initiatives rely on a safe, empowered media ecosystem capable of translating raw public information into meaningful, impactful journalism that serves the public interest.
