Statistics Finland is overhauling its wage data collection by shifting from fragmented sources to a unified system centered on the national Incomes Register. This transition aims to replace several existing separate statistics, such as those for local government and private sector earnings, with a single, harmonized dataset. By utilizing real-time, comprehensive earnings records, the new methodology seeks to clarify data definitions and significantly improve the comparability of wage statistics across different sectors and employment groups. The update is driven by both technological advancements and renewed European Union legislation that expands statistical coverage to include all employers, not just larger enterprises. While the Incomes Register serves as the primary source for earnings data, additional registers and surveys will supplement it to capture essential background variables like occupation and working hours that the register alone may miss. This comprehensive approach ensures that the resulting statistics reflect the entire labor market more accurately, addressing previous gaps in data for small businesses and diverse worker categories. This shift is highly relevant to the open data community as it signals a move toward more granular, real-time, and interoperable public datasets. The integration of the Incomes Register provides a robust foundation for transparency and reproducibility in economic analysis. For developers and researchers, these changes promise a more consistent and accessible stream of labor market data, although users must anticipate changes in historical comparability and a potential one-year lag in final published figures as the new compilation methods take effect.
