South African economy averts recession as mining sector rebounds

South Africa narrowly avoided a technical recession in the fourth quarter, driven by reduced power cuts that aided manufacturing and mining. This slight recovery highlights how infrastructure stability directly impacts industrial output, underscoring the critical link between public utility reliability and economic resilience. Despite this quarterly gain, annual growth remains sluggish, failing to adequately address deep-seated poverty and unemployment. The persistent logistical bottlenecks at state-owned ports and railways continue to hamper exports and supply chains, indicating that structural inefficiencies severely constrain broader economic potential and consumer spending. This case is vital for open data advocates because transparent, timely economic indicators expose governance failures and hold ruling parties accountable. By making performance metrics accessible, open data empowers citizens to scrutinize state-owned enterprises and demand better management of public infrastructure, ensuring accountability before major electoral shifts.
