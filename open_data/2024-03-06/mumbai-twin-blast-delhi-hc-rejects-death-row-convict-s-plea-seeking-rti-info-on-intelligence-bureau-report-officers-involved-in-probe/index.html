Mumbai Twin Blast: Delhi HC Rejects Death Row Convict's Plea Seeking RTI Info On Intelligence Bureau Report, Officers Involved In Probe

The Delhi High Court recently upheld the refusal to disclose specific investigative details to a death row convict under the Right to Information Act, prioritizing the safety and privacy of law enforcement officials. The ruling establishes that disclosing the identities of supervising officers and the existence of certain intelligence reports poses a grave risk to their lives, even decades after the underlying incident. This decision reinforces the principle that national security and personal safety can outweigh public interest in transparency when the latter threatens the physical well-being of state agents. From an open data perspective, this case highlights the significant tension between government transparency and the protection of sensitive operational data. It demonstrates that legal frameworks often exclude specific categories of information from open access, particularly when such data intersects with intelligence gathering and law enforcement operations. The court’s reliance on privacy concerns and potential physical danger illustrates how open data initiatives must navigate complex exceptions where disclosing information could compromise ongoing security protocols or endanger individuals involved in critical state functions. Furthermore, the judgment underscores the evidentiary standards applied to requests for government records, noting that media reports alone do not substantiate the existence of official documents. This is relevant to open data governance as it clarifies that public access to information is strictly regulated by verified official records rather than secondary sources. The decision serves as a cautionary example for researchers and citizens, indicating that while openness is a democratic ideal, it is bounded by strict legal protections designed to safeguard the integrity and safety of state institutions and their personnel.
