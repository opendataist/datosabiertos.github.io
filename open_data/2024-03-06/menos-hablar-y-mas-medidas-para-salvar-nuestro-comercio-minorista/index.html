Menos hablar y más medidas para salvar nuestro comercio minorista

The article highlights the precipitous decline of Spain’s small businesses, particularly in Madrid, driven by the dominance of e-commerce and burdensome regulations that lack corresponding support for small enterprises. This crisis threatens local neighborhoods and requires urgent intervention to prevent the complete disappearance of independent retailers, as current legal and fiscal frameworks fail to ensure their viability. Reversing this trend demands a strategic shift toward specialized, exclusive retail that attracts customers through unique value rather than mere goodwill. Success relies on adapting tax and legal frameworks to guarantee profitability and incentivizing direct purchases. However, this struggle is compounded by restrictive mobility policies for local merchants, who face unfair advantages enjoyed by large delivery companies regarding parking and traffic access. This situation is critical for open data because transparent access to administrative data could expose these regulatory inequalities and support evidence-based policy reforms. By analyzing open datasets on commerce density, traffic enforcement, and business survival rates, stakeholders can advocate for fairer regulations. Data-driven insights are essential to protect local economies and ensure that urban planning supports sustainable, diverse retail ecosystems rather than monopolistic logistical practices.
