FOI Advocates: Bill Exempting Witness Statements On Police Body Cameras Is 'Overly Broad' | CT News Junkie

A proposed Connecticut bill aiming to exempt police body camera recordings of witness statements from public disclosure has faced significant backlash from transparency advocates and legal experts. While proponents argue the legislation simply aligns modern video evidence collection with existing exemptions for written statements, critics contend the broad language threatens to suppress essential information. The core implication is a potential erosion of public trust, as the undefined scope could hide crucial details about police conduct and investigative processes rather than just protecting witness identities. Freedom of Information commissions and advocacy groups warn that the current wording could extend exemptions to all witness-derived data, including 911 calls and internal reports, thereby compromising the transparency of criminal investigations. This overreach risks undermining the fundamental purpose of open government laws. The concern is not merely about protecting witnesses, but about preventing the unnecessary concealment of evidence that helps the public understand how law enforcement determines charges and handles cases, which is vital for accountability in an open society. Furthermore, legal challenges suggest the bill may violate First Amendment protections against prior restraint, particularly regarding the media’s right to report on incident scenes. If passed without narrow, precise language, the legislation could set a dangerous precedent for government censorship and editorial interference. For open data initiatives, this case highlights the critical need for legislation to balance privacy rights with the public’s right to information, ensuring that exemptions are specific and do not create blanket barriers to accessing government records.
