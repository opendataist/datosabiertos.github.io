An AI glossary: the words and terms to know about the booming industry

This glossary clarifies the complex landscape of artificial intelligence by defining key concepts that drive current technological advancements. It distinguishes between narrow AI, which performs specific tasks, and generative AI, which creates new content such as text or images. Understanding these distinctions is vital for users to grasp the shift from traditional content classification to active content creation, highlighting the unprecedented capabilities and public interest surrounding modern AI systems. A significant focus is placed on the ethical and operational challenges inherent in AI development, particularly regarding training data and algorithmic bias. The article emphasizes that low-quality or biased data leads to prejudiced decisions with serious real-world consequences, while also noting issues like AI "hallucinations" and the need for alignment with human values. These insights underscore the critical importance of transparency and accountability in AI systems, urging a more cautious and informed approach to their deployment and regulation. Relevant to open data, this explanation highlights that the quality, sourcing, and curation of training datasets directly influence AI reliability and fairness. It illustrates how open access to diverse, high-quality data can help mitigate bias and improve model performance, while also stressing the need for clear frameworks around data usage. As AI relies heavily on extensive information repositories, promoting open data standards becomes essential for building trustworthy, equitable, and effective artificial intelligence technologies that benefit society as a whole.
