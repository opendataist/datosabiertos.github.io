Domestic abuse victims ‘waiting hundreds of days’ for accommodation

The article highlights a critical failure in Scotland’s domestic abuse response system, where victims face prolonged uncertainty due to delays in securing permanent housing. This systemic bottleneck leaves survivors trapped in temporary accommodation for extended periods, undermining their safety and ability to rebuild their lives. The policy paper advocates for legislative changes to prioritize housing access, aiming to provide the stability necessary for victims to escape violent environments. Significant relevance to open data lies in the methodology used to expose this crisis. The findings were derived from freedom of information requests, demonstrating how transparent access to government-held statistics can reveal hidden societal failures. This case illustrates the power of open data in holding public institutions accountable, as the disclosure of waiting times served as the primary evidence for launching a bipartisan political campaign and subsequent legislative bills. Ultimately, the push for a dedicated Violence Against Women Unit and reclassified legal threats underscores the need for systemic reform driven by empirical evidence. By leveraging publicly available data, advocates successfully demonstrated that current support structures are insufficient. This narrative reinforces the essential role of open data in identifying policy gaps and fostering informed, evidence-based solutions for social welfare and justice improvements.
