Baleares, primera comunidad que elimina su oficina anticorrupción

The Balearic Islands have approved a new law that dissolves its Anti-Corruption Office and replaces it with a Transparency Registry under parliamentary oversight. This change represents a fundamental shift in the governance of the autonomous community, as information on the assets of public officials will no longer be publicly accessible, becoming instead confidential data limited to internal records with restricted access. The measure also eliminates the possibility of filing anonymous complaints, centralizing the custody of case files within the executive administration. The implementation of this legislation creates significant tension between the principles of government openness and political oversight. By restricting public access to information about the assets of elected representatives, the ability of citizens and the press to independently audit the integrity of authorities is diminished. This poses a considerable risk to democratic accountability, as oversight shifts from a transparent public function to a less visible internal administrative process. This case is particularly relevant to the open data movement because it demonstrates how selective withholding of information can erode effective transparency. The transformation of public data into restricted records, even while maintaining the formal obligation to declare them, contradicts international open data standards that prioritize maximum possible disclosure. It serves as a warning about how legal frameworks can be designed to create the appearance of regulation while substantially hiding critical information from public scrutiny.
