EBU demands Meta opt-out over AI scraping terms

EBU demands Meta opt-out over AI scraping terms

The European Broadcasting Union warns Meta’s updated commercial terms could allow the tech giant to scrape broadcaster websites without explicit consent, raising critical concerns about content ownership. This dispute highlights a fundamental tension in the open data ecosystem: the assumption that social media presence grants platforms broad rights to collect underlying data. By potentially linking audience access to content extraction, the proposal threatens the autonomy of data publishers to control how their information is harvested and utilized by third parties. Legal and regulatory authorities are scrutinizing whether these practices violate copyright directives and competition laws governing dominant market positions. The core implication is that technical defaults should not override legal rights for data reuse, particularly when involving AI training. If platforms can unilaterally define data access through broad user agreements, it undermines the principles of transparent and consensual data sharing that are essential for fair digital markets and intellectual property protection. This conflict is highly relevant to open data advocacy because it sets a precedent for how institutional data is treated by major tech infrastructure. It demonstrates the urgent need for clear, granular consent mechanisms in data licensing frameworks. Ensuring that open data principles apply even when data is embedded in proprietary platforms protects the integrity of information sources and ensures that data availability is driven by publisher choice rather than platform coercion.
