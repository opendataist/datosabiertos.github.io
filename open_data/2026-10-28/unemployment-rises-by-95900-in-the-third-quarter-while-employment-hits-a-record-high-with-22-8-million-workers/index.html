Unemployment rises by 95,900 in the third quarter, while employment hits a record high with 22.8 million workers

The analysis of labor data reveals a complex scenario marked by record employment growth, particularly in the services sector and among the foreign-born population. However, this expansion coexists with a significant rise in unemployment, disproportionately affecting men and first-time job seekers. The divergence between job creation and the increase in unemployment reflects structural changes in labor force participation and market dynamics. The relevance of this information for the open data community lies in the need to analyze together indicators that, at first glance, may appear contradictory. Transparency and access to these detailed statistics enable researchers and citizens to better understand underlying inequalities, such as gender gaps or territorial disparities. This facilitates constructive criticism of public policies and their real impact on different demographic groups. In short, public access to such granular data is crucial for monitoring the country’s economic and social health. It allows for the identification of emerging trends and the real-time evaluation of the effectiveness of government interventions. By promoting the dissemination of these figures, a more informed citizenry is fostered, one capable of actively participating in public debate and ensuring that decision-making is based on verifiable evidence rather than subjective perceptions.
