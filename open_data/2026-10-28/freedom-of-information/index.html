Freedom of Information

Freedom of Information

The article outlines the procedural framework for Freedom of Information requests, emphasizing that timely responses are critical as delayed replies are legally treated as refusals. It highlights a tiered fee structure for non-personal data, designed to balance administrative costs with public access, while noting that personal information remains free. This structure ensures that requests are processed efficiently without imposing undue financial burdens on applicants, provided the work involved stays within defined limits. Relevance to open data is found in the council’s commitment to proactive transparency through its publication scheme and website. By making routinely available information accessible outside formal request processes, the authority reduces reliance on individual FOI applications. This approach aligns with open data principles by promoting the early and spontaneous release of public sector information, thereby fostering a more open and accountable governance environment for citizens. However, the process acknowledges inherent limitations, as some records may be withheld due to sensitivity, third-party privacy, or excessive volume. The necessity of refining vague or broad requests ensures that the system remains functional and fair. Ultimately, while full transparency is the goal, the framework balances the right to access information with practical constraints and legal obligations regarding sensitive personal data.
