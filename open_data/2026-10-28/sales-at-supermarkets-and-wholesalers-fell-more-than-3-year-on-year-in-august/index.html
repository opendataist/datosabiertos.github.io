Sales at supermarkets and wholesalers fell more than 3% year-on-year in August

Sales at supermarkets and wholesalers fell more than 3% year-on-year in August

The data reveal a divergence between actual sales volume, which shows year-on-year contractions in supermarkets and wholesalers, and nominal growth driven by inflation. This discrepancy suggests significant pressure on consumers' purchasing power, as they buy fewer goods despite spending more money. This pattern is crucial for the open data sector, as it highlights the critical importance of distinguishing between nominal and real indicators in economic analysis. Transparency in the disaggregation of these data allows society to better understand the impact of inflation on the basic food basket. The availability of these official reports facilitates social auditing and journalistic analysis, preventing erroneous conclusions based solely on the increase in monetary spending. Thus, open data serve as an essential tool for contextualizing real economic hardships against superficial financial indicators.
