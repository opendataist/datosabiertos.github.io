14 Institutions fined GH¢1.3m for non-compliance with RTI Law

The article highlights the growing challenges in implementing Ghana’s Right to Information (RTI) Act, evidenced by significant financial penalties imposed on public institutions for non-compliance. This enforcement mechanism underscores the critical importance of accountability and transparency in public administration. For open data initiatives, this illustrates that mere legislative frameworks are insufficient; active adherence and institutional willingness are required to ensure data accessibility. Without genuine cooperation from state entities, the foundational right to access information remains theoretical, hindering broader transparency goals. Despite these compliance hurdles, the number of information requests has surged, reflecting increased public demand for transparency and engagement with government operations. This trend signals a shift toward a more informed citizenry that actively seeks to monitor public affairs. For open data advocates, this rising demand validates the necessity of robust, accessible data infrastructure. It suggests that when barriers are lowered, public interest flourishes, reinforcing the argument that open data is not just a technical issue but a democratic imperative that empowers citizens. To address implementation gaps, authorities are integrating RTI compliance into performance metrics for civil service leaders and developing digital systems to streamline request processing. These structural changes aim to institutionalize transparency and improve records management across ministries. This is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the shift from passive publication to active, standardized, and digital-enabled data provision. By aligning performance incentives with transparency duties and digitizing workflows, the government creates a sustainable ecosystem where open data can thrive, ensuring long-term accessibility and reliability of public information.
