Council has 'no data' on fire engines held up by LTNs

Recent Freedom of Information requests reveal significant concerns regarding how Low Traffic Neighbourhoods affect emergency response times in Oxford. While reports in London suggested ambulance delays caused by similar barriers, local emergency services in Oxfordshire have declined to provide specific data on potential incidents. This refusal to quantify delays has raised suspicions among local councillors, who fear that infrastructure changes might hinder fire engines and ambulances, thereby putting residents’ safety at risk. The county council argues that calculating specific delays is unfeasible due to numerous unpredictable variables, such as traffic conditions, public reaction to sirens, and the mobile nature of emergency crews. They assert that their teams are fully trained to navigate these zones efficiently, using updated maps and route planning to mitigate impacts. By emphasizing ongoing engagement and regular meetings with service providers, the council maintains that emergency access is managed effectively despite the structural changes introduced to reduce car traffic. This debate is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical need for transparent, quantifiable evidence when evaluating urban policy impacts. When governments implement changes like LTNs, stakeholders require accessible and clear data to assess whether public safety is compromised or improved. Without robust, openly available metrics, it remains difficult to validate claims about effectiveness or address legitimate public concerns, underscoring the importance of accountability in data-driven governance.
