RTI is working, says Kojo Oppong Nkrumah - MyJoyOnline.com

The Ghanaian Minister of Information asserts that the Right to Information Act is functioning effectively, citing substantial data showing thousands of requests processed in recent years. He argues that anecdotal claims of failure ignore the existence of formal mechanisms for reporting unresponsive agencies. This perspective highlights the importance of structured feedback loops in assessing legislative success rather than relying on individual experiences. Public awareness remains a critical challenge, prompting the government to urge media outlets to educate citizens about their rights under the law. By disseminating information on how to request data and seek reviews for delays, stakeholders can ensure the Act operates as intended. This advocacy underscores the need for accessible legal knowledge to empower individuals to exercise their information rights properly. This case is relevant to open data as it illustrates the gap between legal frameworks and public practice. Success depends not just on legislation, but on active promotion and transparent reporting mechanisms. Understanding how citizens navigate these systems helps improve data transparency and accountability in governance, ensuring that open data initiatives truly serve the public interest.
