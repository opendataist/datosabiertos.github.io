Pobreza subió: Uno de cada tres peruanos no puede costear una canasta básica, según el INEI | RPP Noticias

The report reveals that poverty in Peru has not only stagnated but worsened, reversing progress made before the pandemic. With nearly 28% of the population living below the poverty line and millions more vulnerable, the data underscores a significant regression in social welfare. This highlights the critical need for robust statistical tracking to accurately measure the human impact of economic and health crises, demonstrating how open data can expose hidden social disparities and hold institutions accountable for societal well-being. The crisis disproportionately affects rural areas and specific highland and jungle regions, creating stark inequalities between urban and non-urban populations. By identifying these geographic hotspots, the data serves as a vital tool for policymakers and civil society to advocate for targeted resource allocation. The availability of such detailed, localized statistics enables communities and organizations to pressure government agencies to address structural imbalances, ensuring that transparency leads to more equitable distribution of public funds. Ultimately, the methodology used to define poverty—based on the cost of a basic consumption basket—illustrates how open government data can standardize complex social metrics into accessible indicators. When these datasets are freely available, they empower researchers and journalists to verify claims and drive informed public debate. This transparency is essential for fostering trust in statistical institutions and ensuring that social policies are grounded in evidence rather than speculation, thereby strengthening democratic oversight and effective governance.
