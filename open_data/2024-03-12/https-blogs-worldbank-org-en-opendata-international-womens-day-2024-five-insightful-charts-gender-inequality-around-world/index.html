The World Bank’s 2030 Gender Strategy highlights that achieving gender parity is essential for ending poverty and ensuring a sustainable planet. Current data reveals that gender-based violence remains a pervasive crisis, severely impacting survivors’ health, education, and economic stability regardless of national income levels. This systemic inequality undermines global human rights and development goals, demanding immediate and targeted interventions to protect vulnerable populations. Economic barriers continue to restrict women’s full participation in the labor market. The disproportionate burden of unpaid care work traps women in informal, low-paying roles or excludes them from employment entirely, limiting their financial independence and potential for skill development. Addressing this time poverty is critical for improving labor market outcomes and recognizing the true value of domestic contributions to the global economy. Furthermore, women remain significantly underrepresented in leadership positions within both national governance and corporate sectors. Closing these gaps is not merely a matter of fairness but a strategic imperative, as diverse leadership enhances resource management, economic resilience, and competitiveness. This article is vital to open data advocates because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistics are necessary to track progress, identify disparities, and drive evidence-based policy solutions for gender equality.
