Devin Scillian: Gretchen Whitmer, state lawmakers must accept transparency laws | Opinion

Devin Scillian: Gretchen Whitmer, state lawmakers must accept transparency laws | Opinion

The article highlights the critical, yet often undervalued, importance of government transparency during Sunshine Week, framing it not merely as a journalistic concern but as a fundamental pillar of democratic accountability. It emphasizes that when government operations remain hidden, public trust erodes and corruption thrives, making the right to access public records essential for an informed citizenry. The narrative suggests that true openness requires more than symbolic gestures; it demands robust legal frameworks that ensure all branches of government are subject to the same scrutiny as local entities. A central argument focuses on Michigan’s unique failure to hold its governor and legislators accountable under Freedom of Information Act laws. While lower-level public offices must disclose records, the state’s top leaders remain exempt, a disparity that has kept Michigan ranked poorly in ethics and openness for over a decade. Despite bipartisan legislative efforts and past promises from the governor to embrace transparency voluntarily, these measures have largely failed to materialize. This exclusion creates a dangerous precedent where the most powerful officials operate without the checks and balances applied to the rest of government, undermining the principle that sunlight is the best disinfectant. Finally, the piece warns that traditional transparency tools are insufficient against emerging threats like cryptocurrency in political financing. Crypto transactions offer a level of anonymity that evades standard public records requests, creating a "dark money" loophole that complicates oversight. The article concludes by urging citizens to actively demand transparency from their representatives, arguing that open data and accessible records are not just journalistic luxuries but necessary safeguards against corruption and hidden influence in a modern democracy.
