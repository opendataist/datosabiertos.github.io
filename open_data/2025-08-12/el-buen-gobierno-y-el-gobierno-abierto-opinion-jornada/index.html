El buen gobierno y el gobierno abierto | Opinión | Jornada

El buen gobierno y el gobierno abierto | Opinión | Jornada

The article concludes that Peru suffers from a fragile democracy due to the systematic failure to implement "Open Government" principles, despite legal frameworks established since 2003. This chronic non-compliance creates an unconstitutional state where transparency and civic education are ignored, resulting in a governance model that prioritizes political survival over public accountability and effective decentralization. This disconnect highlights a critical gap in open data practice, as the lack of transparency and citizen participation undermines the quality of public administration and electoral processes. Political actors and institutions consistently neglect these obligations, perpetuating a cycle where citizens are treated as mere voters rather than engaged participants. This erosion of trust prevents the development of a robust culture of civic responsibility and hinders the modernization of the public sector through accessible and usable government information. The relevance to open data lies in the urgent need to transform abstract legal mandates into tangible practices of transparency and data accessibility. Without genuine openness, the principles of citizen participation and institutional memory cannot function, leaving democracy vulnerable to manipulation and inefficiency. Strengthening open data is not merely a technical requirement but a foundational step toward restoring democratic legitimacy, ensuring that government actions are visible, understandable, and subject to meaningful public scrutiny and engagement.
