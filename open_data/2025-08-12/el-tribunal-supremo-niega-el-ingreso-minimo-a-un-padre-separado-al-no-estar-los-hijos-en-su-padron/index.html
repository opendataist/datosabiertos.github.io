El Tribunal Supremo niega el Ingreso Mínimo a un padre separado al no estar los hijos en su padrón

El Tribunal Supremo niega el Ingreso Mínimo a un padre separado al no estar los hijos en su padrón

The Spanish Supreme Court has ruled that a father with shared custody cannot claim the Minimum Vital Income as an adult accompanied by two minors, because the children are not registered at his address but with the mother. This decision clarifies that legal provisions strictly tie the assessment of the benefit amount to the domicile of the minors, overriding arguments about shared physical time. The judgment emphasizes the literal application of current regulations, which dictate that children in shared custody belong to the family unit where they are officially domiciled. Consequently, there is no room for alternative interpretations regarding how the benefit is calculated or assigned, ensuring administrative consistency based on official registration data rather than living arrangements. This case is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical importance of accurate, accessible, and interoperable civil registry data in determining social rights. It demonstrates how administrative decisions rely on specific, verifiable data points, such as municipal registration, to apply laws fairly. For open data initiatives, this underscores the need for reliable public datasets that support transparent and legally sound social welfare distributions.
