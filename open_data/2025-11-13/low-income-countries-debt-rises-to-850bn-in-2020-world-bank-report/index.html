Low-income countries’ debt rises to $850bn in 2020- World Bank report

Low-income countries’ debt rises to $850bn in 2020- World Bank report

The World Bank’s 2022 report highlights a critical surge in external debt among low- and middle-income nations during the pandemic year of 2020. This financial strain was exacerbated by massive global stimulus packages intended to mitigate health emergencies and economic fallout, yet many countries were already vulnerable due to pre-existing slow growth and elevated debt levels. Consequently, debt-to-income and debt-to-export ratios worsened significantly, outpacing economic growth and signaling widespread deterioration in debt sustainability indicators across all regions. In response to this crisis, international bodies initiated temporary measures like the Debt Service Suspension Initiative and established a Common Framework for Debt Treatments to assist eligible countries. Despite the rising burden, net inflows from multilateral and bilateral creditors reached decade-high levels, providing essential liquidity. However, experts emphasize that temporary relief is insufficient; a comprehensive approach involving debt reduction, swifter restructuring, and improved transparency is urgently needed to prevent future debt distress and ensure long-term economic stability. This data is vital for the open data community because the International Debt Statistics report offers a standardized, longitudinal view of external borrowing trends for over 120 countries. By making aggregate and country-specific debt metrics publicly available, the report enables analysts and policymakers to monitor lending sources, assess comparability, and identify emerging risks. Such transparency supports evidence-based decision-making, allowing stakeholders to scrutinize financial health and advocate for equitable debt management policies globally.
