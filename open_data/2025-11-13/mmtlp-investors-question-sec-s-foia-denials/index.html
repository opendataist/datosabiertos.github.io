MMTLP Investors Question SEC's FOIA Denials

MMTLP Investors Question SEC's FOIA Denials

The article reveals that the SEC outsourced its handling of an unprecedented wave of Freedom of Information Act requests to C2 Alaska, a contractor tied to an Alaska Native corporation. During a controversy surrounding the failed Meta Materials stock scheme, the agency relied on this external workforce to manage the influx of inquiries from distressed retail investors. This reliance highlights a growing trend where government transparency mechanisms are increasingly mediated by private contractors who operate under unique regulatory exemptions allowing for sole-source awards. Crucially, the use of this contractor resulted in a stark disparity in FOIA outcomes compared to standard requests. While the SEC successfully cleared its general backlog, the specific requests related to the MMTLP case were overwhelmingly denied or heavily redacted using broad privacy and law-enforcement exemptions. This suggests that the outsourcing model may prioritize administrative efficiency and legal protection over genuine public access to information, effectively shielding regulatory decisions from scrutiny during crises. This case is highly relevant to open data and open government principles as it exposes how structural incentives can undermine transparency. It demonstrates that without robust oversight of third-party processors, agencies may exploit contractual relationships to limit accountability. The incident serves as a cautionary tale about the need for clear standards governing how outsourced labor interacts with public records laws, ensuring that cost-saving measures do not come at the expense of citizen access to governmental data.
