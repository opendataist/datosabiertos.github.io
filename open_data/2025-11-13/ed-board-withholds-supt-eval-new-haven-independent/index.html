Ed Board Withholds Supt. Eval - New Haven Independent

Ed Board Withholds Supt. Eval - New Haven Independent

The New Haven Public Schools’ refusal to release Superintendent Madeline Negrón’s performance evaluation highlights a critical transparency gap in public education governance. By withholding the document and obscuring whether a written record exists, the district evades public scrutiny, raising concerns about accountability in administrative decision-making. This situation underscores the tension between internal board deliberations and the public’s right to access official records. This case illustrates a broader trend where school boards attempt to circumvent open-records laws by conducting evaluations exclusively in private sessions without documenting outcomes. Such practices undermine democratic principles by preventing stakeholders from understanding how leaders are assessed and held accountable. The struggle to obtain this single evaluation reveals systemic efforts to shield executive performance reviews from public view, challenging the integrity of public institutional oversight. This incident is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how digital and bureaucratic opacity can obstruct access to essential public information. True open data requires more than just availability; it demands proactive transparency and clear documentation standards. When institutions hide evaluations behind procedural ambiguity, they erode trust and hinder meaningful civic engagement, emphasizing the urgent need for robust data-sharing policies that prioritize public access over administrative convenience.
