The Download: waiting at the US border, and seaweed’s carbon capture shortcomings

The global shift from cash to digital payments risks disenfranchising vulnerable populations, yet smartphone saturation offers a path toward financial inclusion if implemented correctly. The critical issue is whether this transition benefits humanity broadly or deepens existing inequities. This topic matters to open data because transparent, accessible datasets on financial transactions can reveal how digital systems impact different socioeconomic groups. Open data enables researchers to monitor inclusion efforts and hold systems accountable, ensuring technology serves equity rather than exclusion. Ultimately, providing public access to payment data allows societies to evaluate whether digital money bridges or widens the wealth gap. By analyzing these patterns openly, we can advocate for policies that maximize the positive potential of digital finance for all.
