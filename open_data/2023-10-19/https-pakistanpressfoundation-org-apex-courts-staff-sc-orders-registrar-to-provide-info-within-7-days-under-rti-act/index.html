The Supreme Court of Pakistan has reversed a lower court’s decision, ruling that it must provide specific staff and demographic data to a citizen under its constitutional right to access information. Although the Attorney General argued that the statutory Right to Information Act does not apply to the Court because it is established by the Constitution rather than federal law, the bench emphasized that constitutional guarantees supersede statutory limitations. This landmark ruling affirms that the judiciary is not exempt from transparency obligations, reinforcing the principle that access to information is a fundamental right protected under Article 19A. The judgment highlights a critical gap in current regulations, noting that while the Constitution mandates transparency, there are no specific laws or internal regulations governing how the Supreme Court must handle such requests. The Court stressed that withholding information requires justifiable reasons related to public interest, which were absent in this case. By referencing historical precedents of accountability and the modern benefits of administrative efficiency, the ruling suggests that open data initiatives contribute significantly to economic growth and institutional self-accountability, setting a new standard for how public bodies operate. This development is highly relevant to the open data community as it establishes a legal precedent for judicial transparency in Pakistan. It demonstrates that constitutional rights can compel institutions to disclose operational details, even in the absence of specific implementing legislation. The case underscores the importance of aligning statutory frameworks with constitutional mandates to ensure comprehensive accountability. Ultimately, it encourages other public bodies to adopt proactive transparency measures, fostering a culture where data disclosure is the norm rather than the exception, thereby enhancing public trust and democratic engagement.
