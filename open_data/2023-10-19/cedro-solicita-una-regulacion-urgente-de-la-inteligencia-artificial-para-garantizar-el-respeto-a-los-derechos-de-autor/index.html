CEDRO solicita una regulación urgente de la inteligencia artificial para garantizar el respeto a los derechos de autor

Carme Riera points out that authors and publishers are deeply concerned about AI platforms using their works without authorization to train systems. This unauthorized use threatens creators’ right to fair compensation and their intellectual property rights, creating a conflict between technological progress and economic justice for the creative sector. CEDRO calls for immediate regulatory frameworks that ensure transparency, neutrality, and ethical AI development. They seek legal guarantees that protect copyright while preventing algorithmic bias and discrimination, urging political parties to incorporate these standards into upcoming legislation aligned with European norms. This issue is critical for open data because it challenges the current assumption that all publicly available digital content is free for AI training. It underscores the necessity of integrating copyright compliance and ethical governance into open data practices, ensuring that the liberation of information does not come at the expense of creators’ rights and sustainable cultural ecosystems.
