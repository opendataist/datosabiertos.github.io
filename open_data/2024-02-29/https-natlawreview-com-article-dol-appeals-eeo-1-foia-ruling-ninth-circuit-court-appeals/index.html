The Department of Labor is appealing a court order mandating the release of contractors’ EEO-1 diversity reports, arguing they contain proprietary commercial data exempt from disclosure. This legal battle highlights the tension between government transparency and the protection of sensitive corporate information submitted for regulatory compliance. The core dispute centers on whether diversity metrics constitute trade secrets or hold inherent commercial significance. By challenging the lower court’s interpretation, the DOL seeks to establish that such data warrants privacy protections under FOIA exemptions, setting a potential precedent for how employment statistics are treated under federal law. This case is vital to open data initiatives as it underscores the fragility of public access to workforce demographics. If the appeal succeeds, it may restrict the availability of granular diversity data, impacting researchers and advocates who rely on these figures to analyze labor equity and inform policy decisions.
