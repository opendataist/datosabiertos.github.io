Censo revela precariedad del personal de seguridad pública en los municipios; carecen de prestaciones

The article reveals that while municipal police salaries in Mexico have improved significantly between 2012 and 2022, critical gaps in social protections persist. The vast majority of officers lack essential benefits such as retirement funds, life insurance, and medical coverage. This structural vulnerability exacerbates the sector’s high turnover, where replacements struggle to fill the void left by frequent resignations, deaths, or disappearances. Consequently, municipalities face severe institutional weaknesses as they rely on external hiring with steep learning curves to maintain security operations. This data is vital for the open data movement because it underscores the necessity of transparent, detailed public sector statistics beyond mere headcounts. By exposing the disparity between improved wages and missing benefits, the census highlights how granular data can drive policy reforms aimed at human rights and retention. Ultimately, making these specific indicators publicly accessible empowers citizens and researchers to hold governments accountable for the actual working conditions and sustainability of public safety forces.
