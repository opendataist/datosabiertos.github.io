Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical character recognition transforms static images into machine-readable data, bridging the gap between physical print media and digital ecosystems. By automating the conversion of documents like identity papers, it enables businesses to seamlessly integrate textual information into analytical workflows, enhancing operational efficiency and productivity without manual intervention. The technology relies on sophisticated software algorithms that analyze scanned bitmaps, distinguishing characters from backgrounds through pattern matching or feature extraction. This technical precision allows for the accurate translation of glyphs into digital formats, supporting applications ranging from automated form completion to complex data analytics, provided the source documents use compatible standard fonts. For open data initiatives, OCR is vital because it liberates information trapped in unstructured visual formats, making it accessible for public scrutiny and reuse. Converting scanned records into raw, editable text facilitates transparency in government and corporate data, allowing citizens and developers to analyze historical records, verify identities, and build innovative services based on previously inaccessible digital assets.
