The article highlights a critical paradox in Mexico’s municipal public security: while the proportion of personnel has risen, their working conditions have severely deteriorated. The vast majority of officers lack basic labor rights and essential social security benefits, such as healthcare and retirement savings. This precariousness creates a fragile workforce that is ill-equipped to handle the inherent risks of their profession, exposing institutional weaknesses in how public security is managed and supported at the local level. Furthermore, there is a significant discrepancy between the number of officers leaving service and those entering the force. With retention rates plummeting due to high turnover from retirement, death, or disappearance, municipalities struggle to replenish their ranks effectively. The shortage of trained replacements exacerbates existing vulnerabilities, as the influx of new recruits cannot keep pace with the rapid attrition, ultimately compromising the continuity and effectiveness of local law enforcement operations. This data is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how transparent, census-based information can expose systemic governance failures. By making these statistics publicly accessible, citizens and policymakers can demand accountability and evidence-based reforms. Open data serves as a crucial tool for diagnosing institutional crises, moving the conversation beyond anecdotal reports to concrete metrics that justify the urgent need for structural improvements in public security management and labor standards.
