FOIA CONFIRMS: The FBI And The DOJ Uncovered A Massive 2020 Election Ballot Fraud In Michigan

The article claims that Freedom of Information Act requests have revealed that the FBI and Department of Justice identified significant ballot fraud during the 2020 election in Michigan, specifically involving falsified applications linked to the Governor’s campaign. It further alleges that federal authorities suppressed this evidence by transferring the case to state officials who were politically aligned with the accused, thereby preventing legal consequences. The narrative highlights a perceived conspiracy among federal agencies and media outlets to dismiss credible allegations of election irregularities as baseless, drawing parallels to other disputed political narratives. Commenters express frustration that despite alleged discoveries by law enforcement, no arrests were made and the findings were effectively buried, suggesting a systemic effort to protect political interests and maintain the status quo regarding election integrity. This discussion is relevant to open data because it illustrates the tension between transparency mechanisms, such as FOIA, and institutional opacity. It underscores the critical need for independent verification of government-held information and raises questions about how data accessibility can be hindered by bureaucratic decisions, ultimately impacting public trust in democratic institutions and the reliability of official records.
