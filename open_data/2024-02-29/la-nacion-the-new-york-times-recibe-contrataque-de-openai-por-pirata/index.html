La Nación / The New York Times recibe contrataque de OpenAI “por pirata”

OpenAI and The New York Times are embroiled in a crucial lawsuit over copyright in the era of generative artificial intelligence. The California-based company argues that the newspaper violated its terms of use by extracting content, questioning the legality of monopolizing knowledge or the rules of language. This dispute is not merely a corporate conflict but a landmark case that will define how data is processed and protected in AI systems. The case illustrates the fundamental tension between technological innovation and the protection of intellectual property. OpenAI maintains that using copyrighted material to create new products is legal, while the newspaper fears that this practice will discourage the creation of quality journalism by undermining its business model. This interaction highlights the need to clarify how machine learning models interact with intellectual property, a central issue for transparency and ethics in technological development. The relevance to open data lies in the importance of establishing clear frameworks for the access and use of information. If large corporations can use copyrighted content without clear restrictions, the principle of free and equitable access is undermined. This trial could influence future policies that determine whether data and knowledge should remain closed or be made accessible, directly affecting the culture of open data and the sustainability of public information sources.
