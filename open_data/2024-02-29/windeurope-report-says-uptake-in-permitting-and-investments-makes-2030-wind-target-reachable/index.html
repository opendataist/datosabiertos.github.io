WindEurope Report Says Uptake in Permitting and Investments Makes 2030 Wind Target Reachable

In 2023, the European Union achieved a record installation of new wind energy capacity, driven by improved permitting processes, a more favorable investment climate, and strong political commitment through the Wind Power Package. This momentum suggests that while the overall climate targets may be slightly missed, the industry is on track to significantly increase its share of the electricity mix, provided that critical infrastructure bottlenecks are addressed. The most pressing challenge identified is the delay in grid connectivity, which has become the primary obstacle to accelerating wind deployment. Hundreds of gigawatts of new capacity are stuck in connection queues, risking delays in commissioning and undermining the urgency of the energy transition. Consequently, accelerating the expansion of both onshore and offshore electricity grids, along with supporting transport infrastructure, is now identified as the top priority for ensuring timely project completion. This report is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the necessity of transparent, accessible data on permitting, project pipelines, and grid status to enable effective policy-making and investment. The improvement in regulatory frameworks, such as those under the Net-Zero Industry Act, relies on accurate public information to track progress, enforce criteria, and coordinate efforts across member states. Open data serves as the backbone for monitoring these complex transitions, holding governments and industries accountable, and facilitating the collaborative planning needed to overcome infrastructure bottlenecks.
