More than 4,700 short-term lets license applications received by councils

Scotland’s new short-term letting licensing scheme has successfully transitioned from implementation to active regulation, validating thousands of applications by mid-2023. The data reveals a distinct geographic divide, with rural and island communities submitting the vast majority of requests, while major urban centers like Edinburgh and Glasgow show significantly lower uptake. This disparity suggests that regulatory pressures and housing market dynamics impact different regions unevenly, highlighting the need for localized understanding in policy enforcement. The licensing process is currently characterized by a high volume of pending decisions, with nearly half of the validated applications still awaiting determination. Most applicants are existing hosts securing provisional status rather than new market entrants, indicating that the scheme is primarily formalizing current practices rather than stimulating new short-term rental investment. The near absence of refusals underscores a collaborative, albeit cautious, approach by local authorities to ensure compliance without immediately disrupting the existing accommodation supply. This dataset is crucial for open data initiatives as it provides the first comprehensive, standardized view of Scotland’s short-term rental landscape. By making these validation and licensing metrics publicly available, stakeholders can analyze housing supply, tourism impact, and regulatory effectiveness with unprecedented granularity. This transparency supports evidence-based policymaking and allows researchers to assess how legislative changes influence the balance between housing availability and tourism economies.
