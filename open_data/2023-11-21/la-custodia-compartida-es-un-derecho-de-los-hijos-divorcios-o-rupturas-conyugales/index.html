La custodia compartida es un derecho de los hijos. Divorcios o rupturas conyugales

The article emphasizes that family ruptures in Spain involve significant social and psychological complexities, particularly affecting children’s well-being. It highlights that while statistics show a high volume of separations, the human reality involves disrupted development and vulnerability, especially in cases involving gender-based violence or contested custody. The core conclusion is that children’s rights must be prioritized over adult conflicts, ensuring they remain children rather than being burdened with adult decisions or conflict mediation. A critical perspective is offered on custody arrangements, distinguishing between the legal concept of shared custody and the best interests of the child. The text argues that shared custody should not be imposed if it exposes minors to ongoing parental conflict. Instead, the focus must be on maintaining stable emotional, social, and educational environments for the children. Legal frameworks are cited to support the protection of children’s dignity, privacy, and right to be heard, ensuring these rights are not compromised during the restructuring of family dynamics. Relevance to open data lies in the necessity of using high-quality, disaggregated statistical data to inform public policy and social work interventions. By analyzing trends in family ruptures, authorities can better allocate resources such as meeting points and family therapy services. Transparent data allows for identifying at-risk groups, such as children losing access to school meals or facing violence, thereby enabling targeted support systems. Open data facilitates evidence-based decisions that protect vulnerable populations and improve the efficiency of social services in managing complex family situations.
