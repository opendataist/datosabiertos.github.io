Abu Dhabi Reports 7.7% Growth in Non-Oil GDP in Q3

Rising oil prices reflect deep geopolitical instability stemming from stalled US-Iran diplomacy and tensions in the Strait of Hormuz. As diplomatic efforts falter and military threats loom, markets price in significant disruption premiums, highlighting how fragile global energy security remains. This volatility underscores the urgent need for transparent, real-time data on supply chains and political risks to mitigate economic shocks and inform better policy responses in an interconnected world. Simultaneously, speculation regarding a potential US diesel export ban has further unsettled European markets, despite official denials. Experts warn such restrictions could worsen global supply shortages rather than ease prices, demonstrating how opaque trade policies can trigger unintended economic consequences. This scenario illustrates the critical importance of open data in tracking cross-border energy flows, allowing stakeholders to anticipate impacts and demand evidence-based decision-making from governments. This article is relevant to open data because energy markets depend on accessible, accurate information to function efficiently and transparently. When governments withhold data or when diplomatic secrecy obscures trade realities, market participants face heightened uncertainty and inefficiency. Promoting open access to energy statistics, trade flows, and geopolitical indicators empowers researchers, businesses, and citizens to understand systemic risks, advocate for stability, and hold institutions accountable for data transparency in critical infrastructure sectors.
