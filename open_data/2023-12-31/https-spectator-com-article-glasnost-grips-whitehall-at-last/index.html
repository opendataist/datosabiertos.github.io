The article critiques the severe lack of transparency within key UK government bodies, specifically highlighting the Cabinet Office and the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport. Through two recent rulings by the Information Commissioner, it exposes a pattern of excessive bureaucratic resistance to Freedom of Information requests. Officials are shown employing weak justifications, such as invoking personal data protections or national security, to withhold trivial or innocuous information, thereby undermining public trust in governmental openness. These cases demonstrate how public authorities often prioritize secrecy over accountability, failing to adhere to statutory deadlines and even claiming not to hold information they actually possess. The Commissioner’s decisions force the release of data that should have been readily available, signaling that such obstructionist tactics are no longer tolerated. This serves as a stern warning to Whitehall that robust scrutiny applies to all departments, regardless of their perceived importance or the sensitivity of the withheld content. This narrative is highly relevant to open data advocates because it underscores the critical importance of legal enforcement mechanisms in achieving true transparency. While open data initiatives aim to make information accessible, these rulings reveal that without strict adherence to FOI laws, institutions may resist disclosure regardless of digital infrastructure. It highlights the need for continuous vigilance and regulatory pressure to ensure that openness remains a practical reality rather than just a theoretical ideal for government operations.
