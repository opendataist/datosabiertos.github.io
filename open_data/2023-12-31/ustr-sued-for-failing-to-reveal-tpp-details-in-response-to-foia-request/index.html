USTR Sued For Failing To Reveal TPP Details In Response To FOIA Request

The article highlights a critical conflict between government secrecy and public accountability regarding the Trans-Pacific Partnership (TPP). William New and Yale students utilized Freedom of Information Act requests to access draft texts, only to be denied on the grounds that the documents were classified as national security information. This justification is widely viewed as dubious, as trade agreements significantly impact public welfare rather than national defense. The resulting lawsuit underscores the urgent need for transparency in international trade negotiations, which directly affects open data advocacy by challenging the arbitrary classification of public interest information as state secrets. USTR officials defended their secrecy by claiming enhanced transparency through consultation with various stakeholders. However, the author argues this definition is fundamentally flawed, confusing information gathering with information sharing. True transparency requires releasing documents to the public, not just listening to internal deliberations. This distinction is vital for open data proponents, as it exposes the rhetorical tactics used to deny public access. By mislabeling consultation as transparency, agencies obscure the actual content of negotiations, preventing meaningful public scrutiny and debate on policy matters that affect innovation and economic outcomes. The rejection of open negotiation precedents suggests a deliberate effort to serve corporate interests over public benefit. The article contrasts the TPP’s secrecy with successful treaties like the Marrakesh Treaty, which were negotiated openly. This comparison reinforces the argument that secrecy is a choice, not a necessity for leverage. For the open data community, this case illustrates the importance of challenging opaque government practices. It demonstrates how legal action can set precedents for information access, ensuring that future agreements are subject to public oversight rather than hidden behind false national security claims.
