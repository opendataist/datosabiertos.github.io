The data reveals that nearly half of Coahuila’s prison population remains detained awaiting trial, highlighting a persistent systemic bottleneck in the justice process. This situation underscores the critical need for open data initiatives to monitor pretrial detention rates and evaluate the effectiveness of judicial reforms aimed at reducing prison overcrowding. The prevalence of preventive detention, particularly the controversial “official” preventive imprisonment, indicates that despite recent legal shifts, significant barriers to liberty remain. Tracking these figures allows policymakers and civil society to assess whether constitutional changes are effectively protecting rights or merely shifting procedural burdens without resolving the underlying inefficiencies. This article is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, standardized statistics are essential for holding institutions accountable. By making such detailed census data accessible, stakeholders can identify patterns of arbitrary detention, advocate for evidence-based policy changes, and ensure that the justice system respects the presumption of innocence rather than defaulting to punitive incarceration.
