Diario HOY | Unas 137 mil personas salieron de la pobreza multidimensional en el 2023

The article highlights the Multidimensional Poverty Measure (IPM) as a crucial official metric that assesses welfare beyond mere income, evaluating access to work, housing, health, and education. This comprehensive approach reveals significant disparities between rural and urban populations, showing that rural areas bear a disproportionately higher burden of poverty. By tracking these diverse dimensions, the IPM offers a nuanced understanding of social well-being that traditional monetary metrics often miss. Recent data indicates a notable reduction in multidimensional poverty, driven primarily by improvements in working hours, access to cleaner cooking fuels, and better sanitation facilities. These specific gains suggest that targeted interventions in infrastructure and labor conditions effectively alleviate non-monetary deprivations. The analysis underscores that economic progress must be complemented by tangible improvements in living standards to achieve sustainable social development. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the value of transparent, multidimensional indicators in shaping public policy. By making detailed, localized data on poverty dimensions available, governments can identify specific gaps and measure the real-world impact of their programs. Such open information empowers citizens and researchers to hold institutions accountable and advocate for evidence-based solutions that address the complex realities of poverty.
