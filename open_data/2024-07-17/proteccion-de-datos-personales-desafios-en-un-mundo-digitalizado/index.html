Protección de datos personales: desafíos en un mundo digitalizado

The article underscores the critical intersection of human rights and data protection during global crises, highlighting that the rising number of armed conflicts and forced displacements exacerbate the vulnerability of refugees and victims of sexual violence. It emphasizes that safeguarding personal data is not merely a technical issue but a fundamental aspect of humanitarian aid, essential for preventing further victimization, exploitation, and discrimination. By protecting sensitive information, authorities can empower these populations and ensure their dignity is preserved in digital environments. The relevance to open data lies in the necessity of balancing transparency with the strict protection of personally identifiable information for marginalized groups. While open data principles promote accountability and accessibility, this context demonstrates that unrestricted data sharing can endanger lives. Therefore, ethical data handling requires specialized frameworks that prioritize privacy and security, ensuring that data initiatives do not inadvertently expose vulnerable individuals to harm. This approach aligns with international standards, such as those established by the Global Privacy Assembly, which advocate for responsible data management in humanitarian settings. Ultimately, the text calls for enhanced digital literacy and the implementation of specific protective guidelines, such as those developed by Mexican authorities, to support migrants and refugees. These efforts illustrate that open data ecosystems must include robust mechanisms for data minimization and consent, particularly when dealing with crisis-affected communities. By integrating privacy-by-design principles into humanitarian responses, stakeholders can foster trust and facilitate safer reintegration, proving that effective data governance is a prerequisite for sustainable human rights protection in an increasingly digital world.
