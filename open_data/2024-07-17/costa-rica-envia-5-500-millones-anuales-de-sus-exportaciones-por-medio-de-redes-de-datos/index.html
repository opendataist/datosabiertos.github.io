Costa Rica envía $5.500 millones anuales de sus exportaciones por medio de redes de datos

The study reveals a significant structural shift in Costa Rica’s economy, driven by the robust growth of information and communication technology (ICT) service exports. This sector has proven resilient, expanding rapidly even during the pandemic, and now constitutes a substantial portion of the nation’s total exports. The data indicates that specialized services, particularly in administration and IT, are the primary engines of this growth, suggesting that the economy is becoming less dependent on traditional goods and more reliant on digital capabilities. This trend highlights a successful transition toward a knowledge-based economy that leverages remote work and digital infrastructure to compete globally. A critical implication of these findings is the concentration of markets, with the vast majority of ICT exports directed solely toward the United States. This heavy reliance on a single destination poses a risk to economic stability, as shocks in that market could disproportionately affect national revenues. The central bank emphasizes the urgent need for policy measures to diversify both products and geographic markets, potentially expanding into Asia and South America. Diversification is presented not just as an economic opportunity, but as a necessary strategy to enhance resilience against external volatility and ensure long-term sustainable growth. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the importance of accurate, accessible statistical frameworks in tracking emerging economic sectors. The use of detailed surveys and cross-validation data allows policymakers to identify trends, such as the rise of the service sector and employment demographics, which are crucial for evidence-based decision-making. By making such insights available, open data facilitates transparency and helps stakeholders understand how digital transformation impacts labor markets and trade balances. Ultimately, it demonstrates how data-driven analysis can guide public policy to mitigate risks associated with market concentration while promoting inclusive economic development through the digital economy.
