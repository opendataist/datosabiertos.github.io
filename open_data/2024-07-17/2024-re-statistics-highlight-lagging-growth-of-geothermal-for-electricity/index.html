2024 RE statistics highlight lagging growth of geothermal for electricity

2024 RE statistics highlight lagging growth of geothermal for electricity

The International Renewable Energy Agency’s 2024 report highlights a significant disparity in renewable energy adoption, where solar and wind power dominate electricity generation while geothermal energy lags behind. Despite geothermal’s consistent, albeit slow, growth in electricity production, the sector holds substantial untapped potential, particularly in the commercial heat market. This insight underscores the need for diverse renewable strategies, as relying solely on variable sources like wind and solar may not address all energy needs, especially for heating applications where geothermal resources offer reliable, lower-temperature solutions. The analysis reveals that renewable heat generation remains heavily reliant on bioenergy, with fossil fuels still supplying the vast majority of global heat. Geothermal energy, alongside solar thermal, represents a crucial but currently underutilized opportunity to decarbonize this sector. Since geothermal can exploit accessible resources for heating, expanding its use in commercial heat markets could significantly increase renewable penetration. This shift is vital for balancing the energy mix and reducing dependence on fossil fuels in industries that require high-temperature or steady-state heat sources, which are difficult to meet with intermittent power alone. However, current capacity additions fall short of the ambitious goal to triple renewable capacity by 2030, indicating that existing growth rates are insufficient to meet climate targets. For open data communities, this report exemplifies the necessity of comprehensive, transparent datasets to monitor progress and identify gaps across different energy sectors. Access to detailed statistics on capacity, generation, and regional contributions enables policymakers and researchers to accurately assess performance, model future scenarios, and advocate for targeted investments. Ultimately, robust open data infrastructure is essential for driving the systemic changes needed to accelerate the global energy transition.
