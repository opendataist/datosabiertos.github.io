Disappearance of victim in Gambia: ECOWAS Court orders Ghana to release documents to applicant - Ghanaian Times

The ECOWAS Court of Justice recently ordered Ghana to release specific documents regarding the 2005 enforced disappearance of a Ghanaian migrant in The Gambia. This ruling addresses the right to information, mandating the state to provide access to investigation reports and financial records within four months. By enforcing this disclosure, the court affirmed that citizens have a legitimate claim to transparency concerning state actions and historical human rights violations, even when those events occurred outside national borders. Crucially, the court declined jurisdiction over the core allegations of enforced disappearance and the right to truth, as the incidents took place in The Gambia. This distinction highlights a significant legal boundary within regional human rights frameworks: while the right to access information can be adjudicated against a state of nationality, substantive violations occurring in other territories remain outside that specific court’s authority. Consequently, applicants seeking compensation or formal investigations into the disappearance itself saw those claims dismissed, limiting the judgment to transparency obligations rather than substantive justice for the victims. This case is vital for open data advocacy as it demonstrates the legal power to compel state disclosure of sensitive government records, including UN and ECOWAS investigation reports. It establishes a precedent for using regional courts to enforce transparency, proving that citizens can demand access to critical documentation about human rights abuses. For open data communities, this underscores the importance of legal frameworks that protect the right to information, ensuring that historical records of displacement and violence are not kept hidden by states, thereby facilitating accountability and public awareness.
