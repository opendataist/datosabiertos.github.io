FREEDOM OF INFORMATION BILL EXPECTED TO BE TABLED IN PARLIAMENT BY YEAR-END - KULASEGARAN

Malaysia is advancing its Freedom of Information Bill, with plans to introduce it in Parliament later this year. This legislative move follows extensive nationwide consultations involving government bodies, civil society, and academics to refine the law’s scope and implementation. The process aims to balance public transparency with necessary exemptions regarding national security and personal privacy, ensuring the framework serves the public interest effectively. The proposed legislation establishes a clear mechanism for citizens to access government records, supported by an independent oversight body comprised of retired judges. This structure addresses concerns about arbitrary denials of information, providing a formal avenue for appeal and ensuring that transparency initiatives remain accessible and beneficial to all individuals. Such institutional safeguards are critical for building trust between the state and its citizens. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates as it formalizes the legal right to access government-held information. By codifying transparency parameters and creating accountability structures, the bill lays the groundwork for a more open government ecosystem. It signals a shift toward systemic data accessibility, empowering civil society and researchers to engage more deeply with public sector information for advocacy and analysis.
