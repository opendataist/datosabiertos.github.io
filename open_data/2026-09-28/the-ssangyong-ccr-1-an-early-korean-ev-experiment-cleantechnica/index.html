The SsangYong CCR-1: An Early Korean EV Experiment - CleanTechnica

The article highlights the historical significance of the SsangYong CCR-1, a pioneering electric prototype developed in the mid-1990s. By prioritizing lightweight materials and aerodynamic efficiency, the project demonstrated that early electric vehicles could overcome battery limitations through holistic engineering. This serves as a crucial case study for open data enthusiasts, illustrating how transparent documentation of failed or abandoned innovations preserves vital technical insights that might otherwise be lost to history. Although economic crises prevented the car’s production, the CCR1 provided essential engineering experience in electric propulsion and composite construction. The detailed technical records from this era reveal how early automotive pioneers approached zero-emission challenges before modern lithium-ion technology existed. These records offer valuable context for understanding the evolutionary trajectory of electric vehicle design, showing that foundational solutions often precede commercial viability. This history is relevant to open data because it underscores the importance of preserving and sharing legacy technical specifications. As the automotive industry transitions toward electrification, having access to comprehensive data on early experiments helps researchers analyze long-term technological trends. By making such historical data accessible, we enable a deeper understanding of how innovation persists despite market failures, fostering a more informed dialogue about sustainable transport development.
