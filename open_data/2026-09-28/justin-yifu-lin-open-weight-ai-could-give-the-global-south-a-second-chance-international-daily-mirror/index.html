Justin Yifu Lin: open-weight AI could give the Global South a second chance - International | Daily Mirror

Justin Yifu Lin: open-weight AI could give the Global South a second chance - International | Daily Mirror

Justin Yifu Lin argues that the fourth industrial revolution offers a unique opportunity for the Global South to bridge the economic gap with the North, provided it effectively leverages accessible AI technologies. Unlike previous industrial eras that excluded developing economies due to high infrastructure costs, the current landscape features open-weight models that lower barriers to entry. This accessibility allows developing nations to adopt and adapt advanced tools without the prohibitive investments required by dominant commercial models, potentially enabling a "latecomer advantage" where countries can apply the latest technology directly to sectors aligned with their comparative advantages. This shift is highly relevant to open data and open-source movements, as the availability of downloadable, adaptable models empowers local developers to create context-specific solutions without relying on restrictive technology transfer agreements. By fostering a portable ecosystem where code and weights can be freely modified, open-source AI democratizes innovation, allowing regions in Africa, Latin America, and Asia to build applications that address local needs. This decentralization challenges the traditional North-centric control of technological infrastructure and promotes a more equitable distribution of computational power and intellectual resources globally. Ultimately, the article highlights that technological access must be paired with institutional reform to ensure lasting benefit. Lin advocates for new global governance structures, such as those proposed by BRICS, that reflect the economic reality of the Global South rather than imposing outdated Northern frameworks. By combining open-source technology with mutual civilizational learning, developing nations can not only improve productivity but also gain a greater voice in shaping the rules that govern the future digital economy, ensuring that the benefits of AI are shared more broadly.
