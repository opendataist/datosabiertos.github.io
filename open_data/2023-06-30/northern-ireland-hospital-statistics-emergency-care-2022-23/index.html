Northern Ireland Hospital Statistics: Emergency Care (2022/23)

Emergency department statistics for Northern Ireland reveal a significant decline in operational efficiency, characterized by rising wait times despite fluctuating patient volumes. The data indicates that while overall attendance has decreased over the last five years, the proportion of patients waiting over four hours has worsened substantially. Notably, long waits exceeding twelve hours have surged, suggesting systemic bottlenecks in care delivery that persist despite slight year-over-year increases in visit numbers. Ambulance response performance also fell short of established targets, failing to meet critical time benchmarks for emergency calls. This consistent underperformance highlights broader pressures on the healthcare infrastructure, where increased demand and resource constraints contribute to delayed interventions. The inability to sustain acceptable response rates underscores the growing challenge of maintaining timely emergency care during periods of heightened service usage. This report is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates how transparent, longitudinal health metrics can expose critical service failures. By making these statistics publicly available, stakeholders can analyze trends in emergency care accessibility and identify areas requiring urgent policy reform. Such data-driven insights empower communities and policymakers to advocate for better resource allocation and improved patient outcomes in public health systems.
