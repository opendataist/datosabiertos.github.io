Editorial: Graziano should save taxpayers' money, release Komoroski tapes

The Charleston County Sheriff’s Office’s refusal to release inmate conversation recordings highlights a systemic failure to adhere to South Carolina’s Freedom of Information Act. By denying the request based on prior unauthorized disclosure to another news outlet, the agency prioritizes secrecy over statutory compliance, undermining public trust and transparency. Gray Media Group’s decision to sue represents a critical step in enforcing accountability. Since legal victories are common yet rare due to the high cost of litigation, this lawsuit challenges the notion that government agencies can ignore open records laws with impunity. It underscores the necessity for private entities to actively defend the right to access government information. This situation is vital to open_data discussions as it illustrates the barriers preventing the free flow of public information. When agencies withhold data without legal justification, it hinders civic engagement and obscures government operations. Ensuring strict adherence to transparency laws is essential for maintaining an informed citizenry and fostering trust in public institutions.
