Harassment of female journalists still widespread in workplace - Survey

Harassment of female journalists still widespread in workplace - Survey

A recent African Freedom of Information Centre report highlights the persistent safety crisis facing female journalists in East Africa, revealing that sexual harassment and cyberbullying remain rampant despite existing policies. The study demonstrates a significant gender gap in workplace safety, where women face unique threats from editors, sources, and politicians. Crucially, these findings expose a systemic failure in policy implementation, as many safety measures remain redundant and ineffective in protecting women from harassment. The consequences of this insecure environment include widespread self-censorship, particularly in Tanzania, and a culture of silence in Kenya where most victims fear professional repercussions for reporting abuse. This climate not only harms individual journalists but also perpetuates male-dominated newsrooms, limiting diverse voices. The report underscores that without robust enforcement and sensitization of media houses, gender-responsive laws will continue to fail, leaving women vulnerable to unchecked misconduct and intimidation. This article is highly relevant to open data advocates because it illustrates the critical link between transparent oversight and human rights. Reliable, disaggregated data on workplace safety is essential for exposing these disparities and driving accountability. By analyzing survey data through UNESCO indicators, the report provides a model for using evidence-based metrics to advocate for stronger legal frameworks and institutional reforms, emphasizing that open, accessible data is a powerful tool for protecting freedom of expression and ensuring equitable working conditions in the media sector.
