Authors & Copyright Scholars Back 'Internet Archive' in Landmark Legal Battle * TorrentFreak

Authors & Copyright Scholars Back 'Internet Archive' in Landmark Legal Battle * TorrentFreak

The Internet Archive faces a critical legal challenge regarding its Controlled Digital Lending model, which allows libraries to scan and lend digitized copies of books they physically own. While a lower court ruled this practice constitutes copyright infringement, the Archive is appealing, arguing that its services are essential for preserving digital history and maintaining a free, open internet. This battle highlights the tension between traditional copyright enforcement and the modern necessity of digital preservation in an increasingly online world. Supporting the Archive is a coalition of authors and scholars who argue that the current publishing market is imbalanced, with publishers exerting excessive control over access and preservation. They contend that restricting the Archive’s model would diminish public access to knowledge and further consolidate publisher power, undermining the interests of creators who rely on libraries to reach wide audiences. These stakeholders emphasize that fair use protections must extend to digital lending to ensure that libraries can continue their public mission without being stifled by restrictive licensing agreements. This dispute is highly relevant to the open data and open knowledge communities because it tests the legal boundaries of digital ownership and public access. If the ruling against the Archive stands, it could set a precedent that severely limits the ability of non-profit institutions to curate and share digital cultural heritage. The outcome will likely influence how future digital archives operate, potentially restricting the free flow of information and hindering efforts to maintain a comprehensive, accessible record of our collective digital history.
