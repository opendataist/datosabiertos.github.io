Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway experience significantly worse socioeconomic and health outcomes, including lower income, employment challenges, and higher rates of mental distress and loneliness. Despite these tangible hardships, the population remains equally satisfied with life in Norway, demonstrating a paradoxical resilience where objective disadvantages do not diminish subjective well-being. Crucially, immigrants exhibit notably higher trust in state institutions, such as the political system, judiciary, and police, compared to the general population. This strong affiliation suggests that while structural integration faces hurdles, immigrants maintain a profound confidence in the Nordic social contract and governance, reinforcing the stability of the societal framework. This report is vital for open data as it provides a comprehensive, publicly accessible dataset that challenges simplistic narratives about immigrant integration. By highlighting the disparity between poor living conditions and high institutional trust, it enables researchers and policymakers to analyze complex social dynamics. Such transparent data fosters evidence-based discussions on how to improve material conditions without eroding the significant social capital immigrants contribute to Norwegian society.
