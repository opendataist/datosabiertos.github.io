Fecundidad en Costa Rica toca mínimo histórico: apenas 1,12 hijos por mujer en 2024

Costa Rica has entered a phase of ultralow fertility, marking a significant demographic shift that challenges future generational sustainability. This sustained decline over the last decade reflects profound sociocultural and economic transformations, indicating that the nation must adapt to an aging population and shrinking workforce. Simultaneously, the country faces rising mortality rates, including maternal and infant deaths. These increasing health challenges complicate the demographic transition, creating a dual pressure of fewer births and higher death rates. This complex environment underscores the urgent need for integrated public health strategies to address the wellbeing of families and vulnerable groups. This article is relevant to open_data because it highlights how authoritative, transparent statistical reports from institutions like INEC drive evidence-based policy. By making detailed demographic and mortality data publicly accessible, governments enable researchers and citizens to analyze trends, hold leaders accountable, and design solutions for sustainability. Open data serves as the foundation for understanding and responding to critical societal changes.
