Inflation in Mexico is primarily driven by rising prices for fruits and vegetables, resulting from adverse weather conditions and global conflicts. This price increase, along with higher costs for transportation and tourism services, has exceeded analysts' expectations, putting upward pressure on the cost of living. The relevance to open data lies in the need to monitor these economic fluctuations through accessible, up-to-date public statistics. Transparency in price indicators enables citizens and experts to analyze the underlying causes of inflation. This facilitates informed decision-making regarding monetary and wage policies. Without access to high-quality open data, it is difficult to assess the real impact of external factors on the local economy or to anticipate interest rate changes, which are essential for financial stability.
