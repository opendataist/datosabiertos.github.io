Sellos discográficos demandan a sitios que generan música con IA

The emergence of generative music AI platforms, such as Suno and Udio, has triggered an aggressive response from the record industry, which has filed lawsuits in federal courts. The companies allege that these tools train their models using copyrighted works without permission, producing songs that inappropriately mimic the style and voice of recognized artists. This practice threatens to devalue human labor and engage in unfair competition with original creators. This conflict underscores the urgency of addressing transparency in the datasets used to train artificial intelligence models. The industry is seeking millions in damages, highlighting the legal and economic risks associated with the unauthorized use of content. For the open data field, this case is crucial because it emphasizes the need for ethical and legally clear training sources. Promoting access to data free from intellectual property restrictions is essential for developing technologies that respect authorship and avoid lawsuits that could limit open and responsible innovation.
