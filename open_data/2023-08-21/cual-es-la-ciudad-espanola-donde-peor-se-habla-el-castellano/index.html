¿Cuál es la ciudad española donde peor se habla el castellano?

The article highlights significant regional disparities in Spanish language proficiency across Spain, identifying Melilla and Murcia as areas with the lowest command of the official language due to linguistic diversity and external influences. Conversely, northern regions like Asturias and Cantabria demonstrate the highest mastery, challenging stereotypes about southern speech patterns. These variations illustrate that while Spanish remains the dominant global language, its usage and quality are deeply rooted in local sociolinguistic contexts, coexisting with other regional tongues. Regarding open data, this analysis demonstrates the critical importance of granular, georeferenced statistical data for understanding social realities. By breaking down national averages into municipal and regional metrics, the INE provides a nuanced view that broad statistics often obscure. This level of detail is essential for policymakers and researchers aiming to address educational gaps or cultural preservation, showing how accessible datasets can reveal hidden inequalities and inform targeted interventions. Furthermore, the study underscores the value of tracking not just the dominant language, but also the status of co-official and vernacular languages. Open data initiatives that capture information on linguistic diversity and multilingualism are vital for preserving cultural heritage and promoting inclusive education. By making such specific data publicly available, society can better understand the complex relationship between language, identity, and integration, fostering a more informed and multilingual citizenry.
