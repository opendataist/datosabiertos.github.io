The disclosure of presenters’ salaries at RTVE highlights the tension between public transparency and institutional resistance to sharing financial data. Through a request made under the Transparency Law, the remuneration of external professionals was revealed, exposing significant pay gaps and demonstrating that citizens have the right to know how public resources are used, even when the corporation initially attempted to withhold this information. The relevance of this case for the open data community lies in the effective application of open government principles in the face of administrative opacity. The article illustrates how access-to-information mechanisms can compel public entities to be accountable, serving as a critical precedent to show that transparency is not optional but a legal obligation that ensures trust in institutions funded by the state. However, the situation becomes more complex due to the discrimination between external staff and permanent employees, whose information remains hidden under the guise of data protection. Although the Transparency Council ruled in favor of disclosure, prioritizing the public interest over privacy, RTVE has ignored the ruling, underscoring the urgent need to strengthen compliance mechanisms. This non-compliance poses a significant challenge to the integrity of open data, as selective disclosure of information undermines equity and democratic accountability.
