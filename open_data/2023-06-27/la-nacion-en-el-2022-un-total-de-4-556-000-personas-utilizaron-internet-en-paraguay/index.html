La Nación / En el 2022, un total de 4.556.000 personas utilizaron internet en Paraguay

The widespread adoption of internet access and communication technologies in Paraguay highlights a significant digital transformation over the last decade. By integrating demographic and socioeconomic variables into its data collection, the National Institute of Statistics reveals that connectivity has shifted from a niche privilege to a ubiquitous utility, fundamentally altering how the population interacts with information and each other. This massive uptake underscores the critical role of accurate statistical monitoring in understanding societal changes driven by technological advancement. A primary implication of these findings is the persistent urban-rural divide in technological access. While overall usage has surged, rural households lag significantly behind their urban counterparts in essential internet connectivity, despite similar access to mobile phones. This disparity suggests that while basic communication tools are nearly universal, robust digital infrastructure remains unevenly distributed. Recognizing this gap is essential for policymakers aiming to ensure equitable digital inclusion, as connectivity levels directly impact access to education, healthcare, and economic opportunities in underserved regions. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how government statistics can expose structural inequalities and inform targeted public policy. By making detailed breakdowns of usage by age, gender, and location publicly available, authorities enable researchers and citizens to analyze the digital divide beyond aggregate numbers. Such transparency fosters accountability and encourages the development of data-driven solutions to bridge technological gaps, ensuring that digital progress benefits all segments of society rather than concentrating only in urban centers.
