Crime statistics in the Sligo-Leitrim division reveal a complex shift in public safety trends, with violent offenses such as sexual assaults, robberies, and property damage rising significantly. This increase contrasts with a notable decline in other criminal categories, including thefts without violence, fraud, and drug offenses. The divergence highlights the multifaceted nature of local crime dynamics, indicating that while physical threats have escalated, non-violent economic and substance-related crimes have decreased. The reliability of these figures is particularly relevant to open data initiatives because their publication was previously suspended due to significant irregularities in how police recorded crimes. An independent audit revealed that a substantial percentage of reported incidents were either missing from the database or misclassified, undermining the integrity of official statistics. This incident underscores the critical need for rigorous data validation and transparency in public sector reporting to ensure that open datasets accurately reflect reality. For the open data community, this case serves as a vital reminder that raw statistics often require extensive context and quality assurance processes before they can be trusted for public analysis. The resumption of data publication only after a comprehensive review of the underlying systems demonstrates that accessible data must be both available and accurate. Without such safeguards, open data efforts risk propagating errors, thereby reducing their value for policymakers, researchers, and citizens seeking to understand societal trends.
