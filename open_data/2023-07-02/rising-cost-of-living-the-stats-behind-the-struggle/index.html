Rising cost of living: The stats behind the struggle

Jersey’s households are significantly worse off than during the pandemic, as soaring inflation and housing costs have outpaced income growth. Real-term earnings have declined substantially over the last decade, with rising living expenses effectively erasing the protective effect of social benefits. This economic pressure has pushed a quarter of local households into relative low income, revealing a stark deterioration in financial stability for residents compared to pre-lockdown conditions. The data highlights a severe crisis for pensioners, who represent the largest demographic in low-income brackets. Jersey’s rate of pensioner poverty is substantially higher than in the UK and ranks poorly across Europe, indicating that retirement security is failing for many islanders. This disparity underscores how standard social safety nets are insufficient to counteract the specific cost-of-living challenges faced by older residents in this jurisdiction. This report is crucial for open data advocates as it demonstrates how standardized statistical methods enable rigorous cross-border comparisons. By aligning with international definitions, Jersey’s figures allow for direct benchmarking against the UK and broader European contexts, revealing that the Island suffers from extreme income inequality and housing-driven poverty. Such transparent, comparable metrics are essential for holding governments accountable and driving policy reforms aimed at reducing economic disparity.
