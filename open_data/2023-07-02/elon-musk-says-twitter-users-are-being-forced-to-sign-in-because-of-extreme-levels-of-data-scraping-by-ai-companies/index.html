Elon Musk says Twitter users are being forced to sign in because of 'extreme levels' of data scraping by AI companies

Elon Musk has implemented stringent restrictions on Twitter, including mandatory sign-ins and daily viewing limits, to combat aggressive data scraping by artificial intelligence companies. These measures aim to protect Twitter’s intellectual property from unauthorized extraction, which Musk argues fuels the inflated valuations of AI startups while degrading the user experience. By forcing authentication and capping access, the platform seeks to shift power back to its owners and ensure sustainable monetization of its vast data repository. This conflict highlights a critical tension in the open data ecosystem: the clash between unrestricted information access for AI training and the platform’s right to control its own content. The situation demonstrates how social media giants are increasingly treating user-generated content as proprietary assets rather than public goods. Consequently, the era of freely available, open social data for academic or commercial AI development may be ending, replaced by gated, licensed models that prioritize corporate revenue over data openness. The implications for open data are profound, signaling a retreat from the transparency that once characterized the web. As platforms erect barriers to prevent scraping, researchers and developers face significant hurdles in accessing historical and real-time social data without expensive licensing agreements. This trend threatens to fragment the global data landscape, limiting the scope of open-source AI research and reinforcing the dominance of large corporations that can afford to pay for exclusive data rights, thereby reducing diversity and competition in the technology sector.
