The disclosure of widespread private fertility clinics via the Right to Information Act highlights a significant gap in public healthcare infrastructure. This transparency mechanism exposed that private facilities dominate the market, often charging prohibitive fees that burden patients, while government centers remain limited or absent in key regions. The article underscores how data disclosure can reveal systemic inequities in healthcare access. By making hidden market realities public, activists can pressure authorities to address service deficits. This example illustrates that open data is not merely informational but a critical tool for identifying where public investment is urgently needed to prevent exploitation. Consequently, the government is urged to establish affordable public fertility clinics to ensure equitable access. The implication for open data is clear: proactive transparency enables citizens to demand better public services and hold officials accountable. Ultimately, leveraging such data fosters trust and drives policy reforms that prioritize patient welfare over private profit.
