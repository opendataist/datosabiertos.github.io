Internet Archive Loses Appeal In Major Copyright Lawsuit

Internet Archive Loses Appeal In Major Copyright Lawsuit

The US Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit has upheld a ruling against the Internet Archive, confirming that its Controlled Digital Lending program infringes on copyright law. The court determined that mass digitization and lending of copyrighted books without permission deprive creators of revenue and undermine the incentive to produce new works. This decision rejects the Archive’s argument that its model is a necessary digital adaptation of traditional library functions, emphasizing that owning a physical copy does not grant the right to create and distribute unauthorized digital copies. This legal defeat has profound implications for open data and digital preservation efforts. It establishes a critical precedent that challenges the viability of using Controlled Digital Lending as a method for large-scale knowledge sharing. By invalidating this approach, the ruling restricts how institutions can digitize and circulate information, potentially limiting public access to scholarly and cultural materials. Consequently, organizations relying on similar digitization strategies to preserve and share open resources may face significant legal barriers, forcing a reevaluation of how digital archives operate within current copyright frameworks. The relevance to open data lies in the tension between unrestricted access and intellectual property rights. As open data initiatives strive to make information universally available, this case highlights the legal constraints when such efforts involve copyrighted creative works. It serves as a cautionary example for the broader community, illustrating that technical adaptations for accessibility do not automatically exempt them from legal scrutiny. This outcome may influence future policies and operational models for digital libraries, impacting the sustainability of open access projects that balance preservation with compliance.
