The article examines the legal ambiguities surrounding copyright for AI-generated images, concluding that current frameworks are ill-equipped to assign authorship. Since AI lacks legal personhood, determining whether a human contributor meets the threshold for originality remains contested. This uncertainty creates a complex landscape where ownership might fall to developers, users, or simply lapse into the public domain, highlighting a significant gap in existing intellectual property laws regarding autonomous creative systems. Beyond individual authorship, the text emphasizes the systemic risks of AI, particularly the potential erosion of human artistic labor and the privatization of culture. The central concern is not merely job displacement but the consolidation of cultural control through surveillance capitalism and the spread of disinformation. This perspective frames the AI disruption as a democratic crisis, where the lack of clear regulatory boundaries allows corporate interests to exploit creative outputs without adequate accountability or benefit to the original human creators. This discussion is vital to open data because it challenges the assumption that digital content and data models are neutral or freely accessible. The push toward "surveillance capitalism" in the metaverse and AI sectors threatens to enclose knowledge and creative expressions behind proprietary algorithms. Understanding these power dynamics is essential for advocates of open data, as it underscores the need for transparent, democratic governance of digital technologies to prevent the monopolization of cultural and informational resources by a few tech giants.
