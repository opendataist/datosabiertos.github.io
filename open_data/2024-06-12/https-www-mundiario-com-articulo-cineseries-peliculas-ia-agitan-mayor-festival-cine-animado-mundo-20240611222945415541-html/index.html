The Annecy International Animation Film Festival has become the stage for an intense debate on the integration of artificial intelligence into animated cinema. The selection of five films that use AI tools has sparked polarized reactions, raising questions about the impact of this technology on copyright and the future employment of artists. This controversy reflects global concerns about how automation can transform artistic creation and the professional structures of the industry. Despite the criticism, the festival’s leadership defends the inclusion of these works, arguing that technology can support a legitimate artistic vision. They emphasize that the goal is to provoke reflection and dialogue rather than impose early restrictions. This stance seeks to balance technological innovation with the preservation of human creativity, acknowledging that the history of cinema is marked by debates over new tools that eventually become integrated into established artistic practice. This situation is relevant to open data because it underscores the urgency of transparency in the use of algorithms and training data. The discussion highlights the need for clear standards regarding intellectual property and access to information about how these works are generated. Fostering open debate and access to information is crucial for establishing ethical frameworks that protect creators while advancing the evolution of the audiovisual medium.
