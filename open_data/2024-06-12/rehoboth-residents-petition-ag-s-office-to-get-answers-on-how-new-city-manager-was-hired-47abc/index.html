Rehoboth residents petition AG's Office to get answers on how new City Manager was hired - 47abc

A coalition of residents in Rehoboth Beach is demanding transparency regarding the hiring of City Manager Taylour Tedder, citing concerns over his qualifications and compensation. Residents argue that the City Council bypassed established charter requirements for experience and engineering credentials while approving a compensation package that includes an exorbitant salary and a forgivable home loan. This decision was made during closed-door executive sessions, prompting demands for formal answers from the Delaware Attorney General’s Office about how consensus was reached on a hire that appears to contradict municipal code. The conflict highlights a significant tension between local government operations and public accountability. By seeking a formal determination from the Attorney General, the residents are challenging the city’s claim that no laws were broken. The city maintains that it has not violated the Freedom of Information Act and expects the petition to be dismissed. However, the residents insist on uncovering the specific moments when leadership chose to ignore stated qualifications and budgetary constraints, particularly given the city’s recent financial deficits. This case is highly relevant to the open data community as it underscores the critical importance of accessible government records in ensuring democratic oversight. It illustrates how FOIA requests serve as essential tools for citizens to investigate potential misconduct and verify that public officials adhere to their own regulations. The outcome will likely reinforce the necessity of robust transparency mechanisms, demonstrating that open access to hiring processes, salary data, and charter interpretations is vital for preventing arbitrary administrative decisions and maintaining public trust.
