NA weighs new rules on AI data use, news publishers’ rights and IP as collateral

NA weighs new rules on AI data use, news publishers’ rights and IP as collateral

The National Assembly is debating significant amendments to Vietnam’s Intellectual Property Law, highlighting the tension between fostering innovation and preventing financial exploitation. A primary concern involves treating IP as a tradable asset and collateral for loans. Critics warn that allowing self-valuation without rigorous government standards creates loopholes for asset inflation and financial risk. Consequently, there is a strong push for mandatory transparency and independent valuation frameworks to ensure that IP transactions reflect genuine economic value rather than manipulated figures. Simultaneously, the debate addresses the imbalance between traditional press agencies and global digital platforms. Lawmakers argue for granting publishers related rights to control content aggregation and monetization, aiming to secure revenue sharing similar to models in Europe and Australia. This shift seeks to empower newsrooms against data scraping while maintaining exceptions for non-commercial use. The goal is to create a fairer ecosystem where digital platforms compensate creators for using their intellectual assets, thereby sustaining the viability of independent journalism in a digital economy. Finally, the legislation grapples with the ethical and legal implications of Artificial Intelligence, particularly regarding training data and copyright. There is deep skepticism about permitting unrestricted AI training on copyrighted works, with fears that broad exceptions could lead to mass data harvesting and undermine human creators’ incomes. The discussion underscores the need for cautious, well-defined boundaries that protect human creativity while acknowledging AI’s role in development. These debates are crucial for open_data as they establish precedents for how intellectual property, data utility, and digital rights intersect in national policy, influencing global standards for data governance and ethical AI deployment.
