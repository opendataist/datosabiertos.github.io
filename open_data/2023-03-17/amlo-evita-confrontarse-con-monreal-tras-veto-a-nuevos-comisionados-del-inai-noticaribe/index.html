AMLO evita confrontarse con Monreal tras veto a nuevos comisionados del INAI - Noticaribe

AMLO evita confrontarse con Monreal tras veto a nuevos comisionados del INAI - Noticaribe

President Andrés Manuel López Obrador vetoed the appointment of new commissioners at Mexico’s transparency institute, rejecting a political bargain that allocated positions between his coalition and the opposition. He condemned these backroom negotiations as remnants of corrupt practices, insisting that appointments must be based on integrity rather than partisan deals. By refusing to be complicit in what he deemed dishonest selections, he prioritized transparency and accountability over political expediency. This intervention highlights the ongoing tension between executive power and institutional independence in Mexico. The President’s strict adherence to anti-corruption principles challenges established political norms, forcing the Senate to propose new candidates. While critics argue this weakens the institute, the administration maintains that only honest, non-partisan individuals should guard public access to information, ensuring the body remains free from partisan influence. This event is relevant to open data because it directly impacts the governance of transparency institutions. The legitimacy and effectiveness of open data initiatives depend on independent oversight bodies that enforce access to information laws. When political interference compromises these agencies, public trust erodes, hindering the availability and reliability of government data for citizens and researchers alike.
