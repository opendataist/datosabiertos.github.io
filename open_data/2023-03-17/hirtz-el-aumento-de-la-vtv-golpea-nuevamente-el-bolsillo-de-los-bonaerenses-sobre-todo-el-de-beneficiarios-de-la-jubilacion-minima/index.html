Hirtz: “el aumento de la VTV golpea nuevamente el bolsillo de los bonaerenses, sobre todo el de beneficiarios de la jubilación mínima”

The article highlights the severe economic impact of rising inflation and increased vehicle inspection fees on vulnerable populations, particularly low-income pensioners in Buenos Aires. This double burden exacerbates financial strain, making basic legal compliance unaffordable for those with minimum pensions. Furthermore, logistical barriers such as scarce appointment slots and remote inspection centers impose additional travel costs and time losses, disproportionately affecting residents outside urban hubs. These structural difficulties compound the financial hardship, creating a cycle of exclusion from essential state services. This case is relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible information regarding public service pricing and infrastructure location. Without clear, real-time data on fees and service availability, citizens cannot plan effectively, revealing gaps in government transparency that hinder equitable access to mandatory services and amplify social inequality.
