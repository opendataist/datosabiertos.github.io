Así puedes evitar que Facebook e Instagram usen tus datos para entrenar a su inteligencia artificial | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

Meta has begun using data from Facebook and Instagram users to train its generative artificial intelligence models, a decision justified by the concept of legitimate interests. Although this measure aims to enhance the company’s technological capabilities, it has sparked significant controversy among users due to the ethical and privacy implications of leveraging their personal information without explicit and simplified consent. While users have the legal right to opt out of having their data used for these purposes, the opt-out process is complex and unintuitive, undermining user agency. Users must navigate specific settings on each platform and complete detailed forms, often encountering technical barriers or regional variations that hinder the exercise of their rights. This technical complexity creates a power asymmetry, where the ease of accepting automatic surveillance far outweighs the ease of opting out or choosing privacy. This situation is relevant to the open data movement and digital sovereignty, as it illustrates how major technology corporations can prioritize access to private information for their commercial interests under ambiguous legal frameworks. The opacity of data exclusion processes highlights the need for stricter regulation that ensures genuine transparency and accessibility in controlling personal information—principles fundamental to building a truly open data ecosystem that respects citizens’ rights.
