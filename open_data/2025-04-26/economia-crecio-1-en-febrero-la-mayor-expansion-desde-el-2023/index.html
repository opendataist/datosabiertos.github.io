Economía creció 1% en febrero; la mayor expansión desde el 2023

Economía creció 1% en febrero; la mayor expansión desde el 2023

The article highlights a notable rebound in Mexico’s economic activity, marking the strongest monthly growth in nearly two years. This expansion was driven primarily by robust gains in the industrial and construction sectors, signaling a temporary positive shift in secondary economic activities. While the services and primary sectors showed mixed or slower results, the overall momentum provides a short-term boost to confidence, suggesting that the economy is not yet fully constrained by broader global headwinds. However, this immediate improvement masks underlying structural vulnerabilities and significant uncertainty regarding the medium-term outlook. Experts project a slowdown for the remainder of the first semester, citing high real interest rates and an environment of acute instability. The divergence between the government’s optimistic growth forecasts and the more pessimistic projections from major international institutions such as the IMF and World Bank underscores a lack of consensus on the economy’s trajectory, raising concerns about potential stagnation or contraction in the coming year. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical importance of timely, granular, and transparent statistical indicators in managing public expectations. The contrasting narratives between official government projections and independent economic analyses rely heavily on the accessibility and interpretation of raw data such as the IGAE. Clear, unbiased, and widely available economic metrics are essential for fostering informed public debate, ensuring accountability among institutions, and allowing stakeholders to make evidence-based decisions rather than relying solely on speculative forecasts.
