Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Norway’s latest data reveals a striking paradox where immigrants experience significantly poorer living conditions, including lower income, employment gaps, and worse health outcomes, yet report life satisfaction levels equal to the native population. Despite facing systemic disadvantages and higher rates of mental health struggles and loneliness, these individuals maintain strong institutional trust and social affiliation, highlighting the complexity of integration beyond mere material metrics. This divergence underscores that quantitative disparities in socio-economic status do not necessarily correlate with subjective well-being. The findings suggest that immigrants may derive resilience from high confidence in state institutions and a robust sense of belonging, which buffers against the negative impacts of economic and social hardship. Consequently, traditional indicators of success must be interpreted with nuance, recognizing that satisfaction can coexist with structural inequality. For open data, this report exemplifies the critical importance of disaggregated datasets to capture nuanced human experiences. Accessible, detailed statistics allow researchers to identify hidden social dynamics, such as the "immigrant paradox," ensuring policy-making addresses both material gaps and the complex psychological realities of diverse communities.
