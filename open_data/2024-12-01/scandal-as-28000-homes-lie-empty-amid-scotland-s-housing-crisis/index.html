Scandal as 28,000 homes lie empty amid Scotland's housing crisis

The article highlights a severe housing inefficiency in Scotland, where tens of thousands of homes remain vacant while homelessness rates reach historic highs. This discrepancy reveals a systemic failure to utilize existing infrastructure, forcing the government to spend millions on expensive temporary accommodations like hotels rather than addressing the root cause. The narrative underscores a critical misallocation of resources, suggesting that the current approach prioritizes costly interim solutions over sustainable housing strategies. Liberal Democrat politicians criticize the government for allowing this crisis to persist, citing a lack of political will and budget cuts as primary drivers. They argue that bringing long-term vacant properties back into use, alongside new social rent construction, is the most logical response to the soaring number of rough sleepers and families in temporary care. This perspective positions housing availability as a matter of political accountability and efficient public spending, rather than just an absence of supply. This report is highly relevant to the open data community as it demonstrates the power of Freedom of Information requests in exposing government inefficiencies. By making specific datasets regarding vacant properties and homeless applications publicly accessible, citizens and opposition parties can hold authorities accountable. The article serves as a prime example of how transparency and data analysis can drive policy debate, turning opaque administrative failures into actionable public knowledge for social reform.
