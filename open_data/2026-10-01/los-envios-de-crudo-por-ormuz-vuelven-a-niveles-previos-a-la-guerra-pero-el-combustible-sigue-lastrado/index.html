Los envíos de crudo por Ormuz vuelven a niveles previos a la guerra, pero el combustible sigue lastrado

Crude oil exports from the Strait of Hormuz have recovered to pre-conflict volumes thanks to the restructuring of transport routes. The use of ship-to-ship transfers, protected by the U.S. Navy, enables tankers to avoid direct attacks, thereby maintaining international commercial flows despite ongoing Iranian hostility. At the same time, alternative pipelines in Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates have taken on a crucial share of transport, easing pressure on the strait. However, this infrastructure remains vulnerable to attacks, as demonstrated by recent drone-induced disruptions, highlighting the fragility of technical solutions in the face of regional geopolitical instability. This report is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency in tracking logistical flows and real-time monitoring of security events enable the analysis of critical supply chain resilience. The availability of such data facilitates understanding of how markets adapt to crises, making it essential for global risk assessment and informed decision-making regarding energy stability.
