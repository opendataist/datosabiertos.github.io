Avalan Diputados reforma sobre IA y derechos de autor

Avalan Diputados reforma sobre IA y derechos de autor

The Economics Committee approved an initiative that requires Internet service providers to remove content generated by artificial intelligence that infringes copyright. While the executive branch aims to protect intellectual property, the opposition warns that this measure could seriously compromise freedom of expression and access to the Internet, arguing that the burden of responsibility falls disproportionately on connectivity companies. Opposition lawmakers pointed out that the bill does not clearly define defense procedures or specify who determines recidivism, which could lead to the premature disconnection of users without due process. There is concern that, in the face of potential sanctions, companies may prioritize preventive censorship and mass blocking over waiting for judicial rulings, effectively becoming private arbiters of what can be published or thought. This debate is relevant to the open data movement because it illustrates the tension between technological regulation and transparency. While protecting authorship is crucial, imposing opaque filters through private providers can erode the principles of free access to information and net neutrality, affecting auditability and the ethical use of digital platforms for the common good.
