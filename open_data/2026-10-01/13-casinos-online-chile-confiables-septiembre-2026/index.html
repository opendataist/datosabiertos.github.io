The absence of a specific local regulatory framework for online casinos in Chile creates a legal vacuum that forces players to take responsibility for verifying the reliability of platforms, highlighting the importance of operating with transparency. Most of the analyzed operators do not publish their licenses, which increases risk, although there are exceptions with recognized European regulations that offer greater legal certainty and consumer protection in the event of potential disputes. Operational transparency and clarity in financial terms are crucial indicators of trust in this unregulated market. Many sites hide essential details such as withdrawal times or wagering requirements, making it difficult for users to plan accordingly. In contrast, those that explicitly declare their identity verification mechanisms, accept local currency, and detail bonus conditions facilitate a more predictable and fair gaming experience, reducing information asymmetry. This analysis is fundamental for the open data community, as it illustrates how the lack of structured and standardized data on licenses and response times limits citizen auditing and objective comparability. The inability to access verifiable public databases exacerbates the risks associated with online gambling, underscoring the need for initiatives to collect and disseminate open data to empower users and promote accountability in the digital gaming industry.
