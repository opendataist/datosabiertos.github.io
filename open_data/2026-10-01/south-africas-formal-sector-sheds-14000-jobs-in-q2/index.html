South Africa’s formal sector sheds 14,000 jobs in Q2

South Africa’s formal sector sheds 14,000 jobs in Q2

The data reveals a significant decline in overall employment, driven largely by reductions in traditional sectors like manufacturing and trade, while community services and construction experienced modest growth. This shift highlights a structural change in the labor market, where job losses in established industries are not fully offset by gains in newer or public-facing sectors. A notable trend is the divergence between full-time and part-time work, with part-time roles increasing as full-time positions shrink. This suggests an evolving employment landscape where workers are increasingly relying on non-permanent roles, potentially reflecting broader economic instability or changing hiring practices among employers seeking flexibility during uncertain periods. Regarding compensation, gross earnings and wages have seen year-on-year increases, though recent quarterly declines in bonuses indicate volatile pay structures. These figures are relevant to open data initiatives as they demonstrate how granular, sector-specific economic indicators can be aggregated to provide deeper insights into labor market health, enabling better policy formulation and transparency in public sector reporting.
