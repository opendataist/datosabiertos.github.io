Sin datos independientes del Dane no hay política pública para el campo

Sin datos independientes del Dane no hay política pública para el campo

The article denounces the erosion of DANE’s independence by prioritizing political narratives over statistical evidence, a move that compromises the objectivity of national data. This institutional intervention generates a crisis of confidence in which technical rigor is subordinated to the interests of the Executive Branch, transforming the statistical agency into a validator of official narratives rather than a guarantor of factual truth. Budgetary constraints and political pressure directly threaten the viability of the Fourth National Agricultural Census, an essential tool for understanding the structural transformations of Colombia’s rural sector. Without up-to-date and accurate data, the State lacks the empirical foundation necessary to design effective agricultural policies, allocate resources efficiently, and address contemporary challenges such as climate change and rural inequality. This situation is relevant to the principles of open data because it illustrates how political manipulation and a lack of institutional transparency undermine the quality, accessibility, and usefulness of public data. When statistical information ceases to be a verifiable common good and becomes an instrument of narrative control, it weakens the capacity of civil society, academia, and the market to engage in informed debates—debates that are fundamental to a healthy democracy and evidence-based governance.
