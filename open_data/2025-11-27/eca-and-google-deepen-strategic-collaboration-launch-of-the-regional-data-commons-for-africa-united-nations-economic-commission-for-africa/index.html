ECA and Google deepen strategic collaboration: launch of the Regional Data Commons for Africa | United Nations Economic Commission for Africa

The strategic partnership between the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa and Google has launched the Regional Data Commons for Africa, a pivotal initiative designed to overcome the fragmentation of the continent’s statistical systems. By integrating high-impact datasets and leveraging Google’s technology, this effort aims to create an interoperable, AI-enabled public data infrastructure. This move addresses the critical lack of accessible and reliable data, empowering African institutions to produce timely, policy-relevant information necessary for evidence-based development planning. This collaboration extends beyond infrastructure to encompass workforce readiness and ethical governance. Through extensive training programs and policy workshops, the partnership seeks to transform African citizens from passive AI users into active builders, ensuring the continent can responsibly navigate emerging technologies. Concurrently, joint efforts in AI policy-making have helped establish unified regional standards, while cybersecurity initiatives strengthen the preparedness of public officials. These combined actions foster a holistic digital ecosystem that prioritizes both innovation and security. This article is highly relevant to open data as it directly tackles the structural barriers preventing data interoperability across African National Statistical Offices. By promoting a centralized, open data commons, the initiative advocates for breaking down silos and standardizing data formats, which are essential prerequisites for effective open data ecosystems. It demonstrates how public-private collaboration can modernize statistical production, ensuring that open data is not just available but also usable, timely, and capable of driving tangible socioeconomic outcomes through AI integration.
