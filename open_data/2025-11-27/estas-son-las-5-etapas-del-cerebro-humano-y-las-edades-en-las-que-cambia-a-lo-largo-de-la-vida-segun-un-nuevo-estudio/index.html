Estas son las 5 “etapas” del cerebro humano (y las edades en las que cambia a lo largo de la vida), según un nuevo estudio

Estas son las 5 “etapas” del cerebro humano (y las edades en las que cambia a lo largo de la vida), según un nuevo estudio

A recent study from the University of Cambridge challenges the traditional view of continuous brain development by identifying five distinct structural stages throughout a person’s life. By analyzing large-scale datasets, researchers found that the brain undergoes specific inflection points at approximately ages 9, 32, 66, and 83, adapting its neural architecture to support different cognitive functions as we grow, mature, and age. The findings redefine key developmental phases, extending the structural adolescence period until age 32, which is marked by optimal cognitive performance. Later stages reveal a stabilization during adulthood followed by gradual connectivity declines in early and late aging. These insights help explain why certain ages are more vulnerable to mental health issues, learning difficulties, or neurodegenerative diseases, offering a more nuanced understanding of when the brain’s “wiring” is most susceptible to disruption. This research is highly relevant to open data because it relies on the aggregation and comparative analysis of extensive neuroimaging datasets to uncover complex biological patterns. It demonstrates how large-scale, accessible scientific data can drive significant advancements in neuroscience, validating the potential of open methodologies to redefine our understanding of human biology and health vulnerabilities across the lifespan.
