Vehicle safety improvements have stalled, prompting regulators to intensify oversight of emerging technologies, particularly automated driving systems. By mandating detailed crash reporting and utilizing broad information requests, authorities are establishing a rigorous data collection framework. This approach ensures that potential safety defects are identified early, creating a precedent where real-world performance data directly informs regulatory scrutiny and enforcement actions. Parallel to data collection, regulatory standards are evolving to accommodate vehicles without traditional manual controls. Amendments to crashworthiness rules remove barriers for autonomous vehicles, balancing safety requirements with the need for regulatory clarity to foster industry innovation. The creation of specialized offices dedicated to automation safety further signals a strategic shift toward integrating new vehicle designs into existing safety frameworks without compromising established protection levels. This trend is highly relevant to open data advocates as it demonstrates how mandatory, structured reporting generates high-value public datasets. The disclosure of non-confidential incident data provides researchers and the public with empirical evidence on autonomous vehicle safety. Consequently, this regulatory model supports transparency and accountability, showing how open data can empower citizens and experts to evaluate technological safety independently of manufacturer claims.
