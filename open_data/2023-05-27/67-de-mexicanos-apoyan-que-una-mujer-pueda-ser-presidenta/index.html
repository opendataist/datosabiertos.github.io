67% de mexicanos apoyan que una mujer pueda ser presidenta

Despite a measurable increase in social discrimination over the past five years, Mexican society demonstrates a significant willingness to accept female leadership. While nearly a quarter of adults reported experiencing prejudice recently, with women disproportionately affected by gender-based rejection, a strong majority supports the idea of a female head of state. This contrast highlights a complex social dynamic in which individual experiences of bias coexist with broader acceptance of gender equality in high political offices. Physical appearance remains the primary driver of social rejection, surpassing gender, political opinions, and economic status as causes of discrimination. The data underscore that superficial judgments heavily influence interpersonal treatment, reinforcing narratives that equate looks with value. This finding points to deep-seated cultural issues that require targeted interventions, as aesthetic bias continues to undermine equal treatment more than other identity markers previously considered more contentious. This report is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparent, accessible statistics are essential for diagnosing structural social problems. By making this information public, organizations enable policymakers and civil society to design evidence-based strategies for inclusion. This transparency not only validates lived experiences but also establishes a baseline for measuring progress toward a more tolerant society, demonstrating that open data is a critical tool for accountability and social change.
