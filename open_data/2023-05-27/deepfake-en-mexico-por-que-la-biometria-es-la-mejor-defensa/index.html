Deepfake en México, por qué la biometría es la mejor defensa

Mexico has experienced a dramatic increase in voice deepfake cases, underscoring the urgent need to adapt digital security frameworks to threats generated by artificial intelligence. This phenomenon poses a significant risk to identity integrity and information authenticity, affecting both citizens and institutions. The proliferation of these realistic audio manipulations highlights the critical need to develop and adopt advanced technologies capable of distinguishing between authentic and forged voices, thereby maintaining trust in digital interactions. An effective solution lies in the robust implementation of voice biometrics, which has gained traction due to its ability to authenticate users remotely and securely. Beyond simple identification, these tools detect subtle nuances that reveal attacks, such as the playback of pre-recorded audio, offering a proactive layer of defense. Collaboration between the public sector and private companies in developing detection algorithms emerges as a key model for standardizing fraud protection and ensuring the authenticity of sensitive communications. This situation is relevant to open data because it highlights the intersection between transparency, personal data protection, and the integrity of public information. As governments and organizations increasingly rely on digital communication, the quality and authenticity of audio data become a fundamental component of open governance. Ensuring that sources are verifiable through reliable technical standards is essential to preserving the credibility of public data and protecting citizens from disinformation and identity spoofing in the digital age.
