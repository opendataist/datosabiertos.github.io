Mexico’s transparency watchdog, INAI, remains paralyzed due to unconfirmed board appointments, preventing it from convening or processing thousands of pending appeals. This institutional blockage stems from political obstruction by President López Obrador and his party, who effectively aim to dismantle the agency’s oversight capabilities without directly abolishing it. The absence of a quorum significantly undermines Mexico’s democratic transition by restricting public access to both government records and personal data. Without an active body to challenge obstructive executive decrees, the safeguards against corruption and human rights abuses are severely weakened, leaving citizens vulnerable to opaque state actions. This situation is critical to open data advocates because it demonstrates how political maneuvering can halt the enforcement of transparency laws. The paralysis of INAI highlights the fragility of data rights when institutional checks are eroded, illustrating that legal frameworks alone cannot guarantee open data access without functioning, independent regulatory bodies to uphold them.
