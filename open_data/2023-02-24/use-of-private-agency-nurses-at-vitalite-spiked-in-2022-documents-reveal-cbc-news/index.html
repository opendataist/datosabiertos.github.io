Use of private agency nurses at Vitalité spiked in 2022, documents reveal | CBC News

The article highlights a critical staffing crisis in New Brunswick’s health networks, where hospitals are spending millions on temporary agency nurses to prevent service closures and manage emergency department shortages. This reliance on external staffing has sparked controversy due to the significant pay disparity between temporary workers and permanent staff, creating workplace tension and feelings of disrespect among long-term employees who now bear the burden of training and supporting these transient colleagues. Vitalité Health Network spent nearly twelve times more than its counterpart, Horizon Health Network, on agency nurses, a difference attributed to linguistic recruitment barriers and varying local demand. Both networks describe this practice as a necessary, short-term intervention to stabilize workloads and ensure patient safety, with official goals to eliminate agency reliance within two to three years by improving recruitment and retention strategies rather than continuing expensive temporary contracts indefinitely. This issue is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency mechanisms, such as the Right to Information Act, expose financial and operational realities that might otherwise remain hidden. By making spending records and staffing strategies public, open data facilitates public accountability, allows for comparative analysis between organizations, and empowers stakeholders to debate the sustainability of current healthcare funding models and labor practices.
