FOIA Requests Presaged Huge Problems for Baby-Formula Maker Abbott Labs

The article highlights how open data, specifically Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) requests, served as a critical early warning system for public health crises before government agencies could act. By tracking these requests through the FOIAengine database, it becomes evident that private entities, such as specialized law firms, identified safety concerns regarding Abbott Laboratories’ infant formula months before the FDA issued public warnings or recalls. This disparity underscores the vital role of accessible government records in exposing potential dangers that regulators may overlook due to bureaucratic delays or resource constraints. Furthermore, the narrative illustrates the tangible impact of open data on accountability and legal processes. The initial inquiries filed by lawyers not only provided them with essential evidence to launch lawsuits but also signaled to the media and other stakeholders that significant issues existed. As more entities accessed this public information, it triggered a cascade of further investigations, news coverage, and legal actions, demonstrating how transparency drives both corporate liability and public scrutiny. The data reveals a clear timeline where private actors leveraged open records to fill gaps in official oversight, ultimately forcing regulatory intervention after preventable harm had already occurred. This case is highly relevant to open data because it validates the premise that unrestricted access to government documents empowers civil society to monitor corporate conduct and regulatory effectiveness. It suggests that robust open data frameworks are not merely administrative tools but essential mechanisms for public safety and justice. When citizens and journalists can efficiently obtain and analyze agency records, they can hold institutions accountable, ensuring that hidden risks are brought to light and that necessary actions are taken to protect vulnerable populations.
