A legal nonprofit is seeking internal government records to determine if Biden administration officials are redesigning IRS audit algorithms based on racial equity goals. The organization argues that these efforts, driven by executive orders aimed at correcting perceived systemic biases, may inadvertently discriminate against white and Asian taxpayers. By focusing on achieving equal outcomes rather than impartial procedures, the administration risks replacing one form of bias with another. The request targets high-ranking Treasury and IRS officials, alleging that the government has improperly collected demographic data to influence compliance strategies. Critics contend that this approach racializes tax policy by assuming disparate audit rates indicate discrimination, ignoring broader economic factors like income levels and wage structures. They argue that existing data already shows tax credits disproportionately benefit minority families, undermining the justification for altering audit targets to achieve racial parity. This situation is crucial for open data advocates because it highlights the tension between algorithmic transparency and the politicization of government data. If audit algorithms are tuned to produce specific demographic results, it raises serious concerns about the integrity and neutrality of public sector data systems. Ensuring that such systems are open to scrutiny is essential to prevent administrative decisions from being driven by ideological goals rather than objective, non-discriminatory criteria.
