El gobierno abierto | El Deber

El gobierno abierto | El Deber

Carlos Borth proposes the creation of a new political party to overcome the perception of failure of the Bolivian state, focusing on the fight against corruption and the strengthening of regional autonomies. His central proposal introduces the concept of "open government" as an alternative to rebuild citizen trust. This perspective implies a shift in values toward transparency and collaboration, seeking to leverage collective knowledge to improve public management and eliminate administrative secrecy. The relevance of this article to open data lies in its conceptual defense of access to public information as a fundamental pillar of democracy. The text argues that transparency and accountability are inseparable: only through free access to state data can citizens hold their leaders accountable. This stance aligns with international instruments that establish transparency as an indispensable requirement for effective public management and genuine participatory democracy. Finally, the article highlights that freedom of expression and access to information are essential tools for overseeing public affairs and combating corruption. By linking opacity with institutional degradation, it underscores that the release of government information is not merely a legal right, but a practical strategy for enhancing the quality of administration. This reinforces the importance of open data movements as vital mechanisms for ensuring the integrity and efficiency of the state.
