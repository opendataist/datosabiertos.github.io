Los aspectos que serán claves en el futuro de la movilidad en Chile

The study analyzes the state of urban mobility in cities such as Santiago, highlighting that although it is improving, it still falls below global standards. The central conclusion is that Chile needs coordinated governance between the public and private sectors to integrate emerging technologies and sustainable approaches, avoiding excessive regulation that could stifle the innovation necessary for 21st-century development. A flexible and predictable regulatory framework is identified as essential to attract investment, alongside the modernization of infrastructure to support new modes of transport. Moreover, physical and digital security are cross-cutting concerns; the protection of personal data generated by digital platforms is crucial to maintaining user trust in an increasingly digitalized ecosystem. The relevance to open data lies in the fact that transparency and responsible information management are pillars of citizen trust. For mobility to be inclusive and efficient, data must flow under clear frameworks that ensure integrity and confidentiality, thereby enabling the design of user-centered solutions that are accessible to the entire population, regardless of their socioeconomic or physical conditions.
