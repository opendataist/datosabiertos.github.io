Nigeria’s inflation hit 22.79% in June

Nigeria’s inflation surged to a 17-year high, driven largely by food costs and the removal of fuel subsidies. This sharp rise indicates immediate economic pressure on households, although long-term projections suggest the policy shift may eventually stabilize prices as temporary shocks subside. Open data initiatives are crucial here, providing transparent, accessible records of these economic shifts. By monitoring granular, timely datasets, researchers and policymakers can verify official statistics, analyze distributional impacts, and ensure accountability in how subsidy reforms affect diverse population segments. Furthermore, accessible data empowers civil society to track inflation trends independently. This transparency fosters informed public discourse and supports evidence-based policy adjustments, ensuring that economic interventions align with actual living conditions rather than just macroeconomic indicators.
