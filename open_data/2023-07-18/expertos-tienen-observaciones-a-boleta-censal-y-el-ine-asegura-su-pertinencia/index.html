Expertos tienen observaciones a boleta censal y el INE asegura su pertinencia

Demography experts have critically reviewed the preliminary census questionnaire for Bolivia’s 2024 Population and Housing Census, offering suggestions to improve data quality and public trust. Key concerns include the necessity of collecting full names versus first names only, the inclusion of identity card numbers, and the need for clearer questions regarding water access, migration reference periods, and household overcrowding. While some experts argue that certain data points may induce distrust or are statistically redundant, others emphasize the importance of maintaining consistency with previous censuses to ensure comparability in analyzing social and demographic trends over time. The National Institute of Statistics (INE) has engaged in a transparent process, reviewing these observations within the framework of Open Data principles. After technical discussions and consultations with international bodies, the INE concluded that all 58 questions remain pertinent. They highlight that strict statistical secrecy protects individual identities, addressing concerns about privacy. Despite rejecting some suggestions for modification, the institution valued the expert feedback, aiming to balance methodological rigor with the practical need for comparable data that supports public policy development and electoral redistricting. This article is relevant to open data because it exemplifies the tension and synergy between institutional transparency and technical standardization. It illustrates how open consultation with academic and professional sectors can refine data collection methodologies before release. By demonstrating that the INE considered external critiques and justified its decisions through open technical dialogue, the process enhances the credibility and utility of the resulting statistical products, which are essential for equitable governance and accurate international comparisons.
