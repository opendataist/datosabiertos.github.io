Cómo la inteligencia artificial afectaría el plagio y los derechos de autor para los artistas

Artists and creators from various disciplines acknowledge that artificial intelligence has optimized production times, generating uncertainty about the future of work and the essence of creativity. However, they agree that technology cannot replace the personal touch, ethics, or the human capacity to evoke emotion. Therefore, they propose specific categories and global regulatory frameworks to distinguish and protect intellectual authorship. The relevance to open data lies in the debate over the ownership and origin of the data used to train these algorithms. Although the article mentions the need for human curation in training, transparency regarding which datasets are employed is crucial to ensure fairness, protect copyright, and uphold ethics in technological development—issues that are fundamental to public information management. Finally, professionals emphasize the urgency for governments to implement thoughtful regulations, particularly in the Latin American educational context. The focus should be on adapting methodologies to leverage these tools without dehumanizing the creative process, ensuring that open data and emerging technologies serve to expand artistic and social possibilities rather than replace human talent.
