Elderly cohort sees highest increase between 2011 and 2021, while birth rate slows

Malta is experiencing a significant demographic shift characterized by rapid population aging and a declining birth rate. This trend has altered the old-age dependency ratio, indicating a growing challenge for the workforce to support the elderly. Simultaneously, the country is seeing a notable influx of foreign residents, particularly in the prime working-age cohort, which influences both household dynamics and the overall labor market structure. These demographic changes are reshaping social services and economic stability. Economic and social indicators reveal a complex landscape of shifting lifestyles and income disparities. Household sizes are stabilizing around smaller units, while disposable income varies significantly by region, with specific districts leading in economic output. The labor market shows a gender pay gap, and tourism remains a vital economic engine, though visitor patterns have shifted post-pandemic. Increased tourism in Gozo suggests a diversification of travel destinations, reflecting broader changes in how the country engages with international visitors and manages regional economic growth. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it provides granular insights into national well-being, enabling evidence-based policy making. By making such detailed demographic, economic, and tourism statistics accessible, researchers and citizens can analyze trends in housing, education, and employment more effectively. Transparency in this data supports accountability and helps stakeholders understand the impact of social policies, fostering a more informed public discourse on sustainable development and resource allocation in Malta.
