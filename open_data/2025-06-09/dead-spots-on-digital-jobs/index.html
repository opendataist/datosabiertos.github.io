Dead spots on digital jobs

Dead spots on digital jobs

The Philippine Statistics Authority reports a slight increase in unemployment, yet government officials maintain that labor market conditions remain within target ranges. Notably, the latest data reveals that a significant portion of new employment opportunities are being absorbed by older demographics, particularly those aged 55 and above. This trend suggests that while the overall unemployment rate appears manageable, the structural composition of the workforce is shifting, with senior citizens playing an increasingly vital role in sustaining economic activity during periods of political campaigning and seasonal variation. Amidst fears that artificial intelligence and automation might displace workers, the Department of Information and Communications Technology argues that digital transformation is actually generating substantial employment. Officials highlight the existence of millions of "digitally-enabled jobs" and project that improved infrastructure will create millions more. This perspective challenges the narrative of technological unemployment, positing instead that the expansion of digital capabilities serves as a mechanism for job creation rather than reduction, thereby integrating the population into the modern digital economy. The relevance of this article to open data lies in its emphasis on transparency and the strategic use of public information to drive infrastructure policy. By allocating increased budgets for free public internet access and mandating open bidding processes for satellite contracts, the government aims to bridge the digital divide in geographically isolated areas. This initiative relies on making connectivity data and access points publicly available, ensuring that the benefits of the digital economy are distributed equitably. Ultimately, the article underscores how open, accessible data infrastructure is essential for fostering inclusive economic growth and empowering underserved communities through universal digital rights.
