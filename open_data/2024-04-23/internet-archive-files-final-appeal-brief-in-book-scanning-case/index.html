Internet Archive Files Final Appeal Brief in Book Scanning Case

The Internet Archive is appealing a lower court ruling that deemed its Controlled Digital Lending program copyright infringement, arguing that the practice is a transformative and essential extension of traditional library services. By allowing libraries to lend digital copies of owned physical books one patron at a time, the Internet Archive maintains that it preserves the core mission of libraries: providing equitable access while maintaining ownership. This approach ensures that libraries can build permanent, archivable collections that are not subject to the restrictive licenses and arbitrary changes often imposed by commercial e-book providers. A central contention in the appeal is that the digital format does not supplant the market for physical books or licensed e-books, but rather enhances the utility of existing owned assets. The Internet Archive contends that digital lending offers greater efficiency and privacy protections for patrons, which are critical for the integrity of library operations. Furthermore, the organization challenges the characterization of its non-commercial activities as "commercial," asserting that accepting donations and operating as a nonprofit entity does not negate the fair use rights associated with preserving and lending cultural heritage materials. This case is pivotal for the open data and digital library communities because its outcome will define the legal boundaries of digitization and access for non-commercial institutions. A ruling against the Internet Archive could set a dangerous precedent, potentially preventing libraries and archives from making owned physical materials available in digital formats, thereby limiting public access to knowledge and undermining efforts to preserve digital heritage. The decision will likely influence how technology companies and cultural institutions approach mass digitization and fair use rights in the digital age.
