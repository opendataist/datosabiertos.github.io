En el Salvador los homicidios solo tienen un autor, las pandillas - Noticias Prensa Latina

The article challenges the official narrative of El Salvador’s unprecedented security gains, highlighting a stark disconnect between government statistics and broader human rights realities. While authorities celebrate record-low homicide rates attributed to anti-gang measures, independent observers argue these figures systematically exclude significant categories of violence. By omitting deaths during armed clashes, among incarcerated individuals, and newborns, the data presents an artificially sanitized view of public safety that masks the true cost of the state of exception. This selective reporting is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how government transparency can be manipulated to serve political agendas. When statistical methodologies intentionally exclude certain victim demographics or violent contexts, the resulting datasets fail to reflect the actual societal impact of public policies. Such opacity undermines accountability, making it difficult for civil society and international bodies to assess whether security improvements are genuine or merely statistical artifacts designed to legitimize prolonged authoritarian practices. Ultimately, the discrepancy underscores the urgent need for inclusive, comprehensive data collection standards in democratic governance. True progress requires acknowledging all forms of violence, not just those linked to criminal gangs. By ignoring deaths in custody and non-gang-related murders, the state obscures potential abuses of power and human rights violations. Reliable open data must encompass the full spectrum of mortality to ensure accurate policy evaluation and protect citizens from impunity, reinforcing the principle that transparency is essential for genuine justice and trust in public institutions.
