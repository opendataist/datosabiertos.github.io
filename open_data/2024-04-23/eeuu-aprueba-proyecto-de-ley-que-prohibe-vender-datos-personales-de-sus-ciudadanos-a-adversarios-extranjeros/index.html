EEUU aprueba proyecto de ley que prohíbe vender datos personales de sus ciudadanos a “adversarios extranjeros”

United States lawmakers have unanimously approved HR 7520, a landmark piece of legislation designed to protect American citizens’ personal data by strictly prohibiting its sale or transfer to foreign adversaries. This legal framework establishes a robust defense mechanism that blocks the movement of sensitive information—including financial records, biometric data, and digital communications—to countries such as China or Russia. By criminalizing the transaction of such data, the law aims to prevent foreign entities from exploiting American private information, thereby reinforcing national security and individual privacy rights against external threats. The legislative approach goes beyond mere restriction, imposing significant penalties for violations under the authority of the Federal Trade Commission. This enforcement strategy signals a decisive shift in how open data ecosystems interact with geopolitical security, emphasizing that the free flow of information must not compromise national sovereignty. The measure complements recent efforts to restrict apps controlled by foreign entities, illustrating a coordinated strategy to close loopholes that might otherwise allow adversarial access to user data. As a result, companies must now rigorously audit their data practices to ensure compliance with these stringent new boundaries. This development is highly relevant to the open data community, as it redefines the ethical and legal parameters for data distribution. It highlights a growing global trend in which data sovereignty takes precedence over unrestricted information sharing, impacting how open data initiatives are designed and implemented. Professionals must now navigate a landscape where privacy protection and security concerns heavily influence data accessibility policies. Understanding these regulatory shifts is crucial for maintaining responsible open data practices that balance transparency with the imperative to protect citizens from foreign exploitation.
