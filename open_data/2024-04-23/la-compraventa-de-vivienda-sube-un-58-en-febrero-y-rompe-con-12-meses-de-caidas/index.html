La compraventa de vivienda sube un 5,8% en febrero y rompe con 12 meses de caídas

The Spanish housing market has ended a twelve-month downward trend, signaling renewed vitality in transactions. This resurgence is driven by a significant increase in sales of new properties and a modest recovery in the resale sector, suggesting that demand remains robust despite lingering economic uncertainties. However, this positive trend faces constraints due to a sharp drop in available housing supply. This scarcity threatens to exacerbate market tensions, potentially driving prices higher and limiting the overall growth of transactions. Consequently, the sector is expected to undergo a gradual transformation, balancing strong buyer interest against limited inventory in the near term. This news is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical need for accessible, granular datasets to monitor housing availability and price dynamics. Transparent information on transaction volumes and regional variations allows stakeholders to accurately analyze market health. Furthermore, understanding the interplay between supply shortages and interest rate expectations requires reliable public statistics to forecast future trends and inform policy decisions effectively.
