Open source AI: Mission aborted? - The Economic Times

Open source AI: Mission aborted? - The Economic Times

Open-source large language models are facing significant commercial headwinds as enterprises increasingly favor closed, proprietary solutions despite early promises of data control and cost efficiency. Major venture capital data indicates a shift toward dominant players like OpenAI and Anthropic, while the market share for leading open models has notably declined. This trend suggests that the initial enthusiasm for open-source AI is waning in the corporate sector, driven by a desire for robust, supported commercial APIs over the complex maintenance required for self-hosted systems. The primary barriers to widespread enterprise adoption include unresolved security, governance, and legal liability concerns, alongside the steep expertise required for lifecycle management. Although the performance gap between open and closed models has narrowed considerably, open source often still falls short of frontier quality. Furthermore, geopolitical tensions and licensing complexities limit the usability of emerging open models from certain regions, making them less attractive to international businesses seeking reliable, low-risk deployments for sensitive data. This narrative is highly relevant to open_data because it highlights a critical tension between technological accessibility and sustainable business models. The decline in enterprise spending on open-source AI underscores that providing free tools is insufficient without clear monetization strategies. For the open_data community, this serves as a warning that technical superiority and open licensing alone do not guarantee adoption; organizations must address practical barriers like security, support, and legal clarity to ensure open datasets and models remain viable assets in the enterprise ecosystem.
