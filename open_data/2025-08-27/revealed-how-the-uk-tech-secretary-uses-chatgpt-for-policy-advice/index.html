Revealed: How the UK tech secretary uses ChatGPT for policy advice

Revealed: How the UK tech secretary uses ChatGPT for policy advice

The UK government’s use of commercial AI tools like ChatGPT by senior ministers has triggered a landmark legal precedent, revealing that such interactions may be subject to Freedom of Information requests. This development challenges traditional boundaries between personal digital habits and official government business, forcing a re-evaluation of whether AI-generated insights constitute public records. It establishes that the generation of new information via chatbots, unlike passive search engine queries, creates a data trail that transparency advocates can legally demand. The disclosed interactions show that the Technology Secretary utilized AI for substantive policy analysis, specifically regarding barriers to AI adoption in small businesses, as well as for media strategy and technical definitions. While officials argue these tools serve as efficient, labor-saving aids that do not replace comprehensive expert advice, the content highlights significant gaps in public awareness and regulatory confidence. The reliance on external AI for high-level decision-making underscores a broader governmental shift toward integrating artificial intelligence into administrative workflows to enhance efficiency and accessibility. This case is critically relevant to open data because it blurs the line between proprietary commercial services and public sector records. It raises urgent questions about data sovereignty, accountability, and the appropriate scope of transparency laws when private companies host public sector conversations. Consequently, it may compel governments to abandon commercial AI solutions in favor of publicly provided tools to mitigate legal risks, ensuring that all official AI usage remains within a transparent, open, and auditable framework rather than hiding behind private platform privacy policies.
