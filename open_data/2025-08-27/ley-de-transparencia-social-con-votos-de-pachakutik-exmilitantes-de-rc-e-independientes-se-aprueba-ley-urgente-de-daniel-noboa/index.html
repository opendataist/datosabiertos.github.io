Ley de Transparencia Social: con votos de Pachakutik, exmilitantes de RC e independientes se aprueba ley urgente de Daniel Noboa

Ley de Transparencia Social: con votos de Pachakutik, exmilitantes de RC e independientes se aprueba ley urgente de Daniel Noboa

The Law on Social Transparency establishes a new regulatory framework for non-profit organizations, mandating greater transparency in resource management and accountability. Rather than targeting entities for ideological reasons, the state classifies organizations according to risk levels to enforce stricter internal integrity systems. This shift aims to prevent irregular capital flows and strengthen state oversight, fundamentally altering how civil society interacts with public administration and financial controls. The legislation also introduces significant tax reforms and fiscal measures, including the forgiveness of debts owed by dissolved public companies to the Social Security Institute. Additionally, it establishes mechanisms for collecting taxes on undistributed corporate profits and modifies mining regulations to ensure compliance with investment and payment obligations. These provisions seek to optimize the management of public assets and address historical financial gaps within the state’s institutional framework. This article is relevant to open data because it institutionalizes mandatory transparency standards for non-profits, requiring them to disclose accurate information about their structure, financing, and governance. By establishing risk-based reporting obligations and expanding the oversight powers of anti-money laundering units, the law creates a legal foundation for increased data availability. Consequently, it enhances the potential for public scrutiny and integrity monitoring within the social and economic sectors.
