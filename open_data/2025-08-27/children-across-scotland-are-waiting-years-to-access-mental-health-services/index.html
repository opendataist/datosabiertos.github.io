Children across Scotland are waiting years to access mental health services

Children across Scotland are waiting years to access mental health services

The article highlights a severe crisis in Scotland’s child mental health services, where young people face excruciatingly long waits for diagnosis and treatment. This systemic failure disproportionately impacts neurodivergent individuals, as illustrated by the harrowing eight-year journey of a mother seeking an autism diagnosis for her daughter. The narrative underscores how delayed recognition prevents children from understanding their own needs, leading to unnecessary distress and a lack of appropriate support during their formative years. Politically, this delay is framed as a critical policy failure that harms vulnerable populations. Critics argue that ending assessments and imposing multi-year waiting lists effectively denies children access to care while they are still in school, exacerbating their conditions. Despite government claims of meeting national standards and hiring more staff, the reality on the ground suggests that many areas have suspended new intake, leaving families without viable pathways to help and raising serious concerns about the long-term wellbeing of a generation. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it reveals the gap between official metrics and lived experience. While ministers cite median wait times and staffing increases, freedom of information requests expose extreme outliers and systemic closures that aggregated statistics may obscure. Accessing granular, transparent data is essential for holding institutions accountable, validating personal testimonies, and driving the structural reforms needed to ensure equitable access to health services for all children.
