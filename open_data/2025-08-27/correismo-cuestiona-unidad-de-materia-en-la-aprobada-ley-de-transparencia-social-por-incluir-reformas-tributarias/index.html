Correísmo cuestiona unidad de materia en la aprobada Ley de Transparencia Social por incluir reformas tributarias

Correísmo cuestiona unidad de materia en la aprobada Ley de Transparencia Social por incluir reformas tributarias

The recent approval of the Social Transparency Law has sparked intense constitutional challenges, largely because it masks institutionalized impunity under a deceptive name. Critics contend that by tacking on last-minute fiscal and financial reforms, the legislation breaches the principle of thematic unity. This tactic is viewed not as a sincere attempt to enhance openness, but rather as a strategic maneuver to shield high-ranking government officials and their families from future accountability and scrutiny. The most alarming implications involve the deliberate removal of audit trails in tax investigations. By erasing the traceability of financial data, the law effectively prevents these records from being used in subsequent inquiries, thereby ensuring that the country’s most powerful individuals evade investigation or sanctions. This structural change undermines the core purpose of transparency laws, which should facilitate oversight rather than obstruct the investigative processes essential for ensuring legal compliance and maintaining public trust. The relevance to open data lies in the direct threat this legislation poses to data availability and integrity. Open data depends on the principle that information must be accessible, traceable, and usable for public scrutiny and accountability. When legal frameworks actively destroy the digital or bureaucratic records of financial transactions, they dismantle the infrastructure necessary for transparency. Consequently, this move represents a significant setback for data rights, as it prioritizes the protection of specific political interests over the public’s right to access and verify information about government actions and fiscal responsibility.
