Supreme Court Stays Kerala HC Order Which Held Cochin International Airport Ltd To Be A 'Public Authority' Under RTI Act

The Supreme Court has stayed a Kerala High Court ruling that classified Cochin International Airport Limited as a public authority under the Right to Information Act. This legal intervention halts the lower court’s directive, which had affirmed that the state government exercises deep and pervasive control over the airport’s operations despite its corporate structure. By pausing this designation, the Supreme Court is effectively preventing the immediate enforcement of transparency mandates that would compel the airport to disclose sensitive financial and managerial information to the public. This case is pivotal for open data because it interrogates the boundaries of public accountability in entities with mixed public-private ownership models. The central tension lies in whether significant state involvement, such as land acquisition, financial guarantees, and historical origins, overrides the independence of a company’s board of directors. If the High Court’s logic prevails, it establishes a precedent that government-linked corporations remain subject to information disclosure laws, thereby expanding the scope of data accessibility beyond traditional state departments. Conversely, if the Supreme Court upholds the stay, it may reinforce the distinction between commercial enterprises and public authorities, potentially limiting the flow of public-interest data. The outcome will define whether entities functioning like private businesses, yet supported by the state, are exempt from open data requirements. This decision ultimately shapes the transparency landscape for infrastructure projects, determining who holds the right to know and how much government influence dictates information availability.
