No son confiables las cifras de la ONEI sobre accidentes laborales en Cuba, opinan expertos

No son confiables las cifras de la ONEI sobre accidentes laborales en Cuba, opinan expertos

The article concludes that official Cuban statistics on workplace accidents are fundamentally unreliable, as the government omits data from the private sector and misclassifies incidents to artificially lower reported rates. By excluding accidents involving independent workers and categorizing transport-related fatalities as general traffic incidents rather than occupational hazards, the state creates a distorted picture of labor safety. This lack of transparency prevents an accurate assessment of the true human cost and obscures the severe deficiencies in occupational health protocols across the island. The relevance to open data lies in the critical need for transparent, standardized, and inclusive statistical systems. True open data requires not just the publication of figures, but the implementation of rigorous methodologies that account for all economic sectors, including informal or private labor, and adhere to international safety benchmarks. Without comprehensive data collection that captures the reality of all workers, policy-making remains blind to systemic failures, and civil society cannot hold authorities accountable for safety violations or demand necessary improvements in working conditions. Ultimately, the disparity between official reports and independent findings highlights a broader crisis of data integrity. The reliance on opaque metrics allows the regime to mask worsening conditions, such as inadequate protective equipment and the impacts of energy crises, which directly endanger workers' health. This case serves as a stark reminder that open data is meaningless if the underlying information gathering process is manipulated or incomplete, emphasizing the necessity for independent verification and universal coverage in any legitimate statistical framework.
