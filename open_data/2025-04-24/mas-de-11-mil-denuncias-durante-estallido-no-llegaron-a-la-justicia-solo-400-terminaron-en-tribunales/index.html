Más de 11 mil denuncias durante estallido no llegaron a la Justicia: solo 400 terminaron en tribunales

Más de 11 mil denuncias durante estallido no llegaron a la Justicia: solo 400 terminaron en tribunales

The article reveals that the vast majority of investigations into institutional violence following the social unrest have been closed without a judicial resolution. This data, obtained through a transparency request, highlights a severe systemic failure: only a tiny fraction of complaints reached court, leaving victims at a disadvantage due to lack of legal representation. Legal experts and politicians interpret these statistics as either proof of impunity or an unjust burden on the police, reflecting a disconnect between public expectations and the Ministry of Public’s limited resources. The narrative underscores that, despite numerous complaints, very few resulted in convictions, pointing to deep structural inefficiencies within the justice system. This case is crucial for open data, as it demonstrates how transparency laws can expose gaps in systemic accountability. By making official statistics accessible, civil society can challenge institutional narratives, demand better resource allocation, and ensure that data serves as a tool for democratic oversight rather than merely for administrative record-keeping.
