Los pacientes tenían que esperar 113 días en Castilla-La Mancha para ser intervenidos al término de 2024

Los pacientes tenían que esperar 113 días en Castilla-La Mancha para ser intervenidos al término de 2024

The report reveals a complex reality within Spain’s public healthcare system, characterized by a slight overall reduction in surgery waiting lists but a concurrent increase in average wait times. Although fewer patients remain on the lists compared to mid-2024, the duration of their waits has grown, indicating that capacity expansion has not kept pace with demand. This tension highlights systemic pressures, particularly in trauma and plastic surgery, which remain the most delayed specialties, suggesting a structural imbalance in resource allocation across different medical fields. Geographically, the data exposes significant disparities between autonomous communities. Regions such as Extremadura and Andalusia report waiting times far exceeding the national average, whereas Madrid and the Basque Country manage considerably shorter delays. These divergences underscore the uneven distribution of healthcare resources and efficiency at the regional level. The high concentration of patients in certain areas, such as Cantabria, further emphasizes how local administrative and infrastructural capacities critically influence patients’ access to timely care. This information is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates the necessity of granular, accessible datasets for effective public scrutiny and policymaking. By making detailed metrics on wait times and regional performance transparent, stakeholders can identify inefficiencies and advocate for equitable resource distribution. Open data transforms these raw statistics into actionable insights, enabling researchers, journalists, and citizens to hold health authorities accountable and drive evidence-based improvements in healthcare delivery.
