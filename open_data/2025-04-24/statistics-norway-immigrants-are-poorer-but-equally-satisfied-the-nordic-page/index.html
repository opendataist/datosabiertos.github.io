Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly worse living conditions compared to the native population, experiencing lower incomes, poorer housing, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Despite these tangible disadvantages, they report equal life satisfaction and exhibit surprisingly high confidence in national institutions like the judiciary and police. This paradox highlights a complex social dynamic where structural inequities do not necessarily translate into reduced subjective well-being or civic trust. The report is crucial for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the value of disaggregated demographic statistics in revealing nuanced societal trends. By making detailed survey results public, researchers and policymakers can identify specific groups needing targeted support while understanding broader patterns of integration. Access to such granular data empowers civil society to hold institutions accountable and fosters evidence-based debates on immigration and social cohesion. Ultimately, this dataset challenges simplistic narratives by showing that poor material conditions coexist with strong institutional trust among immigrants. Understanding these contradictions requires transparent, accessible data that goes beyond aggregate averages. Open data standards ensure these critical insights remain available for ongoing analysis, promoting a deeper understanding of how different populations experience and perceive their place within society.
