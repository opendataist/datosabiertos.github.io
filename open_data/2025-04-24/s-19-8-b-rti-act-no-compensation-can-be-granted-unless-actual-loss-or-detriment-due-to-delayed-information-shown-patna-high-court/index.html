S.19(8)(b) RTI Act | No Compensation Can Be Granted Unless Actual Loss Or Detriment Due To Delayed Information Shown: Patna High Court

S.19(8)(b) RTI Act | No Compensation Can Be Granted Unless Actual Loss Or Detriment Due To Delayed Information Shown: Patna High Court

The Patna High Court reinforced the legal standard that compensation under the Right to Information Act is not automatic but requires applicants to prove actual loss or detriment caused by delayed information disclosure. By dismissing a petition where the petitioner failed to demonstrate specific harms, the Court clarified that mere delay, without evidenced negative impact, is insufficient grounds for financial awards. This ruling establishes a stricter evidentiary burden for claimants, shifting the focus from procedural failures to tangible consequences for the information seeker. The judgment distinguishes this case from previous rulings where compensation was granted, noting that parity arguments fail when the nature of the requested information differs significantly. The Court highlighted that while other cases involved the non-supply of critical documents like answer books, this applicant received the sought-after registration details, albeit late. Consequently, the failure to challenge the initial commission order further weakened the petition, emphasizing that finality of administrative decisions plays a crucial role in limiting subsequent compensation claims. This decision is highly relevant to open data advocacy because it underscores the limitations of legal mechanisms in ensuring transparency. It suggests that without demonstrable personal harm, the promise of RTI compensation remains theoretical, potentially reducing the incentive for public authorities to prioritize timely data release. For open data initiatives, this highlights the need for robust systemic accountability measures beyond individual grievances, as legal recourse alone may not effectively compel timely and accessible government information without clear evidence of user detriment.
