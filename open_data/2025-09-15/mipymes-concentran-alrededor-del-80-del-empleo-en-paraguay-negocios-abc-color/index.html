Mipymes concentran alrededor del 80% del empleo en Paraguay - Negocios - ABC Color

The analysis reveals a labor market in Paraguay that is heavily reliant on micro and small enterprises, which together employ the vast majority of the workforce. This dominance underscores a structural reality in which informal activities, family-based subsistence units, and small businesses form the economic foundation. Consequently, policy efforts must prioritize sustaining the dynamism of these entities while addressing barriers to formalization, access to credit, and productivity, as they are essential for absorbing labor and driving economic diversification. While micro-enterprises provide stable employment, small and medium-sized businesses offer crucial potential for growth and industrial scaling. However, the transition from small to medium size faces significant hurdles related to labor costs and competitiveness. Meanwhile, large corporations employ a minority of the workforce, relying instead on capital investment and technology rather than mass labor absorption. This distinction highlights the need for targeted support systems that help smaller firms overcome structural limitations and integrate more effectively into formal, high-productivity sectors. This report is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how disaggregated employment statistics expose deep socioeconomic inequalities and structural bottlenecks. By making granular data on firm size and labor segments publicly available, researchers and policymakers can move beyond aggregate figures to understand specific vulnerabilities, such as the precariousness of domestic workers or the stagnation of small businesses. Transparent data enables the design of precise interventions to foster formalization and equitable growth, ensuring that statistical insights directly inform effective economic strategies.
