The scams to look out for this Black Friday - IT-Online

As South Africans continue to spend heavily on Black Friday deals despite economic pressures, the surge in online transactions creates a fertile ground for sophisticated fraud syndicates. The rise in digital commerce, particularly card-not-present transactions, has directly correlated with an increase in scams targeting consumers. This highlights a critical vulnerability in the current shopping landscape, where the convenience of online purchasing often outpaces security awareness, exposing individuals to identity theft and financial loss through deceptive practices. Fraudsters employ various tactics, such as spoofed websites, account verification calls, and billing error scams, to exploit consumer urgency and trust. These methods often rely on social engineering to extract sensitive personal and banking details, allowing scammers to assume identities and drain accounts. The implication is that traditional security measures are insufficient against these evolving threats, as criminals increasingly mimic legitimate brands and use targeted advertising to lure victims into malicious environments, compromising both data privacy and financial security. This situation is highly relevant to open data because the proactive fight against fraud relies on aggregating and sharing incident reports to identify syndicates and improve public safety. Platforms like Yima demonstrate how open, accessible tools can empower users to report scams and verify website safety, creating a community-driven defense mechanism. By centralizing fraud reporting and providing free security resources, organizations can foster a more transparent and secure digital ecosystem, encouraging safer online behaviors and enhancing the overall integrity of consumer data protection.
