The British government is facing intense political pressure after it was revealed that net migration has reached record levels, significantly exceeding previous estimates. This revelation has reignited tensions within the Conservative Party, whose members warn that the inability to fulfill border control promises poses an existential threat to their electoral survival and public legitimacy. The situation underscores the critical importance of statistical transparency in public policy formulation. Official data not only inform political debate but also determine the government’s credibility in the eyes of citizens. The discrepancy between estimated and actual figures highlights how accuracy in data collection is essential for establishing effective and fair responses to complex migration challenges. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates how the late or inaccurate publication of government statistics can generate crises of trust and polarization. It demonstrates that access to clear, timely, and verifiable public information is not merely a technical requirement, but an essential democratic tool to ensure that political decisions are based on observable reality, thereby facilitating accountability and informed deliberation.
