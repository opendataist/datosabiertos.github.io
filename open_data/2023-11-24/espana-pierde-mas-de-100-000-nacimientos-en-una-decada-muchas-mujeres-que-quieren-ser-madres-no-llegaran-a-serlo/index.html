España pierde más de 100.000 nacimientos en una década: "Muchas mujeres que quieren ser madres no llegarán a serlo"

The article highlights a significant demographic shift in Spain, characterized by a sustained decline in birth rates coupled with a rise in maternal age. This trend is primarily driven by structural barriers such as economic instability and housing difficulties, which delay family planning despite strong personal desires for motherhood. Consequently, society is adapting to an aging population where fewer children are being born to older women, reflecting a fundamental change in social and reproductive patterns rather than a lack of intent to have children. The implications of this demographic transformation extend beyond statistics, posing serious challenges for the long-term sustainability of the pension system and increasing pressure on healthcare and social services for the elderly. Experts emphasize that while alarmism is unnecessary, public policies must evolve to support workforce participation and ensure dignified living conditions for all generations. The focus must shift toward creating an environment where young adults can achieve the economic security required to raise families, thereby addressing the root causes of low fertility. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it illustrates how detailed, disaggregated demographic statistics can reveal complex societal issues that aggregate numbers often obscure. By making granular data on age, origin, and marital status accessible, researchers and policymakers can analyze the intersection of economic policy and social behavior more effectively. Open data facilitates a deeper understanding of these trends, enabling evidence-based decision-making that addresses the underlying structural inequalities affecting fertility rates and future social welfare.
