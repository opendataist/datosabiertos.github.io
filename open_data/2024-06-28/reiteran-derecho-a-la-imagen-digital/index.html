Reiteran derecho a la imagen digital

The protection of the right to one’s digital image is essential for preserving personal dignity and autonomy in the technological era. This right establishes that, without explicit consent, no one may publish or use a person’s image, except when there is a justified legal order. This safeguard seeks to balance the ease with which technology enables the capture and dissemination of information, ensuring that individuals retain control over their visual and auditory identity. The relevance to open data lies in the tension between public transparency and individual privacy. By ensuring that images are only disclosed with authorization or a legal mandate, an essential ethical framework is reinforced for the responsible management of personal data. This requires institutions to implement strict anonymization and consent protocols, ensuring that the openness of information does not violate people’s fundamental rights. Moreover, this protection sets a crucial legal precedent by linking privacy with freedom of expression. Recognizing these rights as an integral part of human rights enables the creation of a fairer digital ecosystem. Finally, by prioritizing consent, a culture of respect in data handling is fostered, demonstrating that transparency must always operate within the limits that protect citizens’ integrity.
