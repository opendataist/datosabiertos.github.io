Coinbase sues SEC, FDIC for information relating to crypto regulation

Coinbase has initiated legal action against the SEC and FDIC to compel the release of internal documents regarding their regulatory approaches to the digital asset industry. The company alleges a coordinated effort by these agencies to restrict crypto firms from accessing the federal banking system, a strategy critics term "Operation Chokepoint 2.0." By seeking transparency, Coinbase aims to expose what it perceives as an unfair campaign to stifle growth and clarity in the sector. This move highlights a critical conflict between established financial regulators and emerging crypto businesses regarding access to essential banking infrastructure. The lawsuits stem from denied Freedom of Information Act requests for details on SEC investigations and FDIC directives that discouraged banks from servicing crypto clients. This struggle underscores the broader industry concern that regulatory ambiguity and coordinated pressure are effectively excluding digital asset companies from the mainstream financial economy. This case is vital to open data discussions as it demonstrates the tension between government transparency mandates and agency secrecy. It illustrates how FOIA requests are being used as a strategic tool to uncover hidden regulatory practices and policy coordination. The ongoing battle reveals the challenges of obtaining non-public government records, even when legally entitled, emphasizing the need for improved data accessibility and accountability in financial regulation.
