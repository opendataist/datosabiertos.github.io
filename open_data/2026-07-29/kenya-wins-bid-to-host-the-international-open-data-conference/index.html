Kenya wins Bid to host the International Open Data Conference

The International Open Data Conference (IODC) is transitioning to Africa for the first time, with Nairobi, Kenya, selected as the host for the 2020 event. This decision underscores a significant shift in the global open data landscape, highlighting Africa’s emerging role in shaping data policies. The selection was driven by Kenya’s demonstrated high-level political commitment and consistent leadership in fostering an inclusive data ecosystem that integrates government, private sector, and civil society stakeholders. Central to this move is the conference’s theme, “Bridging Data Communities,” which aims to strengthen South-South collaboration and leverage data to address critical development challenges. By hosting over three thousand participants from around the world, the event seeks to accelerate international cooperation on key issues such as health, agriculture, and gender equality. This gathering serves as a platform to share best practices, technologies, and tactics, ensuring that open data initiatives effectively support global priorities like the Sustainable Development Goals and climate agreements. This development is highly relevant to the open data field as it democratizes access to high-level discourse and innovation, moving the conversation beyond traditional Western hubs. It validates the importance of local leadership and regional context in driving open data adoption, encouraging other developing nations to follow suit. The event promises to enhance global interoperability and impact, proving that open data is a rational economic and political choice essential for inclusive development and leaving no one behind.
