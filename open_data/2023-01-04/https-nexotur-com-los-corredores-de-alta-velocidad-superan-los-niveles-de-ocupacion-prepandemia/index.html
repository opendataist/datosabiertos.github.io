The recovery of Spain’s high-speed rail sector highlights a significant shift in market dynamics following the pandemic. Most routes surpassed pre-pandemic passenger volumes, driven by intense competition among operators such as Ouigo, Renfe, and Iryo. This competitive landscape has altered market shares, with low-cost providers gaining substantial ground. For open data, this trend underscores the necessity of monitoring diverse market players to understand how deregulation influences consumer choice and service accessibility, rather than observing state monopolies. Despite overall growth, regional disparities persist, with some corridors still below historical levels. Meanwhile, commuter and conventional long-distance services showed resilience, boosted by public policy measures such as ticket subsidies. These findings suggest that while commercial competition drives expansion, public service obligations remain crucial for maintaining broad mobility access. Analyzing these varied trends requires granular data to distinguish between commercial successes and policy-driven usage, offering insights into effective transportation strategies. Freight transport, however, experienced a contraction, revealing seasonal vulnerabilities in the logistics sector. Renfe Mercancías maintains dominance, but smaller operators show distinct growth patterns. This data is relevant to open data initiatives because it provides transparency into market concentration and operational efficiency. By making such detailed performance metrics publicly available, researchers and policymakers can evaluate the impact of competition on sustainability and infrastructure utilization, fostering evidence-based decisions for future transport planning.
