Turkey has recorded a dramatic increase in gender-based murders, with a significant majority committed by current or former partners. This alarming trend highlights severe systemic failures in protecting women from intimate partner violence. The data, compiled by feminist organizations rather than official state channels, underscores a critical gap in public health statistics. These independent counts reveal higher rates than official metrics, suggesting that marginalized voices are essential for accurate societal monitoring. This case is vital for open data because it demonstrates how civil society fills information voids left by governments. It emphasizes the necessity of transparent, accessible, and community-driven datasets to effectively track human rights abuses and drive policy change.
