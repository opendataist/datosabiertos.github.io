La ciberseguridad como protección del derecho a los datos personales relativos a la salud · Noticias Jurídicas

The article examines the critical intersection between health data protection and cybersecurity in the healthcare sector, highlighting that the legal framework, primarily the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), classifies medical information as particularly sensitive. This classification obliges institutions to implement rigorous security measures that go beyond mere regulatory compliance, requiring strict confidentiality, explicit consent, and risk management through impact assessments and the appointment of data protection officers. In response to the growing digitalization and connectivity of medical devices, the text identifies structural vulnerabilities such as the use of outdated hardware and poorly designed networks, which expose organizations to cyberattacks with serious consequences for patient privacy and care continuity. To mitigate these risks, pseudonymization is proposed as a key technique that enables data processing for clinical or research purposes without compromising the identity of the data subject, thereby balancing data utility with security. The relevance of this study for open data lies in the need to find secure models that allow the sharing and reuse of health data. By demonstrating how techniques like pseudonymization protect privacy while facilitating access to information, the article offers a practical model for removing legal and technical barriers. This is essential for promoting transparency and data-driven innovation within healthcare, ensuring that the release of information does not infringe upon citizens' fundamental rights.
