FOIA Docs Show Biden Disinfo Board’s Internal Reach, Facebook Connections

The release of documents via a Freedom of Information Act request reveals that the Biden administration’s short-lived Disinformation Governance Board was actively coordinating with major federal agencies and Big Tech executives before its public announcement. Internal communications suggest the board had already initiated workflow planning and engaged with intelligence and security entities, contradicting official claims that it had not yet begun operations. This discrepancy highlights a significant lack of transparency regarding the board’s actual status and activities during its early development phase. The articles highlight concerns that the board’s mandate to address issues like election integrity and vaccine efficacy as "homeland security risks" may have crossed into politicization. By categorizing specific public health and political queries as security threats, the initiative raised serious questions about whether government agencies were maintaining the impartiality required by law. These implications suggest a potential erosion of objective intelligence gathering and civil liberties, as state actors appeared to align with corporate partners to manage information flows in ways that could suppress dissenting viewpoints under the guise of national security. This story is critically relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparency in government-technology partnerships. When agencies collaborate with private corporations to shape public discourse, the lack of accessible, redacted, or unredacted records prevents public accountability. The incident demonstrates how opaque decision-making processes and withheld documentation can obscure the true scope of influence between state and corporate entities, reinforcing the need for robust open data standards to ensure democratic oversight and prevent the misuse of public institutions for political ends.
