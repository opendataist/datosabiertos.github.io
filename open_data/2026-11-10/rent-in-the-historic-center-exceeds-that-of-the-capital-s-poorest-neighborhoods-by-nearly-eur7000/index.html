Rent in the historic center exceeds that of the capital's poorest neighborhoods by nearly €7,000

The article highlights the profound economic divide within the province of Segovia, exemplified by the stark disparity in income between neighboring municipalities such as Villeguillo and Sotosalbos. This financial gap not only reveals significant territorial inequalities but has also widened over time, while other localities have experienced more moderate growth or even declines. This contrast illustrates how geographic location largely determines economic opportunities, challenging the homogeneity suggested by provincial averages. In the urban sphere, the city of Segovia reflects similar dynamics of segregation, where income levels vary considerably depending on the district and residential history. Historic and commercial areas concentrate higher incomes, whereas older or peripheral residential neighborhoods show lower incomes, although not reaching levels of extreme poverty. These internal differences demonstrate that inequality is not exclusive to rural settings but also structures everyday life within the city, creating unequal opportunities even on a small scale. The relevance of this report for open data lies in the importance of having and analyzing detailed, georeferenced datasets to understand the socioeconomic reality beyond general averages. By making granular information on income distribution available, it facilitates the precise identification of inequalities, enabling researchers, policymakers, and citizens to become aware of territorial divergences. This transparency is crucial for designing effective public policies and addressing the specific needs of each community, promoting greater social equity based on accessible and understandable evidence.
