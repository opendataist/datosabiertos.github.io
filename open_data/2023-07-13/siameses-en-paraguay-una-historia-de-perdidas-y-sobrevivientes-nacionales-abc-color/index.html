Siameses en Paraguay: una historia de pérdidas y sobrevivientes - Nacionales - ABC Color

The article documents the recurrent occurrence of conjoined twins in Paraguay over the past few decades, highlighting the medical complexity of their cases and the high mortality rate associated with separation surgeries. By analyzing clinical records spanning from 1996 to the present, it becomes evident that although surgical technology has advanced, survival remains uncertain when infants share vital organs such as the liver or heart, underscoring the vulnerability of these conditions and the delicate nature of medical decision-making involved. The relevance of this text to the field of open data lies in the critical need to systematize and democratize historical and clinical information. The fragmentation of records across different hospitals and media archives hinders precise epidemiological analysis and health planning. Transparent and unified access to this data would enable researchers and healthcare professionals to identify patterns, improve care protocols, and develop better treatment strategies, transforming scattered clinical experience into actionable collective knowledge. Ultimately, the publication serves as a reminder that behind each medical case lies a body of historical information that, if kept opaque, limits scientific progress. Transparency in medical records not only honors the memory of patients and their families but is also essential for building a solid evidence base. This drives the creation of open data repositories that facilitate multidisciplinary research and contribute to reducing mortality in complex congenital conditions through continuous learning derived from past cases.
