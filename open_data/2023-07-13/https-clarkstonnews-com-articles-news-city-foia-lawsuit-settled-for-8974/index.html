Susan Bisio’s persistent efforts to access public records concerning the Millpond Inn Bed and Breakfast resulted in a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit against the City of Clarkston, which settled for nearly nine thousand dollars in attorney fees. This outcome highlights the significant financial burden municipalities face when they fail to comply with transparency laws, underscoring the critical importance of proactive disclosure for local governments. The incident reveals a pattern of resistance from city officials, who previously avoided discussing sensitive land development issues in public sessions despite legal advice to the contrary. Bisio’s history of litigating similar cases indicates that systematic opacity is not an isolated error but a recurring structural failure. This repeated need for judicial intervention demonstrates how inadequate record-keeping practices erode public trust and necessitate costly legal remedies to enforce basic open data rights. For the open data community, this case serves as a stark warning about the consequences of treating public records as discretionary rather than mandatory. It emphasizes the urgent need for robust compliance strategies and specialized expertise to navigate information requests effectively. Ultimately, prioritizing transparency can prevent expensive litigation and ensure that taxpayer resources are preserved rather than spent on legal battles over withheld information.
