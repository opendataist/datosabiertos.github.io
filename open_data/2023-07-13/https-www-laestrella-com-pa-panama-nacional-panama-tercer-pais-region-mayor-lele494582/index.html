The recent presentation of Panama’s census results highlights a transformative shift in ethnic self-identification, particularly among Afro-descendant and Indigenous populations. Contrary to assumptions of exponential demographic growth, the significant increase in individuals recognizing their ethnic heritage reflects a deeper social process of sensitization and pride. This data reveals that accurate self-perception is now more widely expressed, challenging previous criticisms of the census and demonstrating a growing societal comfort with diversity. This evolution underscores the critical importance of precise, accessible demographic data for fostering inclusive governance. Accurate census information enables policymakers to develop targeted strategies that promote equality and inclusion for marginalized groups. By understanding the specific needs and distribution of diverse populations, governments can move beyond generic approaches, ensuring that infrastructure, services, and social programs effectively address the realities of all citizens, including those living outside traditional ethnic territories. The relevance of this article to open data lies in its demonstration of how transparent, high-quality statistical releases drive social progress. When census data is publicly available and accurately interpreted, it serves as a powerful tool for accountability and social awareness. The shift from contested numbers to widely accepted metrics illustrates the value of open data in validating lived experiences, thereby empowering communities and guiding evidence-based policies that reflect the true composition and needs of the nation.
