La inflación cae más de un punto en junio, al 1,9%, y la subyacente se relaja hasta el 5,9%

Spain’s inflation rate has fallen to 1.9%, marking the lowest level since March 2021. This significant deceleration is primarily driven by substantial price drops in transport, food, and housing. While energy-related costs have decreased, food inflation remains relatively high but is trending downward, reflecting broader economic stabilization across all autonomous communities. The decline is largely attributed to lower fuel prices and reduced increases in essential goods compared to the previous year. Conversely, leisure and culture costs have risen slightly due to tourism packages. Despite these sectoral variations, the general trend shows a consistent easing of price pressures nationwide, with the harmonized index also showing marked improvement. This data highlights the temporary relief in living costs, offering a clearer picture of consumer purchasing power dynamics in the current economic climate. This report is relevant to open data because it exemplifies the necessity of accessible, high-quality statistical indicators for public scrutiny. Transparent release of inflation metrics by national institutes allows researchers and citizens to verify economic health independently. Open access to such detailed breakdowns fosters accountability, enables data journalism to uncover regional disparities, and empowers users to build custom analyses that challenge official narratives or identify emerging trends in consumer behavior.
