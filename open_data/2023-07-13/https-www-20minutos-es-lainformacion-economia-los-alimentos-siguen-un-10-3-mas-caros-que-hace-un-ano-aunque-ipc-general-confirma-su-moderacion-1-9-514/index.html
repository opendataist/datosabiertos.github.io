Inflation in Spain has fallen for the first time in two years to below the two percent threshold, consolidating the country as one of the most stable economies in the euro area. This decline, driven primarily by falling energy prices and moderation in food costs, represents a significant reprieve for the purchasing power of citizens and businesses. The trend aligns with the European Central Bank’s objective of ensuring price stability, offering a perspective of economic improvement following a period of high volatility. However, this slowdown, largely attributable to the “base effect” of comparisons with previous years, should not mask the persistence of underlying inflationary pressures. Although the overall indicator is declining, the cost of basic necessities remains considerably higher than it was a year ago, maintaining pressure on household budgets. This delicate situation directly influences fiscal policies, such as the extension of reduced taxes on essential goods, which depend on prices not falling too rapidly so as not to lose their urgent character as social aid. This report is relevant to the field of open data because it highlights the critical importance of transparency and accessibility in macroeconomic indicators. Accurate analysis of data such as the Consumer Price Index (CPI) enables citizens, researchers, and developers to understand the real implications of government policies and market fluctuations. The open dissemination of these data facilitates independent verification, promotes civic education on economics, and fosters the creation of digital tools that translate complex statistics into information understandable for society.
