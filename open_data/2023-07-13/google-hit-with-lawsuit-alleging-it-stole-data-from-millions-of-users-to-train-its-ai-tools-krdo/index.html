Google hit with lawsuit alleging it stole data from millions of users to train its AI tools | KRDO

A major class action lawsuit alleges that Google scraped personal and copyrighted data without consent to train its artificial intelligence models, fundamentally challenging the industry’s reliance on unrestricted data harvesting. The suit argues that using publicly accessible information for AI development infringes on user property rights and copyright laws, creating a legal battleground over the ethical and legal boundaries of data usage in machine learning. This case highlights the critical tension between technological advancement and individual privacy, suggesting that current practices may exploit user data in ways consumers never intended or understood. The plaintiffs seek to halt the commercial use of these AI tools and demand compensation, emphasizing that "publicly available" does equate to "free to use" for any commercial purpose. This implies a potential shift where individuals must have explicit control and opt-out mechanisms for their digital footprints when used for AI training. The article is highly relevant to open data because it questions the assumption that public data is a free resource for AI development. It forces a re-evaluation of open data principles, indicating that while data may be publicly accessible, its secondary use for training models carries significant legal and ethical liabilities. This case could lead to stricter regulations on how open data is utilized, potentially requiring greater transparency, attribution, and consent mechanisms for those building AI systems.
