Unemployment in Costa Rica has remained stable at around 12% since mid-2022, with no significant downward trend. This stagnation highlights persistent structural labor market challenges, indicating that recent economic fluctuations have not translated into tangible job creation or reduction for the broader workforce. The data reveals a stark gender disparity, as women face unemployment rates double those of men. This lack of improvement underscores a systemic inequality that requires targeted policy interventions, as current conditions have failed to close the gender gap or alleviate the disproportionate burden on female job seekers. Furthermore, nearly half of employed individuals work in the informal sector, reflecting deep-seated precarity. This insight is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistics can expose entrenched socio-economic vulnerabilities and drive evidence-based reforms for labor rights and economic inclusion.
