Articles on methanol poisoning reveal serious life-threatening risks. This is relevant to open data because it highlights the urgency of accessibility in public health data, enabling citizens and researchers to monitor health alerts in real time, update information on adulterated products, and facilitate rapid responses that save lives. Authorities emphasize the need to report suspicious businesses, underscoring the importance of open communication channels. Transparency in these reports fosters citizen participation in food safety, a core principle of open data, by empowering communities to protect themselves from chemical risks in beverages and unregulated multi-purpose alcohols. The persistence of the health alert underscores the critical need for interoperable data systems among ministries and regulatory agencies. Open data can improve the traceability of such incidents by analyzing spatial and temporal trends to prevent future outbreaks, demonstrating how free access to government information is vital for public health and institutional accountability.
