National Data Governance Policy: What is it and what are some concerns around it

National Data Governance Policy: What is it and what are some concerns around it

India’s government is advancing a National Data Governance Policy to grant startups and academia access to anonymized data, aiming to stimulate innovation and research. This initiative follows years of deliberation and previous draft frameworks, signaling a persistent effort to balance data utility with governance. By facilitating access to non-personal data, the policy seeks to remove barriers for emerging entities that lack the resources to collect large-scale datasets independently. However, the reliance on anonymization presents significant technical risks, as studies indicate that anonymized data can often be re-identified through sophisticated techniques. This vulnerability challenges the core assumption that such data is safe for broad dissemination. Furthermore, the policy’s scope regarding private entities remains unclear, raising complex questions about intellectual property rights and fair compensation if companies are mandated to share their proprietary data assets with third parties. This article is highly relevant to open data discussions because it highlights the tension between data accessibility and privacy preservation. It underscores the critical need for robust anonymization standards and clear regulatory frameworks that protect individual rights without stifling the open data ecosystem. The ongoing debate illustrates how technical limitations and legal ambiguities can complicate the promise of open data, urging stakeholders to prioritize security and equitable access in future policy designs.
