Canada, Australia, Belgium, pledge to support NEITI on open data policy

The open data community benefits significantly as major diplomatic missions in Nigeria formally endorse the Nigeria Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative’s commitment to transparency. By pledging technical assistance and skills development, Belgium, Australia, and Canada validate the critical role of international cooperation in sustaining robust data infrastructure. This high-level diplomatic support underscores the global importance of accessible, verified information in combating corruption within resource-heavy sectors. Establishing the new NEITI Data Center represents a pivotal shift toward centralized, aggregated, and disaggregated data storage. This facility ensures that historical and current information on oil, gas, and minerals is easily accessible to civil society, media, and government bodies. Such transparency empowers stakeholders to scrutinize industry activities effectively, fostering accountability and informed public discourse regarding national resource management. The initiative also addresses the urgent need for evidence-based decision-making amidst the global energy transition. By enhancing data science capabilities and promoting sustainable advocacy, the project strengthens Nigeria’s ability to manage hydrocarbon dependencies responsibly. This alignment with open data principles not only supports local reform but also serves as a model for how developing nations can leverage international partnerships to ensure equitable and transparent resource governance.
