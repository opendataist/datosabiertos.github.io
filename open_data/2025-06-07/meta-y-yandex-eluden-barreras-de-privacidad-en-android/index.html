Meta y Yandex eluden barreras de privacidad en Android

Meta y Yandex eluden barreras de privacidad en Android

Researchers revealed that apps from Meta and Yandex bypassed Android’s privacy protections to secretly track users’ web activity. This finding demonstrates that companies can collect personal data without consent, circumventing even the security measures established by Google. The exposed vulnerability represents a serious breach of user trust, which experts have compared to malware techniques, underscoring the current fragility of digital ecosystems in the face of aggressive business practices. The relevance to the open data community lies in how these practices distort transparency and ethics in data collection. By operating through undeclared “backdoors,” these actions conceal the true nature of data flows, preventing individuals from understanding what information is extracted and how it is used. This directly undermines the core principles of open data, which seek to ensure clarity and user control over their digital footprint, rather than allowing hidden data collection for advertising profiling. The response includes recommendations to migrate to browsers with strong privacy protections and to prioritize the use of websites over native apps. This incident serves as a critical reminder that technical safeguards alone are insufficient if corporate policies prioritize surveillance over consent. Exposing these weaknesses encourages greater demand for open and auditable standards, which are essential for restoring fairness in the exchange of data between companies and users.
