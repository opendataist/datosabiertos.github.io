Educación y trabajo: urge integración de los sectores más rezagados del empleo informal - Economía - ABC Color

Educación y trabajo: urge integración de los sectores más rezagados del empleo informal - Economía - ABC Color

The article highlights a persistent and significant educational disparity within Paraguay’s labor market, revealing that employment quality is deeply stratified by sector and formality. Public sector employees maintain the highest educational attainment, consistently averaging nearly 15 years of schooling, which underscores the value of formal state employment in human capital development. In sharp contrast, informal workers and unpaid family laborers remain severely disadvantaged, possessing less than half the educational years of their public counterparts, thereby perpetuating a cycle of limited professional mobility and economic vulnerability. This structural inequality suggests that the current labor model heavily favors those with established formal connections, while self-employed individuals and domestic workers face substantial barriers to upward mobility due to lower qualification levels. Although the national average for years of schooling has seen a modest improvement recently, the stagnation among the most vulnerable groups indicates that overall progress is uneven. The widening gap between formal and informal sectors implies that economic growth may not be inclusive, as a large portion of the workforce lacks the necessary skills to compete in higher-productivity environments. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible labor statistics can expose systemic inequities that hinder national development. By making these detailed educational metrics available, policymakers and civil society can better monitor the effectiveness of social programs and advocate for targeted interventions. Open access to such granular data empowers stakeholders to demand evidence-based reforms, ensuring that efforts to close the educational gap are informed by reality rather than aggregated averages that may mask deep-seated disparities.
