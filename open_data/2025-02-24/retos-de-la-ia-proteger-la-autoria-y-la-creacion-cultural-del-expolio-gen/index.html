Retos de la IA: proteger la autoría y la creación cultural del expolio ‘gen’

Retos de la IA: proteger la autoría y la creación cultural del expolio ‘gen’

The Spanish government has withdrawn a proposed decree aimed at regulating generative AI licenses, acknowledging the lack of consensus regarding the use of collective licenses for training models. This retreat highlights the intense conflict between protecting creators' copyright and facilitating AI development, revealing a legislative struggle to adapt European norms to local contexts while balancing technological advancement with ethical considerations. A central issue is the perceived unauthorized appropriation of protected works for algorithmic training, which creators argue constitutes theft and devalues their professional expertise. While some see this technological shift as inevitable, others insist on the necessity of informed consent, advocating for opt-in models that respect authors' rights. This debate underscores the difficulty of establishing fair economic redistribution when large tech companies utilize cultural assets without adequate permission or compensation. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical tension between data openness for innovation and the legal frameworks required to protect intellectual property. It demonstrates that open access to data, such as artistic works, cannot exist in a legal vacuum; instead, it requires robust, negotiated standards that respect ownership. The failure to reach an agreement suggests that future open data initiatives must prioritize clear governance structures and stakeholder dialogue to avoid similar legislative impasses and ensure equitable data usage.
