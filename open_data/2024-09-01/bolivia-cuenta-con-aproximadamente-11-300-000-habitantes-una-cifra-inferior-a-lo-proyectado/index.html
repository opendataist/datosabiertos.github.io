Bolivia cuenta con aproximadamente 11.300.000 habitantes, una cifra inferior a lo proyectado

The recent national census in Bolivia reveals a population significantly lower than official projections, sparking intense political controversy and tension between the central government and regional opposition forces. Critics argue that the discrepancy is not merely statistical but a strategic move to reduce fiscal transfers and political representation to key urban centers, specifically accusing the administration of manipulating data to weaken their influence. This dispute highlights how demographic statistics are directly tied to the distribution of public resources and electoral power, transforming a technical exercise into a high-stakes political battle. Underlying these political tensions are profound societal shifts, particularly the dramatic decline in fertility rates and changing family structures, which contribute to slower population growth. The data indicates that Bolivians are marrying later, divorcing more frequently, and having fewer children, mirroring trends seen in neighboring countries. These demographic realities challenge previous assumptions about rapid population expansion and suggest that future economic and social planning must account for an aging population and smaller household sizes rather than relying on outdated growth models. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of transparency, methodological rigor, and public trust in official statistics. When citizens and political actors perceive data as politicized or opaque, it undermines the integrity of public administration and hinders effective policy-making. Ensuring that census processes are independently verifiable and accessible fosters accountability, allowing society to rely on evidence-based decisions for resource allocation and long-term development strategies.
