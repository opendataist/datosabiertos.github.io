Datos del Censo 2024 dejaron más dudas que certezas

Datos del Censo 2024 dejaron más dudas que certezas

The recent release of the 2024 Census results by the National Institute of Statistics has sparked widespread rejection and demands for a technical audit, as the data show a significantly lower population growth than previously projected. This discrepancy has triggered a credibility crisis, with economists, regional governments, and civil society arguing that the figures do not reflect the evident demographic and economic dynamics observed in everyday life. The lack of alignment with other institutional records, such as voter registration, points to serious problems with the survey’s methodology and execution. The implications of these results extend beyond demographic statistics, directly affecting fiscal policy and the allocation of municipal resources. Since tax sharing is based on population data, nearly half of the country’s municipalities will receive reduced funding, while others will see gains smaller than expected. This creates immediate practical challenges for local governance, as budget planning relies heavily on accurate population metrics to ensure adequate services and infrastructure investments for their constituents. This controversy is highly relevant to open data, as it underscores the critical importance of transparency and methodological rigor in statistical production. When official data are perceived as inconsistent or opaque, public trust erodes, making it difficult to use these figures for evidence-based policymaking. The situation highlights the need for open, accessible explanations of how census data are collected and processed, ensuring that stakeholders can verify the information’s validity and maintain confidence in the statistical systems that underpin national development.
