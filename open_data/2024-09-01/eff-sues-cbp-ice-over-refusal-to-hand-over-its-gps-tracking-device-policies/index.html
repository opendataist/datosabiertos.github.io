EFF Sues CBP, ICE Over Refusal To Hand Over Its GPS Tracking Device Policies

Recent legal proceedings revealed that government agencies employed warrantless GPS tracking on vehicles traveling far inland, exceeding the scope of the border search exception. This unconstitutional surveillance led to the dismissal of charges, highlighting significant flaws in how law enforcement justifies privacy intrusions under existing legal frameworks. Consequently, the public has been denied transparency regarding the specific policies governing such surveillance tactics. The Electronic Frontier Foundation’s attempt to obtain these agency policies through Freedom of Information Act requests was met with refusal and silence. Agencies cited national security exemptions to withhold documents, yet their contradictory responses suggest an effort to maintain secrecy rather than protect legitimate law enforcement operations. This lack of disclosure prevents citizens from understanding the actual standards and limits applied to digital surveillance at borders. This struggle for transparency is crucial for open data advocacy, as it underscores the urgent need for government agencies to make operational policies accessible. Without public access to these rules, accountability mechanisms fail, allowing potential abuses of power to go unchecked. Ensuring that data about government surveillance practices is open and discoverable helps protect civil liberties and maintains trust in democratic institutions.
