Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

This article demonstrates the power of open data in promoting educational transparency and accountability. By leveraging Freedom of Information requests, researchers accessed internal university records that are typically opaque to the general public. This case highlights how releasing specific academic metrics can satisfy public curiosity and allow for a deeper understanding of institutional performance. The availability of such granular data empowers stakeholders, including prospective students and current staff, to make informed decisions based on empirical evidence rather than assumptions. The analysis reveals significant disparities in student attainment across different departments and year groups, with physics and English leading in high grades while foundation years lag behind. It also identifies specific modules with notably low or high success rates, suggesting variations in teaching quality, assessment difficulty, or student preparedness. These patterns imply that standardized grading practices may not account for contextual factors like module type or student cohort size. Understanding these nuances is crucial for addressing educational inequalities and improving curriculum design. Relevance to open data lies in its potential to drive systemic improvement within higher education institutions. When universities make their internal data accessible, it fosters a culture of continuous evaluation and transparency. This openness encourages evidence-based policy changes and supports initiatives aimed at enhancing student outcomes. Ultimately, the article underscores that making educational data public is not just about transparency for its own sake, but about creating a framework for meaningful dialogue and actionable insights that can elevate the quality of education for all students.
