CSIF lamenta el incremento de 15.700 parados en el primer trimestre del año en C-LM

The article highlights a severe labor market crisis characterized by significant job losses, particularly affecting older workers. This dynamic exacerbates intergenerational inequality, as firms prefer younger candidates for cost reasons, leaving youth in precarious positions and pushing older adults into worse contractual conditions or out of the workforce entirely. Prolonged unemployment is emerging as a structural issue, eroding the employability of long-term job seekers and increasing their vulnerability. The union argues that current trends threaten social cohesion, necessitating realistic insertion policies to prevent these temporary hardships from becoming permanent structural barriers to employment. This case is relevant to open data as it underscores the need for transparent, accessible statistics to identify vulnerable groups and evaluate policy effectiveness. Public data on employment demographics enables civil society to hold institutions accountable and advocate for resource reallocation, ensuring that information supports equitable labor market strategies and protects at-risk populations.
