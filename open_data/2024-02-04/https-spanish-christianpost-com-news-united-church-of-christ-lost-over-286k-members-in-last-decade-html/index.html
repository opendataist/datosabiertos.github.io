The United Church of Christ has experienced a significant demographic decline over the past decade, losing hundreds of thousands of members and congregations. This downturn is not merely numerical but structural, marked by the active removal of inactive churches due to new administrative policies and historical tensions with social progressivism. The prevailing narrative suggests that the denomination faces an existential challenge, having shrunk dramatically from its mid-20th-century peak and now adjusting to a smaller-scale reality. However, the data reveal important nuances that counter the narrative of total crisis. Smaller congregations tend to exhibit greater community cohesion and higher levels of voluntary participation, suggesting that the quality of religious engagement may be increasing within the remaining core communities. Moreover, internal diversity remains limited, with an overrepresentation of white members and low representation of other minorities, raising questions about the effectiveness of its current community outreach in reflecting the demographic diversity of its surrounding environment. This report is relevant to open data because it exemplifies how transparency in the collection and publication of sectoral statistics enables critical analysis of complex social trends. By making detailed data on membership, demographics, and institutional management accessible, it becomes possible to audit the organizational health of public or semi-public entities. This accessibility fosters accountability and provides valuable insights for academics and citizens regarding the impact of internal policies on the sustainability of civil and religious institutions.
