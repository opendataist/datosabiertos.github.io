Several prominent open source companies, including HashiCorp, are shifting from traditional open source licenses to non-compete models like the Business Source License. This trend allows commercial entities to restrict competitive use after achieving significant market success, arguing it is necessary to capture value and protect against large technology providers. While this strategy offers short-term financial benefits for shareholders, it fundamentally alters the nature of the software by restricting the freedom to redistribute and modify code for commercial purposes, moving away from the core principles of open source. This shift has triggered widespread community distrust and backlash, as users feel betrayed by the sudden withdrawal of previously granted freedoms. The ambiguity in these new licenses creates legal uncertainty for businesses that rely on the software, prompting calls for forks and the creation of independent foundations to preserve open access. The community emphasizes that these projects were built on years of unpaid volunteer labor, and restricting their use undermines the collaborative ecosystem that drove their initial innovation, potentially damaging the reputation of the entire commercial open source industry. To address this erosion of trust, the article advocates for the adoption of "open charters," which are legal commitments binding companies to remain open source in perpetuity. Unlike standard licenses that can be changed, an open charter ensures that a company’s foundational mission to support public benefit cannot be altered for short-term gain. This mechanism provides a clear distinction between companies genuinely committed to open source and those that may pivot later, offering a sustainable model for balancing commercial viability with the integrity of the open source community. This is critical for open_data advocates who rely on trustworthy, permanently accessible data infrastructure and code to ensure transparency and prevent vendor lock-in or sudden exclusivity in vital technological tools.
