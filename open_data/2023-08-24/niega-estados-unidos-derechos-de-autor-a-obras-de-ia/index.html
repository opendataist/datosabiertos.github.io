Niega Estados Unidos derechos de autor a obras de IA

Niega Estados Unidos derechos de autor a obras de IA

A recent ruling by a U.S. federal court denies copyright protection to works created by artificial intelligence, establishing that human authorship is a fundamental prerequisite. The judge emphasized that the essential link between the human mind and creative expression cannot be bypassed, aligning the United States with the global majority that rejects AI authorship, despite divergent approaches in countries like China. This decision reflects the current legal insistence on human involvement for copyright eligibility. However, this legal stance may fail to address the deeper technological disruption facing creative industries. The threat posed by AI is not a temporary anomaly but a structural shift reminiscent of past innovations like recording technology or the internet, which initially displaced traditional roles before the intellectual property system adapted. Denying protection is an inadequate response to the systemic changes reshaping how art and content are produced and consumed in a rapidly evolving digital landscape. This case is highly relevant to open data and open culture advocates because it highlights the tension between traditional copyright frameworks and emerging automated creation methods. The ultimate resolution suggests that property laws will inevitably evolve to assign rights, potentially to the software creators or coordinating entities, rather than abolishing protection entirely. Understanding these shifts is crucial for navigating future open access models where AI generates significant volumes of data and creative content, challenging existing notions of ownership and accessibility.
