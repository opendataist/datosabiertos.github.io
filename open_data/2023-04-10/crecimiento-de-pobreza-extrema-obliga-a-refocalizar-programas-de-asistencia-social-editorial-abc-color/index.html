Crecimiento de pobreza extrema obliga a refocalizar programas de asistencia social - Editorial - ABC Color

The article highlights a critical divergence in Paraguay's social statistics: while overall poverty rates remained stable, extreme poverty surged significantly. This alarming trend underscores a fundamental failure in current social policies, as substantial state investments are not effectively reaching the most vulnerable populations who desperately need immediate assistance. The text distinguishes between structural poverty, which requires long-term development strategies, and extreme poverty, which demands direct state intervention through subsidies. However, the author argues that existing programs are poorly targeted and plagued by inefficiencies, corruption, and bureaucratic waste. Consequently, despite massive budgets allocated to social services, the resources are being diverted away from those suffering from indigence, leading to a rise in the number of people unable to meet basic nutritional needs. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the necessity of transparent, accessible, and granular data to evaluate policy impact. Without open access to detailed information on beneficiary lists, fund allocation, and outcome metrics, citizens and watchdogs cannot detect the discrepancies between intended goals and actual results. Improving data openness is essential to expose these structural flaws, ensure accountability, and redesign assistance programs that truly address the needs of the most marginalized communities.
