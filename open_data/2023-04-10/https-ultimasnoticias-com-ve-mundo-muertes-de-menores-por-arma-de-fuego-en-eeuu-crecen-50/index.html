The sharp rise in firearm-related deaths among children and adolescents in the United States reveals a public health crisis that exceeds levels recorded since 1999. This increase is not isolated but part of a nationwide trend, suggesting that gun control policies must address the problem systemically rather than focusing solely on specific incidents. The nature of these deaths, with homicides now the leading cause, indicates growing youth vulnerability to gun violence. The implications extend beyond statistics, affecting social security and life expectancy for new generations, and demanding urgent, evidence-based responses rather than those driven solely by public perceptions. This analysis is relevant to the open data movement because it demonstrates how transparency in publishing health data, such as that from the CDC, enables independent organizations like the Pew Research Center to generate critical insights. The accessibility of these data facilitates evidence-based research, allowing civil society and policymakers to understand the true scale of the problem and design informed solutions that protect the most vulnerable.
