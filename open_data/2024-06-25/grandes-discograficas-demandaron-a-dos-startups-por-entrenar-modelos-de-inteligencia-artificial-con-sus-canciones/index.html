Grandes discográficas demandaron a dos startups por entrenar modelos de Inteligencia Artificial con sus canciones

The lawsuits filed by major record labels against Suno and Udio illustrate the fundamental conflict between the creative industry and unregulated technological development. The major labels argue that training AI models on copyrighted works without licenses constitutes a direct violation of intellectual property rights, threatening the economic sustainability of artists and the cultural quality of creative output. This aggressive legal action seeks to establish a legal precedent that would compel artificial intelligence companies to negotiate compensation and obtain explicit consent, marking a turning point in the protection of digital rights in the face of creative automation. This case is crucial for the open-data ecosystem because it questions the legitimacy of using publicly accessible information as the basis for training algorithms. While the open-data movement promotes transparency and universal access, this litigation highlights the need to distinguish between the technical availability of data and the ethical and legal rights governing its use. If courts rule that the datasets used by these AI systems cannot be derived from copyrighted works without authorization, a dissonance will emerge between the ideals of interoperability and the restrictive realities of modern intellectual property. For transparency advocates, the relevance lies in the demand for auditability in AI development processes. The opacity of companies regarding the exact provenance of their training data prevents verification of regulatory compliance and fairness toward original creators. To advance toward a responsible model, it is vital that data collection and usage practices be clear and respectful of underlying rights, demonstrating that technological innovation should not come at the expense of the uncompensated exploitation of human-generated culture.
