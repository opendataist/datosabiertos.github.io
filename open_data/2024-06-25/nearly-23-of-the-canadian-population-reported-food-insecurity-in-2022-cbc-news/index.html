Nearly 23% of the Canadian population reported food insecurity in 2022 | CBC News

Nearly 23% of the Canadian population reported food insecurity in 2022 | CBC News

The report highlights a significant rise in Canadian food insecurity, with nearly a quarter of the population affected in 2022. This increase is driven by soaring inflation, high food prices, and the withdrawal of pandemic-era financial supports, creating a landscape where many households must compromise on the quality and quantity of their food. The burden falls disproportionately on single-parent families and low-income individuals, revealing deep inequalities in how economic pressures impact different segments of society. These findings are critical to the open data community because they demonstrate how transparent, accessible statistics can expose systemic social issues and inform public discourse. By making granular data on household income and food security available, researchers and advocates can hold institutions accountable and advocate for policy changes. The data transforms abstract economic trends into tangible human realities, such as increased reliance on food banks, thereby validating community observations with rigorous empirical evidence. Ultimately, this data underscores the urgency of monitoring social well-being through open, standardized metrics. It serves as a wake-up call that despite Canada’s wealth, basic needs are not being met for millions. The narrative emphasizes that open data is not merely about recording numbers, but about illuminating the gaps in social safety nets, enabling civil society to push for equitable solutions and ensuring that national progress includes the most vulnerable populations.
