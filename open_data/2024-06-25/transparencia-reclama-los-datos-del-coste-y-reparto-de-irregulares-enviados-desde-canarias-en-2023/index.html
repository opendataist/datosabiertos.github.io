Transparencia reclama los datos del coste y reparto de irregulares enviados desde Canarias en 2023

The article highlights a significant victory for transparency and public accountability in migration management. By successfully leveraging requests to the Council for Transparency and Good Government, citizens have forced the Ministry to disclose precise data on the relocation of migrants from the Canary Islands to mainland Spain. This outcome demonstrates that administrative opacity regarding sensitive state operations, such as emergency housing and border control, can be effectively challenged through legal transparency mechanisms, ensuring that basic logistical details are no longer withheld from the public. A central implication of this case is the state’s responsibility to provide verifiable information about resource distribution and financial costs. The council’s ruling mandates the publication of the specific number of relocated individuals per municipality and the actual expenses incurred. This establishes a precedent that the government cannot claim ignorance of its own operational data. It reinforces the principle that when the state assumes custody of individuals during emergencies, it must maintain centralized, accessible records to prevent the overburdening of specific regions and to ensure equitable management of national resources. This case is highly relevant to open data because it validates the practical utility of transparency portals in exposing governance gaps. It shows that structured data requests can compel ministries to move from vague emergency narratives to concrete, quantifiable disclosures. For the open data community, this serves as a proof-of-concept that persistent civic engagement can unlock critical information about public spending and demographic flows, encouraging further demands for structured, granular data in other areas of public administration.
