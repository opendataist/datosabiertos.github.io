Carlos Ferrás, catedrático de Geografía Humana: “España va hacia un desafío de la inclusión”

The National Institute of Statistics projects a significant population increase in Spain over the coming decades, driven primarily by immigration rather than natural birth rates. This demographic shift implies a transition toward a multicultural society, requiring profound social, cultural, and political adjustments to ensure inclusion and respect for human rights amidst growing diversity. Concurrently, the nation faces a serious aging crisis that threatens the sustainability of pension systems and public services like healthcare and education. The gradual reduction of the working-age population necessitates urgent policy reforms to rejuvenate the active workforce, ensuring the financial viability of the social welfare state and maintaining essential public support structures for future generations. This article is relevant to open data because accurate, longitudinal population projections are fundamental for evidence-based policymaking. Reliable demographic datasets enable governments to model fiscal impacts, plan infrastructure, and design inclusive social strategies. Transparency in these statistics empowers researchers and citizens to understand societal changes, fostering informed public debate on immigration, aging, and the equitable allocation of resources in an evolving demographic landscape.
