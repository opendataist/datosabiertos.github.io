La revisión del INE consagra a la economía española como la peor de la UE en desplome y rebote de la 'era Covid'

The statistical review by Spain’s National Statistics Institute (INE) confirms that the Spanish economy was the worst-performing in the European Union during the pandemic period, experiencing the sharpest decline in 2020 and being the only country that had not fully recovered to its pre-pandemic level by the end of 2022. Although an upward revision of the data allowed authorities to claim that this milestone was reached the previous year, the underlying reality reveals a late and fragile recovery, particularly given that the unemployment rate remains double the European average, suggesting that the economic rebound has not translated into a parallel improvement in the labor market. The inefficiency in leveraging European Recovery Funds stands out as a critical consequence of this sluggishness. Despite being the first country to request funding and receiving the largest allocation based on unemployment and low GDP indicators, the implementation of these projects is plagued by doubts about their real impact on economic activity. The European Commission has warned that any future expansion of funds will require strengthened administrative capacity to ensure efficient absorption, highlighting that structural weaknesses in public management have prevented monetary injections from adequately compensating for the damage caused by the health crisis. This analysis is relevant to the open data community because it illustrates how transparency and continuous updating of statistical metrics are essential for assessing the true state of the economy and the effectiveness of public policies. The discrepancy between initial estimates, final revisions, and structural data such as unemployment underscores the need to integrate diverse and up-to-date sources to avoid misleading interpretations. Furthermore, it highlights the importance of linking economic data with administrative performance indicators, enabling citizens and oversight bodies to audit whether public resources allocated for recovery are being used with the promised efficiency, thereby fostering evidence-based accountability.
