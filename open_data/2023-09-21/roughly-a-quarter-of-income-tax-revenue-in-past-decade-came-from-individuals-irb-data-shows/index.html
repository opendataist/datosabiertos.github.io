Roughly a quarter of income tax revenue in past decade came from individuals, IRB data shows

Malaysia’s individual taxpayers have emerged as a critical pillar of the national fiscal landscape, contributing approximately a quarter of total income tax revenue. This consistent support, despite fluctuations in annual contributions, highlights the vital role citizens play in sustaining public finances, contrasting with the corporate sector which remains the primary source of overall tax income. However, the data reveals a concerning stagnation in the number of new taxpayers joining the system over the past decade. With the taxable population comprising only a small fraction of the total demographic, the narrow base poses a risk to long-term financial stability, underscoring an urgent need to broaden participation beyond current contributors to match population growth. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it demonstrates how accessible public records, specifically from data.gov.my, can illuminate complex socioeconomic trends. By making granular fiscal details transparent, open data enables citizens and policymakers to understand systemic challenges, such as the widening gap between population growth and tax base expansion, fostering informed discourse on fiscal policy.
