14 Liverpool primary schools were investigated for RAAC last year

The recent structural investigations into potential cracks in concrete across Liverpool’s schools have concluded with no evidence of problematic autoclaved aerated concrete found. While national concerns prompted widespread closures elsewhere, local authorities successfully confirmed the safety of the specific sites assessed. This outcome underscores the importance of rigorous, localized structural audits in maintaining public trust and ensuring student safety during national infrastructure crises. The process highlights the critical role of transparent data sharing in crisis management. By disclosing which schools were inspected and releasing the findings, the council demonstrated accountability to the community. This openness is vital for open data initiatives, as it allows researchers and the public to verify the effectiveness of safety protocols and understand how resources are allocated during emergency assessments. Furthermore, the clarity regarding funding responsibilities reinforces the need for clear, accessible datasets regarding government liabilities. Knowing that remedial costs are covered by the central government prevents local financial strain and ensures swift action if issues arise. This example serves as a model for how open data can streamline governance, facilitate accountability, and provide citizens with reliable information about the integrity of public buildings they rely on daily.
