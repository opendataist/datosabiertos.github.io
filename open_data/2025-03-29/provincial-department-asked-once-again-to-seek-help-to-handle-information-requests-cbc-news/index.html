Provincial department asked once again to seek help to handle information requests | CBC News

Provincial department asked once again to seek help to handle information requests | CBC News

Nova Scotia’s acting information and privacy commissioner has formally criticized the Department of Opportunities and Social Development for repeatedly failing to meet legal deadlines under the freedom of information legislation. The department’s handling of a massive request, which involves reviewing thousands of pages of records, has resulted in multiple official reports detailing its non-compliance. Despite previous recommendations to hire additional staff and improve resource allocation, the department has ignored these directives, demonstrating a systemic inability to manage its workload effectively. The persistent delays highlight a critical tension between legislative intent and administrative capacity. While the government previously attempted to amend laws to allow refusing frivolous requests, such efforts faced public backlash and were withdrawn. This context underscores the importance of robust implementation of current laws rather than restricting access. The ongoing review of the legislation suggests that policymakers are aware of these structural flaws but have yet to provide a timely solution, leaving transparency mechanisms weakened by bureaucratic inefficiency. This article is highly relevant to open data advocates because it illustrates how inadequate resource allocation directly impedes the realization of transparency rights. Open data initiatives rely on government bodies being able to process and release information efficiently; when departments lack the capacity to respond to standard freedom of information requests, the broader ecosystem of accessible public data suffers. The case serves as a warning that legal frameworks alone are insufficient without the necessary operational support to ensure citizens can exercise their right to know.
