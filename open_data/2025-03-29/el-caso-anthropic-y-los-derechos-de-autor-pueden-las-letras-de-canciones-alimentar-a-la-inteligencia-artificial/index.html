El caso Anthropic y los derechos de autor: ¿pueden las letras de canciones alimentar a la inteligencia artificial?

El caso Anthropic y los derechos de autor: ¿pueden las letras de canciones alimentar a la inteligencia artificial?

The recent court decision in favor of Anthropic in its dispute with music publishers highlights the tension between artificial intelligence innovation and intellectual property protection. By not finding that there is "irreparable harm," the ruling allows technology companies to continue using protected material under the argument of "fair use" to train their models, provided that no clear economic harm or direct substitution in the traditional licensing market is demonstrated. This case underscores the urgent need to update regulatory frameworks to adapt to the digital age. Unlike previous lawsuits concerning literature or images, musical content presents unique challenges due to the repetition and recognizability of its lyrics, which complicates the distinction between machine learning and direct reproduction. The lack of clear precedents leaves creators in a vulnerable position while companies seek a balance between technological progress and copyright rights. The relevance to open data lies in the pursuit of sustainable models that do not exclude knowledge but integrate it ethically. Instead of blocking access, there is a need to create collective licensing systems and ensure transparency in the origin of training data. This development implies that future AI datasets must balance information availability with respect for intellectual property, establishing key precedents for how knowledge is built and shared in the digital society.
