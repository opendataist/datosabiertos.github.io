Fotos al estilo Ghibli: ¿Cómo crear una imagen con IA? ¿Hay problemas con los derechos de autor?

Fotos al estilo Ghibli: ¿Cómo crear una imagen con IA? ¿Hay problemas con los derechos de autor?

The viral spread of images generated by artificial intelligence that mimic the style of Studio Ghibli has sparked a critical debate about the ethical and legal boundaries of technological use. This phenomenon goes beyond mere digital entertainment, as it exposes the lack of a clear regulatory framework governing intellectual property in the automated era. The speed at which these creations are disseminated forces society and lawmakers to reassess how authorship is protected in contexts where human intervention is minimal. The core of the controversy lies in the nature of contemporary artistic creation and the defense of copyright. Since machines lack legal personality, a fundamental question arises: who assumes responsibility when AI tools reproduce protected styles or content without explicit licensing? This situation highlights the vulnerability of traditional artists facing models trained on existing works, questioning the ethical legitimacy of using human creations to feed commercial algorithms without consent or compensation. This article is relevant to the open data movement because it illustrates the inherent tension between the massive use of data and the protection of intellectual property. By discussing whether models should be trained on open or licensed content, the importance of algorithmic transparency and fair access to information is underscored. Understanding these conflicts is essential for developing data policies that balance technological innovation with the moral and economic rights of original creators.
