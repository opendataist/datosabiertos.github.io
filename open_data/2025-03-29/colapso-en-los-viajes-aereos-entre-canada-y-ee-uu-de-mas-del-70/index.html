Colapso en los viajes aéreos entre Canadá y EE.UU. de más del 70%

The article reveals a dramatic collapse in air travel demand between Canada and the United States, driven by the economic uncertainty of the ongoing tariff war and rising safety concerns among travelers. This significant drop in bookings suggests that commercial tensions and political rhetoric are directly influencing consumer behavior, forcing airlines to drastically reduce capacity in response to plummeting interest. The narrative highlights how macroeconomic policies can abruptly alter travel patterns, impacting major carriers that rely heavily on cross-border traffic. By postponing trips due to fears of detention or economic instability, passengers are signaling a loss of trust, which forces the aviation sector to adapt to a new, volatile reality defined by geopolitical friction rather than seasonal trends. This case is highly relevant to open_data because it demonstrates the critical value of transparent, real-time aviation statistics in understanding complex socio-economic dynamics. Analyzing publicly available booking and capacity data allows observers to quantify the tangible impacts of trade policies and social tensions, providing essential evidence for researchers and policymakers assessing the true cost of international relations on mobility and commerce.
