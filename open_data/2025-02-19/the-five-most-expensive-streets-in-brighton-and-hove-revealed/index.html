The five most expensive streets in Brighton and Hove revealed

The five most expensive streets in Brighton and Hove revealed

Analysis of HM Land Registry Open Data reveals significant disparities in property values, where limited transaction volumes create misleading averages for ultra-high-end streets. When filtering for markets with sufficient transaction density, representative price leaders emerge, offering a more accurate reflection of the local housing market’s true tiered structure. These findings highlight the critical importance of data granularity in open datasets. Understanding transaction frequency alongside price is essential for deriving reliable insights, as raw aggregates can obscure market realities when sample sizes are insufficient for statistical validity. The article demonstrates how open housing data enables detailed socioeconomic analysis. By correlating price points with transaction volumes and rental trends, stakeholders can assess market health, detect anomalies, and make informed decisions regarding investment and policy in Brighton and Hove.
