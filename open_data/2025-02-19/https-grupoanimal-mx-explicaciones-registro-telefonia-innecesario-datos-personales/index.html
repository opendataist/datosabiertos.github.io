ES EN

The proposal for mandatory registration of mobile phone users poses a significant risk to citizens' privacy and is considered unnecessary, given that telecom operators are already legally obligated to cooperate with the judiciary. Digital rights experts argue that this measure violates personal data protection, a stance supported by the Supreme Court of Justice in 2022, and lacks real effectiveness against crimes such as extortion, since organized crime typically employs untraceable methods or devices in poorly controlled prison settings. The creation of this centralized database raises fundamental concerns about information security and democratic governance. In the absence of clear guarantees regarding access procedures and without independent institutions safeguarding citizens' rights, there is an increased risk that data could be accessed by unauthorized authorities or be vulnerable to breaches. The lack of robust oversight and transparency mechanisms undermines the public's ability to demand accountability in the event of privacy violations. This article is relevant to the field of open data because it illustrates the dangers of consolidating private records of personal information under state control without adequate transparency and protection frameworks. It highlights the critical importance of having independent authorities to oversee data processing and ensure that public security initiatives do not compromise fundamental rights. Analyzing these controversies helps to understand the need to balance police effectiveness with digital sovereignty and the active protection of individual information against potential abuses of power.
