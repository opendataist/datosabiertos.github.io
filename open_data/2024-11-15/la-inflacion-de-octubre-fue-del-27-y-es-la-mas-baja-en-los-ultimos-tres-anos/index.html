La inflación de octubre fue del 2,7 y es la más baja en los últimos tres años

The National Institute of Statistics and Censuses has reported a significant slowdown in inflation, marking the lowest level in three years. This decline, attributed to strict monetary policies and fiscal adjustments, indicates a process of economic stabilization. The trend suggests that, despite the initial impact, the Argentine economy is transitioning toward a phase of stabilization where prices begin to normalize, reducing the prior distortion caused by the currency devaluation. Economist Federico Vacalebre highlights that this context facilitates the eventual removal of exchange rate restrictions. The drastic reduction in the gap between the official and parallel dollar rates, along with robust reserves, allows for the anticipation of exchange rate unification and convergence toward a more sustainable parity system. This scenario reduces the risk of currency runs, bringing the country closer to a normalized pricing model that balances the exchange rate with residual inflation. This news is relevant to open data because it underscores the vital importance of transparency and the quality of official statistical data, such as that provided by INDEC. For society, investors, and experts to correctly interpret economic signals and trust adjustment policies, it is essential to have accurate, accessible, and real-time indicators. Credibility in the stabilization narrative depends directly on the reliability and openness of public information.
