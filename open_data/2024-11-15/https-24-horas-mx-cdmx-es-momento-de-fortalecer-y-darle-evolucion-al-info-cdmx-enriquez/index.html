Laura Enríquez assumed the presidency of Info CDMX with the critical mission of strengthening the institution and safeguarding the rights to access information and personal data amid uncertainty about its possible extinction. Her strategy prioritizes closeness to citizens and improved handling of requests to public entities, ensuring that democratic gains achieved over the years are not lost. This approach underscores the need to maintain transparency as a fundamental pillar for accountability and effective citizen participation. The relevance of this article to the open data movement lies in the threat facing the National Transparency Platform, which hosts a massive volume of public data at risk of disorganization or loss of control. Legislative uncertainty regarding its future management highlights the urgency of ensuring that these informational assets remain accessible, sustainable, and at the service of the public. Protecting these resources is vital so that civil society and research can continue to use government information as a tool for scrutiny. Finally, the text illustrates how political and legislative changes directly impact open data infrastructure. Although transparency models are imperfect, institutional stability is crucial to avoid backsliding on access standards. The narrative reinforces the idea that the evolution of these systems must occur without sacrificing the integrity of information, ensuring that transparency is not a temporary concession but a guaranteed right, structurally protected against administrative volatility.
