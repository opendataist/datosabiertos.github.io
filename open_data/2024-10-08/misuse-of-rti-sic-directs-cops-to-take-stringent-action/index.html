misuse of RTI SIC directs cops to take stringent action

misuse of RTI SIC directs cops to take stringent action

The State Information Commission has identified a critical trend where individuals weaponize the Right to Information Act to harass citizens under the guise of seeking transparency. By systematically filing excessive requests, particularly regarding illegal constructions, certain applicants exploit bureaucratic compliance to blackmail entities and cause undue distress. This misuse undermines the sanctity of the legislation, transforming a tool for public accountability into an instrument for personal grievance and harassment against ordinary citizens and institutions. To combat this abuse, the Commission has directed police authorities to take stringent action against such manipulators while reiterating the importance of upholding the act’s original intent. The ruling emphasizes that while transparency is vital, the system must not be exploited by those with vested interests who cause unnecessary trouble to the public. Authorities are reminded to balance openness with protection, ensuring that the RTI framework remains robust against bad-faith actors who attempt to circumvent the spirit of the law through volume and repetition rather than genuine civic engagement. This case is highly relevant to open data discussions as it highlights the delicate balance between mandatory disclosure and privacy protection. The Commission’s directive for public bodies to proactively publish information, while masking applicant identities, offers a model for enhancing open data accessibility without enabling misuse. It suggests that proactive, anonymized data sharing can reduce repetitive RTI filings, thereby streamlining government operations and preserving the integrity of open data ecosystems against opportunistic exploitation.
