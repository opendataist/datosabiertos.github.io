«La clase media está en peligro de extinción»

«La clase media está en peligro de extinción»

The article highlights a severe erosion of the middle class in the Balearic Islands, driven by a widening gap between rising living costs—particularly housing—and stagnant wages. Despite economic growth, low productivity and inflation have reduced disposable income, leaving families vulnerable to poverty and unable to cover basic expenses or emergency repairs. This precarious financial state is exacerbated by the unique challenges of insularity and reliance on tourism, which inflate prices without proportionally increasing real earnings. This situation underscores the critical importance of open data in understanding socioeconomic disparities. By providing transparent, standardized metrics on income, housing costs, and poverty risks, open data allows researchers and policymakers to move beyond aggregate statistics. It enables a granular analysis of how specific economic pressures impact different demographic groups, facilitating a more accurate assessment of well-being and inequality across regions. The relevance to open data lies in its capacity to support evidence-based decision-making for complex regional challenges. The author argues that piecemeal policies are insufficient, requiring a multi-level approach grounded in comprehensive data. Accessible, high-quality data is essential for identifying the need for structural reforms, such as diversifying the economy beyond tourism and establishing special regimes to offset insular costs. Ultimately, open data serves as the foundation for designing equitable interventions that address the root causes of economic exclusion.
