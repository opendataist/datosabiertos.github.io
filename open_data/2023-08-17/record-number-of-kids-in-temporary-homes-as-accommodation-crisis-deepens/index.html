Record number of kids in temporary homes as accommodation crisis deepens

Glasgow is experiencing a severe housing crisis, with thousands of children trapped in sub-standard temporary accommodation due to a critical shortage of decent homes. Councils are frequently violating legal orders designed to prevent the use of unfit housing, highlighting a systemic failure to provide safe living conditions. This situation places immense stress on front-line workers and leaves vulnerable families in precarious living arrangements for extended periods. The root cause lies in the acute mismatch between housing supply and demand, exacerbated by underfunding and population migration. Advocates argue that without significant financial intervention and a shift toward long-term social housing solutions, the city faces a worsening humanitarian emergency. The reliance on emergency beds and temporary stays creates a cycle of instability, undermining efforts to tackle poverty and child welfare effectively. This article is vital to the open_data community as it demonstrates the power of Freedom of Information requests in exposing systemic governance failures. By making council breach statistics and housing numbers publicly accessible, data transparency drives accountability and forces political action. It illustrates how open data can validate community grievances, shift public narrative, and pressure governments to address complex social issues with evidence-based solutions.
