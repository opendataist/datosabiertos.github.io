Open Data
ES EN

The article highlights how INEGI uses administrative records from civil registries and forensic services to compile preliminary mortality statistics. This process demonstrates the essential role of open data in public health, as the integration of diverse governmental sources enables accurate and transparent monitoring of vital events and disease trends across Mexico. The main conclusions reveal that cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, and malignant tumors were the leading causes of death in the first half of 2022, with a significant gender disparity showing higher mortality among men. These findings underscore the importance of accessible, high-quality datasets for identifying health priorities and allocating resources effectively to address specific demographic vulnerabilities. The relevance to open data lies in the methodological transparency of combining multiple informants for future definitive cross-checks with health authorities. This case illustrates how interoperable datasets enhance epidemiological surveillance, enabling better policy decisions and demonstrating the value of public information in tracking societal health challenges and improving institutional accountability.
