UMG, Sony, and Capitol launch copyright lawsuit against Internet Archive

Major record labels have sued the Internet Archive, alleging that its digital collection of historic 78-rpm records violates copyright. The plaintiffs argue that the archive prioritizes free public access over legal protections, seeking significant damages for thousands of tracks. This legal action underscores the growing tension between proprietary rights and the digital preservation of cultural heritage. This lawsuit threatens the existence of vital open access repositories, continuing a pattern of aggressive litigation against digital libraries. By challenging the archive’s core mission, these suits risk severely limiting public access to historical media and knowledge. Such legal pressure creates an existential crisis for institutions dedicated to preserving information without restrictive barriers. This conflict is critically relevant to open data advocates, as it highlights the fragility of digital commons under copyright law. If archives cannot freely preserve and share historical records, the integrity of open knowledge infrastructure is compromised. This case serves as a warning about the potential erosion of public access to cultural and informational resources in the digital age.
