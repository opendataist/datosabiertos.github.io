Gobierno Abierto: nuevo paradigma de gobernabilidad democrática

The article argues that true democracy extends beyond voting to include effective citizen control over public officials, a goal often undermined by systemic corruption and opacity. It posits that the legitimacy of governance depends on institutions that prioritize public demand over political interests, requiring robust accountability mechanisms to prevent clientelism and arbitrary decision-making. To achieve this, the text highlights the transformative potential of Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs) in establishing Open Government paradigms. Digital tools facilitate real-time transparency, continuous accountability, and flexible service delivery, thereby bridging the gap between bureaucratic state structures and an informed, connected citizenry. This shift empowers individuals to actively monitor and participate in public administration, fostering a collaborative governance model. This perspective is highly relevant to open data because it identifies data transparency and accessibility as essential ingredients for democratization. By making public information available and usable, governments enable the civic oversight and collaborative innovation necessary to combat corruption and improve public services, ultimately strengthening the social contract through digital empowerment.
