INEI: Más de 53 mil piuranos se convirtieron en pobres en 2023

The article highlights a significant rise in monetary poverty in Peru, driven by political instability, the pandemic, and economic recession. It emphasizes that restoring growth rates above three percent is urgent to regain confidence and protect vulnerable populations, as economic contraction in key sectors like fishing and agriculture directly contributed to this decline. The relevance to open data lies in how public institutions like INEI generate and disseminate transparent statistics that make these socioeconomic shocks visible. By tracking metrics such as poverty lines and inflation impacts, open data enables citizens and experts to verify official claims, hold governments accountable for policy failures, and understand the real human cost of broader economic crises. Furthermore, the data reveals that over ninety-seven million Peruvians now fall below the poverty line, with specific regions disproportionately affected. This transparency allows for targeted policy interventions and fosters public debate on essential social issues. Ultimately, accessible, high-quality data is crucial for diagnosing the severity of the crisis and guiding effective strategies to improve living standards and address the root causes of widespread economic hardship.
