La pobreza en Perú sube por segundo año consecutivo

La pobreza en Perú sube por segundo año consecutivo

Peru’s monetary poverty rate rose significantly in 2023, marking a concerning upward trend not seen in decades. This regression has pushed millions back into conditions where basic necessities are unaffordable, reversing years of social progress. The data highlight a severe economic contraction driven by prolonged social unrest, political instability, and adverse climate events, which collectively disrupted livelihoods across the most vulnerable regions. The controversy surrounding the delayed release of these statistics underscores the critical importance of transparent and timely dissemination of data. When official institutions hesitate to share key socioeconomic indicators, it erodes public trust and hinders effective policy responses. This incident serves as a stark reminder that open data is not merely a technical formality but a fundamental requirement for accountability during times of governmental crisis. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates how opaque information management exacerbates societal challenges. Reliable access to official statistics enables civil society and international bodies to verify claims, hold leaders accountable, and propose evidence-based solutions. Ensuring that such data is freely and promptly available is essential for maintaining democratic integrity and enabling informed decision-making in the face of worsening poverty.
