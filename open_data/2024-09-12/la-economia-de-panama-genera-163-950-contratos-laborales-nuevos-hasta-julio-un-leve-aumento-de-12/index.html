La economía de Panamá genera 163.950 contratos laborales nuevos hasta julio, un leve aumento de 1,2%

La economía de Panamá genera 163.950 contratos laborales nuevos hasta julio, un leve aumento de 1,2%

The article reveals that Panama’s labor market remains significantly below pre-pandemic hiring levels, indicating a sluggish economic recovery. Despite modest recent growth, the sustained gap in job contracts highlights an inability to fully restore historical employment dynamics, suggesting that structural weaknesses continue to hinder robust labor generation compared to previous years. This stagnation is largely attributed to low private investment confidence, driven by rising interest rates and a downgrade in the country’s credit rating. The analysis emphasizes that without a conducive investment climate and liquidity injections, such as timely payments to state suppliers, the government cannot effectively stimulate new hires. Consequently, uncertainty persists, discouraging the business activity necessary for substantial employment increases. This narrative is vital for open data because it demonstrates how transparent access to historical employment statistics and economic indicators enables expert analysis and policy debate. By making these datasets publicly available, stakeholders can track recovery progress, identify bottlenecks like investment confidence, and advocate for informed fiscal decisions. Open data thus serves as a foundational tool for accountability and strategic economic planning, allowing society to understand the real-world implications of official figures.
