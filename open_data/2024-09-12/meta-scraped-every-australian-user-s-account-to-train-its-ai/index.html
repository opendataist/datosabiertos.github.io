Meta scraped every Australian user's account to train its AI

Meta’s global privacy director admitted to scraping publicly available Australian Facebook and Instagram data since 2007 for AI training, noting the absence of an opt-out mechanism unlike those available in the European Union. This disparity highlights how regulatory frameworks directly influence corporate data practices, with Meta explicitly linking its European user protections to the GDPR’s specific legal requirements. The company also acknowledged collecting children’s data when posted on parental accounts, raising significant ethical concerns regarding the privacy of minors. These revelations underscore the critical role of robust privacy legislation in safeguarding user rights, as Meta indicated it would only implement stricter consent measures where such laws mandate them. This case is vital for open data discussions because it illustrates the tension between transparent AI development and personal privacy. It demonstrates that without strong legal frameworks, companies may exploit vast amounts of public data for commercial AI models, potentially undermining user autonomy and necessitating stricter governance to ensure ethical data usage.
