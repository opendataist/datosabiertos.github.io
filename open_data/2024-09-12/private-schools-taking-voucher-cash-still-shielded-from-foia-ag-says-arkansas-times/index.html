Private schools taking voucher cash still shielded from FOIA, AG says - Arkansas Times

Private schools taking voucher cash still shielded from FOIA, AG says - Arkansas Times

The article critiques a recent Arkansas Attorney General opinion declaring that private schools receiving public voucher funds are exempt from the state’s Freedom of Information Act. This ruling effectively shields institutions that manage taxpayer money from public scrutiny, allowing them to withhold financial and operational records despite their role in delivering a public service. The decision highlights a significant transparency gap where private entities avoid the accountability standards applied to traditional public schools. This lack of transparency is particularly concerning regarding open data, as it prevents researchers, journalists, and citizens from accessing critical information about how public funds are utilized in education. Without mandatory data disclosure, it is impossible to verify the efficacy of voucher programs or assess whether these "subprime" schools are meeting academic standards. The absence of comparable data undermines evidence-based policy-making and obscures the true impact of shifting educational resources from public to private sectors. Ultimately, this legal maneuver supports an ideological agenda that prioritizes reduced government oversight over democratic accountability. By denying public records rights to voucher-funded schools, officials create a two-tiered system where tax-supported education operates in the dark. For open data advocates, this case serves as a stark warning that expanding private involvement in public services without corresponding transparency requirements erodes public trust and hinders the ability to monitor the effective use of public resources.
