Bill that ‘destroys’ transparency of Kentucky public records drops one provision as it advances

House Bill 509 advances in Kentucky, proposing to mandate official email accounts for public business while shielding private communications from open records requests. Although a more extreme amendment exempting elected officials entirely was dropped, the core legislation remains controversial. Advocates argue that by legally exempting private phones and apps, the bill creates a significant loophole. This incentivizes officials to use unrecordable methods like texting to conduct government business, effectively allowing them to evade public scrutiny under the guise of privacy protection. The relevance to open data lies in the direct threat this bill poses to the accessibility of government records. By redefining what constitutes a public record and exempting private digital channels, the legislation risks dismantling decades of transparency protections. Experts warn that this legal framework encourages the systematic exclusion of critical information from public databases, undermining the fundamental principle that citizen access to government data is essential for accountability. Ultimately, the bill prioritizes a narrow interpretation of employee privacy over the public’s right to know. Critics, including press associations and conservative advocacy groups, contend that the current open records laws already sufficiently protect privacy without requiring such broad exemptions. If passed, HB 509 will likely normalize the concealment of public business communications, setting a precedent that could significantly reduce the availability of open data for journalists and citizens alike.
