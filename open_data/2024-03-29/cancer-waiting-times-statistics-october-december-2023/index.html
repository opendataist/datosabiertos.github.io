Cancer Waiting Times Statistics: October - December 2023

The recent publication of National Statistics on cancer waiting times in Northern Ireland highlights a critical intersection between health service performance and data infrastructure integrity. The release reveals declining compliance with key waiting time targets for definitive treatments and urgent referrals, reflecting ongoing pressures within the healthcare system. These metrics are essential for public accountability, yet their current presentation is significantly compromised by data gaps, raising questions about the completeness and reliability of official health reporting during periods of systemic transition. A primary complication affecting this dataset is the phased implementation of a new electronic patient record system. Because data from the South Eastern Health and Social Care Trust was unavailable due to this digital migration, all published figures explicitly exclude this region. Consequently, any aggregate statistics presented for Northern Ireland represent only four of the five trusts, creating a fragmented view of the national picture. This exclusion undermines the utility of the data for holistic policy analysis, as it prevents accurate year-on-year comparisons and obscures the true scope of waiting time challenges across the entire jurisdiction. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocates, as it demonstrates how operational changes in public services can disrupt the continuity and comparability of public datasets. The exclusion of a major trust’s data, while operationally necessary, reduces the transparency and utility of the information for researchers, journalists, and citizens. It underscores the urgent need for robust metadata and clear communication when data collection methods change, ensuring that open data remains accurate, comparable, and truly representative of the population it serves, rather than presenting a skewed subset that may mislead public understanding of health service efficiency.
