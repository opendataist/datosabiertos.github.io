Open Data
ES EN

The Open Source Initiative (OSI) asserts that major AI companies, including Elon Musk’s Grok, misleadingly label their products as "open source" because they typically release only model weights while withholding training data and processes. This selective transparency prevents users from verifying the absence of copyright infringement or bias, fundamentally contradicting traditional open source principles that demand full visibility and freedom to modify. Without access to the underlying data, the true nature and safety of these AI systems remain opaque to the public. This ambiguity is driving a critical effort to update the open source definition for the AI era, with experts arguing that data transparency must be a mandatory minimum requirement. The OSI is currently refining these standards to address the unique challenges posed by generative AI, aiming to establish clear criteria that distinguish genuine openness from marketing hype. However, the organization acknowledges the complex legal landscape, noting that companies often withhold data to protect trade secrets and avoid litigation, which complicates the path toward universal compliance. This issue is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the tension between proprietary control and public accountability in the age of artificial intelligence. As governments worldwide develop AI regulations, a standardized and legally recognized definition of open source serves as a vital tool to combat false advertising and ensure ethical standards. Ultimately, clarifying these definitions helps stakeholders discern which technologies truly contribute to the public domain and which merely exploit the label for commercial advantage.
