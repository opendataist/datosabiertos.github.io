Cancer waiting times reaching ‘astronomical’ levels, says charity, amid more treatment delays

Cancer treatment waiting times in Northern Ireland have reached critical levels, with significant delays across all major treatment pathways. Current performance falls far short of established targets, indicating that existing healthcare systems are unsustainable. Without immediate intervention, these backlogs threaten the ability to deliver high-quality care, especially as cancer incidence is projected to double by 2040. The charity emphasizes that relying solely on service expansion is insufficient given the rising demand. Instead, substantial investment in prevention, particularly for skin and lung cancers, is required. These cancers represent major preventable burdens, and early intervention could mitigate future pressures on the healthcare system. Addressing the root causes of disease prevalence is essential to stabilizing service delivery and ensuring long-term viability. This situation highlights a critical challenge for open data initiatives within the public health sector. Transparent, accessible data on waiting times and treatment outcomes allows for rigorous scrutiny of system performance and resource allocation. Open data empowers stakeholders to demand accountability and evidence-based policy changes. By making these statistics widely available, communities can advocate for necessary reforms in prevention and service sustainability, ensuring that data drives tangible improvements in patient care.
