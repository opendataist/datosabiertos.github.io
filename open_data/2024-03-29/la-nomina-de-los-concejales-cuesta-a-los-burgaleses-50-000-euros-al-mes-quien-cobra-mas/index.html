La nómina de los concejales cuesta a los burgaleses 50.000 euros al mes. ¿Quién cobra más?

The recent publication of remuneration data on Burgos’s municipal transparency portal reveals that the city council has spent nearly €400,000 on salaries and allowances for its 27 council members since July 2023. This significant expenditure highlights the financial scale of local political mandates, distinguishing between the eight councilors with exclusive dedication who receive fixed monthly salaries and the 19 part-time members who earn income based on their attendance at commissions, plenary sessions, and government boards. The breakdown of these funds shows a substantial portion allocated to full-time staff, including the mayor, whose salary was notably increased, while part-time members earn variable amounts heavily dependent on their physical presence at administrative events. The data illustrates a clear disparity in earnings, where high attendance correlates with higher income, and underscores the total cost of maintaining the current political team, providing citizens with unprecedented visibility into how public funds are distributed among elected officials. This information is critically relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates the tangible impact of transparency portals in making fiscal accountability possible. By converting complex bureaucratic calculations into accessible public records, such platforms allow civil society to scrutinize government spending, verify the efficiency of public administration, and ensure that taxpayer money is managed responsibly. This case exemplifies how open data transforms opaque political financing into a subject of democratic oversight and informed public debate.
