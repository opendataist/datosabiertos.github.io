From Open Data to AI-Ready Data: Building the Foundations for Responsible AI in Development

From Open Data to AI-Ready Data: Building the Foundations for Responsible AI in Development

The article argues that development data must evolve into "AI-ready" formats to ensure that large language models and other AI systems can accurately access and utilize authoritative, high-quality information. By structuring data for machine interpretability alongside human understanding, this transition mitigates the risk of AI hallucinations that currently plague public discourse. Consequently, decision-makers and the general public gain reliable access to precise insights, fostering transparency and trust in evidence-based policy formulation. To achieve this, three pillars are essential: robust infrastructure for data discovery, comprehensive metadata for clarity, and strict governance to ensure ethical and transparent usage. These standards build upon existing open data frameworks like FAIR principles, extending them to support seamless interoperability between human users and AI applications. This technical foundation allows AI to seamlessly integrate trusted sources, such as those from national statistical offices, rather than relying on unverified internet content. This evolution is critically relevant to the open data community as it redefines the ultimate goal of accessibility. It demonstrates that open data is not merely about publication, but about ensuring utility in an AI-driven world. By adopting these standards, the open data movement ensures that valuable societal datasets remain central to global development efforts, preventing the marginalization of public sector data by private sector AI models and promoting equitable, informed decision-making.
