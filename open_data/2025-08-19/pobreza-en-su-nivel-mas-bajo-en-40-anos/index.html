Pobreza en su nivel más bajo en 40 años

Pobreza en su nivel más bajo en 40 años

President Claudia Sheinbaum has stated that unifying Mexico’s health systems is a necessary step to ensure population well-being, in line with the social advances of her administration. She highlights that poverty has fallen to historic lows thanks to welfare policies and job creation, which has drastically narrowed the gap between the rich and the poor. This favorable social context is presented as a solid foundation for implementing structural changes in the public sector without restrictions. The official goal is to establish universal, integrated healthcare by 2027, eliminating the distinction between insured beneficiaries and the general population. By merging institutions such as the Mexican Social Security Institute (IMSS), the State Employees’ Social Security and Services Institute (ISSSTE), and IMSS-Bienestar, the government seeks to fulfill its constitutional obligation to provide free, high-quality health services, correcting the effects of previous neoliberal policies that limited access to healthcare. This collective effort requires the active collaboration of medical professionals to transform the current health system structure. This issue is relevant to the open data community because transparency in health and poverty indicators is crucial for assessing the real impact of these public policies. Access to reliable data on coverage, efficiency, and health outcomes enables civil society and researchers to verify government claims, promote accountability, and propose evidence-based improvements, ensuring that the health system unification truly benefits citizens.
