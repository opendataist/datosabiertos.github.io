Posting Dead. By 2070 Dead Facebook Users Could Overtake the Living

Posting Dead. By 2070 Dead Facebook Users Could Overtake the Living

Researchers from the Oxford Internet Institute project that by 2070, deceased users will outnumber living ones on Facebook. This demographic shift suggests that digital remains will soon constitute a massive portion of the platform’s user base, fundamentally altering the nature of our online presence. Consequently, society must confront the long-term implications of storing vast quantities of personal data belonging to people who can no longer manage it themselves. The core challenge lies in determining ownership, privacy, and archival value for this "global digital heritage." The study highlights urgent ethical questions regarding who controls deceased profiles and how this data should be curated for future historians and grieving families. Current systems, such as legacy contacts, allow limited memorial management, but these measures are insufficient for the scale of data accumulation expected in the coming decades. This research is vital to the open data community because it frames digital legacy as a critical public interest issue. It urges platforms to collaborate with archivists, ethicists, and historians to develop sustainable frameworks for preserving social history. By addressing the management of digital remains, we can ensure that open data principles extend beyond the living, preserving our collective memory while respecting the privacy and dignity of the deceased.
