Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment in 2021-2022

This article highlights the critical role of transparent institutional data in evaluating academic performance. By leveraging Freedom of Information Requests, independent groups can access granular metrics on student attainment, revealing significant disparities in grading standards across departments. The data shows that Physics and English led in average marks, while Foundation year modules and Japanese lagged significantly behind, indicating that subject choice heavily influences grade outcomes. The analysis further uncovers extreme variations in module-level success, with some first-year art courses awarding zero first-class honors compared to Business Analytics modules where nearly nine out of ten students achieved top marks. These stark contrasts between high-performing Classics and struggling Russian language modules suggest inconsistent assessment rigor or support structures. Such visibility allows students to make informed choices about their educational paths and encourages departments to reflect on their pedagogical effectiveness. This transparency is essential for the open data movement, as it transforms opaque institutional records into accountable public resources. When universities share detailed performance metrics, it fosters a culture of evidence-based improvement and empowers stakeholders to scrutinize educational quality. Ultimately, making this data accessible promotes fairness and helps identify systemic inequities in higher education assessment practices.
