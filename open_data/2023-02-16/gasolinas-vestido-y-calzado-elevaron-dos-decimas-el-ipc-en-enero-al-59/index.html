Gasolinas, vestido y calzado elevaron dos décimas el IPC en enero, al 5,9 %

Inflation in Spain has experienced a slight uptick in January, driven primarily by the increase in fuel prices following the withdrawal of government subsidies and rising prices in the textile sector. However, the moderation in the cost of certain food items, thanks to a reduction in the value-added tax (VAT), has helped to contain the overall increase. This dynamic reflects a tension between the impact of recent energy policies and fiscal efforts to alleviate the burden of basic goods on citizens' wallets. Underlying inflation, which excludes unprocessed food and energy, has reached its highest level in decades, highlighting the persistence of inflationary pressure on structural costs. While the housing group has seen its prices drop significantly due to the reduction in electricity costs, other services such as communications continue to rise. This contrast shows that, although direct energy prices fluctuate, price adjustments in essential goods and services maintain sustained pressure on household economies. This report is relevant to the *open_data* movement because it demonstrates the critical importance of real-time public statistical data for evaluating the effectiveness of government policies. Transparency in indicators such as the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and underlying inflation allows citizens, researchers, and analysts to verify whether adopted measures, such as tax reductions or energy subsidies, are meeting their objectives. Furthermore, open access to this data facilitates independent analysis of disparities among different consumption groups, fostering a more informed society capable of demanding accountability in public economic management.
