El periodismo contra ChatGPT y similares

The New York Times’ lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft marks a turning point in the relationship between artificial intelligence and copyright, raising the critical question of whether the large-scale use of copyrighted content to train language models constitutes infringement or fair use. This case symbolizes the tension between technological innovation and the protection of journalism, as AI corporations leverage human-created works without compensation, threatening the subscription models that fund reliable information. The impact extends beyond economics, affecting the reputation of source outlets due to AI hallucinations that spread false information incorrectly attributed to the original media. This dispute not only questions the sustainability of creative work but also reveals a significant global divide: while the United States debates these legal boundaries, artists, journalists, and writers from other regions are excluded from the process, even though their work and democracies are at stake. This situation is relevant to open data because it highlights the need for transparency in how datasets that feed AI systems are generated and used. If training relies on private resources without permission, it undermines the principle of equitable access to information. Addressing these conflicts is essential to building an artificial intelligence ecosystem that respects intellectual property and fosters global inclusion, ensuring that technological progress does not come at the expense of informational freedom and economic justice.
