Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Norwegian immigrants face significantly poorer living conditions, including lower incomes, worse health, and higher rates of mental distress and loneliness, yet they report satisfaction levels equal to the native population. This paradox highlights that material disadvantages do not necessarily diminish subjective well-being or social integration, challenging simplistic assumptions about the relationship between objective socioeconomic status and happiness among newcomer communities. Despite these hardships, immigrants demonstrate remarkably high confidence in core public institutions like the political system, judiciary, and police, often exceeding trust levels found in the general population. This strong institutional reliance suggests a resilient social contract where immigrants value state stability and governance structures, indicating that social cohesion may be maintained through trust in authority even when personal circumstances are challenging. This report is crucial for open_data initiatives as it provides a comprehensive, nuanced dataset on immigrant integration that goes beyond basic demographic metrics. By revealing the complex interplay between structural inequality and subjective well-being, it offers valuable insights for policymakers and researchers aiming to create inclusive societies, emphasizing the need for disaggregated data to accurately capture diverse experiences within marginalized groups.
