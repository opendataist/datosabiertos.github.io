Costa Rica registra tasa de fecundidad adolescente del 5,3 en 2025, superior a 2024 - Mundo - ABC Color

Recent data highlight a sustained decade-long decline in teenage pregnancy rates in Costa Rica, reflecting significant progress in public health outcomes. This downward trend demonstrates the effectiveness of ongoing interventions and underscores a positive shift in societal indicators regarding adolescent reproductive health. The core strategy for sustaining this progress lies in comprehensive prevention that extends beyond medical services. Officials emphasize the necessity of integrating education, mental health support, and protection against violence to empower young people. By fostering environments where adolescents can safely construct their life projects, society addresses the root causes of early pregnancies rather than merely treating symptoms. This article is crucial for open data because it illustrates how statistical transparency drives policy change. The clear correlation between accessible information, specific public health actions, and reduced birth rates validates the importance of releasing granular demographic datasets. Such data enables researchers and policymakers to measure intervention efficacy, ensuring that resources are allocated efficiently to maintain and improve these vital social outcomes.
