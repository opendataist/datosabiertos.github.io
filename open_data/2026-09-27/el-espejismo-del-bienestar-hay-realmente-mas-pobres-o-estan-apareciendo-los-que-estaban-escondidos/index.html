El Espejismo del Bienestar: ¿Hay realmente más pobres o están apareciendo los que estaban escondidos?

El Espejismo del Bienestar: ¿Hay realmente más pobres o están apareciendo los que estaban escondidos?

Bolivia’s economy is facing structural stagnation, where a contraction in production contrasts with sustained demographic growth. This asymmetry leads to a significant decline in per capita income, clearly showing that the wealth available per person is dropping sharply. This phenomenon is not merely statistical; it translates into an accelerated expansion of poverty, particularly threatening vulnerable segments of the middle class that lack robust social safety nets. The analysis distinguishes between poverty stemming from the actual destruction of jobs and that resulting from the adjustment of prices that had been artificially controlled for years. Widespread subsidies and fixed exchange rates masked real shortages, creating a fragile social stability based on fiscal distortions. Once international reserves were depleted, the government was forced to allow these prices to correct, triggering an immediate rise in the cost of living and revealing the underlying vulnerability that had previously been concealed by the inefficiencies of the prior economic model. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the need to understand the context behind economic metrics. Analyzing data such as GDP and census figures in isolation can lead to misinterpretations; an integrated analytical approach is required to differentiate between genuine productive decline and market adjustments. For policymakers, grasping this distinction is crucial for designing targeted public policies that protect the population without resorting to unsustainable subsidies, thereby ensuring an economic transition that does not devolve into a humanitarian crisis.
