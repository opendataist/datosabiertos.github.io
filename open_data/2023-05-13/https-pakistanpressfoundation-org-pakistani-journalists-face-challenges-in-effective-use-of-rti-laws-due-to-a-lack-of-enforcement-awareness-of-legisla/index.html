The article highlights a critical gap in Pakistan’s Right to Information framework, where constitutional guarantees fail due to weak enforcement and widespread bureaucratic non-compliance. This systemic failure creates a culture of opacity, delaying responses and significantly curtailing public access to essential information. The root cause lies in insufficient awareness among public officeholders regarding their legal responsibilities under RTI laws. Journalists utilize RTI as a primary tool for investigative reporting, yet they encounter substantial obstacles ranging from harassment to gender-based discrimination, particularly in rural areas. Training initiatives aim to equip reporters with better techniques for framing effective requests and verifying sources. However, these efforts are undermined by the absence of strong compliance measures, leaving investigative work vulnerable to intimidation and limited cooperation from authorities. This situation is vital for open data advocates because it demonstrates how legal infrastructure alone cannot ensure transparency without active enforcement and cultural change. It underscores the necessity of integrating open data principles with robust accountability mechanisms to protect information seekers. Without addressing these enforcement gaps and societal barriers, the potential of open data to empower citizens remains severely restricted in such contexts.
