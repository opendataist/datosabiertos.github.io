RTI Commission given prosecutorial powers to sanction defaulting institutions

RTI Commission given prosecutorial powers to sanction defaulting institutions

The Right to Information Commission now possesses prosecutorial powers to sanction institutions for non-compliance with the Right to Information Act, marking a significant enforcement milestone. This legal backing allows the Commission to impose substantial penalties on entities that refuse to grant requested information, thereby strengthening accountability mechanisms. By empowering the regulatory body to act directly against defiant organizations, the framework moves beyond advisory roles to ensure tangible adherence to transparency standards. Public engagement with the law has grown significantly, driven by media, civil society, and academic sectors seeking accountability. To further improve access, the government plans to expand physical offices and introduce an online records management system, reducing barriers for citizens. These infrastructural enhancements aim to streamline the application process, making it easier for individuals to exercise their right to know and fostering a culture of openness in governance. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates as it establishes a robust legal precedent for enforcing transparency. The combination of punitive measures for non-compliance and improved digital infrastructure demonstrates a systemic commitment to making information accessible. It signals that open data is no longer optional for public institutions but a mandatory requirement enforced by law, setting a strong foundation for democratic oversight and civic participation.
