Salida de consejeros será menos costosa, liquidación inferior al Inai | Periódico Zócalo | Noticias de Saltillo, Torreón, Piedras Negras, Monclova, Acuña

The article reveals a stark disparity in severance packages between state and national transparency officials, highlighting how institutional budgets and political neglect directly impact staff compensation. This lack of adequate funding and delayed appointments undermines the operational capacity and morale of local open data institutions. Such financial constraints threaten the stability required to effectively manage public information access. The prolonged understaffing and frozen budgets illustrate the systemic challenges facing subnational transparency bodies. Without sufficient resources and fully constituted councils, these agencies struggle to maintain rigorous oversight. This situation underscores the urgent need for sustained political will and financial support to ensure functional governance structures. This narrative is crucial for the open data community as it demonstrates that technical access initiatives rely heavily on institutional integrity and adequate resourcing. When governments underfund or neglect these entities, public trust erodes, and the right to information becomes practically inaccessible. Understanding these structural barriers helps advocates push for better policy frameworks that prioritize the sustainability of transparency mechanisms.
