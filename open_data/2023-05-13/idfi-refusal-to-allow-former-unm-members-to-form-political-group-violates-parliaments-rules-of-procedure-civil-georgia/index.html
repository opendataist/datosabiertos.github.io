IDFI: Refusal to Allow Former UNM Members to Form Political Group Violates Parliament’s Rules of Procedure - Civil Georgia

The Institute for Development of Freedom of Information argues that the Georgian Parliament’s refusal to register a new political group by two former United National Movement MPs violates procedural rules and hinders parliamentary pluralism. This decision arbitrarily restricts legislators' rights, undermining the inclusive nature of democratic representation where diverse voices should be able to organize freely. By misinterpreting the Rules of Procedure, the ruling majority incorrectly applied limitations regarding electoral blocs to a situation where they do not legally apply. The watchdog emphasizes that regulations only restrict group formation in specific, explicitly defined circumstances, and the current denial lacks legal basis. This highlights how ambiguous or selective enforcement of internal rules can be used to suppress minority political expression within legislative bodies. This case is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and clearly defined institutional regulations. When legal frameworks and procedural guidelines are opaque or inconsistently applied, accountability suffers. Open data initiatives must ensure that such rules are digitized and easily searchable, allowing civil society to monitor compliance, identify irregularities, and advocate for procedural justice and genuine democratic openness.
