UGT advierte de que los puestos vacantes en C-LM han aumentado un 63% y pide mejorar las condiciones laborales

Spain experienced a historic surge in job vacancies in early 2023, yet it remains one of the European countries with the lowest vacancy rates relative to its active population. Despite this apparent abundance of opportunities, significant difficulties persist in filling specific roles, particularly in healthcare, hospitality, agriculture, and construction. This paradox highlights a disconnect between the volume of open positions and the actual capacity of the labor market to absorb them effectively. The core issue lies not in a lack of willing workers, but in poor labor conditions characterized by high precarity and low wages. Structural imbalances between vocational training and market needs, combined with ineffective active employment policies, prevent workers from accepting available positions. Consequently, sectors offering unstable and poorly paid jobs struggle to attract personnel, even amidst overall economic growth. This situation is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates the necessity of linking statistical indicators with qualitative labor analysis. Relying solely on vacancy counts provides an incomplete picture of labor market health. To drive meaningful policy changes, data ecosystems must integrate insights on working conditions and training efficacy, enabling a more holistic understanding of employment challenges and supporting evidence-based structural reforms.
