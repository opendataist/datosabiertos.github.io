La Organización Internacional del Trabajo alertó que lograr la igualdad de género tardaría casi dos siglos

La Organización Internacional del Trabajo alertó que lograr la igualdad de género tardaría casi dos siglos

The International Labour Organization warns that, at the current pace, achieving gender parity in the labor sector will take two centuries, highlighting a critically slow reduction in unemployment gaps over the last three decades. Despite incremental improvements, millions of women continue to face persistent barriers to decent work, with significant disparities in registered employment rates and hierarchical positions. This stagnation underscores the failure of initiatives from recent decades, including the Beijing Declaration, to translate educational gains into actual market equality. Structural inequalities remain entrenched, as women are overrepresented in low-paid sectors and earn significantly less than men for comparable work. The report emphasizes that factors such as unequal care responsibilities, wage gaps, and workplace harassment continue to create unsafe and inequitable environments. These systemic issues prevent women from remaining in the workforce or progressing, particularly in developing countries where informal employment is widespread, despite their higher participation in education. This article is vital for open data because it demonstrates the critical need for accessible, standardized, and disaggregated statistical data to monitor progress toward sustainable development goals. Open data initiatives can expose these hidden disparities, holding governments and institutions accountable for the slow pace of change. By making labor metrics transparent, society can better analyze the impact of policies on gender equality and drive the urgent reforms needed to close the divide, ensuring that data serves as a tool for justice rather than just a record of stagnation.
