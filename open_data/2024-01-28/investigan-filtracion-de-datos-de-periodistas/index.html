Investigan filtración de datos de periodistas

The article highlights a personal data leak involving Mexican journalists, which has prompted an official investigation by the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI). This incident underscores the critical need to safeguard sensitive information against unauthorized disclosures, reinforcing the agency’s role as a guarantor of citizens’ privacy rights. Furthermore, it links sound data management to institutional transparency, emphasizing that poorly organized archives create legal vulnerabilities. The text argues that proper documentation is not merely an administrative task but a constitutional obligation for public officials, ensuring accountability and preventing data breaches during transitions of power. This issue is relevant to open data because it illustrates the paradox of transparency: while open data depends on accessible government records, it must be balanced with strict protection of personal data. Effective archival systems are essential to enable public sharing of information without compromising individual privacy, demonstrating that organized data management is the foundation of trustworthy open government initiatives.
