5.3 magnitude earthquake shakes the Dominican Republic

5.3 magnitude earthquake shakes the Dominican Republic

A moderate-magnitude earthquake shook the Dominican Republic, originating south of El Seibo at considerable depth. This technical detail allowed the seismic waves to extend beyond the epicenter, being felt even in the capital and other distant areas. The perception of the shaking generated immediate alarm, prompting the preventive evacuation of many buildings as a precaution against uncertainty about possible aftershocks. The significance of this event lies in how preliminary data from the National Seismology Center of the UASD facilitate an immediate understanding of the threat. By rapidly disseminating the location and depth, the public information enables citizens to assess the actual risk and take appropriate safety measures. This transparency is essential for managing panic and guiding emergency responses efficiently in a seismically active country. The situation underscores the critical importance of open access to real-time geophysical data. When information about tectonic activity is made available to the population, communities are empowered to make informed decisions that save lives. The availability of these scientific records strengthens national resilience, demonstrating that the open dissemination of natural hazard data is a vital tool for civil protection and social stability in the face of natural disasters.
