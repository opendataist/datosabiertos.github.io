Salto records an unemployment rate of 11.8% and 29.4% informality, but maintains activity at 54.2%

The publication of data from the National Institute of Statistics (INE) reveals that Salto is experiencing one of the most severe labor market tensions in Uruguay’s interior, with a notably higher unemployment rate than the national average and a high prevalence of informal employment. This situation confirms that the local economy is unable to adequately absorb the economically active population, thereby generating social vulnerability and limiting access to fundamental rights for a significant portion of workers. The gap between the capital and the remote interior regions is widening, highlighting that the northern and western littoral areas are hindered by structural and seasonal factors that obstruct the creation of stable jobs. Informality not only reflects instability but also exposes families to economic risks due to the lack of pension coverage, underscoring the urgent need to transform the productive model toward sectors with higher value added. This report is relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how transparency in official data enables the identification of specific regional inequalities and guides effective public policies. By making these metrics accessible, citizens and public officials are empowered to design targeted strategies focused on training and business strengthening, facilitating evidence-based decision-making to reduce socioeconomic disparities.
