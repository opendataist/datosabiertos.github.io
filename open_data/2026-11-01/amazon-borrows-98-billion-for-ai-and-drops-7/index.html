Amazon borrows $98 billion for AI and drops 7%

Amazon borrows $98 billion for AI and drops 7%

Amazon’s recent financial performance highlights a paradigm shift in the technology sector, where the explosive growth of services like AWS clashes with the unsustainability of its current financing strategy. Despite a significant increase in cloud computing revenues, the massive debt incurred for artificial intelligence infrastructure has sparked market skepticism. Investors no longer reward technological expansion alone; they demand immediate profitability, penalizing stock prices when financial costs and amortization exceed cash flow generation. This retreat in investor enthusiasm has become widespread across the industry, affecting giants such as Alphabet and Meta, and extending to the financing of startups like OpenAI. The practical consequence is much stricter scrutiny of companies that leverage debt rather than equity to fund their development. The market is penalizing uncertainty regarding long-term return on investment, forcing corporations to demonstrate that their enormous expenditures on data centers will quickly translate into tangible profits to maintain their valuation. The relevance of this article for open data advocates lies in its illustration of the underlying economic and operational constraints of digital infrastructure. By revealing how debt and amortization impact the margins and stability of platforms that host and process data, the text offers a critical perspective on the sustainability of the technology ecosystem. Understanding these financial dynamics is essential for assessing the long-term viability of open data initiatives and the real accessibility of cloud computing, beyond growth headlines.
