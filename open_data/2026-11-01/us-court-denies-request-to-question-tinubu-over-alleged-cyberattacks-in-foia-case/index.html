US Court denies request to question Tinubu over alleged cyberattacks in FOIA case

US Court denies request to question Tinubu over alleged cyberattacks in FOIA case

A United States federal court has denied a transparency activist’s request to depose Nigerian President Bola Tinubu regarding alleged cyberattacks on his website. The judge ruled that such discovery was irrelevant to the underlying Freedom of Information Act lawsuit, which focuses on government records about a 1990s narcotics investigation rather than digital security incidents. This decision reinforces the legal boundary that FOIA proceedings must remain strictly tied to the specific records being sought, preventing litigants from using the process to investigate unrelated criminal allegations. The core legal dispute centers on the FBI and DEA’s withholding of documents related to a 1993 heroin ring investigation involving the President. Although the agencies submitted hundreds of pages of records to the judge for private review, many remain redacted or under seal. The court must now determine whether these withholdings, justified by privacy laws and law enforcement protections, are legally valid. This highlights the tension between public interest in historical government records and the strict legal exemptions protecting personal privacy and sensitive investigative techniques. This case is highly relevant to the open data movement as it demonstrates the practical challenges of securing transparency through FOIA litigation. It illustrates how governments can utilize procedural rules and privacy exemptions to limit the scope of disclosure, even when records exist. The ruling serves as a cautionary tale for transparency advocates, showing that simply having records is not enough; they must navigate complex legal hurdles to ensure data is released in a usable and unredacted form.
