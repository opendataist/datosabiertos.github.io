Este es el pueblo más rico de Teruel (y de todo Aragón), según el INE

The article highlights significant economic disparities within Aragon, contrasting urban centers with depopulated rural areas. It identifies Ejulve, a small municipality in Teruel, as having the highest average income per capita in the region, surpassing wealthier provincial capitals. This finding underscores the complex reality of Spain’s “empty Spain,” where remote locations can exhibit unexpected economic indicators due to unique demographic and structural factors. The relevance to open data lies in the comparative analysis of statistical methodologies. The INE’s Atlas uses microdata from surveys to calculate per-person income for smaller towns, whereas tax agencies rely on self-declared data from larger populations. This discrepancy demonstrates the value of diverse open datasets in revealing nuanced socioeconomic patterns that traditional fiscal records might obscure, providing a more comprehensive view of regional wealth distribution. Ultimately, the piece illustrates how open access to detailed administrative statistics enables researchers and citizens to challenge conventional assumptions about rural poverty. By understanding the underlying calculation methods, users can better interpret geographic economic variations. This transparency fosters informed policy discussions and public understanding of how different data sources complement each other to paint a fuller picture of societal well-being and regional development trends.
