El crecimiento económico de México, ¿a dónde vamos?

The article questions the sustainability of Mexico’s preliminary economic growth, arguing that positive figures are meaningless if they do not translate into a real improvement in citizens’ quality of life. It emphasizes the urgent need for an equitable distribution of wealth and the implementation of sector-specific incentives to prevent deep regional inequalities, ensuring that all states can achieve similar levels of well-being rather than concentrating benefits in specific areas. A fundamental criticism is directed at the lack of a government strategy in education and taxation, which are essential for competing globally and financing critical infrastructure. The text points out that the current model prioritizes resources for the federal government over stimulating business activity, while high-impact projects yield low returns and incur high costs. This absence of comprehensive planning, combined with unresolved issues in health and infrastructure, suggests that current progress may be temporary and dependent on external factors or post-pandemic recovery, rather than stemming from a solid structural transformation. The relevance of this analysis for the field of open data lies in the transparency and traceability of public information. For citizens to critically evaluate claims about well-being and the efficiency of state projects, access to detailed, up-to-date, and verifiable government data is indispensable. Opacity in budget execution and real economic outcomes hinders accountability; therefore, the dissemination of robust open data is vital for civil society to contrast official indicators with lived reality and demand evidence-based policies.
