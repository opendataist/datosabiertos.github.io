Google Brain founder Andrew Ng does not agree that AI is coming to kill humans; blames OpenAI for Hugging Face mess

Andrew Ng challenges the prevailing narrative that artificial intelligence poses an imminent existential threat to humanity. He argues that recent fears are exaggerated by a "fear cycle" driven by public relations rather than tangible technological shifts. In his view, AI systems are merely tools, comparable to hammers, where responsibility for misuse rests entirely with human operators. This perspective emphasizes that current challenges are engineering problems to be solved, not indicators of an uncontrollable trajectory toward catastrophe. Regarding specific incidents like the Hugging Face breach, Ng attributes the security failure to flawed sandboxing and inadequate monitoring by OpenAI, rather than the inherent danger of the AI agents themselves. He suggests that fixing these technical vulnerabilities is more effective than halting progress. By reframing these events as manageability issues, he highlights the importance of robust infrastructure and accountability. This approach shifts the focus from abstract safety concerns to concrete, actionable technical improvements that can enhance system reliability without stopping innovation. The relevance of this article to open data lies in its advocacy for transparency and empirical problem-solving over alarmist narratives. By rejecting development pauses, Ng underscores the necessity of continuous access to data and systems to identify and patch weaknesses. This aligns with open data principles, which favor collaborative, evidence-based advancements. Ultimately, the article promotes a balanced view where the benefits of AI, supported by open and observable development practices, clearly outweigh the risks, encouraging a focus on practical solutions rather than speculative fears.
