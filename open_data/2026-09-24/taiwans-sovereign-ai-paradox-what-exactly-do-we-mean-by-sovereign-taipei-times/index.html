Taiwan’s sovereign AI paradox: What exactly do we mean by sovereign? - Taipei Times

Taiwan is pursuing "sovereign AI" to maintain control over its narrative, culture, and data, reducing reliance on US and Chinese technology giants. This initiative addresses the risk that foreign models, trained on biased or insufficient local data, misrepresent Taiwanese values and history. By prioritizing local knowledge sovereignty, Taiwan aims to ensure AI systems accurately reflect democratic principles and regional context, protecting national identity from external political assumptions. The strategy involves fine-tuning open-weight base models with a curated local corpus rather than building systems from scratch. This approach successfully improves performance on Taiwan-specific benchmarks, demonstrating that localized training enhances understanding of geography and culture. However, significant gaps remain compared to global frontier models, highlighting the trade-off between technological capability and the desire for independent, transparent AI infrastructure that prioritizes local control over raw processing power. Ultimately, Taiwan is shifting toward a "sovereign system" that combines localized models with retrieval-augmented generation. This hybrid approach allows AI to access trusted local databases for complex queries, balancing accuracy with data sovereignty. This case is vital for open data discussions as it exemplifies a growing global trend: leveraging open-source models and transparent data practices to achieve technological independence, rather than defaulting to proprietary foreign solutions that may compromise local autonomy and privacy.
