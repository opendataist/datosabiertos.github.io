Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

The article reveals a paradox where immigrants in Norway experience significantly poorer living conditions yet report equal life satisfaction to the native population. This highlights a complex reality where structural disadvantages in employment, housing, and income do not necessarily translate into personal dissatisfaction, challenging assumptions about well-being metrics. Despite economic gaps, immigrants exhibit higher confidence in state institutions and a strong affiliation with Norway, contrasting with lower interpersonal trust. However, this resilience coexists with disproportionately high rates of mental health issues and loneliness. These disparities underscore the need for targeted social support systems that address psychological well-being alongside economic integration. This data is crucial for open_data as it demonstrates how comprehensive, disaggregated statistics can uncover nuanced social dynamics. By making such detailed indicators publicly available, policymakers and researchers can better understand the intersection of material conditions and subjective well-being, fostering more informed and equitable public services and social policies.
