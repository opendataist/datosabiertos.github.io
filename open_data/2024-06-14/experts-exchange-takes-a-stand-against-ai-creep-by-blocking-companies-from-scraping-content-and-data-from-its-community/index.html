Experts Exchange takes a stand against AI-creep by blocking companies from scraping content and data from its community

The article highlights a growing ethical crisis in the digital landscape, where AI companies aggressively scrape content from user-generated Q&A communities to train large language models without consent or compensation. This practice undermines the original creators of valuable knowledge and risks generating harmful, context-free information, as AI systems often lack the human judgment necessary for safe and accurate advice. Consequently, there is a pressing need to protect digital commons from being exploited for corporate profit at the expense of user trust and safety. Experts Exchange is presented as a counter-movement, strictly prohibiting AI data scraping and ensuring all content comes from verified human experts. By maintaining a private, community-funded model rather than relying on external investors or selling data, the platform guarantees that contributions remain the intellectual property of their creators. This approach prioritizes authentic human interaction over automated processing, offering a reliable source of technical knowledge that avoids the pitfalls of hallucinations and unethical data practices associated with generative AI. This article is highly relevant to open data advocates because it challenges the assumption that all publicly accessible online content should be free for unrestricted machine learning training. It underscores the importance of establishing clear boundaries around data ownership and usage, demonstrating that sustainable, ethical alternatives to open scraping exist. By showing how community-supported platforms can thrive without exploiting user data, the piece argues for the necessity of consent-based data frameworks and the protection of human-generated knowledge against unauthorized commercial exploitation.
