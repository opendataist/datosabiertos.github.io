Google AI Gemini parrots China’s propaganda

Google AI Gemini parrots China’s propaganda

Google’s AI assistant Gemini exhibited a disturbing asymmetry in its Mandarin-language responses, parroting Beijing’s official propaganda regarding human rights and Taiwan while remaining silent on similar critical questions about China. In contrast, the model readily discussed US issues and did not refuse questions when prompted in English. This linguistic bias suggests that the training data for the Mandarin model is heavily influenced by Chinese state-controlled narratives, effectively acting as a tool for censorship and disinformation rather than neutral information retrieval. This discrepancy highlights a critical vulnerability in open data ecosystems: the risk that AI models may inherit and amplify the biases of their training sources. If Western technology companies do not rigorously curate and audit the data used to train multilingual models, these systems can unintentionally serve as force multipliers for authoritarian influence. The incident demonstrates how opaque training datasets can lead to outputs that contradict established diplomatic facts, undermining the integrity of global information systems and the reliability of AI tools for cross-cultural communication. The event is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the urgent need for transparency in data sourcing and algorithmic auditing. As lawmakers and experts demand greater accountability, this case illustrates why open standards for training data quality and neutrality are essential. Without strict filters against manipulated state narratives, open data initiatives risk compromising democratic values, proving that accessible data must also be vetted for ideological bias to remain a trustworthy foundation for technological innovation.
