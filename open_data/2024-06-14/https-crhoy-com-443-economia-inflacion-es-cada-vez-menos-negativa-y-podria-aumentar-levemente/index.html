The article highlights that Costa Rica’s inflation remains exceptionally low, with the Consumer Price Index rising only marginally in May due to specific cost increases in fuel and transportation. Economists emphasize that the country has experienced twelve consecutive months of negative year-over-year inflation, a trend likely to persist temporarily. This sustained deflationary pressure indicates that current price levels are well below the Central Bank’s target range, signaling a significant deviation from standard economic expectations. Experts agree that this low inflation environment creates ample room for monetary policy adjustments. There is a strong consensus that the Central Bank should consider further reducing its Monetary Policy Rate to align with the neutral interest rate required by the current economic context. Although the Bank has signaled potential caution due to exchange rate dynamics and portfolio dollarization, maintaining the current rate appears inconsistent with the inflation data. The analysis suggests that a modest reduction in interest rates would be more economically coherent and supportive of financial stability. This discussion is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical role of transparent, high-quality economic indicators in guiding monetary decisions. Accurate and accessible inflation metrics allow for independent verification by economists and the public, fostering trust in institutional policies. When data is open and clear, it facilitates better debate on whether central banks are acting in accordance with their mandates, ensuring that policy decisions like interest rate adjustments are grounded in objective economic realities rather than opaque speculation.
