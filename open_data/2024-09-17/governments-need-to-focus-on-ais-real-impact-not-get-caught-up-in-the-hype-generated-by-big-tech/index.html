Governments need to focus on AI’s real impact, not get caught up in the hype generated by Big Tech

The article argues that Statistics Canada’s recent report on AI’s impact on professions is fundamentally flawed and misleading. It suggests that the analysis relies on outdated categories that prioritize technological exposure over the reality of corporate business models. By focusing on whether AI complements or replaces human work, the report ignores the crucial fact that many "automated" systems still depend heavily on hidden human labor, rendering the optimistic or pessimistic predictions meaningless. The author highlights that the distinction between human and machine labor is often obscured by corporate strategies aimed at reducing costs rather than enhancing productivity. Examples from autonomous vehicles and customer service software reveal that companies frequently deploy remote human workers to simulate AI efficiency while charging based on full automation. This practice creates a false narrative about job security, as the underlying structure of these jobs remains dependent on human intervention, just in less visible ways. This content is relevant to open data because it exposes the danger of using incomplete or biased datasets to inform public policy. The article underscores the need for transparency and accurate data regarding how technology is actually implemented in the workplace. Open data initiatives must go beyond surface-level metrics to reveal the true labor dynamics behind technological claims, ensuring that government decisions are based on reality rather than corporate hype. Without this critical scrutiny, data-driven policies may fail to protect workers and address the real challenges posed by digital transformation.
