La población japonesa mayor de 65 años alcanza la cifra récord de 36,25 millones

The article highlights Japan’s unprecedented demographic shift, in which the elderly population has reached a historic peak, accounting for nearly one-third of the total citizenry. This rapid aging, driven by declining birth rates and continued population decline, poses severe challenges to the sustainability of the national social security system. In response, the government is implementing significant policy changes, including expanded childcare benefits and financial incentives, to encourage childbirth and mitigate long-term economic pressures. A critical response to the resulting labor shortage is the increasing participation of seniors in the workforce. With one in four individuals over the age of 65 still employed, many sectors are adapting to retain experienced workers beyond the traditional retirement age. This trend reflects both the necessity of maintaining economic stability and the effectiveness of current government policies that incentivize extended careers, particularly in the healthcare and retail sectors. This case is highly relevant to open data, as it underscores the importance of transparent, accessible demographic statistics for informed public debate and policy-making. Reliable data enables citizens and political actors to understand the severity of the crisis, evaluate the effectiveness of proposed measures, and ensure accountability in how resources are allocated to address complex societal shifts.
