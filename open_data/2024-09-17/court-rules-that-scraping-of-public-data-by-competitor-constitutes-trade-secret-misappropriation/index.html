Court Rules That Scraping of Public Data by Competitor Constitutes Trade Secret Misappropriation

Court Rules That Scraping of Public Data by Competitor Constitutes Trade Secret Misappropriation

This ruling clarifies that scraping publicly accessible data can constitute trade secret misappropriation when the volume extracted creates a competitive threat equivalent to what humans cannot feasibly obtain. The Eleventh Circuit distinguished between manual access, which is permissible, and automated bot attacks that copy millions of variable-dependent quotes in usable formats. This establishes that while individual public data points may not be protected, the large-scale compilation and organization of such data can qualify as a protected trade secret if acquired through improper means like aggressive scraping. The decision is highly relevant to open data practices because it introduces a significant legal risk for entities relying on mass data extraction from open sources. It signals that public availability alone does not guarantee immunity from trade secret claims if the scraping effort compromises the integrity of the underlying database compilation. Developers and businesses must now consider that accessing data at a scale that mirrors the creator’s proprietary effort may be deemed improper, potentially criminalizing legitimate data aggregation efforts that cross certain volume thresholds. Furthermore, the court’s remand of copyright claims regarding the arrangement of source code adds another layer of complexity to open data ecosystems. By suggesting that the specific organization of data elements might be copyrightable, the ruling impacts how developers interact with public APIs and web structures. This case serves as a cautionary tale, demonstrating that the legal landscape for scraping is nuanced, requiring careful navigation between open access norms and intellectual property protections to avoid liability for misappropriation.
