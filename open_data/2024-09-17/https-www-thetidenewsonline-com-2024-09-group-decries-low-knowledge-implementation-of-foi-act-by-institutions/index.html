Open Data
ES EN

The Freedom of Information Act remains significantly underutilized in Nigeria, undermining the transparency and accountability essential for effective democratic governance. Despite being enacted over a decade ago, public institutions have failed to maximize its implementation, leading to widespread opacity in how government services are delivered. This lack of compliance prevents citizens from accessing vital information, thereby weakening public trust and hindering the full realization of open government principles. To address this persistent gap, stakeholders are launching targeted interventions, including a national workshop for high-ranking federal and state officials. These sessions aim to educate decision-makers on their legal obligations regarding record-keeping and information disclosure. By focusing on those with authority over public data, the initiative seeks to institutionalize a culture of openness, ensuring that bureaucratic barriers do not obstruct the flow of information to the citizenry. This effort is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical link between legislative frameworks and actual data accessibility. Without strict adherence to freedom of information laws, open data initiatives remain theoretical rather than practical realities. Mobilizing media support and engaging civil society are crucial steps toward transforming policy into actionable transparency, ensuring that public sector data is not just available but actively used to enhance civic engagement and governmental integrity.
