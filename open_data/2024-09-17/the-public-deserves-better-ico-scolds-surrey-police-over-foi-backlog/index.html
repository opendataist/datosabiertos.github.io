‘The public deserves better’ – ICO scolds Surrey Police over FOI backlog

Surrey Police has received a formal reprimand from the Information Commissioner’s Office for failing to meet Freedom of Information compliance standards. The regulator highlighted a significant backlog where only a minority of requests are answered within the legal timeframe, with some pending for up to two years. This serious departure from expected standards demonstrates a systemic failure to prioritize transparency and public accountability. The core issue lies in the police force’s inability to provide a concrete strategy to address these delays despite repeated warnings. By neglecting to implement effective measures to clear the backlog, the organization has triggered enforcement actions that could lead to contempt of court charges. This highlights the critical importance of operational integrity in public data management, showing that procedural neglect directly undermines the rule of law and public trust. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the consequences of poor governance in information release. Open data initiatives rely on the premise that public bodies are accessible and responsive; when administrative failures obstruct basic information rights, the entire ecosystem of transparency suffers. Ensuring robust data handling processes is not just a regulatory requirement but a fundamental necessity for maintaining societal confidence in open government practices.
