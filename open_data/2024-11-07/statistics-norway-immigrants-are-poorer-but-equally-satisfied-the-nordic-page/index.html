Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Norwegian immigrants face significantly disadvantaged living conditions compared to the general population, struggling with lower incomes, poorer employment, and substandard housing. Despite these material hardships, they report satisfaction levels comparable to natives, indicating that subjective well-being is not strictly tied to objective economic status. This paradox suggests that cultural adaptation and social integration may play crucial roles in maintaining life satisfaction amidst structural inequalities. The data reveals a concerning public health disparity, as immigrants experience higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Specific groups from the Middle East show particularly elevated stress levels, highlighting the need for targeted psychological support services. This mental strain contrasts sharply with their strong institutional confidence, as immigrants demonstrate higher trust in the political system, judiciary, and police than native Norwegians. This suggests that while personal social circles may feel isolated, faith in state structures remains robust. This report is vital for open data because it underscores the importance of transparent, comprehensive statistical monitoring of vulnerable demographics. By making detailed insights into the lived experiences of immigrants publicly accessible, authorities and researchers can identify gaps in social policy and resource allocation. Open access to such nuanced data empowers stakeholders to advocate for evidence-based interventions that address both the economic disparities and mental health challenges faced by immigrant communities.
