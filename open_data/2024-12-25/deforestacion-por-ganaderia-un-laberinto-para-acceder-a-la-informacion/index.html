Deforestación por ganadería: un laberinto para acceder a la información

Deforestación por ganadería: un laberinto para acceder a la información

The article concludes that the absence of a unified, environmentally focused livestock traceability system in Colombia prevents the public from verifying whether commercial meat contributes to Amazon deforestation. This opacity not only hinders informed consumer choices but also obstructs effective citizen oversight of environmental management, allowing legal and illegal deforestation practices linked to cattle ranching to continue unchecked, despite their significant ecological and social impacts. The primary barriers stem from a fragmented information landscape, where critical data is held privately by companies citing trade secrets or scattered across inefficient public agencies. The current traceability framework, designed solely for animal health, fails to link individual animals to specific land parcels or environmental indicators. Consequently, even when data exists, it is often incompatible, unintelligible to non-experts, or withheld, creating a transparency gap that leaves the public unable to hold both state and private actors accountable. This issue is central to open data because it illustrates how the absence of accessible, interoperable, and standardized public information undermines democratic rights and environmental protection. By emphasizing the need for data to be released openly and comprehensibly, the text aligns with the principles of the Escazú Agreement, arguing that transparent, high-quality data is a prerequisite for empowering civil society to demand sustainable practices and prevent environmental degradation in vulnerable regions.
