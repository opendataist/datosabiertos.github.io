Se confirmó que Villarruel dijo la verdad sobre el traspaso de mando y cae la operación del gobierno para salvar a Kueider

The article highlights how access to public information enabled the debunking of a government narrative and the resolution of a political dispute over the legality of a Senate session. Deputy Maximiliano Ferraro exercised his right to request official documents to confirm that Vice President Victoria Villarruel was officially notified of the change in command hours after a senator’s expulsion had taken place. This finding, based on notarial records, invalidates the government’s prior claims that the Vice President had been informed of the President’s trip earlier, which was central to its attempt to annul the vote. This revelation has significant implications for transparency and the use of public data as a tool for political oversight. By demonstrating that the government’s official version was incorrect regarding the timing of communications, the case exemplifies how the proactive request for public records can expose inconsistencies in state management. This strengthens accountability, as it enables legislators and citizens to verify the truthfulness of officials’ statements through concrete documentary evidence rather than relying solely on press releases or informal accounts. The relevance to open data lies in the critical importance of accessible government records for ensuring democratic integrity. When official data on origins and timelines are available and auditable, the room for misinformation and political maneuvers based on fabricated facts is reduced. This incident illustrates that transparency is not merely an abstract principle but an operational mechanism that allows for the correction of procedural errors or fraud, ensuring that institutional decisions are based on verifiable facts and clear records.
