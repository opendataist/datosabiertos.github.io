Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

The report highlights a significant paradox in Norway: immigrants face notably poorer living conditions regarding employment, housing, and health compared to the general population, yet report equal levels of satisfaction with life in the country. This disconnect underscores that material disadvantage does not necessarily correlate with dissatisfaction, revealing a complex relationship between socioeconomic status and subjective well-being among migrant communities. Despite lower incomes and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness, immigrants in Norway demonstrate remarkably high confidence in state institutions, including the political system, judiciary, and police. This strong institutional trust and affiliation suggest that social cohesion and integration may be driven more by faith in governance structures than by immediate economic parity or personal social connectivity. This article is highly relevant to open data because it showcases the critical value of disaggregated statistical analysis. By making detailed survey data available, researchers and policymakers can identify nuanced patterns, such as specific nationalities facing higher mental health challenges or distinct trust dynamics. Open access to these metrics enables targeted interventions and fosters a deeper understanding of societal inequalities that aggregate national statistics might otherwise obscure.
