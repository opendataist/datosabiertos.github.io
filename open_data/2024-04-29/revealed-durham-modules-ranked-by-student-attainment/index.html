Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

The article demonstrates the practical value of Freedom of Information requests in promoting institutional transparency. By accessing and analyzing previously hidden academic data, researchers can uncover significant disparities in student attainment across different departments and modules. This highlights how open data initiatives enable the public to scrutinize university performance metrics that are typically buried in internal reports, fostering greater accountability. Key findings reveal stark contrasts in educational outcomes, with Physics and English leading in high honors, while Foundation years and certain modern languages lag significantly. Such disparities suggest that factors like assessment methods or student preparation heavily influence results. The data exposes inconsistencies in grading standards, providing a clear picture of where students excel or struggle, which is essential for identifying systemic issues within the higher education landscape. This content is relevant to open data because it illustrates how citizen-led journalism and public records requests can democratize information. When universities release granular data on module performance, it empowers stakeholders, including prospective students and educators, to make informed decisions. Ultimately, this case study reinforces the importance of accessible, detailed data in driving improvements in teaching quality and ensuring equitable opportunities for all students.
