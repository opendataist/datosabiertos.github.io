OpenAI presenta un nuevo modelo de derechos de autor que permite el uso de obras con copyright

OpenAI presenta un nuevo modelo de derechos de autor que permite el uso de obras con copyright

OpenAI has updated its policy for Sora, allowing the AI to generate videos from copyrighted works unless rights holders explicitly notify their desire to opt out. This approach shifts the burden of protection onto creators, who must act on a case-by-case basis and cannot request blanket prohibitions for their entire catalogs. The distinction between copyright and personal image rights reflects a competitive strategy against rivals like Google, prioritizing rapid technological deployment. Although this stance creates friction with Hollywood and legal experts, it aligns with recent judicial interpretations that favor AI training as fair use, provided the output is transformative. This development is relevant to open data because it establishes a crucial precedent regarding the interoperability and reuse of public and protected information in AI systems. The decision underscores the tension between data openness for technological advancement and intellectual property rights, inviting the community to reflect on how to balance open innovation with the protection of human creativity in an increasingly automated environment.
