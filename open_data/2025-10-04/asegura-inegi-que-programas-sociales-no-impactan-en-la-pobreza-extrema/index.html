Asegura Inegi que programas sociales no impactan en la pobreza extrema

Asegura Inegi que programas sociales no impactan en la pobreza extrema

The decline in extreme poverty in Mexico between 2016 and 2024 is primarily driven by increases in individual income rather than by social assistance programs. This finding reveals a critical disconnect, as the majority of people living in extreme poverty do not benefit from government aid. Consequently, the effectiveness of current social welfare strategies is called into question, highlighting that mere financial transfers are insufficient without ensuring broader socioeconomic stability. At the same time, access to healthcare and social security has deteriorated significantly, with related deprivations nearly doubling over the period. This regression underscores a systemic failure to protect fundamental human rights, leaving vulnerable populations without essential services. The data indicate that, despite some recovery from historical peaks, the country remains on a backward trajectory regarding basic health access, exacerbating the fragility of the poor who lack safety nets. This information is vital for open data initiatives, as it challenges simplified narratives about success in poverty reduction. By providing granular insights into which demographics are excluded from support and which rights are most violated, such data enables more targeted policy interventions. It emphasizes the need for transparent metrics that go beyond headline poverty rates to reveal structural gaps in social inclusion, facilitating evidence-based advocacy for comprehensive welfare reform rather than superficial economic fixes.
