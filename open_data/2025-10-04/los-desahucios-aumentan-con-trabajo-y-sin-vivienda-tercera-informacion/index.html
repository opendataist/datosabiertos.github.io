Los desahucios aumentan, con trabajo y sin vivienda - Tercera Información

Los desahucios aumentan, con trabajo y sin vivienda - Tercera Información

Spain is facing a severe housing crisis, as evidenced by a sharp increase in mortgage foreclosures and rental evictions. This surge shows that high housing costs are now affecting ordinary families trying to buy homes, not just investors, due to high interest rates and low wages. At the same time, rental costs are overwhelming tenants, with a significant portion of renters spending an excessive share of their income, highlighting a systemic failure in which both buying and renting have become unaffordable for large segments of the population. The crisis is worsened by the dominance of private equity firms and banks that acquired properties at low prices after the 2008 housing bubble. These entities profit from soaring prices, creating a feedback loop that drives up market costs and limits access for ordinary citizens. The concentration of ownership in speculative funds prevents market stabilization, forcing families into debt or eviction while institutional actors capitalize on scarcity, thereby deepening social inequality and destabilizing communities across the country. This situation is relevant to open data because transparent, standardized, and accessible datasets on housing transactions, rental prices, and ownership concentrations are essential to expose these disparities. Without open data, it is difficult to monitor the impact of speculative investment or evaluate the effectiveness of proposed policy solutions. Data transparency empowers citizens and policymakers to demand structural reforms, such as social housing targets and rent controls, ensuring that housing is recognized as a right rather than a speculative asset.
