INEC: Inversión Extranjera Directa en Panamá sube 3,5 % en 2023, y cae para la Cepal un 22 %

INEC: Inversión Extranjera Directa en Panamá sube 3,5 % en 2023, y cae para la Cepal un 22 %

Panama recorded its highest foreign direct investment (FDI) inflows in years, consolidating a positive trend that surpasses 2022 figures. This dynamic reflects the country’s ability to remain attractive to international capital, with a predominance of continental sources and a diversified flow ranging from banking to free trade zones. The accumulated growth indicates robustness in the country’s capacity to attract resources, reinforcing its position as a key financial and logistical hub in the region. It is crucial to distinguish between the total amount invested and net flows, which experienced a slight contraction driven mainly by a decline in inflows from the Americas. However, this negative trend was offset by a significant rebound in investments from Europe and Asia, highlighting a geographic reconfiguration of capital. This nuance is essential for understanding that, although gross inflows remain high, the sustainability of investment depends on a regional balance that avoids exclusive dependence on traditional markets. This report is relevant for open data because it illustrates the complexity and multidimensional structure of the national macroeconomy. Data from the National Institute of Statistics and Census (INEC) demonstrate how transparency in publishing financial flows disaggregated by origin, sector, and entity type enables more precise analysis of economic impact. By making these variables accessible, it facilitates accountability and the development of research on the effectiveness of incentive policies, which are essential for evidence-based economic governance.
