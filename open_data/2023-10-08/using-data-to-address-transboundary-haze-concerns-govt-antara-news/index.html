Using data to address transboundary haze concerns: govt - ANTARA News

Indonesia’s Ministry of Environment and Forestry highlights its reliance on open data from regional meteorological centers and satellite imagery to accurately monitor and address transboundary haze pollution. By utilizing accessible, scientific datasets, the government can objectively verify whether smoke crosses international borders, thereby fostering transparency and trust among ASEAN neighbors. This data-driven approach allows authorities to respond to environmental concerns based on facts rather than speculation, ensuring that public communication is grounded in verifiable evidence. The accessibility of these open data sources enables all stakeholders, including neighboring countries, to independently confirm the status of haze dispersion. This shared reliance on common, transparent information sources supports the principles of the ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution, which emphasizes coordinated monitoring and assessment. When nations operate from the same verified data, it facilitates smoother diplomatic cooperation and mutual understanding, reducing tensions and enabling more effective regional emergency responses. This case underscores the critical role of open data in solving complex, cross-border environmental challenges. By making monitoring data publicly available, governments can empower civil society and international partners to validate official actions, promoting accountability and collaborative problem-solving. Ultimately, this transparency not only strengthens regional partnerships but also enhances the overall effectiveness of disaster management strategies through shared scientific knowledge and verified operational insights.
