Avon & Somerset Police recruitment 'racially discriminatory'

A Freedom of Information request has exposed significant racial disparities in the Avon & Somerset Constabulary’s recent recruitment campaign, revealing that Black and Asian candidates faced substantially higher rejection rates than their white counterparts. This systemic bias undermines the force’s stated goal of creating a diverse workforce that reflects the community it serves. The findings highlight a disconnect between recruitment targets and actual outcomes, suggesting that current assessment procedures may inadvertently disadvantage minority ethnic groups despite the presence of supportive outreach efforts. The data indicates that minority applicants are not only applying at proportional rates but are also being filtered out at significantly higher frequencies, pointing to potential structural issues within the selection process rather than a lack of interest. This situation is particularly concerning given that police leadership has previously acknowledged institutional racism within the organization. The evidence suggests that without immediate and thorough investigation into these disproportionate outcomes, the force risks failing to deliver on its commitment to equitable representation and eroding public trust among marginalized communities. This revelation is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent access to government employment statistics can expose hidden inequalities that organizations may overlook or minimize. By making application and acceptance rates public, civil society and media can hold public institutions accountable for their diversity claims. It underscores the necessity of rigorous data transparency to identify adverse impact in hiring practices, ensuring that recruitment processes are fair, unbiased, and truly representative of the population they aim to serve.
