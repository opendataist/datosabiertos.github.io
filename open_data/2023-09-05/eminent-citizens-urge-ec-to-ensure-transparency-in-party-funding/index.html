Eminent citizens urge EC to ensure transparency in party funding

Prominent activists and former officials argue that India’s current electronic voting system lacks the transparency required for genuine democratic integrity. They contend that the existing Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail is insufficient, as the paper slips are too small, transient, and rarely counted. This setup fails to ensure that votes are cast, recorded, and counted as intended, undermining public trust in the electoral process. The core demand is for a fully verifiable paper trail that voters can physically hold and verify, followed by a comprehensive manual count of these slips before results are announced. Additionally, advocates stress the need for procedural fairness, such as prior notice for voter list deletions. These measures are deemed essential to prevent arbitrary changes and ensure that the electoral rolls accurately reflect the citizenry, safeguarding the right to vote against potential manipulation. This discourse is critically relevant to open data because it highlights the necessity of accessible, auditable, and transparent datasets in governance. Without open, verifiable records of every vote cast, the integrity of election data remains opaque and unchallengeable. Ensuring that voting mechanisms produce tangible, preserved data supports accountability, allowing citizens and observers to audit results independently, which is a foundational principle of open government and digital transparency.
