La nueva disputa entre la Usach y el CPLT tras caso Loncon: universidad negó entrega de datos de académicos con año sabático | The Clinic

The University of Santiago de Chile is currently involved in a legal dispute over its refusal to disclose information regarding academic sabbatical leaves. This conflict highlights a recurring pattern of institutional opacity, as the university has previously resisted transparency demands in high-profile cases. By withholding data, the institution prioritizes administrative secrecy over public accountability, creating a significant barrier to understanding how public funds are used for academic benefits. At the heart of this issue is the tension between the right to information and the protection of personal data. The university argues that releasing specific details about individual academics infringes on privacy rights, citing legal exemptions to block disclosure. However, transparency authorities have challenged these claims, emphasizing that the public interest in monitoring institutional practices outweighs individual privacy concerns in this context. This legal battle underscores the difficulty of balancing privacy protections with the necessity of administrative transparency in public education. This case is critically relevant to open data because it tests the boundaries of data accessibility within Chile’s public sector. It demonstrates how institutions can leverage privacy laws to obscure operational metrics, hindering efforts to establish comprehensive open data standards. The outcome will determine whether academic institutions must treat sabbatical data as public information or can classify it as private, setting a precedent for future transparency initiatives in higher education.
