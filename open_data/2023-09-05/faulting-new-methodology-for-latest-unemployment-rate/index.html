Faulting new methodology for latest unemployment rate

The article critiques the National Bureau of Statistics’ new methodology for calculating unemployment in Nigeria, arguing that redefining "employed" as anyone working just one hour a week renders the data disconnected from economic reality. By drastically lowering the threshold for employment and expanding the labor force age limit, the report produces misleadingly low unemployment figures that fail to reflect the widespread poverty and lack of viable income opportunities faced by the majority of citizens. This approach creates a narrative of full employment that contradicts the lived experiences of millions, raising serious doubts about the validity and utility of the statistics. The disconnect between the new metrics and actual living conditions undermines public trust in official economic indicators. Critics highlight that an hour of weekly work cannot generate sufficient income to sustain a household, especially given widespread non-compliance with minimum wage laws. Consequently, the methodology is viewed not as a scientific improvement but as a diplomatic tool to mask severe economic distress. The absence of comprehensive data on the total number of unemployed individuals further exacerbates skepticism, as policymakers appear to prioritize theoretical compliance with international standards over practical, actionable insights for the population. This case is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it illustrates the critical importance of contextual accuracy and public transparency in statistical collection. When data collection methods ignore local socio-economic realities or lack broad, verifiable coverage, the resulting datasets become unreliable for decision-making and erode civic engagement. For open data ecosystems to function effectively, methodologies must be rigorously explained, grounded in tangible outcomes, and sensitive to the specific cultural and economic contexts they aim to measure, ensuring that published statistics serve the public interest rather than political convenience.
