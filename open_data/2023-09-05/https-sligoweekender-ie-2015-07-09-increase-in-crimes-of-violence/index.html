The article highlights a complex shift in crime reporting within the Sligo-Leitrim division, revealing simultaneous increases in violent offenses and decreases in property-related crimes. Crucially, it addresses the context of data reliability, noting that official statistics resumed publication only after a rigorous review of the PULSE recording system. This review uncovered significant historical irregularities, including misclassifications and unrecorded incidents, which previously necessitated a suspension of data release. This relevance to open data lies in the critical importance of data governance and integrity. The incident demonstrates how systemic errors in administrative records can distort public understanding of safety trends. It serves as a cautionary tale for open data initiatives, emphasizing that accessible statistics are only valuable if they are accurate, consistently recorded, and transparently audited. Without such rigor, open data risks misleading stakeholders and policymakers. Ultimately, the article underscores the need for robust quality assurance in public datasets. The correction of past recording errors illustrates that open data is not a static product but a process requiring continuous monitoring and improvement. For open data advocates, this case validates the necessity of third-party reviews and clear metadata standards. It reminds us that trust in public information depends heavily on the underlying methods used to collect and classify that information, ensuring that openness does not compromise accuracy.
