Explainer: What is OCR, and how does it work? | Biometric Update

Optical character recognition transforms static image data into searchable, editable text, addressing critical limitations in managing digital information. This capability is vital as businesses increasingly rely on digitized print media to streamline operations and enhance productivity. By eliminating manual intervention, organizations can efficiently process complex documents and integrate them into broader analytical frameworks. The technology operates by converting scanned images into binary formats, distinguishing characters from background noise. It employs algorithms like pattern matching or feature extraction to identify letters and numbers, ensuring accurate translation into machine-readable formats. This technical precision allows for the automation of form completion and the seamless conversion of physical documents into digital assets. This article highlights OCR’s role in modernizing open data infrastructure by bridging the gap between physical records and digital accessibility. It underscores the importance of converting unstructured visual data into structured text, which is essential for transparent governance and automated verification. Understanding these mechanisms supports efforts to make identity documents and other sensitive records more accessible and analyzable for public use.
