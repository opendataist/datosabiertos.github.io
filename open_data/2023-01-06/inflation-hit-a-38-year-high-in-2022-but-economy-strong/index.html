Inflation hit a 38 year high in 2022 but economy strong

The Central Statistics Office’s 2022 data reveals a year marked by severe inflationary pressures, driven primarily by volatile energy and food costs. This economic turbulence significantly impacted key sectors, causing drastic price hikes in fertilizer and construction materials, which altered industry behaviors despite some recovery in housing completions. These figures highlight the urgent need for transparent, accessible economic indicators to help businesses and policymakers navigate post-pandemic financial instability. Simultaneously, the report underscores the resilience of domestic travel and public transport usage, which have largely returned to pre-pandemic levels. While property prices surged to historic highs, the nuanced growth metrics offer a clearer picture of the underlying economy by distinguishing between multinational-influenced GDP and domestic demand. Such granular, publicly available data is essential for understanding the true state of national wealth and consumer behavior beyond superficial aggregates. This compilation is critically relevant to open data initiatives as it demonstrates how standardized, freely accessible statistical releases empower stakeholders to make informed decisions. By providing detailed breakdowns of inflation, housing, and transport, the CSO facilitates evidence-based policy and market analysis. Open access to such comprehensive datasets ensures that the complexities of economic recovery are visible to all, fostering transparency and accountability in public discourse and private investment.
