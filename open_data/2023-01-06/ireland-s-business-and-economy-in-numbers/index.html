Ireland's Business and Economy in Numbers

The article highlights a complex economic landscape characterized by high inflation and shifting construction dynamics. It reveals that while rising energy and food costs pressured household budgets and construction inputs, the housing sector paradoxically saw increased completion rates despite a significant drop in new planning permissions. This divergence suggests that current housing supply is being driven by projects authorized in previous periods, facing headwinds from elevated material costs and reduced future approval volumes. Furthermore, the report underscores the structural duality of the Irish economy, particularly the contrast between Gross Domestic Product growth and Gross National Product decline. This disparity reflects the outsized influence of multinational corporations on statistical aggregates, which can mask underlying domestic economic conditions. Simultaneously, household wealth has grown alongside corporate tax revenues, yet this financial accumulation exists within a broader context of global trade dependencies and ongoing recovery from pandemic-induced disruptions in sectors like tourism and hospitality. This dataset is vital for open data initiatives as it provides a granular view of national economic health beyond surface-level metrics. By making detailed statistics on inflation, housing, and GDP components publicly available, it enables researchers, policymakers, and the public to critically analyze the gap between headline growth and lived economic reality. Such transparency fosters accountability and allows for more informed public discourse on how macroeconomic indicators translate into microeconomic impacts for citizens and businesses.
