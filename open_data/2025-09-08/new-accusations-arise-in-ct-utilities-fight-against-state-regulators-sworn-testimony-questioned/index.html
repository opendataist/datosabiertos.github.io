New accusations arise in CT utilities fight against state regulators. Sworn testimony questioned.

New accusations arise in CT utilities fight against state regulators. Sworn testimony questioned.

A contentious dispute has emerged between Connecticut utilities and the Public Utility Regulatory Authority (PURA) over allegations that Chair Marissa Gillett has centralized control over regulatory processes. Utilities claim an internal directive restricted other commissioners from consulting staff experts without approval, arguing this undermines impartiality and leads to unfair rate decisions. PURA firmly denies the existence of such a policy, asserting that all commissioners have full access to staff, though they cannot produce the disputed document, which witnesses insist exists while PURA’s general counsel states it never did. This disagreement is compounded by the disappearance of text messages between Gillett and a legislator, which utilities argue suggest improper collaboration and attempts to evade public records laws. Although Gillett claims the deleted texts referred to legislative drafts, critics point to discussions about opinion pieces as evidence of bias. These transparency issues, alongside the missing email, have fueled accusations that Gillett is bypassing statutory obligations to exercise unilateral power, prompting utilities to file complaints alleging intentional withholding of public records. This conflict is highly relevant to open data because it highlights critical failures in transparency and accountability within regulatory bodies. The inability to locate or verify key documents creates a significant barrier to public oversight and independent verification of governmental actions. For open data advocates, this situation underscores the necessity of robust record-keeping policies and immediate public access to agency communications to prevent the potential manipulation of regulatory processes and ensure trust in public institutions.
