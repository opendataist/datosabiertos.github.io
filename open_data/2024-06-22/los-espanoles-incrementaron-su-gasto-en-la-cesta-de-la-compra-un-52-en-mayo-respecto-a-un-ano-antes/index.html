Los españoles incrementaron su gasto en la cesta de la compra un 5,2% en mayo respecto a un año antes

The article reveals a shift in Spanish consumer behavior characterized by rising prices driving total spending, while demand growth remains modest. Crucially, households are increasingly favoring private-label brands over manufacturer brands, indicating a strategic response to inflation. This trend is evident as store-brand sales grow significantly in both value and volume, whereas national brands stagnate or decline, highlighting a structural change in purchasing power and brand loyalty amid economic pressure. Simultaneously, the retail landscape is undergoing a digital transformation, with online sales surging dramatically compared to traditional formats like hypermarkets, which are losing ground. This channel shift reflects evolving convenience preferences, complemented by specific product demand trends such as strong growth in dairy and health-related items. These dynamics illustrate how physical distribution channels are adapting to new consumer habits, moving away from purely in-store experiences toward integrated digital strategies. This data is highly relevant to open_data because it demonstrates the value of granular, real-time consumer insights for transparency and market analysis. Access to such detailed breakdowns of prices, volumes, and channel performance allows researchers, policymakers, and businesses to understand inflationary impacts and distribution shifts accurately. Open availability of this information fosters accountability in retail markets and supports evidence-based decisions regarding economic health, supply chain efficiency, and consumer welfare.
