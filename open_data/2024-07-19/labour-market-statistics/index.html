Labour Market Statistics

Labour Market Statistics

Northern Ireland’s labor market shows a mixed but generally improving picture, with payrolled employment and earnings rising significantly over the year. Unemployment and economic inactivity rates both decreased, while the employment rate improved, suggesting a strengthening overall job market. However, these headline improvements were not statistically significant, and weekly hours worked remain below pre-pandemic levels. Recent monthly data reveals a rise in the claimant count rate, driven primarily by administrative changes to Universal Credit thresholds rather than underlying economic weakness. Conversely, while proposed redundancies remain lower than the previous year, confirmed redundancies have nearly doubled annually. Despite this sharp increase, the total number of confirmed redundancies is comparable to historical trends seen just before the pandemic, indicating that current levels are within expected parameters rather than signaling a new crisis. This data is relevant to open data initiatives as it highlights the critical importance of transparent, multi-source economic indicators. By making diverse datasets—such as payroll records, survey results, and redundancy notifications—freely available, researchers and policymakers can better distinguish between temporary statistical fluctuations and genuine structural labor market trends. Open access to these varied metrics allows for more robust analysis, ensuring that decisions are based on comprehensive evidence rather than relying on a single, potentially misleading, headline figure.
