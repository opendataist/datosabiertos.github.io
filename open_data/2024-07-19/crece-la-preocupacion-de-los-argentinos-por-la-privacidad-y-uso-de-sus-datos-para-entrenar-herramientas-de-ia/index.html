Crece la preocupación de los argentinos por la privacidad y uso de sus datos para entrenar herramientas de IA

Crece la preocupación de los argentinos por la privacidad y uso de sus datos para entrenar herramientas de IA

The study reveals that Argentine internet users are increasingly concerned about privacy, particularly regarding the unauthorized use of their personal data to train artificial intelligence models. This anxiety has led a significant majority of users to abandon specific applications, highlighting a growing distrust toward platforms that handle personal information without adequate transparency or user control. Despite these strong privacy fears, there is a notable disconnect in user behavior and awareness. Many respondents remain unaware that their data fuels AI development, and a substantial portion would still consent to having their information used for AI training. This paradox suggests that while users value privacy in theory, practical barriers and a lack of clear understanding often override their initial caution, creating a complex landscape for data ethics. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical need for ethical transparency in how datasets are sourced and utilized. As open data initiatives often involve aggregating and analyzing user information, understanding public sentiment and consent mechanisms is vital. The findings emphasize that trust is a prerequisite for successful data ecosystems, urging developers to prioritize informed consent and clear communication to align technological progress with societal expectations of privacy and safety.
