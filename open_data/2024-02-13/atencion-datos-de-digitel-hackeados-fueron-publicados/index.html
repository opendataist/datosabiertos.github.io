¡ATENCIÓN! | Datos de Digitel hackeados fueron publicados

The breach of Digitel’s systems highlights the severe risks associated with data hoarding. When attackers deployed Medusa ransomware, they threatened to leak sensitive financial and personal records unless a ransom was paid. This incident demonstrates how centralized data storage creates high-value targets, emphasizing the urgent need for robust cybersecurity frameworks to protect both corporate and individual privacy. Furthermore, the event underscores the tension between corporate transparency and user trust. Although Digitel attempted to contain the threat, service disruptions and fears of data exposure eroded confidence. This scenario illustrates why open data initiatives must prioritize security standards; without them, public access to information can inadvertently expose vulnerable populations to exploitation or fraud. Relevant to open data, this case serves as a critical cautionary tale. It argues that publishing datasets requires rigorous anonymization and safety protocols. If organizations cannot secure their own data, public repositories become unsafe. Therefore, the open data movement must advocate for stronger data governance to ensure that transparency does not compromise individual rights and organizational integrity.
