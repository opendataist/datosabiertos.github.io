SNP accused of presiding over 'rotten culture of secrecy' amid FoI responses anger

The article highlights critical failures in the Scottish Government’s adherence to Freedom of Information protocols, particularly during the pandemic. The Scottish Information Commissioner issued a severe intervention after evidence presented to the UK inquiry revealed that decision-making processes were often undocumented or lost. This systemic lapse deprived the public of transparency and frustrated their legal right to access information, fundamentally undermining the spirit of open governance. A central implication for open data is the danger of relying on informal communication channels, such as WhatsApp, for official business. These practices pose significant risks to accountability because unrecorded discussions cannot be retrieved for public scrutiny or historical record. The Commissioner’s investigation aims to rectify these procedural flaws, emphasizing that retaining clear records of how decisions are reached is essential for maintaining public trust and ensuring that lessons learned are preserved for future policy-making. Political fallout underscores the tension between government secrecy and public accountability. Opposition parties accuse the administration of fostering a culture of cover-up, while government officials defend their record by citing high compliance rates and the necessity of applying legal exemptions. This conflict illustrates the broader challenge in the open data landscape: balancing operational flexibility and privacy with the imperative for transparent, accessible, and permanently archived public records.
