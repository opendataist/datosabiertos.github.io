Google Gemini Warning: Don't Share Confidential Information

Google’s Gemini apps inform users that their input, including text, audio, and images, may be reviewed by human annotators and incorporated into AI training datasets. This practice allows the company to enhance its generative models but means that any data processed could potentially be seen by humans or used to improve future products. Consequently, users are explicitly warned against sharing confidential or sensitive information, as these interactions are not guaranteed to remain private from both the company and external reviewers. Users retain some control over their data by adjusting settings to limit storage duration or opting out of saving conversations to their Google Account. However, disabling activity logging does not eliminate all data retention, as information may still be kept briefly for service stability or shared with integrated third-party services under their respective privacy policies. This creates a complex landscape where individual privacy choices may not fully prevent data from being utilized across Google’s ecosystem or by external partners involved in the user’s interactions. This information is critical for the open data community because it highlights the significant privacy risks inherent in using commercial AI tools that rely on user-generated content for model improvement. It underscores the necessity for transparency in how AI companies handle input data and suggests that individuals must actively manage their digital footprints to avoid unintended data exposure. For advocates of data ethics, this serves as a clear example of how proprietary systems can compromise user privacy despite existing safeguards, reinforcing the need for robust data protection standards in AI development.
