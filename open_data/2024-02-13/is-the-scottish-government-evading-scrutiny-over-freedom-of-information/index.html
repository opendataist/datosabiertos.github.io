Is the Scottish Government 'evading scrutiny' over Freedom of Information?

The article argues that the Scottish Government must prioritize fixing fundamental transparency practices rather than dismissing calls for broad legislative reform. Recent controversies, including the mishandling of official communications via WhatsApp and perceived secrecy surrounding ministerial devices, have severely damaged public trust. These incidents highlight a critical disconnect between official assurances of compliance and the reality of evading scrutiny, suggesting that procedural failures are more urgent than structural changes to Freedom of Information laws. A central implication is that the current interpretation of public accountability is insufficient for the digital age. The government’s insistence that existing legislation is robust ignores the need for modernization, particularly regarding personal devices used for state business. When officials joke about deleting messages or claim business was not conducted on personal platforms while evidence suggests otherwise, it undermines the integrity of the FOI system. This behavior signals that the culture of openness is lacking, regardless of the statutory framework in place. For the open data community, this narrative underscores that data availability is meaningless without institutional honesty and accessible governance. Trust is restored not merely through meeting response deadlines, but through proactive transparency and willingness to engage with oversight bodies. The article suggests that without a genuine cultural shift toward openness, efforts to expand data access or reform laws will fail to address the root cause of public skepticism, making authentic transparency the prerequisite for any successful open data initiative.
