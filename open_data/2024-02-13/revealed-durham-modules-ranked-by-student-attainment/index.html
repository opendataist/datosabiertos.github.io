Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

This article demonstrates the power of open data in democratizing access to institutional performance metrics. By utilizing a Freedom of Information Request, public entities can uncover granular insights into academic outcomes, transforming opaque administrative records into transparent, searchable datasets. This practice highlights how accessible information empowers students, researchers, and the public to critically evaluate educational standards and resource allocation across different departments. The analysis reveals significant disparities in student attainment, with Physics and English leading in overall achievement while foundational years and certain language modules lag behind. These findings underscore the variability in grading standards and module difficulty across disciplines. Such data exposes the nuanced landscape of higher education performance, allowing stakeholders to identify areas of excellence and struggle. It serves as a reminder that aggregate averages often mask the complex realities faced by individual students and specific courses. Relevance to open data lies in its ability to foster accountability and informed decision-making. When academic statistics are publicly available, it encourages universities to maintain high standards and supports prospective students in making educated choices. Furthermore, this transparency aids in benchmarking and policy formulation. The article illustrates that releasing detailed educational data is not merely about reporting numbers but about creating a comprehensive resource for societal scrutiny and continuous improvement within the academic sector.
