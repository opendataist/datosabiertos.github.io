561 inmates on death row, highest in two decades in India - Pakistan Observer

The surge in India’s death row population reveals a critical disconnect between trial and appellate judicial processes. While trial courts continue imposing severe capital sentences, higher courts show increased scrutiny and intervention, indicating systemic flaws in initial verdicts. This widening gap suggests that institutional capacity for administering capital punishment is lagging behind the volume of sentencing. The persistent crisis at the trial level underscores the need for reform to ensure judicial consistency and reduce reliance on the death penalty. Understanding these statistics is vital for open data initiatives, as it highlights the importance of transparent, accessible criminal justice records. Reliable data enables civil society to monitor trends, advocate for policy changes, and hold institutions accountable for their sentencing practices.
