Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly harsher material realities than the native population, including lower income, poorer employment conditions, and inferior housing. Despite these socioeconomic disparities and notably higher rates of mental health struggles and loneliness, immigrants report satisfaction levels with life in Norway that are comparable to the general public. This paradox suggests that objective living conditions do not solely dictate subjective well-being, highlighting the resilience of immigrant communities amidst structural inequality. The study further reveals a complex relationship between immigrants and societal structures. While immigrants generally place higher trust in political institutions, the judiciary, and the police than natives, they exhibit lower interpersonal trust. This distinction underscores that institutional confidence can remain high even when social cohesion among peers is weaker. These findings challenge simplistic narratives about integration, showing that immigrants can feel securely embedded in the state’s framework while still experiencing social isolation and economic disadvantage. This research is vital for open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistics can reveal nuanced social dynamics that raw income figures might obscure. By making detailed survey data publicly available, researchers and policymakers can move beyond superficial metrics to understand the multidimensional nature of integration. Open data facilitates a deeper scrutiny of the gap between lived experience and statistical reality, enabling more informed and empathetic policy decisions that address both material needs and social belonging.
