Paul McCartney expresa su temor a que la IA plagie a los artistas

Paul McCartney urges the UK government to reject proposed copyright changes that would allow AI companies to use creative works for training models unless artists explicitly opt out. He argues that this shift undermines artists’ control, diminishes their economic rights, and threatens the integrity of the UK’s creative industries by enabling tech giants to profit from content without providing fair compensation to creators. The controversy highlights the tension between fostering AI innovation and protecting intellectual property rights. While the government seeks to position Britain as an AI leader by ensuring developers have broad access to high-quality creative data, creators and media organizations are organizing to demand stricter protections. This debate centers on whether current laws adequately safeguard human artistry against automated exploitation. This issue is crucial to open data because it questions the ethics of using publicly or commercially available creative works as training data for open or proprietary AI systems. It challenges the open data ethos by emphasizing that data derived from human creativity must respect authorship and revenue sharing, urging a balance where technological advancement does not erase the rights and livelihoods of individual creators.
