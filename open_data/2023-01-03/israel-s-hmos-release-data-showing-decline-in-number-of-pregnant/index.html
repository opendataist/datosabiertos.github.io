Israel's HMOs release data showing decline in number of pregnant...

The article presents compelling evidence of a significant decline in first-trimester pregnancies and subsequent births in Israel and several European countries, beginning shortly after the initiation of the COVID-19 vaccination rollout. By synthesizing data from multiple Israeli health maintenance organizations and international studies, the author argues that this demographic shift correlates strongly with vaccination campaigns rather than viral infection rates, suggesting potential physiological impacts on fertility. Central to this analysis is the revelation that adverse effects, such as menstrual cycle disruptions, may extend far beyond the initially described short-term duration. Internal medical discussions highlighted in the report indicate that a majority of reported cases experience long-term changes lasting over three months, with some instances worsening upon rechallenge. This challenges official narratives that dismissed these side effects as transient, raising serious questions about the long-term reproductive safety of mRNA vaccines. This information is critically relevant to the open data movement because it demonstrates how citizen-led Freedom of Information requests can bypass institutional secrecy to expose publicly significant health trends. The case underscores the importance of transparent, accessible healthcare data in enabling independent verification of public health measures and holding authorities accountable, particularly when internal warnings regarding safety signals are initially suppressed or downplayed.
