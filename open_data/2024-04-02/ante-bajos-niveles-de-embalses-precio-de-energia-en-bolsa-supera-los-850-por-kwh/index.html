Ante bajos niveles de embalses, precio de energía en bolsa supera los $850 por kWh

The critical state of water reservoirs due to El Niño has caused electricity spot prices to spike drastically, far exceeding ideal levels. This surge highlights the vulnerability of hydro-dependent energy grids to climate variability, demonstrating how environmental stressors directly impact market stability and consumer costs through immediate supply-demand imbalances. Industry groups warn that continued dry conditions will increase reliance on thermal generation, raising operational costs and systemic risks. This scenario underscores the urgent need for diversified energy sources and robust infrastructure management to ensure reliable power supply during extended dry seasons, preventing recurring economic shocks. This case is vital for open data initiatives because it exemplifies the real-world consequences of transparently accessible hydrological and energy market data. Public availability of real-time reservoir levels and price fluctuations enables stakeholders to monitor risks, advocate for policy changes, and promote resilience in national energy planning through informed, data-driven decision-making.
