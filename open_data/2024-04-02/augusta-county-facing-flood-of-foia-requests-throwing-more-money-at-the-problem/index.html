Augusta County, facing flood of FOIA requests, throwing more money at the problem

The article highlights a troubling pattern in Augusta County’s resistance to public transparency, where leadership prioritizes secrecy over the community’s right to know. By attempting to withhold records of an illegal closed meeting concerning a supervisor’s resignation, the county demonstrates a systemic reluctance to accept accountability. This approach mirrors a defensive stance against legitimate journalistic inquiries, suggesting that officials are more interested in shielding administrative errors than fostering trust through openness. At the heart of the conflict is the misuse of privacy exemptions to justify withholding potentially incriminating evidence, specifically regarding alleged sexual harassment. The court’s reversal, based on vague privacy claims, raises serious concerns about whether sensitive but irrelevant information is being protected under the guise of employee privacy. This legal maneuvering undermines the intent of freedom of information laws, creating a barrier to truth that disproportionately affects victims and the public who have a right to understand how tax dollars and personnel decisions are managed. Relevance to open data lies in the county’s proposal to hire a dedicated FOIA technician rather than reforming opaque practices. Rather than addressing the root cause of unnecessary secrecy by conducting more business in public, the administration seeks to bureaucratize the obstruction of information. This highlights a critical challenge in the open data movement: technology and staffing cannot compensate for a cultural lack of transparency. True openness requires proactive disclosure and clear data governance, not just efficient processing of requests for hidden information.
