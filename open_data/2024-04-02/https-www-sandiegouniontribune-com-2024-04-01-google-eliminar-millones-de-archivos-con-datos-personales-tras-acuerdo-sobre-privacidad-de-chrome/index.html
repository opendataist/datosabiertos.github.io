Open Data
ES EN

Google has agreed to delete billions of personal records collected through the Chrome browser, settling a class-action lawsuit that accused the company of illegally tracking users’ activity, even when they used incognito mode. This settlement involves deleting stored data and improving transparency regarding privacy options, setting an important precedent in the regulation of digital surveillance. This case is crucial for open data and corporate transparency, as it compels major technology companies to make their data collection practices visible. By requiring the company to clearly disclose its use of private browsing modes, it fosters a culture in which users can understand and control their digital footprints. Transparency in data handling is essential for society to evaluate and demand accountability in the treatment of personal information. Although there was no direct monetary compensation for consumers, the estimated value of the settlement reflects the impact of restricting data collection for advertising purposes. The significance lies in how this precedent may influence future privacy laws and the ethical management of data. By limiting indiscriminate data collection, it protects the integrity of individual information, establishing stricter standards for interoperability and responsible data access in the digital age.
