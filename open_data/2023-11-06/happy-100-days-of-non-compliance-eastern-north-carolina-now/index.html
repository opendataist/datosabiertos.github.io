Happy 100 Days of Non-Compliance | Eastern North Carolina Now

This article highlights a significant failure in governmental transparency, centering on the Washington Housing Authority’s refusal to comply with a Freedom of Information Act request regarding the expenditure of public funds. The narrative underscores the critical importance of open data in preventing corruption, illustrating how the lack of accessible financial records allows potential conflicts of interest and misuse of taxpayer money to remain hidden from the public eye. The core issue involves serious allegations of impropriety, including a clear conflict of interest where the Mayor’s wife leads the authority receiving public funds, and a Council Member allegedly profiting from transactions involving those same funds. By withholding details on trip expenses and financial flows, the authority prevents citizens from verifying whether public resources were used appropriately, thereby eroding trust in local governance and demonstrating the dangers of opaque administrative processes. The relevance to open data lies in the article’s assertion that mandatory transparency is essential for accountability. Without accessible, comprehensive data on government spending, voters cannot effectively hold officials responsible, leading to environments where power can corrupt without consequence. The author uses this situation to advocate for stricter adherence to information disclosure laws, arguing that open data is not merely a bureaucratic formality but a fundamental requirement for a functioning democracy and the rule of law.
