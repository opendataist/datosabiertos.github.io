It Is Important To Protect India’s Democratic Strengths Regarding Right To Information| Countercurrents

The article highlights a stark contradiction in India’s democratic framework: while the Right to Information (RTI) was historically celebrated as a vital tool for accountability and poverty alleviation, it is now critically weakened by systemic neglect. The Supreme Court’s recent designation of the RTI Act as a “dead letter law” stems from chronic vacancies in Information Commissions, which paralyze the appeal process and undermine public access to government data. This institutional paralysis threatens to erase the hard-won gains of transparency that empowered citizens to expose corruption and ensure equitable resource distribution. The relevance to open data lies in the recognition that transparency is not merely a legal formality but a grassroots imperative for social justice. The narrative traces how early RTI efforts, led by movements in Rajasthan and other states, successfully utilized public records to hold local officials accountable, restore misappropriated funds, and improve public service delivery. These historical successes demonstrate that when data is accessible and usable, it acts as a powerful mechanism for correcting systemic inequities and strengthening civic engagement, proving that open governance is essential for sustaining democratic culture. Ultimately, the current crisis of unfilled commissions represents a dangerous regression from the nation’s commitment to an open society. The article argues that restoring the functional capacity of information bodies is urgent to prevent the erosion of a democratic pillar. By reinvigorating the RTI infrastructure, India can recover its status as a leader in transparency, ensuring that the flow of public information remains a robust safeguard against corruption and a fundamental right of every citizen.
