CEGAIP tendrá que rendir informes trimestrales al Congreso

San Luis Potosí reformed its transparency laws to require quarterly reports from the state’s information access commission. This shift from annual to more frequent submissions enables the legislative body to monitor performance more closely, ensuring accountability and continuous oversight of transparency initiatives. The amendment clarifies the commission’s role in fostering a culture of openness within the legislature. By defining these specific competencies, the law aims to strengthen procedural rigor and support the commission in more effectively fulfilling its mandate. This update is relevant to open data because it establishes a framework for regular, public reporting on information access. Such transparency mechanisms are essential for building trust in public institutions and ensuring that data governance practices are visible, auditable, and consistently improved over time.
