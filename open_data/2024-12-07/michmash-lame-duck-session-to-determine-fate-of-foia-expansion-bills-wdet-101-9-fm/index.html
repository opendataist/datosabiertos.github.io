MichMash: Lame-duck session to determine fate of FOIA expansion bills - WDET 101.9 FM

Michigan’s executive and legislative branches are currently exempt from Freedom of Information Act requests, rendering the state among the least transparent in the nation. Although the Governor and legislative leadership express support for reform, significant political will is lacking, casting doubt on whether expanding public access will be prioritized in upcoming policy agendas. This opacity is particularly relevant to open data advocates, as it creates structural barriers to obtaining government information without proactive transparency measures. Without legal mandates forcing disclosure, citizens and researchers cannot easily access the underlying data that informs public policy, stifling accountability and independent analysis. Consequently, the lack of bipartisan commitment suggests that open data initiatives in Michigan will face prolonged stagnation. Until legislative exemptions are removed, the flow of critical information remains restricted, highlighting the urgent need for legal reforms that prioritize data accessibility as a fundamental right of governance rather than a discretionary privilege.
