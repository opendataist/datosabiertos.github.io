Online library drops its legal battle to provide free e-books without publishers' permission

The Internet Archive’s high-profile copyright case concluded with its decision not to appeal, effectively ending its "controlled digital lending" model. Publishers view this as a decisive victory, confirming that scanning and sharing copyrighted works without permission constitutes infringement rather than fair use. This ruling significantly restricts how online libraries can manage digital collections, prioritizing strict copyright enforcement over broad public access initiatives. It sets a legal precedent that challenges the viability of similar programs aiming to democratize knowledge through unauthorized digital distribution. For the open data movement, this outcome highlights the fragility of infrastructure built on contested legal interpretations. It underscores the critical need for clear, permissive licensing frameworks to ensure long-term preservation and accessibility of digital cultural heritage without fear of litigation.
