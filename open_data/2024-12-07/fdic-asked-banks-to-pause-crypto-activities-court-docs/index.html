FDIC asked banks to ‘pause’ crypto activities — Court docs

The release of internal FDIC documents confirms that US regulators explicitly instructed banks to halt cryptocurrency-related services in 2022, citing regulatory uncertainty. This revelation substantiates industry claims of "Operation Chokepoint 2.0," demonstrating a coordinated regulatory effort to restrict digital asset participation within the traditional financial system rather than leaving such exclusions to individual bank discretion alone. The transparency gained through this Freedom of Information Act lawsuit highlights the tension between opaque regulatory enforcement and public accountability. By exposing these "pause letters," the case underscores how hidden directives can effectively debank crypto firms, raising significant legal and ethical questions about due process and the arbitrary exercise of supervisory power by federal agencies. This incident is vital for the open data movement as it illustrates the critical necessity of FOIA in uncovering non-public government actions that impact market access. It demonstrates how accessible government records can expose systemic barriers to emerging technologies, empowering stakeholders to demand clearer, more equitable regulatory frameworks and ensuring that supervisory expectations are transparent rather than concealed behind redactions.
