Here's a quick glance at unemployment rates for November, by Canadian city

Statistics Canada released national and metropolitan unemployment data for November, revealing a national rate of 6.8 percent alongside city-specific figures. The report provides a snapshot of labor market conditions across major Canadian urban centers, highlighting regional variations in employment stability during this period. Crucially, Statistics Canada cautions that these metropolitan figures may fluctuate significantly due to reliance on small statistical samples. This limitation underscores the inherent uncertainty in localized employment metrics, suggesting that short-term changes in specific cities should be interpreted with caution rather than taken as definitive trends. This data is relevant to open data because it exemplifies the critical importance of metadata and statistical literacy when accessing public datasets. Understanding the limitations behind published statistics, such as sample size effects, is essential for accurate analysis and prevents the misinterpretation of localized economic indicators in broader policy or research contexts.
