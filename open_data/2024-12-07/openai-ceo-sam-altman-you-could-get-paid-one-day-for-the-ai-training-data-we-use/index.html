OpenAI CEO Sam Altman: You could get paid one day for the AI training data we use

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman: You could get paid one day for the AI training data we use

Sam Altman argues that a new economic standard is urgently needed to compensate original creators when their work is used to train large language models. He suggests that just as athletes benefit from their name and image, writers and artists should receive revenue when AI generates content emulating their style. This proposal highlights a critical ethical shift in how intellectual property is valued within the rapidly evolving AI landscape. The implementation faces significant challenges, including establishing fair compensation structures and preventing system abuse. While some companies, like ProRata, are developing algorithms to track and distribute revenue based on specific content contributions, the industry lacks a unified approach. The uncertainty surrounding these mechanisms reflects the broader struggle to balance technological advancement with creator rights, leaving many questions about feasibility and adoption timing. This discourse is highly relevant to open data because it addresses the fundamental question of data sovereignty and licensing. If AI systems require permission and payment to use creative works, it sets a precedent for how all data sources, including open datasets, should be treated. It challenges the notion that publicly available data is free to exploit without consent, urging a more transparent and equitable framework for data usage that respects the origins of information used to build intelligent systems.
