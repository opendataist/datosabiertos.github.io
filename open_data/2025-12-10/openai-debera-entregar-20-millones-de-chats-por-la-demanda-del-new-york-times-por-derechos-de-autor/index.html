OpenAI deberá entregar 20 millones de chats por la demanda del New York Times por derechos de autor

OpenAI deberá entregar 20 millones de chats por la demanda del New York Times por derechos de autor

A federal judge in New York has ordered OpenAI to hand over millions of anonymous ChatGPT conversations, rejecting the company’s attempt to restrict access to this data. This ruling favors The New York Times in its lawsuit alleging unauthorized use of copyrighted material, establishing that transparency is essential to verify whether AI models reproduce protected content, and prioritizing judicial relevance over the company’s privacy arguments. This decision represents a significant global precedent, as it could define how major artificial intelligence companies obtain, license, and use information to train their models. The case directly impacts competitors such as Anthropic and Perplexity, compelling them to reconsider their data acquisition strategies and safeguards to avoid unauthorized reproductions, thereby affecting the current legal framework governing technological development. The relevance to open data lies in the push for transparency regarding the origin and use of data, which are fundamental to the ethical and legal integrity of AI systems. By demanding access to operational records, the ruling promotes accountability in training processes, challenging the opaque use of protected information. This reinforces the need for clear frameworks that balance technological innovation with intellectual property rights and privacy, ensuring that data access serves to audit and regulate technology, rather than merely exploit it.
