La Estrategia de Disuasión Focalizada para reducir la violencia familiar en México

La Estrategia de Disuasión Focalizada para reducir la violencia familiar en México

The article highlights how structural violence against women in Mexico has historically been overlooked due to systemic underreporting and fear, often escalating to fatal outcomes like femicide. It argues that this pervasive violence requires a paradigm shift from treating incidents as private matters to addressing them as critical human rights violations. By recognizing the intergenerational impact and the link to other social vulnerabilities, the text emphasizes the urgent need for institutional accountability rather than relying on victim initiative. To address this, Zacatecas has implemented a focused deterrence strategy built on inter-institutional coordination and proactive policing. Central to this approach is a unified registry that consolidates data from various agencies to assess risk levels for aggressors. This system enables law enforcement to move beyond reactive measures, instead employing differentiated interventions based on severity. By centralizing information, authorities can monitor high-risk cases closely and ensure that warnings translate into immediate legal consequences if violence recurs, thereby disrupting the cycle of abuse. This initiative is vital to open data because it demonstrates how structured, shared datasets can directly inform public safety policy. By integrating disparate sources—such as emergency calls, police reports, and legal measures—into a cohesive registry, the strategy illustrates the power of interoperable information systems in real-world applications. It serves as a compelling case study for how open and accessible data governance can enhance transparency, improve decision-making, and ultimately protect vulnerable populations through evidence-based, coordinated action.
