Council owed more than £38m in unpaid council tax as year sees sharp rise

Council owed more than £38m in unpaid council tax as year sees sharp rise

Buckinghamshire Council faces a significant surge in council tax arrears, which has climbed to over £38 million across the last five years. This debt nearly quadrupled in the most recent financial year alone, largely driven by the cost-of-living crisis that has left many households struggling to meet basic expenses. While the overall in-year collection rate remains high, the accumulating backlog indicates increasing financial strain on residents. The rising debt threatens the stability of local public services, as council tax constitutes the majority of the authority’s external income. With nearly three-quarters of funding derived from this source, any shortfall directly impacts essential provisions such as housing support, road maintenance, and care for vulnerable citizens. The council emphasizes its commitment to balancing robust debt recovery with compassionate support for those in financial difficulty. This situation highlights critical challenges in open data transparency regarding public finance and social equity. Accurate, accessible data on arrears allows citizens and researchers to track the real-world impact of economic policies on local governance. By making these financial metrics openly available, stakeholders can better understand the tension between fiscal responsibility and social welfare, fostering informed public discourse on how local authorities manage revenue during periods of widespread economic hardship.
