PIB de México crece 0.6 % en el segundo trimestre de 2025, revela el Inegi

Mexico’s economy avoided a technical recession in the second quarter of 2025, posting modest quarterly growth. Although the industrial sector contracted, positive performance in the primary and tertiary sectors enabled the country to achieve consecutive quarters of annual expansion. This stability, despite uncertainty surrounding potential U.S. tariffs, suggests resilience rather than robust economic health, as analysts caution that avoiding recession does not equate to overall prosperity or strong underlying fundamentals. The data highlight a complex economic landscape in which services and agriculture drove growth while manufacturing struggled. International institutions offer divergent forecasts for the coming years, reflecting ongoing concerns about stagnation versus recovery. This nuanced performance underscores the Mexican economy’s vulnerability to external shocks and domestic political changes, indicating that while the immediate threat of contraction has passed, long-term stability remains precarious and dependent on navigating significant geopolitical and trade uncertainties. This information is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the critical importance of accessible, timely, and transparent official statistics for accurate economic assessment. Reliable data from national institutes enable policymakers, investors, and researchers to monitor real-time economic health, evaluate the impact of external factors such as trade policies, and make informed decisions. Open data fosters accountability and facilitates a deeper understanding of sectoral dynamics, ensuring that public discourse is grounded in verified facts rather than speculation.
