Authors & Copyright Scholars Back 'Internet Archive' in Landmark Legal Battle * TorrentFreak

Authors & Copyright Scholars Back 'Internet Archive' in Landmark Legal Battle * TorrentFreak

The legal battle between the Internet Archive and major publishers regarding its Controlled Digital Lending program highlights a critical tension in digital preservation and access. While lower courts ruled against the Archive’s self-scanning model, copyright scholars and the Authors Alliance argue that this approach constitutes fair use. They contend that libraries have historically operated within copyright law to lend materials, and the Internet Archive’s digital efforts simply extend this tradition to the digital realm, ensuring that public interest and scholarly access are not unduly restricted by commercial constraints. Supporters of the Internet Archive emphasize that the current publishing landscape suffers from significant market failure, where publishers wield excessive power over access, preservation, and pricing. By attempting to ban library lending practices, publishers are consolidating control that disrupts the balance between creators, librarians, and readers. The amicus briefs suggest that fair use is essential to correct this imbalance, allowing libraries to serve their public mission without being forced into restrictive licensing agreements that prevent the permanent archiving and lending of digitized works. This case is highly relevant to open data and open knowledge movements because it tests the boundaries of how digital cultural heritage can be preserved and shared. If the Internet Archive loses, it sets a precedent that could severely limit the ability of institutions to maintain open access to scholarly and public domain materials. Upholding the Archive’s model ensures that the benefits of the digital age remain accessible to all, rather than being locked behind commercial paywalls, reinforcing the principle that knowledge should be a public good rather than a restricted commodity.
