Las concentraciones en Ferraz de apoyo a Sánchez tras la carta el 24 y 25 de abril fueron ilegales

The article reveals that pro-government demonstrations in Madrid were deemed illegal because the organizers failed to submit the mandatory prior notice to local authorities. This legal assessment highlights a significant gap between the procedural requirements for public assemblies and the actual conduct of political events, emphasizing that adherence to notification laws is a strict condition for legitimacy, regardless of the cause or timing of the gathering. Furthermore, the investigation into potential sanctions against the organizers was deferred to the Ministry of the Interior, underscoring the separation of responsibilities between territorial administration and law enforcement. This procedural split illustrates the complexity of administrative accountability, where one body determines legality based on bureaucratic compliance, while another is responsible for penalizing violations, creating a fragmented oversight mechanism. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency portals serve as critical tools for accessing government decisions and justifications. By scrutinizing official responses, citizens can verify administrative consistency and uncover discrepancies between stated laws and executive actions. The incident exemplifies how open data initiatives can empower civil society to monitor government adherence to democratic norms and ensure that political mobilizations comply with established legal frameworks.
