Publication of the Quarterly Northern Ireland Outpatient, Inpatient And Day Case, and Diagnostic Waiting Times Statistics - Position on 31 March 2024

This report highlights significant challenges in Northern Ireland’s healthcare system, revealing that waiting times for outpatient, inpatient, and diagnostic services have largely exceeded established targets. The data indicates a persistent backlog, with substantial percentages of patients waiting far beyond recommended periods for initial consultations and treatments. These trends suggest systemic pressures that hinder timely access to care, reflecting broader inefficiencies in service delivery and resource allocation across the region’s health trusts. A critical factor influencing data integrity is the ongoing transition to a new electronic patient record system. The South Eastern Trust was excluded from these figures because it was undergoing a migration to completely digitized records, rendering its data unavailable for this period. This temporary gap underscores the complexities and disruptions inherent in modernizing health infrastructure, demonstrating how technological upgrades can temporarily impact the availability and comparability of critical public health metrics. This article is vital for open data initiatives as it illustrates the importance of context when interpreting health statistics. It emphasizes that data releases are not static snapshots but are affected by administrative processes like system migrations, which can exclude specific regions and compromise longitudinal analysis. For open data practitioners, this case serves as a reminder to clearly document methodological changes and exclusions, ensuring that users understand the limitations and reliability of the information before drawing conclusions about healthcare performance.
