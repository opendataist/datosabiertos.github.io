AI could make FOIA obsolete

The article critiques the archaic and hostile nature of current Freedom of Information Act processes, arguing that they fail to meet modern expectations for instantaneous information access. Instead of relying on tedious, reactive request systems that often obscure data through redactions and fees, the author highlights the potential of new platforms like Citizen Portal. These tools demonstrate how aggregating public meeting videos and creating searchable transcripts can democratize access to government proceedings, making transparency immediate and user-friendly. The core proposal shifts the paradigm from reactive disclosure to proactive transparency. By mandating that agencies publish documents digitally and making them easily searchable, the government can eliminate the bottleneck of formal requests. This approach recognizes that citizens should not need to navigate complex bureaucratic hurdles or hire lawyers merely to understand how their local libraries are funded or how policies are debated. This perspective is vital for open data because it frames transparency as a technological imperative rather than just a legal compliance issue. Embracing AI for record retrieval and encouraging experimental solutions can significantly reduce the time and cost of accessing public records. Ultimately, aligning government operations with modern digital capabilities is essential for rebuilding public trust and ensuring that the people’s records remain truly accessible.
