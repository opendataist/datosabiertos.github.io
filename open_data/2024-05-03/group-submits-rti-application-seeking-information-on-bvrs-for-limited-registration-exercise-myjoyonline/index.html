Group submits RTI application seeking information on BVRs for limited registration exercise - MyJoyOnline

Election Watch Ghana has utilized Right to Information mechanisms to demand the serial numbers and deployment locations of Biometric Verification Register kits for the upcoming limited voter registration exercise. By seeking specific logistical details, the organization emphasizes that true electoral transparency requires public access to granular data regarding the tools used to register new citizens, rather than just broad procedural announcements. This push for open data is critical because it enables independent monitoring and accountability within the democratic process. When civil society groups can access precise information about voting infrastructure, they can effectively verify its usage and prevent potential manipulation or misallocation of resources. Such openness empowers citizens and agents to ensure the integrity of the registration phase, fostering greater trust in the electoral system. The relevance to open data lies in establishing a precedent that electoral logistics should be publicly accessible by default. Treating this information as open data supports the broader goal of building a stronger, more responsive democracy where citizens can actively participate in oversight. Ultimately, this initiative highlights how transparent data practices are fundamental to maintaining accountability and ensuring fair elections.
