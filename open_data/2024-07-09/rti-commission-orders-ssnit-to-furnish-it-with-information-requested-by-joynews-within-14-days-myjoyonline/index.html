RTI Commission orders SSNIT to furnish it with Information requested by JoyNews within 14 Days - MyJoyOnline

The Right to Information Commission mandated the Social Security and National Insurance Trust to release specific data regarding the sale of its hotel stakes after the Trust unjustly denied a request by JoyNews. The Trust had rejected the appeal by claiming no information officer existed, a stance the Commission deemed a direct violation of the 2019 Right to Information Act. This ruling reinforces that public institutions must maintain designated officers to handle transparency requests, ensuring citizens can access government-held information without bureaucratic obstacles. The Commission ordered the release of critical details about the transaction, including bidder lists, evaluation criteria, and financial proposals. By exempting only board meeting minutes from this disclosure, the decision highlights a balance between public accountability and internal deliberative privacy. This distinction is vital for understanding how open data frameworks operate, as it clarifies which categories of institutional records are subject to public scrutiny and which remain protected to preserve effective governance processes. This case is highly relevant to open data because it establishes a legal precedent for enforcing transparency in public sector financial dealings. It demonstrates that legislative tools exist to compel institutions to disclose operational details, thereby preventing opacity in asset management. For advocates of open government, this ruling validates the necessity of robust information access laws to ensure that public funds and assets are managed with full visibility, reinforcing trust through enforced data availability rather than voluntary disclosure.
