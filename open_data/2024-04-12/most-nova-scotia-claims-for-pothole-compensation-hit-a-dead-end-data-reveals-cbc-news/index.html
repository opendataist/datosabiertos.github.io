Most Nova Scotia claims for pothole compensation hit a dead end, data reveals | CBC News

Most Nova Scotia claims for pothole compensation hit a dead end, data reveals | CBC News

Data reveals that the Nova Scotia government overwhelmingly denies compensation claims for pothole damage on provincial roads, with only a small fraction of applications receiving payouts. This systemic rejection leaves the majority of affected motorists to absorb significant repair costs, despite evidence that roads are often in poor condition and hazardous to drive. The current policy prioritizes administrative convenience over public restitution, creating a barrier for citizens seeking redress for vehicle damage caused by inadequate infrastructure maintenance. The core controversy stems from a rigid 14-day repair window, which the government uses to argue that no negligence occurred if the defect was reported recently. Claimants argue this standard is unfair, as it effectively grants the province immunity from liability even when immediate danger exists. This approach forces individuals to bear the financial burden of public infrastructure failures, exacerbating the economic strain on working-class drivers who rely on their vehicles for employment and daily life. This issue is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as FOI requests were essential to expose the vast discrepancy between the volume of claims and the meager number of approved payouts. The lack of transparent, accessible data prevents the public from understanding the true scope of the problem, while political discussions about independent adjudicators remain stalled. Open data is crucial here to hold the government accountable, ensuring that decision-making processes regarding road safety and financial liability are visible and subject to public scrutiny.
