Meta's plan to train its AI on all your old Facebook data is raising eyebrows among privacy advocates

Meta's plan to train its AI on all your old Facebook data is raising eyebrows among privacy advocates

Meta faces intense scrutiny in Europe as an advocacy group demands regulators halt a policy enabling the scraping of historical user data for AI training. This move shifts the burden to users to manually opt out, challenging the EU’s stringent consent standards under the General Data Protection Regulation. The controversy highlights the tension between rapid AI development and fundamental privacy rights, affecting millions of European citizens who now risk having their personal information used without explicit permission. This conflict underscores critical challenges for open data initiatives that rely on ethical transparency and user trust. When corporations claim legitimate interests to bypass consent mechanisms, it undermines the principle of informed data sharing essential for responsible innovation. The legal battles in multiple countries could set a precedent for how AI models access social media data, potentially restricting the scope of publicly available information used for research and development. Ultimately, the outcome may reshape the balance between corporate AI ambitions and individual sovereignty over digital footprints. If regulators enforce stricter opt-in requirements, it could limit the volume of raw data accessible to AI developers, forcing more transparent and compliant data practices. This case serves as a vital reminder that open data ecosystems must respect legal frameworks and user autonomy to maintain societal legitimacy and long-term sustainability.
