Nepal sees major decline in Indian tourist arrivals due to fragile law and order situation

Nepal’s tourism sector experienced a notable contraction in Indian visitor numbers and only slight growth in overall international arrivals during the first ten months of 2025. This trend highlights the significant economic vulnerability of Himalayan nations to sudden geopolitical or social instability, as the primary source market for regional tourism suffered substantial declines despite India remaining the largest contributor to the industry. The sharp downturn was directly triggered by recent civil unrest, which severely damaged Nepal’s reputation as a safe destination. High-profile incidents, including the destruction of major international hotels, caused immediate cancellations and long-term hesitation among prospective travelers. This case demonstrates how fragile political narratives and perceived safety risks can rapidly disrupt established tourism flows, underscoring the critical need for transparent, real-time crisis communication in the travel sector. For open data initiatives, this article illustrates the importance of linking macro-level tourism statistics with contextual event data. By integrating granular arrival records with timestamps of civil unrest, researchers can quantify the economic impact of social instability on regional economies. Such data-driven insights are essential for developing robust risk models and informing policy decisions that prioritize stability and transparency in developing tourism markets.
