El dilema del crecimiento para México

El dilema del crecimiento para México

Mexico’s economy is experiencing a significant slowdown, marked by a contraction in the third quarter of 2025 and persistently weak growth trends. This deceleration stems from a combination of domestic structural weaknesses, such as reduced public investment and declining household purchasing power, alongside external pressures from US trade policies. The resulting uncertainty has stifled business confidence, particularly in the manufacturing sector, while international organizations forecast minimal growth for the coming years. The trade tensions between the US and Mexico have escalated into a protective standoff, with threats of high tariffs on automotive and industrial goods counteracted by Mexican retaliatory measures. This environment undermines the potential benefits of nearshoring, as increased production costs and geopolitical volatility deter foreign investment. Instead of acting as a catalyst for recovery, trade protectionism risks isolating the economy, raising internal prices, and further reducing export competitiveness. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights how economic narratives and policy decisions are often driven by opaque or delayed statistical interpretations. Reliable, transparent, and timely public data is essential for businesses to navigate these volatile conditions and for citizens to demand accountability. Without clear access to accurate economic indicators, stakeholders cannot effectively assess the true impact of trade wars or structural reforms, hindering informed decision-making and democratic oversight of national development strategies.
