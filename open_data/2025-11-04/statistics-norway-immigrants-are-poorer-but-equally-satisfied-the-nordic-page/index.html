Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

This article highlights a significant paradox in immigrant integration within Norway. Despite facing substantially poorer living conditions across key metrics such as employment, housing, and health, immigrants report levels of life satisfaction comparable to the native population. This disconnect suggests that satisfaction is not solely determined by material well-being but is influenced by other social and psychological factors. Crucially, the data reveals that immigrants experience higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness than the general population. While they face systemic disadvantages, they simultaneously demonstrate unusually high trust in major state institutions, including the political system and judiciary. This strong institutional confidence and affiliation with Norway indicates a complex integration dynamic where societal trust remains high despite personal hardships. For open data advocates, this case study underscores the value of granular, disaggregated data. By tracking specific demographics, researchers can identify hidden disparities in mental health and loneliness that aggregate national statistics might obscure. This level of detail is essential for crafting targeted social policies that address the unique challenges immigrant communities face, ensuring that data usage promotes equitable outcomes rather than masking systemic inequalities.
