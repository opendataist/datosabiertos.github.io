Defensa no recoge datos de cuántos militares cambian de sexo porque lo considera discriminatorio

Defensa no recoge datos de cuántos militares cambian de sexo porque lo considera discriminatorio

The article highlights a significant transparency barrier within the Spanish Ministry of Defense regarding the tracking of military personnel who have legally changed their registered sex under the new Trans Law. Despite public interest in understanding the demographic impact of this legislation, the Ministry argues that its personnel system only records binary gender and lacks specific data on the volume of changes. Consequently, they claim that providing the requested statistics would require creating new data categories, which they assert could introduce discriminatory biases and lacks legal grounding, thereby refusing to release the figures. This refusal led to a formal appeal before the Council for Transparency, which ultimately sided with the Ministry. The Council determined that the institution genuinely does not possess the specific data requested, as its administrative systems are designed solely for basic identification rather than tracking legal gender transitions. The decision underscores a critical limitation in how government bodies manage and report sensitive demographic information, suggesting that current administrative frameworks are insufficient for capturing nuanced social changes mandated by recent equality laws. This case is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it illustrates the tension between the public’s right to know and the administrative limitations of existing data infrastructures. It serves as a cautionary example for data governance, showing that simply having a transparency law is not enough if the underlying data architecture does not support the collection of relevant, non-discriminatory metrics. It emphasizes the need for robust, inclusive data standards that can accurately reflect diverse populations without compromising security or legal boundaries, ensuring that open data efforts remain both feasible and ethically sound.
