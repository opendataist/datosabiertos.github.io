Lanzan el “Portal de Datos Abiertos” que permite acceder a información del Ministerio Público

The Bolivian Public Ministry has launched the first open data portal for its justice system, marking a historic shift toward transparency. By providing downloadable and verifiable information on crimes and legal processes, the institution aims to replace secrecy with an “open book” approach. This initiative builds public trust through evidence rather than mere claims, transforming justice from a “black box” into a participatory and accountable framework. The platform prioritizes privacy while empowering universities, media, and civil society to analyze criminal trends and policy effectiveness. This enables stakeholders to propose evidence-based solutions and verify institutional performance. Consequently, citizens can actively question and contribute to judicial outcomes, fostering a democratic relationship between the state and society grounded in verifiable facts and shared responsibility. This development is crucial for open data advocates as it demonstrates how government entities can leverage accessible datasets to enhance civic engagement. It proves that transparency strengthens institutional legitimacy and encourages collaborative problem-solving. By setting a precedent for judicial accountability, the portal offers a replicable model for other sectors seeking to align administrative practices with open government principles and data-driven governance.
