Combatir el crimen, pero con garantías de transparencia

Combatir el crimen, pero con garantías de transparencia

The central conclusion is that while implementing a biometric CURP in Mexico is a necessary step for modernizing identification and addressing the crisis of over 110,000 missing persons, it must be executed under strict transparency and data protection standards. Without rigorous safeguards, this powerful tool risks violating rights rather than saving lives, emphasizing that the urgency of solving disappearances cannot justify the erosion of civil liberties. The most critical implication is the need to limit access to this vast biometric database to specific authorities focused solely on locating and identifying victims, coupled with public and auditable contracting processes to prevent corruption. By learning from international models that balance security with institutional discipline and checks and balances, Mexico can avoid falling into the trap of mass surveillance. Independent oversight mechanisms, such as citizen audits and clear sanctions for abuse, are essential to ensure the system serves justice rather than enabling authoritarian control or illegal spying. This topic is vital to open data because it highlights the tension between centralized data aggregation and individual privacy rights. It underscores the importance of applying open data principles—not just making data available, but ensuring it is used ethically, with accountability and public scrutiny—to prevent technology from becoming a tool for oppression. The debate reflects a broader challenge in digital governance: how to leverage big data for social good while maintaining democratic oversight and protecting citizens from state overreach.
