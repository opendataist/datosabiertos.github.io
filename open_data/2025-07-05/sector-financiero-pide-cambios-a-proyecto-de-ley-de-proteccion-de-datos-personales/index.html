Sector financiero pide cambios a proyecto de Ley de Protección de Datos Personales

Sector financiero pide cambios a proyecto de Ley de Protección de Datos Personales

The legislative debate in Paraguay over the Personal Data Protection Law highlights a critical tension between establishing robust individual privacy rights and maintaining a business-friendly regulatory environment. Financial sector representatives, particularly banks, argue that the proposed legislation risks creating administrative bottlenecks and legal overlaps with existing credit regulations. They warn that excessive bureaucracy and restrictions on accessing public data could hinder credit evaluation processes, potentially discouraging foreign investment by making the country appear less attractive to global capital compared to jurisdictions with lighter regulatory burdens. Conversely, proponents emphasize the urgency of enacting the law to safeguard citizens' rights in an era of increasing cyber threats and digital risks. They argue that establishing clear consent mechanisms and a dedicated oversight framework provides essential guarantees for data subjects. The Ministry of Information and Communication Technology defends the structure of the proposed agency, asserting that it will complement rather than conflict with existing financial regulators by adopting a policy of state non-expansion and focusing solely on overarching data protection standards. This article is highly relevant to the open data community as it illustrates the complex balancing act required when designing data governance frameworks. It underscores the challenge of harmonizing privacy protections with the principles of transparency and interoperability necessary for data-driven economies. Understanding these political and economic trade-offs is crucial for developing open data policies that respect individual rights without stifling innovation or international competitiveness.
