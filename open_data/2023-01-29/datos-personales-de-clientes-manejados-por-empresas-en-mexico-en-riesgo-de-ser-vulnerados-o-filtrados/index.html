Datos personales de clientes manejados por empresas en México, en riesgo de ser vulnerados o filtrados

The article highlights a critical vulnerability in the data management strategies of businesses across Mexico and Latin America, where a lack of specialized personnel dedicated to personal data protection exposes organizations to significant legal and security risks. Instead of assigning this responsibility to experts, companies often delegate it to departments like sales, marketing, or legal, which lack the specific technical expertise required. This fragmentation, combined with deficient cybersecurity tools, creates an environment where confidential customer information is susceptible to breaches, leading to potential fraud and severe reputational damage. Relevance to open data lies in the urgent need for transparent and secure frameworks that balance data utility with individual privacy rights. The text emphasizes that while privacy is central to digital strategies, true accountability requires moving beyond superficial compliance. By adopting advanced technologies like blockchain for data verification and implementing robust classification and encryption tools, organizations can ensure data integrity. This shift is essential for maintaining public trust, as the handling of personal data is increasingly viewed through the lens of fundamental human rights and dignity under international standards like those of the OAS. Ultimately, the core conclusion is that data privacy is no longer just a technical necessity but a strict legal and ethical obligation. Companies must actively adapt to local regulations and international principles by providing users with genuine control over their information. Implementing transparent privacy notices, strengthening access controls, and ensuring technological solvency are vital steps. Without these measures, businesses remain vulnerable to sanctions and security failures, undermining the foundation of trust necessary for healthy digital ecosystems.
