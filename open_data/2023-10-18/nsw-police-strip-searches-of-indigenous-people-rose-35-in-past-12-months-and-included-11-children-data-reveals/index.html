NSW police strip-searches of Indigenous people rose 35% in past 12 months and included 11 children, data reveals

New South Wales police data reveals a disturbing surge in the strip-searching of Indigenous Australians, particularly children, highlighting severe systemic disparities. First Nations people are disproportionately targeted, comprising a tiny fraction of the population but a significant portion of search records. This trend includes a troubling increase in searches involving minors, raising urgent concerns about the abuse of power and the infliction of long-term trauma on vulnerable youth. The practice is widely condemned as invasive and harmful, often lacking proper justification or adherence to protective guidelines. An independent audit found that compliance with privacy and dignity standards is alarmingly low, with poor record-keeping further obscuring accountability. These findings underscore the urgent need for immediate policy reforms to protect children and ensure law enforcement actions are both lawful and respectful of human rights. This situation is critical to open data because it demonstrates how transparency mechanisms, like freedom of information laws, are essential for exposing institutional failures. Without accessible, granular data, systemic discrimination against marginalized communities would remain hidden. Making such statistics public not only informs advocacy but also compels governmental accountability, ensuring that policies affecting civil liberties are scrutinized and reformed through evidence-based public discourse.
