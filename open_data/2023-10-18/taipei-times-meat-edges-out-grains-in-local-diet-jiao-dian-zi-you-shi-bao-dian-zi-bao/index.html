《TAIPEI TIMES》Meat edges out grains in local diet - 焦點 - 自由時報電子報

Taiwanese dietary patterns have shifted fundamentally, with meat consumption surpassing grain intake for the first time since records began. This transition highlights a move toward higher animal protein sources, particularly chicken, which has become the most consumed meat, while traditional staples like rice continue to decline. The rise in poultry and beef consumption reflects evolving consumer preferences, driven by health consciousness and a gradual abandonment of cultural taboos against beef. Increased availability and marketing of diverse meat options, alongside the promotion of alternative staples like yams in modern food services, have diversified the local food landscape significantly. This article is relevant to open data as it illustrates how aggregated statistical reports can reveal profound societal and cultural shifts. By analyzing open government data on consumption habits, researchers can understand broader trends in public health, agricultural policy impact, and changing lifestyle behaviors, enabling more informed decisions regarding food security and nutritional guidelines.
