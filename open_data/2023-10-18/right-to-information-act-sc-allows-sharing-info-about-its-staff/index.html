Right to Information Act: SC allows sharing info about its staff

The Supreme Court of Pakistan has ruled that citizens have the right to access information regarding the court’s staff, reinforcing that institutions must justify any refusal to disclose data rather than automatically denying it. This decision establishes that transparency serves as a mechanism for introspection and self-accountability, directly benefiting public institutions by promoting openness. By ordering the disclosure of specific staff details, the court affirmed that such information is of public importance and does not contradict public interest, thereby setting a significant precedent for administrative transparency within the judiciary. The judgment clarifies that while specific legislation may not explicitly apply to the apex court, the constitutional right to information under Article 19A remains fully operative. The court emphasized that excluding the Supreme Court from these provisions would effectively abridge a fundamental right guaranteed to citizens. This interpretation aligns with global democratic standards where access to information is viewed as essential for accountability and safeguarding against corruption, ensuring that even the highest judicial body remains answerable to the public it serves. This ruling is critically relevant to open_data advocates as it validates the principle that public bodies, including independent judiciaries, must operate with sufficient openness to allow for public scrutiny. It underscores that data regarding personnel and resource utilization is not inherently private but belongs to the public domain when it concerns matters of collective importance. Consequently, this decision strengthens the legal framework for transparency, encouraging broader access to institutional data and reinforcing the idea that openness is a prerequisite for trust in democratic systems.
