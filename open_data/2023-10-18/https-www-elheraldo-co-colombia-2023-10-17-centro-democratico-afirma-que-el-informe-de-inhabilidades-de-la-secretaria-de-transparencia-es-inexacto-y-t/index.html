The Centro Democrático party strongly rejects a recent transparency report alleging that 553 of its candidates are ineligible for regional elections, labeling the data as biased and inaccurate. The party argues that the Transparency Secretariat exceeded its legal authority, asserting that only electoral authorities, the Attorney General’s office, or judges can officially determine candidacy disqualifications. This disagreement highlights a critical tension regarding who holds the legitimate power to validate political eligibility amidst the open data landscape. Central to the dispute is the verification of specific candidates’ contracts with the state. The party maintains that its internal rigorous vetting processes, cross-checked with public procurement records and legal certificates, prove that the cited individuals are eligible. By challenging the specific data points used to declare ineligibility, the party underscores the importance of data accuracy and the potential for public confusion when transparency entities publish unverified or contested information just before elections. This conflict is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how public sector information, intended to foster accountability, can become weaponized in political strategies. The incident reveals the fragility of open data ecosystems when data quality is disputed, prompting a need for clearer standards in how transparency data is collected, verified, and disseminated to prevent the manipulation of electoral processes through informational ambiguity.
