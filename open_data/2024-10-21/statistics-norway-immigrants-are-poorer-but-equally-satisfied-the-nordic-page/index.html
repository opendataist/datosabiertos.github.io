Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significant disadvantages in employment, housing, and income, yet report life satisfaction levels equal to the native population. This paradox highlights a complex integration dynamic where material hardship does not necessarily correlate with dissatisfaction, suggesting that cultural adaptation or community support may buffer socioeconomic struggles. Mental health disparities are pronounced, with immigrants experiencing higher rates of depression, anxiety, and loneliness compared to the general public. Despite these personal challenges, they demonstrate unusually high trust in national institutions like the judiciary and police. This strong institutional confidence indicates a resilient civic engagement, contrasting sharply with their lower interpersonal trust and emotional well-being. This data is crucial for open_data initiatives focused on social equity. It reveals that standard economic metrics alone fail to capture the full picture of immigrant integration. Comprehensive, accessible datasets are needed to identify specific vulnerability groups and design targeted interventions that address mental health and loneliness without undermining the strong civic trust immigrants place in public systems.
