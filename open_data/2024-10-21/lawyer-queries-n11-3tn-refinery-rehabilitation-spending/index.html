Lawyer queries N11.3tn refinery rehabilitation spending

Lawyer queries N11.3tn refinery rehabilitation spending

A Lagos-based lawyer has utilized Freedom of Information legislation to demand transparency from the Nigerian National Petroleum Company regarding the stalled state of government-owned refineries. The request challenges official figures on trillions of naira spent on rehabilitation since 2010, questioning why facilities remain non-functional despite massive financial investments and repeated political assurances of imminent completion. This legal move highlights the critical role of FOI laws in holding state-owned enterprises accountable for public resource management. The inquiry extends to specific financial discrepancies, including a billion-dollar loan earmarked for the Port Harcourt refinery and alleged failures to remit taxes or subsidies. By contrasting these expenditures with the profitability of private refineries and recent fuel price hikes, the lawyer underscores systemic inefficiencies and potential mismanagement. This approach demonstrates how citizens can leverage legal frameworks to investigate economic policies and demand justification for significant fiscal decisions that directly impact the populace. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates as it illustrates the practical application of information access rights to expose governance gaps. It reinforces the necessity of transparent, accessible data regarding public infrastructure spending to combat misinformation and ensure fiscal accountability. Such initiatives empower civil society to verify official narratives, fostering a culture where open data serves as a tool for democratic oversight and ethical governance in resource-rich nations.
