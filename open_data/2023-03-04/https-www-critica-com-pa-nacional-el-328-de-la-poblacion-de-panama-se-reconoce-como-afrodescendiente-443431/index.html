The preliminary data from Panama’s national census reveal significant demographic shifts, highlighting that nearly one-third of the population identifies as Afro-descendant and 14.4% as Indigenous. This detailed breakdown underscores the country’s multicultural reality, moving beyond simple headcounts to capture the complex social fabric that defines its communities. The successful completion of the door-to-door enumeration phase, reaching approximately 90% of the estimated population, demonstrates a robust data collection infrastructure. Despite previous pandemic-related delays, the high coverage rate of both people and housing provides a reliable foundation for future policy decisions and resource allocation. This article is relevant to open data because it exemplifies how transparent, accessible census information empowers researchers and citizens. By publishing preliminary figures early, the national statistics institute fosters public trust and enables informed analysis of social equity, ensuring that marginalized groups are accurately represented in national datasets and future planning.
