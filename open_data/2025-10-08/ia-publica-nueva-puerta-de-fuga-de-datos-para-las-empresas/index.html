IA Pública, nueva puerta de fuga de datos para las empresas

IA Pública, nueva puerta de fuga de datos para las empresas

The widespread adoption of generative artificial intelligence in Latin America presents a significant paradox: while it drives productivity, it simultaneously exposes organizations to severe cyber risks. The core issue lies in employees voluntarily inputting sensitive internal data, such as critical databases and confidential documents, into public AI platforms. This practice inadvertently feeds proprietary information into systems that do not guarantee privacy or security, creating a direct pathway for data leakage and exploitation by cybercriminals. This vulnerability is exacerbated because generative AI platforms often index user interactions, potentially exposing sensitive corporate queries alongside personal data. Consequently, companies face a dual challenge of defending against traditional malicious threats while managing the human factor, where the desire for efficiency overrides security protocols. The lack of human oversight in these interactions means that confidential institutional knowledge can be leaked without immediate detection, significantly increasing the exposure of enterprises to intellectual property theft and regulatory breaches. This situation is highly relevant to the open data movement because it highlights the critical boundary between transparency and security. As open data principles encourage the sharing of information for societal benefit, this case demonstrates the dangers of mixing public, unverified AI tools with protected institutional data. It underscores the necessity for robust governance frameworks that distinguish between open datasets suitable for public AI training and restricted information requiring strict confidentiality, ensuring that the drive for open innovation does not compromise individual privacy or organizational integrity.
