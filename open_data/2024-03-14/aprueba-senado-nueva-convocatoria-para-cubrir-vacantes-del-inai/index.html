Aprueba Senado nueva convocatoria para cubrir vacantes del INAI

The arrest of a former police commissioner in Juchitán, accused of shielding criminal groups, highlights the deep entanglement between local authorities and organized crime. This case underscores the critical need for transparency in law enforcement records to expose corruption and ensure accountability within public security institutions. Such cases reveal how opaque municipal structures facilitate illicit activities, hindering effective justice. Open data initiatives are vital in this context, as they enable civil society to monitor official appointments, budgets, and enforcement outcomes, making it more difficult for corrupt networks to operate without scrutiny. Ultimately, this arrest illustrates that sustainable security reform requires more than operational raids. It demands rigorous, accessible public data that empowers citizens to monitor state actions, ensuring that legal processes serve the public interest rather than shield powerful actors from justice.
