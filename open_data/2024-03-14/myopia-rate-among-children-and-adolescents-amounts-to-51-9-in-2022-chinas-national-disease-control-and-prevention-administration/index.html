Myopia rate among children and adolescents amounts to 51.9% in 2022: China’s National Disease Control and Prevention Administration

China’s recent data reveals a promising decline in the overall myopia rate among children and adolescents, signaling the effectiveness of ongoing national prevention and control initiatives. This positive trend is particularly notable in the reduced proportion of high myopia cases, suggesting that comprehensive health strategies are successfully mitigating severe vision issues in the youth demographic. The government attributes this improvement to a robust framework of mandatory eye monitoring, increased physical activity requirements, and stricter regulations on school recess. By ensuring students have adequate outdoor time and regular vision screenings, authorities aim to address the root causes of visual fatigue. These policy-driven interventions highlight a systemic shift toward prioritizing student well-being through structural changes in daily school routines and enhanced public health education. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the value of large-scale, standardized health monitoring systems. The transparency of national statistics allows for public accountability and evidence-based policy refinement. Furthermore, it underscores how accessible, aggregated datasets can reveal societal health trends, enabling researchers and policymakers to track the impact of interventions over time and ensure that public health goals are being met through verifiable metrics.
