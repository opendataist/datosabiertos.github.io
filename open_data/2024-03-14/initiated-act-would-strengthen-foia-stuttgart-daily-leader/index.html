Initiated act would strengthen FOIA | Stuttgart Daily Leader

Arkansas is facing challenges in maintaining its robust freedom of information laws, prompting a bipartisan coalition to propose two distinct measures for the upcoming election. The first is a constitutional amendment designed to entrench transparency protections by requiring a supermajority legislative vote and public ratification for any future changes. This structural safeguard aims to prevent elected officials from easily rolling back existing rights, ensuring that access to government information remains resilient against political shifts. The second proposal is an initiated act that directly amends current statutes to close significant loopholes in public access. Key provisions include stricter deadlines for record release, broader definitions of public meetings that capture digital communications, and increased penalties for bad-faith denials of information. The measure also establishes a new Transparency Commission with enforcement powers to help citizens resolve disputes and hold officials accountable for non-compliance. This initiative is highly relevant to open data because it seeks to modernize and harden the legal framework governing public records. By reducing secrecy in school boards and government cybersecurity discussions, and by creating an independent body to enforce compliance, these proposals aim to ensure that data remains accessible and usable for citizens and media. Ultimately, strengthening these laws supports the core open data principle that government information should be transparent, available, and difficult to restrict arbitrarily.
