El precio del alquiler crece un 7,3% en febrero: ¿dónde sube y dónde baja?

Recent data reveals a critical surge in Spanish rental prices, marking a historic high where average costs exceed 12 euros per square meter for the first time in 17 years. This alarming trend, driven by an interannual increase of over seven percent, indicates a market heating up dangerously due to a significant imbalance between supply and demand. The situation is particularly acute in major urban centers like Madrid and Barcelona, which lead the country in cost, signaling a broader crisis of affordability that affects nearly all regions. Regional analysis shows that price hikes are ubiquitous, with every autonomous community experiencing year-over-year increases and several surpassing double-digit growth rates. While metropolitan areas and tourist hubs bear the brunt of the highest absolute costs, even traditionally lower-cost interior provinces are witnessing dramatic percentage jumps. This widespread escalation suggests that the housing shortage is not confined to major cities but is a structural issue permeating the entire national real estate landscape, pushing rental expenses beyond sustainable levels for many households. This report is highly relevant to open_data because it highlights the vital role of transparent, granular real estate metrics in diagnosing socioeconomic challenges. By making detailed rental indices and regional breakdowns publicly available, such data empowers researchers, policymakers, and citizens to understand housing market dynamics and advocate for effective interventions. Access to this information is essential for holding stakeholders accountable and developing evidence-based strategies to address the growing affordability crisis and correct market imbalances.
