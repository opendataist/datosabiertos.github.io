INAI afirma que el acceso a información no debe ser politizado | Noticias de México | El Imparcial

The article highlights a critical threat to fundamental rights in Mexico, where attacks on the National Institute for Transparency are framed as an attempt to eradicate the public's right to know and personal privacy. This political pressure undermines institutional autonomy, forcing judicial intervention and exposing citizens to risks like identity theft and digital violence, particularly affecting vulnerable groups such as women. This issue is vital to open data because transparency and privacy are the twin pillars of a healthy digital ecosystem. Without robust, independent guardianship, data protection laws become ineffective, eroding trust in public institutions. Open data initiatives cannot thrive if citizens fear their information will be exploited or leaked without consequence, making institutional independence a prerequisite for reliable data governance. Furthermore, the erosion of these rights stifles civic participation and accountability. When the mechanisms ensuring access to information are weakened, the flow of public data necessary for informed decision-making is disrupted. Protecting these frameworks ensures that open data remains a tool for empowerment rather than a vector for surveillance, maintaining the integrity of democratic processes and social safety in an increasingly digital world.
