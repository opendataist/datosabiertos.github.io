On Sunshine Week, Michigan lawmakers consider FOIA reform

Michigan lags significantly in government transparency, prompting the absence of Sunshine Awards this year due to persistent secrecy among state leaders. This lack of openness highlights a critical failure to empower citizens and align with constitutional governance principles, as highlighted by press associations monitoring state conduct. Two proposed bills aim to extend existing public records laws to include the governor and legislators, alongside appointing dedicated coordinators for each chamber. These measures seek to dismantle current barriers to information, ensuring that executive and legislative branches operate under the same disclosure standards as local governments, thereby enhancing accountability. This issue is vital for open data advocates because current exemptions allow officials to withhold personal notes from public meetings, creating significant data gaps. Expanding transparency laws would not only improve public trust but also ensure that valuable legislative records become accessible, supporting a more informed citizenry and robust democratic engagement through unrestricted data availability.
