PDP Reacts To Alleged NYSC Certificate Forgery Against Enugu Governorship Candidate, Mbah

An advocacy group has utilized the Freedom of Information Act to challenge the eligibility of a gubernatorial candidate, alleging his National Youth Service Corps certificate submitted to the election commission was forged. This incident underscores the critical intersection between transparency mechanisms and electoral integrity, highlighting how FOI requests can serve as tools for political accountability while simultaneously raising questions about the verification of personal credentials in democratic processes. The candidate’s party vehemently rejects these claims, dismissing them as baseless smears designed to obscure their poor election prospects. They assert the candidate completed his mandatory service and argue that the document in question bears clear signs of fabrication, including informal language and lack of official signatures. This defensive stance illustrates the common political tactic of attributing such challenges to desperate opposition efforts rather than addressing the substantive concerns raised by civil society actors. This conflict is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates the practical power and limitations of information transparency in governance. It reveals how access to public records can trigger legal and political disputes, emphasizing the need for robust, accessible, and verifiable digital record-keeping systems. Furthermore, it highlights the importance of verifying data sources to prevent misinformation, showing that while open data empowers citizens, it also requires careful handling to ensure the authenticity of information used in sensitive political contexts.
