The Mexican Transparency Authority ordered the Mexican Social Security Institute (IMSS) to disclose the names of Cuban doctors, ensuring the right to public information without compromising their privacy through data dissociation. This resolution underscores the importance of balancing state transparency with the protection of individual rights, a fundamental principle in open data frameworks that seek to maximize access to information without undermining human dignity. The case reveals the political and social controversy surrounding international health agreements, which have been criticized for marginalizing local personnel and affecting Cuba’s healthcare capacity. These disputes highlight how bilateral agreements can generate tensions in labor relations and human rights, demonstrating that the publication of data related to public contracts must carefully consider the ethical and social implications for the professionals involved. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the real-world challenge of applying transparency in sensitive contexts. It shows that access to information is not merely a technical issue, but a complex process that requires ethical safeguards to prevent harm, reinforcing the need to design data policies that respect both public accountability and the comprehensive protection of human privacy.
