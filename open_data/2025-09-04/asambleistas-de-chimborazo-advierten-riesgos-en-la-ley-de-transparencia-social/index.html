Asambleístas de Chimborazo advierten riesgos en la Ley de Transparencia Social

Asambleístas de Chimborazo advierten riesgos en la Ley de Transparencia Social

The National Assembly’s approval of the Social Transparency Law mandates the official registration of foundations and collective groups, a move critics argue threatens freedom of association. Legislators from Chimborazo warn that this regulation risks politicizing control and criminalizing essential community organizations that currently fill vital service gaps left by the state. The legislation excludes specific rural entities but imposes broad restrictions on groups working in health, education, and culture. Critics emphasize that legitimate social actors supporting citizens should not be conflated with entities involved in irregular funding, advocating for stronger institutional intelligence rather than limiting civil society participation. This article is relevant to open data as it highlights the tension between transparency initiatives and the protection of civil liberties. It underscores the importance of designing data registries that ensure accountability for financial flows without infringing on the rights of grassroots organizations or compromising the openness required for a healthy democratic society.
