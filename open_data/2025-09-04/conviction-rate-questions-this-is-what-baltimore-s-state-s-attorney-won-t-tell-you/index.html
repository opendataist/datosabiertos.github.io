CONVICTION RATE QUESTIONS | This is what Baltimore's State's Attorney won't tell you

CONVICTION RATE QUESTIONS | This is what Baltimore's State's Attorney won't tell you

The article examines the controversy surrounding the Baltimore State’s Attorney’s Office, which highlights its recent criminal convictions while facing scrutiny over the transparency of its data. By showcasing a high volume of felony convictions, the office aims to demonstrate a tough stance on violence. However, this selective reporting has raised doubts among experts about whether the metrics accurately reflect true accountability for violent criminals. Experts argue that the published statistics lack necessary context, specifically omitting crucial details about dismissed cases and plea bargains. Without data on how many charges were dropped or reduced to lesser offenses, it is impossible to fully evaluate the effectiveness of prosecution strategies. This lack of comprehensive information makes it difficult to determine if serious offenders are facing meaningful consequences or if they are escaping full accountability through reduced charges. This situation is particularly relevant to open data advocates because it illustrates the dangers of incomplete data release. When government entities choose which metrics to publish, they risk obscuring the full picture of judicial performance. True transparency requires the public availability of all relevant statistics, including dismissals and plea deals, to allow for independent verification and a honest assessment of justice system outcomes.
