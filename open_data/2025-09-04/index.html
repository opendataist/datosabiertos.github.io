Why Open Data Protects Our Children’s Future

The Data That Shapes Our Future: Why Open, Transparent, and Accessible Information Matters for Our Children

We teach our children to believe in fairness and honesty. We want them to think the world works correctly. But today, the truth is often hidden. It is locked behind paywalls or kept secret by computer programs. For those of us who care about our communities, open data is very important. It helps us tell the difference between a world where power hides in the dark and a world where people can see what leaders are doing.

The world teaches us a clear lesson: free and open data makes life better. When data is hidden or changed, people lose trust in the systems that run our lives. This is important for everyone who wants to understand how things work. Open data has both good points and bad points.

Open data gives people proof they can check. A study by the World Bank shows that open government data helps public services work better. It also stops corruption. This is because citizens can watch how the government spends money. This honesty builds trust in the economy. For example, Australia shared its national accounts. This let experts check the GDP growth accurately. People could then have real debates about money policies. Users benefit from this clarity. They can discuss society’s well-being instead of listening to political tricks.

However, open data also brings legal risks. An investigation found that big AI companies used millions of songs without permission. This is copyright theft. People who use this data face legal trouble. It is confusing to know what data is public versus what data is legal to use. We need to rethink copyright laws for AI. We must use licensed datasets to respect creators. If you want to know the legal rules, our guide on [legal] What licence open data carries, and what you may do with it (/guides/open-data-licences/) explains how to handle these issues.

Closed computer models slow down progress. Big firms in the US use closed systems. This stops new ideas and fairness. In contrast, open systems in China help science move forward. Users benefit from open data because it stops a few groups from controlling society’s tools. Open data builds trust and new ideas. The East African Open Data Conference showed how open data helps reach global goals. These meetings create teams for fair development.

But hiding parts of the truth hurts accountability. In Baltimore, the State’s Attorney’s Office only shared data about high felony convictions. They did this to look tough on crime. Experts say this is misleading. It hides how well the courts actually work. By leaving out dismissed cases, the office stops people from checking if justice is fair. Users cannot see if serious offenders face real consequences without the full picture. This incomplete info makes it impossible to verify fairness.

Money can also block access. New rules in the US might make it harder to get government info. These changes limit who can see records. When money gets in the way, transparency fails. Journalists and citizens lose access to important files. To learn how to get this info, our guide on [access] How to file a FOIA request — and what to do when it is denied (/guides/how-to-file-a-foia-request/) gives you steps to secure the records you need.

Job numbers show another side of open data. In the US, job openings dropped to 2020 levels. This suggests a frozen job market. But hiring rates are still stable. High-frequency labor stats help experts track the economy. Reliable public access to these details ensures economic insights are true. To help you understand such data, our guide on [analysis] How to read a statistic before you quote it (/guides/read-a-statistic/) teaches you how to find the truth in numbers.

Users must think carefully about these factors. Open data provides the tools for accountability and smart decisions. It lets citizens see the reality behind the numbers. But it also requires users to understand legal rules and spot biased reporting. By demanding true data, users can ensure technology helps real life. We must support open principles in AI. We must fight for rules that let the public get government records. When data is shared, it becomes a key tool for fair progress.
