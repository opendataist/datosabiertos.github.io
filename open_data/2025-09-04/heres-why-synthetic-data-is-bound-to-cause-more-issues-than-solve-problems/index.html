Here’s why synthetic data is bound to cause more issues than solve problems

Here’s why synthetic data is bound to cause more issues than solve problems

The article argues that while synthetic data offers speed and scalability for training AI, it is fundamentally limited because it reflects existing assumptions rather than complex, unpredictable reality. In critical sectors like infrastructure and manufacturing, this disconnect creates blind spots, leading to systems that perform well in controlled simulations but fail when deployed in real-world environments. The author contends that relying on artificial data undermines the trust and reliability required for autonomous decision-making in high-stakes operational contexts. Instead of prioritizing synthetic generation, the piece advocates for a "reality-first" approach grounded in actual field data and spatial intelligence. By using sensors and digital twins that capture live environmental nuances, AI systems can learn from genuine anomalies and evolving conditions rather than theoretical models. This shift ensures that AI adapts to the true complexity of physical spaces, moving beyond prediction engines to responsive tools that continuously learn from lived experience and immediate context. This focus on real-world data is crucial for open data initiatives because it emphasizes traceability, measurability, and transparency. Unlike synthetic datasets, which often obscure their origins and assumptions, real-world data provides clear audit trails necessary for regulatory compliance and public trust. As AI becomes more embedded in society, open, verifiable data sources become the ethical foundation for credibility, ensuring that intelligent systems are accountable and capable of reflecting the actual world they serve.
