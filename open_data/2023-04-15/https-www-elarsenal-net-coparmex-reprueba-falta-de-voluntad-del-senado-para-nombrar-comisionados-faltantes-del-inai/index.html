The article highlights a critical paralysis within Mexico’s National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI), caused by the Senate’s failure to appoint missing commissioners. This inoperability severely compromises the institution's ability to function, directly hindering its constitutional mandate to protect citizens' rights to public information and data privacy. The lack of legal quorum prevents the institute from resolving numerous appeals, thereby denying justice to thousands of individuals and undermining democratic accountability. This situation is particularly alarming as it represents a blatant disregard for judicial resolutions ordering these appointments, despite repeated warnings and potential sanctions. The delay is attributed to political maneuvering rather than administrative necessity, suggesting an intent to weaken autonomous institutions. By withholding these essential appointments, legislative bodies are effectively blocking the operational capacity of a key safeguard against corruption and a guardian of human rights. The relevance to open data is profound, as the INAI serves as the primary guarantor for data protection and transparency in the public sector. Its paralysis creates a vacuum where personal data rights and information access are left unprotected, weakening the framework necessary for accountable governance. Restoring the institute is essential not only for resolving immediate legal grievances but also for maintaining the structural integrity of Mexico’s open data ecosystem, ensuring that citizens can effectively exercise their rights in a digital democracy.
