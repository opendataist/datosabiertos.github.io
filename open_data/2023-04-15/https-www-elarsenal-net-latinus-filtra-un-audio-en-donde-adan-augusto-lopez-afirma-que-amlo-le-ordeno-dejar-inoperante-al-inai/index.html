The article reveals that Mexico’s president ordered the Secretary of the Interior to intentionally render the National Institute of Transparency inoperable. By vetoing new commissioners, the administration aimed to create a political impasse, avoiding the appointment of individuals who might oppose their agenda. This strategy highlights a deliberate effort to weaken independent oversight mechanisms through executive obstruction rather than legal reform. This situation is highly relevant to open data because transparency institutes are the guardians of public information rights. When these bodies are paralyzed, citizens lose the ability to request and receive government data effectively. The erosion of such institutions directly threatens the foundational principles of open government, as it removes the structural safeguards necessary for accountability and public scrutiny of state actions. Ultimately, this case demonstrates how political interests can compromise democratic transparency structures. The deliberate disabling of an open data authority undermines public trust and restricts access to essential civic information. It serves as a critical warning about the vulnerability of open data ecosystems to executive interference, emphasizing the need to protect these institutions from partisan manipulation to ensure genuine governmental transparency.
