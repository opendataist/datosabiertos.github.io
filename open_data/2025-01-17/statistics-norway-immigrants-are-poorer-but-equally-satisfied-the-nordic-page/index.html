Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly disadvantaged living conditions compared to the native population, including lower incomes, poorer housing, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Despite these material and psychological challenges, this group demonstrates a paradoxical resilience, reporting life satisfaction levels equal to or higher than the general public. This suggests that well-being metrics cannot be solely predicted by economic or social hardships, highlighting the complexity of measuring immigrant integration beyond simple statistics. A critical finding is the unusually high confidence immigrants place in national institutions such as the political system, judiciary, and police, which exceeds the trust levels seen in the broader population. While interpersonal trust remains lower among immigrants, their strong institutional affiliation indicates a unique relationship with the state that fosters social cohesion. This dichotomy between personal struggle and institutional loyalty offers valuable insights into how marginalized groups navigate and stabilize their place within societal structures. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of granular, multidimensional datasets that capture not just economic indicators but also subjective well-being and institutional trust. Open data initiatives must prioritize accessible, comprehensive surveys to reveal these nuanced disparities and prevent simplistic narratives about immigrant integration. By making such detailed social metrics available, policymakers and researchers can better design interventions that address the specific psychological and structural barriers immigrants face, ensuring data-driven solutions are holistic and effective.
