The Sligo-Leitrim division experienced a significant rise in violent crimes, including sex offenses, assaults, and robberies, alongside increases in property damage and drunk driving. Conversely, thefts, fraud, and drug offenses declined during the same period, highlighting a complex shift in local criminal activity patterns that warrants careful analysis by public safety officials. Crucially, the Central Statistics Office resumed publishing these figures only after conducting a comprehensive quality review of the Garda recording system, PULSE. This pause addressed previous irregularities where a substantial percentage of reported crimes were either unrecorded, incorrectly classified, or mislabeled as non-crimes. The resumption signals restored confidence in the administrative data’s integrity and accuracy following rigorous internal audits. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the critical importance of data governance and transparency in public sector statistics. Without verified data quality, open datasets can mislead policymakers and the public. The scenario illustrates how technical audits and systematic corrections are essential prerequisites for releasing reliable, trustworthy open data that can be safely used for research and civic accountability.
