MC propone regular la Estrategia Nacional de Seguridad; plantea consultas y audiencias públicas | El Universal

MC propone regular la Estrategia Nacional de Seguridad; plantea consultas y audiencias públicas | El Universal

A lack of regulatory clarity in Mexico’s National Public Security Strategy creates uncertainty and hinders effective implementation. Lawmakers argue that current constitutional provisions are too vague, leading to arbitrary policies rather than evidence-based planning. This legislative gap prevents the integration of local institutions and academic perspectives, resulting in a strategy that lacks diagnostic rigor and measurable goals. The proposed initiative seeks to establish clear criteria for designing, implementing, and evaluating the strategy. It emphasizes the need for objective indicators, defined timelines, and collaborative processes involving the public, social, and private sectors. By mandating transparent consultations and rigorous data analysis, the reform aims to replace transient political moves with a long-term, federalist approach grounded in empirical evidence. This development is crucial for open data because it highlights the urgent need for standardized, accessible metrics in public safety governance. The push for objective indicators and transparent evaluation mechanisms demonstrates how data availability and quality are foundational to effective policy-making. Without these open, verifiable standards, security strategies remain opaque and difficult to assess or improve systematically.
