Alessandra Rojo de la Vega alista censo de ambulantes; presenta plan de seguridad

Alessandra Rojo de la Vega alista censo de ambulantes; presenta plan de seguridad

The article highlights a decisive administrative shift in Cuauhtémoc toward rigorous urban data collection and public safety, aiming to resolve long-standing governance gaps. By launching comprehensive censuses for both abandoned properties and street vendors, the local government seeks to establish an accurate, granular understanding of the neighborhood’s physical and economic landscape. This move from vague estimates to precise, street-by-street mapping represents a critical step in applying open data principles to solve complex urban issues, ensuring that policy decisions are grounded in verified reality rather than speculation. A central theme is the tension between legal constraints and administrative order, specifically regarding the thousands of vendors protected by court injunctions. The administration acknowledges that while it cannot forcibly remove every vendor, it is committed to cataloging all individuals operating in public spaces, regardless of their legal status. This emphasis on thorough documentation implies that a reliable, transparent database is essential for enforcing regulations fairly and for understanding the true scope of informal commerce, which directly impacts urban planning and social cohesion. Furthermore, the initiative links spatial data with security strategies by identifying neglected buildings as hubs for criminal activity. The government plans to monitor these locations closely to dismantle networks involved in extortion and trafficking, reinforcing the idea that public safety relies on knowing exactly where resources are needed. This approach underscores the relevance of open data by demonstrating how accessible, accurate information empowers authorities to coordinate effectively, restore public trust, and create a safer, more orderly urban environment for all residents.
