It’s time Bangladeshi youth used their right to information

It’s time Bangladeshi youth used their right to information

The article highlights a critical gap in Bangladesh’s post-interim government landscape, where citizens, particularly the youth driving recent political change, remain largely unaware of their legal right to access government information. Despite the RTI Act being the primary mechanism for ensuring transparency and accountability, its potential is underutilized. The authors argue that revitalizing this tool is essential for the new administration to meet public demands for reform and to prevent a return to previous governance issues. By recounting several successful past cases, the text demonstrates the transformative power of the RTI Act in exposing corruption, enforcing labor rights, and recovering stolen public funds. These examples illustrate how individual inquiries can lead to significant legal and administrative actions, such as the demolition of illegally constructed buildings or the correction of fraudulent loan schemes. The narrative emphasizes that the law is not merely theoretical but a practical instrument capable of holding powerful entities accountable and rectifying systemic injustices. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of active public engagement with government-held information. It suggests that while legal frameworks for data access exist, their impact depends on citizen awareness and the functional capacity of oversight bodies. Filling vacant commissioner posts and promoting literacy around the RTI Act are crucial steps to ensure that open data principles translate into tangible democratic accountability and effective governance.
