Reino Unido creará una biblioteca pública de datos para entrenar modelos de IA

Reino Unido creará una biblioteca pública de datos para entrenar modelos de IA

The British government proposes establishing a public national data library to facilitate the training of artificial intelligence models by businesses and researchers. This initiative aims to boost technological innovation and the growth of national startups by providing access to high-impact public datasets. The plan explicitly allows for the commercial use of these resources, marking a strategic shift toward monetizing public knowledge as an economic driver. A central and controversial aspect is the inclusion of anonymized health data from the National Health Service. Authorities argue that, since these records are de-identified, they are not strictly subject to certain privacy regulations, thereby streamlining their use for developing predictive medical tools. The goal is to improve clinical care through disease prevention and the optimization of hospital resources, presenting the availability of this sensitive information as a unique opportunity for significant healthcare advances. However, this measure raises critical risks for individual data protection and ethics in the handling of public information. Experts warn about the ease of re-identifying patients through data-matching techniques, even when the information appears anonymous. This tension between the public interest in technological progress and the security of citizens’ privacy underscores the urgent need for robust regulatory frameworks in the open data sector. Transparency and user consent are essential to maintaining social trust in the face of the commercialization of sensitive data.
