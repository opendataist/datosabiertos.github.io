Croydon accused of 'failing residents' over 200 Freedom of Information Act breaches

Croydon Council has been issued an enforcement notice by the Information Commissioner’s Office for failing to meet its legal obligations under Freedom of Information legislation. The authority demonstrated systemic non-compliance by allowing a significant backlog of public queries to accumulate, often missing statutory response deadlines. This failure to provide timely answers undermines the transparency expected of local government bodies. This situation highlights the critical importance of open data frameworks in ensuring public accountability. When authorities fail to process information requests promptly, they effectively restrict citizen access to knowledge about council actions. Such opacity erodes trust and prevents the community from holding decision-makers accountable, illustrating why robust mechanisms are necessary to enforce data accessibility and timely disclosure. Consequently, the council is compelled to implement a comprehensive action plan to address the root causes of these delays. They must resolve the outstanding backlog within six months and demonstrate sustained improvement in their processing capabilities. Without strict adherence to these transparency standards, the authority risks further legal escalation, emphasizing that open data compliance is a mandatory, not optional, aspect of public service delivery.
