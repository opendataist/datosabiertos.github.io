De esta manera, Google entrena su inteligencia artificial Bard para competir contra ChatGPT

Google has updated its privacy policy to allow the use of public data in training its artificial intelligence models and to personalize ads on its platforms. This change is fundamental to the open data ecosystem, as it redefines how technology corporations can extract, process, and monetize information that was previously freely accessible. The distinction between public and private data becomes increasingly blurred, affecting user transparency and consent regarding the use of their digital footprint. Furthermore, the company has refined its data collection practices to improve the accuracy of its services, such as Bard, by integrating contextual variables like device location. This technical evolution demonstrates how the integration of heterogeneous and public data enhances AI functionality, increasing the relevance of responses while also raising the complexity of data management and protection. Users must pay close attention to these settings to control which publicly shared or private data are used to feed these systems. This article is relevant to the open data community because it illustrates the growing tension between technological innovation driven by access to large volumes of information and the preservation of individual privacy. It serves as a practical example of how legal and policy updates by major platforms directly impact data use ethics, prompting reflection on the need for clear regulatory frameworks that protect citizens without stifling the development of intelligent tools.
