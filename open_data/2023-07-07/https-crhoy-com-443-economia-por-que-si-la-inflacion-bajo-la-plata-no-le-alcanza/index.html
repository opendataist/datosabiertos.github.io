The article explains that although inflation rates have decreased compared to the previous year, the financial situation for households has worsened because real incomes have also fallen. This simultaneous decline in price growth and purchasing power means that wages no longer cover the cost of basic goods as they did before, forcing consumers to restrict spending or switch to cheaper brands. Consequently, the relief expected from lower inflation is nullified by the erosion of workers' ability to afford their usual consumption levels. The relevance of this data to open data lies in the transparency and accessibility of official statistical records from institutions like the Central Bank and the Social Security Fund. These organizations publish detailed indicators, such as real salary trends and employment figures, which allow analysts and the public to verify economic health beyond headline inflation numbers. By making datasets on nominal versus real wages available, the government enables a deeper understanding of how macroeconomic indicators directly impact individual livelihoods and market dynamics. Ultimately, the loss of real income is attributed to sluggish domestic economic growth and significant job losses in the non-export sectors. The data reveals a disconnect where employment statistics may appear stable due to labor force reduction, but actual income potential has shrunk. This highlights the importance of correlating multiple open data sources to understand the true state of the economy, demonstrating how transparent access to diverse economic indicators is crucial for accurate public policy analysis and consumer awareness.
