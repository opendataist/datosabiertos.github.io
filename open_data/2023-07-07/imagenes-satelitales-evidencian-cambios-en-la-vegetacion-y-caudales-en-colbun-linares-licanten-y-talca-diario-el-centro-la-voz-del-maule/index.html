Imágenes satelitales evidencian cambios en la vegetación y caudales en Colbún, Linares, Licantén y Talca - Diario El Centro - La Voz del Maule

The Data Cube Chile platform has successfully leveraged open satellite imagery to monitor environmental changes following severe storms in central and southern Chile. By utilizing both radar and optical sensors, researchers provided unprecedented visual evidence of vegetation shifts and river flooding in the Maule region. This capability demonstrates how open data infrastructure can offer rapid, high-resolution insights into natural disasters, enhancing our understanding of territorial impacts. A key implication is the platform’s ability to enable precise comparative analysis through false-color radar imaging, which penetrates atmospheric conditions to reveal water extent and vegetation health. The high compatibility with analysis libraries allows researchers to perform complex measurements and estimations of affected areas. This flexibility underscores the value of open datasets in facilitating diverse scientific investigations and improving disaster response mechanisms. This article highlights the critical role of open data in global climate research and territorial comprehension. By integrating historic satellite records with modern open data standards, the project fosters transparency and collaboration in environmental monitoring. It illustrates how accessible, standardized data empowers the scientific community to address pressing challenges like climate change, promoting informed decision-making and broader public engagement with critical ecological issues.
