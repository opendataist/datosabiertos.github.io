OMT: Casi el 5% de la fuerza laboral en la aviación son mujeres

The article highlights a severe gender disparity in the aviation industry, noting that women represent a tiny fraction of the workforce, particularly in leadership and technical roles, despite their significant presence in tourism overall. This imbalance underscores a systemic bias where women are largely confined to cabin crew positions while remaining excluded from command and engineering jobs, reflecting a broader societal issue that requires urgent institutional intervention to dismantle. To address this inequality, international bodies like the ICAO and the ILO are collaborating through initiatives such as the Global Aviation Gender Summit. These efforts aim to promote decent work, advance Sustainable Development Goals, and encourage the full integration of women into all aviation sectors. The commitment from government officials emphasizes that public institutions must lead by example in achieving gender parity, recognizing that true inclusivity is essential for a fair and representative industry. This topic is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates how transparent, comparable statistics are crucial for identifying structural inequalities and measuring progress. By making data on employment demographics publicly accessible, stakeholders can hold organizations accountable, drive evidence-based policy changes, and ensure that diversity initiatives are not just symbolic but backed by verifiable improvements in representation across the aviation sector.
