AHT partició de la ReformBnB que pide mayor transparencia, registro y controles | Noticias de turismo REPORTUR

The first Global ReformBnB assembly, held in Kyoto, established guidelines for governments to regulate short-term tourist rentals. The main focus is to achieve transparency and create effective control registries, addressing the lack of legislation that allows digital platforms to operate without disclosing accurate data, which hinders government oversight and fairness in the sector. This regulatory gap generates unfair competition against formal hotels, as short-term rentals often do not comply with safety standards, do not register staff, and do not pay taxes. The consequences include risks for travelers, such as scams and lack of insurance, as well as affecting local housing access by driving up costs due to the reduced supply available to residents. This news is relevant to open data because it highlights the urgency of making official accommodation records public. Without transparent and accessible data, it is impossible to monitor health risks, guarantee labor rights, or balance the market. The lack of verifiable information prevents evidence-based decision-making, affecting citizen safety and fair competitiveness in the tourism sector.
