Low staff occupancy at County Council offices ‘unacceptable’, says MS

A Freedom of Information request revealed that Conwy County Borough Council’s flagship offices operate at low occupancy levels, averaging only 33 percent post-pandemic. This significant underutilization of expensive new facilities, particularly the costly Coed Pella building, has drawn sharp criticism from a local Member of the Assembly. She argues that the continued low usage represents an unacceptable waste of taxpayer money, especially given that legal restrictions requiring remote work have long ended. The MS advocates for a strategic shift, suggesting that if occupancy rates do not improve, the council should lease unused spaces to private businesses. This approach aims to generate revenue from what she characterizes as a poor investment and pressures the administration to reconsider its hybrid working model. The core concern highlights the financial risk associated with maintaining large physical office infrastructures when workforce presence remains consistently low. This situation is highly relevant to open data initiatives, as it demonstrates the critical role of freedom of information requests in holding public bodies accountable. By exposing specific usage statistics, the data reveals a gap between policy goals and actual operational efficiency. It underscores how transparent access to government metrics can drive political debate and potentially reshape public spending decisions regarding real estate and environmental claims.
