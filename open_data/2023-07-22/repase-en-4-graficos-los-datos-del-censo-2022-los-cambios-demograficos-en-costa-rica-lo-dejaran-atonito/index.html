Repase en 4 gráficos los datos del Censo 2022: los cambios demográficos en Costa Rica lo dejarán atónito

The results of the Costa Rican census highlight a significant demographic shift toward an aging population and declining birth rates, while urbanization concentrates residents in specific provinces. Although the statistical institute acknowledges only partial coverage, it maintains the data’s robustness, presenting a revised population estimate that indicates substantial growth since the previous decade. This evolution suggests long-term changes in social structure, impacting future planning for healthcare and family housing dynamics. These findings are critical for the open-data community, as they demonstrate the complexities of large-scale statistical operations and the necessity of transparently communicating limitations such as partial coverage. Understanding how data gaps influence final estimates helps developers and analysts assess the reliability of official datasets, ensuring that applications built on this information account for potential discrepancies or estimations rather than treating them as absolute counts. Furthermore, the data reveal that household sizes are decreasing despite an increase in the total number of dwellings, pointing to broader socioeconomic trends. For open-data initiatives, this reinforces the importance of contextualizing demographic metrics within their historical and operational framework, enabling users to derive meaningful insights about societal changes while remaining aware of the methodological constraints inherent in census methodologies.
