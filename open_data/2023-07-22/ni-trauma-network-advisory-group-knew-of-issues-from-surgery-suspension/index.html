NI Trauma Network advisory group knew of issues from surgery suspension

The withdrawal of emergency surgical services at South West Acute Hospital has severely impacted patient access and ambulance operations, with authorities finding no viable alternatives to reverse the decision. This shift forces major trauma patients to travel longer distances, creating significant operational strain on ambulance crews and delaying critical care for those requiring immediate damage control surgery. Internal records reveal that potential solutions, such as transferring patients to Sligo University Hospital, were deemed unfeasible due to legal and geographical barriers. Consequently, the lack of alternative locations exacerbates the burden on the Northern Ireland Ambulance Service, highlighting systemic failures in regional emergency planning and resource allocation within the health trust. This article underscores the critical importance of open data in holding public institutions accountable. By exposing hidden operational constraints and decision-making flaws through freedom of information requests, transparent data allows communities to challenge unjustified closures. It demonstrates how accessible information empowers citizens to demand better healthcare services and exposes the real-world consequences of administrative choices on emergency response capabilities.
