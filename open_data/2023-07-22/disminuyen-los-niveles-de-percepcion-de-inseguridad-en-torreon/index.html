Disminuyen los niveles de percepción de inseguridad en Torreón

The article highlights a significant positive trend in Torreón, where citizens’ perception of insecurity has declined—a metric crucial for evaluating public safety policies. By analyzing data from national statistical institutes, authorities confirm that local security measures are effectively reducing residents’ subjective sense of vulnerability. This shift is particularly notable, as it places the city well below the regional average for perceived insecurity, suggesting that current policing strategies and community engagement initiatives are yielding measurable social benefits. Furthermore, the report details concrete operational successes, including a reduction in specific crime rates, such as residential and commercial robberies, during the reporting period. The municipality has achieved numerous arrests and referred cases to judicial authorities, demonstrating active enforcement capacity. Additionally, the expansion of proximity policing through neighborhood committees fosters a collaborative environment between citizens and law enforcement. These actions collectively contribute to a safer urban environment, reinforcing the importance of data-driven decision-making in maintaining public order. This content is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the practical application of public statistics in municipal governance. The use of INEGI’s National Urban Public Safety Survey exemplifies how transparent, accessible data can validate government performance and inform policy adjustments. By linking official metrics to local administrative outcomes, the case study underscores the value of open datasets in fostering accountability, enabling citizens to verify claims of improved security, and promoting trust in institutional transparency through evidence-based reporting.
