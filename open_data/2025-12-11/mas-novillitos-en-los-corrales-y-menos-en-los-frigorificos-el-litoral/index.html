Más novillitos en los corrales y menos en los frigoríficos | El Litoral

Más novillitos en los corrales y menos en los frigoríficos | El Litoral

The article highlights a significant strategic shift in the livestock industry, moving away from finishing lighter cattle toward retaining animals longer in feedlots to produce heavier beef. This adjustment aims to add more kilograms per animal, responding to high prices and specific market demands for quality in export and catering sectors. Consequently, the supply of traditionally lighter cattle available for immediate slaughter has decreased, as producers prioritize long-term weight gain over quick turnover. This transition is expected to tighten meat supply during the upcoming holiday season and summer months, likely driving prices higher as the heavy cattle targeted for these periods will not be ready until early next year. The reduction in available light cattle contrasts with the strategic accumulation of stock for heavier finishes, creating a temporary scarcity that influences market dynamics and pricing structures differently than usual seasonal trends. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, real-time statistics on animal movements—such as transfers to slaughterhouses, feedlot entries, and breeding farms—are crucial for detecting early signs of structural changes in production chains. Access to this granular data allows stakeholders to anticipate market adjustments, understand the drivers behind price volatility, and evaluate the potential risks of premature livestock liquidation, fostering more informed decision-making and policy responses in the agricultural sector.
