Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Norwegian immigrants face significantly disadvantaged living conditions regarding employment, housing, health, and income compared to the general population. Despite these structural inequalities and higher rates of mental health struggles and loneliness, immigrants report life satisfaction levels comparable to native Norwegians. This paradox highlights a complex resilience where poor objective indicators do not correlate with low subjective well-being, suggesting that satisfaction is driven by factors beyond material conditions. Furthermore, immigrants demonstrate notably higher confidence in key public institutions, including the political system, judiciary, and police, contrasting with lower interpersonal trust. This strong institutional affiliation and sense of belonging to Norway indicate that social cohesion mechanisms are functioning effectively, even when economic integration remains incomplete. The data reveals a nuanced social landscape where institutional trust serves as a stabilizing force amidst socioeconomic disparities. This article is vital for open data initiatives as it underscores the necessity of disaggregated, accessible datasets to uncover such hidden societal dynamics. By making detailed demographic and psychosocial data available, researchers and policymakers can better address specific community needs rather than relying on aggregate averages that mask significant inequalities. Transparent, granular data enables targeted interventions to improve living conditions while preserving the strong social integration and trust observed within immigrant populations.
