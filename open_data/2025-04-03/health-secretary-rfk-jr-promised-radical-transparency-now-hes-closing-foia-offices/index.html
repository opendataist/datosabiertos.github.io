Health Secretary RFK Jr. Promised Radical Transparency. Now He’s Closing FOIA Offices

Health Secretary RFK Jr. Promised Radical Transparency. Now He’s Closing FOIA Offices

The systematic dismantling of critical staff at the Department of Health and Human Services, particularly those managing Freedom of Information Act requests, poses a severe threat to public access to government data. By eliminating the personnel responsible for processing these statutory disclosures, the administration effectively neutralizes a primary mechanism for citizen oversight. This erosion of capacity means that legally mandated transparency is being replaced by administrative silence, preventing the public from obtaining records through established legal channels. This operational blackout coincides with a broader ideological shift toward secrecy, as leadership explicitly devalues independent scrutiny in favor of controlled narratives. With key scientific and regulatory experts dismissed, the integrity of health data collection is compromised, and decision-making processes are shielded from public comment. The administration’s claim that enhanced transparency will eliminate the need for FOIA requests contradicts the reality of reduced institutional capacity, suggesting that the goal is not openness, but the unilateral control of information without accountability. For the open data community, this development is a critical warning regarding the fragility of transparency frameworks under political pressure. It demonstrates how administrative restructuring can be used to bypass legal obligations to disclose information, effectively privatizing governance by removing public oversight mechanisms. As independent verification becomes harder and data accessibility decreases, the ability of researchers, journalists, and citizens to hold power accountable is significantly diminished, highlighting the urgent need to protect institutional knowledge and data access rights.
