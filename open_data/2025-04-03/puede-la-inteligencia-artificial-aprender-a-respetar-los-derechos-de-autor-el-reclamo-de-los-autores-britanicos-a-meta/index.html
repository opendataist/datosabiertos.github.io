¿Puede la inteligencia artificial aprender a respetar los derechos de autor? El reclamo de los autores británicos a Meta

¿Puede la inteligencia artificial aprender a respetar los derechos de autor? El reclamo de los autores británicos a Meta

The central conflict described involves Meta’s Llama 3 model being trained on copyrighted literary works without the authors’ consent, sparking significant backlash from the UK creative community. Writers argue that this unauthorized use violates intellectual property rights and exploits their labor, highlighting a critical imbalance where technological giants benefit from protected content while creators face legal and economic vulnerability. This incident underscores the urgent need for regulatory frameworks that define how artificial intelligence can legally access and utilize public data. The fear is that current or proposed "opt-out" policies could institutionalize the appropriation of creative assets, potentially undermining the economic viability of human-generated content. Consequently, there is a strong push for legislative intervention to ensure transparency and accountability in AI development practices. This case is highly relevant to open data as it exposes the tension between the free flow of information necessary for AI training and the protection of individual rights. It illustrates that "open" datasets derived from pirated or non-consensual sources pose significant ethical and legal risks. True open data initiatives must therefore prioritize lawful acquisition and fair compensation mechanisms to maintain trust and sustainability in the digital ecosystem, ensuring that data openness does not come at the expense of creator rights.
