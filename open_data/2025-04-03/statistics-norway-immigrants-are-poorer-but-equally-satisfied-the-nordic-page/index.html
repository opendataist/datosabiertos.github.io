Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significant disparities in socioeconomic status and well-being compared to the native population. Data reveals that individuals with an immigrant background generally experience lower incomes, poorer employment prospects, and substandard housing. These structural disadvantages are mirrored in health metrics, where immigrants report substantially higher rates of mental health struggles and loneliness, indicating a stark gap in their material and psychological living conditions despite their integration into society. Remarkably, this demographic group exhibits equal or even higher satisfaction with life in Norway compared to the general population. They demonstrate strong institutional trust, particularly in the political system, judiciary, and police, alongside a robust affiliation with the nation. This paradox highlights that subjective well-being and civic trust can remain high even when objective living standards are compromised, suggesting that perceived belonging and safety may offset some of the burdens associated with economic and health challenges. This report is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of transparent, comprehensive statistics in capturing nuanced social realities. By making detailed information on inequality, health, and trust publicly accessible, open data facilitates deeper analysis of integration outcomes. It allows researchers and policymakers to move beyond simple aggregates, fostering informed debates on how to address specific vulnerabilities while recognizing the resilience and positive societal attachments of immigrant communities.
