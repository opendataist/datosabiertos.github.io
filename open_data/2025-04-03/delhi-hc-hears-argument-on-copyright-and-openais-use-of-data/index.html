Delhi HC Hears Argument on Copyright and OpenAI’s Use of Data

Delhi HC Hears Argument on Copyright and OpenAI’s Use of Data

The Delhi High Court hearing between Asian News International and OpenAI highlights the critical legal ambiguity surrounding data usage in artificial intelligence development. The core dispute centers on whether training Large Language Models constitutes copyright infringement, specifically regarding the distinction between protecting facts versus the linguistic expression of those facts. This case is pivotal for open data because it challenges the foundational assumption that statistical patterns derived from public information can be used freely without licensing, potentially restricting the availability of high-quality training datasets for AI research. ANI argues that OpenAI’s models rely on linguistic probabilities and syntax, which constitute protected expression rather than unprotected facts. Conversely, OpenAI contends that its training process is a non-expressive use that extracts only structural patterns like grammar and correlation, not specific creative content. This dichotomy is vital for the open data community, as a ruling favoring the protection of statistical patterns could create significant legal barriers for developers using publicly available data to build transparent, accessible AI systems. The jurisdictional argument further complicates the landscape, with OpenAI asserting that data processing occurring outside India falls beyond the reach of local copyright laws. If courts determine that training data extraction is an infringement regardless of location, it could lead to fragmented global standards for data access. Ultimately, this case defines whether open data remains a viable resource for machine learning innovation or becomes subject to restrictive proprietary controls, impacting the future of democratized AI development.
