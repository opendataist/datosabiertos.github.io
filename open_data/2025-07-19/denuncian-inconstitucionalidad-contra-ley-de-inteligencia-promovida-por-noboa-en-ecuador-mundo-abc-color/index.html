Denuncian inconstitucionalidad contra Ley de Inteligencia promovida por Noboa en Ecuador - Mundo - ABC Color

Denuncian inconstitucionalidad contra Ley de Inteligencia promovida por Noboa en Ecuador - Mundo - ABC Color

Human rights organizations in Ecuador have challenged a new intelligence regulation, arguing that it enables mass data collection without judicial oversight. This lack of control violates privacy rights and international standards, creating a dangerous precedent in which the state can arbitrarily monitor citizens. There is concern that such unchecked power serves as a tool for political persecution, disproportionately targeting marginalized and historically vulnerable groups under the guise of national security. The legislation raises alarms regarding the confidentiality of communications and the potential involvement of foreign entities in intelligence operations, which threatens national sovereignty. By concentrating authority in the hands of the intelligence director without effective external controls, the law fosters a framework of impunity. Civil society warns that similar precedents from past administrations have already been used to suppress journalists and activists, suggesting that this new regulatory framework lacks sufficient guarantees to respect human rights. This case is highly relevant to open data and digital rights advocacy because it highlights the critical tension between state surveillance and individual privacy. It underscores the necessity for transparent, accountable mechanisms in data governance to prevent abuse. Understanding these legal vulnerabilities is essential for promoting policies that protect citizens from unauthorized information harvesting and ensure that security measures do not erode fundamental democratic liberties and open information principles.
