TikTok enfrenta nuevos problemas legales en Europa; lo demandan por uso de datos personales | El Universal

TikTok enfrenta nuevos problemas legales en Europa; lo demandan por uso de datos personales | El Universal

This article highlights ongoing legal challenges against major Chinese tech platforms, specifically TikTok, AliExpress, and WeChat, for failing to comply with European data protection regulations. The non-governmental organization Noyb has filed complaints in multiple European countries, arguing that these companies withhold user data access despite GDPR requirements. Unlike competitors such as Shein and Temu, these specific applications have ignored or provided incomplete responses to data requests, prompting authorities to initiate strict enforcement actions. The core implication is a significant shift in how data sovereignty is enforced against global technology giants. By demanding adherence to transparency laws, regulators are establishing that profit-driven data collection cannot override individual privacy rights. The potential for massive fines, reaching up to four percent of global revenue, serves as a critical deterrent. This approach signals that non-compliance with privacy standards will result in severe financial and reputational consequences, forcing companies to align their practices with legal expectations. This case is highly relevant to open data as it underscores the tension between corporate secrecy and the public’s right to information. Open data principles advocate for transparency and accessibility, yet this scenario shows how corporations restrict data flow even when users exercise their legal rights to access their own information. The legal pressure exerted here reinforces the necessity of regulatory frameworks that mandate data openness, ensuring that privacy protections do not become tools for obscuring data practices or hindering public scrutiny of digital services.
