Experts cast doubt on safety of Boeing 737 MAX ungrounding

FlyersRights.org is challenging the FAA’s refusal to disclose critical Boeing 737 MAX testing documents, arguing that the agency incorrectly used trade secret exemptions to withhold information despite public transparency pledges. This legal battle highlights a significant conflict between government secrecy claims and the need for independent expert evaluation following the catastrophic crashes that killed 346 people. The organization contends that high-level commitments by FAA and Boeing officials to transparency override assertions of proprietary secrecy. By seeking these records, FlyersRights aims to restore eroded public confidence and ensure that safety assessments are conducted openly, rather than being obscured by corporate confidentiality claims that contradict sworn statements made to Congress. This case is crucial for open_data as it tests the boundaries of public access to safety-critical information held by regulators and manufacturers. It establishes a precedent for how transparency promises can override traditional exemptions, potentially empowering citizens and experts to demand greater accountability and visibility in aviation safety processes.
