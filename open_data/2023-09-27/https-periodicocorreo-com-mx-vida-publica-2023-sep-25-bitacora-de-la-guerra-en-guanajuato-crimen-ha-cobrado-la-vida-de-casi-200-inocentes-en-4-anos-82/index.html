Open Data
ES EN

The article highlights the critical role of open data in exposing the true cost of organized crime in Guanajuato. By leveraging leaked military records to create "La Base Oculta," civil society organizations transformed opaque government information into a transparent, accessible database. This initiative allows for independent verification of state narratives, demonstrating that open data is not just about access, but about empowering citizens to hold institutions accountable for their security policies and their human toll. The analysis reveals a stark reality where thousands of violent incidents occurred, resulting in significant civilian casualties. Contrary to official narratives focusing solely on combatants, the data underscores the heavy burden borne by innocent populations. This insight is vital for open data as it shows how structured information can challenge official statistics, providing a more nuanced understanding of violence that includes non-combatant victims and systemic failures in investigation and justice. Ultimately, the disparity between reported crimes and opened investigations points to a crisis in state response. The relevance to open data lies in its ability to document these gaps effectively. By making detailed records public, such projects facilitate rigorous monitoring of government efficacy. This ensures that public discourse remains grounded in verifiable evidence, fostering a more informed citizenry capable of demanding better governance and more effective security strategies based on transparent, auditable facts.
