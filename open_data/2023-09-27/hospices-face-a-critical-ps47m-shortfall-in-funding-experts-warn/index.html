Hospices face a ‘critical’ £47m shortfall in funding, experts warn

Freedom of Information requests reveal a significant funding shortfall for adult hospices in England, with government support failing to keep pace with soaring operational costs. Data indicates that Integrated Care Boards have effectively reduced real-term funding by millions over two years, despite rising expenses for salaries and energy. This discrepancy means hospices are spending substantially more than they receive, creating an unsustainable financial model that threatens the continuity of essential end-of-life care services across the country. The consequences of this underfunding are severe, potentially forcing vulnerable patients away from specialized hospice care and into emergency hospital departments. Without adequate resources, hospices cannot maintain vital services like "hospice-at-home," leading to distressing outcomes for dying individuals and their families. This shift not only harms patient well-being but also places an excessive and expensive burden on the wider National Health Service, highlighting the critical need for fair and sufficient funding to prevent the collapse of palliative care infrastructure. This report is highly relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates the tangible impact of transparent information gathering on public policy. By leveraging freedom of information laws, advocacy groups can uncover systemic inequities and hold local health authorities accountable for their spending decisions. The case illustrates how accessible, granular data can drive necessary political action, ensuring that marginalized groups receive the resources they are owed and that government commitments to equitable service provision are enforced rather than ignored.
