El Corte Inglés comunica un ataque informático con acceso a datos personales de sus clientes

El Corte Inglés comunica un ataque informático con acceso a datos personales de sus clientes

El Corte Inglés has confirmed a cyberattack that affected an external supplier, allowing criminals to access customers’ personal and contact data. Although the company promptly remedied the incident and ensured that no payments can be made using the compromised information, this event highlights the inherent vulnerability of digital supply chains, where the security of a single entity can jeopardize the privacy of millions of users. This incident fits into a growing trend of cyberattacks targeting the retail sector in Spain, following security breaches at other major companies. The organization acknowledges in its financial documents that such events are not only frequent but also pose a substantial risk to its reputation and operations. The increasing sophistication of these attacks underscores the fragility of systems that store large volumes of financial and identifying information, calling into question the effectiveness of traditional defensive measures. In the realm of open data, this news is crucial because it illustrates the ethical and practical limits of information availability. While transparency is a fundamental value, the mass exposure of personal data through security breaches demonstrates that centralizing sensitive information—even in third-party infrastructure—generates systemic risks. This reinforces the need to apply privacy-by-design principles and to carefully assess the impact of opening data when it interacts with highly sensitive commercial databases, ensuring that transparency does not become a backdoor for criminal exploitation.
