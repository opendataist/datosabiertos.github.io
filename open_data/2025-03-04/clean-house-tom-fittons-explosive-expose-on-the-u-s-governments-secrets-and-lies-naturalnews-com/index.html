“Clean House”: Tom Fitton’s explosive expose on the U.S. government’s SECRETS and LIES – NaturalNews.com

“Clean House”: Tom Fitton’s explosive expose on the U.S. government’s SECRETS and LIES – NaturalNews.com

Tom Fitton’s book argues that the Obama administration significantly undermined its pledge for openness by prioritizing political convenience over transparency. Through rigorous litigation and Freedom of Information Act requests, Judicial Watch exposed a systemic pattern of secrecy and obstruction, demonstrating that executive power was often wielded to shield officials from accountability rather than to serve the public interest. This contrast between rhetorical promises and actual governance highlights the fragility of democratic trust when oversight mechanisms are bypassed by the executive branch. The investigation revealed how specific high-profile scandals, including the handling of the Benghazi attack and the misuse of private email servers, were managed through deliberate obfuscation. By withholding critical documents and manipulating narratives, the administration attempted to mitigate political fallout, illustrating how government entities can exploit legal processes to delay and obscure the truth. These cases serve as stark examples of the consequences when institutional checks fail to enforce transparency, leading to diminished public confidence in official records and communications. This narrative is vital for open data advocates as it underscores that technical accessibility of information is insufficient without legal and cultural enforcement. It demonstrates that data remains hidden not just due to technical barriers, but through intentional administrative resistance that requires persistent activism to overcome. Understanding these historical precedents helps explain current challenges in data openness, emphasizing that sustained pressure from watchdog groups and civil society is essential to maintain government integrity and ensure that transparency mechanisms remain effective tools for accountability.
