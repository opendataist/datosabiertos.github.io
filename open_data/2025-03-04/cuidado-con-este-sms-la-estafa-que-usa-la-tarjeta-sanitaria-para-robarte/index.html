Cuidado con este SMS: la estafa que usa la tarjeta sanitaria para robarte

Cuidado con este SMS: la estafa que usa la tarjeta sanitaria para robarte

Phishing represents a critical threat in the digital age, where cybercriminals employ social engineering to impersonate official entities and steal sensitive information. This technique exploits distrust and urgency, using communications that perfectly mimic public agencies to induce victims into revealing financial and personal data. The sophistication of these scams, which include the creation of fake websites indistinguishable from legitimate ones, makes identification difficult and the consequences—such as identity theft—severe and costly to mitigate. Prevention and digital education are the most effective weapons against this crime. It is emphasized that users should maintain a healthy skepticism toward urgent messages, remembering that official institutions never request private information via SMS or direct links. Verifying the authenticity of web addresses and contacting entities directly through their official channels, rather than using those provided in suspicious messages, are fundamental steps for protection. User diligence in these verifications is crucial to intercepting scams before data is compromised. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the need for transparency and verification in digital communications, values central to open data initiatives. By promoting education on how to distinguish official sources from fraud, it fosters a culture of verifiable trust, which is essential for the adoption of tools and platforms based on public data. Combating phishing involves protecting the integrity of digital identity, a prerequisite for citizens to safely interact with open government services. Without trust in the authenticity of sources, the potential of open data to enhance transparency and civic participation is severely limited, as vulnerability to scams erodes faith in digital institutions.
