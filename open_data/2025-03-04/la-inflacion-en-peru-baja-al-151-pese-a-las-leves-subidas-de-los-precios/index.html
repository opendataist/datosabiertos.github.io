La inflación en Perú baja al 1,51 %, pese a las leves subidas de los precios

Peru achieved a decline in inflation to 1.51% in February, continuing a downward trend that kept prices well below the central bank’s target ceiling. This stabilization indicates robust macroeconomic control, driven primarily by reductions in essential sectors like food and utilities, which offset slight increases in discretionary spending categories such as entertainment and dining. The overall price level decrease suggests that monetary policy measures are effectively curbing inflationary pressures without stifling economic activity. The data reveals a nuanced landscape where specific regions and sectors experienced divergent price movements. While metropolitan areas like Lima saw modest rises in service-related costs, other urban centers reported significant deflation in basic goods. This geographic disparity highlights the varying impact of economic factors across the country, yet the aggregate result remains favorable. The broad-based nature of these price changes, affecting hundreds of household items, underscores the comprehensive reach of the inflationary environment. This article is relevant to open data because it relies on publicly accessible statistical indicators, such as the Consumer Price Index (CPI), which are crucial for transparency and accountability. Access to this detailed, disaggregated data allows researchers, journalists, and citizens to verify government claims about economic health and monitor cost-of-living changes in real-time. Such openness empowers users to analyze trends, challenge narratives, and make informed decisions based on evidence rather than political rhetoric.
