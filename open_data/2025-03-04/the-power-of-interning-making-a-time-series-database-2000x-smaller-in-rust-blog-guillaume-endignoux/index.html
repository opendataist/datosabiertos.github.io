The power of interning: making a time series database 2000x smaller in Rust | Blog | Guillaume Endignoux

The power of interning: making a time series database 2000x smaller in Rust | Blog | Guillaume Endignoux

Open data initiatives often generate massive datasets, yet many public repositories lack documentation on efficient data storage or reuse strategies. This case study highlights a significant gap: while open APIs provide valuable real-time insights, the resulting data volumes can become unwieldy, hindering long-term sustainability and accessibility for developers and researchers. The author demonstrates that standard JSON formats, despite their ubiquity, lead to substantial memory inflation when processed. By applying the interning pattern in Rust, the study achieves a two-thousand-fold compression ratio. This approach replaces repetitive textual and structural elements with lightweight indices, dramatically reducing the storage footprint without compromising data integrity or query speed. This work is crucial for the open data community as it proves that sophisticated software engineering techniques can optimize public resources. It encourages maintainers of open data platforms to consider compression and structured storage early in their pipeline design. Such optimization ensures that large-scale public datasets remain lightweight, faster to download, and more cost-effective to host, thereby promoting broader and more sustainable reuse of public information.
