Married without kids? It’s becoming a lot more common in Singapore these days

Married without kids? It’s becoming a lot more common in Singapore these days

The article highlights a significant societal shift in Singapore where marriage is increasingly decoupled from the expectation of parenthood, resulting in a rapid rise in childless married couples. This trend is driven by a prioritization of personal freedom, professional aspirations, and the perceived burdens of parenting, rather than just biological constraints. The data indicates that the proportion of ever-married women aged 40 to 49 without children has doubled over two decades, reflecting a deep-seated change in values where individual fulfillment often supersedes traditional familial duties. This demographic transformation poses substantial challenges for national policy, particularly as the government attempts to boost fertility rates through financial incentives. The persistent decline in the total fertility rate and the diminishing impact of cultural factors, such as the Dragon Year effect, suggest that economic support alone is insufficient to reverse these attitudes. Consequently, society may need to adapt to smaller family structures by relying on immigration or technological advancements like AI to mitigate the effects of a shrinking workforce and changing social dynamics. For open data communities, this report underscores the critical importance of granular, longitudinal datasets in tracking evolving social norms and demographic behaviors. It demonstrates how statistical evidence can reveal discrepancies between government policy intentions and actual societal trends, such as the gap between support schemes and the root causes of low birth rates. Understanding these nuanced drivers is essential for creating responsive data products that help policymakers and researchers address complex, multifaceted social issues beyond simple population counts.
