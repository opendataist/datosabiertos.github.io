The article highlights widespread insecurity in Oaxaca de Juárez, where a significant majority of residents perceive their environment as dangerous. This sentiment is particularly acute among women, who report higher levels of fear compared to men, indicating a gendered disparity in the experience of public safety. Statistical evidence reveals a rise in specific crimes, such as robberies and home invasions, with ATMs and public markets identified as high-risk locations. These data points underscore the tangible threats citizens face daily, moving beyond subjective feelings to documented patterns of violence and theft that erode community trust. This information is vital for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates how transparent, accessible statistics empower citizens to understand urban safety trends. By making such granular data public, stakeholders can advocate for targeted policy interventions, fostering accountability and enabling communities to make informed decisions about their well-being and local governance.
