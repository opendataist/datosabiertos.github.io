Tarija necesita 29.424 agentes censales, se registraron sólo el 13,59%

The National Institute of Statistics emphasizes that conducting the upcoming census fundamentally depends on mass participation rather than institutional effort alone. This highlights a critical aspect of open data infrastructure: the legitimacy and accuracy of national statistics rely on the active engagement of local citizens as enumerators. Without this widespread human network, the state cannot effectively capture the true demographic reality of the country. The campaign aims to mobilize the general population, particularly those living in remote communities, to serve as volunteer census agents. By requiring individuals to georeference their locations near their homes, the initiative ensures that data collection is carried out by people familiar with the specific contexts of their neighborhoods. This localized approach seeks to bridge the gap between official institutions and remote populations, fostering trust and improving the quality of the gathered information. This model is highly relevant to open data because it democratizes the statistical process, turning citizens into active participants in the generation of public knowledge. It demonstrates how open data initiatives can leverage community involvement to achieve comprehensive coverage, ensuring that the resulting datasets are not merely government products but reflections of collective civic action. Ultimately, this synergy between public participation and state planning is essential for creating transparent, accurate, and usable public data.
