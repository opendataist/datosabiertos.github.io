OpenAI will train its AI models on the Financial Times' journalism

The Financial Times has partnered with OpenAI to license its journalism for AI model training and product development. This agreement establishes that AI platforms must compensate publishers for their content, moving away from the previous norm of unconscionable web scraping. By ensuring attribution and linking, the deal sets a precedent for ethical data usage in generative AI. This collaboration highlights the critical importance of licensed high-quality data for improving AI accuracy. As AI companies seek to train models on current, reliable news, they are increasingly forging financial partnerships with major publishers. This trend suggests a shift toward formalizing the relationship between tech giants and media creators, potentially legitimizing publisher content as a valuable asset in the AI era. The article is highly relevant to open data because it contrasts proprietary, licensed data usage with open access principles. While the FT’s paywall remains, the partnership effectively distributes its content through AI interfaces, challenging traditional access models. This dynamic raises significant questions about transparency, data ownership, and whether AI-driven dissemination undermines the economic sustainability of journalism that relies on restricted, non-open distribution channels.
