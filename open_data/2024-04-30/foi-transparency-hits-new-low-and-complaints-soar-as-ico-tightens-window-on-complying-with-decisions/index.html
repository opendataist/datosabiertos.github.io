FOI transparency hits new low and complaints soar as ICO tightens window on complying with decisions

The landscape of data transparency has deteriorated significantly, characterized by a sharp decline in the full release of requested information and a surge in public dissatisfaction. Although the volume of Freedom of Information requests has reached historic highs, the proportion of cases resulting in complete data provision has been cut in half since the law’s inception. This trend indicates that public bodies are increasingly withholding or partially redacting information, suggesting a systemic barrier to achieving true transparency despite growing public demand for accountability. Simultaneously, the regulatory framework is struggling to cope with this escalating pressure, as complaints regarding request handling have hit record levels. The Information Commissioner’s Office faces an overwhelming caseload, leading to slower response times and missed statutory deadlines. This bottleneck highlights a critical misalignment between the volume of information sought and the administrative capacity to process it, undermining the effectiveness of oversight mechanisms and eroding trust in the system’s ability to enforce openness. This article is crucial for open data advocates because it exposes the gap between legislative intent and practical reality. It demonstrates that without improved public body compliance and more efficient regulatory processes, open data initiatives risk becoming ineffective. The findings suggest a urgent need for structural reforms and technological integration to streamline requests, ensuring that the principle of "information delayed is information denied" is actively addressed rather than merely acknowledged.
