Desempleo de México se ubicó en 2,8% en abril de 2023

Mexico’s April unemployment rate dropped significantly compared to the previous year, reaching 2.8% of the economically active population. This decrease reflects a positive trend in labor absorption, with millions more individuals employed than during the same period in 2022, despite a slight monthly increase. The data highlights an expanding workforce participation and a growing economy that is effectively integrating new job seekers. Informality also saw a notable decline, falling below 55% as service sectors absorbed the majority of new jobs. Gender disparities remain evident, with male participation rates substantially higher than female rates. These dynamics provide crucial context for understanding Mexico’s economic resilience and the structural challenges within its labor market, particularly regarding the quality and stability of employment opportunities. This report is vital for open data initiatives as it demonstrates the importance of accessible, standardized labor statistics for socioeconomic analysis. By making such granular data publicly available, policymakers and researchers can track inequality and economic shifts over time. Transparent labor metrics enable better-informed decisions regarding social programs and economic strategies, ensuring that development efforts address the root causes of unemployment and informal work.
