Rechaza Comisión Permanente nombrar a comisionados del Inai

The Mexican Senate has rejected a proposal to convene an extraordinary session to appoint commissioners for the National Institute of Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI). Despite a judicial order mandating this action, the legislative majority, led by Morena, refused to comply, arguing that the judiciary is overstepping its constitutional bounds by interfering with legislative powers. This decision highlights a deep institutional conflict between the legislative and judicial branches, with lawmakers asserting their independence against what they perceive as undue judicial pressure. The core implication of this standoff is the potential weakening of institutional checks and balances within Mexico. By prioritizing political sovereignty over legal mandates, the ruling party signals that electoral and administrative decisions may remain unresolved despite court rulings. This creates a precarious environment where the rule of law appears subordinate to political convenience, undermining the predictability and stability required for effective governance. The refusal to execute the judicial order suggests that political allegiance may trump legal obligation in high-stakes appointments. This situation is critically relevant to open data because the INAI is the primary guardian of transparency and information access rights. Without a fully functional institute, citizens face significant barriers to obtaining public information, which hinders accountability and civic participation. The stagnation of commissioner appointments delays crucial oversight mechanisms, effectively freezing the enforcement of data protection laws. Consequently, the lack of a working INAI erodes public trust in transparency initiatives, making it difficult to ensure that open data policies are upheld and enforced effectively across all levels of government.
