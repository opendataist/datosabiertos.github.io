Jurisprudencia, algoritmos y derechos fundamentales

Jurisprudencia, algoritmos y derechos fundamentales

The article highlights a critical legal precedent in Spain, where the Supreme Court mandated the public disclosure of the source code for the BOSCO algorithm, which manages social electric bonuses. This ruling establishes that algorithms used in public administration are subject to transparency laws, obliging the government to reveal their underlying logic. As a result, citizens and oversight bodies can scrutinize automated systems that significantly impact fundamental rights, shifting the paradigm from opaque "black boxes" to accountable digital governance. Beyond mere transparency, the decision emphasizes that public access to algorithmic information enhances system security and improves code quality. By allowing independent scrutiny, the law encourages administrators to implement stricter security measures and correct vulnerabilities early. This approach ensures that the design and control of these programs are robust, preventing failures that could unjustly deny social benefits or distort public processes, thereby reinforcing the rule of law in the digital age. This case is highly relevant to open data because it legally codifies the principle that algorithmic transparency is a prerequisite for democratic accountability. It challenges the traditional view of proprietary code by asserting that when algorithms serve public functions, their source code must be open. This empowers civil society to audit government AI, ensuring that technological efficiency does not override fundamental rights, and sets a global standard for open governance in the era of automated administration.
