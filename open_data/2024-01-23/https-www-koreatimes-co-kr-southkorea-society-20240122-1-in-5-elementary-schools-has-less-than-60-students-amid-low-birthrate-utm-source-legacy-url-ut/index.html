The article highlights a severe crisis in South Korea’s education system, driven by the world’s lowest fertility rate. Elementary schools are rapidly shrinking, with a significant portion hosting fewer than sixty students. This demographic collapse signals an impending wave of closures and resource consolidation across middle and high schools. The implications for educational planning are profound, requiring a fundamental restructuring of infrastructure to accommodate a drastically reducing student population over the coming years. In response to this demographic shift, the government has expanded financial incentives for childbearing, including increased cash bonuses and welfare programs. However, despite these measures, public spending on family welfare remains low compared to other OECD nations. Experts argue that current efforts are insufficient, urging a substantial increase in government expenditure to match the levels seen in countries with higher fertility rates. Without a dramatic escalation in support, the downward trend in population growth is likely to continue unchecked. This report is critically relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how transparent statistical tracking drives public policy. By making birth rates and school enrollment figures accessible, policymakers can identify systemic risks and advocate for evidence-based solutions. Open data serves as the foundation for this awareness, allowing citizens and experts to hold governments accountable and push for the necessary fiscal changes to address the nation’s demographic emergency.
