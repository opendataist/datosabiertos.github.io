Sturgeon-era WhatsApp deletion 'may have subverted FoI regime'

The systematic deletion of WhatsApp messages by senior UK officials, including former First Minister Nicola Sturgeon, has raised serious concerns about transparency and public accountability. Evidence presented to the UK Covid Inquiry suggests that routine inbox cleaning resulted in the permanent loss of critical records, despite previous commitments to preserve all correspondence for future investigations. This practice undermines the integrity of official proceedings and raises questions about potential cover-ups regarding decision-making processes during the pandemic. Jamie Hamilton, the Scottish Information Commissioner, expressed deep concern that these actions may subvert the principles of the Freedom of Information Act. He emphasized that proper record-keeping is essential for open government and maintaining public trust. By allowing raw communications to disappear while only distilled decisions were archived, the public may be denied access to information they are legally entitled to, compromising democratic oversight and the ability to learn from past crises. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the urgent need for robust, transparent digital preservation policies within public institutions. If key communications are routinely deleted or deemed exempt from disclosure, the dataset available for public scrutiny becomes incomplete or biased. Ensuring that all relevant data remains accessible supports accountability, enabling researchers and citizens to fully understand the rationale behind government actions and fostering greater trust in political institutions.
