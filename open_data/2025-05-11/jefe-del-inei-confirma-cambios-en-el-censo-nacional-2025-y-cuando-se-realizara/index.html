Jefe del INEI confirma cambios en el Censo Nacional 2025 y cuándo se realizará

The upcoming National Census marks a significant shift from traditional, one-day operations to a three-month digital process. By equipping thousands of enumerators with GPS-enabled tablets, the institute aims to collect real-time data on population dynamics, including shifting family structures, the lingering impacts of the pandemic on education and employment, and the presence of both regular and irregular foreign migrants. This extended timeline allows for more comprehensive coverage, ensuring that diverse living situations, including those without fixed housing, are accurately recorded. A key innovation is the provision of virtual data entry options via QR codes when residents are absent, ensuring total coverage even in remote or risky areas. The census will gather detailed information on housing conditions, household characteristics, and demographic traits such as ethnicity and disability status. Special coordination for satellite connectivity in rural zones highlights the commitment to inclusivity, while security concerns in high-risk territories may result in specific areas being excluded to protect personnel and data integrity. This article is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it establishes the foundational dataset for future public policy and social analysis. The secure, encrypted handling of this massive influx of digital information sets a precedent for managing large-scale government datasets. Furthermore, the transparency regarding data protection mechanisms and the potential for real-time aggregation offers valuable insights into how modern statistical agencies can balance data utility with privacy, serving as a critical reference for standards in public sector data governance and accessibility.
