ES EN

The indefinite suspension of Mexico’s INAI critically undermines democratic accountability and the protection of citizens’ personal data. By dissolving autonomous institutions, the current administration prioritizes political control over constitutional rights, effectively removing the independent oversight necessary for a functioning democracy. This shift risks eroding public trust and leaves citizens vulnerable to arbitrary government actions without recourse. The government’s reluctance to share information, evidenced by numerous instances of legal non-compliance, highlights a systemic failure to uphold transparency obligations. Relying on executive-controlled bodies for audit duties creates a conflict of interest, as the state essentially monitors itself. This lack of impartial verification weakens institutional integrity and prevents the public from holding leaders accountable for their actions and expenditures. This situation is crucial to the open data discourse because it illustrates the dangers of politicizing information infrastructure. True open data requires institutional independence and legal enforcement to ensure accessibility and reliability. Without autonomous guardians, data remains a tool of state power rather than a public resource, jeopardizing the foundational principles of transparency and civic engagement essential for modern democratic societies.
