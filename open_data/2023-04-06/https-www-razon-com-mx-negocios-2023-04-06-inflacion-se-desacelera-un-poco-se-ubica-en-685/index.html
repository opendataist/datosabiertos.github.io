Inflation in Mexico shows a clear downward trend, marking its lowest level since late 2021 and slowing below market expectations. Although prices remain far from the central bank’s official target, the accelerated moderation suggests an imminent shift in monetary policy, anticipating the end of the interest rate hiking cycle. This relative stability, driven by lower energy and food costs, offers a positive signal regarding medium-term price control. Meanwhile, countries in Latin America and the Caribbean have established a strategic alliance to combat regional inflation by facilitating trade and eliminating sanitary barriers. This coordinated effort aims to reduce the cost of basic goods and agricultural inputs, prioritizing the well-being of the most vulnerable populations in the face of global shocks. The cooperation seeks to optimize logistics and improve access to financing, recognizing that food security and trade efficiency are essential pillars for collective economic stability. This article is relevant to the field of open data because it underscores the critical importance of transparency and information sharing for effective governance. The creation of technical working groups to analyze trade flows and the continuous monitoring of economic indicators depend on accurate, accessible, and comparable data across nations. Without robust and standardized open data, it is impossible to conduct the necessary analyses to design policies that reduce inflation and improve resource distribution in the region.
