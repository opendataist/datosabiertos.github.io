CGT advierte sobre los datos de siniestralidad laboral y califica de "muy preocupantes" las 664 muertes hasta octubre - Tercera Información

The article highlights a sharp increase in workplace fatalities and injuries, underscoring a critical failure in current occupational safety frameworks. By analyzing recent mortality statistics, the central argument emerges that the current system prioritizes administrative efficiency over human life, creating an urgent need for structural reform rather than incremental adjustments. This data serves as empirical evidence of systemic neglect, validating demands for immediate and radical changes in how labor safety is monitored and enforced. A primary concern is the privatization of prevention systems and mutual insurance entities, which critics argue introduces corporate interests into medical and safety decisions. The narrative insists that these functions must be fully integrated into the public health system and managed by state bodies. This shift is proposed to ensure that criteria for attending to work-related injuries are strictly medical and public-interest driven, eliminating conflicts of interest that currently compromise worker protection and the integrity of accident investigations. Furthermore, the text calls for strengthened state inspection, particularly targeting high-risk sectors and small enterprises that often lack union representation. The relevance to open data lies in the transparency these statistics provide: when government-held data on accidents and deaths is publicly accessible, it becomes a powerful tool for civil society to demand accountability. Open data does not just record tragedy; it exposes systemic gaps, forcing policymakers to address discrepancies between legal frameworks and actual workplace realities through evidence-based advocacy.
