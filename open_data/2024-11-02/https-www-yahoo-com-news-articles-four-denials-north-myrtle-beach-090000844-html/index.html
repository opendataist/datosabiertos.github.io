The City of North Myrtle Beach has engaged in a pattern of legally dubious FOIA denials regarding former city manager Mike Mahaney’s personnel file and text messages exchanged by elected officials. Despite state law allowing for the redaction of truly private information, the city withheld entire records and obscured metadata, claiming an unreasonable invasion of privacy. Legal experts argue that government communications and official salaries are public records, and the city’s refusal to disclose basic contact information or use proper redaction techniques violates open government standards. This incident is highly relevant to open data advocates because it highlights the tension between bureaucratic secrecy and public transparency. When governments misinterpret privacy exemptions to shield administrative decisions, they undermine public trust and accountability. The arbitrary withholding of data, such as the names and numbers of individuals communicating with officials, demonstrates how easily access laws can be manipulated to prevent scrutiny of leadership conduct and financial expenditures. The broader implication is that without strict adherence to legal protocols, open data initiatives remain fragile. The city’s reliance on blanket denials rather than targeted redaction suggests a systemic resistance to transparency. This case serves as a critical reminder that true open data requires not just the existence of laws, but the diligent and lawful implementation of access requests, ensuring that citizens can effectively monitor their public servants.
