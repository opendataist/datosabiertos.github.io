The shrinking number of mortgage advisers in the UK: Key concerns and insights – Davidson

The number of mortgage advisers in the UK has plummeted to levels not seen since the financial crisis, driven by income instability, excessive workload, and an aging workforce. This decline threatens the stability of the housing market, as brokers provide essential personalized guidance that automated lender channels cannot replicate at scale. Despite lenders shifting focus to digital platforms, the majority of consumers still rely on human intermediaries, making the sustainability of this profession critical for maintaining market health. Recruitment struggles and high stress levels have created a cycle where potential new entrants are deterred by the sector's financial risks and lack of comprehensive training pathways. Existing brokers face increasing pressure without proportional compensation, leading many to leave the industry. The absence of structured onboarding for non-traditional candidates exacerbates the shortage, leaving firms without the resources to adapt to market fluctuations or capitalize on growth opportunities effectively. This trend is highly relevant to open data because it highlights a significant gap in the transparency of financial service infrastructure. While mortgage rates and approval metrics are often publicly available, detailed data on the sustainability, workforce demographics, and operational challenges of intermediary networks remains scarce. Improving open data access in this area would allow stakeholders to better understand the systemic risks facing the advice sector and support policy decisions that ensure the continued availability of trusted financial guidance.
