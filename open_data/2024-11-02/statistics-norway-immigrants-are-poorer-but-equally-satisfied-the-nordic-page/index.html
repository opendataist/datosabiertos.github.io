Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions compared to the native population, experiencing lower incomes, worse employment prospects, and substandard housing. Despite these material disadvantages, they report levels of life satisfaction equivalent to the general public, challenging assumptions that objective hardship directly dictates subjective well-being. This resilience highlights a complex social dynamic where quality of life is not solely defined by economic metrics. Beyond economic factors, immigrants demonstrate higher prevalence of mental health issues and loneliness, alongside markedly higher trust in state institutions like the judiciary and police. This disparity in institutional confidence suggests that while immigrants may struggle with personal isolation and psychological distress, they maintain strong political affiliation and faith in the systems governing their lives. These findings reveal a nuanced relationship between marginalized groups and state structures, contrasting personal hardship with civic trust. This article is relevant to open data because it showcases how publicly available statistics can uncover counterintuitive social patterns, such as high satisfaction amidst poverty. Transparent data publication allows researchers and policymakers to identify these specific vulnerabilities and strengths, enabling targeted interventions that address mental health and loneliness without undermining the existing trust immigrants place in public institutions.
