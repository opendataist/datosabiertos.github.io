The release of New South Wales’s new housing data portal reveals that homelessness has nearly doubled, exposing a stark contradiction between surging vulnerability and existing resources. Contrary to the prevailing argument that construction alone solves the crisis, the data shows hundreds of thousands of vacant dwellings could immediately house those in need. This challenges the notion that supply constraints are the primary barrier, highlighting instead that the issue lies in distribution and policy priorities rather than mere building volume. The portal further complicates the narrative by redefining social housing, often obscuring the privatization of public housing through terminology that blends public and community sectors. This lack of transparency hinders effective advocacy and accurate assessment of the true state of affordable housing. By using the portal to expose these discrepancies, advocates can demand clearer definitions and hold the government accountable for the erosion of genuine public housing stock, which remains a tiny fraction of total dwellings. This article is crucial to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, accessible information can dismantle misleading narratives about housing affordability. By making housing vacancy, rental costs, and demographic data publicly available, the portal empowers civil society to verify claims and advocate for immediate, viable solutions. It underscores the necessity of open data in driving policy change, proving that visibility into resource availability is essential for challenging systemic failures and securing housing rights.
