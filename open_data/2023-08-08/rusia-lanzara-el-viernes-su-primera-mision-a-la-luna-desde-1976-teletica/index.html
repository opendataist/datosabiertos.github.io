Rusia lanzará el viernes su primera misión a la Luna desde 1976 | Teletica

Russia is launching its first robotic lunar mission in decades, aiming to return to the Moon’s challenging south pole to analyze soil and conduct long-term scientific research. This endeavor marks the beginning of a new program designed to strengthen cooperation with China, effectively shifting geopolitical alignments in space exploration amid ongoing tensions with Western nations. The significance of the launch stems from the broader context of international isolation and technological competition. Following the invasion of Ukraine, European space agencies severed cooperation, leaving Russia to rely on new partnerships and its own capabilities to compete with emerging private entities and established global powers. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights how geopolitical shifts and sanctions impact international scientific collaboration. It underscores the potential consequences for transparency and data sharing, as restricted partnerships may lead to fragmented datasets or reduced access to shared planetary research, affecting the global open science ecosystem.
