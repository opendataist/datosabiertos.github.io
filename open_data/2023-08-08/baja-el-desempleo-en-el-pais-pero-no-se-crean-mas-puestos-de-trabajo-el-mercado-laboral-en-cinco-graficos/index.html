Baja el desempleo en el país, pero no se crean más puestos de trabajo: el mercado laboral en cinco gráficos

The article reveals that although the official unemployment rate has declined, this improvement is misleading. The drop in unemployment figures is mainly due to a shrinking labor force rather than the creation of new jobs. As a result, the main conclusion is that the apparent progress conceals a deeper stagnation in job creation, as many people have left the workforce altogether, making the lower unemployment rate an artifact of disengagement rather than a sign of economic health. Moreover, the data highlight a persistent decline in both formal and informal employment, along with a significant gender gap in labor force participation. Women and young people are disproportionately affected by these challenges, with women experiencing a substantial drop in labor force involvement. Employment levels have recovered slowly and have not returned to pre-pandemic levels, indicating structural weaknesses in the labor market that fail to adequately support vulnerable demographic groups. This analysis is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical importance of contextualizing raw statistics with complementary indicators. Presenting only the unemployment rate can lead to incorrect policy conclusions or public misinterpretation. Access to multidimensional datasets—such as those including labor force participation, informal employment trends, and demographic breakdowns—enables analysts to uncover the true state of the economy and prevent negative trends from being obscured by seemingly positive figures.
