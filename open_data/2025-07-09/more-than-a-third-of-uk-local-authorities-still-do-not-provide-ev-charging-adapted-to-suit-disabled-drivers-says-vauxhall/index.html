More than a third of UK local authorities still do not provide EV charging adapted to suit disabled drivers says Vauxhall

The article highlights a critical disparity in the UK’s electric vehicle infrastructure, revealing that the vast majority of local councils fail to provide on-street chargers that meet national accessibility standards. Despite a significant national increase in charging points, the proportion of facilities adapted for disabled drivers has remained stagnant. This lack of accessible infrastructure poses a substantial barrier for millions of disabled motorists, many of whom rely entirely on public charging networks because they lack off-street parking at home. For open data initiatives, this situation underscores the necessity of granular, standardized data collection regarding physical accessibility. Current reporting often misses vital details about charger compliance with specific standards, such as those required for wheelchair users. Without transparent, high-quality datasets that explicitly track accessibility features alongside general installation metrics, policymakers and urban planners cannot accurately identify gaps or ensure inclusive urban development. The gap between total infrastructure growth and accessible option availability illustrates how aggregated data can obscure systemic inequalities. Ultimately, the research calls for urgent action to integrate accessibility requirements into charging strategies. By collecting and sharing specific demand data from disabled drivers, stakeholders can advocate for infrastructure that supports equitable electrification. Ensuring that public charging networks are truly inclusive is essential to prevent a "cliff edge transition" where disabled citizens are left without viable mobility options as the country shifts away from internal combustion engines.
