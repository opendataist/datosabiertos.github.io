Arte hecho con Inteligencia Artificial no será protegido en México por institucióndes de derechos de autor ni propiedad intelectual, son dominio público, establece la Suprema Corte

Arte hecho con Inteligencia Artificial no será protegido en México por institucióndes de derechos de autor ni propiedad intelectual, son dominio público, establece la Suprema Corte

The Mexican Supreme Court ruled that AI-generated content lacks copyright protection because the law restricts authorship to natural persons. Consequently, works created exclusively by artificial intelligence are considered public domain, meaning no individual or entity can claim exclusive rights over them, regardless of financial investment in the platform used for generation. This precedent clarifies that true originality requires human creativity, intellect, and emotion, which AI systems inherently lack as they merely process pre-existing data. While the court left open the possibility of protecting human-AI collaborative works with significant creative input, it firmly established that purely algorithmic outputs remain freely accessible for public use and dissemination. This decision is crucial for open data initiatives, as it ensures AI-generated materials cannot be enclosed within private intellectual property regimes. By keeping these outputs in the public domain, the ruling promotes greater transparency, accessibility, and free exchange of digital content, supporting the principles of open access in the evolving landscape of digital media and information technology.
