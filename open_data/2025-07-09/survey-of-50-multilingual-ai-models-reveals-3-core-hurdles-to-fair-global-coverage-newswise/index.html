Survey of 50+ Multilingual AI Models Reveals 3 Core Hurdles to Fair Global Coverage | Newswise

Survey of 50+ Multilingual AI Models Reveals 3 Core Hurdles to Fair Global Coverage | Newswise

Multilingual large language models promise global accessibility, yet their potential is currently constrained by uneven training data, imperfect cross-language alignment, and embedded biases. These structural flaws mean that while high-resource languages like English dominate, speakers of dozens of other languages face reduced accuracy and fairness. Without addressing these core hurdles, AI systems risk excluding vast segments of the global population, undermining the equitable application of technology in critical sectors like education and healthcare. The review highlights that current models struggle to create universal meaning representations, often suffering from a "curse of multilinguality" where performance degrades as language diversity increases. Furthermore, models inevitably absorb social and cultural biases from their training texts, leading to potentially harmful outputs. This imbalance suggests that simply scaling up models is insufficient; instead, the focus must shift toward creating balanced, diverse corpora and developing stronger alignment methods that treat all languages with equal rigor and respect. This research is vital to the open data movement because it underscores that data equity is a prerequisite for ethical AI. It calls for open, transparent, and diverse datasets that empower underrepresented language communities rather than reinforcing existing digital divides. By advocating for balanced data and robust debiasing techniques, the article supports the open data principle that information should be accessible and usable by all, ensuring that technological advancements benefit everyone, not just a linguistic elite.
