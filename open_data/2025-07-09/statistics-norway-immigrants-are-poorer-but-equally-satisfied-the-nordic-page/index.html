Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

The article reveals a significant paradox in Norwegian society: despite immigrants facing substantially poorer living conditions, they report life satisfaction and institutional trust comparable to or exceeding the native population. This finding highlights a critical disconnect between objective socioeconomic indicators, such as income and housing quality, and subjective well-being. For open data initiatives, this underscores the necessity of combining statistical metrics with qualitative survey data to fully capture social dynamics, as relying solely on economic figures would mask the resilience and social integration of immigrant communities. Furthermore, the data identifies specific vulnerabilities regarding mental health, noting that immigrants experience higher rates of psychological distress and loneliness compared to the general populace. However, these challenges coexist with a strong sense of belonging and high confidence in national institutions. This complexity demonstrates that open data must be disaggregated to reveal nuanced realities, ensuring that policy decisions address both the disparities in material living standards and the unique psychological needs of diverse demographic groups within a society.
