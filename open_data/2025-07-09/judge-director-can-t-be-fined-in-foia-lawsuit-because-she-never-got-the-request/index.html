Judge: Director can't be fined in FOIA lawsuit because she never got the request

Judge: Director can't be fined in FOIA lawsuit because she never got the request

A judge ruled that a legislative executive director cannot be fined for failing to respond to a Freedom of Information Act request because she never received it. The court determined the request was sent to an incorrect email address, and since the director acted in good faith and complied once the error was known, she is not liable for penalties. This decision highlights the critical importance of precise communication channels in public administration. The ruling underscores a significant challenge in open data accessibility: the burden of proof regarding "receipt" often falls on the citizen rather than the agency. If courts require individuals to prove their requests were delivered, it creates a barrier to transparency, especially when requests are made electronically or verbally. This legal standard can inadvertently shield government bodies from accountability if administrative errors or outdated contact information go unnoticed. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates as it illustrates how technical or procedural missteps can hinder the flow of public information. It suggests that robust, centralized, and clearly communicated contact protocols are essential for ensuring transparency. Without such systems, citizens may face unnecessary legal hurdles to access basic government records, ultimately undermining the principles of open governance and public trust.
