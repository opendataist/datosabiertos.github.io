Fire service warned of concrete collapse risk last year, emails show

Revealed correspondence indicates that Scottish officials received warnings about the structural dangers of reinforced autoclaved aerated concrete over a year ago. The Scottish Fire and Rescue Service highlighted risks of collapse and fire instability, yet the government opted for a slow review process while England closed schools immediately. This delay has been criticized as a wasted opportunity to proactively address the hazard. The documents show that local councils, particularly West Lothian, had already identified affected buildings and initiated safety measures. Despite these local efforts and explicit alerts from fire services, central authorities did not act swiftly to implement a national response. This disconnect suggests a failure to utilize early warnings effectively, leaving public safety at risk during a critical period for assessment. This issue is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how freedom of information requests can expose systemic governance failures. Transparency allows citizens and opposition parties to hold leaders accountable for inaction when early risk indicators were available. It underscores the importance of accessible public records in ensuring that safety concerns are not ignored and that government decisions are scrutinized against known evidence.
