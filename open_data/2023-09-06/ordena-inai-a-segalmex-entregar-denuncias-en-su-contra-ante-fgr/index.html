Ordena INAI a Segalmex entregar denuncias en su contra ante FGR

The National Institute for Transparency has ordered the Mexican Agency for Food Security to release documents concerning complaints filed against Diconsa and Liconsa. This directive addresses specific claims made by the agency’s director regarding the status of these legal actions, which had previously been withheld under the justification of protecting ongoing administrative investigations. The ruling prioritizes public access over institutional secrecy, ensuring transparency in the management and safeguarding of public resources against misuse. The decision underscores the critical importance of accountability in government operations, particularly where significant funds are at stake. By revoking the initial five-year confidentiality period, the institute highlights that the public interest in detecting corruption outweighs the benefits of withholding information. This move challenges the narrative that restricting access to data is necessary for effective internal controls, demonstrating instead that openness is fundamental to identifying and addressing serious irregularities. This case is highly relevant to open data because it establishes a precedent that data related to potential crimes and public spending cannot be arbitrarily obscured by bureaucratic procedures. It reinforces the principle that transparency mechanisms must actively dismantle barriers to information, ensuring that citizens and oversight bodies can scrutinize government actions. Ultimately, it validates open data as an essential tool for combating corruption and maintaining democratic trust in public institutions.
