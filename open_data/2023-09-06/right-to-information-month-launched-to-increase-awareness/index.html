Right to Information Month launched to increase awareness

Ghana’s Right to Information Commission has launched an awareness campaign centered on the critical role of digital platforms in democratizing access to information. By highlighting the online space as a catalyst for transparency, the initiative seeks to empower citizens to engage more effectively with public institutions. This focus underscores a shift toward leveraging technology to break down barriers, ensuring that information held by government bodies and publicly funded organizations is more accessible and responsive to the needs of the populace. The campaign emphasizes that digital transformation is not merely a technological upgrade but a fundamental change in how accountability and good governance are practiced. By fostering deeper dialogue and interactive sessions, the Commission aims to address challenges such as misinformation, privacy concerns, and the need for digital literacy. The goal is to cultivate an environment where citizens are active participants in democracy, capable of verifying information and holding institutions to higher standards of transparency through modern communication channels. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the practical application of open data principles in enhancing civic engagement and governance. It highlights the necessity of accessible, accurate, and authentic digital information as a cornerstone of modern democratic societies. Understanding this context is vital for open data advocates, as it demonstrates how structured, open information systems can reduce inequality, combat disinformation, and empower communities to make informed decisions, thereby reinforcing the value of transparent data ecosystems.
