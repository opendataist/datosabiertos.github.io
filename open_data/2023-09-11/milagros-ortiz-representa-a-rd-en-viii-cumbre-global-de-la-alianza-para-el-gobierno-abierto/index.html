Milagros Ortiz representa a RD en VIII Cumbre Global de la Alianza para el Gobierno Abierto

The adoption of the International Open Data Charter by the Dominican Republic signifies a strategic commitment to strengthening democratic resilience and institutional integrity. By aligning with global open government standards, the nation acknowledges that transparency is not merely a technical requirement but a fundamental pillar for rebuilding citizen trust. This move addresses the growing disillusionment with political systems by fostering a collaborative environment where civil society and government entities can jointly tackle corruption and enhance public service quality through shared accountability. The implementation of open data policies aims to create significant social and economic value by ensuring information is freely accessible, reusable, and redistributable. This approach directly supports the Inter-American Program for Open Data, empowering both state institutions and citizens to prevent and combat corruption effectively. By prioritizing data accessibility, the government seeks to stimulate sustainable development and improve the overall quality of life, transforming raw data into a tangible asset for community prosperity and informed decision-making. This article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the tangible political and social implications of adopting international transparency frameworks. It demonstrates how open government initiatives are increasingly linked to broader democratic goals, such as reducing corruption and enhancing civic participation. The case of the Dominican Republic serves as a practical example of how structured data policies can operationalize the principles of open data, proving that transparency is essential for building resilient, inclusive, and accountable governance systems in a digital era.
