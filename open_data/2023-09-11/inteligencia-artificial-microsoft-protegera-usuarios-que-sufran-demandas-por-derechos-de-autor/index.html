Inteligencia artificial: Microsoft protegerá usuarios que sufran demandas por derechos de autor

Microsoft has announced that it will assume legal responsibility for copyright infringement lawsuits when its commercial customers use Copilot. This measure aims to eliminate the legal uncertainty that could hinder the adoption of generative artificial intelligence in corporate environments. By protecting organizations, the company promotes the dissemination of knowledge and technological development, ensuring that creators continue to receive fair compensation for their works. This decision represents a paradigm shift in digital risk management, transferring the burden of intellectual property from the user to the tool provider. Microsoft commits to defending and indemnifying its clients if they are sued, provided they have used the integrated security safeguards. This approach prioritizes the trust necessary for companies to integrate these technologies into their daily workflows without fear of significant penalties. This initiative is relevant to the open data community because it sets a precedent regarding who is liable for data and content generated by automated systems. As AI is trained on both public and private information, clarifying liability is crucial to maintaining transparency and trust in digital platforms. This model could influence future regulations, defining the ethical and legal boundaries of artificial intelligence use within the shared information ecosystem.
