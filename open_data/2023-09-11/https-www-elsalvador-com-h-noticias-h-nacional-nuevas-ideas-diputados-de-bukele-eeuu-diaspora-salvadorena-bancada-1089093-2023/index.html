The article highlights a significant tension between political maneuvering and constitutional integrity in El Salvador, focusing on legislative efforts to promote President Nayib Bukele’s re-election despite explicit constitutional prohibitions. This initiative underscores a broader trend in which the executive branch actively challenges established democratic norms, using state resources and political influence to reshape legal frameworks for personal political gain, rather than adhering to the principle of alternating power. Public opinion data reveals a concerning shift in societal values, with the president’s figure surpassing even religion and the Constitution in national priority. This “cult of personality” indicates that the current political climate has prioritized individual leadership over institutional stability, suggesting that the populace’s trust is increasingly placed in a single leader rather than in the democratic systems designed to protect them. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparency mechanisms can fail when political will overrides the rule of law. It emphasizes the critical need for independent, accessible data monitoring to expose discrepancies between official rhetoric and constitutional reality, ensuring that citizen oversight can identify and counteract the erosion of democratic safeguards when standard institutional checks are compromised.
