El padrón electoral en riesgo (…otra vez)

The electoral register constitutes the backbone of democracy, ensuring the integrity of electoral processes by guaranteeing that only eligible citizens vote and preventing fraud. Its reliability is indispensable for the exercise of political rights, from participation to the formation of political parties, and it determines the operational logistics of elections, being fundamental to maintaining public confidence in the legitimacy of elections. The history of this registry shows its evolution into one of the most reliable worldwide, born out of a crisis of credibility to eradicate political manipulation. However, there are significant risks in attempting to transfer its management to governments or link it to centralized biometric identity systems, as proposed by certain current legislative reforms. The executive branch's dependence on the custody of these data opens the door to possible partisan interventions, compromising the neutrality that has characterized the electoral system. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the tension between transparency, security, and the sovereignty of citizen information. It highlights the importance of maintaining independent electoral registers free from political interference, emphasizing that the protection of personal data is vital for preserving democracy. The discussion reflects global challenges on how to manage large sensitive databases without sacrificing institutional trust or individual privacy.
