Consistent location tagging is essential for accurate spatial analysis, yet discrepancies in naming conventions, such as using outdated political titles or varying regional definitions, create significant barriers to interoperability and comparative research. For open data initiatives to be truly effective, they must adhere to strict normalization protocols. This includes maintaining a single, up-to-date canonical name for each region and ensuring that hierarchical relationships between countries, states, and zip codes are clearly defined. Without these standards, datasets from different sources cannot be reliably merged, leading to fragmented insights and potential errors in policy-making or academic study that depends on precise geographic context.
