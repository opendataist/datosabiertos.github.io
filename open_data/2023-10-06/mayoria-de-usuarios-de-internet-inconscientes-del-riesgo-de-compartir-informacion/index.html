Mayoría de usuarios de internet, inconscientes del riesgo de compartir información

Martha Patricia Larrarte Castañeda warns that technological advancements in digital advertising carry significant risks when personal data is misused. Sophisticated cybercriminals exploit these systems for fraud and identity theft, often bypassing legal consent frameworks by impersonating legitimate entities. The article highlights how users unknowingly facilitate these crimes by sharing private information and photos on social media without understanding the security implications. A primary driver of this vulnerability is user negligence, where individuals fail to configure privacy settings or ignore data protection policies to access digital services. Criminals leverage these oversights to extract sensitive details such as locations, vehicle license plates, and even biometric data from simple interactions. This lack of awareness transforms casual digital footprints into entry points for serious illicit activities, demonstrating the critical gap between public awareness and technical reality. This narrative is crucial for open data discussions because it exposes the dark side of data accessibility. While open data promotes transparency, this context reveals how poorly protected personal information can be weaponized. It underscores the urgent need for robust ethical guidelines and security standards in data ecosystems, ensuring that the benefits of shared information do not come at the cost of individual privacy and safety.
