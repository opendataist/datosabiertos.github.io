SAG-AFTRA has rejected the non-consensual use of artificial intelligence by major studios, denouncing the fact that actors’ intellectual property is being used to train algorithms without providing any economic benefits. This conflict over copyright and residuals represents the main obstacle in union negotiations, highlighting an ethical crisis in today’s entertainment industry. Public complaints from stars such as Tom Hanks and Zelda Williams illustrate the seriousness of the problem, where deepfakes and voice recreations are created without approval. These actions underscore the urgency of protecting human identity and talent from indiscriminate automation, marking a turning point for image and voice rights in the digital realm. This situation is relevant to the open data movement because it highlights the critical need for transparency and consent in the use of personal data to feed algorithmic models. By exposing how images and voices are used without authorization, the case reinforces the demand for ethical frameworks that protect the integrity of individual data—a fundamental principle for ensuring accountability and fairness in any open or closed data ecosystem.
