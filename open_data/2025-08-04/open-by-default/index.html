Open by default

Open by default

The article illustrates how the lack of formal access to information laws leaves citizens vulnerable and anxious, as seen in the unresolved doubts following a residential gas explosion. It argues that a Freedom of Information Bill is essential not merely for journalists, but for everyday individuals to seek accountability regarding safety, land use, and environmental hazards. This legal framework would empower citizens to demand transparency in government decisions that directly impact their daily lives and well-being. Beyond individual rights, the text highlights how open data and transparency laws drive broader societal and economic benefits. By shifting government culture from secrecy to an "open by default" approach, public data can be leveraged by businesses, researchers, and innovators. The author demonstrates that when government datasets, such as restaurant hygiene grades, are made accessible, private companies can integrate them into consumer apps, creating indirect value for the public and fostering digital transformation without requiring citizens to understand the underlying governance mechanisms. Relevance to open_data lies in the argument that legislative mandates are crucial for sustaining open data initiatives. Currently, bureaucratic hurdles and secrecy laws discourage civil servants from sharing even non-sensitive information. Establishing an open data framework through law ensures that public records are proactively disclosed and easily accessible, unlocking the full value of government work. This approach transforms raw public data into a resource that enhances governance, supports innovation, and ensures that the benefits of transparency are felt by all members of society.
