AMLO bajó la desigualdad social con el aumento al salario mínimo, la Pensión del Bienestar y otros programas sociales que continuó Claudia Sheinbaum; alcanza nivel histórico más bajo en 2024, según Inegi

The article reports that Mexico achieved its lowest income inequality levels since 1984, driven primarily by robust growth in wages and social transfers. This significant reduction in disparity highlights the effectiveness of policies focused on boosting household earnings and providing direct support to lower-income families, fundamentally altering the economic landscape by ensuring that the poorest segments of society experienced substantially higher income growth compared to wealthier groups. Despite the overall improvement in distribution metrics, the data reveals that severe economic gaps and gender pay disparities remain persistent challenges within the national structure. While average household incomes have risen considerably, particularly relative to pre-pandemic levels, the contrast between the highest and lowest income deciles underscores that structural inequities have not been fully resolved, indicating that progress in aggregate statistics does not automatically equate to uniform well-being across all demographics. This report is highly relevant to open data as it demonstrates the critical role of high-quality, longitudinal statistical transparency in evaluating public policy impact. By making granular data on income distribution, expenditure patterns, and demographic disparities publicly accessible, institutions enable independent analysis, foster accountability, and allow citizens to verify whether macroeconomic claims align with lived realities, thereby strengthening democratic oversight and informed civic engagement.
