Calculator: How rich are the French?

Insee’s latest report reveals that French household wealth is heavily skewed toward real estate, which constitutes the majority of total assets. While net wealth remains substantial, significant disparities exist across age groups and income levels, with older generations holding far more value than younger adults. The data highlights that property ownership, often inherited due to specific legal structures, drives much of the national patrimony rather than liquid savings. This concentration underscores a critical nuance in understanding economic equity. The poorest half of the population owns only a tiny fraction of national assets, contrasting sharply with the top tiers. This inequality is not merely about income but reflects accumulated asset values, particularly real estate. Such insights are vital for policymakers aiming to address wealth distribution gaps that persist despite relatively average salary figures. These findings are highly relevant to open data initiatives because they illustrate the importance of granular, transparent statistics in revealing hidden social realities. By making detailed wealth breakdowns accessible, open data allows citizens and researchers to challenge simplistic narratives about equality. It enables a deeper analysis of how inheritance laws and asset structures influence societal wealth, fostering more informed public discourse and evidence-based policy discussions regarding economic justice.
