El consumo público sostiene al PIB tras la caída del sector privado y hace dudar al INE

El consumo público sostiene al PIB tras la caída del sector privado y hace dudar al INE

The article reveals that the reported robust economic growth for Spain in 2022 is provisional and highly unstable, largely because it relies heavily on public expenditure that is not yet fully measured. This uncertainty highlights a critical flaw in how economic health is currently assessed, as the surge in official figures depends on incomplete data regarding government spending rather than a genuine recovery driven by private consumption or business activity, which actually contracted. This situation has significant implications for open data transparency and trust in statistical institutions. The frequent and substantial revisions of the same year’s data demonstrate that initial estimates can be misleading, potentially created to serve political narratives rather than reflecting reality. Such volatility undermines the reliability of public statistics, emphasizing the urgent need for more rigorous, timely, and accessible data sources that allow independent verification rather than relying on delayed or partial information. Ultimately, this case underscores the necessity for open data initiatives to prioritize completeness and timeliness in public sector metrics. When key economic indicators depend on variables that are not immediately available, the resulting data becomes fragile and susceptible to manipulation. Strengthening open data frameworks to ensure that government expenditure and other public inputs are tracked and released in real-time would prevent such drastic post-hoc corrections and restore confidence in national economic reporting.
