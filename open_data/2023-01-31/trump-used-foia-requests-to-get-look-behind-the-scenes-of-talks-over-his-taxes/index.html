Trump Used FOIA Requests to Get Look Behind the Scenes of Talks Over His Taxes

The article reveals that former President Donald Trump utilized Freedom of Information Act requests to obstruct the release of his personal tax returns, seeking insight into the IRS’s internal deliberations. This strategy highlights an ironic misuse of public records laws; rather than relying on the statutory powers granted to the executive branch for data access, Trump employed a transparency tool typically reserved for citizens to delay and complicate the congressional request for his financial history. This unconventional approach allowed the former president to delay the IRS review process while ensuring his returns remained confidential until he left office. The subsequent release of six years of tax documents exposed that he paid little to no federal income taxes during his presidency, despite numerous potential audit risks. The IRS never completed an examination of Trump while in office, raising significant questions about the agency’s independence and the effectiveness of internal safeguards against political interference. This incident is critically relevant to the open data community as it demonstrates how legal mechanisms designed for transparency can be weaponized to withhold accountability. It underscores the importance of robust open data policies that prevent powerful individuals from exploiting procedural loopholes to hide information from the public. The case serves as a cautionary tale for policymakers, illustrating the need to distinguish between legitimate information requests and tactics used to obstruct democratic oversight and financial transparency.
