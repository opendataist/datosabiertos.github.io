Exempted Organisations Can’t Be Asked To Disclose Outcome Of Complaint About Money Laundering, Hawala Transactions Under RTI Act: Delhi High Court

The Delhi High Court has clarified that intelligence and security organizations exempt under the RTI Act cannot be compelled to disclose complaint outcomes unless the allegations specifically involve corruption or human rights violations. This ruling establishes a strict boundary for transparency, determining that economic offenses like money laundering or tax evasion fall outside the exceptions to exemption. Consequently, applicants cannot access investigative results from these agencies through standard RTI channels, even if the Central Information Commission had previously suggested otherwise. This decision is highly relevant to open data advocates because it highlights the critical distinction between general government transparency and the secrecy surrounding national security apparatuses. It underscores that while the RTI Act aims for accountability, it explicitly carves out significant blind spots for entities tasked with maintaining security. The judgment reinforces the legal framework that prioritizes state secrecy over public scrutiny for specific types of financial crimes, limiting the scope of accessible public information. Ultimately, the case illustrates the challenges in balancing transparency with security mandates. For open data initiatives, this precedent emphasizes the need for precise legal categorization when seeking information. It warns that assuming all government operations are equally subject to disclosure requests is incorrect, as specific statutory exemptions protect certain sensitive areas from public review, thereby restricting the flow of open data regarding economic intelligence operations.
