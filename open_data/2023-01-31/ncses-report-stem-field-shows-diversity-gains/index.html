NCSES Report: STEM Field Shows Diversity Gains

The National Science Foundation’s latest report reveals a complex landscape for diversity in U.S. STEM fields. While there has been measurable progress in the representation of women and certain minority groups in both education and employment over the past decade, significant disparities remain. Underrepresented minorities and people with disabilities continue to be underrepresented relative to their share of the general population, highlighting persistent equity challenges within the sector. A notable methodological shift in this year’s analysis is the broader definition of STEM work, which now includes technical roles that do not require a bachelor’s degree. This expansion provides a more comprehensive view of the workforce, revealing that while underrepresented groups make up a substantial portion of non-degree STEM jobs, these positions often suffer from lower wages and higher unemployment. Conversely, women remain significantly underrepresented in high-paying, degree-required fields like engineering and computer science, indicating that progress is uneven across different occupational sectors. This data is critically relevant to open data initiatives because it underscores the importance of accessible, comprehensive, and well-defined datasets in tracking social equity. By expanding definitions and providing transparent metrics on inclusion, the report demonstrates how open data can expose systemic gaps and inform policy decisions. The release of such detailed, objective information empowers researchers, policymakers, and advocates to identify specific areas where intervention is needed to foster a more equitable and innovative scientific community.
