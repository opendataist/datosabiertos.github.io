Amnistía Internacional urge a México cumplir sentencia de CorteIDH sobre arraigo y prisión preventiva

The Inter-American Court of Human Rights has ruled against Mexico’s use of *arraigo* and mandatory preventive imprisonment, declaring them incompatible with international human rights standards. Amnesty International urges immediate constitutional reform to eliminate these measures, emphasizing that they violate the presumption of innocence and foster unjust detention based on suspicion rather than evidence. This decision establishes a crucial legal precedent for the Mexican justice system, demanding alignment with global norms. This ruling highlights the systemic failure of current legal mechanisms, which prioritize incarceration over actual justice. By removing these coercive tools, the legal framework can better protect personal liberty and integrity, preventing the prolonged detention of individuals without proven guilt. The implication is a necessary shift toward due process, ensuring that deprivation of liberty is strictly regulated and justified, thereby reducing the human rights violations embedded in current judicial practices. This development is highly relevant to open data initiatives because transparency and accountability are essential for monitoring state compliance. Access to reliable, disaggregated data on detentions, court rulings, and prison populations allows civil society and international bodies to verify whether the reforms are effectively implemented. Open data serves as a critical tool to expose remaining systemic abuses, track the reduction of unjust imprisonments, and ensure that the judicial system truly adheres to the human rights protections mandated by the court’s historic judgment.
