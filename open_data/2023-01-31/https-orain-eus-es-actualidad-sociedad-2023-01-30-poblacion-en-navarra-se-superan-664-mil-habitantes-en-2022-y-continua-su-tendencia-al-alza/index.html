Navarre has recorded a sustained increase in its registered population, driven primarily by immigration, which has significantly diversified the region’s demographic profile. The Chartered Community shows a growing proportion of foreign-born residents, with a notable shift toward Asian and African origins, reflecting changes in migration flows in recent years. This external dynamism contrasts with the natural aging of the native population, marking a clear generational dichotomy within the territory. The data reveal that the foreign-born population is considerably younger than the native population, concentrating mainly in working-age groups and slightly reducing the relative weight of older age groups in the overall society. This asymmetric age structure poses significant challenges for the sustainability of welfare systems and the planning of public services, as the demand for elderly care increases while the younger generations contributing human capital are largely immigrants. This information is crucial for open data, as it illustrates how official administrative records enable the cross-referencing of complex demographic variables to analyze deep social trends. By accessing disaggregated data by nationality and age, researchers and citizens can better understand social integration and the true composition of the community, promoting transparency and evidence-based analytical capacity to improve public governance.
