Sedena debe transparentar contrataciones, pide el INAI

Sedena debe transparentar contrataciones, pide el INAI

The National Institute for Transparency ruled that the Secretary of National Defense must release documents concerning contracts for remote information monitoring services. This decision underscores that such state activities likely require judicial orders to respect constitutional rights to communication privacy. By disclosing these contracts, the government ensures transparency in the use of public resources and reveals the scope of surveillance measures. Citizens must be able to evaluate how their rights to privacy and free expression are protected. Authorities have an obligation to provide detailed information about surveillance services, not just general procedures, so that the public can understand the legal application of communication interception. This level of detail is essential for democratic accountability and trust in state institutions. This case is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical need for transparent, accessible public records regarding government surveillance. Open data initiatives can facilitate civic oversight of sensitive security contracts, ensuring that digital privacy rights are safeguarded through verifiable transparency. It demonstrates how making specific administrative data available empowers society to monitor potential abuses of power.
