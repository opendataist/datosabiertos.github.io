El PP pide más transparencia con las pensiones: si el Gobierno no da más datos, votará en contra

The People’s Party is inclined to oppose the pension reform due to the government’s lack of transparency and refusal to share underlying economic data beyond a basic presentation. This opacity prevents adequate scrutiny of the policy’s technical viability, which is already questioned by independent analyses. Furthermore, the reform is criticized for being implemented unilaterally, bypassing necessary social and economic agreements and excluding the consensus of the Pact of Toledo. Consequently, the party demands a rigorous, documented justification to understand the financial foundations before casting its vote. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it highlights how withholding transparent, accessible information undermines democratic accountability. True public trust requires more than simplified summaries; it demands the release of comprehensive datasets that allow for independent verification and meaningful civic engagement with major legislative changes.
