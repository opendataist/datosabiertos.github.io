La OCD cuestiona que el INE niegue presencia de “veedores” en el censo experimental

The tension between the Citizen Observation of Democracy (OCD) and the National Institute of Statistics (INE) highlights the conflict between state technical control and citizen participation in national statistical processes. The INE rejects the presence of local observers, citing regulations that protect the confidentiality of individual data and stipulate that only the State can oversee the census. This stance limits civil society’s ability to exercise its right to transparency and direct supervision during the experimental phase, creating a disconnect between institutional management and public expectations for access to information. This discrepancy reveals a significant barrier to accountability in the production of official data. By denying citizen observation, the INE not only restricts the democratic exercise of social oversight but also misses a crucial opportunity to incorporate external recommendations that could improve the logistics and public perception of the census operation. The lack of alignment between departmental and national authorities regarding the existence of observer roles adds confusion, underscoring the need for clear regulations to balance data protection with informational openness. This case is relevant to the open data movement because it illustrates the structural challenges to governmental transparency. If statistical institutions close their processes to independent scrutiny, public trust is weakened and the democratic quality of the generated information is diminished. Promoting models where civil society can verify methods without accessing sensitive data is essential to ensure censuses that are inclusive, reliable, and truly serve the citizenry.
