Quintana Roo, entidad federativa con menos muertes por cada 100 mil habitantes en 2024: INEGI - Noticaribe

Quintana Roo, entidad federativa con menos muertes por cada 100 mil habitantes en 2024: INEGI - Noticaribe

The article reveals a significant national increase in mortality in Mexico, driven primarily by chronic non-communicable diseases such as heart conditions, diabetes, and cancer. These health issues represent the leading causes of death across all genders and ages, particularly affecting individuals over 65. The data highlights a critical public health burden, indicating that lifestyle-related illnesses are overtaking infectious diseases as the primary drivers of mortality in the country. Regional disparities are evident in how these health crises manifest geographically. While some states exhibit higher standardized rates for specific causes like heart disease or suicide, the overall trend points to a widespread need for preventive healthcare strategies. The report underscores that despite variations between entities, the structural dominance of cardiovascular and metabolic disorders remains a consistent challenge nationwide, requiring targeted policy interventions that address both treatment and prevention. This dataset is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the power of granular, publicly accessible statistical information in driving evidence-based decision-making. By making detailed mortality records available, institutions like INEGI enable researchers, policymakers, and civil society to identify health inequalities and allocate resources more effectively. This transparency fosters accountability and supports the development of data-driven solutions to improve public health outcomes and address the social determinants of health.
