Spain’s housing crisis is clearly evident in the substantial increase in average mortgage amounts, signaling that families are bearing significantly heavier financial burdens to purchase homes. This trend is especially pronounced in regions such as Madrid and Andalusia, where foreign investment further drives up price inflation, making homeownership increasingly unaffordable for local buyers. The relevance to open data lies in the need for transparent, detailed public records to track these socioeconomic changes. By analyzing accessible datasets on credit trends and regional disparities, policymakers and citizens can identify systemic inequalities and hold institutions accountable. Ultimately, this article highlights how data exposes the rising cost of living. Open data initiatives are essential for democratizing information, enabling society to grasp the true impact of housing policies and fostering informed debate on social welfare and economic sustainability.
