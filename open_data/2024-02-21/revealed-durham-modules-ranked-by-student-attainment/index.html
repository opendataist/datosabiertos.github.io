Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

This article demonstrates how Freedom of Information Requests can unlock granular academic data, revealing significant disparities in student attainment across different university modules. By analyzing average marks and grade distributions, it highlights that Physics and English achieved the highest overall performance, while foundation years and specific languages lagged behind. This transparency allows for a deeper understanding of how various disciplines and course structures influence student success, offering critical insights for educational stakeholders. The data further illustrates stark variations in performance at different undergraduate levels, from zero first-class honors in certain first-year art modules to exceptional success in third-year logic and second-year classics. Such detailed breakdowns expose specific strengths and weaknesses within the curriculum, suggesting that some teaching methods or assessment formats may inherently favor certain outcomes. These findings underscore the importance of examining granular performance metrics rather than relying solely on broad institutional averages to gauge academic quality. This analysis is highly relevant to the open data movement as it exemplifies the power of public sector information in enhancing educational accountability. When universities make detailed module-level data accessible, it empowers prospective students, policymakers, and researchers to evaluate institutional effectiveness with greater precision. Ultimately, this case serves as a model for how leveraging open data can drive informed decision-making and foster a more transparent academic environment, encouraging continuous improvement in teaching standards and student support systems.
