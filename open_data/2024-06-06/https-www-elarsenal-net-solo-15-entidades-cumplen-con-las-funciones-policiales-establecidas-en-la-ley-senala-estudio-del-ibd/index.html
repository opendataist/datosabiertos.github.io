A study reveals that few Mexican states align their police forces with national security mandates, highlighting a systemic gap between legal frameworks and actual deployment. This misalignment suggests that police operations often lack the integrated approach to prevention and social proximity required by law. Consequently, the absence of standardized functions across most entities undermines public safety efficacy and accountability. This disconnect emphasizes the urgent need for comprehensive data transparency regarding police personnel allocation and role definitions to ensure legal compliance and improve community trust in law enforcement institutions. This article is vital for open data, as it demonstrates how structured, accessible statistical information from government censuses can expose governance inefficiencies. By making these discrepancies visible, open data empowers civil society to demand better resource management and standardized public security practices.
