Q&A: Xiao Qiang on the anniversary of Tiananmen Square and the right to information in China

Q&A: Xiao Qiang on the anniversary of Tiananmen Square and the right to information in China

The article highlights the release of the "Statement of Principles for the Right to Information in China" by Circle 19, a coalition of independent media experts. This manifesto asserts that access to information is historically intrinsic to Chinese society and poses a critical threat to the Chinese Communist Party’s authoritarian legitimacy. By challenging the state’s monopoly on truth, the document argues that the suppression of reliable information endangers China’s future and underscores the regime’s reliance on censorship to maintain power. At the core of this suppression is the "Great Firewall," which acts as an invisible wall comparable to the historical Berlin Wall, preventing citizens from accessing uncensored global news and dissenting voices. The text emphasizes that the regime views an open information environment as existential danger, particularly regarding historical events like the Tiananmen Square protests. Consequently, the firewall and broader censorship mechanisms are maintained not merely for control, but to prevent the widespread circulation of content that could undermine the government’s narrative and invite demands for political liberalization. This struggle for free expression is directly relevant to open data as it illustrates the severe barriers to accessing unbiased information in heavily censored regions. The efforts of activists to archive censored materials and develop circumvention technologies demonstrate the vital role of independent data preservation and open access in resisting authoritarian control. For the open data community, this context reinforces the importance of supporting information freedom, as unrestricted data flow is essential for accountability, historical truth, and the realization of universal human rights in digitally isolated societies.
