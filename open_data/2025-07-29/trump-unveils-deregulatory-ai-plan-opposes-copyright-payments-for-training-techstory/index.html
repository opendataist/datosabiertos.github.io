Trump Unveils Deregulatory AI Plan, Opposes Copyright Payments for Training - TechStory

Trump Unveils Deregulatory AI Plan, Opposes Copyright Payments for Training - TechStory

Donald Trump argues that AI companies should not be required to pay for copyrighted materials used in training models, likening it to natural learning. This stance prioritizes rapid American innovation over the concerns of authors and publishers, warning that strict copyright enforcement would hinder competitiveness against China. By positioning IP rights as secondary to national strategic goals, the administration seeks to remove regulatory hurdles that could slow down the development of US AI technology. The "America’s AI Action Plan" aggressively promotes infrastructure growth by easing environmental regulations and streamlining permits for data centers. It also seeks to establish a unified federal standard for AI governance, discouraging state-level restrictions that might fragment the market. This approach emphasizes deregulation and global expansion, aiming to secure US leadership in AI markets by allowing domestic companies to operate with fewer constraints and deploy technologies more freely worldwide. This article is highly relevant to open_data because it highlights a political shift that favors unrestricted data access and algorithmic freedom over privacy or licensing restrictions. By downplaying copyright and focusing on infrastructure, the policy environment may increasingly prioritize the free flow of data and computational resources. This creates both opportunities for open data innovation and risks regarding the unchecked use of information, shaping how data ecosystems balance commercial interests with ethical and legal standards.
