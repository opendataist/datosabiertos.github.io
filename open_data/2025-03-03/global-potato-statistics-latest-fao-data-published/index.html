Global potato statistics: Latest FAO data published

The article highlights the FAO’s FAOSTAT database as a critical resource for accessing comprehensive global agricultural statistics, specifically focusing on potato production metrics. It underscores the importance of using standardized, internationally recognized datasets to analyze food security and agricultural trends. The narrative emphasizes that while official national figures may vary slightly, FAO data provides a sufficiently accurate baseline for most analytical purposes, enabling researchers and policymakers to conduct independent searches and derive meaningful insights without relying solely on localized reports. China and India emerge as the dominant producers, illustrating how centralized data collection allows for clear identification of major contributors to global supply chains. By presenting production volumes and harvested areas across nearly a hundred countries, the article demonstrates the utility of open data in revealing economic disparities and agricultural efficiencies worldwide. This comparative perspective helps stakeholders understand regional dependencies and potential vulnerabilities in the potato sector, fostering a more informed dialogue on sustainable farming practices and trade dynamics. This resource is highly relevant to the open data community because it exemplifies how transparent, accessible datasets empower global analysis. By making complex agricultural information publicly available, the FAO enables diverse users to verify claims, perform custom analyses, and contribute to evidence-based decision-making. The article serves as a practical guide on navigating these open repositories, reinforcing the value of interoperable standards and free access to vital socio-economic indicators for driving innovation and accountability in the global food system.
