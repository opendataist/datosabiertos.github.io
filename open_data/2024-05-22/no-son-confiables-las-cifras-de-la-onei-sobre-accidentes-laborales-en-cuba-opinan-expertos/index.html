No son confiables las cifras de la ONEI sobre accidentes laborales en Cuba, opinan expertos

No son confiables las cifras de la ONEI sobre accidentes laborales en Cuba, opinan expertos

The article highlights a significant discrepancy between official Cuban labor accident statistics and independent assessments, suggesting that the reported decline in workplace incidents is largely illusory. Experts argue that the government systematically manipulates data by excluding accidents involving private sector workers and certain transportation incidents from labor statistics. This lack of transparency and the absence of an internationally recognized recording system make official figures unreliable, obscuring the true scale of occupational hazards faced by Cuban employees. Furthermore, the analysis reveals that structural failures, such as prolonged power outages and inadequate safety equipment, are creating dangerous working conditions that likely increase accident rates. The exclusion of private enterprises from safety reporting means that risks in cooperatives and small businesses remain invisible to regulators. Consequently, the official narrative of improved safety contradicts the reality of workers facing physical deterioration, excessive stress, and a severe lack of protective measures across various industries. This content is crucial for open data advocates because it demonstrates how the absence of standardized, transparent, and comprehensive data collection leads to misinformation and undermines worker safety. It emphasizes the necessity of independent verification methods and international auditing standards to ensure that public statistics accurately reflect societal conditions. Without reliable, open, and inclusive data frameworks, it is impossible to effectively address labor rights violations or implement meaningful safety reforms.
