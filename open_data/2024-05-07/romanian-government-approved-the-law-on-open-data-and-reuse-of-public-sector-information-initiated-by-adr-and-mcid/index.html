Romanian Government approved the Law on open data and reuse of public sector information, initiated by ADR and MCID

Romania has enacted new open data legislation fully transposing EU directives to modernize the national framework. This legal update aligns domestic policies with current digital advancements, ensuring that public sector information is readily available for reuse. The move establishes a robust foundation for leveraging publicly available data to drive broader economic and societal progress. The primary goal is to stimulate digital innovation, particularly within the artificial intelligence sector, by empowering private enterprises to create novel IT products and services. By removing barriers to public data, the law aims to enhance transparency and facilitate direct monitoring of administrative performance. This approach seeks to improve public services and foster a more accountable government structure through intelligent data processing. This development is vital for open data advocacy as it demonstrates how rigorous legal frameworks can successfully translate high-level digital strategies into actionable national policy. It highlights the critical role of impartial oversight bodies in guaranteeing free access to information. Ultimately, this case illustrates how open data acts as a catalyst for digital transformation, benefiting both citizens and businesses through enhanced transparency and innovation.
