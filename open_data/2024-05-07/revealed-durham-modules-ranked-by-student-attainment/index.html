Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

This article demonstrates the power of Freedom of Information requests in unlocking institutional transparency. By leveraging publicly accessible datasets, external researchers can analyze detailed academic performance metrics that are typically kept internal. This case highlights how open data initiatives empower the public to scrutinize university standards, turning raw administrative records into actionable insights for students and stakeholders. The data reveals significant disparities in student attainment across different disciplines and academic levels. Physics and English led in overall performance, while foundation years and certain language modules lagged behind. Furthermore, first-year assessment outcomes varied wildly, with some modules awarding no top grades while others saw near-universal success among third-year peers. These inconsistencies suggest that grading standards and module difficulties are not uniform across the curriculum. Relevance to open data lies in its potential to drive accountability and informed decision-making within higher education. When such detailed breakdowns of success rates and average marks are made public, it encourages institutions to examine their own practices critically. This transparency allows prospective students to make better choices and fosters a culture of continuous improvement by exposing hidden variations in educational quality and assessment rigor.
