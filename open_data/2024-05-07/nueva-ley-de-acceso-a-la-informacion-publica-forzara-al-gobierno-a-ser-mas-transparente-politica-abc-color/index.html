Nueva ley de acceso a la información pública forzará al gobierno a ser más transparente - Política - ABC Color

Nueva ley de acceso a la información pública forzará al gobierno a ser más transparente - Política - ABC Color

The President of Paraguay has partially vetoed a proposed expansion of the Public Access to Information Law, specifically rejecting a clause that would mandate the publication of detailed citizen data. The executive branch argues that this requirement infringes upon the constitutional right to privacy and conflicts with the statistical secrecy protected by the National Institute of Statistics (INE). This legal challenge creates significant uncertainty regarding the scope of transparency, highlighting the delicate balance between governmental openness and individual data protection rights. The controversial clause sought to open highly granular datasets to public scrutiny, including weekly electronic banking transactions, lists of public land beneficiaries, and detailed records of public works contracts and ministerial meetings. Additionally, the bill aimed to publish comprehensive data on public debt, prison conditions, human rights reports, and extensive environmental metrics. These proposals reflect an ambitious drive toward radical transparency, intending to provide citizens with deep insights into government operations and resource management. This dispute is critically relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how existing legal frameworks, particularly statistical secrecy laws, can clash with new transparency mandates. It underscores the necessity for open data policies to explicitly define boundaries between public accountability and personal privacy. The outcome will set a precedent for how granular administrative and financial data can be shared, influencing the development of ethical standards for data publication in governance contexts.
