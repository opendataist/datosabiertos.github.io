Lectura fácil: la puerta de acceso a los derechos de ciudadanía para las personas con discapacidad

The article highlights a successful initiative in Aragon that transforms complex administrative documents into easy-to-read formats, significantly enhancing accessibility for individuals with intellectual disabilities and reading difficulties. By adapting legal and healthcare texts, the "Gobierno Fácil" program ensures that vulnerable citizens can fully exercise their rights and participate in society. This effort goes beyond simple translation, addressing the deep barriers created by bureaucratic jargon that often exclude people from essential services and civic engagement. The project’s impact was internationally recognized when it received a top award at the World Open Government Summit in Estonia, validating its model as a best practice in Europe. This accolade underscores that cognitive accessibility is not a niche activity but a vital component of inclusive governance. It demonstrates that simplifying language has a tangible, positive effect on society, benefiting not only those with disabilities but also the elderly and non-native speakers who struggle with technical terminology. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates a critical dimension of data accessibility: user comprehension. Open data initiatives must ensure that information is not just published in raw formats but is presented in ways that diverse audiences can understand and use. By prioritizing inclusive design and collaborating with civil society groups, governments can transform opaque data into actionable knowledge, ensuring that transparency truly serves all citizens, regardless of their cognitive abilities or linguistic background.
