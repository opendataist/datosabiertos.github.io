Panamá registró una inflación de 0,2% en agosto de 2023

Recent data from Panama’s National Institute of Statistics and Census (INEC) show that urban inflation has stabilized, with a modest monthly increase that aligns with a controlled annual trend. This stability suggests that the immediate pressure on consumer purchasing power is manageable, creating a more predictable economic environment for both households and businesses. Key drivers of these price changes include significant increases in transportation costs, particularly fuel, as well as rises in food items and energy services. While some categories, such as clothing and telecommunications, experienced slight deflation, the overall upward trend in essential goods underscores the volatility inherent in basic living expenses. The nuanced shifts across different sectors indicate that inflation is not uniform, affecting certain parts of the economy more heavily than others. This report is highly relevant to open data initiatives because it exemplifies the need for transparent, accessible, and standardized statistical releases. By providing detailed breakdowns of price variations across numerous consumer categories, institutions empower researchers, journalists, and citizens to independently analyze economic trends. Such granular, publicly available data fosters accountability and enables the community to use raw information for deeper socio-economic insights, rather than relying solely on aggregated summaries.
