How UConn decided who got housing and who was waitlisted

How UConn decided who got housing and who was waitlisted

This article reveals significant disparities in University of Connecticut’s housing allocation, highlighting how administrative transparency directly impacts student equity. By ending guaranteed housing for upperclassmen while maintaining capacity for enrollment growth, the university created a system where late applicants, commuters, and long-term residents are systematically deprioritized. The reliance on Freedom of Information Act requests to expose internal prioritization criteria underscores a critical gap in open data practices, where essential institutional decisions remain obscured from public scrutiny until litigation forces disclosure. The core implication is that housing allocation functions as a scarce resource managed through opaque internal metrics rather than clear, accessible public policy. When critical data regarding waitlist priorities and demand fluctuations are withheld by default, students are left navigating complex, shifting rules without full visibility into their chances of securing accommodation. This lack of proactive data sharing erodes trust and demonstrates how institutions can inadvertently—or intentionally—maintain power by controlling information flows, preventing stakeholders from making informed decisions or holding leadership accountable for resource distribution. This case is vital for the open data community as it exemplifies the necessity of proactive government and university data disclosure. It illustrates that without mandated transparency, critical information about student welfare remains locked behind administrative barriers. Open data advocates must push for policies that require institutions to publish real-time allocation metrics, priority criteria, and demand statistics, ensuring that all community members have equitable access to the information necessary to understand and influence institutional operations.
