'No Chief Information Commissioner In CIC', Petitioners Flag Unfilled Vacancies, Supreme Court Hearing On Oct 27

'No Chief Information Commissioner In CIC', Petitioners Flag Unfilled Vacancies, Supreme Court Hearing On Oct 27

Persistent vacancies within Information Commissions established under the Right to Information Act critically undermine the machinery designed to ensure government transparency. The Supreme Court is actively addressing this non-fulfillment, noting that key posts, including the Chief Information Commissioner, remain unfilled across multiple states. This staffing crisis has paralyzed the commissions' ability to function effectively, leaving thousands of appeals and complaints unresolved. The delay in appointments directly impacts citizens' fundamental right to access information held by public authorities. Without fully constituted commissions, the accountability mechanisms intended to check executive power are weakened, leading to administrative bottlenecks and reduced public trust. The Court’s intervention highlights the urgent need for timely constitutions to prevent the erosion of these statutory safeguards. This development is vital to open data advocates, as functional Information Commissions are the primary enforcers of transparency laws. Their paralysis threatens the availability and accessibility of public records, essential components of an open data ecosystem. Ensuring these bodies are fully staffed is crucial for maintaining the integrity of citizen-led oversight and promoting a culture of radical transparency in governance.
