La pobreza registra una baja muy fuerte en Argentina: 31,6% en el primer semestre de 2025

La pobreza registra una baja muy fuerte en Argentina: 31,6% en el primer semestre de 2025

Official reports indicate a significant decline in poverty rates in Argentina during the first half of 2025. However, experts argue that these figures may overstate social relief due to inaccuracies in current measurement methodologies. The primary concern lies in the disconnect between official price indices and actual household expenditures, suggesting that consumption and salary data might not accurately reflect the true economic reality faced by citizens. This discrepancy highlights a critical issue in how economic well-being is tracked and reported. When official inflation metrics fail to align with real-world costs, policymakers risk making decisions based on flawed premises. Consequently, the perceived improvement in living standards may not fully capture the financial adjustments and sacrifices required by the middle class under current fiscal policies. This context is vital for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates the necessity of transparent and precise statistical methodologies. Reliable open data must accurately reflect ground realities to prevent misleading conclusions about social progress. Ensuring that public datasets address methodological gaps helps build trust and enables more effective, evidence-based policy-making that truly serves the population.
