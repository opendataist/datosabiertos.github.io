Intendente de Mayor Otaño niega que su ciudad sea la más pobre de Itapúa - Nacionales - ABC Color

Intendente de Mayor Otaño niega que su ciudad sea la más pobre de Itapúa - Nacionales - ABC Color

The National Institute of Statistics reports that Mayor Otaño faces significant multidimensional poverty, a finding that local authorities strongly contest. The municipal government disputes the methodology, arguing that the population data used in the study are inaccurate and underestimate the actual number of residents. This disagreement highlights critical challenges in how official statistics are compiled and validated at the municipal level. Officials emphasize the district’s robust agricultural economy and infrastructure, claiming that the reported poverty rates do not reflect local realities. By highlighting their status as a major production hub with active commerce and services, they argue that current metrics fail to capture the true socioeconomic conditions. This tension underscores the importance of precise data collection to avoid misrepresenting community welfare. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the friction between official statistical outputs and local stakeholder perceptions. It raises essential questions about data transparency, source verification, and the inclusion of granular local knowledge. Improving the alignment between national datasets and ground-level realities is crucial for creating more accurate, actionable, and trusted open data resources.
