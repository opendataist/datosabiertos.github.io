Attorney General responds to Multimedia’s request for information on missing Ghanaian fisheries observer - MyJoyOnline.com

The Attorney General’s office clarified that it does not conduct investigations but rather provides legal advice based on dockets submitted by law enforcement agencies. By stating that the police completed their work in 2021 and forwarded the case for final review, the response highlights a critical disconnect between investigative bodies and the legal framework. This distinction is vital for understanding how transparency mechanisms function, as it defines the limits of information available from the judiciary versus the executive’s investigative arm. Despite this administrative clarification, the case reveals significant gaps in accountability regarding illegal fishing activities. The disappearance of fisheries observer Emmanuel Essien, who documented illicit practices, remains unresolved after three years. The failure to secure his whereabouts or pursue justice underscores how the lack of accessible data on state investigations can hinder the exposure of environmental crimes. Without clear, timely information, the public cannot verify whether due process is being followed or if evidence is being suppressed. This article is highly relevant to open_data because it demonstrates the practical challenges of enforcing Right to Information laws in complex criminal cases. It illustrates how government responses can use procedural technicalities to withhold substantive details, thereby limiting public scrutiny. For open data initiatives, this case emphasizes the need to push for more granular, searchable data on investigation statuses and outcomes, ensuring that citizens can effectively monitor state actions and hold agencies accountable for unresolved high-stakes cases.
