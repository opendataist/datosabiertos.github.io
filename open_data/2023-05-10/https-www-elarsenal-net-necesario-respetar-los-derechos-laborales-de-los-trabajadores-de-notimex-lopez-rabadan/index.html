The potential extinction of Notimex represents a severe blow to democratic values, directly undermining the public’s right to access information and the principles of transparency. This move signals a centralization of power and media control, which threatens civil liberties and weakens the institutional framework necessary for a healthy democracy. The disappearance of such a historic public agency removes a critical reference point for objective journalism in Latin America, eroding trust in state communications. The situation highlights severe mismanagement and a lack of accountability regarding public resources. Allegations of improper fund usage and unpaid salaries suggest that administrative failures have paralyzed operations, leaving workers without rights or compensation. This negligence demonstrates how poor governance can stifle the ability of state media to provide timely and truthful news to various platforms, thereby compromising the quality of public discourse and the integrity of information dissemination. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the tangible risks when public institutions fail to maintain operational continuity and financial transparency. It underscores the necessity of robust mechanisms to protect public information infrastructure from political manipulation and administrative neglect. Ensuring that such agencies remain functional and accountable is essential for preserving the open flow of information, which is a cornerstone of an informed society and effective democratic oversight.
