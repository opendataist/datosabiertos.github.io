Details slim on potential KCSO custody death

This article highlights critical barriers to open data when law enforcement investigations override public transparency rights. Agencies across South Carolina are withholding essential records regarding a potential custody death, citing ongoing probes as justification. This demonstrates how active investigations are frequently used to delay or deny access to information that citizens have a right to view, significantly hindering accountability. The situation reveals a systemic issue where government bodies prioritize investigative secrecy over immediate public disclosure. Despite requests for personnel files and incident reports, officials maintain that materials cannot be released until the probe concludes. This creates a vacuum of information, leaving the public unaware of basic facts about deaths occurring under official supervision and undermining trust in institutional integrity. This case is highly relevant to open data advocates because it illustrates the tension between privacy, security, and transparency. It underscores the need for clearer policies that allow for the release of non-sensitive data during investigations. Without such safeguards, open data principles erode, as agencies can indefinitely withhold records, preventing journalists and citizens from fulfilling their role in monitoring government actions.
