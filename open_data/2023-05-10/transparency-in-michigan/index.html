Transparency in Michigan

Michigan’s government currently lacks transparency, ranking last nationally for openness as the state legislature and judiciary remain exempt from Freedom of Information Act laws. The governor’s office is also shielded from public scrutiny, creating a significant gap in accountability compared to other states. Although multiple bills have passed the House with bipartisan support, they consistently stall in the Senate, revealing deep political resistance to exposing official communications to the public. This legislative gridlock highlights how transparency reforms are being weaponized for political gain rather than public good. Even when both parties introduce identical legislation, the lack of cross-party cosponsorship suggests that lawmakers are prioritizing partisan maneuvering over ethical governance. This dynamic prevents meaningful progress, leaving citizens with no access to critical records despite widespread calls for reform and repeated promises from elected officials to increase openness. This situation is critically relevant to open data because it demonstrates how legal exemptions directly suppress the availability of public information. Without mandatory disclosure, government data remains hidden, hindering public oversight and trust. True open data requires not just technical capacity, but legislative will to remove barriers that protect officials from scrutiny. Ensuring that all government entities are subject to the same transparency laws is essential for fostering accountability and enabling citizens to effectively monitor their representatives.
