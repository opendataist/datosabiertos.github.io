El 26% de los partos en Durango son de menores de 20 años de edad

The statistical data from Durango reveals a significant shift in family structures, characterized by a decline in traditional marriages and a notable rise in cohabitation and single motherhood. This transformation highlights changing social norms and suggests that conventional family models are becoming less dominant in favor of more diverse household arrangements, impacting societal planning and support systems. A critical finding is the high prevalence of teenage motherhood, placing Durango among the regions with the highest rates for mothers under twenty. This demographic trend indicates pressing challenges related to youth education, healthcare access, and socioeconomic stability, requiring targeted public policies to address the unique needs and vulnerabilities of young families. Furthermore, the data emphasizes the significant reliance on public healthcare services for childbirth among single mothers, alongside varying levels of timeliness in birth registration. These insights are relevant to open data because they demonstrate how transparent, accessible statistics can identify social inequalities and guide evidence-based policymaking, ensuring that resources are effectively allocated to support vulnerable populations and improve public health outcomes.
