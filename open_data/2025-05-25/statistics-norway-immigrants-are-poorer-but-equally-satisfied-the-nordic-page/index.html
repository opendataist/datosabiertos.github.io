Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions compared to the general population, including lower income, worse employment and housing standards, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness. Despite these socioeconomic disadvantages and distinct health challenges, immigrants report being equally satisfied with their lives in Norway, suggesting a complex resilience that defies simple correlations between material conditions and personal well-being. Furthermore, this demographic exhibits strong institutional confidence, displaying higher trust in the political system, judiciary, and police than native Norwegians, alongside a deep affiliation with the country. This paradox highlights that social integration and satisfaction are not solely dependent on equal economic standing, as immigrants maintain high expectations of public institutions despite experiencing greater personal adversity. This data is vital for open_data initiatives as it underscores the necessity of granular, disaggregated statistics to reveal nuanced social realities that aggregate national averages often obscure. By making such detailed findings accessible, researchers and policymakers can better understand the multifaceted nature of immigrant integration, moving beyond simplistic metrics to address specific gaps in health, housing, and social connection through targeted, evidence-based interventions.
