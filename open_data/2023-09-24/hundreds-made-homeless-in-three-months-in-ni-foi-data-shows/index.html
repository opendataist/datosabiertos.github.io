Hundreds made homeless in three months in NI, FOI data shows

Recent Freedom of Information data reveals a sharp rise in homelessness across Northern Ireland, driven primarily by failures in the private rental sector. The figures indicate that hundreds of individuals lost their housing within a single quarter, creating a daily crisis that overwhelms social support systems and highlights the fragility of tenant protections in the current economic climate. The surge in evictions and unaffordable rents is directly linked to significant reductions in discretionary housing support, which disproportionately affect vulnerable populations in deprived areas. These cost-cutting measures have stripped essential financial safety nets from thousands of households, leading to severe distress and placing immense pressure on already strained social housing waiting lists, thereby exacerbating the broader affordability crisis. This incident is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how FOI requests can expose critical gaps in government welfare and housing policy. By making these statistics public, advocates can challenge discriminatory budgetary decisions and demand accountability, illustrating the vital role of transparency in driving political intervention to protect tenants and address systemic housing failures.
