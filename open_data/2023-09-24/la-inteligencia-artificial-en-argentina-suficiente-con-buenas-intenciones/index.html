La Inteligencia Artificial en Argentina: ¿suficiente con buenas intenciones?

Argentina has launched a new program to enhance transparency and data protection in artificial intelligence, aligning with international standards and embedding principles such as equity, security, and human oversight into public policy. This initiative aims to provide both the public and private sectors with tools that foster AI development while simultaneously safeguarding citizens’ rights to privacy and data integrity. However, the article warns that legislative intent alone is insufficient to manage the profound societal and technological shifts driven by AI. Without robust management and significant investment, these frameworks risk becoming empty shells. The author argues that merely listing good practices is not enough; the state must actively mitigate risks through implemented programs, as current preventive actions for data protection remain largely absent or reactive. This text is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical tension between technological innovation and the ethical handling of personal information. It underscores that for open data ecosystems to remain trustworthy, governments must move beyond symbolic regulations to enforce concrete, well-funded governance models that balance economic growth with rigorous privacy standards.
