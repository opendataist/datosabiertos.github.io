Fraudulent text messages impersonating government authorities are circulating, tricking citizens into clicking malicious links to steal personal and financial data under the guise of paying traffic fines. This scam highlights the critical importance of verifying official communication channels to protect individual privacy and prevent identity theft, a core concern in open data governance regarding secure citizen interaction. Authorities urge the public to ignore unsolicited payment requests and report them as spam, emphasizing that legitimate notifications arrive through verified email addresses or official portals. This guidance underscores the necessity of transparency and trust in public services, ensuring that citizens can distinguish between authentic government outreach and deceptive attempts to exploit personal information. The relevance to open data lies in maintaining the integrity of public institutions and safeguarding citizen trust. By promoting awareness of secure digital practices, governments reinforce the safe handling of public information. This approach ensures that open data initiatives do not inadvertently expose users to cyber threats, preserving both data security and the legitimate flow of information between the state and its constituents.
