Andrés Velencoso: “Siempre habrá quien me vea como un modelo que pretende ser actor”

Andrés Velencoso: “Siempre habrá quien me vea como un modelo que pretende ser actor”

The article challenges the romanticized notion that acting is merely an intuitive or uncomplicated craft, arguing instead that it requires rigorous technical training, persistent effort, and a methodical approach. By comparing actors to athletes who refine precise movements behind the scenes, the text emphasizes that true performance stems from disciplined practice rather than innate talent alone. This perspective validates the professionalization of the arts, shifting the focus from mystical inspiration to observable skill acquisition. This view is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the importance of documenting and analyzing the visible processes of creative work. In media studies, understanding acting as a learnable, repeatable technique allows researchers to collect data on rehearsal methods, directorial feedback, and performance outcomes. Such data can reveal patterns in how technical proficiency correlates with critical reception, moving beyond subjective criticism to evidence-based analysis of artistic methodology. Furthermore, the discussion highlights the value of transparency in creative industries. By demystifying the actor’s process, it encourages a culture where behind-the-scenes efforts are recognized as legitimate subjects of study. This aligns with open data principles by advocating for the accessibility of information regarding how artistic decisions are made, fostering a more informed public discourse about the labor and strategy involved in producing cultural content.
