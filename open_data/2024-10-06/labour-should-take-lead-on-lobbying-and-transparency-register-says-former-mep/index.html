Labour should take lead on lobbying and transparency register, says former MEP

Labour should take lead on lobbying and transparency register, says former MEP

Malta’s political leaders are urged to prioritize establishing a mandatory lobby and transparency register rather than focusing solely on criticizing European Parliament President Roberta Metsola. Former MEP Cyrus Engerer highlights that previous domestic pledges for such a register have failed to materialize, despite his own successful implementation of open meeting records during his tenure. This domestic inaction stands in stark contrast to the rigorous public disclosure standards he maintained, underscoring the need for local government and opposition parties to lead by example in promoting genuine governance and accountability. Concurrently, significant concerns have been raised regarding Metsola’s adherence to ethical protocols, particularly as her office appears exempt from the transparency rules applied to other MEPs. Critics argue that this exemption allows her to avoid scrutiny regarding her husband’s role as a lobbyist for a cruise line, a sector closely tied to parliamentary decisions. The perceived inconsistency in enforcing these rules suggests a selective approach to transparency, where stringent expectations are demanded of adversaries while exempting oneself from similar oversight. This article is highly relevant to open_data as it illustrates the critical intersection between political transparency and data accessibility. It emphasizes that true accountability requires not just the existence of regulations, but the public availability of concrete data, such as meeting minutes and conflict-of-interest declarations. Without open, machine-readable records of lobbying interactions, citizens cannot effectively monitor potential influences on policy, making open data essential for preventing hypocrisy and ensuring democratic integrity.
