En febrero aprobarán leyes sobre transparencia y protección de datos personales

En febrero aprobarán leyes sobre transparencia y protección de datos personales

The Mexican government is restructuring its transparency framework following the elimination of the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information and Personal Data Protection (INAI), shifting oversight responsibilities to a new government secretariat and extending information obligations to the electoral institute. This legislative shift aims to maintain public access to information while integrating electoral bodies into the transparency regime, ensuring accountability for political parties and election processes. A critical implication for open data is the heightened risk regarding personal data privacy. While the transition of public data raises concerns, the legislator emphasizes that private sector data poses a greater threat due to potential exploitation by technology giants. This highlights an urgent need for robust regulatory frameworks that protect citizen data from commercial misuse, prioritizing the security of information held by private entities over those in the public sector. This development is crucial for open data advocates as it signals a potential contraction in independent oversight and a centralization of data control. The reliance on legislative unanimity to protect transparency rights suggests a fragile environment for data freedom. Understanding these structural changes is essential for monitoring how the redefinition of "obligated subjects" and the weakening of autonomous institutions may impact public access and data governance in Mexico.
