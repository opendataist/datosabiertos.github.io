Ghana Marks International Day for Universal Access to Information

Ghana Marks International Day for Universal Access to Information

This article highlights the critical role of information integrity and universal access in fostering democratic governance, particularly within the context of Ghana’s digital transformation. It emphasizes that reliable data is not merely a resource but a foundational element for accountability, preventing information disorder, and ensuring social stability. By linking access to information with the prevention of misinformation, the text underscores how transparency serves as a safeguard against information pollution in the modern age. The narrative centers on the operational mandate of Ghana’s Right To Information Commission, established to enforce constitutional rights and combat systemic barriers like poor records management and bureaucratic reluctance. Stakeholders gathered to address these challenges, advocating for simplified application processes that do not disadvantage rural or underserved populations. The discussion also explored the responsible use of emerging technologies to protect privacy and national security while enhancing public access, demonstrating a commitment to practical solutions over theoretical ideals. This content is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the legal and institutional frameworks necessary to make government data accessible and usable. The emphasis on enforcing disclosure laws and engaging diverse stakeholders reflects best practices in open governance, where data transparency is actively promoted rather than passively assumed. By linking these efforts to Sustainable Development Goal 16, the article reinforces the global consensus that open data is essential for achieving inclusive growth, protecting fundamental freedoms, and building trustworthy public institutions.
