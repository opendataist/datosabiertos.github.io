Desocupación en Mendoza: los sectores más golpeados y el fenómeno del segundo empleo

Desocupación en Mendoza: los sectores más golpeados y el fenómeno del segundo empleo

The recent labor data reveals a complex economic landscape in Mendoza, characterized by a slight rise in activity and employment rates alongside a marginal increase in unemployment. While these mixed signals indicate difficulties within a challenging national context, the region maintains the lowest unemployment levels among major Argentine conglomerates. This resilience is largely attributed to a diversified economic matrix, particularly in services and technology, which has helped prevent the surge in joblessness seen in other provinces during previous periods. However, significant structural inequalities persist, most notably affecting women who face disproportionate barriers to formal, full-time work due to caregiving responsibilities. Small and medium-sized enterprises act as the primary pillars of private employment, demonstrating high retention rates despite adverse conditions. To support workforce integration, government subsidy programs successfully assist thousands of young and inexperienced workers monthly. Yet, these efforts are insufficient alone, as many professionals seek secondary jobs to offset declining purchasing power, and youth encounter substantial entry barriers into the formal labor market. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical need for transparent, accessible statistical indicators like the EPH to inform public policy. Open access to such granular labor metrics allows researchers and citizens to monitor social inequalities, evaluate the efficacy of employment subsidies, and assess regional economic resilience. By making this data openly available, stakeholders can better understand the interplay between macroeconomic stability, private sector dynamics, and social support systems, fostering more informed civic engagement and accountability in governance.
