Más actividad física, pero...

The INEGI’s recent report reveals that while the percentage of physically active Mexicans has risen slightly, it remains significantly lower than it was a decade ago. This trend highlights a concerning regression in public health habits, suggesting that despite minor recent improvements, society is not yet returning to the higher levels of physical engagement seen previously. The data underscores a persistent gap between current activity rates and those observed ten years ago, indicating that long-term behavioral shifts toward exercise have not been fully sustained. Gender disparities and accessibility factors play a crucial role in these findings. Men report higher participation rates than women, and while a majority of active individuals acknowledge the existence of decent public facilities, a significant portion of the population abandons exercise due to lack of time, work-related fatigue, or health issues. These barriers point to structural and lifestyle challenges that hinder consistent physical activity, emphasizing the need for policies that address time management and workplace wellness alongside infrastructure development. This article is relevant to open_data because it exemplifies how government-generated statistical data provides essential insights into societal well-being and infrastructure efficacy. By making such detailed metrics publicly available, INEGI enables researchers, policymakers, and civil society to analyze trends, identify inequalities, and design evidence-based interventions. Open access to this information fosters transparency and supports informed decision-making regarding public health strategies and urban planning for sports facilities.
