El Centro de Inundaciones de Google que las pronóstica 5 días antes de que sucedan

The recent flooding tragedy in Spain underscores the urgent need to improve early warning systems. Google’s Flood Hub tool demonstrates that it is feasible to predict extreme events five days in advance using artificial intelligence, which could save lives and reduce the humanitarian impact of such natural disasters by providing the public and authorities with a crucial window for action. The success of this model lies in the integration of multiple public and open data sources, ranging from global weather forecasts to satellite imagery and historical records. Google Research has managed to train a system that, thanks to open access to this diverse information, can generate accurate predictions for river basins worldwide, even in regions where local data are scarce or nonexistent, thereby demonstrating the power of international data collaboration. This article is relevant to the open data community because it validates the use of open datasets as essential inputs for developing socially impactful technological solutions. It shows how interoperability among public data from various space and meteorological agencies enables the creation of accessible and scalable tools, reinforcing the argument that transparency and free access to geographic information are fundamental pillars for climate change resilience.
