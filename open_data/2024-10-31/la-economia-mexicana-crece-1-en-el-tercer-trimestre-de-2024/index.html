La economía mexicana crece 1% en el tercer trimestre de 2024

Mexico’s economy showed modest growth in the third quarter of 2024, expanding by one percent in real terms compared to the previous quarter. This expansion was primarily driven by the primary sector, which exhibited significant momentum, while secondary and tertiary activities experienced more subdued growth. Annual figures indicate a positive trend, with year-to-date performance reflecting a steady, albeit slow, economic recovery across various sectors. Key indicators highlight resilience in specific areas, including robust private consumption, historically low unemployment rates, and a surge in foreign direct investment, particularly in manufacturing and tourism. Despite these positive developments, the government maintains that public finances remain under pressure due to geopolitical risks and weather-related events. However, fiscal management has remained disciplined, with improvements in tax collection helping to stabilize the debt-to-GDP ratio, suggesting that responsible fiscal policy is mitigating some external vulnerabilities. This article is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the importance of transparent, timely statistical releases from institutions such as Mexico’s National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI). The analysis relies entirely on publicly available official data to interpret complex economic dynamics, demonstrating how accessible government statistics enable analysts and citizens to track economic health, evaluate policy effectiveness, and make informed decisions based on empirical evidence rather than speculation.
