This article presents the privacy and data processing policy of a Spanish web portal, detailing how personal information is collected, managed, and protected. It establishes that while most data collection is mandatory for service provision, specific usage data and cookies are gathered automatically to facilitate site functionality and analytics. The policy emphasizes that users bear responsibility for any third-party data they share, while the operator commits to implementing strict security measures to prevent unauthorized access to or alteration of stored information. The text outlines the legal bases for data processing, ranging from explicit user consent to legitimate interests and contractual obligations. It specifies that data is processed for advertising, statistical analysis, and content visualization, often involving third-party providers located in the United States. Crucially, it acknowledges that users subject to higher protection standards, such as those in the European Union, retain specific rights to access, rectify, restrict, or delete their data, as well as the right to object to commercial processing without justification. This document is relevant to open data initiatives because it highlights the tension between transparency and privacy compliance. While it provides detailed contact information and legal bases, it focuses on user restrictions and data retention periods rather than data openness. For open data practitioners, this serves as a critical reminder of the robust legal frameworks, such as the GDPR, that govern personal data, contrasting sharply with the principles of free access and reuse that define the open data ecosystem.
