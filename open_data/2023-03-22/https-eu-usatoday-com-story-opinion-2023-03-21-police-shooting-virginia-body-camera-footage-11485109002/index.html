The article argues that police departments must prioritize immediate, transparent communication to maintain public trust, particularly following fatal engagements. The author contends that delaying the release of information, such as body camera footage, fosters suspicion and deteriorates community relations. By defaulting to dissemination rather than withholding details for arbitrary deadlines, law enforcement can prevent the erosion of confidence that often follows tragic incidents. Effective crisis management requires providing accurate and complete facts quickly, rather than relying on piecemeal updates or narratives that may later prove false. The author contrasts the fragmented response in the Timothy McCree Johnson case with the proactive transparency shown in the Tyre Nichols incident. This comparison illustrates how swift, honest leadership can mitigate anger and foster peace, whereas secrecy or premature, inaccurate storytelling significantly damages institutional credibility. This perspective is vital to open data because it underscores that transparency is not merely a policy preference but a fundamental component of public safety. When police agencies withhold data or delay its release, they violate the principle that community trust relies on accessible truth. Open data practices in law enforcement should therefore be standardized to ensure that all relevant information is available to the public as soon as it is verified, enabling accountability and preserving the social contract.
