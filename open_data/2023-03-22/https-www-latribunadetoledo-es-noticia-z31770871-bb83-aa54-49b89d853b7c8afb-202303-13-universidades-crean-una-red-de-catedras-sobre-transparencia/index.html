The creation of the Spanish Network of Open Government University Chairs and Institutes represents a significant step forward in standardizing and deepening the principles of transparency and good governance. This collective seeks to generate synergy among various public universities to foster a culture of open government, combining academic research with the training of new professionals. Its main objective is to build an administrative model that prevents corruption through integrity standards and oversight, ensuring that institutions respond to social demands with a focus on public utility and accountability. It is crucial to highlight that this initiative addresses the gap between existing legislation and its actual implementation. Although there have been improvements in transparency in recent years, significant deficiencies persist, particularly in the provision of open data that citizens can freely use, as well as in the timely response to information requests. The network aims to close this gap by establishing common quality standards and facilitating the exchange of knowledge among regions, enabling administrations to learn from one another and overcome the lack of means or resources that currently limit effective compliance. The relevance of this article to the open data movement lies in its structural focus on the accessibility and practical use of public information. By emphasizing the need for open formats and combating the merely decorative use of open government, it underscores that true transparency requires data to be understandable and usable by civil society. This institutional and academic collaboration is vital for transforming transparency from a passive legal requirement into an active tool for citizen participation, ensuring that information generated by institutions truly serves to improve democracy and public efficiency.
