Estados Unidos lo tiene claro: una imagen generada por IA a partir de texto no tiene autoría humana - El Sureño

The Copyright Office states that images created exclusively through artificial intelligence (AI) are not eligible for copyright protection, as automatic generation lacks human authorship. When comparing text-based prompts to commissions given to artists, it is clarified that the user acts more like a director than the original creator, thereby excluding these works from traditional automatic copyright protection. However, the legal framework leaves room for human modification. If an artist makes substantial and original changes to the initial AI-generated output, those contributions may be protected. This nuance is crucial, as it validates subsequent creative work that transforms an algorithmically generated image into a work with recognizable authorship, provided the modifications go beyond mere mechanical reproduction. This regulatory evolution is fundamental to open data, as it defines the boundaries of what can be freely reused or commercialized. By determining which parts of AI datasets are in the public domain or privately owned, it directly impacts ethics, licensing, and the integration of these resources into open information ecosystems, ensuring that both technological innovation and the rights of human creators are respected.
