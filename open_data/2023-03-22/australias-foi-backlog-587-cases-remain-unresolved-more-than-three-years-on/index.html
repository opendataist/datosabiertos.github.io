Australia’s FOI backlog: 587 cases remain unresolved more than three years on

The Australian Information Commissioner’s Office is facing a severe backlog that has paralyzed its ability to review freedom of information requests, with hundreds of cases remaining unresolved for over three years. This systemic failure has been highlighted by litigation challenging the agency’s handling of critical public interest documents, revealing a transparency crisis where citizens cannot obtain independent oversight of government secrecy. The delays are not merely administrative inconveniences but represent a fundamental breakdown in the mechanisms designed to hold power accountable. This stagnation stems from years of underfunding and understaffing, which created a legacy workload that current restructuring efforts have struggled to address effectively. Despite recent funding increases, the agency has not significantly expanded its workforce, leading to prolonged inactivity where some cases sit untouched for years. The inability to meet statutory timelines has eroded public trust and contributed to high-level resignations, underscoring the urgency of reforming the operational capacity of the oversight body. This situation is critically relevant to open data because freedom of information is the legal backbone of transparency. When review mechanisms fail, citizens lose the primary avenue to access withheld government records, effectively locking data away from public scrutiny. Resolving these delays is essential to ensure that the principles of open government remain functional, allowing citizens to verify claims and understand decisions made in their name. Without an efficient, independent review process, open data initiatives risk becoming hollow, as the data remains inaccessible or unverified by a compromised oversight system.
