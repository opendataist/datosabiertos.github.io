Pintard: PM using inflation as excuse to stall on FOIA

The article highlights a political clash in The Bahamas over the delayed implementation of the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA). The opposition leader argues that the government is using the cost of living crisis as a pretext to avoid fulfilling a key election promise regarding transparency. By prioritizing immediate economic relief over legislative accountability, the administration faces accusations of lacking the will to enact meaningful open data reforms. Critics contend that the technical and resource challenges cited by officials are fabricated excuses for an administration intent on withholding information. Despite having dedicated staff and existing budget allocations, the government has failed to launch the pilot program fully, leaving public access to records in limbo. This stagnation suggests a deliberate choice to maintain opacity rather than empower citizens with the right to scrutinize government actions and expenditures. This dispute is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the tension between political convenience and the institutional necessity of transparency. The FOIA represents a critical framework for making government data accessible, yet its indefinite delay demonstrates how policy can be stalled when political motives conflict with public interest. It serves as a cautionary example of how open data initiatives require sustained political commitment rather than just technical readiness to succeed.
