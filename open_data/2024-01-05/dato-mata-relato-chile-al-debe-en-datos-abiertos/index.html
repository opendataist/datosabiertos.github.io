Dato mata relato: Chile al debe en datos abiertos

The article argues that open data is a fundamental asset for governments to create public value, enabling better policy design, societal oversight, and state legitimacy. Effective data management proved crucial during the pandemic and is essential for sectors such as security, education, and taxation. Without robust data strategies, authorities operate blindly, missing opportunities to engage citizens and build trust through transparent, evidence-based decision-making. However, Chile faces a significant challenge in this area, as evidenced by its low performance in the OECD OURdata Index 2023. The country scored significantly below the OECD average, particularly in governmental support for data reuse, where it received the lowest possible rating. This gap indicates a failure to meet formal requirements for data availability and accessibility, leaving Chile far behind peer nations in adopting practices that allow data to be reused and repurposed effectively. This disparity is critical because open data underpins the digital transformation of the state. Without reliable, accessible information, the government cannot deliver citizen-centric services or base policies on evidence. The article concludes that Chile’s poor ranking jeopardizes its digital transition, emphasizing that the lack of open data creates a structural weakness that hinders the state’s ability to function efficiently and responsively in the modern era.
