Indian-American mathematician C R Rao awarded International Prize in Statistics at 102

Calyampudi Radhakrishna Rao has been awarded the prestigious International Prize in Statistics for his foundational work that revolutionized statistical theory over seven decades ago. His three seminal results, including the Cramer-Rao lower bound and the Rao-Blackwell Theorem, established essential frameworks for estimating quantities and optimizing data analysis. These contributions remain critical today, serving as the bedrock for modern statistical practices across numerous scientific disciplines. The profound relevance of Rao’s work extends into the realm of open data through its application in information geometry, a field pivotal to modern data science and artificial intelligence. By providing mathematical tools to efficiently extract insights from complex datasets, his theories enable better optimization and understanding in areas such as signal processing and machine learning. This ensures that data-driven decisions are more accurate, transparent, and effective, directly supporting the integrity and utility of open data initiatives in solving real-world problems. This recognition highlights how enduring theoretical advancements continue to drive innovation in contemporary technology. As science increasingly relies on big data and computational models, the principles established by Rao ensure that these tools remain robust and reliable. Awarding this prize underscores the importance of foundational mathematical contributions to the open science community, demonstrating that deep theoretical work is essential for advancing the quality and accessibility of data analysis globally.
