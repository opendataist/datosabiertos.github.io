The article highlights a significant regression in telework adoption within Castilla-La Mancha, revealing a substantial decline in remote employment compared to previous years. Despite a slight increase relative to pre-pandemic levels, the region remains well below national averages, with Madrid leading in adoption rates. This data indicates that remote work has not become a permanent structural shift in this specific area, reflecting broader trends where telework fails to consolidate as a standard labor practice. The relevance to open data lies in the critical need for granular, transparent statistics to identify regional disparities and monitor the evolution of the labor market. Without accessible, detailed datasets, it is difficult to assess whether policy interventions are effective or if specific territories are being left behind in the digital transformation of work. Publicly available employment surveys, when properly analyzed, expose these gaps, empowering stakeholders to demand evidence-based regulations rather than relying on anecdotal evidence or aggregate national figures that mask local realities. Furthermore, the union emphasizes that the lack of comprehensive regulation poses serious risks to workers’ rights, including the right to digital disconnect and inadequate provision of equipment. The narrative underscores that telework is not merely a logistical arrangement but a legal and health issue requiring robust oversight. Many workers lack guaranteed protections, highlighting the urgency for standardized data collection on working conditions to ensure that the benefits of remote flexibility do not come at the cost of employee well-being and fair labor standards.
