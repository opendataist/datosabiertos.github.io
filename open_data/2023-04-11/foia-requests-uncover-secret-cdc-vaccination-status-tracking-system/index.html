FOIA Requests Uncover Secret CDC Vaccination Status Tracking System

Freedom of Information Act requests revealed that the CDC confirmed the use of medical codes to tag and track individuals based on their vaccination status, specifically distinguishing between unvaccinated and partially vaccinated citizens. This disclosure highlights a significant shift in public health data management, where standard classification systems are utilized not just for clinical statistics, but for granular population surveillance. The confirmation underscores how administrative health identifiers can be leveraged to monitor compliance with public health directives, raising questions about the scope of government data collection capabilities during a crisis. The reliance on these tracking mechanisms implies a broader infrastructure for citizen monitoring that extends beyond immediate emergency response. By integrating vaccination status into standard medical coding systems, the distinction between clinical care and social control becomes blurred. This approach suggests that public health agencies possess the technical capacity to maintain detailed registries of individual health behaviors, which could potentially be expanded to other areas of public interest or used to incentivize compliance through economic or social means. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates because it exposes the hidden layers of government data processing that are often inaccessible to the public without legal pressure. It illustrates the critical need for transparency in how public health information is classified, stored, and shared. Understanding these underlying data structures is essential for ensuring accountability, protecting civil liberties, and preventing the misuse of health data for non-medical purposes, thereby reinforcing the importance of open access to government records.
