FBI: frontera de Texas, más segura que el interior de EE.UU.

FBI: frontera de Texas, más segura que el interior de EE.UU.

Communities along the Texas border exhibit significantly lower rates of violent crime and homicide compared to major inland U.S. cities and national averages. This trend points to safer streets, which in turn supports stronger schools, robust local economies, and greater community stability for families. The data underscore a broader decline in national homicide rates, with specific border cities reporting substantial reductions or even zero incidents. These statistics challenge prevailing narratives by demonstrating that border areas are among the safest places to live, work, and raise children in the country. This information is vital for open data initiatives, as it employs transparent statistics to counter misinformation. Providing accessible, verifiable evidence enables citizens to grasp actual safety trends, fostering trust in public institutions and facilitating informed civic discourse grounded in facts rather than political rhetoric.
