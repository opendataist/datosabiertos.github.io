La “Asamblea ciudadana Santander saludable” rechaza el proyecto de Zona de Bajas Emisiones aprobado por el ayuntamiento - Tercera Información

La “Asamblea ciudadana Santander saludable” rechaza el proyecto de Zona de Bajas Emisiones aprobado por el ayuntamiento - Tercera Información

The coalition argues that Santander’s Low Emission Zone (ZBE) is fundamentally flawed because it affects only a negligible fraction of the city, ignoring the streets with the highest pollution levels. By excluding the most contaminated areas, the current proposal fails to address the root causes of air and noise pollution, rendering the measure ineffective for protecting public health. This geographical limitation undermines the zone’s primary objective, leaving the majority of citizens exposed to harmful environmental conditions despite the existence of a regulatory framework. Critically, the lack of transparency regarding accumulated environmental data prevents an objective assessment of the city’s actual ecological status. The authorities have withheld crucial measurements from new monitoring stations funded by European grants, creating an information asymmetry that invalidates the technical rigor of the ordinance. This opacity suggests that the project lacks scientific backing, raising concerns that the legislation is merely cosmetic rather than a genuine response to the urgent health crisis affecting the population. This situation is highly relevant to open data principles, as it highlights how withholding public information obstructs democratic oversight and effective policy-making. The coalition’s demand for full data publication underscores the necessity of accessible, reliable datasets to validate environmental claims and ensure accountability. Without transparent access to these metrics, citizens cannot verify the efficacy of interventions, reinforcing the argument that open data is essential for fostering trust and enabling evidence-based decisions in urban governance.
