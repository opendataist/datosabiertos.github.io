Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway face significantly poorer living conditions compared to the general population, characterized by lower employment rates, reduced income, and inferior housing standards. Despite these structural disadvantages, they report equal life satisfaction and demonstrate surprisingly high confidence in national institutions, such as the judiciary and police. This paradox highlights a complex integration dynamic where material disparities do not necessarily correlate with subjective well-being or civic trust. However, public health data reveals a distinct vulnerability, with immigrants experiencing twice the rate of mental health issues and loneliness as the native population. Certain groups show particularly high prevalence of anxiety and depression, pointing to specific psychosocial stressors that warrant targeted intervention. These health disparities suggest that while institutional trust remains strong, personal social connections and mental resilience remain areas of significant concern for many newcomers. This article is vital to open data discussions because it illustrates how standardized statistical tools, like the Hopkins Symptoms Checklist, can uncover nuanced human experiences within large datasets. By making these comprehensive living condition surveys publicly available, Statistics Norway enables researchers to analyze intersectional factors affecting integration. Such transparency allows for evidence-based policy-making that addresses both objective economic gaps and subjective social needs, ensuring data serves as a tool for holistic societal improvement.
