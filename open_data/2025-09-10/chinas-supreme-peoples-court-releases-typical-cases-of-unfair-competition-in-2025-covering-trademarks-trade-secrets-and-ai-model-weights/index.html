China’s Supreme People’s Court Releases Typical Cases of Unfair Competition in 2025 Covering Trademarks, Trade Secrets and AI Model Weights

China’s Supreme People’s Court Releases Typical Cases of Unfair Competition in 2025 Covering Trademarks, Trade Secrets and AI Model Weights

The Supreme People’s Court recently released typical cases to standardize the application of anti-unfair competition laws, emphasizing consistent judicial interpretation. A landmark ruling clarified that registering a company name identical to an influential trademark constitutes commercial use and unfair competition, even if actual business operations have not yet commenced. This decision strengthens protection against "free-riding" by allowing courts to curb deceptive naming practices at their source, preventing market confusion before it escalates. Another significant case addressed the illicit acquisition of technical secrets through anonymously established competing entities. The court established that former employees who use stolen proprietary data and software to launch a new venture are jointly liable for substantial damages. This judgment reinforces the burden of proof in trade secret disputes and highlights the legal risks associated with misappropriating intellectual property to gain a competitive edge, ensuring that corporate espionage yields no financial reward. Furthermore, the rulings affirmed that complex, optimized technical solutions remain protected as trade secrets even if individual components are publicly known. By protecting foreign rights holders equally with domestic entities, the court signaled a commitment to robust intellectual property enforcement. These decisions are critical for open data discussions as they define the legal boundaries between publicly available information and protected proprietary assets, ensuring that data ecosystems respect ownership rights while fostering fair competition.
