Exportaciones de carne bovina superan los $us 220 millones

Bolivia’s meat sector has emerged as a vital economic pillar, achieving record export volumes that underscore the industry’s robust growth and international competitiveness. This surge demonstrates the nation’s ability to secure high-quality market access, surpassing previous annual milestones and establishing Bolivia as a reliable global supplier in a traditionally competitive field. The expansion relies heavily on strategic sanitary agreements, particularly with major markets such as China, while negotiations with countries like Chile and Egypt aim to further diversify trade channels. This diversification strengthens the national economy without compromising domestic supply, balancing external revenue with internal food security needs. This development is relevant to open data because it highlights the critical role of official statistical agencies, such as the National Institute of Statistics (INE), in providing transparent, verifiable metrics. Open access to such trade data enables researchers and policymakers to monitor economic trends, validate the success of sanitary protocols, and foster accountability in international trade negotiations, thereby enhancing informed decision-making.
