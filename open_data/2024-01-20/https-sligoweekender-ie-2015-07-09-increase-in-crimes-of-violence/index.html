Crime statistics for the Sligo-Leitrim region reveal a significant rise in violent offenses, including sexual offenses, assaults, robberies, and property damage. Conversely, non-violent crimes such as theft, fraud, and public order offenses have decreased during the same period. This divergent trend highlights a shift in local criminal activity patterns, emphasizing the need for targeted policing strategies that address the specific nature of these increases. The resumption of published data follows a suspension due to identified irregularities in how Gardaí recorded crimes on the PULSE system. A comprehensive review addressed these issues, correcting classification errors and accounting for unrecorded incidents. This restoration of data integrity is crucial, as previous methodological flaws could have distorted the perceived safety landscape and hindered accurate resource allocation. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates the critical importance of data quality and transparency in public sector reporting. It illustrates how technical errors in data collection systems can compromise statistical validity, potentially leading to misguided policy decisions. For open data initiatives, this case underscores the necessity of rigorous validation processes and continuous monitoring to ensure that published statistics accurately reflect reality, thereby maintaining public trust and supporting evidence-based governance.
