China is experiencing an accelerated demographic contraction, with a recent doubling of the rate of population decline. This phenomenon stems from decades of low birth rates, exacerbated by past policies and the current economic reality, where high living costs and career priorities discourage family formation. Despite government incentives and the relaxation of child-restriction policies, these measures have proven ineffective against the material pressures faced by urban youth. The relevance to open data lies in the need for transparency and access to reliable demographic and socioeconomic information. In a context where the government has temporarily suspended the publication of unemployment figures, public access to accurate data on birth rates, mortality, and the labor market is crucial. The lack of open and up-to-date data hinders rigorous analysis of structural trends, making it difficult to truly understand the impact of public policies on citizens’ quality of life and social stability. This population crisis has profound implications for future economic sustainability, affecting the labor force, pension systems, and public health. China’s historical reliance on sustained population growth is now colliding with a new era of stagnation, necessitating evidence-based governance. Open data enables civil society, academics, and policymakers to monitor these key variables, fostering informed debates and more effective policy proposals to address the challenges of aging and economic contraction.
