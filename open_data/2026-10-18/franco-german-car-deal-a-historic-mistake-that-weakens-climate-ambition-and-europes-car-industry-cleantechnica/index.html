Franco-German Car Deal A ‘Historic Mistake’ That Weakens Climate Ambition And Europe’s Car Industry - CleanTechnica

Franco-German Car Deal A ‘Historic Mistake’ That Weakens Climate Ambition And Europe’s Car Industry - CleanTechnica

The transportation and environment organization T&E strongly condemns a recent agreement between France and Germany to relax EU car CO₂ standards and local content requirements for electric vehicles. Critics argue this policy represents a historic error that threatens to dismantle Europe’s automotive industry rather than protect it. By allowing cars assembled in the EU with Chinese batteries to retain the “Made-in-EU” label, the deal undermines the strategic autonomy the bloc seeks to maintain against global competitors. This regulatory loosening is expected to significantly slow the transition to electric mobility, reducing the projected market share of EVs and limiting the availability of affordable models for consumers. Furthermore, the lowered requirements for European-sourced battery cells and components jeopardize the viability of local gigafactories. By setting ambitious targets too late and at levels far below current manufacturing realities, the agreement risks destroying demand certainty for European battery producers, potentially leading to substantial capacity losses and job cuts across the supply chain. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates as it highlights the critical need for transparent, standardized metrics in tracking industrial compliance and environmental impact. Robust open datasets are essential for holding governments accountable to their stated climate and industrial goals, ensuring that “Made-in-EU” labels reflect genuine local production rather than assembly loopholes. Without accessible, high-quality data on supply chains and emissions, society cannot effectively monitor whether policies are achieving their intended economic or ecological outcomes, leaving the door open for decisions that prioritize short-term political gains over long-term strategic resilience.
