SERAP to 36 govs, Wike: Account for over N40Trn LGA allocations

The Socio-Economic Rights and Accountability Project has mandated state governors and the Federal Capital Territory Minister to publicly disclose the actual distribution of federal funds allocated to local governments. This demand stems from long-standing concerns about opacity regarding how these resources reach their intended destinations, highlighting a critical gap in financial transparency that has persisted since the return to democratic governance. By requiring this disclosure, the organization seeks to empower citizens with accurate data on public expenditure and holding leaders accountable for the management of communal resources. This issue is vital to the open data movement because it exposes systemic barriers to accessing vital public financial information. Without transparent records of fund allocation and spending, citizens cannot effectively monitor whether resources are being used for intended development projects or are being misappropriated. The lack of accessible, detailed data on government finances undermines public trust and prevents civil society from utilizing evidence to advocate for better governance and efficient resource utilization at the local level. Furthermore, the call for data release is directly linked to combating corruption and strengthening democratic institutions. When financial flows remain hidden, it facilitates the diversion of public funds into private pockets, eroding the rule of law and the fundamental rights of citizens to benefit from national wealth. Ensuring that data regarding these allocations is open and accessible is essential for detecting fraud, preventing embezzlement, and fostering a culture of accountability where government actions are subject to public scrutiny and legal oversight.
