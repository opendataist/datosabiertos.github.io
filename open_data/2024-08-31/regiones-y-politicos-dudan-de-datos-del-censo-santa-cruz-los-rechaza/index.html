Regiones y políticos dudan de datos del Censo; Santa Cruz los rechaza

The release of Bolivia’s 2024 Census results by the National Institute of Statistics (INE) has sparked significant controversy, as the reported population figures are substantially lower than the institution’s own projections and independent estimates. This discrepancy has triggered widespread skepticism among regional leaders and opposition figures, who argue that the data lacks transparency and fails to adequately represent certain demographics, particularly in key departments like Santa Cruz. The central government has defended the validity of the results, urging against political manipulation, while demanding a thorough technical justification for the divergences. This situation highlights critical challenges regarding data reliability and public trust in official statistics. When authoritative datasets contradict established projections, it creates uncertainty about the methodological rigor and quality of the census execution. The resulting tensions underscore the importance of independent verification and the need for clear, accessible explanations of statistical methods to ensure that official numbers are perceived as accurate and unbiased by all stakeholders. For the open data community, this case illustrates the necessity of making census methodologies and underlying data publicly available for scrutiny. It emphasizes that robust open data practices require not just the publication of final figures, but also the provision of detailed technical documentation and error margins. Without such transparency, stakeholders cannot validate results, leading to prolonged disputes over resource allocation and political representation, which ultimately undermines the utility and credibility of official statistics.
