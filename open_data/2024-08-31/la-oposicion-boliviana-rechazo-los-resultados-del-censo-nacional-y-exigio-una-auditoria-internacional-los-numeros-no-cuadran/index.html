La oposición boliviana rechazó los resultados del censo nacional y exigió una auditoría internacional: “Los números no cuadran”

La oposición boliviana rechazó los resultados del censo nacional y exigió una auditoría internacional: “Los números no cuadran”

The recent publication of Bolivia’s national census results has sparked significant political controversy, underscoring the critical role of accurate open data in democratic governance. The government maintains that the process was technically rigorous and internationally endorsed, while opposition figures accuse the administration of manipulating figures and committing fraud to influence the distribution of political power. This dispute highlights how statistical transparency is essential for maintaining public trust, as discrepancies between official data and independent estimates fuel allegations of political bias. These demographic figures directly determine the allocation of public resources and parliamentary seats, making the integrity of the data a matter of substantial national interest. By establishing population trends—particularly the rapid growth of urban areas such as Santa Cruz and La Paz—the census serves as a foundational tool for budgetary decisions and legislative representation. Consequently, any perceived deviation in these numbers does not merely affect statistical accuracy but has tangible implications for political equity and the fair distribution of state funding among regions. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocacy because it illustrates the real-world consequences of non-transparent or disputed datasets. When statistical processes lack sufficient openness or face credible allegations of manipulation, the resulting data becomes unreliable for civic planning and accountability. It reinforces the necessity for independent verification, accessible methodologies, and international oversight to ensure that census data serves as a neutral, factual basis for governance rather than a tool for political advantage.
