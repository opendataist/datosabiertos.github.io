Cuentas claras, amistades largas: Los municipios bonaerenses que van de regular para mal en transparencia fiscal

Cuentas claras, amistades largas: Los municipios bonaerenses que van de regular para mal en transparencia fiscal

Half of the evaluated municipalities in Buenos Aires demonstrate low compliance with transparency standards, frequently publishing financial data with significant delays. These entities, which constitute the lower half of the ranking, generally fail to meet statutory deadlines for disclosing their economic and financial situations, indicating insufficient adherence to open fiscal regulations despite having information available on their websites. This lack of timely disclosure hinders effective citizen oversight and accountability. The law mandates the regular publication of budgets, execution reports, debt stocks, and public employment levels with minimal delay. When municipalities miss these quarterly updates, it creates an information gap that prevents the public from understanding how public funds are being managed and spent in real time. This assessment is vital for the open data movement because it highlights the gap between legal transparency requirements and actual digital governance practices. By benchmarking municipalities, such studies expose systemic weaknesses in public information accessibility. This drives advocacy for stricter enforcement and improved data practices, ensuring that citizens can access timely, usable, and complete fiscal data to hold their local governments accountable.
