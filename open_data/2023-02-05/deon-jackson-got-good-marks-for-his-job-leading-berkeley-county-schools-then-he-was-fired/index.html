Deon Jackson got good marks for his job leading Berkeley County schools. Then he was fired.

Berkeley County school board members largely rated former superintendent Deon Jackson proficient or accomplished in management and community outreach just weeks before his termination. This discrepancy between high performance evaluations and the subsequent firing highlights potential inconsistencies in how public officials assess leadership effectiveness versus their political decisions. The incident underscores the critical importance of transparency in local governance. By revealing that the majority of the board recognized Jackson’s competence despite later voting to remove him, it questions the integrity of the dismissal process and suggests that operational performance metrics may not align with the political motivations behind personnel changes. For the open data community, this story illustrates the value of Freedom of Information Act requests in exposing hidden administrative narratives. It demonstrates how accessing raw evaluation data can reveal contradictions in public sector decision-making, encouraging greater scrutiny of government records to ensure accountability and inform citizens about the true basis of significant leadership changes.
