Colombia has reached a critical threshold in digital connectivity, with internet penetration surpassing 75% and social media usage nearing universal adoption among the adult population. This widespread access transforms the internet into an essential infrastructure, deeply embedding digital platforms into the daily lives and social fabric of Colombians. The data confirms that online connectivity is no longer a niche activity but a mainstream norm, reflecting significant societal integration of digital technologies. The dominance of major social media platforms such as Facebook, YouTube, and TikTok highlights how central these tools are for communication, information consumption, and commercial interaction. While usage metrics continue to grow, there are nuanced shifts in advertising reach and demographic engagement, indicating a maturing digital ecosystem. These trends suggest that social networks are becoming increasingly powerful conduits for public discourse and market dynamics, requiring robust oversight to address associated societal challenges. This article is relevant to open data because it illustrates the immense value and necessity of accurate, transparent statistical monitoring in highly connected societies. Reliable open datasets enable policymakers, researchers, and civil society to understand digital behaviors, identify inequalities in access, and develop effective regulations. Without such data, understanding the impact of digital inclusion and managing the social consequences of pervasive internet use would remain imprecise and reactive rather than evidence-based.
