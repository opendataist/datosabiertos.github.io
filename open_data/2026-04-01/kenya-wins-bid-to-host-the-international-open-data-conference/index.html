Kenya wins Bid to host the International Open Data Conference

Kenya wins Bid to host the International Open Data Conference

The International Open Data Conference has selected Nairobi, Kenya, as its host for 2020, marking a historic shift as the premier global open data gathering occurs in Africa for the first time. This decision highlights the growing recognition of Africa’s critical role in the global data ecosystem and underscores a strategic move toward South-South collaboration. By bringing thousands of participants to the continent, the event aims to demonstrate how local innovation and technology can address persistent developmental challenges, signaling Africa’s readiness to lead in bridging diverse data communities. The selection reflects Kenya’s demonstrated political will and consistent leadership in championing open government principles, driven by high-level commitments to inclusive data ecosystems. By prioritizing collaboration between the government, private sector, academia, and civil society, Kenya illustrates that open data is not merely a political gesture but a rational economic choice essential for progress. This focus on multi-stakeholder engagement ensures that data initiatives are robust, sustainable, and directly aligned with national development priorities and community needs. This milestone is highly relevant to open data as it expands the geographic and cultural scope of the global open data movement, moving beyond traditional Western-centric hubs. It validates the potential for diverse regions to drive innovation and policy through accessible data, reinforcing the importance of interoperability and inclusive participation. Ultimately, hosting the conference in Africa emphasizes that open data is a universal tool for development, capable of empowering emerging economies to leverage information for equitable growth and social impact.
