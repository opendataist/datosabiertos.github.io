China’s AI fever drives wider adoption across society, but is it mostly hype?

China’s AI fever drives wider adoption across society, but is it mostly hype?

China’s recent political and economic landscape has been defined by an intense surge in artificial intelligence adoption, driven largely by the breakthrough success of the open-source model DeepSeek. This momentum was prominently showcased during the Two Sessions parliamentary meetings, where government officials pledged substantial financial support, including a massive trillion-yuan venture capital fund. The prevailing narrative suggests that beneath the current hype lies genuine market demand, with policymakers viewing AI as a critical engine for revitalizing the economy and enhancing productivity across diverse sectors, from manufacturing to agriculture. The availability of cost-effective, open-source models is democratizing access to advanced AI tools, enabling small and medium-sized enterprises to integrate these technologies into their workflows. While Chinese companies report high rates of AI usage compared to their US counterparts, full implementation remains a challenge. However, the shift toward open-source solutions is expected to accelerate deployment in traditional industries and public services, allowing local governments and businesses to derive tangible economic benefits without being locked into proprietary systems or requiring cutting-edge hardware. This development is highly relevant to the open data and open source communities as it validates the strategic advantage of transparent, accessible technology stacks in accelerating global adoption. The case demonstrates how removing barriers like high costs and hardware dependencies can unlock widespread innovation and practical application. By prioritizing open-source models, China is not only stimulating domestic economic growth but also influencing global trends toward more inclusive and scalable AI ecosystems, proving that openness can drive substantial real-world impact.
