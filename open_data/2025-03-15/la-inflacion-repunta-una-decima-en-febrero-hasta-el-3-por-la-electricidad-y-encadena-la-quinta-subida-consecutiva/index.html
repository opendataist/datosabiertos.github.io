La inflación repunta una décima en febrero hasta el 3% por la electricidad y encadena la quinta subida consecutiva

Inflation in Spain rebounded in February, driven primarily by a significant increase in electricity prices due to the return of the general VAT rate. This trend contrasts with the moderation of prices in sectors such as fuels and food, where sharp declines in olive oil prices stand out, suggesting a certain stabilization in basic goods amid energy volatility. This development is relevant to the field of open data because it highlights the critical importance of transparent and accessible macroeconomic indicators for social analysis. When the public can easily access detailed breakdowns—such as variations by autonomous community or by consumption group—it facilitates a more nuanced understanding of the economic situation. The availability of such data enables citizens and analysts to distinguish between broad-based price increases and specific trends in their local environments, fostering a more informed citizenry. Moreover, the rigorous and periodic publication of these indices by institutions such as the National Statistics Institute (INE) demonstrates how statistical transparency is essential for maintaining institutional trust. By providing clear metrics on purchasing power and underlying inflation, governments can better communicate their economic policies. Access to accurate and disaggregated data is crucial for society to critically assess whether economic growth truly translates into tangible improvements in citizens’ quality of life.
