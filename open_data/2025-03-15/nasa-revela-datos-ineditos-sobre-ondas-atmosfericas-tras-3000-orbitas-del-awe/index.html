NASA revela datos inéditos sobre ondas atmosféricas tras 3,000 órbitas del AWE

NASA revela datos inéditos sobre ondas atmosféricas tras 3,000 órbitas del AWE

NASA’s AWE mission has released its first large-scale dataset of scientific observations from the International Space Station, marking a milestone in atmospheric observation. This public availability offers the first global view of atmospheric gravity waves, a phenomenon previously studied mainly from specific points on Earth’s surface. By providing near-planetwide coverage, these data enable researchers to analyze the energy and motion of these disturbances at kilometer scales, opening new avenues for understanding the interaction between the lower atmosphere and outer space. The significance of this advance for open data science lies in its potential to improve space weather predictability and protect essential technologies. By understanding how atmospheric disturbances influence the space environment, the impact on critical systems such as satellite communications and GPS navigation can be mitigated. The release of millions of images and temperature measurements sets a precedent for using large volumes of scientific data to address complex problems that directly affect society, demonstrating how open information can translate into technological security and operational precision. Moreover, the analysis process has spurred the development of specialized software to clean and align observations, removing visual interference such as cloud reflections. This collaborative effort between researchers and students illustrates how open data fosters methodological innovation and global cooperation. As the scientific community explores these seasonal variables, an invaluable resource is being consolidated for more accurately predicting weather-related disruptions, underscoring the vital importance of openly sharing information to anticipate technological and environmental risks.
