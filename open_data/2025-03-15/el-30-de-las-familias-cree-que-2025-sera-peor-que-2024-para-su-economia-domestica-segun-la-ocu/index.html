El 30% de las familias cree que 2025 será peor que 2024 para su economía doméstica, según la OCU

El 30% de las familias cree que 2025 será peor que 2024 para su economía doméstica, según la OCU

Household economic solvency remains precarious despite a marginal improvement, with pessimism dominating expectations for the coming year. The study reveals a critical contraction of the middle class, as fewer families feel financially secure while struggles to meet basic expenses persist. This trend indicates that most households continue to face significant challenges in maintaining their quality of life and saving money. The relevance to open data lies in the necessity of transparent, accessible metrics for tracking societal well-being. Reliable indices like the one analyzed allow for the public monitoring of economic health across different regions and demographics. Open data initiatives ensure that such vital information regarding housing, health, and mobility costs is not just collected but made available for scrutiny, fostering accountability and informed public debate on financial inequality. Ultimately, the data highlights persistent regional disparities and the fragility of domestic budgets. By making these findings openly accessible, stakeholders can better understand the scope of the "middle-class squeeze" and the specific burdens weighing on families. This transparency is essential for designing effective policies and for citizens to advocate for solutions that address the root causes of economic insecurity and unequal distribution of resources.
