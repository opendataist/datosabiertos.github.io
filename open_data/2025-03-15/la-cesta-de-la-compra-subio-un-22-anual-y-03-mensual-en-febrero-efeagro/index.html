La cesta de la compra subió un 2,2 % anual y 0,3 % mensual en febrero - EFEAgro

La cesta de la compra subió un 2,2 % anual y 0,3 % mensual en febrero - EFEAgro

The recent data reveal a nuanced inflation landscape: general consumer prices rose to 3%, driven largely by electricity costs, while food inflation remained comparatively lower at 2.2%. This divergence indicates that the current price surge is not uniformly affecting all sectors, suggesting structural differences in how energy shocks are transmitted compared to the food market. Within the food sector, significant volatility stems from specific categories rather than broad-based increases. Essential items such as fruits and coffee saw sharp monthly rises, whereas staples like oils and sugars experienced substantial annual price declines. These opposing trends highlight the uneven impact of market forces, creating distinct pockets of affordability and expense that complicate household budgeting. This report is vital for open data initiatives, as it underscores the importance of granular, sector-specific transparency. Understanding these disparate trends allows researchers and policymakers to move beyond aggregate metrics, enabling more precise analyses of economic resilience and the targeted effectiveness of interventions aimed at mitigating inflationary pressures on vulnerable populations.
