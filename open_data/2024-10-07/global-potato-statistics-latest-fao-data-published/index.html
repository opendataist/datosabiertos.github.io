Global potato statistics: Latest FAO data published

The article highlights the significance of the Food and Agriculture Organization’s global agricultural database as a vital resource for tracking worldwide potato production. By aggregating data from numerous nations, it provides a standardized view of yield volumes and harvested areas, allowing stakeholders to identify major producers like China and India. This centralized approach simplifies the complex task of monitoring global food supply chains and agricultural trends without relying on disparate national reports. A key implication for the open data community is the accessibility of these statistics through the FAOSTAT platform. The database is structured to allow users to perform custom searches, filtering by crops or regions, which empowers researchers and analysts to extract specific metrics relevant to their work. Although minor discrepancies may exist compared to official government figures, the FAO data offers a reliable, consistent baseline for comparative analysis across borders. This resource is particularly relevant to open data initiatives because it demonstrates how publicly accessible, standardized datasets can support transparency in global agriculture. By making such extensive production data available online, organizations enable better decision-making for policymakers, economists, and food security experts. The ability to freely explore and analyze this information fosters informed discussions about global food distribution and helps highlight shifts in agricultural dominance or productivity over time.
