Polémico: gafas de realidad aumentada revelan información privada de las personas con solo mirarlas

The new augmented reality glasses unveiled by Meta represent a significant technological leap, enabling real-time interactions with the surrounding environment without the need for smartphones. However, their widespread deployment raises fundamental concerns about individual privacy. The ease with which these devices can capture visual information from the outside world transforms any public space into a potential risk area for the exposure of personal data. An experiment conducted by students demonstrates that by combining facial recognition with public databases, it is possible to identify strangers and access intimate details such as addresses and email addresses simply by looking at them. This vulnerability reveals that current technology not only identifies faces but also immediately links physical identity to one’s digital footprint, creating a comprehensive profile of individuals who may lack consent or awareness regarding such data collection. This case is relevant to the open data movement because it exposes the duality between technological transparency and privacy protection. While access to information is a cornerstone of open data, this incident underscores the urgent need to regulate how open data is integrated with biometric surveillance technologies. Ethical considerations in handling information must take precedence over technical innovation to prevent the openness of data from becoming a tool for mass, unjustified exposure.
