Revealed: Bar exam earned BCI up to Rs 70 cr ($10m) revenues in 6 years from nearly 3 lakh candidates

Revealed: Bar exam earned BCI up to Rs 70 cr ($10m) revenues in 6 years from nearly 3 lakh candidates

The article reveals that the Bar Council of India generated substantial annual revenue from the All India Bar Examination, raising concerns about the financial motives behind the exam's administration. This disclosure stems from a Right to Information request filed by a law student, which forced the council to release data that had previously been withheld. The significant income suggests that the examination serves as a major revenue stream rather than solely a professional qualification metric, highlighting potential conflicts of interest in how legal regulatory bodies operate and fund themselves. Fee structures have increased multiple times while intervals between exams expanded, indicating a strategic adjustment in pricing and scheduling. The data shows a sharp rise in both the number of candidates and the cost per registration, with recent hikes being particularly steep. This trend implies that access to legal practice is becoming increasingly expensive, potentially creating financial barriers for candidates. The lack of consistent concessions for repeat exam takers in later editions further emphasizes a shift toward maximizing earnings from participants rather than supporting their professional development. This case is highly relevant to open data principles as it demonstrates how transparency mechanisms can expose opaque financial practices within public institutions. By leveraging freedom of information laws, individuals can uncover hidden economic incentives that influence policy decisions, such as fee hikes and exam scheduling. This instance underscores the importance of data accessibility in holding regulatory bodies accountable and ensuring that their actions align with public interest rather than private or institutional gain. Open data serves as a critical tool for democratic oversight in specialized sectors like law.
