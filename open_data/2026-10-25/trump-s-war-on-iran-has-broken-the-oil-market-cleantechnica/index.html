Trump's War on Iran Has Broken the Oil Market - CleanTechnica

Trump's War on Iran Has Broken the Oil Market - CleanTechnica

The article argues that global oil demand has undergone a fundamental shift, transitioning from inelastic to elastic. Historically, consumption remained stable regardless of price fluctuations, only dropping during severe economic crises. However, recent data indicates that demand is now significantly more responsive to fuel prices, shrinking substantially even without an overarching recession. This suggests that modern economies are becoming less dependent on oil, driven by structural changes and efficiency improvements rather than just temporary market shocks. This elasticity implies that the threshold for "demand destruction" is lowering. High fuel prices that previously failed to curb consumption in earlier decades are now sufficient to cause sharp declines in usage. Consequently, the price level required to stabilize the market is dropping, meaning producers may face reduced revenue at lower barrel prices than expected. This dynamic accelerates the move away from fossil fuels, as consumers and industries readily switch to alternatives or reduce activity when costs rise modestly. This development is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical role of transparent, accessible market analytics in detecting such paradigm shifts. Reliable data from institutions like the EIA and IEA allows analysts to challenge entrenched narratives, such as the assumption of perpetual oil demand growth. By providing clear evidence of changing consumer behavior, open data empowers stakeholders to understand the accelerating energy transition and the geopolitical consequences of market realities, independent of political bias or industry lobbying.
