Dan Estocada Final al INAI

Dan Estocada Final al INAI

The article highlights the planned dissolution of the National Institute for Transparency, Access to Information, and Personal Data Protection (INAI) by the current administration. This decision represents a significant shift in governance, transferring the agency’s functions to standard government departments despite protests from its leadership and civil society advocates. The move is presented as a fiscal consolidation measure, with savings redirected to social welfare funds, marking a pivotal change in how public information is managed and protected. This structural alteration is critical for open data because it dismantles an independent body specifically designed to ensure transparency and safeguard citizens’ right to access government information. Critics argue that eliminating such autonomous organizations erodes accountability mechanisms, potentially leading to a regression in democratic norms. By subordinating transparency functions to executive branches, the reform risks reducing the objectivity and accessibility of public data, thereby weakening the infrastructure that supports informed citizenship and government oversight. The article also notes that the INAI’s disappearance is part of a broader initiative to dissolve several other autonomous commissions, signaling a systemic reduction in institutional checks and balances. This trend underscores the political priority placed on centralizing control over specialized regulatory and informational bodies. For the open data community, these changes imply a future where data availability and integrity may depend more on political discretion than on independent legal guarantees, fundamentally altering the landscape for civic engagement and data-driven analysis.
