Dark web: Guía práctica para proteger sus datos personales de ciberdelincuentes

The article highlights how personal data ends up on the dark web through large-scale data breaches, phishing, and malware, emphasizing that the anonymity of this network facilitates the illegal trade of information without users’ prior knowledge. The exposure of such data carries serious risks, including identity theft and financial fraud, demonstrating the inherent vulnerability of information in today’s digital ecosystem. To mitigate these risks, an immediate and proactive response is recommended, including the urgent change of passwords, the activation of multi-factor authentication, and continuous monitoring of bank accounts. Effective protection depends on combining robust technical measures with good digital hygiene practices, such as being skeptical of unsolicited communications and carefully managing privacy settings on social media to reduce the attack surface. This topic is fundamental to the field of open data because it illustrates the fragile line between information availability and individual privacy. As data becomes more democratized and openly shared, the need to understand how this exposure can be exploited by malicious actors becomes critical. The analysis underscores the importance of implementing data governance frameworks and cybersecurity tools that protect citizens, ensuring that transparency does not compromise personal security in a hyperconnected world.
