¡No, no eres tú, es el planeta! El 2024 se perfila para ser el más cálido de todos los tiempos: OMM

The World Meteorological Organization confirms that 2024 is on track to be the warmest year on record, with temperatures temporarily exceeding the critical 1.5°C threshold above pre-industrial levels. This alarming trend underscores the accelerating pace of climate change, driven by increasing greenhouse gas concentrations, and signals a shift toward a new reality characterized by unprecedented extreme weather events such as floods, heatwaves, and intense storms. The implications are severe, as these climatic shifts exacerbate health crises, deepen social inequalities, and threaten sustainable development, disproportionately affecting vulnerable populations. The rapid loss of ice, rising sea levels, and ocean warming further amplify risks, demonstrating that every fraction of warming intensifies dangers. This situation serves as a stark warning that current trajectories undermine global stability and economic resilience, demanding urgent attention to mitigate cascading consequences. This article is highly relevant to open data because it highlights the critical need for transparent, accessible, and standardized climate information. Reliable, openly shared datasets from international organizations enable citizens, researchers, and policymakers to monitor these vital trends, foster public awareness, and drive informed decision-making. Ensuring data accessibility empowers communities to understand risks and advocate for necessary climate action based on clear, undeniable evidence.
