Planned Parenthood must comply with Missouri AG request for patient records, court rules

Planned Parenthood must comply with Missouri AG request for patient records, court rules

A Missouri appeals court has ruled that Planned Parenthood Great Rivers must comply with civil investigative demands from the state Attorney General regarding gender-affirming care for minors. This decision overturns the organization’s reliance on blanket claims that federal patient privacy laws prevent them from disclosing records. The court determined that while individual privacy rights are protected, they do not constitute an absolute exemption from state investigations into potential deceptive medical practices. The ruling clarifies that providers can satisfy legal obligations by submitting de-identified records rather than unredacted patient data. The appellate judges noted that the organization failed to provide a proper privilege log to identify specific documents requiring protection, which weakened their legal defense. This establishes that covered entities bear the burden of complying with subpoenas while navigating HIPAA constraints, rather than claiming total immunity from scrutiny under consumer protection laws. This case is significant for open data advocacy because it delineates the boundary between legitimate public oversight and the protection of sensitive health information. It demonstrates how courts can balance transparency in healthcare practices with patient confidentiality, setting a precedent for how investigative bodies may access medical data in future inquiries. By mandating the production of anonymized datasets, the ruling supports the principle that essential public health information should remain accessible for regulatory purposes without compromising individual privacy.
