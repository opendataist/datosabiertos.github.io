IA y derechos de autor: ¿uso justo o abuso encubierto?

IA y derechos de autor: ¿uso justo o abuso encubierto?

The expansion of artificial intelligence raises a fundamental ethical dilemma concerning copyright and intellectual property. While major technology companies push to broaden the concept of "fair use" in order to train their models without consent or compensation, a dangerous double standard is emerging. The industry justifies this practice using geopolitical arguments about competitiveness, yet it ignores the fact that innovation should not be based on the free appropriation of the creative work of artists, writers, and musicians, thereby calling into question the ethical legitimacy of their accelerated growth. The opacity surrounding data usage and the lack of clear regulations leave creators in a vulnerable position, facing corporate indifference and governmental passivity. This asymmetry not only threatens the economic viability of creative industries but also endangers cultural diversity and digital sovereignty. Without mechanisms for transparency and fairness, technological development risks becoming concentrated in the hands of a few, creating global dependence that mortgages the creative future and consolidates monopolies that fail to recognize the inherent value of original works. This issue is crucial for the open data movement, as the opacity in AI training contradicts the principles of transparency and equitable access that the movement promotes. If the data used to train proprietary models are collective cultural resources, their unilateral exploitation undermines the democratic governance of information. Promoting ethical practices, such as explicit consent and fair compensation, is essential to ensure that artificial intelligence serves the common good rather than concentrating power, guaranteeing that technological progress is not built upon the silent expropriation of human creativity.
