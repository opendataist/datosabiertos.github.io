El Proyecto Hertha y la supervisión IA con anonimización en sistemas de pago retail

El Proyecto Hertha y la supervisión IA con anonimización en sistemas de pago retail

The article examines the tension between state fiscal control and individual privacy in the era of digital finance, highlighting the European Union's push for Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs). Unlike physical cash, which ensures anonymity, digital transactions leave permanent records that authorities can monitor in real time. This shift transforms money from a neutral medium of exchange into a tool for continuous surveillance, raising critical ethical questions about the extent to which governments should track citizens' economic activities to combat illicit finance and terrorism. A key development in this landscape is the Hertha Project by the Bank for International Settlements, which utilizes artificial intelligence and synthetic datasets. By employing advanced analytics and privacy-enhancing technologies such as differential privacy, the project aims to detect suspicious patterns instantly while minimizing false positives. This approach seeks to balance regulatory efficiency with the protection of personal data, marking a significant evolution from traditional sampling methods to comprehensive, algorithmic oversight of financial flows. This discussion is vital for open data because it illustrates the complex trade-offs inherent in modern data ecosystems. It challenges developers and policymakers to design systems that maximize transparency and security without infringing on fundamental privacy rights. The Hertha model demonstrates how open data techniques, when combined with ethical constraints and anonymization, can support public interest goals. Ultimately, it urges the open data community to engage with the technical and legal frameworks that define the limits of state power in the digital age.
