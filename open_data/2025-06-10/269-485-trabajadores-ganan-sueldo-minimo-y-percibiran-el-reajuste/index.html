269.485 trabajadores ganan sueldo mínimo y percibirán el reajuste

The article highlights a significant disparity in the Paraguayan labor market, where nearly half of all salaried workers earn below the legal minimum wage. This statistic underscores the urgency of salary adjustments, as a substantial portion of the workforce is currently not protected by statutory income floors. The impending minimum wage increase, calculated based on inflation, directly benefits those currently earning the statutory floor, yet it leaves a large segment of employees still below this threshold, revealing systemic gaps in income distribution and worker protection. Relevance to open data lies in how public statistics drive these critical socioeconomic decisions. The National Statistics Institute’s employment data and the Central Bank’s inflation reports provide the empirical foundation for policy debates. Transparency in these datasets allows stakeholders to verify claims, understand the scale of underpayment, and assess the real impact of inflation on purchasing power. When official figures are openly accessible and rigorously analyzed, they empower civil society and government bodies to demand more equitable labor policies rather than relying on opaque calculations. Ultimately, the discussion reveals a tension between rigid inflation-based adjustments and the need for living wages that reflect actual consumption needs. Authorities are exploring new methodologies, such as a specialized worker basket, to supplement traditional indices. This shift suggests that open, accessible data on household consumption patterns is essential for developing more nuanced and fair economic models. By leveraging transparent statistical evidence, policymakers can move beyond standard inflation metrics to craft solutions that genuinely address the cost-of-living challenges faced by vulnerable workers.
