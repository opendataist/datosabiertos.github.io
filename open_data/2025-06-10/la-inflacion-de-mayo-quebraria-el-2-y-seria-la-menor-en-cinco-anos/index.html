La inflación de mayo quebraría el 2% y sería la menor en cinco años

The upcoming release of the May Consumer Price Index by INDEC is expected to show a significant deceleration in inflation, potentially dropping below or reaching the 2% threshold. This projection, supported by private analysts and economic institutions, suggests a return to a downward trend after previous months saw higher rates. If accurate, this figure would represent the lowest monthly inflation rate in five years, marking a notable shift in Argentina’s economic trajectory and signaling a break from the persistent price increases seen in recent periods. Several economic consultancies predict similar results, with estimates ranging between 1.8% and 2.1%. These forecasts highlight a broad consensus that inflation is finally breaking the psychologically significant two percent barrier. Key drivers for this slowdown include exchange rate unification, reduced taxes, and stable market expectations. The data indicates that specific sectors, such as food and beverages, are also showing moderation, contributing to a more stable overall price environment compared to the high volatility of recent years. This article is highly relevant to open data as it underscores the critical importance of transparent, high-quality statistical releases from national institutes like INDEC. In an environment filled with private estimates and economic speculation, official data serves as the essential anchor for public trust and informed decision-making. Furthermore, it demonstrates how open, accessible inflation metrics allow researchers, citizens, and markets to analyze economic health independently, verifying policy impacts and fostering accountability in governance.
