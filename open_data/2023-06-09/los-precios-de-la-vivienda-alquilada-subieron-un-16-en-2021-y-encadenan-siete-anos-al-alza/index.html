Los precios de la vivienda alquilada subieron un 1,6% en 2021 y encadenan siete años al alza

The rental housing price index rose by 1.6% in 2021, marking the seventh consecutive year of increases. While this growth did not reach the peak seen in 2019, it highlights a sustained upward trend across Spain. The data reveals that new contracts experienced significantly higher price hikes compared to existing ones, indicating distinct market dynamics between fresh leases and those already in place. Larger homes also saw steeper increases than smaller units, reflecting specific demand pressures on property size. Regionally, the Community of Valencia recorded the highest price surge, followed closely by Castilla-La Mancha and Murcia. In contrast, major urban centers like Madrid and Catalonia saw more moderate gains. Over the period from 2015 to 2021, provincial capitals like Valencia and Palma de Mallorca led in cumulative price growth. This geographic disparity underscores how local economic factors and housing supply constraints drive rental costs differently across the country, rather than through a uniform national trend. This article is relevant to open data because it relies on a statistical model designed to overcome data gaps, specifically the absence of comparative data for previously unrented properties. By using tax agency records to estimate rental values where direct comparisons are missing, the INE demonstrates how open and administrative data can be integrated to create comprehensive economic indicators. This approach ensures that rental market analyses remain robust and representative, even when historical transactional data is incomplete, thereby enhancing the reliability of public statistical tools for policy and research.
