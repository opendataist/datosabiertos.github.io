No more drinking from the VC tap: Jill Berry on the importance of profitability at new startups

The startup landscape has shifted dramatically from growth-at-all-costs to immediate viability and profitability. Investors and macroeconomic conditions now prioritize clear paths to revenue and liquidity, rendering the era of easy venture capital funding obsolete. For new companies, especially in the open data sector, demonstrating a robust financial model and rapid time-to-market is essential for survival, as grants and subsidies become less reliable. In the open data space, granular energy usage data is unlocking significant value beyond traditional financial metrics. By analyzing real-time peaks and troughs, businesses can provide precise, personalized insights for solar adoption, eliminating guesswork and accelerating decision-making. This shift from vague estimates to accurate, consented data sharing reduces friction for consumers, enabling seamless connections to service providers without the need for physical installations or complex hardware. This trend highlights the growing importance of strategic partnerships over solitary development in the open economy. Companies are increasingly finding that wholesaling data capabilities or integrating them as features for other software providers is more efficient than building everything internally. This collaborative approach accelerates innovation and market reach, demonstrating that open data thrives not just as a standalone product but as an integrated component that simplifies user experiences and drives collective industry growth.
