Judge: DOGE works in ‘unusual secrecy,' must comply with FOIA - Washington Examiner

Judge: DOGE works in ‘unusual secrecy,' must comply with FOIA - Washington Examiner

A federal judge has ordered the Department of Government Efficiency to preserve records and provide status updates, highlighting significant transparency concerns. The ruling addresses fears that DOGE staff, accustomed to private-sector norms, may be neglecting federal document retention policies. This includes the use of encrypted messaging apps and external servers, actions that suggest a potential disregard for public accountability standards essential to open data practices. The court emphasized that despite claims of limited authority, DOGE exercises substantial power over federal agencies, as evidenced by high-profile personnel changes and public statements by leadership. This discrepancy between official descriptions and actual operational impact underscores the necessity for rigorous oversight. Without clear transparency, the public and legislators cannot effectively assess how these unprecedented interventions affect government functions and resource allocation. While the Department of Justice argues that FOIA requests should be deferred, the judge’s interim injunction signals that secrecy regarding DOGE’s operations is unacceptable. This case is vital for open data advocates because it establishes a precedent for holding powerful executive entities accountable. It reinforces the principle that government efficiency initiatives must not bypass established legal obligations for information access and record preservation, ensuring that transparency remains integral to democratic governance.
