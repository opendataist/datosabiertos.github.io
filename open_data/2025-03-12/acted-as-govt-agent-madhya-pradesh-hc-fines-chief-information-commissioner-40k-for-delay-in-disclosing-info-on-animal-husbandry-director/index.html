'Acted As Govt Agent': Madhya Pradesh HC Fines Chief Information Commissioner ₹40K For Delay In Disclosing Info On Animal Husbandry Director

'Acted As Govt Agent': Madhya Pradesh HC Fines Chief Information Commissioner ₹40K For Delay In Disclosing Info On Animal Husbandry Director

The court overturned a decision by the Chief Information Commissioner, ruling that government agencies must provide information within thirty days of receiving a request, regardless of internal processing delays or late fee demands. By rejecting the argument that time spent issuing fee notifications should be excluded from this statutory deadline, the judgment establishes that procedural inefficiencies cannot be used to deny timely access to public records. This reinforces the principle that the burden of prompt compliance lies squarely with the state authorities rather than the citizen seeking transparency. Additionally, the judiciary severely reprimanded the state for maintaining factual inaccuracies in its defense, highlighting a lack of accountability within public information offices. The imposition of significant costs on the Chief Information Commissioner serves as a strong deterrent against negligent adjudication and administrative apathy. These measures underscore the critical need for rigorous adherence to procedural standards and honest documentation in handling right to information applications. This ruling is highly relevant to open data advocates as it strengthens the enforceability of transparency laws by prioritizing actual receipt of requests over bureaucratic delays. It clarifies that financial barriers introduced after the statutory period has expired do not absolve agencies of their duty to disclose, thereby ensuring that open data initiatives remain accessible and efficient. The decision promotes a culture of responsibility, encouraging institutions to streamline their data release processes to avoid legal penalties and uphold public trust in governance transparency.
