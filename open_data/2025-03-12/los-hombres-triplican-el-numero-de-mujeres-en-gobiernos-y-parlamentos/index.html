Los hombres triplican el número de mujeres en gobiernos y parlamentos

Los hombres triplican el número de mujeres en gobiernos y parlamentos

The report concludes that global progress toward gender equality in political leadership remains critically insufficient, with a significant regression in recent years. Despite decades of advocacy, men continue to dominate government and parliamentary roles by a ratio of at least three to one. This stagnation and slight decline indicate a systemic failure to achieve parity, undermining the democratic ideal of inclusive decision-making and highlighting that the promise of equal political participation remains unfulfilled thirty years after the Beijing Declaration. The analysis reveals deep regional disparities and persistent gender biases in the allocation of ministerial portfolios. While some regions show moderate representation, others lag significantly, and women are largely confined to "soft" ministries such as social affairs, while retaining minimal presence in power centers such as defense, finance, and foreign affairs. This structural segregation suggests that even when women enter politics, they are often excluded from the core levers of state power, perpetuating a hierarchy that marginalizes their influence on critical national and international policies. This data is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible, and gender-disaggregated datasets to hold governments accountable. Reliable statistics from organizations like the Inter-Parliamentary Union allow activists, policymakers, and civil society to identify specific gaps, monitor trends, and advocate for evidence-based reforms. Without open access to this information, it is impossible to accurately assess the scale of exclusion or measure the impact of interventions aimed at dismantling the systemic barriers preventing women from exercising equal political power.
