Ciberseguridad en México: alerta por aumento de estafas online

Ciberseguridad en México: alerta por aumento de estafas online

Mexican mobile users face cyberattack rates significantly higher than the global average, driven by widespread social engineering and AI-based fraud. This alarming reality has triggered a unanimous demand for enhanced application security, with users viewing developers as primarily responsible for protecting their digital experience. The surge in awareness highlights urgent vulnerabilities requiring immediate attention to restore trust and safety. In response to these threats, consumers are increasingly vigilant, routinely checking applications for privacy and security before use. A vast majority explicitly demands transparency and accountability, indicating they would abandon apps that fail to adequately protect their data. This shift reflects a growing skepticism toward developers who do not prioritize user protection, signaling that technical adequacy is no longer sufficient without clear ethical responsibility. This article is relevant to open data as it underscores the critical intersection between transparency and security. Open data initiatives must address these consumer concerns by promoting verifiable standards and accountability in digital services. By fostering trust through transparent practices, stakeholders can mitigate the fear of data misuse and ensure that open data ecosystems respect user privacy while maintaining robust defenses against modern cyber threats.
