Reducir la pobreza y transitar hacia una mejor calidad de vida

Reducir la pobreza y transitar hacia una mejor calidad de vida

The article underscores the critical role of accurate data in measuring poverty, emphasizing that international standards incorporate social transfers—such as cash or in-kind benefits—into household income assessments. This integration provides a more realistic picture of access to basic needs, ensuring that policy evaluations reflect actual living conditions rather than nominal earnings alone. Without capturing these variables, assessments of social welfare remain incomplete and potentially misleading. Despite improvements in data coverage and transparency, poverty remains widespread, with a significant portion of the population still lacking basic necessities. The data reveal a troubling trend: economic growth is not translating into genuine well-being, as many people rely on temporary social safety nets rather than sustainable employment. While children and the elderly benefit from these transfers, this masks deeper structural problems, including precarious labor conditions for working-age adults and compromised educational outcomes for youth. This analysis is crucial for open data initiatives because it demonstrates how granular, transparent datasets can expose the limitations of fragmented social policies. It argues that data should not merely track poverty reduction but also highlight quality of life and long-term sustainability. By making these relationships visible, open data empowers citizens and policymakers to demand holistic solutions that address root causes, ensuring that social protection complements rather than substitutes for robust economic and social systems.
