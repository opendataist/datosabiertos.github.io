The INEGI revealed that the maquiladora industry allocates 96 percent of its budget to importing inputs, primarily from the U.S., a structural challenge for Claudia Sheinbaum’s administration amid extreme dependence that condemns workers to low wages and accelerates the exodus of young Mexican talent.

The article reveals a structural contradiction in the Mexican manufacturing industry: although domestic sales have grown significantly, the country remains extremely dependent on foreign inputs. The vast majority of resources are allocated to international suppliers, which prevents the retention of wealth and the consolidation of genuine industrial independence, leaving the local economy vulnerable to corporate decisions made abroad. This dependence has profound social consequences, limiting the valuation of employment and fostering brain drain. By operating primarily as basic assembly centers, companies offer low wages and limited career opportunities, which stagnates technological development and weakens the economic base of border cities, making them fragile in the face of global crises. This case is relevant to open data because it highlights the importance of monitoring and analyzing the composition of supply chains through official statistics. Transparency in data on import expenditures versus local purchases is crucial for identifying structural gaps, designing effective public policies, and fostering industrial sovereignty based on quantitative evidence.
