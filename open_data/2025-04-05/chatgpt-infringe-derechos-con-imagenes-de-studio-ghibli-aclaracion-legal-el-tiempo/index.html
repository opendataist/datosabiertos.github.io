¿ChatGPT Infringe Derechos con imágenes de Studio Ghibli? Aclaración Legal - El Tiempo

¿ChatGPT Infringe Derechos con imágenes de Studio Ghibli? Aclaración Legal - El Tiempo

The viral trend of using AI to replicate Studio Ghibli’s art style highlights a critical legal ambiguity regarding intellectual property in the age of generative technology. While adopting a general aesthetic is legally permissible, reproducing specific protected elements like characters or scenes constitutes infringement. This distinction underscores the urgent need for clear guidelines on how artistic inspiration crosses into unauthorized replication when mediated by algorithms. Responsibility for potential violations is shared between users and developers, creating a complex liability landscape. Users face risks when commercializing AI-generated content without proper licensing, while developers may be held accountable if their models were trained on copyrighted works without consent. This dual accountability emphasizes that the tool itself is not exempt from legal scrutiny, forcing both creators and technology providers to exercise greater caution and transparency in their operations. This issue is highly relevant to open data because it challenges the current norms of data scraping and model training. It forces a re-evaluation of whether datasets used to train AI systems respect creators' rights or exploit public information indiscriminately. As the debate intensifies, it calls for more robust frameworks that balance innovation with the protection of intellectual property, ensuring that the development of open technologies does not come at the expense of original artistic creators.
