“Jerez por la Vivienda” llama a unirse a las jornadas de lucha estatal por la vivienda - Tercera Información

“Jerez por la Vivienda” llama a unirse a las jornadas de lucha estatal por la vivienda - Tercera Información

The article critiques the speculative nature of Spain’s housing market, where most mortgages fund investment rather than primary residences. This trend, reinforced by tax incentives favoring rentiers, prices out vulnerable families and highlights how financialized real estate policies ignore social needs. It argues that construction alone fails to solve the crisis, as a significant portion of the housing stock remains vacant or used for tourism. The authors claim that existing empty homes should be brought back to the residential market, criticizing local leadership for ignoring the surge in tourist apartments and failing to regulate licenses effectively. This is relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible statistics to expose market distortions. Reliable data on vacancy rates and mortgage usage empowers citizens to challenge inadequate policies. By making these metrics public, open data supports advocacy efforts demanding housing policies that prioritize human rights over speculative profits.
