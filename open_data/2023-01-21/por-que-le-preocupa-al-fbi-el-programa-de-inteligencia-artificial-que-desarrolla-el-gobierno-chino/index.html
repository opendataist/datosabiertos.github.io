Por qué le preocupa al FBI el programa de inteligencia artificial que desarrolla el gobierno chino

Christopher Wray, Director of the FBI, warned during the Davos Forum about the geopolitical risks posed by artificial intelligence developed in China. He pointed out that this technological progress is not subject to the rule of law and is based on the alleged illegal accumulation of intellectual property and confidential data stolen over the years. This stance reflects growing U.S. concern regarding Beijing's strategic capabilities in this emerging sector. The relevance to open data lies in the tension between global technological collaboration and national security. While open access to data and models accelerates innovation, accusations of economic espionage underscore the critical need for robust ethical and legal frameworks. Professionals must consider how to protect the integrity of datasets while fostering transparency, ensuring that information does not become a tool of state coercion or a source of disinformation. Ultimately, the debate drives the need to establish clear international standards for data and AI exchange. Without rules that guarantee the legitimate origin of information and respect for property rights, the open-source community faces existential challenges. The solution does not lie in isolation, but in building digital infrastructures that balance open innovation with legal certainty and data sovereignty.
