Con tecnología no sacrificamos la privacidad

Con tecnología no sacrificamos la privacidad

The rejection of an unconstitutionality action allows the Central Bank of Costa Rica (BCCR) to receive banking and tax data without anonymizing them, thereby concentrating sensitive information within a single institution. This centralization poses significant risks of power abuse targeting political profiles and attracts cybercriminals seeking to exploit technical vulnerabilities or management errors, exposing the country to potential large-scale security breaches. To mitigate these dangers, pseudonymization is proposed as a viable technical alternative. This method employs a trusted third party to replace real identifiers with artificial codes during data transfer, ensuring that neither the originating entities nor the BCCR can directly link the data to specific individuals. In this way, the referential integrity necessary for reliable statistics is maintained, eliminating the ability to conduct personal tracking without sacrificing the quality of aggregated analysis. This proposal is crucial for the open data movement because it balances the transparency and utility of public data with the protection of individual privacy. By adopting international standards such as pseudonymization, Costa Rica could strengthen citizen trust and demonstrate that access to government information can coexist with robust security safeguards, establishing an ethical and technical precedent for the responsible handling of sensitive data in the digital age.
