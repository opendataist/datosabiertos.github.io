Inspectores habían aprobado plan de emergencia de Camp Mystic dos días antes de la tragedia

Following the tragedy at Camp Mystic, where more than two dozen people lost their lives, it was revealed that inspectors had approved the camp’s emergency plan just days before the disaster. Although the camp formally complied with state requirements regarding evacuation procedures, the lack of specific details in public reports raises serious doubts about the actual effectiveness of its protocols in the face of concrete and recent weather alerts. The investigation highlights a critical gap in oversight and public access to information, as disaster plans, although legally required, are not archived or made available for external scrutiny beyond the minimum regulatory requirements. This opacity, combined with a lack of clear communication with authorities regarding weather monitoring, suggests that bureaucratic compliance does not guarantee operational safety, especially in an environment prone to flash floods. This case is relevant to open data because it illustrates the limits of government transparency in risk management. The inability to access specific emergency plans prevents independent auditing and systemic learning after disasters. To improve public safety, it is essential that data on safety protocols not only be collected by the state but also be open, accessible, and verifiable by the community and the press, ensuring that regulatory compliance translates into real protection rather than merely filed paperwork.
