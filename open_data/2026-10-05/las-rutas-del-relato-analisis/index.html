Las rutas del relato | Análisis

Las rutas del relato | Análisis

The article challenges the narrative of transparency and administrative order surrounding road management in Entre Ríos, contrasting it with official information revealed by the Provincial Directorate of Roadways itself. Records show that, despite claims of efficiency, the administration lacks clarity regarding cost evolution, featuring massive contract modifications and a concentration of suppliers that creates areas of opacity. This discrepancy suggests that mere compliance with legality does not guarantee real transparency, as citizens cannot easily reconstruct the circuit of public spending. Furthermore, it is highlighted that no public tenders were held in 2024 for road maintenance, resorting instead to exceptional direct contracting. This contradicts the discourse of predictability and order, revealing a significant gap between the image of orderly management and actual practice. The lack of detail in the supporting documentation for these decisions deepens doubts about the administration’s true efficiency, prompting reflection on whether transparency is limited to responding to formal requests or entails clear and comprehensible accountability. The relevance to *open data* lies in the fact that the formal availability of information does not equate to its usefulness for citizen scrutiny. The case illustrates that data must be sufficiently complete and detailed to allow the reconstruction of processes, not merely to meet legal requirements. The gap between the proclaimed transparency and the opaque complexity of the figures underscores the need for open data to genuinely facilitate understanding of public spending, preventing “sophisticated opacity”—achieved through the provision of fragmented or insufficient information—from substituting for effective accountability.
