Internet Archive's digital book lending violates copyright, US judge rules

Internet Archive's digital book lending violates copyright, US judge rules

A U.S. judge ruled that the Internet Archive’s practice of scanning copyrighted books and lending them digitally constitutes infringement, rejecting the organization's claim of "fair use." The court determined that providing digital copies did not transform the works sufficiently, as these e-books effectively replaced authorized library licenses rather than serving a distinct educational purpose. This decision establishes a significant legal boundary for digital preservation and access, confirming that while physical book lending remains permissible, bulk digitization of protected works without permission violates copyright laws. The ruling challenges the Internet Archive’s model of controlled digital lending, which aimed to expand access during library closures by allowing concurrent digital borrowing beyond traditional limits. This case is highly relevant to open data and open access movements because it highlights the tension between maximizing information availability and respecting intellectual property rights. It signals that efforts to democratize access to scholarly and cultural materials must navigate complex legal frameworks, potentially impacting how open initiatives scale and distribute digital content in the future.
