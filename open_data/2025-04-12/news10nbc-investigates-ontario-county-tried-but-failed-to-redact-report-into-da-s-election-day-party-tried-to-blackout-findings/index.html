News10NBC Investigates: Ontario County tried but failed to redact report into DA's Election Day party, tried to blackout findings

News10NBC Investigates: Ontario County tried but failed to redact report into DA's Election Day party, tried to blackout findings

The Ontario County investigation confirmed that the District Attorney and his staff violated workplace conduct policies by hosting an unprofessional party with alcohol in a courtroom and jury room. This breach of professional standards raises serious concerns about the misuse of taxpayer resources and the erosion of public trust in a fundamental law enforcement institution, highlighting how internal misconduct can directly undermine the integrity of the judicial system. Despite the county’s attempts to conceal these findings through excessive redaction of an official report, the core violations remained visible. The investigation revealed that leadership not only engaged in prohibited behavior but also failed to cooperate with the inquiry by discussing the matter among staff during working hours. This lack of transparency and resistance to accountability demonstrates a systemic failure in oversight, suggesting that those in power may prioritize protecting their reputation over adhering to established ethical guidelines. This case is critically relevant to open data because it illustrates the tangible value of freedom of information laws in exposing administrative failures. When governments attempt to hide misconduct through redaction, persistent journalism and public scrutiny can often pierce these opaque barriers, ensuring that critical information about public servant behavior remains accessible. It underscores that transparency is not just a procedural formality but a necessary check on power, allowing citizens to hold elected officials accountable for actions that contradict their public duties.
