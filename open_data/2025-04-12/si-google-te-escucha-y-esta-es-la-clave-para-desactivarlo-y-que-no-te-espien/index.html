Sí, Google te escucha y esta es la clave para desactivarlo y que no te espíen

The article highlights the tension between the convenience of Google Assistant and the significant privacy risks inherent in its always-on listening capabilities. It explains that the system often records private conversations due to "false activations," storing audio data that is used to train AI models and sometimes reviewed by human annotators. This reveals how user intimacy is commodified for technological improvement, raising serious concerns about data security and the potential for misuse or accidental exposure of sensitive personal information. Beyond voice commands, the platform aggregates extensive contextual data, including precise location, device identifiers, and information from connected applications and contacts. This comprehensive data collection allows for hyper-personalized services but creates a detailed digital profile of the user’s life. The implication is that achieving this level of convenience requires a substantial surrender of personal autonomy, as the system continuously monitors the environment to provide contextual relevance, blurring the line between helpful assistance and intrusive surveillance. This topic is crucial for the open data community because it exemplifies the challenges of data minimization and transparency in large-scale tech ecosystems. It underscores the necessity for users to actively manage their digital footprints through specific configuration settings, such as disabling voice activity storage or restricting microphone permissions. By understanding these mechanisms, individuals can reclaim control over their information, advocating for a balanced approach where technology serves without compromising fundamental privacy rights.
