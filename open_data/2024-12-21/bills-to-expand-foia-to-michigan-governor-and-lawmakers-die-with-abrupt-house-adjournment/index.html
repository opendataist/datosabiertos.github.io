Bills to expand FOIA to Michigan governor and lawmakers die with abrupt House adjournment

Legislation to expand Michigan’s Freedom of Information Act died in the state House, leaving the governor’s office and Legislature exempt from public records laws. This failure prevents Michigan from aligning with national standards, as it remains one of the few states excluding these major branches from transparency requirements. The bills had previously secured bipartisan support in the Senate, demonstrating a broad consensus on the need for improved government accountability. Despite significant cross-ideological collaboration among lawmakers and diverse advocacy groups, the early adjournment of the legislative session caused this critical reform to collapse, highlighting the fragility of open data initiatives during political transitions. This outcome is highly relevant to open data advocates, illustrating how procedural timing can override substantive policy agreements. It underscores the ongoing struggle to maintain comprehensive public access to governmental information and emphasizes the necessity of sustained, cross-partisan pressure to ensure that digital transparency frameworks include all key government entities.
