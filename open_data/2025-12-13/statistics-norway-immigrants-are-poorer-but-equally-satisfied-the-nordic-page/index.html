Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

Immigrants in Norway experience significantly poorer living conditions, including lower income, housing standards, and higher rates of mental health issues and loneliness compared to the general population. Despite these substantial socioeconomic and psychological challenges, this demographic reports life satisfaction levels equal to those of the native population, highlighting a complex disconnect between objective material well-being and subjective happiness. This paradox is further complicated by the finding that immigrants exhibit notably higher confidence in core state institutions, such as the political system, judiciary, and police, than native Norwegians. This strong institutional trust and affiliation suggest that integration is not merely about economic parity but involves a deep sense of belonging and faith in societal structures, even when individual circumstances remain difficult. These insights are highly relevant to open data because they demonstrate how transparent, comprehensive statistical reporting can reveal nuanced social realities that single-dimensional metrics might obscure. By making detailed survey data accessible, researchers and policymakers can move beyond simplistic narratives of failure or success. Instead, they can understand the multifaceted nature of integration, fostering evidence-based strategies that address specific barriers while leveraging existing strengths like institutional trust.
