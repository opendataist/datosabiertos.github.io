Masivo uso de la Ley de Transparencia: 10.738 casos iniciados y más de 6 millones de visitas al portal | Puranoticia.cl

Masivo uso de la Ley de Transparencia: 10.738 casos iniciados y más de 6 millones de visitas al portal | Puranoticia.cl

Chile is experiencing a significant surge in the use of transparency laws, with citizens increasingly accessing public information regarding government spending and decisions. This trend is supported by improved efficiency in handling complaints, as authorities deliver requested data more frequently and quickly through alternative dispute resolution mechanisms. The reduced rate of inadmissible cases also indicates that the public is using these tools more effectively and with greater precision, reflecting a maturing engagement with legal rights. The relevance to open data lies in the systemic shift toward accountability and the empowerment of citizens as active demanders of information. The state’s enforcement actions, including sanctions against non-compliant entities, demonstrate a concrete institutional response to correct opaque practices. This regulatory framework ensures that the right to know is not just theoretical but actively enforced, thereby strengthening the infrastructure of transparency and reinforcing public trust in democratic institutions. Furthermore, the consolidation of a transparency culture extends to lobbying activities, although recent data suggests a potential decrease in the usage of related online portals. The overarching implication is that access to information has become a fundamental tool for civic oversight, changing the relationship between the state and individuals. By normalizing the request for accountability, the transparency framework fosters a more informed society capable of demanding responsible governance from authorities at all levels.
