Fraser Eadie: Scotland has had housing association FOI since 2019 - what can England learn?

The introduction of Social Tenant Access to Information Requirements in England marks a significant shift toward greater transparency in social housing management, mirroring Scotland’s earlier adoption of freedom of information laws for registered social landlords. This convergence highlights a critical transition where tenants gain the legal right to request detailed information about the management of their homes, forcing housing associations to confront the realities of their internal data systems and record-keeping practices. Scotland’s experience demonstrates that while fears of an overwhelming volume of requests often do not materialize, the primary challenge lies in the ability to locate and assemble accurate, comprehensive records from disparate sources. The complexity increases significantly when management functions are outsourced, requiring landlords to ensure contractors maintain clear documentation. Consequently, the effectiveness of transparency laws depends less on the quantity of inquiries and more on the reliability and accessibility of the underlying data regarding maintenance, inspections, and decision-making processes. This article is highly relevant to the open data community as it underscores that true accountability requires robust, interconnected data infrastructures rather than mere policy changes. It illustrates that without standardized, timely, and linked records, organizations struggle to provide trustworthy accounts of their operations. For open data advocates, this serves as a practical case study in the necessity of designing systems that facilitate easy retrieval and integration of information across different stakeholders, ensuring that transparency is operationalized through sound data governance rather than becoming an administrative burden.
