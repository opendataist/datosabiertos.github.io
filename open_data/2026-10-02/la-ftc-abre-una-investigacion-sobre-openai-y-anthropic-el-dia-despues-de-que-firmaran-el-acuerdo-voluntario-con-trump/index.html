La FTC abre una investigación sobre OpenAI y Anthropic — el día después de que firmaran el acuerdo voluntario con Trump

La FTC abre una investigación sobre OpenAI y Anthropic — el día después de que firmaran el acuerdo voluntario con Trump

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC) has launched a formal investigation into major artificial intelligence companies, questioning the effectiveness of recently signed voluntary safety agreements. This action contradicts the self-regulation stance promoted by the White House and reveals a disconnect within the government: one agency seeks to protect consumers, while another encourages technological innovation without immediate restrictions. The contrast between political support for the industry and regulatory oversight suggests that supervision based on self-imposed standards may be insufficient in the face of emerging risks. The FTC is evaluating whether these companies have violated the law by allowing their models to cause harm to users, leveraging the FTC’s authority to act even in the absence of specific legislation. The investigation was already underway before public security incidents, indicating a structural concern for consumer protection rather than a reactive response. By demanding evidence and sworn testimony, the FTC aims to determine executives’ responsibility for the autonomous behavior of their algorithms, establishing a crucial precedent regarding who is accountable for AI actions. This development is particularly relevant to the open data sector, as it calls into question the real transparency of AI systems. The obligation to testify could force companies to disclose details about how their models are trained and deployed—fundamental elements typically protected as trade secrets. If the FTC demonstrates that certain practices are deceptive or unfair, it could pave the way for demanding higher standards of openness and explainability, balancing technological innovation with the accountability necessary for public trust.
