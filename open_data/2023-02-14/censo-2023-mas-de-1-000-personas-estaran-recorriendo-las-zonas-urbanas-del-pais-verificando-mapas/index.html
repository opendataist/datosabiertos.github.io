Censo 2023: más de 1.000 personas estarán recorriendo las zonas urbanas del país verificando mapas

Uruguay begins preparing for its national census with a geospatial verification phase, in which census takers confirm the accuracy of addresses by cross-referencing them with data from public utilities. This priority process aims to correct errors in the base maps, ensuring that subsequent logistics efficiently cover the entire national territory. By limiting interaction to the validation of locations without collecting personal data, the National Institute of Statistics (INE) ensures the fundamental accuracy needed for a complete and reliable demographic data collection. The relevance of this article to open data science lies in the potential of census results to inform evidence-based public policies. A highly precise georeferenced population enables the analysis of detailed spatial patterns, facilitating the strategic planning of essential services such as education and health. The quality of the data infrastructure established in this initial stage determines the future utility of public datasets, promoting transparency and efficiency in the allocation of state resources. Finally, the methodology combines digital self-census with traditional fieldwork, aiming to maximize citizen participation and reduce operational burden. The success of the process depends on balancing technological adoption with physical presence in areas not reached digitally. By learning from regional experiences and optimizing personnel recruitment, the INE seeks to overcome historical coverage barriers, ensuring that the final data accurately reflect the diversity and needs of Uruguayan society.
