International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women: The reality faced by Mexican women

International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women: The reality faced by Mexican women

The publication of official data on homicides against women in Mexico, although showing a downward trend in recent years, reveals a structural crisis in which firearms remain the predominant means of perpetrating these crimes. This report highlights the importance of distinguishing between general homicide statistics and femicides, emphasizing that gender-based killings represent the culmination of a continuum of violence. Transparency in these data allows for the identification of significant regional disparities, revealing that security conditions and state responses vary drastically depending on geographic location. The relevance of this article for open data lies in its ability to demystify figures and demand a critical interpretation of public information. By explaining that the indicators originate from different stages of the justice system and do not represent a single, continuous tracking of cases, INEGI demonstrates how data openness must be accompanied by clear technical documentation. This is essential to prevent misunderstandings and ensure that citizens and civil society organizations use the information for rigorous analysis rather than as sensationalist headlines. Finally, the use of diverse sources, such as death statistics and judicial records, underscores the need to integrate multiple datasets to achieve a holistic understanding of the problem. This methodology promotes accountability by showing not only the victims but also institutional responses, ranging from preliminary investigations to final sentences. In an open data context, this multidimensional transparency is crucial for designing effective public policies and measuring the real impact of gender-based violence prevention strategies.
