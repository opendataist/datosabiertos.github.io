Economic activity fell 1.2% in September, confirming a technical recession

Economic activity fell 1.2% in September, confirming a technical recession

Argentina’s economic activity contracted year-on-year in the third quarter, technically confirming the onset of a recession with two consecutive periods of seasonally adjusted decline. Although annual GDP still shows a slight positive expansion, analysts have revised their 2026 growth forecasts downward, pointing to substantially lower growth than initially expected. This scenario reflects a broad-based slowdown, with only specific sectors such as mining and construction managing to outperform. The divergence among sectors is notable, with some areas like fishing experiencing sharp declines that drag down the overall indicator. However, the slight rebound in September offers cautious signs of recovery heading into the end of the year, suggesting that the economy could experience a modest rebound in the coming months. This dynamic underscores the current fragility of the national productive fabric in the face of structural challenges. This report is relevant for data transparency because it highlights the role of INDEC (National Institute of Statistics and Censuses) as the official source for key indicators such as the EMAE (Monthly Aggregate Activity Index). The detailed sectoral breakdown enables civil society and researchers to analyze the true state of the economy, fostering accountability and the use of verified public information for political debate and informed decision-making.
