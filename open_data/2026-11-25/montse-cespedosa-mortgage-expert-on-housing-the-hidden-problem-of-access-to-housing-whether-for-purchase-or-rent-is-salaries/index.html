Montse Cespedosa, mortgage expert, on housing: "The hidden problem of access to housing, whether for purchase or rent, is salaries

The article dismantles the thesis that the housing crisis is solely due to housing prices, arguing that the real obstacle is the erosion of purchasing power resulting from the discrepancy between inflation and wage growth. Although the expert claimed that wages grew by only 3.5%, official data confirm a labor cost increase closer to 4%, which nuances her diagnosis but reinforces her central conclusion: there is a real loss of savings capacity and market access for households. It is crucial to distinguish between sale prices and rental prices to avoid erroneous generalizations. While the price of homes for sale has risen by more than 12%, rentals show a more moderate, though still high, dynamic, especially in new contracts. This difference implies that policies for accessing homeownership should not be uniform, as inflationary pressures affect each segment of the real estate market differently, debunking the idea that a one-size-fits-all solution works for both cases. The relevance of this analysis for open data lies in the need to contrast anecdotal claims with verifiable, updated official statistics. The exercise demonstrates how the imprecise use of economic indicators, such as mixing data from different quarters or confusing sales with rentals, can distort public perception. Promoting transparent access to sources like the National Statistics Institute (INE) is essential so that citizens can critically evaluate media narratives and better understand the complex socioeconomic realities that impact their well-being.
