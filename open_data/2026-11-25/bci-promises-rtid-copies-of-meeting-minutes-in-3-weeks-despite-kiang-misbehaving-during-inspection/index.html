BCI promises RTI’d copies of meeting minutes in 3 weeks, despite ‘Kiang misbehaving’ during inspection

BCI promises RTI’d copies of meeting minutes in 3 weeks, despite ‘Kiang misbehaving’ during inspection

The Bar Council of India has reversed its stance on a Right to Information request by Legally India, agreeing to provide copies of sought documents despite previously refusing access. The regulator initially blocked inspection, citing alleged misbehavior by the journalist’s companion, and justified its refusal based on past criticisms leveled by the outlet against the Council. This backtrack indicates a pragmatic shift toward compliance, prioritizing the "ends of justice" over punitive measures regarding procedural disputes or past conflicts. The underlying dispute highlights the tension between regulatory bodies and the press, where accusations of misconduct are used to deny transparency. By allowing the identification and provision of documents rather than enforcing a ban on further inspection, the authority acknowledges the legal necessity of disclosure. This outcome suggests that while institutional resistance may persist, formal appeals can successfully override arbitrary refusals to hide records, particularly when framed as matters of justice rather than mere administrative convenience. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates the fragility of transparency mechanisms when controlled by entities with defensive motivations. It underscores that access to information often requires persistent legal navigation rather than automatic availability. For open data advocates, it serves as a reminder that regulatory bodies may exploit procedural grounds or subjective interpretations of behavior to obstruct access, making robust appeal processes essential for maintaining accountability and public oversight.
