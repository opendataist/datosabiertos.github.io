Justice estimates that more than 41,200 sentence-reduction benefits were granted between 2003 and 2026 - La Tercera

Justice estimates that more than 41,200 sentence-reduction benefits were granted between 2003 and 2026 - La Tercera

The Ministry of Justice has delivered to the Senate more than 1,900 anonymized decrees related to sentence reductions during the administration of President José Antonio Kast. This action responds to a request from the committee tasked with analyzing the constitutional accusation against the Minister of Justice, Fernando Rabat, complementing information provided in previous weeks. The delivery includes both granted benefits and rejected requests, covering a variety of offenses and not exclusively sexual crimes against minors. The significance of this delivery lies in the transparency of administrative processes and public accountability. Senator Raúl Soto emphasized that anonymizing personal data is sufficient to protect privacy, thereby questioning the relevance of keeping the documents confidential. This underscores the importance of allowing public information to be freely scrutinized, ensuring that the justice system operates with clarity and without undue opacity in decisions that affect society. This episode is relevant to the field of open data because it illustrates the tension between the protection of individual privacy and the public’s right to know about government management. By discussing whether the anonymized version meets transparency standards, the principle is reinforced that government data should be accessible by default. The pressure to make these records public demonstrates citizens’ demand for governance based on verifiable information and free access to public administration.
