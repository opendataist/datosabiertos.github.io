Cayetana Álvarez de Toledo accuses Sánchez of attempting to manipulate the voter registry and warns: “The People’s Party will exercise maximum vigilance over this electoral process.

The article highlights the political tensions surrounding electoral matters ahead of Spain’s general elections, focusing on the Popular Party’s allegations concerning the integrity of the electoral register. Cayetana Álvarez de Toledo questions the neutrality of the process and of the Prime Minister, suggesting attempts to manipulate the voter roll in order to maintain power. This distrust has led the Popular Party to strengthen its monitoring of the elections, pledging to ensure a clean and fraud-free process. The relevance of this issue for open data lies in the critical need for transparency and public access to information about census data. The lack of confidence in the accuracy and neutrality of electoral data underpins these political accusations, demonstrating how opacity or the perception of bias in government datasets can erode democracy. The demand for electoral guarantees reflects a citizenry and opposition calling for verifiable and auditable data. Finally, the text emphasizes that the dispute is not only ideological but also technical and procedural, involving the management of sensitive databases. Transparency in the handling of these records is essential to restore institutional trust. This case illustrates how the quality, accessibility, and clarity of open data are fundamental to legitimizing democratic processes and mitigating misinformation or unfounded suspicions about the electoral system.
