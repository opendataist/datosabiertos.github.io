Data shows patients waiting more than three year for diagnosis

Health boards face unsustainable delays in neurodevelopmental assessments for children, with waiting periods exceeding three years. This data highlights a critical systemic failure where resource constraints prevent timely diagnoses for autism and ADHD, leaving families in prolonged uncertainty. The situation underscores the urgent need for reform, as the current model cannot meet the growing demand for specialized support without significant additional funding or structural changes. To address this crisis, services are shifting criteria to prioritize only those children with co-occurring mental health needs. Those who do not meet these stricter thresholds will be redirected to alternative supports while new specialist services are developed. This strategic pivot acknowledges that the existing assessment framework is no longer viable and aims to stabilize service delivery by focusing on acute cases while preparing for a broader, more robust neurodevelopmental support system. This case is relevant to open data as it demonstrates how transparency through freedom of information requests exposes gaps in public health infrastructure. By making waiting list statistics and referral criteria public, stakeholders can hold institutions accountable and advocate for necessary policy adjustments. Such data visibility is essential for driving evidence-based reforms in healthcare, ensuring that vulnerable populations receive the resources and timelines they require for proper diagnosis and care.
