Secret DOJ answer on Trump’s torn-up records finally comes out: report

Secret DOJ answer on Trump’s torn-up records finally comes out: report

The Justice Department twice refused to launch criminal investigations into allegations that Donald Trump destroyed presidential records, despite internal warnings from Archives officials. This decision highlights significant tensions between executive actions and legal accountability, revealing how institutional mechanisms can fail to enforce statutory obligations even when evidence of potential violations is present. The reluctance to pursue charges underscores the complex interplay between national security reviews and record-keeping laws. By prioritizing other investigative avenues, the Department of Justice effectively allowed serious questions about document destruction to go unprosecuted, demonstrating how prosecutorial discretion can impact the preservation of historical truth and governmental transparency. This case is critical to open data because it illustrates the fragility of public record accessibility when political power interferes with legal enforcement. Understanding these dynamics is essential for advocates who rely on transparent, unaltered government documents to hold leaders accountable, emphasizing the need for robust safeguards against the destruction or obstruction of accessible historical data.
