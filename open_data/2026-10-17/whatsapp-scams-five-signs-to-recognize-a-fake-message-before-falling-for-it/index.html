WhatsApp scams: five signs to recognize a fake message before falling for it

WhatsApp scams: five signs to recognize a fake message before falling for it

The article highlights the growing sophistication of digital scams through deceptive messages on messaging platforms and social media, where cybercriminals impersonate identities to steal sensitive data or take control of bank accounts. Official authorities warn that no institution will ever request passwords or verification codes through these channels, emphasizing that direct verification and skepticism toward urgent requests are the first lines of defense against financial fraud. Key warning signs are identified, such as suspicious links mimicking legitimate websites, promises of unrealistic benefits, and requests for money from compromised accounts of acquaintances. The narrative stresses that the visual appearance of the sender does not guarantee authenticity, urging users to verify the web address, look for spelling errors, and confirm the interlocutor’s identity through independent channels before taking any action or making any transfer, thereby avoiding traps designed to exploit panic or greed. This information is particularly relevant to the open_data community, as it underscores the critical importance of integrity and trust in digital ecosystems. By promoting digital literacy and transparency about how identities are falsified, it reinforces the need to build more resilient data infrastructures and robust identity verification mechanisms. This contributes to an environment where open access to information does not compromise user security, fostering a culture of caution and shared responsibility in handling sensitive personal data.
