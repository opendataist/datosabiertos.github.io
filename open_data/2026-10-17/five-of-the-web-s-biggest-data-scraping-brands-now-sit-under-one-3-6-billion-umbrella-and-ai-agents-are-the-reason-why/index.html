Five of the web's biggest data-scraping brands now sit under one $3.6 billion umbrella, and AI agents are the reason why

Five of the web's biggest data-scraping brands now sit under one $3.6 billion umbrella, and AI agents are the reason why

Major web data providers, including Oxylabs and Honeygain, have consolidated under the new umbrella organization SourceSource to better navigate the exploding demand driven by AI agents. This shift highlights a critical trend where artificial intelligence systems require continuous, real-time access to public web data to function effectively. By unifying their operations, these formerly competing brands aim to establish a collective infrastructure capable of handling massive data request volumes, signaling that open web access has become foundational to the current generation of AI development. This consolidation serves as a strategic defense against evolving legal and ethical challenges regarding web scraping. With recent high-profile lawsuits alleging that tools bypass anti-scraping measures, the group aims to present a unified front in advocating for responsible and accessible public data rights. By negotiating access rules from a position of clustered strength, SourceSource seeks to prevent a scenario where only the largest tech corporations can afford licensing deals, thereby preserving the open nature of the web for a broader range of developers and enterprises. For the open data community, this development underscores the increasing tension between data accessibility and corporate control. The move raises important questions about transparency and competition, as former rivals now share common oversight while maintaining independent product strategies. It suggests that the future of open web data may increasingly be shaped by large, consolidated entities capable of lobbying for favorable regulations, potentially influencing how freely AI systems can interact with publicly available information.
