Before Ofcom asked for age checks, gambling sites had been running them for six years

The gambling industry in Britain demonstrates that robust age assurance is achievable when regulators enforce strict, pre-transaction verification mandates. Unlike social media platforms, which have stalled in implementing viable solutions, licensed gambling sites must confirm user identity before any deposit or gameplay. This regulatory pressure has established a clear precedent that age verification is not merely optional but a necessary condition for market access, effectively closing the window for underage participation through mandatory checks rather than self-declaration. This model reveals that age verification serves as more than a safety gate; it enables nuanced, age-specific product controls. By confirming identity upfront, operators can apply differentiated limits, such as stricter betting caps for younger adults, which require precise, verified age data to function correctly. For social networks, this implies that features and access levels could be dynamically adjusted based on verified age bands, moving beyond binary access restrictions to more sophisticated, personalized user experiences grounded in accurate demographic data. The relevance to open data lies in the balance between privacy and regulatory compliance. While the gambling sector leverages existing anti-money laundering infrastructure to manage sensitive identity data, social platforms lack such frameworks, leading to friction and user drop-off. The case illustrates that successful age assurance relies less on technological innovation and more on firm regulatory enforcement. It suggests that without mandatory, standardized verification protocols, open data initiatives regarding user safety and age assurance will remain fragmented and ineffective, highlighting the need for structured, legally binding data handling practices.
