Have a Meta business account? You're consenting to AI scraping now, legal experts say - ZDNET

Have a Meta business account? You're consenting to AI scraping now, legal experts say - ZDNET

Meta has updated its commercial terms to explicitly permit automated web scraping of websites associated with its business accounts, a move that effectively mandates data access for publishers wishing to remain on the platform. Implementing this change without an opt-out mechanism, the policy allows Meta’s bots to crawl, index, and utilize content from news sites and other publishers for training and improving AI models. This expansion of access occurs just before the deadline for enforcement, signaling a significant shift in how tech giants acquire high-quality journalistic data without negotiating individual licensing agreements. This approach highlights the ongoing tension between content creators and AI developers regarding data ownership and compensation. While publishers have historically relied on copyright protections and licensing deals to monetize their content, Meta’s strategy relies on broad contractual language to bypass these norms. The lack of a clear opt-out forces publishers into a binary choice: accept the scraping of their intellectual property or lose access to Meta’s advertising and audience reach. This dynamic underscores the power imbalance where platform operators define the rules of data engagement, often at the expense of content creators' autonomy. This development is critically relevant to the open data movement because it illustrates the erosion of voluntary data sharing norms in favor of compulsory extraction. Open data principles typically emphasize transparency, consent, and controlled access, yet Meta’s terms prioritize unrestricted algorithmic harvesting under the guise of term consolidation. The situation raises urgent questions about how "open" digital ecosystems can function when dominant platforms unilaterally dictate terms that undermine creator rights and intellectual property protections.
