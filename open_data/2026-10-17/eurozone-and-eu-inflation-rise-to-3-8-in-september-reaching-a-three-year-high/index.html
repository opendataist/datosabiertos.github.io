Eurozone and EU inflation rise to 3.8% in September, reaching a three-year high

Eurozone and EU inflation rise to 3.8% in September, reaching a three-year high

Inflation in the euro area and the European Union reached its highest level since late 2023, driven primarily by rising energy and fresh food prices. This increase suggests a slowdown in price stabilization, posing new challenges for economic policy and citizens' purchasing power amid macroeconomic uncertainty. Spain's inflation rate is unfavorable compared to the European average, reflecting structural disparities in cost management among the bloc's major economies. These regional disparities indicate that economic convergence mechanisms require adjustments, as some countries face significantly higher inflationary pressures than their neighbors. This issue is relevant to open data because it demands transparency and public access to detailed, up-to-date economic statistics. Only through open and comparable data can the effectiveness of public policies be audited and civil society enabled to analyze competitiveness gaps among member states.
