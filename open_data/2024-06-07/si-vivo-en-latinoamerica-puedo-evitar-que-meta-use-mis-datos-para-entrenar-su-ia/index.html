Si vivo en Latinoamérica, ¿puedo evitar que Meta use mis datos para entrenar su IA?

Meta has begun using data from posts on Facebook and Instagram to train its generative artificial intelligence, sparking global concern over the unauthorized use of information and the options available to opt out of this practice. However, the ability for users to restrict the use of their personal data through explanatory forms is only available in Europe and some cities in the United States. This lack of options in Latin America has sparked debates about the legal and ethical implications of handling user data for those who cannot access these measures. Meta’s announcement details that the content of posts and photos with descriptions will be used to develop its AI models, although private messages will not be used. This situation has raised questions about the limits of privacy and the need for explicit consent, particularly regarding minors and intimate material. The artistic community has also expressed dissatisfaction over the risk that their works may be used without consent in AI technology. This issue is relevant to open_data because it highlights the tension between the development of AI technologies and the protection of privacy. Decisions about which data can be used and how users can opt out are crucial for setting precedents in the handling of open data. Open debates about the legal and ethical scope of these practices underscore the importance of ensuring that users are informed and make conscious decisions about the information they share on social media.
