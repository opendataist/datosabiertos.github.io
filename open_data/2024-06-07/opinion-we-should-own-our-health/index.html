[OPINION] We should own our health

The article argues that the traditional provider-centric model of health record management fundamentally disempowers patients by treating their medical data as institutional property rather than personal assets. This approach restricts access to fragmented information, creates administrative burdens, and prevents individuals from fully understanding or managing their health. Shifting the paradigm to recognize patients as the true owners of their data is essential for restoring agency, enabling informed medical decisions, and facilitating seamless care across different facilities. Inspired by successful models like Taiwan’s health smart card, the authors propose a digital prototype for the Philippines that consolidates lifetime medical records into a secure, portable format. This technology aims to streamline healthcare delivery, reduce waiting times, and lower administrative costs while allowing patients to track their health proactively. By placing data at the patient’s fingertips, the system empowers individuals to choose their care providers freely and supports better disease management, particularly for those with chronic conditions. For open data advocates, this piece highlights the critical tension between data utility and individual rights. It underscores that open data initiatives in healthcare must prioritize security, interoperability, and inclusivity to avoid exacerbating digital divides or enabling surveillance. True progress requires building trust through robust safeguards and ensuring that digital transformation does not leave underserved populations behind, thereby aligning technological efficiency with ethical data stewardship and patient autonomy.
