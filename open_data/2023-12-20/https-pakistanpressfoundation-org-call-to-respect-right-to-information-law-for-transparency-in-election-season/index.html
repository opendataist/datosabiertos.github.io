The article highlights the critical failure of powerful public institutions in Pakistan to comply with the Right to Information Act, undermining democratic transparency. Despite court rulings and commission orders, entities like the Ministry of Defence continue withholding information about officers' post-retirement benefits, citing unfounded security concerns. This selective disregard for legal mandates effectively neutralizes the law, preventing meaningful accountability. This defiance is particularly alarming as it sets a dangerous precedent for bureaucratic behavior. When influential bodies ignore transparency obligations, it signals impunity to the wider government apparatus, encouraging a culture of secrecy. Such actions erode public trust and compromise the integrity of the electoral process, which relies heavily on open and accessible government data to ensure fairness. The situation is vital to open data discussions because it illustrates the gap between legislative intent and practical implementation. Without strict enforcement mechanisms, open data laws remain symbolic rather than functional. Resolving these conflicts requires stronger oversight and political will to ensure that information rights are treated as fundamental, ensuring that public data remains accessible to citizens rather than shielded by institutional resistance.
