Netflix has announced the publication of total viewing hours for the vast majority of its catalog, a significant move driven by pressure from the creative industry during the recent Hollywood strikes. This initiative aims to address the lack of transparency that had fostered distrust among actors and writers, who are demanding higher royalties based on the actual success of their productions. By revealing detailed data, the platform seeks to legitimize economic compensation and set a precedent for openness toward its production partners. The report highlights that original content dominates viewership, with sustained demand for both recent releases and older titles, while also reflecting the strong influence of non-English language content. By sharing global and regional metrics, Netflix provides deeper insights into audience preferences, enabling creators to better understand what resonates with viewers and adjust their development strategies accordingly. This transparency is crucial for the open-data ecosystem in the entertainment industry, as it establishes a standard for sharing engagement metrics beyond the traditional Top 10 lists. Disclosing this data not only benefits creators by allowing them to verify the performance of their works, but also addresses advertisers’ need for accurate information in advertising-based business models. Finally, it fosters a culture of accessible data that could inspire other platforms to adopt similar practices of accountability and operational clarity.
