Una IA puede estimar la muerte prematura de una persona

Researchers have developed an artificial intelligence model capable of analyzing vast amounts of personal data to predict life events with remarkable accuracy, including the likelihood of premature mortality and personality traits. This advancement demonstrates that human life can be interpreted as a narrative sequence of events, enabling computer systems to organize socioeconomic and health information to anticipate individual futures. The study reveals that these algorithms outperform other existing neural networks, setting a new standard in predictive capacity based on complete human histories. However, the implementation of such technologies raises serious ethical dilemmas that must be addressed before practical use. The massive collection of intimate data generates fundamental concerns about privacy, the protection of confidential information, and the inherent biases in the datasets used. The authors emphasize that, although the results are consistent with traditional sociological findings, the potential to influence or discriminate against individuals requires a deep understanding of the moral and legal implications, avoiding premature applications in areas such as health risk assessment. This article is crucial for the open data movement because it illustrates the tension between the analytical potential of public datasets and the need for responsible governance. By showing how the integration of diverse information can generate powerful insights, it highlights the urgency of establishing transparent ethical frameworks. Public debate on the democratic use of these technologies is essential to ensure that innovation in data science respects individual rights and serves the common good, rather than perpetuating inequalities or privacy violations.
