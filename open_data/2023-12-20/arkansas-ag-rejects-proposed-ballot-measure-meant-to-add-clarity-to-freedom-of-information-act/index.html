Arkansas AG rejects proposed ballot measure meant to add clarity to Freedom of Information Act

Arkansas Attorney General Tim Griffin has rejected proposed ballot initiatives aimed at enhancing government transparency, citing ambiguous language and unconstitutional structures. The proposed Arkansas Government Transparency Act and a companion constitutional amendment were deemed insufficient for the 2024 statewide ballot because they lacked precise legal definitions and contained partisan framing. Griffin argued that the term "transparency" was used persuasively rather than informatively, and that vague definitions of public meetings and communications would create legal confusion rather than clarity for citizens and officials. Beyond linguistic issues, Griffin identified significant constitutional violations within the proposals. The plan to establish a Government Transparency Commission with judicially appointed members was found to breach the separation of powers, as it improperly involved the judicial branch in non-judicial administrative functions. Additionally, the measure’s procedural rules for court appeals infringed upon the Arkansas Supreme Court’s exclusive authority to govern court procedures, rendering those specific provisions unenforceable under state law. This rejection is highly relevant to open data and access initiatives, as it highlights the critical importance of legal precision and constitutional compliance when advocating for policy changes. It demonstrates that well-intentioned transparency efforts can be derailed by poor legislative drafting, regardless of their democratic intent. For advocates, this underscores the necessity of navigating complex state constitutional boundaries and ensuring that transparency mandates are legally robust, clear, and non-partisan to survive judicial and attorney general scrutiny.
