Question in Assembly seems like legislator seeking info under RTI Act: Himachal Deputy CM

The article highlights a significant overlap between legislative inquiries and the Right to Information Act, suggesting that complex government data often requires RTI mechanisms rather than standard parliamentary procedures. This implies that formal assembly questions may be insufficient for obtaining detailed administrative records, as evidenced by the volume of documents required to answer a single query. Historically, this issue has been noted by opposition parties who argue that RTI offers a more efficient and comprehensive route for gathering information than waiting for government replies in the legislature. The Deputy Chief Minister’s observation that a legislative question resembled an RTI request underscores the growing reliance on transparency laws to access granular public sector data. This narrative is relevant to open_data because it illustrates the practical limitations of political transparency in favoring statutory disclosure frameworks. It suggests that open_data initiatives must look beyond political debates to support robust, accessible digital systems that allow citizens and officials to retrieve detailed information directly, bypassing the inefficiencies of traditional questioning protocols.
