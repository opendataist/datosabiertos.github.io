Here's a list of November inflation rates for selected Canadian cities | CityNews Toronto

Canada’s national inflation settled at 3.1 percent in November, though provincial metrics showed significant volatility due to small sample sizes. This highlights a critical limitation in open data practices: granular, location-specific statistics often lack the statistical robustness required for reliable analysis without broader aggregation. Stakeholders relying on local economic indicators must recognize that these figures may fluctuate wildly, potentially misleading interpretations of regional price stability. Consequently, open data initiatives should prioritize sample size disclosures to ensure users understand the inherent uncertainty in hyper-local metrics. Relevance to open data lies in the necessity for transparent metadata that explains statistical confidence. Users must be empowered to discern when local data is representative versus merely suggestive, fostering more accurate public policy decisions and preventing the misuse of underpowered statistical samples.
