How Open Data Builds Fairer Societies: Madrid, Paraguay, and Argentina

We often think of open data as a boring, technical topic for computer programmers and city workers. But this view misses the point. Open data has the power to create a fairer and more honest world. When public information is free and easy to use, it becomes a tool for social change. Three recent examples show how communities use this data to help people.

Madrid funds people who reuse public information. The city does more than just publish lists of data. It actively supports citizens. Madrid gave grants to help people create new apps and research projects. This treats public data as a living asset, not just an old archive. The city made the rules for using this data clear. Now, students and citizens can create services that help society. This program shows that easy access to data helps democracy grow. It supports both technical tools and research. If you want to know the specific rules for using this data, read our guide on what licence open data carries, and what you may do with it.

Paraguay’s Ministry of Public Prosecution shared new data this year. They reported nine cases of feminicide. This data shows where and who is affected by this violence. It reveals that intimate partners are usually the attackers. These crimes mostly happen in private homes. The report also mentions seventeen orphaned children. This shows the long-term pain these tragedies cause. Publishing detailed statistics makes these crimes more human. It helps us see the victims and their families. This analysis reveals unfair social structures. It helps leaders create policies to protect vulnerable people. However, you must read these numbers carefully. Do not confuse real trends with random accidents. If you want to advocate for change based on facts, not just headlines, read our guide on how to read a statistic before you quote it.

General Pueyrredon, Argentina, shows how open Wi-Fi data helps citizens. The city has strong policies for digital inclusion. The number of users grew significantly, and the service stayed stable. This shows that people trust this infrastructure. Local leaders use connection data to plan better services. They adjust to what citizens need. This strategy follows the Open Government Partnership framework. It uses open data to drive innovation. It connects transparent government with citizen participation. For people without fast internet at home, public Wi-Fi is a lifeline. It gives them access to information and community. If you want to connect to these data sources technically, follow the steps in our guide on how to use an open-data portal’s API: CKAN, DCAT and OpenDataSoft.

These examples show that open data empowers users. It helps people hold institutions accountable. It helps us understand the root causes of social issues. It ensures that digital participation is a right for everyone.
