Los salarios le ganaron por poco a la inflación en febrero

Although formal wages recorded nominal growth in February that slightly exceeded overall inflation, the economic reality reveals a significant erosion of purchasing power on a year-over-year basis. Workers in the informal sector are the most affected, as they lack collective bargaining mechanisms to offset the mismatch between their incomes and the cost of living, thereby deepening labor inequality and the precariousness of their living conditions in the face of widespread price increases. Moreover, the adjustment of relative prices for basic services such as energy, transportation, and food has disproportionately impacted household budgets. This dynamic forces workers to allocate a larger share of their income to essential expenses, drastically reducing the money available for other goods and services. As a result, a sharp contraction in private consumption is anticipated, indicating that nominal stability does not translate into real economic well-being for the majority of the population. This report is crucial for the field of open data because it exposes the limitations of the standard aggregate inflation measure, which fails to capture the disparity in price impacts across different goods and labor sectors. Transparency in data disaggregated by informality and expenditure category enables a more accurate assessment of social justice and the effectiveness of economic policies. Without access to detailed and disaggregated information, the analysis of economic equity remains superficial and obscures the true welfare gaps affecting the vulnerable population.
