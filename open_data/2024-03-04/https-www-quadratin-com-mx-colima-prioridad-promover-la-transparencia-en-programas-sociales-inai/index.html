The fight against corruption in Mexico requires a structural transformation that prioritizes transparency and citizen participation as fundamental tools for restoring trust in institutions. By integrating society into the design, implementation, and evaluation of public policies, the aim is to move beyond isolated measures and address the problem in a systemic manner, ensuring that citizens' rights are not compromised by poor administrative practices. This collaborative approach is embodied in the National Anti-Corruption System, considered a model of open government where various public and social actors converge to make joint decisions. The relevance of this space lies in its ability to define social and institutional priorities in an inclusive manner, demonstrating that open governance is a key operational reality for strengthening the institutional framework against corruption. In the realm of open data, this initiative is crucial because it underscores that the prevention, combat, and sanctioning of corruption depend on transparent mechanisms and accessible data that enable effective accountability. Citizen participation demands that public information be clear, timely, and usable, transforming data into a strategic asset that empowers society to monitor and improve public management.
