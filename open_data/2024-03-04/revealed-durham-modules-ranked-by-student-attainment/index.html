Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

This article demonstrates the power of open data by revealing specific university performance metrics obtained through a Freedom of Information Request. It highlights significant disparities in academic attainment across different departments and year levels, showing that physics and English lead in top-grade awards while foundation years and modern foreign languages often lag. By publishing these granular results, the source underscores how transparent access to institutional data can expose inequalities in grading practices that were previously opaque to students and the public. The data further illustrates that performance varies drastically even within individual courses, ranging from modules where no first-class grades are awarded to those where nearly all students achieve them. This variability suggests that assessment standards and course structures differ immensely, impacting student outcomes regardless of ability. Such detailed breakdowns allow for a deeper understanding of how specific pedagogical approaches or exam formats influence success rates, providing critical insights for educators aiming to standardize fairness. Relevance to open data lies in the potential for this transparency to drive accountability and improvement within higher education. When institutions release raw performance statistics, stakeholders can scrutinize equity in grading, identify systemic biases, and advocate for more consistent academic standards. This case serves as a compelling example of how leveraging FOI laws and publishing open datasets can empower communities to hold educational bodies responsible for their performance metrics.
