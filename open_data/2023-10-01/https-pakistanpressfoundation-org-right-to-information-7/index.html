The International Day for Universal Access to Information highlights a critical link between transparency and human rights, reinforcing the notion that access to information is a fundamental pillar for democratic values. This observance serves as a global reminder that informed citizenry is essential for social justice, positioning information not merely as a commodity but as a necessary tool for building inclusive knowledge societies worldwide. In Pakistan, the Right of Access to Information Act establishes a legal framework obligating public bodies to disclose data, aiming to combat bureaucracy and foster accountability. Despite these provisions, practical implementation faces significant hurdles, including bureaucratic resistance, outdated colonial-era restrictions, and widespread public unawareness. These challenges often lead to arbitrary refusals of information, undermining the law’s potential to empower ordinary citizens and journalists who seek to expose irregularities and ensure proper oversight. This article is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the gap between legal mandates and actual data availability in public institutions. It underscores that open data initiatives cannot succeed through technology alone; they require robust legal enforcement and active civic engagement. The experiences described highlight the urgent need for governments to move beyond passive compliance toward proactive transparency, ensuring that data is not only accessible but also usable for holding power accountable and reducing corruption.
