Enhancing Legislative Framework and practices for access to public information in Georgia

Georgia’s current legislation regarding public information access is outdated, creating significant barriers to transparency and accountability. Experts emphasize that despite previous commitments, a lack of political will has stalled necessary updates. Urgent reforms are required to align national laws with international standards, ensuring citizens can effectively exercise their right to know and fostering a culture of governmental openness. Regarding open data specifically, the discussion highlighted critical gaps in policy coordination and standardization. There is a pressing need for a unified legislative framework and consistent data standards to overcome existing practical challenges. Without these foundational elements, the potential of open data to drive innovation and public engagement remains severely limited, leaving the country behind global benchmarks for data accessibility and utility. This event is crucial for the open_data community as it underscores the interdependence of legal frameworks and technical standards. It demonstrates that sustainable open data initiatives require not just technical solutions, but robust political commitment and multi-stakeholder cooperation. By identifying specific obstacles and promoting best practices, the discourse provides a roadmap for improving transparency, ultimately ensuring that open data serves the public good rather than remaining an underutilized resource.
