Authors & Copyright Scholars Back 'Internet Archive' in Landmark Legal Battle * TorrentFreak

Authors & Copyright Scholars Back 'Internet Archive' in Landmark Legal Battle * TorrentFreak

The legal dispute between the Internet Archive and major publishers centers on the legitimacy of Controlled Digital Lending, a practice allowing libraries to lend digitized copies of physically owned books. A recent lower court ruling deemed this practice copyright infringement, threatening to dismantle a crucial mechanism for public access to digital culture. However, the Internet Archive is appealing, arguing that its model aligns with traditional library functions and should be protected under fair use principles to ensure equitable access to knowledge. Supporters argue that the current publishing ecosystem is skewed by the disproportionate power held by major publishers, who often restrict libraries through restrictive licensing rather than allowing genuine ownership. Various coalitions, including the Authors Alliance and copyright scholars, contend that disabling the Internet Archive’s services would further concentrate control over content, limiting author visibility and reader access. They emphasize that fair use exists specifically to correct such market failures, ensuring that the public interest is not overridden by corporate demands for absolute control. This case is vital for open_data as it challenges the boundaries of digital preservation and the rights of institutions to manage their collections without excessive corporate interference. If the Internet Archive loses, it sets a precedent that could severely limit how open archives preserve and provide access to digital information, potentially fragmenting the public domain. The outcome will determine whether libraries can continue to innovate in providing free, private, and permanent access to cultural heritage in the digital age, or if they will be forced into restrictive, paywalled models that hinder open scholarship and historical preservation.
