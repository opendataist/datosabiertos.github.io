Freedom of expression: removal of online content by Indian authorities raises serious concerns

Freedom of expression: removal of online content by Indian authorities raises serious concerns

India’s digital landscape is witnessing a significant erosion of free expression, driven by an aggressive state apparatus that increasingly treats legitimate dissent as a threat to national security. Recent data reveals hundreds of government requests for online content removal, accompanied by the physical arrest and detention of journalists covering protests. This trend indicates a systemic shift where critical speech challenging state policies or exposing misconduct is reclassified as incitement to disorder, prompting authorities to deploy police power rather than engage in democratic dialogue. The mechanisms for this suppression are multifaceted, relying heavily on internet shutdowns during unrest and the application of broad anti-terror and defamation laws. These measures disproportionately impact marginalized communities and serve to protect the government’s image by silencing scrutiny of both state officials and allied businesses. Consequently, the environment for open discourse has narrowed significantly, with constitutional protections under Article 19 being diluted through administrative overreach and the routine blocking of digital information under the guise of public order. This situation is critically relevant to open_data because it highlights how transparency tools can be weaponized to restrict access rather than facilitate it. The use of Right to Information requests to monitor content removal efforts, alongside the strategic litigation needed to challenge these laws, underscores the importance of data in accountability. However, the frequent internet shutdowns also disrupt data availability, creating blind spots that hinder civil society’s ability to monitor human rights abuses. Defending open data requires not just access to information, but the legal and technical infrastructure to ensure that digital spaces remain open forums for debate, free from arbitrary state censorship.
