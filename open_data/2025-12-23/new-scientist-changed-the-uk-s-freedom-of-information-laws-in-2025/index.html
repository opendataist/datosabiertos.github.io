New Scientist changed the UK's freedom of information laws in 2025

New Scientist changed the UK's freedom of information laws in 2025

A recent Freedom of Information request concerning UK Technology Secretary Peter Kyle’s ChatGPT usage has established a significant legal precedent for government transparency regarding artificial intelligence. By successfully distinguishing between private and official interactions, the outcome confirmed that AI conversations conducted for public duties are subject to disclosure. This ruling challenges traditional interpretations of data privacy, demonstrating that the growing integration of generative AI into civil service operations does not exempt officials from existing open data obligations. The case highlights the urgent need for accountability as governments rapidly adopt AI tools for daily workflows. While officials often claim these technologies improve efficiency, they also introduce risks such as information hallucinations and biased outputs. Without transparent oversight, it becomes impossible to verify the accuracy or integrity of decisions influenced by automated systems. This precedent empowers researchers and journalists internationally to scrutinize ministerial use of AI, ensuring that public servants remain answerable for their digital interactions. However, the broader implication reveals a tightening of information access, with authorities increasingly relying on exemptions to reject requests related to AI. As departments grow more cautious, the tension between operational secrecy and public accountability intensifies. For the open data community, this scenario underscores the critical importance of maintaining robust legal frameworks that prevent technological adoption from becoming a shield against transparency. Ensuring that AI usage remains visible and auditable is essential for preserving trust in public institutions.
