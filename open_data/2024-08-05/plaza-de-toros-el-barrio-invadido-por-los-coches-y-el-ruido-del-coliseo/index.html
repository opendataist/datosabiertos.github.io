Plaza de Toros: el barrio «invadido» por los coches y el ruido del Coliseo

The Coliseu neighborhood faces significant urban and social challenges driven by demographic shifts and inadequate infrastructure. As young families move in alongside a substantial elderly population, the area struggles with narrow streets, severe parking congestion, and a lack of green spaces, which the local government threatens to replace with more parking rather than addressing the core quality-of-life deficits. These physical stresses are compounded by noise pollution from illegal tourist rentals and events at the nearby arena, while sanitation issues further degrade the living environment. In response to these hardships and the isolation common among seniors, the neighborhood association has mobilized diverse entities, including health and social services, to create a robust volunteer network. This collaborative platform offers various activities, fostering essential support systems that mitigate loneliness and strengthen community bonds among vulnerable residents. This case is highly relevant to open data because it illustrates the critical intersection between community-identified problems and public policy. By documenting specific grievances—such as parking violations, noise levels, and service gaps—the association generates qualitative data that demands transparency from authorities. Open data initiatives can leverage this grassroots information to monitor compliance with regulations, evaluate the impact of urban planning decisions, and ensure that resource allocation aligns with the actual needs of the demographic, promoting accountability and more effective governance.
