Sin obras y sin equipamiento: el Gobierno cortó el envío de fondos a empresas estatales - Diario Panorama

The Argentine government is actively reducing financial transfers to state-owned enterprises, with a specific focus on halting capital expenditures to accelerate their "saneamiento" or financial restructuring. This strategy aims to lower the state deficit and make these companies more attractive for potential privatization by the private sector. By cutting off investment funds, the administration seeks to demonstrate that these entities can operate with minimal public support, thereby justifying their sale or concession to private operators. This policy is closely linked to the administration’s broader goal of transferring public assets to private hands, even beyond those explicitly mandated by the Base Law. The lack of capital for maintenance and upgrades implies that many state firms are being kept in a state of operational dependency or decline, further encouraging their acquisition. Although some companies still receive funds for current expenses, such as payroll or voluntary retirements, the restriction on long-term investment signals a deliberate shift away from state-led development in favor of a streamlined, smaller public sector footprint. This situation is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the critical role of transparent public budgeting in monitoring government actions. Platforms like the Open Budget allow civil society to verify the discrepancy between allocated budgets and actual spending, revealing how fiscal austerity measures are implemented. Without access to such granular financial data, it would be impossible to track the extent of the withdrawal of state resources or to assess the true progress and impact of the privatization strategy on public infrastructure and services.
