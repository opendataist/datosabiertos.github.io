The article highlights the dominant shift of internet traffic toward mobile applications, which has made these platforms the primary target of cyberattacks. This concentration of critical activities, ranging from banking transactions to personal management, has led to a significant increase in the frequency and sophistication of exploited vulnerabilities, creating an urgent need to strengthen defense mechanisms to protect users’ sensitive information and the integrity of businesses. The relevance to open data lies in the implicit criticism of the lack of transparency and access to effective security data. The text emphasizes that the implementation of biometric verification systems and mobile SDKs is insufficient if not complemented by real-time threat intelligence. There is a clear demand for developers and providers to share detailed telemetry on attacks and vulnerabilities, enabling more agile, evidence-based responses rather than relying on static, closed measures that often prove ineffective against the evolving landscape of cybercrime. The main conclusion is that the lack of investment in advanced protection tools not only exposes organizations to financial and reputational risks but also perpetuates a cycle of digital insecurity. To mitigate these risks, it is imperative to adopt a proactive approach where data visibility into threats enables early detection and coordinated response. Without this openness in information sharing and collaboration on security, companies will continue to incur greater losses and erode consumer trust in the digital economy.
