La Ley de Transparencia choca con Juan Carlos I: el Gobierno veta las preguntas que afectan al emérito

La Ley de Transparencia choca con Juan Carlos I: el Gobierno veta las preguntas que afectan al emérito

The article reveals that former King Juan Carlos I remains shielded by an official mantle of silence, as the Spanish government has systematically denied requests for information regarding the cost and details of his security through the Transparency Portal. These denials are based on longstanding agreements that classify protection plans for public figures as confidential, arguing that disclosure could jeopardize both the individuals being protected and the security personnel responsible for their safety. The relevance to data transparency lies in the restrictive interpretation of the Transparency Law. Rather than making public data on state expenditure available, the administration invokes national security arguments to justify opacity, setting a precedent in which financial and operational information related to the monarchy falls outside public scrutiny. This demonstrates how security exemptions can be used to conceal institutional spending, thereby limiting the public’s capacity for oversight. This situation underscores the tension between the right to public information and the document classification practices employed by high-level institutions. The inability to access basic information about the maintenance of the former monarch’s security highlights deficiencies in the actual implementation of transparency regulations. For the open data community, this case underscores the need to review confidentiality thresholds and ensure that exemptions do not become a routine tool for hiding the use of public resources, thus ensuring greater accountability in the public sector.
