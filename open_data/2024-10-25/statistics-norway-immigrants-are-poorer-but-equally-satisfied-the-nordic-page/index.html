Statistics Norway: Immigrants Are Poorer But Equally Satisfied - The Nordic Page

The article reveals a paradox where immigrants in Norway, despite facing significantly poorer socioeconomic conditions and higher rates of mental distress and loneliness, report satisfaction levels and institutional trust comparable to or exceeding the native population. This discrepancy challenges simple correlations between objective living standards and subjective well-being, highlighting the complex resilience and social integration of immigrant communities. High confidence in national institutions suggests strong political affiliation, yet underlying health disparities remain a critical concern. The data indicates that specific immigrant groups face disproportionate burdens of anxiety and isolation, pointing to targeted social needs that current satisfaction metrics do not fully capture. Understanding these nuanced differences is essential for crafting effective public health and integration policies. This report is vital to open_data because it demonstrates how publicly available statistical surveys can uncover hidden social dynamics and inform evidence-based policymaking. By making detailed demographic data accessible, it enables researchers and civil society to scrutinize integration outcomes, hold governments accountable, and advocate for resources where objective hardships exceed subjective reported satisfaction, fostering a more informed and equitable society.
