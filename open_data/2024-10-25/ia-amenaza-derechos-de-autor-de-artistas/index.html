IA amenaza derechos de autor de artistas

IA amenaza derechos de autor de artistas

Numerous renowned artists and authors have issued a joint warning about the threat that generative artificial intelligence poses to creative industries. Their primary concern lies in the unlicensed use of copyrighted works to train models, which they view as an unfair appropriation that jeopardizes creators’ livelihoods. They demand that this practice be banned to protect their copyright and ensure that the value generated by their work is properly recognized. This situation is critically relevant to the open data movement, as it highlights the tensions between the culture of free access to information and the need to protect individual intellectual property. The debate forces a reexamination of current data collection and training models, underscoring the lack of transparency and consent in how human creations are used. This drives the demand for more ethical frameworks that balance technological innovation with respect for authorship—a central challenge for any responsible open data policy. Despite the substantial capital invested in AI infrastructure, companies resist paying for the data they use, creating a power imbalance vis-à-vis tech giants. Artists, lacking equivalent resources, struggle to influence intellectual property protection policies, fearing that regulation will favor corporate interests. This dynamic underscores the urgency of developing clear standards that ensure fair compensation and attribution, which are essential for sustaining creative ecosystems in the digital age.
