Sobre la estadística cruceña de que somos más de 4 millones, ICE cree que no deben existir grandes divergencias entre sus resultados y los del censo | El Deber

The conflict highlights a critical tension between national and subnational statistical autonomy in Bolivia. Rubén Peña of the ICE rejects Humberto Arandia’s claims from the INE that Santa Cruz’s population estimates are exaggerated, emphasizing that the ICE data reflects a methodologically sound local survey, not a census. This dispute underscores the constitutional right of autonomous entities to generate their own statistics, challenging the central institute’s authority to dismiss regional methodologies without transparency. This issue is highly relevant to open data because it questions the interoperability and trustworthiness of official statistics. When national bodies invalidate regional data based on undefined error parameters, it undermines data integrity and public trust. The situation illustrates how political disagreements can obscure technical validation, making it difficult for developers and researchers to access reliable, comparable datasets. Ultimately, the article stresses that statistical competence is exercised, not negotiated. The insistence on local autonomy demands that open data ecosystems respect diverse methodological approaches across different governmental levels. For open data to be truly effective, institutions must prioritize methodological transparency and collaborative validation over centralized control, ensuring that all statistical sources are treated as valid contributions to the broader public knowledge base.
