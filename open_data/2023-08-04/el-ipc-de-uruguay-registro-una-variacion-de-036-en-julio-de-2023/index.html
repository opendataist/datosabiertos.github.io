El IPC de Uruguay registró una variación de -0,36% en julio de 2023

Uruguay’s consumer price index (IPC) showed a slight monthly decrease in July 2023, with significant drops in food and beverage prices driven by lower costs for vegetables, fruits, and non-alcoholic drinks. Clothing and footwear also contributed to this decline, while housing and transport costs remained relatively stable or saw minor reductions. These fluctuations reflect seasonal adjustments and specific market dynamics across essential goods, influencing household purchasing power. This data is crucial for open data initiatives because it highlights the importance of accessible, granular statistical information. When detailed breakdowns of inflation drivers are publicly available, it enables researchers, journalists, and citizens to verify official figures and understand economic trends deeply. Transparency in such micro-level data fosters accountability and allows for more accurate economic analysis. Ultimately, the release of this detailed IPC report supports evidence-based decision-making. By providing clear insights into which sectors are driving price changes, the open dissemination of INE’s data empowers stakeholders to assess the real impact of inflation on daily life. This accessibility is vital for promoting trust in public institutions and ensuring that economic policies are grounded in transparent, verifiable facts.
