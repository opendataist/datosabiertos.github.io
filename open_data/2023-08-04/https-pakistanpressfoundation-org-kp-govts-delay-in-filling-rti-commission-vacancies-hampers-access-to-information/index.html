The article highlights a critical failure in the implementation of the Right to Information Act in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, where the commission intended to enforce transparency has itself become a barrier. Journalists report persistent non-responses from government departments and inaction by the RTI Commission when filing complaints, creating a cycle of impunity. This dysfunction undermines the legal right to access public information, leaving citizens feeling misled by a law that promises accountability but delivers silence. Structural deficiencies, such as a lack of appointed commissioners and severe funding shortages, paralyze the commission’s ability to enforce the law effectively. Although the legislation allows decisions to proceed despite vacancies, the absence of a full quorum hinders strict enforcement actions like fines or salary stops. Consequently, the commission struggles to conduct awareness campaigns or provide training, limiting its capacity to support the public’s right to know and ensuring that bureaucratic delays remain unchecked. This opacity poses a significant threat to open data principles and democratic transparency. When journalists cannot access accurate information, misinformation spreads, and trust between the government and the public erodes. The case illustrates why robust institutional resources and political will are essential for open data ecosystems; without them, legal frameworks remain ineffective, preventing citizens from monitoring public fund usage and holding officials accountable.
