Ruiz-Healy Times: La mediocridad de la educación aumentará con deficientes libros de texto

Although Mexico has achieved record levels of educational attainment, with increased average years of schooling and reduced dropout rates across most education levels, this quantitative progress masks a severe crisis in educational quality. The data indicate that while more students are remaining in the system and class sizes are shrinking, these improvements have not translated into effective learning outcomes. Consequently, the nation faces a paradox: access to education has expanded significantly, yet the actual knowledge acquired by students remains critically deficient. International and national assessments reveal that Mexican students perform far below global standards in mathematics, science, and literacy. Despite favorable structural indicators, the majority of students lack the competencies necessary for higher education or professional excellence. This disconnect highlights a systemic failure in which institutional metrics do not reflect pedagogical effectiveness. The absence of recent large-scale evaluations further obscures the current state of learning, making it difficult to address the deepening mediocrity that threatens the country’s human capital development. This article is relevant to open data because it underscores the critical necessity of transparent, accessible, and continuous educational statistics. Without open, standardized, and publicly available data from authoritative bodies, policymakers and citizens cannot effectively diagnose educational deficiencies or hold institutions accountable. The discontinuation of key national exams and reliance on outdated or incomplete reports demonstrate how the lack of open data infrastructure hinders evidence-based decision-making, ultimately impeding efforts to improve educational quality and ensure equitable opportunities for all students.
