Here’s How India's Data Protection Bill Threatens Right To Information

The Digital Personal Data Protection Bill significantly weakens India’s Right to Information framework by allowing government officials to deny RTI requests based on privacy grounds. This amendment removes the critical safeguard that previously permitted disclosure when larger public interest justified it, effectively creating a blanket "right to deny" for Public Information Officers. Consequently, citizens can no longer access information that legislative bodies already possess, fundamentally altering the balance between individual privacy and public accountability. This shift poses a serious threat to transparency and anti-corruption efforts, as the vague definition of personal information enables officials to obscure details regarding welfare programs and fund misappropriation. The lack of clarity regarding jurisdiction between the Information Commission and the Data Protection Board further complicates grievance redressal, leaving applicants in a legal limbo. Without clear mechanisms to challenge these denials, the amendment risks rendering essential oversight tools ineffective and hindering the public’s ability to monitor government actions. This development is highly relevant to open data advocates because it illustrates how data protection laws can be weaponized to restrict access to public information rather than enhance it. It highlights the critical need for legislation that explicitly protects the public’s right to know while ensuring privacy, rather than allowing privacy provisions to override transparency mandates. The ambiguity surrounding this amendment serves as a cautionary example for how poorly designed regulations can undermine democratic accountability and the principles of open governance.
