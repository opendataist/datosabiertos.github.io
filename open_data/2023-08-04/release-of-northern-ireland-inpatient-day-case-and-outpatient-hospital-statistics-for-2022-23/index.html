Release of Northern Ireland Inpatient, Day case and Outpatient Hospital Statistics for 2022/23

The Department of Health’s latest report reveals a significant rebound in Northern Ireland’s healthcare activity post-pandemic, marked by increased inpatient admissions, theatre cases, and outpatient attendances compared to previous years. Notably, there was a substantial rise in procedures delivered through the independent sector, highlighting a growing reliance on external providers to manage demand. Simultaneously, the persistence of virtual outpatient options alongside a return to face-to-face consultations illustrates a structural shift in service delivery models that continues to evolve. Crucially, the data exposes operational challenges within the Health Service, including longer average lengths of stay and high rates of missed or cancelled appointments. While hospital occupancy remains high, the fluctuation in bed availability suggests ongoing capacity constraints. The persistent cancellation rates, both by hospitals and patients, indicate systemic inefficiencies and resource pressures that impact patient experience and service reliability, pointing to the need for improved scheduling and capacity management. This publication is vital for open data advocates as it demonstrates the importance of accessible, granular health statistics for public accountability. By disaggregating data by trust, specialty, and sector, the report allows for detailed analysis of healthcare performance and trends. Transparent access to such comprehensive datasets empowers researchers, policymakers, and citizens to evaluate the effectiveness of health interventions, understand resource allocation, and hold institutions accountable for service delivery outcomes.
