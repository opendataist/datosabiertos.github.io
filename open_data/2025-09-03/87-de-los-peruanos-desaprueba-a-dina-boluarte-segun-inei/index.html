87 % de los peruanos desaprueba a Dina Boluarte, según INEI

87 % de los peruanos desaprueba a Dina Boluarte, según INEI

The article highlights a profound crisis of legitimacy in Peru, where President Dina Boluarte faces overwhelming public disapproval driven by severe economic hardship and controversial governance. This widespread discontent is not isolated but represents a national consensus, indicating a deep erosion of trust in state institutions and reflecting a significant disconnect between the executive branch and the citizenry it serves. The underlying cause of this political instability is the deteriorating quality of life for the majority of Peruvians, who are struggling to meet basic needs due to declining incomes and the depletion of personal savings. As families are forced to reduce consumption and increase working hours just to survive, the government’s inability to alleviate financial pressure becomes the primary driver of social unrest and dissatisfaction, overshadowing any political narrative the administration may attempt to construct. This case is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how rigorous, transparent statistical evidence from national institutes can objectively verify public sentiment and expose the true state of a nation’s social fabric. By making such detailed indicators accessible, open data frameworks enable civil society, researchers, and policymakers to move beyond anecdotal reports and address the structural issues affecting democracy and institutional trust with factual precision.
