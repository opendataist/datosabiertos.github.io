In 2024, Chihuahua expanded its forensic services by significantly increasing its personnel and budget, reflecting a strong regional commitment to strengthening justice infrastructure. This allocation of resources supports operational capacity, even as the number of cadavers processed declined, suggesting improved efficiency or shifts in crime patterns. The data highlight the correlation between funding and staffing in public security, illustrating how governments leverage open data to measure institutional performance. Tracking these metrics enables citizens and policymakers to assess whether increased investment translates into tangible improvements in forensic capabilities. This case demonstrates the value of transparent, granular data in evaluating the effectiveness of public services. By comparing year-over-year changes in resources and outcomes, stakeholders can derive actionable insights into how financial decisions impact critical societal functions, thereby fostering accountability and informed civic engagement.
