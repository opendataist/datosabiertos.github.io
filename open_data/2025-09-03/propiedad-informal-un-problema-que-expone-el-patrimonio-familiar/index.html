Propiedad informal, un problema que expone el patrimonio familiar

Propiedad informal, un problema que expone el patrimonio familiar

The article highlights that a significant portion of Mexico City residents lack legal title to their homes, creating profound insecurity for families. This uncertainty prevents homeowners from selling, inheriting, or accessing credit, while exposing them to criminal risks such as land dispossession and excluding them from government housing subsidies. The core issue stems not only from economic barriers but also from cultural misconceptions about the cost and nature of notarial processes, which deter many from seeking formalization. This legal ambiguity severely undermines asset security, as informal ownership leaves families vulnerable to exploitation and unable to legally defend their property against illicit actors. Without formal documentation, individuals cannot prove ownership, leading to significant financial and social instability. The situation reflects a widespread gap between actual occupancy and registered ownership, leaving millions without the legal certainty necessary to protect their patrimony or integrate their assets into the formal economy. This topic is crucial for open data because transparent, accessible land registries are essential tools for verifying ownership and reducing informality. Open government data initiatives can empower citizens by providing clear information on legal procedures, rights, and available support mechanisms. By making registry data and administrative processes more visible and understandable, authorities can help bridge the trust gap, facilitate legal formalization, and ultimately enhance property rights protection for vulnerable populations.
