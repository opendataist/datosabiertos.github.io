Limón y calabacita, de los productos que más subieron

This article highlights recent shifts in Mexican consumer prices, noting that while certain food items like lemon and zucchini saw significant increases, others such as chayote and green tomatoes dropped sharply. These fluctuations contribute to the overall annual inflation rate, which currently stands at 5.16%. The report distinguishes between underlying and non-underlying price indices, revealing different trends in goods versus services and agricultural products. The data reveals regional variations, with states like Tamaulipas and Quintana Roo experiencing higher-than-average price changes. Meanwhile, the underlying inflation index grew slightly, whereas non-underlying prices fell. This divergence helps economists understand whether price changes are driven by core economic forces or volatile supply-side factors affecting agriculture and energy tariffs. Relevance to open data lies in the updated methodology. The index now uses a new basket of goods and weighting structure based on 2022 household spending patterns. This transition demonstrates how public institutions must regularly refresh datasets to reflect current societal realities. Such transparency ensures that economic indicators remain accurate and useful for researchers analyzing contemporary consumption behaviors and policy impacts.
