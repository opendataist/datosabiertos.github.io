Freedom of Information requests reveal significant pest issues across North Wales hospitals, with Wrexham Maelor and Ysbyty Glan Clwyd experiencing hundreds of call-outs for rats, flies, and seagulls. Despite some reduction in frequency over time, these incidents highlight ongoing maintenance challenges within aging healthcare infrastructure. The data indicates that structural deficiencies, such as inadequate ventilation and bed-spacing, persist, compounding infection risks beyond mere pest presence. This transparency underscores the critical role of open data in holding public institutions accountable. By making operational statistics accessible, journalists and the public can scrutinize service delivery and infrastructure quality. The release of specific pest control figures forces a confrontation with the reality that older hospital buildings struggle to meet modern infection prevention standards, a concern amplified by recent reports linking estate age to Covid-19 spread. Ultimately, this article demonstrates how open data facilitates deeper societal understanding of public health failures. It moves the conversation from isolated incidents to systemic issues regarding building management and patient safety. For the open data community, this case illustrates the power of FOI requests to uncover hidden operational metrics, driving informed debate on necessary investments in healthcare infrastructure and regulatory oversight.
