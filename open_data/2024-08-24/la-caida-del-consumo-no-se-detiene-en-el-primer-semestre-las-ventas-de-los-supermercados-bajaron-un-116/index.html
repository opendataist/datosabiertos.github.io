La caída del consumo no se detiene: en el primer semestre las ventas de los supermercados bajaron un 11,6%

The article reveals a significant divergence in consumer behavior across different retail sectors in Argentina. Supermarkets and wholesale stores have experienced a severe crisis, with sales plummeting due to a sharp decline in real purchasing power. Although nominal wages have recently surpassed inflation, they have not recovered the buying capacity lost during the recent currency devaluation. Consequently, the vast majority of product categories, from meats to electronics, have failed to outpace high inflation rates, forcing consumers to reduce their spending on essential goods. In contrast, shopping malls have emerged as an unexpected exception to this trend, recording a slight real growth in sales. This resilience is largely driven by categories related to leisure, entertainment, and apparel, which have grown well above inflation rates. While essential food items continue to struggle in both retail and wholesale channels, discretionary spending remains relatively stable in mall environments, suggesting that consumers are prioritizing experiential and non-essential purchases over basic necessities as their financial situation deteriorates. This data is crucial for open data initiatives as it highlights the importance of granular, sector-specific analysis to understand economic health beyond aggregate indicators. By making detailed sales and inflation data publicly accessible, researchers and citizens can critically evaluate the real impact of fiscal policies and currency fluctuations on different segments of society. Such transparency allows for better accountability and enables more accurate modeling of economic trends, fostering a deeper public understanding of how macroeconomic changes affect specific industries and consumer welfare.
