"La tecnología avanza más rápido que la ley": los retos legales de la Inteligencia Artificial y la propiedad intelectual

The article highlights the growing tension between the rapid expansion of artificial intelligence and the protection of intellectual property. It depicts a scenario in which AI tools generate literary and musical works that mimic the style of living authors, thereby infringing copyright and generating unauthorized revenue. This phenomenon challenges existing legal frameworks, revealing a disconnect between technological advancement and current regulatory capacity, which leaves creators vulnerable to the large-scale use of their works to train algorithms without their consent. The absence of clear legislation complicates the enforcement of copyright infringements, particularly when users hide behind anonymous profiles on social media. Although detection tools exist, they are insufficient to curb the widespread dissemination of artificially generated content. The legal expert emphasizes that, under the originality requirement of copyright law, works autonomously created by AI cannot be protected, raising a legal vacuum regarding who holds the rights and how the interests of human creators can be safeguarded. This situation is relevant to open data because it illustrates the ethical and legal risks of using copyrighted data to train machine learning models without adequate oversight. The lack of transparency regarding the origin of data, along with the inability to audit or prevent the misuse of sensitive information, underscores the need for governance frameworks that balance technological innovation with the protection of individual rights. For the open data ecosystem, this reinforces the urgency of establishing clear standards on the provenance and ethical use of information in the development of artificial intelligence.
