Canada, Australia, Belgium, pledge to support NEITI on open data policy

International diplomatic missions in Nigeria have formally pledged their support for the Nigeria Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative’s new Open Data Policy. This commitment, made by ambassadors from Belgium, Australia, and Canada, underscores the critical role of global cooperation in enhancing transparency within the nation’s extractive sector. By backing the newly established data center, these nations aim to strengthen institutional frameworks that promote accountability and reduce corruption in oil, gas, and mineral industries. The core of this initiative is a centralized data center designed to serve as a comprehensive repository for all historical and current industry information. By aggregating data into accessible formats, the facility ensures that civil society organizations, media outlets, government agencies, and the public can easily monitor extractive sector activities. This move directly implements the EITI 2023 Standards, shifting the focus toward systematic disclosure and evidence-based decision-making to sustain robust public knowledge of resource revenues. This development is highly relevant to open data principles as it demonstrates how structured, accessible data infrastructure empowers civil society and fosters international accountability. The initiative highlights the transition toward sustainable advocacy, where transparent data availability enables better monitoring of gender and environmental impacts. Ultimately, this partnership illustrates how open data serves as a vital tool for developing nations to manage resource dependencies and engage effectively with the global community in the face of energy transitions.
