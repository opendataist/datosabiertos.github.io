La licitación pública cayó un 52% hasta octubre en Castilla-La Mancha con 91 millones de euros

Public infrastructure bidding has shifted decisively toward local governments, which have significantly increased their investment while national ministries have retreated. This decentralization highlights a broader trend in which municipal entities are driving construction activity against a backdrop of reduced central state spending. This reallocation is crucial for open data, as it underscores the necessity of granular transparency. Understanding these shifts requires accessible, standardized datasets from local authorities rather than relying solely on aggregate national figures. Ultimately, the rise in local procurement underscores the importance of monitoring sub-national trends. Open data initiatives must adapt to capture this distributed activity, ensuring that policy insights reflect the actual distribution of public funds across different administrative levels.
