El sector de los plásticos se reivindica: "Nuestros productos representan solo un 0,8% de los residuos en España"

El sector de los plásticos se reivindica: "Nuestros productos representan solo un 0,8% de los residuos en España"

The article argues that the plastic industry’s negative public perception is unwarranted, emphasizing that plastics constitute only a tiny fraction of total waste and serve as a crucial solution to societal and environmental challenges rather than being the problem. By highlighting their contributions to sustainability in sectors such as medicine and food security, the industry seeks to dismantle stigmas and correct what it views as discriminatory and unjust legislation. This narrative shift aims to reframe plastics as essential partners in achieving technological and logistical progress while ensuring responsible lifecycle management. Relevant to open data, this discourse underscores the critical need for transparency and accessible data on waste management statistics. When official figures, such as those published by the National Statistics Institute, are presented, they serve as vital evidence for balancing public perception against industrial realities. Open access to accurate environmental data empowers stakeholders to move beyond emotional narratives, fostering informed debates on recycling policies and regulatory frameworks. Without reliable, publicly available data, society risks making decisions based on misconceptions rather than empirical facts, thereby hindering effective environmental governance. Ultimately, the event illustrates how industry associations leverage data and awards to demonstrate compliance and innovation. By showcasing companies that minimize their environmental footprint, the sector attempts to build trust through demonstrable actions supported by verifiable metrics. This interaction between industry claims and independent statistical analysis highlights the role of open data in validating sustainability efforts. It suggests that for an inclusive and fair transition to a circular economy, both public institutions and private industries must rely on shared, transparent data to drive policy and consumer behavior toward genuine sustainability.
