The article highlights the critical role of the Freedom of Information Act in fostering government accountability and transparency. By encouraging citizens to actively monitor public expenditure and project implementations, the law serves as a vital mechanism for holding officials responsible. This citizen engagement ensures that public records are maintained accurately and that governance follows proper business management practices, directly supporting the principles of open data by making public financial information accessible and verifiable. Despite the legal power granted to citizens to access information, significant challenges remain regarding implementation and awareness. Many high-ranking civil servants lack sufficient knowledge of the law, leading to unnecessary litigations, fines, and potential jail terms for refusing information requests. This gap underscores the urgent need for comprehensive training and sensitization campaigns. Without adequate understanding among government staff, the potential for open data initiatives to function effectively is severely compromised, as legal barriers often stem from ignorance rather than malicious intent. The domestication of the federal act at the state level empowers residents to demand details on how public funds are utilized, though procedural differences like extended response times exist. Advocates emphasize the necessity of robust testing and broader awareness across all sectors to strengthen the law’s application. For open data advocates, this situation illustrates the importance of not just having data laws, but ensuring they are understood, respected, and actively utilized by both the public and the government to drive genuine transparency and civic participation.
