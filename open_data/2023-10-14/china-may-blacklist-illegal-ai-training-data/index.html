China May Blacklist Illegal AI Training Data

China’s proposed security specifications for generative AI introduce a stringent threshold, banning models trained with more than a small percentage of illegal or harmful information. This initiative requires rigorous security evaluations for all content used in training publicly accessible AI, effectively mandating the blacklisting of sources containing censored, terrorist, or destabilizing material. The proposal demands explicit consent for utilizing personal and biometric data, alongside measures to prevent intellectual property violations. By integrating oversight from multiple government bodies, the regulations aim to balance the promotion of AI innovation with the protection of national security and citizen rights, while explicitly covering only publicly available services. This development is critical for open data discussions as it establishes a global precedent for high-compliance governance in AI training datasets. It highlights the growing intersection between data sourcing ethics, censorship, and regulatory enforcement, signaling that transparency and legal compliance in data pipelines will become fundamental barriers to AI development worldwide.
