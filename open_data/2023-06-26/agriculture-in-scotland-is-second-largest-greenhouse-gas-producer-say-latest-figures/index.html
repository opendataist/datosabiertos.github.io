Agriculture in Scotland is second largest greenhouse gas producer say latest figures

Scotland has missed its latest emissions reduction target, raising significant concerns about its ability to meet the ambitious net-zero goal by 2045. Although long-term progress since 1990 is evident, the recent rise in emissions highlights the difficulty of sustaining decarbonization momentum. This recurring shortfall suggests a critical gap between governmental rhetoric and the actual pace of necessary environmental action, signaling that current strategies are insufficient to avert climate emergencies. The data reveals that post-lockdown economic recovery led to increased emissions in transport and residential sectors, while agricultural methodologies underwent important methodological updates. These changes underscore the complexity of measuring environmental impact accurately. Stakeholders argue that precise baselines are essential for tracking genuine progress, emphasizing that farmers and crofters are already adapting practices to sequester carbon. However, their continued success depends heavily on accessing resources and policy support to transition toward more sustainable farming methods effectively. This article is highly relevant to open data because it demonstrates how transparent, publicly available statistics drive accountability and public discourse. The controversy surrounding the figures, including debates over measurement methodologies like GWP*, illustrates the necessity of clear, accessible data for evaluating policy effectiveness. Open data allows civil society and NGOs to scrutinize government claims, ensuring that environmental targets are not just aspirational but grounded in verifiable reality, thereby fostering trust and urging decisive investment.
