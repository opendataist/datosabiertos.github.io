Rex Patrick to challenge denial of FoI requests based on ministerial portfolio changes

The article highlights a critical legal challenge against a systemic loophole in freedom of information laws that allows governments to withhold documents simply by changing ministerial portfolios. When a minister leaves office, documents in their personal capacity are often deemed outside the successor’s possession, rendering them immune to public disclosure. This practice severely undermines transparency and accountability, as it enables large swathes of government records to remain secret without substantive justification, effectively using administrative turnover as a shield against scrutiny. This issue is particularly relevant to open data advocates because it demonstrates how procedural delays and bureaucratic structural flaws can actively obstruct data accessibility. In this specific case, excessive review times by the information commissioner allowed portfolio changes to occur, triggering the legal exemption. The ruling suggests that the current framework prioritizes administrative convenience over public right-to-know, creating a significant gap in government accountability. Such loopholes prevent citizens and journalists from accessing vital information needed to hold officials responsible for their decisions. Ultimately, the case seeks to establish that job changes should not invalidate the public’s right to access government information. For the open data community, this legal battle underscores the urgent need for robust, time-bound data access protocols that are immune to political maneuvering. Ensuring that digital and physical records remain accessible regardless of personnel shifts is essential for maintaining a functional democracy where information flows freely to the public.
