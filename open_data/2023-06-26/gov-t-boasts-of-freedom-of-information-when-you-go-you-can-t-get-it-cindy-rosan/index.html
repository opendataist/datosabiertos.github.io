Gov't boasts of freedom of information '& when you go you can't get it'- Cindy Rosan

The article highlights a significant disconnect between the Virgin Islands government’s public commitments to transparency and the practical difficulties citizens face in accessing information. Despite promises to implement Freedom of Information legislation, critics argue that the current system fails to deliver, with government departments often refusing or delaying requests for public records without valid justification. This gap undermines the rule of law, as individuals lack effective mechanisms to enforce their right to information, forcing them into costly legal battles to obtain data that should be freely available. The failure to pass the legislation signals a lack of political will to prioritize open governance, leaving citizens with limited recourse against administrative opacity. This issue is crucial for the open data community because it illustrates how legislative gaps hinder data accessibility. Without robust legal frameworks and enforcement mechanisms, even well-intentioned transparency pledges remain ineffective. For open data advocates, this case serves as a warning about the necessity of strong legal infrastructure to ensure genuine public access to government information.
