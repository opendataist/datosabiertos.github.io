The article highlights a significant struggle for transparency regarding public broadcasting, specifically concerning the salaries of presenters at RTVE. While external contractors' earnings were eventually revealed through a freedom of information request, the corporation initially refused to disclose the incomes of its own staff members. This selective disclosure created an inconsistent application of transparency rules, favoring external talent over internal employees despite both performing public-facing roles. The core conflict lies in RTVE's refusal to obey a binding resolution from the Transparency Council. The council ruled that the public’s right to know how resources are managed supersedes the employees' right to privacy, especially for positions of public trust. However, RTVE continues to withhold these specific salary data, effectively disobeying the regulatory body without facing immediate legal consequences. This defiance underscores a systemic resistance within the corporation to full accountability regarding public funds. This situation is crucial for the open data community as it illustrates the gap between legislative intent and practical enforcement. It demonstrates that simply having access to information laws does not guarantee data availability if institutions actively resist compliance. The case serves as a vital example for activists and researchers, emphasizing the need for stronger enforcement mechanisms and public pressure to ensure that transparency initiatives result in actual, accessible data rather than being blocked by institutional opacity.
