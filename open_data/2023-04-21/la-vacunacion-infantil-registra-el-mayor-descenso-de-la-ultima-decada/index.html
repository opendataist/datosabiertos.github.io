La vacunación infantil registra el mayor descenso de la última década

La vacunación infantil registra el mayor descenso de la última década

Childhood vaccination in Latin America and the Caribbean has experienced an alarming decline over the past decade, transforming the region from a global leader into one with the lowest coverage rates. This drop, exacerbated by the pandemic and social mistrust, has left millions of children unprotected against preventable diseases that were once believed to be eradicated. The lack of immunization not only jeopardizes public health but also increases the vulnerability of the most marginalized populations, reversing decades of progress in health. This phenomenon illustrates the fragility of public health systems in the face of sociopolitical crises and the spread of misinformation. The decline in vaccination rates demonstrates that access to basic health services depends not only on the availability of vaccines but also on public trust, economic stability, and the operational capacity of health systems. When these pillars weaken, even regions with successful track records can see the resurgence of controlled epidemics, highlighting that health is an interdependent common good. This situation is crucial for the field of open data, as it underscores the need for transparency, timely access to verifiable information, and robust monitoring systems to detect inequalities. Open data enable the identification of unvaccinated children, efficient resource allocation, and the counteraction of misinformation with clear evidence. Without accessible and reliable data, it is impossible to implement fair corrective strategies or measure the real impact of public policies, making open data a fundamental tool for restoring vaccination coverage and safeguarding social well-being.
