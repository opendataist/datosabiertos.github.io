Un error de RTVE sobre los salarios de los presentadores del Benidorm Fest coloca en la diana a Rodrigo Vázquez y provoca el enfado de Inés Hernand

The article highlights significant discrepancies in RTVE’s transparency reporting regarding the salaries of Benidorm Fest presenters. Initially, erroneous data suggested that presenter Rodrigo Vázquez received a substantially higher fee than his colleague Inés Hernand, sparking public controversy. Although RTVE corrected this mistake, clarifying that the actual pay gap was smaller, the disparity remained significant, with another presenter earning 35,000 euros more than Hernand. This incident underscores the fragility of public trust when open data is initially inaccurate, even if corrections follow. The underlying issue points to systemic inequalities in compensation within public broadcasting, a trend that has persisted across multiple editions of the festival. Despite the corrections, the structural imbalance in remuneration continues to fuel criticism regarding gender- or role-based discrimination in public sector contracts. The narrative reveals that transparency portals serve not just as repositories for data, but as critical touchpoints where citizens scrutinize the fairness and efficiency of public resource allocation, making data integrity a matter of social justice. This case is relevant to open data because it demonstrates how errors in published datasets can directly impact public perception and trust in governmental institutions. It emphasizes the necessity of rigorous verification processes in open data initiatives to prevent misinformation and ensure that citizens have access to reliable, transparent information. Ultimately, the incident illustrates that open data is not merely about publishing figures, but about maintaining the accuracy and ethical standards required to support democratic accountability and informed public discourse.
