A recent national survey reveals that half of the Mexican population trusts President Andrés Manuel López Obrador more than local governors or mayors. This marks a significant shift in political confidence, indicating strong support for the central administration compared to regional leaders during the first quarter of the year. Data from the national statistics institute highlights geographic disparities in public trust, with northern and southwestern cities showing higher confidence levels. Conversely, major urban centers like Mexico City and Guadalajara recorded significantly lower trust scores, reflecting a complex divide in public opinion across different regions of the country. This development is highly relevant to open data, as it introduces a new metric for measuring confidence in public officials. By integrating this data category into official surveys, the government enables citizens and researchers to openly analyze political trends. This transparency supports informed civic engagement and allows for deeper investigation into how public trust correlates with governance and security policies.
