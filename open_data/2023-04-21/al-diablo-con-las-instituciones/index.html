"¡Al diablo con las instituciones!"

The article argues that the current administration’s efforts to dismantle independent oversight bodies fundamentally undermine public transparency and accountability. By proposing the absorption of the INAI, a specialized agency dedicated to protecting citizens' information rights, into the Secretary of Public Function, the government risks replacing citizen-focused data access with internal bureaucratic controls. This shift threatens the millions of information requests handled annually, which have been crucial for exposing corruption and ensuring that public resources are used correctly, thereby weakening the mechanisms that allow civil society to hold the state accountable. Furthermore, the suppression of educational evaluation systems and the classification of major infrastructure projects under national security obscure vital public data. These actions hide details regarding school quality and the specific spending of billions in public funds, particularly concerning projects like the Maya Train. By transferring management of these funds to military-led entities and reducing oversight, the administration limits public scrutiny and increases the risk of misallocation, as military organizations are not structured to provide the detailed financial transparency required in civil governance. This narrative is critical to open data because it highlights how political decisions can directly restrict the availability of public information, turning opaque governance into a deliberate policy choice. When states actively dismantle tools that facilitate data access and hide spending details, they erode the trust between citizens and institutions. Preserving independent bodies and maintaining accessible, granular datasets is not merely a technical issue but a democratic imperative, as transparency is the foundation of informed citizen participation and effective anti-corruption efforts.
