Revealed: Durham modules ranked by student attainment

Open data, specifically derived from Freedom of Information Requests, provides a transparent mechanism for analyzing academic performance trends within universities. By leveraging publicly accessible datasets, institutions like Palatinate can reveal detailed insights into undergraduate attainment rates across various departments and years. This approach highlights how data transparency allows for the benchmarking of educational outcomes, enabling stakeholders to understand performance disparities without relying on opaque institutional reports. The analysis underscores significant variations in student achievement, identifying Physics and English as top-performing departments while noting lower attainment in foundation years and specific languages. It also exposes stark contrasts in module difficulty and grading rigor, ranging from high success rates in business and classics to near-zero first-class awards in certain art and language courses. These findings suggest that grading standards are not uniform, raising questions about the consistency of academic expectations across different disciplines and year levels. This information is crucial for the open_data community as it demonstrates the practical value of FOI-driven transparency in education. It empowers students and researchers to make informed decisions about course selection and workload management based on historical evidence rather than anecdotal advice. Furthermore, it advocates for greater institutional accountability, showing how public access to granular academic data can drive improvements in curriculum design and assessment fairness.
