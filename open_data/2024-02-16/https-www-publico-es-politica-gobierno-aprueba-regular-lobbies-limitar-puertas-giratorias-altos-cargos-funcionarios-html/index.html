The Spanish government has approved a draft law to regulate, for the first time, the activities of interest groups, commonly known as lobbies. This initiative aims to improve democratic quality and transparency by establishing a public and mandatory register managed by the Office for Conflicts of Interest. The objective is to clarify the rules governing the relationship between those seeking to influence decision-making and public officials, ensuring that these interactions are conducted under principles of integrity and with full visibility for citizens. The legislation introduces strict measures to prevent conflicts of interest, such as limiting “revolving door” practices by prohibiting senior officials from engaging in lobbying activities on matters related to their previous responsibilities for two years after leaving office. Additionally, it establishes a legislative footprint system to document legislative changes resulting from such pressures, along with a code of conduct that prohibits practices like the offering of gifts. In this way, the aim is to ensure that lobbying activities are legitimate and do not compromise the impartiality of the administration. This article is relevant to open data because it consolidates the creation of an electronic, public, and accessible register of interest groups. By digitizing and opening this information through the Transparency Portal and the OCI website, the management of political influence is transformed into a set of open and standardized data. This enables external scrutiny, academic research, and citizen oversight through data analysis tools, fostering greater accountability in the relationship between the public sector and private interests.
