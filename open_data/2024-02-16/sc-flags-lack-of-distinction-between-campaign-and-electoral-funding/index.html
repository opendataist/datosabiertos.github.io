SC flags lack of distinction between 'campaign and electoral funding'

The Supreme Court struck down the electoral bond scheme, emphasizing that transparency is crucial for a healthy democracy. The ruling highlights that political funding involves more than just election campaigns, making the distinction between campaign and general party expenses essential. By obscuring donor identities, the previous system hindered voters' ability to make informed decisions, thereby exacerbating the link between economic inequality and political inequality. The Court rejected the government's argument that the scheme was merely an economic policy with limited judicial review. Instead, it affirmed that the measure was an electoral reform requiring strict scrutiny. The judgments found the scheme’s justification—protecting donors from retaliation—to be inconsistent and flawed, as secrecy itself restricts the public’s right to know. True protection cannot be achieved through opacity, which ultimately empowers those in power to access donor information disproportionately. This decision is highly relevant to open_data because it establishes a legal precedent mandating the disclosure of political financing information. It reinforces the principle that public interest in understanding political influence outweighs the claim of donor privacy. By ruling that transparency is the antidote to undemocratic practices, the judgment underscores the necessity of open data initiatives in ensuring accountability and equal participation in the political process.
