Supreme Court strikes down electoral bonds scheme: Explained in 5 points

The Supreme Court declared the electoral bonds scheme unconstitutional, ruling that it violates citizens' right to information and free speech. By allowing anonymous political donations, the system obscured vital funding details, thereby hindering voters' ability to make informed choices. This decision underscores that transparency is essential for democratic integrity, as secrecy in political financing undermines the fundamental right to know who influences policy. The judgment mandates the immediate cessation of new electoral bonds and orders the disclosure of past donation records to the Election Commission for public release. This unprecedented transparency requirement aims to expose potential quid pro quo arrangements and economic inequalities in political engagement. The court emphasized that absolute exemptions from disclosure cannot justify the lack of accountability, ensuring that citizens can scrutinize the financial backing of political entities. This ruling is highly relevant to open data advocacy, as it establishes legal precedents for mandating the publication of government-held financial records. It reinforces the principle that access to information is not merely a convenience but a constitutional right necessary for effective civic participation. Consequently, it validates the push for transparent, publicly accessible datasets regarding political funding to prevent corruption and ensure equitable democratic processes.
