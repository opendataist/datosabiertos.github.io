Electoral Bonds | Supreme Court Rejects Union's Argument That Citizens Have No Right To Know About Political Funding

The Supreme Court has declared the electoral bond scheme unconstitutional, ruling that anonymous political funding violates voters' fundamental right to information under Article 19(1)(a). The judgment establishes that transparency in political financing is not merely a bureaucratic formality but an essential component of democratic freedom. By anonymizing contributions, the scheme deprived citizens of critical context needed to make informed choices, thereby undermining the effectiveness of their vote. A central theme of the verdict is the inseparable link between money and politics, which creates risks of quid pro quo arrangements where financial support influences policy outcomes. The Court reasoned that voters must have access to data regarding political donations to assess whether there is a correlation between funding sources and legislative decisions. This insight empowers the electorate to hold representatives accountable, ensuring that public interest is not compromised by hidden financial incentives from powerful contributors. This decision is highly relevant to open data initiatives as it legally reinforces the principle that civic information should be accessible and transparent to ensure democratic integrity. It validates the use of media and public reporting to disseminate government data, shifting the burden of accessibility away from individual scrutiny. Consequently, this ruling supports the broader open data movement by affirming that unrestricted public access to political financing data is a constitutional necessity, fostering greater accountability and trust in democratic institutions.
