La inmigración empuja a Soria a ganar 769 habitantes en un año y vuelve a rebasar las 90.000 personas

The article highlights that immigration has become the primary driver of demographic growth in Soria and the broader Castile and León region, reversing the natural population decline. By offsetting losses among Spanish nationals with significant gains in foreign residents, these areas avoided the stagnation observed elsewhere. This trend underscores a structural shift, whereby demographic sustainability in rural areas increasingly depends on international mobility rather than natural birth rates. The data reveal a stark contrast between local successes and national averages. While national figures show modest growth driven by foreigners, rural provinces such as Soria managed to exceed 90,000 inhabitants for the first time. This success stands out against declining populations in other provinces, illustrating how targeted immigration can revitalize struggling local economies and maintain critical population thresholds that would otherwise be threatened by depopulation. This narrative is crucial for open data initiatives, as it demonstrates the power of public statistics to inform regional planning and social integration policies. Analyzing the correlation between migration flows and demographic recovery allows researchers to identify successful models of sustainable growth. Furthermore, it emphasizes the need for accessible, granular datasets that enable stakeholders to understand not only *who* is moving, but *why* certain regions thrive, thereby supporting evidence-based decisions in addressing future demographic challenges.
