Council U-turn on revealing final cost for Nuneaton's Bermuda bridge project

Warwickshire County Council has refused to disclose the final costs of the Nuneaton bridge project, citing commercial sensitivity and potential prejudice to third-party business interests. This decision contradicts earlier statements that true costs would be revealed upon completion, highlighting a disconnect between administrative promises and transparency obligations. By classifying the data as confidential, the council prioritizes protecting contractors' financial details over public accountability. The project’s cost has ballooned from an original estimate of £3.7 million to over £16 million, indicating significant budget overruns. Although the bridge has opened, the council maintains that final accounts are still being processed with contractors. This delay in releasing financial data prevents the public from understanding the extent of mismanagement or inefficiencies during the construction phase, raising concerns about fiscal oversight in public infrastructure projects. This situation is highly relevant to open data advocates because it exemplifies how commercial exemptions can be used to withhold essential public spending information. When government entities withhold cost data under the guise of protecting third-party interests, it undermines the principle that citizens have a right to know how their money is spent. Ensuring that project costs are accessible promotes greater transparency, facilitates better public scrutiny, and holds local authorities accountable for managing taxpayer funds responsibly.
