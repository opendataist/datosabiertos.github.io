Public Right, Private Privilege: Commercial Entities are Biggest FOIA Users

Public Right, Private Privilege: Commercial Entities are Biggest FOIA Users

This analysis reveals that commercial entities, particularly specialized information aggregators, dominate Freedom of Information Act requests to the Food and Drug Administration and the Securities and Exchange Commission. Rather than individual journalists or researchers, a small cluster of service companies and data brokers submits the majority of these inquiries. This trend highlights how modern transparency efforts are increasingly driven by automated data collection and competitive intelligence rather than traditional investigative journalism or public interest advocacy. The data further illustrates significant differences in agency protocols, such as the SEC’s allowance for unidentifiable requesters, which skews its statistics toward unknown entities. Meanwhile, law firms and news media remain consistent but secondary sources, often using FOIA requests as part of broader legal or investigative strategies. Financial institutions show lower but potentially high-impact request volumes, suggesting their inquiries carry specific predictive value for market trends despite their frequency. This information is relevant to open data advocates because it exposes the industrialization of public records access. When a few private entities monopolize the aggregation and distribution of government data, it raises concerns about equitable access and the commercialization of public information. Understanding these dynamics helps the open data community advocate for policies that ensure transparency tools remain accessible to the public and independent researchers, not just corporate intermediaries.
