U.S. presidential debate: press freedom is absent from campaigns yet crucial to debate

The United States faces a critical juncture for press freedom, ranking at a historic low as global protections erode. This decline underscores an urgent need for robust public policy, positioning the U.S. to lead by addressing the specific vulnerabilities journalists face today. The situation highlights why open data and transparent governance are essential for holding power accountable and ensuring a free press can operate without fear of retribution or suppression. Key reforms include protecting journalistic sources through federal shield legislation and redefining the Espionage Act to prevent it from being weaponized against publishers. Simultaneously, policymakers must address the safety of journalists in conflict zones and during domestic protests, while also establishing technical standards for AI to combat misinformation. These measures are crucial for maintaining the integrity of information ecosystems and protecting the individuals who gather and disseminate truth. The relevance to open data lies in the intersection of information integrity and governmental transparency. Without secure sources and protection from surveillance, journalists cannot effectively report on public matters, including data-driven investigations into government actions. Strengthening these legal and technological safeguards ensures that the flow of information remains open, verifiable, and protected, which is fundamental to a functioning democracy and the reliable use of public data for societal benefit.
