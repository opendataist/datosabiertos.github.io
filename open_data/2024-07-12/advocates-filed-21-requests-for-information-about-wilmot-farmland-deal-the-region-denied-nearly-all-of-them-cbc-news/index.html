Advocates filed 21 requests for information about Wilmot farmland deal. The region denied nearly all of them | CBC News

The Region of Waterloo faces intense scrutiny for denying freedom of information requests regarding its controversial plan to acquire farmland in Wilmot Township. Critics argue that the high rate of denials raises serious concerns about the transparency and legality of the land acquisition process. By withholding critical documents, the region is accused of keeping constituents in the dark about essential steps and potentially incomplete investigations into a massive infrastructure and industrial project. Central to this conflict is the region’s defense that negotiations with landowners must remain confidential, a stance opponents view as an unreasonable barrier to public accountability. The group Fight for Farmland seeks access to data on environmental impacts, infrastructure costs, and alternative site evaluations, asserting that such information should already be public. They contend that secrecy prevents citizens from understanding how tax-funded studies and planning decisions affect local resources and farmland preservation. This situation highlights the critical tension between government operational secrecy and the principles of open data. It underscores the necessity of robust transparency frameworks in public administration, ensuring that citizens can access information about significant land use changes. Protecting open data access in these contexts is essential for holding municipal authorities accountable and fostering trust in governance decisions that deeply impact community resources and private property rights.
