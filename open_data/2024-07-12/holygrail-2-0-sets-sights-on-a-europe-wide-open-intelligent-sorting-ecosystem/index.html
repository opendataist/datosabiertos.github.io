HolyGrail 2.0 sets sights on a Europe-wide open intelligent sorting ecosystem

The HolyGrail 2.0 initiative and GS1 are establishing an open data architecture to revolutionize packaging waste sorting. By adopting the widely used GTIN standard, they aim to create a unified system that accurately directs discarded materials into the correct recycling streams. This move addresses the urgent need for enhanced sorting infrastructure to meet European recycling mandates. Open standards and interoperable data exchange are central to this strategy, allowing diverse stakeholders from brand owners to recyclers to collaborate effectively. Defining clear roles and responsibilities ensures accountability across the complex value chain. This collaborative framework is essential for scaling intelligent sorting solutions and integrating various players into a cohesive digital ecosystem. This development is crucial for open data as it demonstrates how shared, transparent standards can drive sustainability. By prioritizing interoperability, the initiative fosters innovation and competition while laying the groundwork for a circular economy. It highlights the vital role of open data in creating scalable, environmentally responsible industrial systems.
