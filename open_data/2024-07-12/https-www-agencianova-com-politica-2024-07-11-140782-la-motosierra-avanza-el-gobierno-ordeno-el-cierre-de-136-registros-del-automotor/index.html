The state eliminates physical vehicle registration centers, centralizing services in a single national registry. This reform digitizes administrative processes, making in-person visits optional and streamlining documentation for citizens. By removing mandatory physical records, the government reduces bureaucracy and centralizes data management. This shift prioritizes digital accessibility, allowing users to conduct transactions online rather than visiting local offices. This case illustrates open data principles by reducing redundant physical records and consolidating information into a unified national system. It demonstrates how deregulation can enhance public access to services through digital infrastructure and centralized data governance.
