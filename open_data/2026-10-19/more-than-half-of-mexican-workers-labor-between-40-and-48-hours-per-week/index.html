More than half of Mexican workers labor between 40 and 48 hours per week

More than half of Mexican workers labor between 40 and 48 hours per week

The INEGI’s 2025 Intercensal Survey highlights that half of Mexico’s labor force works between forty and forty-eight hours per week, placing the debate over the gradual reduction of the workweek to forty hours by 2027 in a context of uncertainty. Specialist Jorge Sales Boyoli warns that it is impossible to determine with precision how many employees will be directly affected, since aggregate data do not break down the exact distribution of hours within this broad range. This statistical uncertainty underscores the need for finer-grained transparency in work-time recording. The transition to a forty-hour workweek will not be marginal; it will require significant adjustments in productive organization. Therefore, the implementation of electronic timekeeping systems and detailed monitoring mechanisms becomes crucial to assess the real impact and facilitate companies’ adaptation during this reform process. Furthermore, the analysis incorporates the variable of commute time, noting that for many workers this factor is as significant as the workweek itself. This perspective broadens the discussion on the reorganization of working time, suggesting that policies must holistically consider workers’ everyday employment experiences. This approach is vital for designing balanced labor reforms that improve quality of life without compromising productivity. This article is relevant to open data because it highlights the limitations of aggregate data in the face of the need for granularity in official statistics. For social reforms to be effective and equitable, access to disaggregated and detailed data is essential. Transparency in labor information enables society and experts to accurately analyze market realities, thereby grounding policy decisions in solid, detailed evidence.
