NUJS students RTI admin’s alleged burial of first-ever university review report, receive no response

The article highlights a significant transparency failure at the National University of Juridical Sciences, where the administration refuses to disclose a crucial review commission report despite it being submitted months ago. This lack of access underscores the critical importance of open data policies within educational institutions, as students are actively using the Right to Information Act to demand accountability and visibility into administrative operations that directly impact their academic environment. The controversy reveals how opaque governance can hinder institutional improvement, particularly when reviews address long-standing issues. By withholding such documents, the administration prevents stakeholders from understanding systemic challenges or celebrating potential reforms. This situation demonstrates that without mandated public access to institutional records, meaningful dialogue and effective oversight become nearly impossible, eroding trust between students and leadership. Ultimately, this case serves as a potent example of why open data is essential for democratic accountability in education. It illustrates the power of legal tools like RTI in breaking down information barriers imposed by unresponsive authorities. The students’ efforts emphasize that public scrutiny is necessary to ensure that critical administrative documents remain accessible, fostering a culture of transparency rather than secrecy in university management.
