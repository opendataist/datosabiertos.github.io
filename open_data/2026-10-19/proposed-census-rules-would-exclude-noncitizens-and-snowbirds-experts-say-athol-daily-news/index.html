Proposed census rules would exclude noncitizens and snowbirds, experts say - Athol Daily News

Proposed census rules would exclude noncitizens and snowbirds, experts say - Athol Daily News

U.S. Census Bureau proposals to exclude noncitizens from the population count would severely distort data in states like Massachusetts. By omitting a significant portion of the foreign-born population, the revised regulations would result in a massive undercount comparable to losing an entire major city. This inaccuracy undermines the fundamental integrity of the decennial census, which serves as the baseline for all future population estimates and demographic analyses. The exclusion of millions of residents carries profound financial and political consequences. Since census data dictates congressional apportionment and the distribution of federal funds, an inaccurate count translates to substantial economic losses for the state and reduced political representation. The potential loss of over a billion dollars annually in federal funding highlights how such methodological changes directly impact resource allocation and governance, creating long-term instability for public services and infrastructure planning. Furthermore, alterations to residence criteria and the enumeration period threaten to skew data by favoring southern states with seasonal migrant populations. These changes risk misrepresenting where individuals truly live and contribute, particularly affecting northern states with large "snowbird" populations. This relevance to open data lies in the critical need for accurate, unbiased statistics; when foundational datasets are manipulated, subsequent research, policy-making, and societal understanding based on that data become unreliable, eroding trust in public information systems.
