What are we worried about? INEGI survey reveals that young people are not thinking about having children or getting married

What are we worried about? INEGI survey reveals that young people are not thinking about having children or getting married

The 2025 Intercensal Survey conducted by Mexico’s National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) reveals a profound demographic transformation in Mexico City, characterized by a marked decline in fertility and accelerated population aging. This phenomenon is accompanied by a structural shift in household composition, with rising rates of singlehood and declining marriage rates, reflecting the erosion of traditional extended family support networks and the influence of urban living costs. Sociological explanations point to hyperindividualization and economic barriers, such as unaffordable housing, which discourage the formation of conventional family units. At the same time, the labor market shows increased participation, particularly among women, predominantly in the services sector, suggesting that residents of Mexico City prioritize personal autonomy and employability over inherited social and economic structures. This update is crucial for the field of open data, as it demonstrates how official statistical databases enable the monitoring of long-term social trends. By making these indicators accessible, it facilitates the analysis of public policies better aligned with contemporary realities, promoting transparency and evidence-based decision-making to address the challenges of a constantly evolving metropolis.
