ICIR tasks journalists on utilizing FOI Act

ICIR tasks journalists on utilizing FOI Act

The Freedom of Information Act serves as a critical investigative tool for journalists, enabling them to uncover truth and promote transparent governance. By actively requesting information from public entities, media professionals can significantly reduce corruption and abuse of power. This legal framework empowers citizens with the data needed for informed decision-making, thereby strengthening democratic accountability and encouraging responsible official conduct through increased scrutiny. However, implementation faces significant hurdles, including bureaucratic inefficiencies and a lack of standardized response mechanisms within government agencies. While the law mandates prompt replies, rigid timelines often clash with complex administrative realities, leading to delayed or denied information. Additionally, specific exemptions exist to protect certain sensitive records, balancing public access against broader national interests. These structural challenges highlight the gap between legislative intent and practical application in the public sector. This discussion is highly relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of proactive disclosure and enforceable compliance standards. Without consequences for non-compliance or incentives for transparency, the potential for open data remains unrealized. Strengthening these systems ensures that public information is accessible, reliable, and usable for journalism and civic engagement, ultimately fostering a culture of openness that benefits society as a whole.
