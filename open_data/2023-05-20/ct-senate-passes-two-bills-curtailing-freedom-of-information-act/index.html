CT Senate passes two bills curtailing Freedom of Information Act

CT Senate passes two bills curtailing Freedom of Information Act

Proposed legislation aims to restrict Freedom of Information Act disclosures to protect public employees and university researchers from harassment. This shift raises significant concerns for open data advocates, as limiting transparency can obscure public accountability regarding employee conduct and research integrity. The bills seek to exempt residential addresses of public workers and shield academic research records from disclosure. While proponents argue this prevents malicious targeting, critics warn it unnecessarily hinders legitimate journalism and public oversight, potentially shielding inappropriate behavior or conflicts of interest from community scrutiny. For open data, this trend threatens to reduce the availability of vital public records. Expanding exemptions undermines the principle that government data should remain accessible, making it harder to monitor how public resources are managed and ensuring that transparency does not erode under claims of privacy protection.
