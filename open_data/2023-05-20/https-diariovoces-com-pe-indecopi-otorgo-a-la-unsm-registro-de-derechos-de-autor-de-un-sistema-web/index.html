The Universidad Nacional de San Martín has recently obtained intellectual property rights for two distinct innovations, underscoring the institution’s commitment to leveraging technology for social and environmental impact. First, faculty inventors developed a web platform dedicated to preserving and disseminating the tangible and intangible cultural heritage of Lamas. This digital tool directly addresses the needs of the local community, serving as an accessible channel for sociocultural exchange and the safeguarding of Amazonian traditions. Second, the university secured a utility model patent for a telematic network system designed to quantify organic carbon in soil through CO₂ detection. This hardware solution integrates environmental monitoring sensors that transmit data wirelessly to a central server. By automating the measurement process, the system improves the accuracy and efficiency of carbon quantification, offering a scalable technological approach for environmental research and agricultural sustainability. These achievements are significant for open data, as they demonstrate how localized, problem-solving innovations generate structured, usable information assets. The cultural platform promotes public access to heritage data, while the environmental system produces standardized datasets crucial for climate studies. Both examples illustrate how institutional research can create open, replicable frameworks that empower communities and advance scientific understanding through transparent data generation.
