Senate Votes to Shield More Records from Freedom of Information Act | CT News Junkie

Connecticut lawmakers are advancing legislation that significantly restricts public access to government information by expanding exemptions under the state’s Freedom of Information Act. These proposals aim to shield higher education research records and the home addresses of all public sector employees from disclosure. While proponents cite safety concerns and harassment issues as justification, critics argue that these measures disproportionately reduce transparency and hide information from citizens without addressing underlying harassment laws. The first proposal creates a new exemption for state universities, allowing them to withhold research records based on testimony that current FOI requests border on harassment. Opponents counter that existing laws already protect individuals from threats and argue that creating new exemptions merely serves to obscure information. This debate highlights the tension between protecting individual privacy and maintaining the core principle that public records should remain accessible to ensure government accountability. Simultaneously, an existing exemption regarding employee home addresses is being broadened to cover all public workers, a move supported by labor unions but opposed by legislators who view it as a step backward for open government. For the open data community, these developments are critical because they signal a legislative trend toward reducing data availability. Such restrictions limit the ability of researchers, journalists, and citizens to analyze government operations, ultimately weakening the transparency that underpins democratic oversight and informed public discourse.
