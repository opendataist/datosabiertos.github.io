Hay que hacerlos cumplir: Urge Padrón de Deudores Alimentarios en Veracruz

Veracruz currently lacks a registry of child support debtors, with data showing that most court-ordered payments remain unpaid. This gap prevents authorities from enforcing accountability, prompting local legislators to urge judges to establish an official state registry. Such a system is vital for preparing national integration and ensuring legal compliance across the region. The proposed legislative reform aims to restrict employment, property sales, and travel for those who fail to meet their family obligations. By denying public sector jobs and political candidacies to debtors, the measure seeks to deter non-payment through significant social and professional consequences. This approach transforms unpaid debts into serious legal liabilities rather than mere private disputes. This case is relevant to open data because transparency in public registries is essential for effective policy enforcement. Without accessible, accurate records, governments cannot monitor compliance or protect vulnerable families. Creating open, standardized databases for social obligations ensures accountability and allows citizens to verify legal standing, fostering trust in judicial and administrative systems.
