Canada has quietly raised foreign interference with China 48 times in two years - National | Globalnews.ca

Diplomatic records reveal that Canadian officials have consistently engaged their Chinese counterparts regarding concerns over foreign interference and surveillance since late 2022. These quiet but frequent representations, including formal letters and discussions on overseas police stations, demonstrate that Beijing was aware of Ottawa’s apprehensions. This approach highlights a strategic preference for maintaining open, ongoing dialogue even with adversarial governments, rather than relying solely on public confrontation or severed ties. This diplomatic channeling serves as a critical mechanism for managing complex geopolitical relationships while addressing security threats. By keeping communication lines active, Canadian officials aim to signal disapproval of meddling in domestic politics without escalating tensions to a breaking point. This method underscores the complexity of modern diplomacy, where managing persistent threats requires continuous engagement and nuanced negotiation rather than isolated incidents of protest. This transparency is vital for open data because it provides empirical evidence of government responsiveness to security challenges. Public access to these records allows citizens to scrutinize how institutions handle foreign interference, fostering accountability in national security practices. As inquiries conclude, releasing such detailed documentation ensures that the public understands the balance between diplomatic necessity and the protection of democratic integrity, setting a precedent for future transparency in sensitive state affairs.
