Daniel Noboa emitió el reglamento a la Ley de Transparencia Social, que regula a las organizaciones de la sociedad civil

Daniel Noboa emitió el reglamento a la Ley de Transparencia Social, que regula a las organizaciones de la sociedad civil

The Ecuadorian government has enacted regulations for the Social Transparency Law, establishing a legal framework to monitor non-profit organizations. This measure aims to prevent irregular capital flows by requiring registration, risk classification, and strict oversight of resource usage. Consequently, entities must now navigate enhanced state control to ensure financial integrity and prevent the misuse of funds. Despite its economic urgency, the law faces significant legal challenges. Multiple constitutional lawsuits have been filed by human rights groups and indigenous leaders, arguing that the new provisions violate fundamental rights. These actions seek to invalidate specific articles, highlighting the tension between state security measures and civil liberties. This development is crucial for open data advocates because it expands the scope of mandatory public information. By digitizing and regulating non-profit data, the state creates a centralized repository accessible for transparency. However, the ongoing legal battles may delay data availability, emphasizing the need for vigilance to protect the public’s right to information against restrictive interpretations.
