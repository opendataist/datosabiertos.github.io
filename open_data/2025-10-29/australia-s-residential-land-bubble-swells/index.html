Australia's residential land bubble swells

Australia's residential land bubble swells

Recent national accounts reveal that Australian residential land values have surged dramatically, now comprising a significantly larger share of the housing stock than building structures. This shift indicates that soaring land costs, rather than construction expenses, are the primary driver behind the doubling of total housing asset values relative to GDP over the past three decades. Consequently, the housing market reflects a structural imbalance where land inflation dictates overall property prices. These escalating land costs create significant supply-side constraints, effectively shifting aggregate housing supply curves leftward and hindering the construction of new dwellings needed for population growth. This "land cost-push inflation" reduces the total capacity to build homes at any given price point, exacerbating the existing deficit in available housing. The market is thus constrained by the high price of the underlying land resource rather than the physical structures themselves. This context is vital for open data because transparent land valuation metrics expose the root causes of housing unaffordability and policy failure. By disaggregating land values from structural costs, researchers and citizens can critically assess government responses, such as high immigration levels, which worsen structural shortages without addressing the underlying land supply issues. Access to such granular data empowers public scrutiny of how land economics directly impacts social outcomes and economic stability.
