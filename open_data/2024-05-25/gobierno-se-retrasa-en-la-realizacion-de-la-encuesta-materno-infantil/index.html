Gobierno se retrasa en la realización de la Encuesta Materno Infantil

Gobierno se retrasa en la realización de la Encuesta Materno Infantil

The delay in appointing a permanent head to Guatemala’s National Statistics Institute (INE) has stalled the crucial Maternal and Child Health Survey, a process coordinated by the institution. While health authorities and security sectors are financially and operationally prepared to launch the study, the lack of INE leadership prevents the execution of this essential national effort. This administrative vacuum creates a significant bottleneck, halting data collection that has been pending for a decade. Relying on outdated statistics from ten years ago to guide current public policy generates serious inaccuracies in resource allocation and intervention strategies. Analysts emphasize that using obsolete data misleads decision-makers, potentially causing funds to be distributed based on historical patterns that no longer reflect the current nutritional reality. Consequently, governments risk implementing ineffective policies that fail to address the widespread and evolving nature of child malnutrition across the country. This situation is vital for the open data community as it highlights how administrative governance directly impacts the availability, accuracy, and timeliness of public information. When critical datasets remain locked due to bureaucratic delays, transparency suffers, and civic oversight becomes difficult. Ensuring that statistical institutes function independently and efficiently is essential to provide the reliable, up-to-date data required for evidence-based decision-making and effective social accountability.
