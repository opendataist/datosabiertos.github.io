Tiene el Instituto los días contados

Tiene el Instituto los días contados

The article details the constitutional reform that mandates the immediate extinction of autonomous transparency institutes, such as the Institute of Transparency and Access to Information of Chihuahua (IChITAIP), by March 2025. This significant institutional restructuring aims to eliminate functional duplication within the public administration by integrating these bodies into central executive departments or statistical agencies. Consequently, the specialized oversight currently provided by independent state bodies will be absorbed by the federal executive branch, fundamentally altering the organizational landscape of transparency governance in Mexico. The reform shifts the responsibility for guaranteeing access to information and protecting personal data from independent organisms to central government dependencies. While maintaining the legal validity of existing agreements and respecting the labor rights of current staff, the new framework establishes that internal control and review authorities will now reside within the centralized administration. This centralization implies that the mechanisms for reviewing access denials and enforcing data protection will no longer operate under independent guarantees, potentially impacting the autonomy and perceived impartiality of these oversight functions. This development is critically relevant to open data because it challenges the structural independence that traditionally safeguards the transparency of public information. When the entities responsible for enforcing openness become part of the executive branch, there is an inherent risk of conflicts of interest, as they would be overseeing the very institutions that generate the data. This consolidation raises concerns about the integrity, accessibility, and neutrality of open data ecosystems, suggesting a shift from independent oversight to administrative internal control, which could weaken the robustness of transparency rights.
