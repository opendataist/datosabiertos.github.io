The article addresses a significant polling error by the Des Moines Register, which incorrectly predicted a narrow Democratic lead in Iowa before Donald Trump won decisively. This miss led to the pollster’s retirement, yet Trump pursued a legal claim alleging consumer fraud. The author argues this lawsuit is frivolous, as inaccurate data does not constitute fraud, and suggests the true intent is to intimidate media outlets into self-censorship. For the open data community, this incident highlights the critical importance of transparency in polling methodologies and error margins. It serves as a cautionary tale about how statistical inaccuracies can be weaponized to undermine trust in data sources. Maintaining rigorous standards and clear communication about uncertainty is essential to preserve the integrity of public data against political manipulation. Ultimately, the piece emphasizes that accountability for poor data lies in professional reputation and public scrutiny, not in retaliatory litigation. By distinguishing between honest error and malicious intent, the article reinforces the value of factual accuracy in democratic discourse. This perspective is vital for open data advocates who strive to ensure information remains reliable and accessible, resisting efforts to distort truth for political gain.
