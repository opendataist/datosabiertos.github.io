Slowing U.S. wage growth in last quarter of 2022 may reduce need for larger interest rate hikes, experts say | amNewYork

Slowing wage growth signals a potential economic deceleration, influencing Federal Reserve decisions on interest rates to balance inflation control against recession risks. This shift suggests upcoming smaller rate hikes, reflecting a delicate transition in monetary policy as inflation shows signs of easing. The Federal Reserve aims to cool price pressures while sustaining growth, using borrowing costs to manage liquidity. By moderating rate increases, the central bank hopes to guide the economy away from overheating, ensuring that inflation declines without triggering severe downturns or disrupting recovery efforts. For open data enthusiasts, this dynamic highlights the critical need for real-time, granular economic datasets. Transparent access to wage and inflation metrics allows independent analysts to monitor policy impacts, fostering public accountability and enabling better-informed community insights into how macroeconomic shifts affect local labor markets and financial stability.
