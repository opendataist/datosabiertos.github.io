The introduction of a digital payment system for public spaces raises serious concerns regarding the security and management of sensitive personal data. Experts warn that collecting financial and geolocation information without transparent privacy policies exposes citizens to risks such as identity theft and unauthorized data sharing, reflecting a historical pattern of inadequate information treatment in the region. This situation is critical for open data principles because it highlights the lack of accountability in public technological initiatives. Despite the significant volume of data being gathered, key details regarding storage duration, access control, and anonymization remain undisclosed. Furthermore, the absence of basic security certificates and legal usage policies on the consortium’s website undermines trust and demonstrates a failure to prioritize user privacy and data protection standards. The narrative is disrupted by a separate, unrelated report on severe weather conditions in Asunción, where storms caused trees to fall and blocked streets. However, the primary implication for open data discourse lies in the urgent need for municipal transparency and robust cybersecurity frameworks. Without clear governance and secure infrastructure, the digitization of public services risks compromising citizen rights rather than enhancing accessibility.
