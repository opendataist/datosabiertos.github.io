Informalidad alcanza a 33 millones de personas en México, es el punto más alto en 2025

Informalidad alcanza a 33 millones de personas en México, es el punto más alto en 2025

Mexico’s growing labor informality highlights a critical structural failure in the national economy, where over half of the workforce lacks social protections and decent wages. This persistent gap between population growth and formal job creation undermines overall productivity and social security, signaling that current market conditions are insufficient to absorb new workers into stable employment. The data reveals a steady annual increase in informal employment, contrasting with marginal improvements in unemployment rates. This trend demonstrates that while overall joblessness remains low, the quality of employment is deteriorating. The lack of formal opportunities forces more individuals into precarious conditions, deepening socioeconomic inequalities and reflecting a systemic inability of the labor market to generate the necessary number of quality jobs. This situation is vital for the open data community because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible employment statistics to drive policy reform. Reliable data allows researchers and advocates to identify these structural inefficiencies and push for evidence-based interventions. By making such labor metrics open, society can better monitor progress, hold institutions accountable, and design strategies that effectively transition workers from the informal to the formal economy.
