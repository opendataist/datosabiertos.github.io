The Freedom of Information Act Is Failing Due to Government Bloat

The Freedom of Information Act Is Failing Due to Government Bloat

The Freedom of Information Act is failing to ensure government transparency, as bureaucratic inefficiency has drastically increased request wait times. These excessive delays undermine the law’s core purpose of holding federal agencies accountable to the public. By allowing responses to stretch into months or years, the system effectively silences citizens seeking crucial information, rendering the mechanism of open inquiry largely obsolete. The scale of this failure is evident in the exploding backlog of unprocessed requests across federal departments. Such delays are not merely technical glitches but often stem from systemic understaffing and, in some instances, deliberate evasion by officials who obstruct access to records. This widespread administrative bloat transforms a right into a privilege, preventing timely disclosure and obscuring the actions of those in power from public scrutiny. This situation is critically relevant to open data advocates because it highlights the gap between legal mandates for transparency and actual data availability. When governments hoard or lose records, they prevent the creation of accessible, reusable datasets. Achieving true open data requires not just legal frameworks, but functional, well-resourced systems that prioritize rapid, verifiable data release, ensuring that information remains a public asset rather than a guarded secret.
