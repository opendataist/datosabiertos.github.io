Un registro civil para todos: JCE garantiza acceso abierto a datos vitales

Un registro civil para todos: JCE garantiza acceso abierto a datos vitales

The Dominican Republic’s Central Electoral Board has launched Onarec, a digital observatory for the National Civil Registry system, marking a significant step toward open data governance. This platform centralizes vital statistics—including births, marriages, divorces, and deaths—enabling citizens to access this information weekly from anywhere in the world. By modernizing these records, the initiative aims to ensure equitable and timely access to fundamental rights, transforming how the state manages and shares critical demographic data with the public. The core implication of this move is the democratization of information, removing traditional geographical and administrative barriers that previously hindered access to identity-related documents. This shift not only strengthens legal security but also enhances institutional transparency and trust. By making data openly and consistently available, the government is fostering a more inclusive environment in which citizens can exercise their right to information without facing the logistical challenges that existed under the previous centralized and less accessible system. This development is highly relevant to open data, as it demonstrates how public institutions can leverage digital tools to promote transparency, innovation, and citizen participation. Open data enables evidence-based public policy design through detailed, up-to-date statistics and facilitates interoperability among government systems. Ultimately, Onarec serves as a model for how releasing raw, accessible data can drive democratic development, allowing society to harness information as a powerful engine for social progress and accountable governance.
