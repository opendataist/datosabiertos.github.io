Industria musical alemana le gana un juicio a OpenAI

Industria musical alemana le gana un juicio a OpenAI

The Munich court has set a historic precedent by ruling that OpenAI infringed copyright by using song lyrics to train its language models and reproducing them in ChatGPT’s responses. This decision, which rejects the company’s defense that the data was learned indirectly without direct copying, confirms that the unauthorized ingestion of copyrighted works for artificial intelligence development constitutes a legal violation, obliging the company to compensate rights holders. This ruling is pivotal for the open data ecosystem and digital intellectual property, as it challenges the supposed neutrality and free access to information used to feed algorithms. By recognizing that the “indirect perception” of a work is sufficient to consider it reproduced, the judgment highlights the need for transparency in training datasets. This forces technology companies to reconsider their practices, shifting from mass data harvesting toward models that respect licenses and provide economic compensation to original creators. The relevance for the open data community lies in the boost this case gives to European regulation, such as the AI Act, seeking to balance technological innovation with authors’ rights. The ruling sends a clear global signal about the importance of ethical data governance, demonstrating that access to technology does not justify the exploitation of intellectual property. This milestone lays the groundwork for demanding stricter transparency and licensing standards in the use of creative content, affecting not only the music industry but any sector reliant on protected data.
