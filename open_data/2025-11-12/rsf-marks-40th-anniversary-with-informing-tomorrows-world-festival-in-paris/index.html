RSF marks 40th anniversary with “Informing Tomorrow’s World” festival in Paris

RSF marks 40th anniversary with “Informing Tomorrow’s World” festival in Paris

The Reporters Without Borders festival at the Gaîté Lyrique highlights the urgent need for transparent, reliable information in an era defined by disinformation and technological disruption. By convening global journalists and activists, the event emphasizes that independent journalism is essential for maintaining public trust and navigating complex challenges like artificial intelligence and media consolidation. This dialogue underscores the critical role of open access to information in fostering a resilient and informed society. The event’s international scope reinforces the necessity of borderless solidarity networks that protect press freedom and support threatened journalists worldwide. By gathering representatives from numerous countries, the initiative illustrates how collaborative defense mechanisms are vital for sustaining free speech across diverse geopolitical landscapes. This collective effort demonstrates that protecting the right to report is a universal responsibility, crucial for empowering communities and ensuring that marginalized voices remain heard in the global discourse. Additionally, the festival uses art and photojournalism to humanize the struggle for truth, linking visual storytelling with the broader mission of open data principles. By showcasing works that reflect societal fissures and resilience, the program argues that accessible, unbiased narratives are foundational to democratic engagement. This approach reinforces the relevance of open data by demonstrating that transparent, shareable information—whether statistical or visual—is indispensable for understanding reality and driving meaningful social change in tomorrow’s world.
