Hipotecas a 50 años, ¿son la solución para comprarse una casa en Mallorca?

Hipotecas a 50 años, ¿son la solución para comprarse una casa en Mallorca?

The article argues that extending mortgage terms to fifty years is not the definitive solution to the housing accessibility crisis in the Balearic Islands. While longer terms reduce monthly installments, they do not address the core structural barrier: the substantial initial savings required, often around thirty percent of the property's value. This upfront capital requirement prevents many families from entering the market, regardless of their ability to afford lower monthly payments over a longer horizon. Furthermore, the economist highlights the significant long-term financial cost of this strategy. Extending the loan duration drastically increases the total interest paid, often resulting in borrowers paying nearly double the original loan amount. Consequently, while extending the term might slightly lower the income threshold needed for approval, it exacerbates the overall debt burden without resolving the fundamental issue of high property prices and entry costs. This analysis is relevant to open data because it underscores the necessity of transparent, accessible datasets on housing costs, savings requirements, and loan structures. To evaluate policy interventions effectively, researchers and policymakers need granular data to simulate how changes in financial parameters affect different socioeconomic groups. Open data enables a clearer understanding of these trade-offs, ensuring that housing solutions are based on comprehensive evidence rather than superficial metrics like monthly payments alone.
