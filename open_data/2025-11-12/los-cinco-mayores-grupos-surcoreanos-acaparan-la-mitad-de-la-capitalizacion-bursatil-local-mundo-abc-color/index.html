South Korea’s stock market has experienced significant growth, fueled by surging valuations in the semiconductor, shipbuilding, and defense sectors. This expansion is heavily concentrated among major conglomerates, particularly Samsung and SK, which have captured a disproportionate share of total market capitalization. The trend underscores a deepening dependency on a few key corporate groups and specific industrial sectors. This concentration highlights a structural shift in the national economy, where wealth accumulation is increasingly tied to a limited number of powerful entities. The dominance of these groups suggests that broader market indices may not accurately reflect the performance of the wider corporate landscape, potentially masking inequalities in value distribution. For open data enthusiasts, this case illustrates the critical need for granular, accessible datasets to analyze market dynamics beyond aggregate indices. Transparent data reveals how economic power consolidates, enabling researchers and policymakers to monitor monopolistic tendencies and understand the true scope of market concentration in emerging economies.
