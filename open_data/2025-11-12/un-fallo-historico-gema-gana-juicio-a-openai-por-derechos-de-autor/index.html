Un fallo histórico: GEMA gana juicio a OpenAI por derechos de autor

Un fallo histórico: GEMA gana juicio a OpenAI por derechos de autor

A German court has ruled that OpenAI infringed copyright laws by using song lyrics to train its artificial intelligence models, setting a crucial legal precedent in Europe. The court rejected the company’s argument that it did not store specific data, concluding that the memorization and reproduction of these works constitute direct violations, thereby granting creators the right to receive compensation for the unauthorized use of their intellectual property. This ruling implies that AI training is not exempt from copyright regulations, forcing technology companies to reconsider their data acquisition practices. By recognizing authors’ rights in the face of technological development, the decision prioritizes fair remuneration for creative artists over the unrestricted availability of content for algorithm training, marking a turning point in the legal accountability of the sector. The relevance to open data lies in the urgent need to balance open innovation with the protection of intellectual property rights. This case underscores that datasets used to train AI must have appropriate licenses, directly affecting how public or protected data are shared and accessed in open-source projects, and demanding greater transparency regarding the origin of the information used to develop artificial intelligence models.
