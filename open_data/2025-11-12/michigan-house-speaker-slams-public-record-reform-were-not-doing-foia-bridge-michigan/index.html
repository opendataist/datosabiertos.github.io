Michigan House Speaker slams public record reform: ‘We’re not doing FOIA’ - Bridge Michigan

Michigan remains an outlier as one of only two states exempting its legislature and governor from Freedom of Information Act requests. House Speaker Matt Hall has effectively blocked recent bipartisan efforts to expand public records access, reversing his previous support for such reforms. By prioritizing alternative ethics measures over transparency legislation, Hall ensures that the state’s commitment to open government records will not be strengthened through the legislative process this year. The failure to pass these bills highlights the critical role of legislative bodies in maintaining democratic accountability. Without statutory changes, citizens lack the legal mechanism to scrutinize how tax dollars are spent or how lobbyists influence policy. This exemption contributes to poor national rankings for anti-corruption systems and public trust, demonstrating that without robust open data frameworks, the risk of hidden governance practices increases significantly. This situation underscores the vital importance of open data as a foundational element of transparent governance. The struggle in Michigan illustrates how legislative inaction can stall public oversight, forcing advocates toward costly and uncertain ballot initiatives. It serves as a reminder that accessible, comprehensive data on government actions is essential for holding officials accountable and ensuring that the public can make informed decisions about their representatives.
